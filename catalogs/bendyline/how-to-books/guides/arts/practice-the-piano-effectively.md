# Practice the piano effectively

The concert pianist Josef Hofmann wrote down the practice habits that worked for him, and Robert Schumann left a set of short rules for young musicians. Both agree that how you practice matters more than how long. Concentrated, attentive work beats hours of mechanical repetition.

## What you need

- A piano kept in tune. Schumann: "Take care always to have your instrument well tuned."
- A clean keyboard. Hofmann says to wash it before you play, because "you cannot do clean work on an unclean keyboard."
- A bench or chair that lets you sit straight.
- Your music, a pencil, and a metronome for occasional checks.

## Steps

1. Practice in the morning if you can. Hofmann found the "mental freshness gained from sleep" a great help.
2. Never practice more than an hour, or at most two, at a stretch. Rest briefly after every half hour; five minutes is often enough. After a session, Hofmann takes a walk and thinks "no more of music" so the new work can settle.
3. Sit straight but not stiff, with your feet ready at the pedals. Keep your arms and shoulders loose and your hands nearly level. Curve your fingers gently and strike the keys near their centers with the fingertips.
4. Warm up with scales in all keys, playing each at least twice, slowly and then a little faster. Don't play fast until both hands are perfectly even and free of wrong notes. Hofmann limits finger exercises to half an hour a day at most and treats them only as "preliminary warmers."
5. Find the weak spots in the pieces you are learning, and practice those first.
6. Put each repaired passage back into its setting. Play it with one measure before and one after, then two on each side, then three, until it holds up inside the piece.
7. Listen to every note. Hofmann insists that each note be heard "not mentally but physically," and that speed must always wait on that. Practice mostly with "restrained power," and play loudly only now and then to build endurance.
8. Treat slow practice as "the basis for quick playing," but try faster tempos from time to time. Then return to slow practice to clean up anything that slipped.
9. Use the metronome only to test whether a passage holds steady, then turn it off.
10. Memorize in stages. Analyze a short piece's form and play it carefully from the music several times. Then stop for a few hours and try to hear it in your head. Fill any gaps by reading the score away from the piano, then try again later. Don't skip over a shaky spot; go back and fix it.
11. Keep pieces you have learned alive by playing each a few times a week, cycling through them rather than repeating one.

## From the source books

- Hofmann suggests a three-hour day of two hours in the morning and one in the afternoon. He would spend the morning on technique and the hardest passages, and the afternoon on interpretation.
- He warns against practicing "systematically," playing the same things in the same order every day, because it dulls spontaneity. As a warm-up, he finds five minutes with the hands in hot water works as well as exercises.
- Schumann's rules complement Hofmann's. Train your ear until you can name the notes of a bell or a cuckoo, and learn to hum a piece without the instrument. "Play *strictly in time*!" Play easy pieces well rather than hard ones badly. Read a new piece through before playing it at sight, and when you are tired, rest instead of forcing more work.
- The two disagree a little about time. Schumann stresses strict time, while Hofmann wants a flexible "individual pulse-beat" that still adds up to the right overall length.

## Safety and modern practice

- Stop if you feel pain, numbness or lasting tension in your hands, wrists or forearms. Overuse injuries are common among pianists. Shorter sessions and a relaxed technique help prevent them.
- If you warm your hands in water as Hofmann did, use comfortably warm water, not hot.
- Clean keys with a barely damp, soft cloth, never a soaking one or harsh cleaners, especially on old ivory keys.
- Have an acoustic piano tuned by a qualified technician at least once a year. Keep digital pianos and headphones at moderate volume to protect your hearing.
