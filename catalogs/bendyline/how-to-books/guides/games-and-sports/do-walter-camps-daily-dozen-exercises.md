# Do Walter Camp's Daily Dozen exercises

Walter Camp's "Daily Dozen" is a short set of setting-up exercises that he says was tested on thousands of men. The twelve movements fall into four groups, and the names in each group start with the same letter. Each one is done slowly, so it stretches you without tiring you out. Camp promised that "a conscientious fifteen minutes a day" would do more good than "strong-man stunts."

## What you need

- Enough clear floor space to stretch your arms out to the sides and touch the floor.
- Loose clothing.
- A steady count, said aloud by you or a partner. Most movements are timed to "One, two, three, four."

## Steps

1. Stand at "Attention." Put your heels together with the feet turned out at about 60 degrees, and keep your knees straight but not stiff. Push the back of your neck back, roll the shoulders down and back, lift the chest and pull the stomach in a little.
2. Group I has three positions. "Hands" is Attention with the arms hanging. "Hips" puts your hands on your hips, thumbs back. "Head" puts your hands behind your neck with the elbows back. Go through them a few times, then do them as fast as you can while keeping each one correct. Camp calls this the "Speed Test."
3. The rest start from "Cross": hold your arms straight out to the sides at shoulder height with the elbows locked.
4. Grind: from Cross, turn your palms up. To a count of ten, draw 12-inch circles with your fingertips, pivoting from the shoulders. Then reverse.
5. Grate: from Cross, breathe in while you raise your arms to 45 degrees and rise onto the balls of your feet. Breathe out and come back down. Do it ten times.
6. Grasp: hands behind your head. Over four counts, bend forward from the waist as far as you can with your head up. Come back up over four counts, then bend back only as far as is comfortable. Do it five times.
7. Crawl: raise your left arm straight up and drop your right arm to your side. Bend to the right, sliding your right hand down your leg toward the knee and curving your left arm over your head. Repeat on the other side.
8. Curl: with your heels about 12 inches apart, breathe in while you curl your fists up into your armpits and press your head back. Then reach forward, breathe out, bend at the waist and sweep your arms back and up. Straighten up. Do it five times.
9. Crouch: with your heels about 12 inches apart, bend your knees and sink almost to your heels on your toes, keeping your back upright. Rise. Do it ten times.
10. Wave: lace your fingers overhead with your arms against your ears. Bending only at the waist, draw a 24-inch circle with your hands: forward, right, back, left. Do five circles each way.
11. Weave: from Cross, with your feet apart, twist left. Then bend to touch your right fingertips to the floor between your feet, with your left arm pointing up. Alternate sides, ten times in all.
12. Wing: raise your arms out to the sides and up while you breathe in fully. Swing them down as you bend forward and breathe out, pressing them back and up. Straighten up slowly. Do it five times, breathing through your nose.

## From the source books

- Camp says the benefit comes from "steady, deliberate, but not extreme stretching," not from snap. Every movement except the Speed Test should be slow.
- At first, don't slide your hand far down your leg in Crawl, and keep the Wave modest for a week or so. Typical faults are letting the head poke forward, letting the arms sag below shoulder level in Grate, and leaning farther forward than back in Wave.
- Each chapter ends with "health maxims." Some still hold up, such as breathing through the nose. Others are period opinion, such as drinking water only between meals.

## Safety and modern practice

- Stay within a pain-free range, especially in the backward bends. If you have back, neck, knee or balance problems, or have had recent surgery, ask a clinician first.
- Don't strain while holding your breath in Curl and Wing.
- Crouch is a deep squat. Go only partway down if your knees hurt, and hold onto a chair if your balance is unsteady.
- Treat the Daily Dozen as a mobility routine. Current US guidelines also call for about 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week, plus muscle-strengthening exercise on two days.
