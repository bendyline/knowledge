# Knit a garter-stitch scarf

A garter-stitch scarf is the classic first knitting project. Every row is plain knit stitch, giving a thick, reversible fabric of ridges that lies flat. You need only three skills: casting on, knitting and binding off.

## What you need

- Wool yarn. The *Handbook of Wool Knitting and Crochet* uses Shetland wool for its "Sport Scarf," with optional contrasting colors for stripes
- A pair of knitting needles. The Handbook calls for "No. 3½ or No. 4 bone needles"
- Scissors, a blunt yarn (tapestry) needle, and extra yarn for fringe

## Steps

1. Buy all the yarn at once, with the same dye lot number on every skein. *Begin to Knit* warns that lots that look alike in the skein often differ in the finished work.
2. Wind skeins into loose balls, winding over your fingers and changing direction often so the yarn is not stretched.
3. Measure a tail for casting on: about 1/2 inch per stitch for fine yarn, or 1 inch per stitch for heavy yarn.
4. Make a slip knot on one needle. Hold the tail over your left thumb and the working yarn over your left forefinger. Put the needle up through the loop on your thumb, catch the yarn on your forefinger, draw it through, drop the thumb loop and snug the stitch. Repeat.
5. Cast on 84 stitches for the Handbook's Sport Scarf. For a lighter, more open scarf, cast on 48 or 50 stitches on No. 4 needles.
6. Hold the needle with the stitches in your left hand and keep the yarn behind the work. Put the right needle into the front of the first stitch from left to right, wrap the yarn under and over its point, draw the loop through and slip the old stitch off.
7. Knit every stitch across, turn the work, put the full needle in your left hand and knit the next row the same way. Knitting every row is garter stitch, and two rows make one ridge.
8. To add stripes, join the new color at the start of a row. Work a few stitches, leave about 4 inches of each end, and tie the ends firmly to weave in later.
9. Keep knitting until the scarf is as long as you want. The Sport Scarf works 64 rows of the main color between bands of stripes, such as four rows of cream and two of brown repeated five times.
10. Bind off loosely: knit two stitches, lift the first over the second and off the needle, knit one more and repeat. When one stitch is left, cut the yarn and pull it through.
11. Thread each loose end into the yarn needle and run it through several stitches on the wrong side.
12. For fringe, cut 6-inch strands and knot one into each stitch at both ends.

## From the source books

- The *Handbook of Wool Knitting and Crochet* also describes a two-needle cast-on, which "many like" when there are a great many stitches, and suggests doubling the yarn for an edge that gets hard wear.
- The Handbook slips the first stitch of each row where edges will be joined, and says garter stitch looks especially good knitted through the back of each stitch.
- The Handbook's garter-stitch paragraph confuses garter with stockinette (alternating knit and purl rows). *Begin to Knit* gives the standard definition: "K each st, K each row."
- *Begin to Knit* advises practicing first with heavy yarn and thick needles. If you set the work aside for days, rip back a few rows before restarting so the tension stays even.
- Cornelia Mee's *Exercises in Knitting* gives a striped "comforter" (an old word for a long scarf) of 48 stitches: 20 rows of one color, then alternating 12-row stripes, with a knitted fringe sewn to each end. She works it in "double knitting," a different stitch from garter.

## Safety and modern practice

- Old needle numbers do not match modern sizes. Choose needles from the size printed on your yarn label, and knit a small sample to check that the fabric feels right.
- To freshen the finished scarf, follow the yarn label. *Begin to Knit* says to squeeze gently, never twist or wring, press out the water in a towel, then dry it flat on a towel.
- Keep long scarves away from moving machinery and open flame, and avoid them on very young children because of strangulation risk.
