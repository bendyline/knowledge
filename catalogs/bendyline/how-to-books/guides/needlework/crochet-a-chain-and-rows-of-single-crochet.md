# Crochet a chain and rows of single crochet

Almost all crochet starts from a chain, and single crochet is the simplest stitch for building solid fabric on it. A small practice strip teaches the motions, and with them you can already make something like the narrow doll's scarf in the Mary Frances book.

## What you need

- Soft wool yarn. The Mary Frances book uses four-fold Germantown wool, a medium-weight yarn
- A smooth crochet hook. Mary Frances uses a bone hook "about a number 5"
- Scissors and a blunt yarn needle
- For the doll's scarf: one color for the body and a contrasting color for fringe

## Steps

1. Hold the hook in your right hand much like a pen. Run the yarn over your left forefinger, under the second finger, over the third and under the little finger, and hold the work between your left thumb and second finger.
2. Make a loop on the hook. Point the hook away from you and turn it completely around to catch a loop, then pinch the crossed threads with your left thumb and second finger.
3. To chain, catch the yarn with the hook (this is "wrapping" the yarn) and pull it through the loop on the hook. Snug the new loop and repeat. Keep each loop the same size.
4. Chain 15 for a practice piece, or 7 for the doll's scarf.
5. Start the first row of single crochet in the second chain from the hook, skipping one.
6. Wrap the yarn and draw a loop through the chain, so there are two loops on the hook. Wrap again and pull through both loops.
7. Work one single crochet into each remaining chain to the end of the row.
8. At the end of the row, chain one, then turn the work over so the row just made points the same way as the hook. The Mary Frances book says turning chains are always needed "to keep the edges even."
9. Work the next row by putting the hook under both threads at the top of each stitch in the row below.
10. Repeat rows until the piece is long enough. The doll's scarf has 6 stitches in each of 62 rows.
11. To fasten off, break the yarn, chain one and pull the end all the way through that chain.
12. For fringe, fold two 3-inch strands in half, pull the fold partway through an end stitch with the hook, then pull the four ends through that loop and tighten. Always pull fringe through from the same side.
13. When you are ready for a taller stitch, try double crochet. Wrap the yarn first, insert the hook in the third chain from the hook, and draw up a loop. Wrap and pull through two loops, then wrap and pull through the last two.

## From the source books

- **Names differ between books.** The Mary Frances book uses the names most US patterns use today. The *Handbook of Wool Knitting and Crochet* uses older English names, so its "double crochet" is the US single crochet and its "treble" is the US double crochet. The Handbook's own "single crochet" is a slip stitch that pulls through work and loop together. Check which system a pattern uses before you start.
- The Handbook's English teacher said to hold "nothing but" the hook in the right hand and never rest those fingers on the work, which keeps yarn work "soft and fluffy."
- The Handbook's turning rule: one chain for its "double," two for a half treble, three for a treble, and one more for each extra "draw."
- It advises choosing a smooth hook that carries the yarn without catching. A hook that is too large makes the work "sleazy" (limp and open).
- If your stitches come out too tight, the Mary Frances book says to use a larger hook. If a finished piece curls, lay it under a damp cloth overnight.

## Safety and modern practice

- Today's hooks are labeled in millimeters. Use the hook size printed on your yarn label rather than an old book number.
- Keep a relaxed grip and take breaks. A tight hold on a thin hook for long sessions can strain the hand and wrist.
- Beads and other small parts on doll items, like the Mary Frances beaded chain necklace, are a choking hazard. Keep them away from babies and toddlers.
