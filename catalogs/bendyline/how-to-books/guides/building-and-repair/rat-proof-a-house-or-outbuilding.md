# Rat-proof a house or outbuilding

Rat proofing means building rats out instead of killing them. The USDA authors Silver, Betts and Crouch give two principles: make the outside of gnaw-proof materials with every opening closed or screened, and leave no "dead spaces" inside where rats can hide. Rats that find no shelter near food tend to leave on their own.

## What you need

- Metal trash cans with tight lids
- Concrete and cement mortar for floors, foundations and patching
- Galvanized sheet metal or flashing; galvanized hardware cloth or heavy woven wire
- Gratings or screens with openings no larger than 1/2 inch square
- Door closers or springs, and metal sheathing for the bottoms of doors
- Blocks or platforms to raise stored materials at least 18 inches off the ground

## Steps

1. Clean up outside first. Keep all garbage in tight metal cans, remove trash piles, and raise lumber, firewood and stored goods on platforms 18 inches or more off the ground. Move woodpiles and manure or hay piles well away from wherever food is kept.
2. Remove or open up shelters near the ground. Wooden floors, sidewalks, porches and steps set only a few inches off the soil are prime nesting sites. Replace them with concrete, or raise them so the space beneath is open to light.
3. Inspect the basement or crawl space. Repair broken floors with concrete and fit tight covers on floor drains.
4. Close every hole in the foundation and walls with cement mortar. Pay particular attention to gaps around pipes and wires, around window and door frames, and unpointed joints in stone or brick, especially behind porches and platforms.
5. Screen windows, vents and ventilators with gratings or mesh no coarser than 1/2 inch.
6. Make outside doors fit snugly, sheath their bottoms with metal, and fit springs or closers so they stay shut.
7. Block hollow walls at the bottom. In frame walls that open into a basement or crawl space, fill the stud spaces 8 to 10 inches above the sill with concrete or masonry, or nail galvanized sheet metal across them at the sill, joists and floor. This is the same fire stopping that many codes require.
8. Close openings where pipes and wires pass through floors and partitions with metal flashing. Open up or seal boxed-in plumbing, and the spaces behind and under built-in cabinets and counters.
9. Deal with the space under the building. If the floor framing is less than 2 feet above the ground, either raise the building on piers to give 2 feet of open clearance, or build a continuous concrete foundation or "curtain wall" around the outside. Carry it at least 2 feet below and 1 foot above grade, since rats seldom burrow deeper than 2 feet.
10. Stop climbers. Where rats can climb rough walls or corners, fix a smooth band of sheet metal about 8 inches wide; rats cannot grip it. On posts or piers, fit metal collars reaching at least 9 inches out from the post.
11. In areas with roof rats, which climb well, also screen attic vents and unused chimney flues and close openings under the eaves.

## From the source books

- The authors found that fibrous insulating boards lining farm buildings greatly increased infestations, because rats gnaw through them easily and nest inside.
- For slatted corncribs, they line the inside with galvanized woven wire of two or three meshes to the inch, or run wire netting 2 feet up from the foundation topped by an 8-inch metal band.
- They advise raising poultry nests 2 feet or more and feed hoppers at least 1 foot, so rats find no shelter under them. Rats feeding at a poultry house may live 50 feet or more away.

## Safety and modern practice

- Check for signs of rats still inside before sealing the last opening, so none are trapped in the walls. Call a professional if an infestation is established.
- Rodent droppings and nests can carry disease, including hantavirus. Do not sweep or vacuum them dry. Ventilate the space, wear gloves and an N95 or better respirator, soak the material with disinfectant, then wipe it up.
- Modern guidance uses 1/4-inch galvanized hardware cloth where mice are also a concern. Steel wool or copper mesh packed into small gaps and sealed with mortar or sealant also works; plain foam or caulk alone is chewed through.
- Keep crawl-space vents screened, not blocked, so the space stays dry. Foundation and framing changes may need a building permit.
- Wear eye protection and cut-resistant gloves when cutting sheet metal and mesh.
