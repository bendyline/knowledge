# Prevent fires at home

Most house fires start small and from ordinary carelessness. Ethel Peyser listed the causes as "carelessness, ignorance and panic." The household writers' remedy is a habit of small checks. Keep fuel away from heat, watch anything that burns or plugs in, and keep the means to fight a small fire close at hand.

## What you need

- Metal cans with tight lids for oily rags, polishes and cleaning fluids, and a separate metal can for ashes.
- Fire extinguishers. Thomas and Renee Ormsbee want one on each floor, plus extras for the kitchen and cellar.
- Fireplace screens that fit the opening.
- A flashlight.
- The fire department's number, posted by the phone.

## Steps

1. Clear the attic, basement, closets and outbuildings of rubbish at least once a year. Store what you keep neatly, with clear passages between boxes.
2. Put polishes, cleaning fluids, treated dust cloths and oily rags in a covered metal container when you are not using them. Peyser warns that if rags soaked with oil or grease are left lying around, "spontaneous combustion will take place."
3. Empty ashes only into metal cans or onto a fireproof floor, never into wooden boxes or barrels. Peyser takes them out of the building at least once a week.
4. Keep stoves and heaters well clear of walls and furniture. Set them on a fireproof base, and never dry clothes or kindling on or against them.
5. Fit screens to all open fireplaces and stoves, especially where there are children.
6. Have chimneys, flues and stovepipes cleaned and inspected before the heating season. Watch for loose joints, rust holes and crumbling mortar.
7. Keep curtains and drapes away from candles and portable heaters. Never leave a candle burning unwatched.
8. Check electrical cords and replace any that are frayed or broken. Do not run cords over hooks or nails. Do not overload circuits, and never replace a fuse with a larger one or bypass it.
9. Unplug the iron as soon as you finish, pulling the plug rather than the cord.
10. Never look for a gas leak with a match or candle. Turn on a flashlight before you go near gas or stored fuel.
11. Keep gasoline and other fuels out of the house in closed, approved metal cans. Clean clothes with flammable fluids only outdoors, if at all.
12. Mount extinguishers where they are easy to reach, and make sure everyone knows how to use them. Remember that water spreads burning grease, oil or gasoline.
13. Post the fire department's number by the phone. Practice what to say and how to give clear directions to the house.
14. Before bed, check that the stove, heaters, fireplace and candles are all out. One family in the Ormsbees' book kept a checklist on the back of the front door.

## From the source books

- Peyser blamed hidden electrical faults for "almost more fires than any other one cause." She advised having wiring inspected by a recognized electrical inspector.
- Peyser recommended placing a heating stove 24 to 30 inches from combustible surfaces, at least 6 inches off the floor, with a fireproof mat at least 36 inches square to catch embers.
- The Ormsbees note that most house fires of their day started in the cellar. They also describe an early heat-sensitive "fire detector" that rang an alarm bell.
- Enquire Within advises that anyone escaping a fire should "creep or crawl along the room with your face close to the ground." If clothing catches fire, the wearer should roll up in a rug, carpet or woolen garment.
- Enquire Within also says not to open windows or doors in a burning room, because the draft feeds the fire.
- Several of these writers recommend asbestos boards, roofing and wall packing as fireproofing. Asbestos is now known to cause cancer and is no longer used this way.

## Safety and modern practice

- Install smoke alarms on every level, inside each bedroom and outside sleeping areas. Test them monthly and replace each unit after 10 years. Fit carbon monoxide alarms if you have fuel-burning appliances or an attached garage.
- Plan two ways out of every room, choose a meeting place outside, and practice the plan. If there is a fire, get out, stay out and call 911. Fight a fire only if it is small and you have a clear exit behind you.
- For clothing on fire, "stop, drop and roll," covering your face. Smother a pan fire with a lid and turn off the burner. Never use water on it.
- Leave electrical repairs to a licensed electrician, and have older wiring checked. Replace old fuse panels as code requires.
- Do not disturb old materials that may contain asbestos. Have them tested by a professional.
