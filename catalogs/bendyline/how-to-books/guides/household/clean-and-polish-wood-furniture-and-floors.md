# Clean and polish wood furniture and floors

Wood finishes last longest with frequent dusting, very little polish and plenty of rubbing. Water is used sparingly and dried immediately. Elizabeth Hale Gilman's housekeeping book for girls and Harper's Household Handbook agree on this gentle routine. They save washing and refinishing for when a surface truly needs it.

## What you need

- A small, soft, flat brush, such as a varnish brush, for carvings and cracks.
- Very soft cotton or flannel cloths, and a chamois.
- A dry mop, or a broom with a cloth bag over it, for floors.
- Mild soap and a basin of lukewarm water.
- A furniture polish or paste wax.
- For waxed floors, a weighted polisher. Gilman makes one from a heavy wood block on an old broom handle, padded underneath with old flannel, towels or carpet.
- Felt pads for furniture feet.

## Steps

1. Brush out carvings, cracks and corners with the small brush before you wipe anything.
2. Wipe polished furniture with a very soft cloth on every regular cleaning day.
3. Wipe unpolished wood only with a dry cloth, or one barely dampened if the room is very dusty. Gilman warns that oil, polish or even water can darken or spot it.
4. Polish furniture about once a month, more often if it gets hard use. Use the polish sparingly and rub briskly. Gilman's rule for floors applies here too: "much rubbing and little oil."
5. Wash a finish only when it looks clouded or grimy. Use lukewarm mild soapsuds, wet only a small area at a time, and dry it at once with a soft cloth. Gilman then polishes with a chamois.
6. Wipe painted woodwork now and then with slightly warm, mild suds. Do not soak it, and dry it as soon as it is washed.
7. Dust bare floors every day if you can, with a dry mop or a bagged broom.
8. Polish a waxed floor after dusting by working the weighted polisher over every inch until the shine returns.
9. Polish an oiled floor after dusting with a soft, thick cloth just dampened with polish. When you finish, the floor should feel smooth to the hand, not oily. If you used too much, rub it again with a dry cloth.
10. Keep water off polished and waxed floors. Gilman washes a hardwood floor only just before refinishing, with tepid water, mild soap and a well-wrung cloth.
11. Refinish only the worn patches when one area gets heavy wear. The final polishing blends them in.
12. Glue rounds of felt under the feet of furniture without casters so they do not mark the floor.

## From the source books

- Gilman's homemade polishes include kerosene alone, two parts linseed oil to one part kerosene, and half turpentine with half crude oil. She also gives the old French mixture of one tablespoon of vinegar to three of sweet oil (olive oil), which she uses on leather upholstery too.
- She warns that oily polishing cloths "should not be shut up closely in a box or closet for they are liable to spontaneous combustion."
- Harper's washes old mahogany, walnut and cherry about every six months, "wetting only a little space at a time." It rubs the polish in "until the surface burns your hand," and washes and polishes dining tables in steady use monthly.
- Harper's makes felt pads from old soft hats and notes that ready-made pads were already sold.
- Gilman thought carpets were harder to keep clean than bare floors, because dust that shows on a floor is simply buried in a carpet.

## Safety and modern practice

- Do not polish indoors with kerosene, turpentine or crude oil. They are flammable, their fumes are harmful, and they leave residue. Use a commercial furniture polish or paste wax, and open a window when you apply it.
- Rags soaked with linseed oil, oil stains or oil finishes can heat up and catch fire on their own. Spread them flat outdoors to dry, or seal them in a metal can filled with water, and dispose of them as local rules direct.
- Most modern floors are sealed with polyurethane rather than wax or oil. Clean them with a barely damp microfiber mop and the cleaner the manufacturer recommends. Do not wax them or use oil soaps, because a later recoat may not stick.
- Test any cleaner or polish on a hidden spot first, and never mix cleaning products.
