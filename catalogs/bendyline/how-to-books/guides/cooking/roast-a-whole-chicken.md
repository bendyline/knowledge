# Roast a whole chicken

Roasting suits a chicken of 3 pounds or more. A blast of high heat browns the skin, then steady, gentler heat with frequent basting cooks the meat through without drying it. Trussing keeps the bird compact so it cooks evenly. The browned drippings left in the pan make the gravy.

## What you need

- A cleaned, ready-to-cook chicken of 3 to 4 pounds, with the giblets and neck
- Salt and pepper
- For the coating: 3 tablespoons butter, creamed with 2 tablespoons flour, plus a little extra flour for the pan
- For basting: ¼ cup butter melted in ⅔ cup boiling water, and more boiling water as needed
- Kitchen string, a roasting pan with a rack, and a meat thermometer
- Optional bread or cracker stuffing
- For gravy: 4 tablespoons flour and 2 cups stock simmered from the giblets, neck and wing tips

## Steps

1. Put the giblets, neck and wing tips in cold water and simmer them until tender. This makes the stock for gravy.
2. Pull out any pinfeathers with the point of a small knife. Pat the bird dry inside and out.
3. Rub the inside of the cavity with salt and pepper.
4. If you are stuffing the bird, spoon stuffing loosely into the neck skin and the body. Leave room for it to swell. Close the openings with skewers or a few stitches of cotton string.
5. Truss the bird. Fold each wing tip back behind the first wing joint so the wing makes a triangle against the body. Tie the ends of the drumsticks together and pull them down to the tail. Tuck the neck skin under the back.
6. Set the chicken on its back on the rack. Rub it all over with salt, then spread the breast and legs with the butter-flour paste. Dust the bottom of the pan with flour.
7. Roast in a very hot oven until the skin and the flour in the pan are well browned.
8. Reduce the heat. Baste every 10 to 15 minutes, first with the butter-and-water mixture and then with the fat in the pan. Add a cup of boiling water whenever the pan flour is close to burning.
9. Turn the bird now and then so it browns evenly on all sides.
10. Test for doneness. The source books roast until the breast is tender and the legs are easily pierced with a fork; a 4-pound chicken takes about 1½ hours. Today, check that a thermometer in the thickest part of the thigh reads 165°F.
11. Lift the chicken onto a warm platter, cut and pull out the strings, and let it rest while you make the gravy.
12. Make the gravy. Pour the liquid from the pan and skim off 4 tablespoons of fat. Return the fat to the pan and brown 4 tablespoons of flour in it. Stir in 2 cups of giblet stock, cook 5 minutes, season, and strain. For giblet gravy, add the cooked giblets, finely chopped.

## From the source books

- The Woman's Institute course wants roasting chickens of at least 3 pounds, and sends very old, tough fowl to the stewpot instead.
- That course pours a cup of water into the pan after searing and bastes with it every 10 to 15 minutes. It also sews the stuffing in with a darning needle and white cord.
- Fannie Farmer dredges the bird with flour two or three times during roasting for a thick, crisp crust. For a glazed finish, she spreads it with butter only and skips the flour.
- Farmer's simple stuffing is 1 cup cracker crumbs moistened with ⅓ cup butter melted in ⅓ cup boiling water, seasoned with salt, pepper and sage, savory or marjoram. She warns that crackers swell and can burst the skin if the bird is packed too full.
- Farmer keeps the leftover pan fat for frying potatoes or basting the next chicken.

## Safety and modern practice

- "Very hot" in the old books means roughly 450°F. Most cooks today brown the bird at 425-450°F, then finish it at about 350°F.
- Cook chicken to 165°F in the thickest part of the thigh, away from the bone. If the bird is stuffed, the center of the stuffing must also reach 165°F, or bake the stuffing separately in a dish.
- Current USDA advice is not to rinse raw poultry, since splashing spreads bacteria around the sink. The old books rinsed it under running water. Pat the bird dry instead, and wash your hands, boards and tools in hot soapy water after handling it.
- Thaw a frozen bird in the refrigerator, not on the counter. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours.
