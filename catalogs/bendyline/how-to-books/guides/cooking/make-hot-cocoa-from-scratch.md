# Make hot cocoa from scratch

Cocoa powder will not dissolve if you dump it into hot milk. It clumps and floats. The cooking-school method is to make a smooth paste of cocoa, sugar and a little boiling water first, boil it briefly to cook the cocoa, and then combine it with scalded milk. A final beating, called "milling," raises a froth that keeps the skin from forming.

## What you need

For six cups, the Woman's Institute "rich cocoa" or Fannie Farmer's "reception cocoa":

- 4 cups milk
- 3 tablespoons unsweetened cocoa
- 1/4 cup sugar
- A few grains of salt
- 1/2 to 3/4 cup boiling water
- A few drops of vanilla (optional), plus whipped cream to serve (optional)
- A heavy saucepan for the milk, ideally aluminum or enameled, which the Woman's Institute says scorches milk less. You also need a small pan for the paste, and a rotary egg beater or whisk

## Steps

1. Scald the milk. Heat it until small bubbles appear around the edge and it steams, without letting it boil.
2. Meanwhile, mix the cocoa, sugar and salt in the small pan.
3. Pour in just enough boiling water to stir the mixture into a smooth paste with no lumps, then add the rest of the water.
4. Bring the paste to a boil and boil 1 minute, or 2 to 3 minutes for a rich, mellow flavor, stirring all the while.
5. Pour the cocoa into the scalded milk and stir.
6. Beat the cocoa with the rotary beater or whisk for about 2 minutes, until a fine froth covers the top.
7. Add the vanilla, if using, and serve at once, with whipped cream if you like.

## From the source books

- The Woman's Institute gives several strengths. Its plain cocoa is 2½ tablespoons each of cocoa and sugar stirred into 4 cups of boiling water and served with cream; it calls this the quickest and cheapest. Its breakfast cocoa uses half milk and half water.
- For hot chocolate, both books melt 1½ squares (1½ ounces) of unsweetened chocolate and stir in 1/4 cup sugar, salt and boiling water. They boil it briefly, then add scalded milk and mill it. The Woman's Institute uses 2 cups of water to 2 cups of milk, and Farmer 1 cup of water to 3 of milk.
- The Woman's Institute's "creamy cocoa" adds 1 tablespoon of flour to 4 tablespoons of cocoa for body. It cooks the mixture 5 minutes to remove the raw starch taste.
- Both books allow evaporated or condensed milk diluted with water in place of fresh milk. With sweetened condensed milk, cut the sugar.
- For iced cocoa, the Woman's Institute pours cooled cocoa over cracked ice and tops it with sweetened whipped cream. It also boils leftover cocoa with extra cocoa and sugar into a syrup for flavoring.
- Farmer recommends milling with a "Dover egg-beater" for two minutes. The froth prevents the scum she calls "so unsightly."

## Safety and modern practice

- Milk boils over and scorches quickly. Use moderate heat and a pan with room to spare, and stay with it.
- Let cocoa cool to a comfortable drinking temperature before serving it to children.
- Cocoa contains theobromine and some caffeine. Keep cocoa, chocolate and leftover drinks away from dogs and cats, to whom theobromine is toxic.
- A stick blender or milk frother does the "milling" quickly. Keep the blade fully under the surface to avoid spattering hot liquid.
