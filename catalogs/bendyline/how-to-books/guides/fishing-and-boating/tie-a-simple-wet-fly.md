# Tie a simple wet fly

A wet fly is a sunk trout fly with a slim body, a soft hackle and a pair of feather wings swept back over the hook. Tying one teaches nearly every basic move in fly tying: starting the thread, tying in materials, winding a body and a hackle, and finishing the head.

## What you need

- A fly-tying vise (Gregg prefers the cam-lever type), hackle pliers, two pairs of fine scissors, and a needle set in a handle
- Wet-fly hooks
- Tying silk (thread). Gregg uses size 00 for most flies and the finer 000 or 0000 for small ones.
- Sticky fly-tying wax
- A few fibers for the tail
- Oval or flat tinsel for ribbing
- Silk floss, wool or chenille for the body
- A soft, webby hen hackle
- A matched pair of wing quills, one from a left wing and one from a right (duck, goose or turkey)
- Head lacquer

## Steps

1. Clamp the hook firmly in the vise.
2. Wax the thread. Start it about 1/8 inch behind the hook eye and wind close, smooth turns down the shank to above the barb.
3. Tie in the tail with Gregg's pinch method. Hold the fibers on top of the hook between thumb and finger and bring the thread up between your thumb and the material. Form a loose loop over the top and down between your finger and the material, then pinch tight and pull the loop down. Repeat once.
4. With two more turns, tie in the ribbing tinsel at the rear.
5. Wind the thread a few turns forward, tie in the body material, then wind the thread back to the front and make a half hitch.
6. Wind the body material forward, back and forward again to build a smooth body that tapers toward the rear. Tie it off with two or three turns and a half hitch, and trim the end. If you use floss, untwist it and use only a third or half of the strands.
7. Spiral the ribbing forward over the body in even open turns, tie it off and trim it.
8. Strip the soft fluff from the base of the hackle. Tie it in by the butt under the hook with the shiny side forward, so the feather stands on edge.
9. Grip the tip with hackle pliers and wind four or five turns. Tie off and trim the tip.
10. Reach under the hook and pull all the hackle fibers down to the underside. Take a few thread turns to hold them sloping back.
11. Cut a slip about 1/4 inch wide from each wing quill. Put the slips together with the tips even and the curved (concave) sides facing each other. Trim them so the tips reach the bend of the hook or a little beyond.
12. Tie the wing on top of the hook with the same pinch loop you used for the tail, so it does not twist or split.
13. Build a small, smooth tapered head. Finish with two or three half hitches and give the head two coats of lacquer.

## From the source books

- Gregg saves hackles by stripping the fibers off the stem and tying them in under the hook like a small beard, the way bucktail hair is tied in. That lets him use hackles that are too large to wind.
- For a palmered fly, tie the hackle in by its tip at the rear and spiral it forward over the whole body. Many dry flies and Gregg's "Bi-visibles" are tied this way.
- Gregg says first attempts can be cut off the hook with an old razor blade, so it pays to practice on good hooks.
- Blacker's much older method waxed the silk with a folded piece of leather holding the wax. He tied the fly onto a length of gut rather than an eyed hook. He tied the wings on first in reverse, turned them up and split them with a needle. He made the body from hare's fur mixed with yellow mohair twisted onto the silk, and used two mallard fibers about 3/8 inch long for the tail.

## Safety and modern practice

- Keep fingers clear of hook points, and store hooks and blades away from children and pets.
- Head lacquers and cements often contain solvents. Use them with good ventilation, cap the bottle between uses, or switch to water-based cements. If you use UV-curing resin, wear eye protection rated for the UV lamp.
- Feathers of many wild birds are protected by law, including under the Migratory Bird Treaty Act in the United States. Buy materials from fly-tying suppliers rather than collecting feathers yourself.
- Work under a good lamp, and take breaks to rest your eyes.
