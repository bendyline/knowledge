# Write a business letter

A business letter asks someone to act: answer an inquiry, fix an order, consider an application. The 1920s letter-writing manuals boil the craft down to a few rules. Know what you want, say it plainly and courteously, and use the standard layout so nothing distracts from the message.

## What you need

- Good plain white paper, or a simple letterhead with your name, address and phone number
- The recipient's name, title and address, spelled as on their own letterhead
- Any reference you are answering, such as their letter's date or an order number
- A dictionary or spell checker for anything you are unsure of
- A pen with black or blue ink for the signature

## Steps

1. Before writing, put the purpose into one sentence. Crowther gives just two rules for the business letter: "Know what you want to say" and "Say it."
2. Write the heading: your street address, city, state and the date. On printed letterhead, add only the date.
3. Below the heading, at the left margin, write the inside address: name and title, company, street, city and state. Braddy suggests addressing the firm plus a name or position ("Attention: Purchasing Manager") so someone else can handle it if that person is away.
4. Add the salutation at the same margin, followed by a colon. When you know the name, use it: Braddy prefers "Dear Mr. Brown" to "Dear Sir."
5. Open by naming the subject or reason for writing, with consideration for the reader. Crowther contrasts the curt "Yours of the 14th regarding the shortage in your last order" with "We regret to learn from your letter of March 14th that there was a shortage in your last order."
6. Give each point its own paragraph. Keep to one subject per letter so it can be filed and found; two subjects call for two letters.
7. Strip out stock phrases: "we beg to advise," "your letter is at hand," "as per," "same" used as a pronoun, "enclosed herewith," "contents noted," and "inst." or "ult." for months. Write "We have your letter of May 10" and "according to our agreement."
8. End with a direct sentence, not a trailing phrase like "Hoping to hear from you." "Please investigate the delay at once" says what you want.
9. Add the complimentary close, such as "Yours truly," "Very truly yours" or "Sincerely yours," with only the first word capitalized and a comma after it.
10. Sign by hand in ink, with your name and position typed beneath. Sign the same way every time.
11. Leave margins of at least an inch on the left and half an inch on the right, with the letter sitting slightly above the middle of the page. Proofread, and confirm the signature, enclosures, address and postage.

## From the source books

- Crowther says a business letter exists "to convey a thought that will lead to some kind of action." Most, she warns, are "either too wordy or too curt."
- Braddy claims that half of all business letters "should never be written at all," and that careless, incomplete ones turn a two-letter transaction into six or eight.
- Braddy says to write "we" for a firm and "I" only for near-personal matters, and to stay consistent.
- Both authors call "Dictated but not read" at the foot of a letter a discourtesy to the reader. Crowther adds: never write in the heat of anger. "Sleep on it."
- Some period conventions have changed. Crowther treats "Gentlemen" as the greeting for any firm, even one of men and women, and gives rules for a married woman to sign as "Mrs. James Rundell." Today you write to a named person or a role ("Dear Hiring Manager") and sign with your own name. Crowther also says never to abbreviate state names on the envelope. The US Postal Service now asks for the two-letter state codes.

## Safety and modern practice

- Most business correspondence now goes by email. The same structure works, with a specific subject line doing the job of Braddy's "Re:" line.
- Never put full account numbers, passwords or government ID numbers in a letter or email. Quote only the last few digits.
- Keep a dated copy of any letter about money, contracts or complaints. A written offer, acceptance or demand can have legal weight.
