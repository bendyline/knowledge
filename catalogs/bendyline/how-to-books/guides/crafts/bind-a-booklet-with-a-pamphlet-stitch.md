# Bind a booklet with a pamphlet stitch

The simplest book is a single "section": a few folded sheets nested inside a cover and sewn through the fold. Bean and Brodhead's course for beginners starts here, because it teaches measuring, folding and sewing in one short project. The result makes a good notebook, sketchbook, journal or scrapbook.

## What you need

- 3 to 7 sheets of paper for the pages. Each folded sheet gives four pages
- A sheet of heavier cover paper, a little larger than the pages
- Linen thread, silk or raffia for sewing
- A needle, and a pin or awl for piercing
- A ruler, pencil and sharp scissors or knife

## Steps

1. Decide what the booklet is for, then its size. A long, narrow page suits lists and pocket notes, and a larger page suits writing or maps. Fold a scrap sheet to try proportions.
2. Draw a plan of the open, unfolded sheet with its measurements. A single sheet folded through the center is called a folio.
3. Plan the cover so that it projects 1/8 to 1/4 inch beyond the pages on every side.
4. Cut the cover and one page sheet exactly on your lines.
5. Lay the cut sheet on top of the remaining paper and push a pin through all the layers at each corner. Rule lines between the pin holes on each sheet and cut. This is easier than measuring every sheet.
6. Fold each sheet in half, nest them one inside another, and set the bundle inside the folded cover with the cover edges projecting evenly.
7. With the needle or awl, pierce a hole at the center of the fold. Pierce another near each end, between 1/2 and 1 1/2 inches from the edge, depending on the booklet's size.
8. Sew from the inside out through the center hole. Go back in through one end hole, come out again through the center hole, and go in through the other end hole.
9. Pull the thread snug along the fold. Tie the two ends inside in a square knot near the center, over the long stitch.
10. Letter the cover if you like. Use rather large Roman capitals for the title, with its base line one-fifth to one-quarter of the way down the cover. Put your name in smaller capitals below the center.

## From the source books

- Bean and Brodhead practice the title on a separate sheet first and cut it out to try positions before tracing it onto the cover. They advise against enclosing the title in a box, though "a single or double line as a border" looks well.
- For a painted cover, they tint drawing paper with a watercolor wash before cutting it out, so streaks at the edges can be trimmed away. They warn that blue "is very likely to streak."
- They turn the same booklet into a thin scrapbook. Magazine stories are trimmed and pasted in pairs to narrow strips of thin paper, which are then stitched through the back.
- Vaughn's first-grade "nature book" uses three or four holes along the fold and a cord in a large needle. Go in at the top hole and out at the bottom, then back through each hole in turn, looping around the long thread, and tie the ends at the top.
- Cockerell, writing for professional binders, binds very thin single-section books by folding matching end papers and a waste sheet around the section. He pastes a strip of linen to the back and sews through the fold. The linen and waste can then be fixed into boards for a hard cover.

## Safety and modern practice

- Cut paper with a sharp knife against a metal ruler on a cutting mat, keeping your fingers behind the blade. Pierce holes against a scrap board rather than into your lap.
- Fold paper with the grain running parallel to the spine. It folds more cleanly and the pages lie flatter.
- For journals and keepsakes, choose acid-free paper and linen or polyester thread so the booklet does not yellow or fall apart.
