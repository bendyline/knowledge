# Weave a rag rug from fabric strips

Rag rugs turn cloth into a sturdy, washable floor covering: fabric strips are the filling (weft), woven across a strong twine warp. Candace Wheeler, who promoted rag rug weaving as a farmhouse industry, insisted on well-joined, even strips and carefully planned colors rather than "hit or miss."

## What you need

- Cotton cloth: new remnants, or sound parts of old sheets, shirts and dresses with the thin places cut away
- Strong cotton twine or carpet warp, close to the main color of the rug
- A frame loom with notched bars at head and foot. Mattie Todd's school loom also has a rod along each side to keep the edges straight
- A sewing machine or needle and thread, scissors, a tape (bodkin) needle and a ruler

## Steps

1. Tear the cloth into strips rather than cutting it, because tearing keeps the width even. Wheeler finds an inch-wide strip of ordinary cotton right for a floor rug, and half that for lighter hangings.
2. Join strips end to end. Lap them 1/4 to 1/2 inch and stitch twice, once along each edge of the lap, so no raw ends poke out of the finished rug. Selvedge ends can simply be overhanded together.
3. Wind each color into its own ball.
4. Plan the colors. Wheeler's rule is to make one color predominate and to make the border "always stronger," that is, darker or deeper, than the center. Her example No. 1: 6 inches of plain dark blue, 6 inches of half-inch stripes of dark blue and white, a center of clouded blue, the border repeated, and a 4-inch warp fringe.
5. String the warp as one continuous strand, leaving several inches at each end for fastening. For heavy rags, Todd wraps it around every third notch, which spaces the warp threads 1/2 inch apart. Place the first and last threads directly over the side rods.
6. Check the tension by pressing on the warp with your hand. It should feel firm but spring back slightly. Too loose makes uneven work, and too tight crowds the last few inches.
7. For a fringe, string the warp in knotted pairs, leaving 2 to 3 inches beyond the head and foot bars, or 4 to 5 inches for a knotted fringe.
8. Thread a strip on the tape needle and weave over one warp thread and under the next. At each side, pass the strip around the rod and the edge thread to keep the edges straight. Do not let the strips twist.
9. On the return row, go under the threads you went over before. Push each row down snug against the last.
10. To match the borders at both ends, measure from the foot bar to the end of the first border, then mark the same distance down from the head bar with a colored thread. You can also count the loops on each side rod, since two passes across make one loop.
11. To fix a mistake, run the needle back under the same threads rather than pulling strips out, which stretches them.
12. Weave a little past your measurements, since later rows push earlier ones down. Lift the rug off and tie each twine warp end to its neighbor, letting the fringe cover the knots.

## From the source books

- Wheeler says the old way of tacking carpet rags together with coarse thread "will not answer" for good rugs. The filling must be smooth, without lumps or rag ends.
- She values bias-cut strips as "more elastic" than straight ones, though they take longer to prepare.
- She warns that a faded warp spoils the best rug. Some colored warps bleed and should be steeped in warm water first.
- Wheeler wove her rugs on a full-size floor loom in two-yard lengths with a border and fringe at each end. She advises learning to set up such a loom from someone who already weaves, since warping "must be *seen* to be understood."
- Todd joins narrow woven strips into larger rugs by basting them on oilcloth and sewing the edge warps together invisibly.

## Safety and modern practice

- Wash and dry all cloth before tearing it, both for cleanliness and so the finished rug does not shrink unevenly.
- Put a non-slip rug pad under any rag rug used on a smooth floor, and keep loose fringe away from stairs.
- If you cut strips with a rotary cutter, use a mat and close the blade guard between cuts.
