# Tool and model a design on leather

Firm leather, when damp, takes and holds an impression almost like wax. In "modeled" leather the design is outlined with a knife and rounded with smooth tools so it stands in low relief, often against a punched background. Georges de Récy's *The Decoration of Leather* describes the French method.

## What you need

- Cowhide or calf leather tanned without oils or fats. De Récy prefers oak or sumac tannage, which keeps leather supple and holds its color
- A smooth slab of stone or marble to work on (he prefers a lithographic stone)
- Thick tracing paper and a metal tracing point or a very hard 6H pencil
- An incising knife, an "opener" (a blunt tool that widens a cut), and two or three modelers
- A background punch with a fine check or dotted face, set in a wooden handle
- A soft sponge and clean water

## Steps

1. Draw the design on thick tracing paper. Keep it bold and simple, since fine detached points and curled-up edges are fragile in leather.
2. Fasten the tracing by folding its top edge over the edge of the leather and gluing it there. That way it cannot shift, but you can lift it to check progress.
3. Wet the whole surface evenly with the damp sponge, passing over it several times. If only part is wetted, a ring forms around that area and leaves a permanent mark.
4. Go over every line of the design once with the tracing point, without doubling any line. Lift the paper now and then. If the traced line looks light rather than dark, the leather is too dry and needs damping again.
5. Remove the tracing. Cut the outlines with the incising knife held upright so the cut is vertical, and never deeper than one-third of the leather's thickness.
6. Stop each cut just short of where two lines meet, such as the notches of a leaf. If you cut right into the angle, the point tends to lift and curl. You can finish those points with the modeler instead.
7. With the leather well damped, slide the blunt tip of the opener along each cut with a gentle gliding motion. It acts as a wedge and parts the edges evenly.
8. Model the relief. Pass the modeler across the inner edge of each cut to round it softly, and press the outer edge down so it merges into the background. Damp the leather often, but not so much that water oozes up under the tool.
9. Texture the background with the hand punch. Set its edge exactly against the outline and tilt the handle slightly, so extra pressure falls on the edge next to the design, which tends to rise again. Press steadily with your wrist and overlap the impressions so no plain leather shows.
10. Let the leather dry flat. De Récy says good leather "will keep the modelling permanently when it is once dry."

## From the source books

- De Récy warns that the least touch of a tool leaves a mark on damp leather, and it cannot always be removed.
- Too-deep cuts can show a light line of exposed inner leather, which he stains to match.
- For thin leather that should not be cut, he marks the outline with the tracing point or a slanted opener. He then lays the leather face up on a cake of wax and models it by lowering the background around the design.
- For high relief, he wets thick leather on both sides and rubs it from the back with a steel ball tool, holding it in his fingers or laying it face down on a tightly packed sand bag. He then fills the hollows with a paste padding.
- He prefers modelers of nickel-plated steel or brass, because plain steel can rust and stain wet leather.
- For color, he advises keeping to about three colors: ground, ornament and highlights. To keep dye from spreading along a cut line, he coats the line lightly with paste first.

## Safety and modern practice

- Today, "vegetable-tanned tooling leather" is the kind to buy. Chrome-tanned leather will not hold tooled impressions well.
- Keep knife blades sharp, cut away from your other hand, and work on a stable surface.
- De Récy colors leather with acids and metal salts, and decorates it with heated points. Choose modern water-based leather dyes and finishes instead, use them with ventilation and gloves, and handle any hot tool on a heatproof stand.
