# Set up a pigeon loft

A pigeon "loft" is any room or small building where pigeons live, from a corner of a barn to a purpose-built house. Carl Dare's guide to squab breeding and the U.S. Army manual The Homing Pigeon agree that it must be dry, airy and light, with clean water, a bath, nests and careful feeding.

## What you need

- A dry room or small house, with the floor at least a foot above the ground and doubled so it is airtight.
- Windows covered with stout muslin, or porous covers for cold, windy weather.
- For a "fly" (an outdoor wire yard): posts about 8 feet high and 2-inch-mesh poultry netting for the sides and top.
- Shelf boards and 1-inch-square cleats for nest boxes.
- Short straw or coarse hay for nesting material, kept in a covered crate.
- Clean sand for the floor.
- A pigeon drinking fountain.
- A bath pan about 4 inches deep.
- A grain mixture and grit.

## Steps

1. Choose a site where water does not stand around or under the building. Raise the floor at least a foot so the rooms stay dry and rats cannot burrow beneath.
2. Light and air the loft. Dare finds muslin windows as warm as glass. The Army manual keeps the front open in good weather and covers the openings with porous material in severe cold.
3. Build a fly against one side, about 10 feet wide and 12 to 30 feet long, roofed with the same netting. Cut openings about 8 inches square near the top of the wall, with 6-inch landing shelves inside and out and sliding doors to close them.
4. Fit perches, allowing about 10 percent more perching space than you have birds.
5. Build at least two nests for each pair, because pairs often have eggs in one nest while raising young in another. Dare's nests are 1-foot cubes, made of shelves resting on cleats fastened to upright boards. A strip across the front keeps the nesting material in.
6. Spread sand on the floor, about an inch deep. It keeps the floor clean and adds to the grit.
7. Supply water in a fountain at least 1½ inches deep, because pigeons drink in long draughts. Change it three times a day in warm weather and twice in cool weather. Empty it at night when it might freeze.
8. Offer a bath about an hour after the morning feed on warm days. Remove it after about an hour and scrub the pan, so the birds do not drink dirty water.
9. Hand-feed twice a day, lightly in the morning and more in the evening. Scatter one handful at a time on the sand, and stop when the first bird leaves to drink.
10. Keep new birds in a separate compartment at first. Handle each one, and get them used to you and to fixed feeding times.
11. Follow a daily routine. Inspect the loft, scrape up the droppings, sift the sand and spread fresh, then give fresh water.
12. To catch a bird, take it calmly from the front while it is on a perch or nest, with your thumbs over its back and fingers under its body. Never grab at birds in flight.

## From the source books

- Dare has seen good lofts made from piano boxes, and one fly built on a stable roof where rain washed it clean.
- Dare cleaned thoroughly once a month, because each cleaning disturbed the birds. He whitewashed the interior twice a year.
- The Army manual says pigeons are "more manageable if kept a little hungry," and that many good birds have been spoiled by overfeeding.
- One Army feed mixture is 25 percent small corn, 25 percent Canada peas, 30 percent maple peas and 20 percent vetch, with grit fed separately.
- The Army manual warns never to wind string or a rubber band around a pigeon's leg, because it cuts off circulation.
- Dare recommends tobacco stems as nesting material to discourage insects. The Army manual adds quassia chips to the bath water. Neither is needed with good hygiene.

## Safety and modern practice

- Dried pigeon droppings and feather dust can cause lung disease, including hypersensitivity pneumonitis ("bird fancier's lung"), psittacosis and fungal infections. Wear a well-fitting N95 respirator and gloves when scraping or sweeping, dampen dry droppings first, and wash your hands afterward.
- Use clean straw or pine needles rather than tobacco stems, which contain nicotine. Do not use homemade lice washes or chemical "lice-killers." Ask an avian veterinarian about parasites and vaccinations.
- Check local ordinances before keeping pigeons, and keep the loft clean so it does not draw rats or bother neighbors.
