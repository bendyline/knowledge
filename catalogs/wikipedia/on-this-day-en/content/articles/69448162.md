**Andre Douglas** (born December 11, 1985) is an American [systems engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_engineering), a [Coast Guard Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Reserve) officer, and [NASA astronaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_astronaut), and was chosen as a backup crew member for the [Artemis II](41391511.md) mission under NASA's [Artemis program](60758751.md).[^cite_note-1] In 2026, he was announced as a mission specialist on the [Artemis III](50493439.md) mission.[^cite_note-2]

## Personal life and education

Douglas was born in [Miami](53846.md), Florida, and raised in [Chesapeake, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake,_Virginia). He graduated from [Western Branch High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Branch_High_School) in 2004, where he was a member of the [National Honor Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Honor_Society).[^cite_note-NASA_bio-3]

He earned a [bachelor’s degree](188874.md) in [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering) from the [U.S. Coast Guard Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Academy) in 2008, where he also served as regimental commander.[^cite_note-NASA_bio-3] He was on the Men's Track Team and Chess Team.<https://www.linkedin.com/in/andre-douglas-619a923a/>

Douglas later completed four post-graduate degrees: a master's degree in mechanical engineering and a master’s degree in [naval architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_architecture) and [marine engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_engineering), both from the [University of Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Michigan) (2012), a master's degree in [electrical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering) and [computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_engineering) engineering from [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) (2019), and a doctorate in [systems engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systems_engineering) from [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University) (2021).[^cite_note-NASA_bio-3][^cite_note-4]

As of April 2025, Douglas is married and has two sons. In his free time, he enjoys [soccer](10568.md), [basketball](3921.md), [kayaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayaking), [hiking](147313.md), [running](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running), [ping-pong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ping-pong), [billiards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billiards), [chess](5134.md), and watching [movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movies).[^cite_note-NASA_bio-3]

## Career

### U.S. Coast Guard

After graduating from the academy in 2008, Douglas served as an officer in the U.S. Coast Guard, holding operational and engineering roles. His first assignment was aboard the [Coast Guard Cutter Vigilant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Vigilant_\(WMEC-617\)) based out of [Cape Canaveral, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral,_Florida), where he participated in migrant interdiction, humanitarian relief, and drug enforcement operations. He later worked at the Coast Guard Marine Safety Center, conducting hundreds of technical reviews for maritime compliance and providing global support on maritime casualty response as a naval architect.[^cite_note-NASA_bio-3]

### Applied Physics Laboratory

In 2015, Douglas transitioned out of active duty and joined the [Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University_Applied_Physics_Laboratory) (APL), contributing to space exploration, underwater robotics, and ocean systems missions. He played a key role in enhancing U.S. ballistic missile defense, piloted unmanned surface vessels in Navy operations, and supported NASA's [Double Asteroid Redirection Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Asteroid_Redirection_Test) (DART) mission. He also contributed to MEGANE, a gamma-ray and [neutron spectrometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutron_spectrometer) for Japan’s [Martian Moons eXploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martian_Moons_eXploration) spacecraft. His research focused on autonomous swarm behaviors in unmanned systems, predicting and quantifying their performance over time. As part of the Lunar Surface Innovation Consortium at APL, he collaborated with NASA to assess lunar surface needs and guide technology development.[^cite_note-NASA_bio-3][^cite_note-5]

## NASA career



Douglas (second from left) with the other backup and prime crewmembers of Artemis II after a news conference in December 2024

On December 6, 2021, Douglas was selected as one of 10 candidates in the [NASA Astronaut Group 23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Group_23). He reported for duty in January 2022[^cite_note-6] and completed astronaut candidate training on March 5, 2024. After graduation, Douglas was [commissioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioned_officer) as a [commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_\(United_States\)) in the [Coast Guard Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Reserve). In addition to his NASA duties, he will serve on an advisory group to the [Commandant of the Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandant_of_the_Coast_Guard) focusing on autonomous maritime systems, public affairs, and recruiting.[^cite_note-7]

On July 3, 2024, NASA announced Douglas as the backup crew member for [Artemis II](41391511.md), the first crewed test flight of the [Artemis program](60758751.md). He trained alongside NASA astronauts [Reid Wiseman](23437180.md), [Victor Glover](39700888.md), and [Christina Koch](39700916.md). If one of them had been unable to participate in the mission, Douglas would have taken their place.[^cite_note-8] On launch day, Douglas also served as a member of the closeout crew as an Astronaut Support Person, who strapped the other astronauts into their seats as they boarded the Orion capsule.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] The approximately 10-day Artemis II test flight launched on NASA's [Space Launch System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Launch_System) (SLS) rocket, testing the [Orion spacecraft’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_\(spacecraft\)) life-support systems, and validating the capabilities and techniques needed for humans to live and work in deep space. The mission launched on April 1, 2026, at 6\:35\:12 PM EDT.[^cite_note-NASA_Launch_Time-11]

### Artemis III

On June 9, 2026, NASA announced Douglas as a mission specialist of the [Artemis III](50493439.md) mission to low Earth orbit, along with NASA astronauts [Randy Bresnik](6926275.md) and [Frank Rubio](54253195.md) and [ESA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESA) astronaut [Luca Parmitano](22874956.md).[^cite_note-12]
