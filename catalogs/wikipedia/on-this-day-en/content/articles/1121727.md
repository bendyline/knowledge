**USS *Charlotte* (SSN-766)**, a [*Los Angeles*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles-class_submarine) [submarine](28825.md), is the fourth ship of the [United States Navy](20518076.md) to be named for [Charlotte, North Carolina](57447.md). The contract to build her was awarded to [Newport News Shipbuilding and Dry Dock Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_Newport_News) in [Newport News, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_News,_Virginia) on 6 February 1987 and her keel was laid down on 17 August 1990. Sponsored by Mrs. Mary McComack, she was [launched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_ship_launching) on 3 October 1992 and [commissioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_commissioning) on 16 September 1994. She arrived at her homeport of [Naval Station Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Base_Pearl_Harbor%E2%80%93Hickam) on 17 November 1995.[^cite_note-3]

On 4 March 2026, during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md) *Charlotte* [engaged and sank](82572206.md) the Iranian frigate [IRIS *Dena*](74329424.md), becoming the first United States naval vessel to sink an enemy vessel with a torpedo since the [Pacific theater of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War), and the [second nuclear submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Conqueror_\(S48\)) to do so in history.[^cite_note-Dena-4][^cite_note-Torp-5]

## History



*Charlotte* at the north pole

On 29 November 2005, *Charlotte* arrived in [Norfolk, Virginia](57898.md), having taken the northern route from [Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Harbor), under the [Arctic](36971.md) ice cap. Along the way, she surfaced at the [North Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pole) through 61 inches of ice, a record for a [*Los Angeles*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles-class_submarine) [submarine](28825.md).[^cite_note-Charlotte_North_Pole-6]

On 24 October 2007, *Charlotte* returned to Pearl Harbor from [Norfolk Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Naval_Shipyard) after nearly two years in a Depot Modernization Period.[^cite_note-7]

*Charlotte* has completed a total of five Western Pacific deployments.[^cite_note-WaPo-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-N2016-12] In February 1998, she was deployed to the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) as part of a multinational military buildup of naval, air, and land forces that included more than 30 American warships and two [carrier battle groups](7772.md).[^cite_note-WaPo-8] *Charlotte* and the other American and British warships were deployed as a deterrent in case [Iraqi President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_President) [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) failed to honor his commitment to the United Nations to allow arms inspectors into Iraq.[^cite_note-13] She completed a six-month deployment to the Western Pacific on 13 May 2016.[^cite_note-N2016-12]

*Charlotte* participated in [RIMPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIMPAC) 2022.[^cite_note-14]

### 2026 Iran War

*Charlotte* [sank](82572206.md) the Iranian frigate [IRIS *Dena*](74329424.md)[^cite_note-15] off the coast of Sri Lanka in March during the 2026 Iran war.[^cite_note-Dena-4] In doing so, she became the first U.S. Navy submarine to sink an enemy vessel in combat since the Pacific theater of World War II and the second-ever nuclear submarine to [sink an enemy vessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinking_of_ARA_General_Belgrano).[^cite_note-Torp-5] On July 21, 2026 the acting Secretary of the Navy [Hung Cao](76401211.md) awarded the [Navy Unit Commendation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Unit_Commendation) to the crew of the USS Charlotte stating, “The most powerful weapons in our Navy are often the ones never seen—and never heard. This recognition reflects their professionalism, discipline, and unwavering dedication to the mission.”[^cite_note-16]
