**Hauts-de-France** (French: [\[o d(ə) fʁɑ̃s\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/7c/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-GrandCelinien-Hauts-de-France.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-GrandCelinien-Hauts-de-France.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/7c/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-GrandCelinien-Hauts-de-France.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-GrandCelinien-Hauts-de-France.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Picard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picard_language): *Heuts d'Franche*; lit. 'Heights of France'), also referred to in English as **Upper France**,[^cite_note-3] is the northernmost [region of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_of_France), created by the territorial reform of French regions in 2014, from a merger of [Nord-Pas-de-Calais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nord-Pas-de-Calais) and [Picardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picardy). Its [prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_in_France) is [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille). The new region came into existence on 1 January 2016, after [regional elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_French_regional_elections) in December 2015.[^cite_note-4] The [Conseil d'État](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conseil_d'%C3%89tat_\(France\)) approved Hauts-de-France as the name of the region on 28 September 2016, effective the following 30 September.[^cite_note-voix-5][^cite_note-Decree_confirming_new_region_name-6]

With 6,009,976 inhabitants as of 1 January 2015 and a [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) of 189 inhabitants per [km<sup>2</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_kilometre), it is the third most populous region in France and the second-most densely populated in [metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France) after its southern neighbour [Île-de-France](1716427.md). It is bordered by [Belgium](3343.md) to the north and by the [United Kingdom](31717.md) to the northwest through the [Channel Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Tunnel), a railway tunnel crossing the [English Channel](9230.md). The region is a blend mixture of French and (southern-) Dutch cultures.

## Toponymy

The region's [interim name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_title) *Nord-Pas-de-Calais-Picardie* was a [hyphenated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyphenated_name) [placename](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placename), created by hyphenating the merged regions' names—*[Nord-Pas-de-Calais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nord-Pas-de-Calais)* and *[Picardie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picardy)*—in alphabetical order.[^cite_note-reform_law-7]

On 14 March 2016, well ahead of the 1 July deadline, the regional council decided on *Hauts-de-France* as the region's permanent name.[^cite_note-voix-5][^cite_note-reform_law-7] The provisional name of the region was retired on 30 September 2016, when the new name of the region, Hauts-de-France, took effect.[^cite_note-Decree_confirming_new_region_name-6]

## Geography

The region covers an area of more than 31,813 km<sup>2</sup> (12,283 sq mi). It borders [Belgium](3343.md) ([Flanders](10878.md) and [Wallonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallonia)) to the northeast, the [North Sea](21179.md) to the north, the [English Channel](9230.md) to the west, as well as the French regions of [Grand Est](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Est) to the east-southeast, [Île-de-France](1716427.md) to the south, and [Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy_\(administrative_region\)) to the west-southwest. It is connected to the [United Kingdom](31717.md) ([England](9316.md)) via the [Channel Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Tunnel).

### Departments

Hauts-de-France comprises five departments: [Aisne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aisne), [Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nord_\(French_department\)), [Oise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oise), [Pas-de-Calais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pas-de-Calais), and [Somme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somme_\(department\)).

## Economy

The [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of the region was 161.7 billion euros in 2018, accounting for 6.9% of French economic output. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was 24,200 euros or 80% of the EU27 average in the same year. The GDP per employee was 101% of the EU average.[^cite_note-8]

## Transport

### Air

There are only 2 operating airports in the region which are [Lille Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille_Airport) and [Paris–Beauvais Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris%E2%80%93Beauvais_Airport). Residents in the region normally use other major airports such as [Brussels Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels_Airport) and Paris's [Charles de Gaulle Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle_Airport) which both can easily be reached by train and car.

### Rail

The region is an important transport corridor between France, Belgium and the United Kingdom. [Lille-Europe station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille-Europe_station) is served by TGV and continental [Eurostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostar) high-speed rail services linking the region with Paris, Brussels and Amsterdam. There are further connections to London via the [Channel Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Tunnel).

### Sea

The ports of [Calais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Calais) and [Dunkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunkirk) provide passenger and freight ferry services across the English Channel, while [LeShuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeShuttle) provides a car-train service between Coquelles and Folkestone through the Channel Tunnel.
