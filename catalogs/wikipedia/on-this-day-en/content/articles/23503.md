**Portland** ([/ˈpɔːrtlənd/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*PORT-lənd*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is the [most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Oregon) [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) in the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [Oregon](26811621.md). Located in the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest) at the confluence of the [Willamette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willamette_River) and [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River) rivers, it is the [28th-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population) in the United States, sixth-most populous on the [West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States), and fourth-most populous in the Pacific Northwest (after [Seattle, Washington](11388236.md); [Vancouver, Canada](32706.md); and [Surrey, Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey,_British_Columbia)), with a population of 652,503 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-QuickFacts-12] The [Portland metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_metropolitan_area,_Oregon), with over 2.54 million residents, is the 26th-largest [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) in the nation. Almost half of Oregon's population resides within the Portland metro area.[^cite_note-13] It is the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [Multnomah County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multnomah_County,_Oregon), the most populous in the state.&#x20;

The Portland area was shaped by massive [ice-age floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missoula_floods). It was inhabited by the [Chinookan peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinookan_peoples). [European American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Americans) settlers arrived in the 1840s. Following a coin toss,[^cite_note-14] Portland was named after [Portland, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Maine), which was itself named after England's [Isle of Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Portland). In the 19th century, Portland became a major port and lumber center and gained a reputation for saloons and [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime). The 20th century saw population growth, [World War II](32927.md) shipbuilding, [Japanese-American internment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_of_Japanese_Americans), [organized crime](22625.md), and a [hippie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippie)/[counterculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterculture) movement. Since the 1990s, it has experienced growth in the [technology sector](44211754.md) and rising housing costs due to gentrification.&#x20;

Portland is located near volcanic features such as [Mount Tabor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tabor_\(Oregon\)) and is also close to active faults. The city is divided into six addressing sectors and comprises diverse [neighborhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_of_Portland,_Oregon). The city's economy is driven by technology, athletic and outdoor brands, shipping, and craft industries. Social issues include [homelessness](19394651.md) and property crime, although recent trends indicate a relative low violent crime rate.[^cite_note-portland.gov2025-15][^cite_note-\:1-16][^cite_note-\:3-17][^cite_note-18] The city prioritizes [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning), [sustainability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainability), and diverse transportation options, while managing growth with an [urban growth boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_growth_boundary). Portland also has numerous schools and universities, media outlets, healthcare facilities, and global sister-city connections.&#x20;

The city operates with a [mayor–council government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor%E2%80%93council_government) system, guided by a [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Portland,_Oregon) and 12 [city councilors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_City_Council_\(Oregon\)), as well as [Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_\(Oregon_regional_government\)), the only directly elected [metropolitan planning organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_planning_organization) in the United States.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Its climate is marked by warm, dry summers and cool, rainy winters. This climate is [ideal for growing roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roses_in_Portland,_Oregon), and Portland has been called the "City of Roses" for more than a century.[^cite_note-\:0-21] Portland is known for its vibrant music and performing arts scene, extensive parks including [Forest Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Park_\(Portland,_Oregon\)), and professional sports teams such as the [Trail Blazers](72897.md), [Timbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Timbers), and [Thorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Thorns_FC).&#x20;

## History

Main article: [History of Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Portland,_Oregon)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Portland,_Oregon).

### Before European settlement

During the prehistoric period, the land that would become Portland [was flooded after the collapse of glacial dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missoula_floods) from [Lake Missoula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Missoula), in what would later become Montana. These massive floods occurred during the last ice age and filled the [Willamette Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willamette_Valley) with 300 to 400 feet (91 to 122 m) of water.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllenBurnsSargent2009175–89-22]&#x20;

Before American settlers began arriving in the 1800s, the land was inhabited for many centuries by two bands of indigenous [Chinook people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinook_people) – the [Multnomah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multnomah_people) and the [Clackamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clackamas_people).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMarschner2008187-23] The Chinook people occupying the land were first documented [in 1805](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition) by [Meriwether Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meriwether_Lewis) and [William Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Clark).[^cite_note-anderson-24] Before its European settlement, the Portland Basin of the lower Columbia River and Willamette River valleys had been one of the most densely populated regions on the Pacific Coast.[^cite_note-anderson-24]&#x20;

### Establishment



[Pioneer Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_Courthouse), 1886



1890 map of Portland

Large numbers of pioneer settlers began arriving in the Willamette Valley in the 1840s via the [Oregon Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Trail) with many arriving in nearby [Oregon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_City,_Oregon).[^cite_note-25] A new settlement then emerged ten miles from the mouth of the Willamette River,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEScott189061-26] roughly halfway between Oregon City and [Hudson's Bay Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson%27s_Bay_Company)'s [Fort Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Vancouver). This community was initially referred to as "Stumptown" and "The Clearing" because of the many trees cut down to allow for its growth.[^cite_note-27] In 1843, [William Overton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Overton_\(Portland_founder\)) saw potential in the new settlement but lacked the funds to file an official land claim. For 25 cents, Overton agreed to share half of the 640-acre (2.6 km<sup>2</sup>) site with [Asa Lovejoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asa_Lovejoy) of [Boston](24437894.md).[^cite_note-28]&#x20;

In 1844, Overton sold his remaining half of the claim to [Francis W. Pettygrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_W._Pettygrove) of [Portland, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Maine). Both Pettygrove and Lovejoy wished to rename "The Clearing" after their respective hometowns. This controversy was settled with a coin toss that Pettygrove won in a series of two out of three tosses, thereby providing Portland with its namesake.[^cite_note-naosum.org-3] The coin used for this decision, now known as the [Portland Penny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Penny), is on display in the headquarters of the [Oregon Historical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Historical_Society). At the time of its incorporation on February 8, 1851, Portland had over 800 inhabitants,[^cite_note-Gibson-29] a steam sawmill, a log cabin hotel, and a newspaper, the *[Weekly Oregonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oregonian)*. [A major fire swept through downtown in August 1873](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_1873), destroying twenty blocks on the west side of the Willamette along Yamhill and Morrison Streets, and causing \$1.3 million in damage,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEScott1890160-30] roughly equivalent to \$34.9 million today.[^cite_note-inflation-US-31] By 1879, the population had grown to 17,500 and by 1890 it had grown to 46,385.[^cite_note-32] In 1888, the first steel bridge on the [West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States) was opened in Portland,[^cite_note-33] the predecessor of the 1912 namesake [Steel Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Bridge) that survives today. In 1889, Henry Pittock's wife, Georgiana, established the Portland Rose Society. The annual Portland Rose Festival is held here in June.[^cite_note-34] The movement to make Portland a "Rose City" started as the city was preparing for the 1905 [Lewis and Clark Centennial Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Centennial_Exposition).[^cite_note-\:0-21]&#x20;

Portland's access to the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) via the Willamette and Columbia rivers, as well as its easy access to the agricultural [Tualatin Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tualatin_Valley) via the "[Great Plank Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canyon_Road)" (the route of current-day [U.S. Route 26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_26_in_Oregon)), provided the pioneer city with an advantage over other nearby ports, and it grew very quickly.[^cite_note-35] Portland remained the major port in the Pacific Northwest for much of the 19th century, until the 1890s, when Seattle's deepwater harbor was connected to the rest of the mainland by rail, affording an inland route without the treacherous navigation of the Columbia River. The [lumber industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumber_industry) was also a prominent economic presence, due to the area's large population of [Douglas fir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_fir), [western hemlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_hemlock), [red cedar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thuja_plicata), and [big leaf maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acer_macrophyllum) trees.[^cite_note-anderson-24]&#x20;



Portland waterfront in 1898

Portland developed a reputation early in its history as a hard-edged and gritty [port town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_town).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohn201216-36] Some historians have described the city's early establishment as being a "[scion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lineal_descendant) of [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England); an ends-of-the-earth home for the exiled spawn of the eastern established elite."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohn201210-37] In 1889, *[The Oregonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oregonian)* called Portland "the most filthy city in the Northern States", due to the unsanitary sewers and gutters,[^cite_note-mac1885-38] and, at the turn of the 20th century, it was considered one of the most dangerous port cities in the world.[^cite_note-kennedy-39] The city housed a large number of saloons, [bordellos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordello), gambling dens, and boarding houses which were populated with miners after the [California gold rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_gold_rush), as well as the multitude of sailors passing through the port.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohn201216-36] By the early 20th century, the city had lost its reputation as a "sober frontier city" and garnered a reputation for being violent and dangerous.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohn201216-36][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChandler2013-40]&#x20;

### 20th-century development



The [White Eagle Saloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hryszko_Brothers_Building) (c. 1910), one of many in Portland that had reputed ties to illegal activities such as [gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling) rackets and [prostitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitution)

[^cite_note-41]



[Burnside Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnside_Street), 1937

Between 1900 and 1930, the city's population tripled from nearly 100,000 to 301,815.[^cite_note-DecennialCensus-42]&#x20;

Portland enjoyed an economic and industrial surge during [World War II](32927.md). Ship builder [Henry J. Kaiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_J._Kaiser) had been awarded contracts to build [Liberty ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_ship) and [escort carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escort_carrier), and three [Kaiser Shipyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Shipyards) were constructed in the Portland area—the [Oregon Shipbuilding Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Shipbuilding_Corporation), [Swan Island Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Island_Shipyard), and [Vancouver Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Shipyard).[^cite_note-ohs2003toll-43] During this time, Portland's population rose by over 150,000, largely attributed to recruited laborers.[^cite_note-ohs2003toll-43] The wartime shipbuilding boom in Portland also drew a large influx of [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) migrants. This migration significantly expanded the city's Black population and laid the foundation for new community institutions, even as workers often faced discrimination in hiring and job assignments.[^cite_note-44]&#x20;

During World War II, Portland also housed an "assembly center" from which up to 3,676 people of Japanese descent were dispatched to [internment camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_of_Japanese_Americans) in the heartland. It was the first American city to have residents report thus,[^cite_note-Pac-45] and the [Pacific International Livestock Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_International_Livestock_Exposition) operated from May through September 10, 1942, processing people from the city, northern Oregon, and [central Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Washington).[^cite_note-46] General [John DeWitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_L._DeWitt) called the city the first "Jap-free city on the West Coast".[^cite_note-Pac-45]&#x20;

At the same time, Portland became a notorious hub for underground criminal activity and [organized crime](22625.md) in the 1940s and 1950s.[^cite_note-ellis-47] A 1957 *[Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_\(magazine\))* magazine article focusing on [crime boss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_boss) [Jim Elkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Elkins_\(Oregon_criminal\)) became the basis of a fictionalized film titled *[Portland Exposé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Expos%C3%A9)* (1957).[^cite_note-ellis-47]&#x20;

During the 1960s, an influx of [hippie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippie) subculture began to take root in the city in the wake of [San Francisco](49728.md)'s burgeoning [countercultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterculture) scene.[^cite_note-1960s-48] The city's [Crystal Ballroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Ballroom_\(Portland,_Oregon\)) became a hub for the city's [psychedelic culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_culture), while [food cooperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_cooperative) and listener-funded media and radio stations were established.[^cite_note-60s-49] A large [social activist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_activism) presence evolved during this time as well, specifically concerning [Native American rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_rights), [environmentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism) causes, and [gay rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_rights).[^cite_note-60s-49] By the 1970s, Portland had well established itself as a progressive city, and experienced an economic boom for the majority of the decade; however, the slowing of the housing market in 1979 caused demand for the city and state timber industries to drop significantly.[^cite_note-70s-50]&#x20;

### Since 1990



Aerial view of Portland and its bridges across the Willamette River

In the 1990s, the technology industry began to emerge in Portland, specifically with the establishment of companies such as [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel), which brought more than [US\$](18717338.md)10 billion in investments in 1995 alone.[^cite_note-51] In the late 1990s, the Portland area was rated the fourth-least affordable place in the United States to purchase a new home.[^cite_note-52] After 2000, Portland experienced significant growth, with a population rise of over 90,000 between 2000 and 2014.[^cite_note-USCensusEst2014-53] The city's increasing reputation for culture established it as a popular city for young people, and it was second only to [Louisville, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky), as one of the cities to attract and retain the highest number of college-educated people in the United States.[^cite_note-miller-54] Between 2001 and 2012, Portland's gross domestic product per person grew by fifty percent, more than any other city in the country.[^cite_note-miller-54]&#x20;

The city acquired a [diverse range of nicknames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicknames_of_Portland,_Oregon) throughout its history, though it is most often called "Rose City" or "The City of Roses"[^cite_note-cityrecorder-55] (unofficial nickname since 1888, official since 2003).[^cite_note-Oreg-June2003-56] Another widely used nickname by local residents in everyday speech is "PDX", the airport code for [Portland International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_International_Airport). Other nicknames include Bridgetown,[^cite_note-bridgetown-57] Stumptown,[^cite_note-endoftheoregontrail-58] Rip City,[^cite_note-59] Soccer City,[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] P-Town,[^cite_note-Oreg-June2003-56][^cite_note-62] Portlandia, and the more antiquated Little Beirut.[^cite_note-STAP-63]&#x20;

#### 2020 George Floyd protests

Main article: [George Floyd protests in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests_in_Portland,_Oregon)

See also: [2020 deployment of federal forces in the United States § Activities in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_deployment_of_federal_forces_in_the_United_States#Activities_in_Portland,_Oregon)



George Floyd protests at the [Mark O. Hatfield Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_O._Hatfield_United_States_Courthouse), July 2020

From May 28, 2020, until spring 2021,[^cite_note-64] there were daily [protests](266137.md) about the [murder of George Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_George_Floyd) by police, and racial injustice. There were instances of looting, vandalism, and police actions causing injuries. One protestor was killed by an opposing one.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] Local businesses reported losses totaling millions of dollars as the result of vandalism and looting.[^cite_note-69] Some protests caused injury to protesters and police. In July, federal officers were deployed to safeguard federal property; their presence and tactics were criticized by Oregon officials, who demanded they leave, while lawsuits were filed against local and federal law enforcement alleging wrongful actions by them.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]&#x20;

On May 25, 2021, a protest to commemorate the one-year anniversary of Floyd's murder caused property damage, and was followed by a number of arrests.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]&#x20;

## Geography

### Geology

See also: [Geology of the Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_the_Pacific_Northwest)

Portland lies on top of a dormant volcanic field known as the [Boring Lava Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boring_Lava_Field), named after the nearby [bedroom community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter_town) of [Boring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boring,_Oregon).[^cite_note-volcano-76] The Boring Lava Field has at least 32 cinder cones such as [Mount Tabor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tabor,_Portland,_Oregon),[^cite_note-77] and its center lies in southeast Portland. The rocks of the Portland area range in age from late [Eocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eocene) to more recent eras.[^cite_note-78]&#x20;

Multiple shallow, active [faults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)) traverse the Portland metropolitan area.[^cite_note-banse-79] Among them are the [Portland Hills Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Hills_Fault) on the city's west side,[^cite_note-80] and the [East Bank Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Bank_Fault) on the east side.[^cite_note-81] According to a 2017 survey, several of these faults were characterized as "probably more of a hazard" than the [Cascadia subduction zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascadia_subduction_zone) due to their proximities to population centers, with the potential of producing [magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moment_magnitude_scale) 7 [earthquakes](10106.md).[^cite_note-banse-79] Notable earthquakes that have impacted the Portland area in recent history include the 6.8-magnitude [Nisqually earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Nisqually_earthquake) in 2001, and a 5.6-magnitude earthquake that struck on March 25, 1993.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]&#x20;

Per a 2014 report, over 7,000 locations within the Portland area are at high risk for landslides and [soil liquefaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_liquefaction) in the event of a major earthquake, including much of the city's west side (such as [Washington Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Park,_Portland,_Oregon)) and sections of [Clackamas County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clackamas_County,_Oregon).[^cite_note-seventh-84]&#x20;

### Topography

Portland is 60 miles (97 km) east of the Pacific Ocean at the northern end of [Oregon](26811621.md)'s most populated region, the Willamette Valley. Downtown Portland straddles the banks of the Willamette River, which flows north through the city center and separates the city's east and west neighborhoods. Less than 10 miles (16 km) from downtown, the Willamette River flows into the Columbia River, the fourth-largest river in the United States, which divides Oregon from Washington state. Portland is approximately 100 miles (160 km) upriver from the Pacific Ocean on the Columbia.&#x20;

Though much of downtown Portland is relatively flat, the foothills of the [Tualatin Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tualatin_Mountains), more commonly referred to locally as the "West Hills", pierce through the northwest and southwest reaches of the city. [Council Crest Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_Crest_Park) at 1,073 feet (327 m) is often quoted as the highest point in Portland; however, the highest point in Portland is on a section of NW Skyline Blvd just north of [Willamette Stone Heritage site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willamette_Stone).[^cite_note-85] The highest point east of the river is [Mount Tabor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tabor_\(Oregon\)), an extinct volcanic cinder cone, which rises to 636 feet (194 m). To the west of the Tualatin Mountains lies the [Oregon Coast Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Coast_Range), and to the east lies the actively volcanic [Cascade Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_Range). On clear days, [Mt. Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hood) and [Mt. St. Helens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_St._Helens) dominate the horizon, while [Mt. Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Adams_\(Washington\)) and [Mt. Rainier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Rainier) can also be seen in the distance.&#x20;

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has an area of 145.09 square miles (375.78 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 133.43 square miles (345.58 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 11.66 square miles (30.20 km<sup>2</sup>) is water.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_files-86] Although almost all of Portland is within [Multnomah County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multnomah_County,_Oregon), small portions of the city are within Clackamas and [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_County,_Oregon) counties.[^cite_note-87]&#x20;

### Climate



Climate chart for Portland

Portland has a [warm-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm-summer_Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Csb),* falling just short of a [hot-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) (Köppen *Csa)* with cool and rainy winters, and warm and dry summers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnderson2014138-88] This climate is characterized by having overcast, wet, and changing weather conditions in fall, winter, and spring, as Portland lies in the direct path of the stormy westerly flow, and warm, dry summers when the [North Pacific High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pacific_High) reaches its northernmost point in mid-summer.[^cite_note-fao.org-89] Portland's [USDA Plant Hardiness Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zone) is 8b, with parts of the Downtown area falling into zone 9a.[^cite_note-Portland_OR_Extremes-90]&#x20;

Winters are cool, cloudy, and rainy. The coldest month is December with an average daily high temperature of 46.9 °F (8.3 °C), although overnight lows usually remain above freezing by a few degrees. Evening temperatures fall to or below freezing 32 nights per year on average, but very rarely below 18 °F (−8 °C). There are only 2.1 days per year where the daytime high temperature fails to rise above freezing; the mean for the lowest high is at the exact freezing point of 32 °F (0 °C).[^cite_note-NOWData-91] The lowest overnight temperature ever recorded was −3 °F (−19 °C),[^cite_note-Portland_OR_Extremes-90] on February 2, 1950,[^cite_note-NOWData-91] while the lowest daytime high temperature ever recorded was 9 °F (−13 °C) on January 15, 1888.[^cite_note-NOWData-91] The average window in which freezing temperatures may occur is between November 15 and March 19, allowing a growing season of 240 days.[^cite_note-NOWData-91]&#x20;

Annual snowfall in Portland is 4.3 inches (10.9 cm), which usually falls between December and March.[^cite_note-fivethirtyeight.com-92] The city of Portland avoids snow more frequently than its suburbs, due in part to its low elevation and the [urban heat island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island) effect. Neighborhoods outside of the downtown core, especially in slightly higher elevations near the [West Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tualatin_Mountains) and [Mount Tabor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tabor_\(Oregon\)), can experience a dusting of snow while downtown receives no accumulation at all. The city has experienced a few major snow and ice storms in its past, with extreme totals having reached 44.5 in (113 cm) at the airport in 1949–50 and 60.9 in (155 cm) at downtown in 1892–93.[^cite_note-PDX_monthly+seasonal_snow-93][^cite_note-downtown_monthly+seasonal_snow-94]&#x20;



Portland's climate is [conducive to the growth of roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roses_in_Portland,_Oregon). Pictured here is the [International Rose Test Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Rose_Test_Garden).

Summers in Portland are warm, dry, and sunny, though the sunny warm weather is short-lived, from mid-June to early September.[^cite_note-95] Because of its inland location 62 miles (100 km) from the coast, as well as the protective nature of the [Oregon Coast Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Coast_Range) to its west, Portland summers are less susceptible to the moderating influence of the nearby Pacific Ocean. Consequently, Portland occasionally experiences [heat waves](293270.md), with temperatures rising above 90 °F (32 °C) for a few days. However, on average, temperatures reach or exceed 80 °F (27 °C) on only 61 days per year.&#x20;

On June 28, 2021, Portland recorded its all-time record high temperature of 116 °F (47 °C) and its warmest daily low temperature of 75 °F (24 °C) during a [major regional heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Western_North_America_heat_wave).[^cite_note-96] The record had been broken for three consecutive days with daytime highs of 108 °F (42 °C) on June 26 and 112 °F (44 °C) on June 27; the previous record of 107 °F (42 °C) was set on July 30, 1965, and matched on August 8 & 10, 1981.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]&#x20;

Spring and fall can bring variable weather including high-pressure ridging that sends temperatures surging above 80 °F (27 °C) and cold fronts that plunge daytime temperatures into the 40s °F (4–9 °C). However, lengthy stretches of overcast days beginning in mid-fall and continuing into mid-spring are most common. Rain often falls as a light drizzle for several consecutive days at a time, contributing to 157 days on average with measurable (≥0.01 in or 0.25 mm) precipitation annually. [Thunderstorms](70807.md) are uncommon and [tornadoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Portland-Vancouver_tornado) are very rare, although they do occur.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMass2008138-99][^cite_note-100]&#x20;

### Cityscape

See also: [Architecture of Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Portland,_Oregon); [List of tallest buildings in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Portland,_Oregon); and [Downtown Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Portland)

Portland's cityscape derives much of its character from the many bridges that span the Willamette River downtown, several of which are historic landmarks, and Portland has been nicknamed "Bridgetown" for many decades as a result.[^cite_note-bridgetown-57] Three of downtown's most heavily used bridges are more than 100 years old and are designated historic landmarks: [Hawthorne Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne_Bridge) (1910), [Steel Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Bridge) (1912), and [Broadway Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_Bridge_\(Portland,_Oregon\)) (1913). Portland's newest bridge in the downtown area, [Tilikum Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilikum_Crossing), opened in 2015 and is the first new bridge to span the Willamette in Portland since the 1973 opening of the double-decker [Fremont Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont_Bridge_\(Portland\)).[^cite_note-108]&#x20;

Other bridges that span the Willamette River in the downtown area include the [Burnside Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnside_Bridge), the [Ross Island Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Island_Bridge) (both built 1926), and the double-decker [Marquam Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquam_Bridge) (built 1966). Other bridges outside the downtown area include the [Sellwood Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sellwood_Bridge) (built 2016) to the south; and the [St. Johns Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Johns_Bridge), a [Gothic revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_revival) suspension bridge built in 1931, to the north. The [Glenn L. Jackson Memorial Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_L._Jackson_Memorial_Bridge) and the [Interstate Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Bridge) provide access from Portland across the Columbia River into Washington state.&#x20;

### Addressing sections



The five previous addressing sectors of Portland, prior to the addition of South Portland

The Willamette River, which flows north through downtown, serves as the natural boundary between East and West Portland. The denser and earlier-developed west side extends into the lap of the [West Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tualatin_Mountains), while the flatter east side extends for roughly 180 blocks until it meets the suburb of [Gresham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham,_Oregon). In 1891 the cities of Portland, [Albina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albina,_Oregon), and [East Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Portland,_Oregon) were consolidated, creating inconsistent patterns of street names and addresses. It was not unusual for a street name to be duplicated in disparate areas. The "Great Renumbering" on September 2, 1931, standardized street naming patterns and divided Portland into five "general districts", North, Northwest, Northeast, Southeast, and Southwest (which includes [downtown Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Portland)). House numbers were also changed from 20 per block to 100 per block and adopted a single street name on a grid. For example, the 200 block north of Burnside is either NW Davis Street or NE Davis Street throughout the entire city.[^cite_note-109]&#x20;

With the recent addition of South Portland, Portland now has six addressing sections. All addresses and streets within the city are prefixed by N, NW, NE, S, SW or SE with the exception of [Burnside Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnside_Street), which is prefixed with W or E. The Willamette River divides the city into east and west while Burnside, which traverses the entire city lengthwise, divides the north and south. Northeast, Southeast, Southwest, and Northwest Portland are respectively north or south of Burnside, and east or west of the Willamette. North Portland is a triangular peninsula bounded on the north by the Columbia River, on the east by N Williams Avenue, and on the west by the Willamette River as it bends to the northwest. South Portland was established on May 1, 2020, from a former part of Southwest Portland where the Willamette bends east of the nominal North-South meridian. House numbers on east–west streets in this area formerly had a leading zero, which was dropped as the street prefix (including north–south streets) was changed from Southwest to South. For example, the current address of 246 S California St. was formerly 0246 SW California St. and the current address of 4310 S Macadam Ave. was formerly 4310 SW Macadam Ave.&#x20;

The new South Portland addressing section was approved by the Portland City Council on June 6, 2018[^cite_note-110] and is bounded by [SW Naito Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naito_Parkway), SW View Point Terrace and the [Tryon Creek State Natural Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tryon_Creek_State_Natural_Area) to the west, SW Clay Street to the north, the Willamette River to the east, and city limits to the south.[^cite_note-111] In 2018, the city's Bureau of Transportation finalized a plan to transition this part of Portland into South Portland, beginning on May 1, 2020, to reduce confusion by 9-1-1 dispatchers and delivery services.[^cite_note-112] With the addition of South Portland, all six addressing sectors (N, NE, NW, S, SE and SW) are now officially known as sextants.[^cite_note-113]&#x20;

### Neighborhoods

See also: [Neighborhoods of Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_of_Portland,_Oregon)



Pearl District (left) from the Steel Bridge

The five previous addressing sections of Portland, which were colloquially known as quadrants despite there being five,[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] have developed distinctive identities, with mild cultural differences and friendly rivalries between their residents, especially between those who live east of the Willamette River versus west of the river.[^cite_note-116]&#x20;

[The Pearl District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_District,_Portland,_Oregon) in [Northwest Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_District,_Portland,_Oregon), which was largely occupied by warehouses, light industry and railroad classification yards in the early to mid-20th century, now houses upscale [art galleries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_gallery), restaurants, and retail stores, and is one of the wealthiest neighborhoods in the city.[^cite_note-117] Areas further west of the Pearl District include neighborhoods known as Uptown and Nob Hill, as well as the Alphabet District and NW 23rd Ave., a major shopping street lined with clothing boutiques and other upscale retail, mixed with cafes and restaurants.[^cite_note-118]&#x20;



Lloyd District from downtown Portland

Northeast Portland is home to the [Lloyd District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_District,_Portland,_Oregon), [Alberta Arts District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Arts_District), and the [Hollywood District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_District_\(Portland,_Oregon\)). North Portland is largely residential and industrial. It contains [Kelley Point Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelley_Point_Park), the northernmost point of the city. It also contains the [St. Johns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Johns,_Portland,_Oregon) neighborhood, which is historically one of the most ethnically diverse and poorest neighborhoods in the city.[^cite_note-119]&#x20;

[Old Town Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_Chinatown) is next to the Pearl District in Northwest Portland. In 1890 it was the second largest Chinese community in the United States.[^cite_note-120] In 2017, the crime rate was several times above the city average. This neighborhood has been called Portland's skid row.[^cite_note-121]&#x20;



The [United States National Bank Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Bank_Building), downtown Portland

Southwest Portland is largely residential. [Downtown district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Portland,_Oregon), made up of commercial businesses, museums, [skyscrapers](29485.md), and public landmarks represents a small area within the southwest address section. Portland's South Waterfront area has been developing into a dense neighborhood of shops, condominiums, and apartments starting in the mid-2000s. Development in this area is ongoing.[^cite_note-122] The area is served by the [Portland Streetcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Streetcar), the [MAX Orange Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAX_Orange_Line_\(TriMet\)) and four [TriMet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TriMet) bus lines. This former industrial area sat as a [brownfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownfield) prior to development in the mid-2000s.[^cite_note-123]&#x20;

Southeast Portland is largely residential, and consists of several neighborhoods, including [Hawthorne District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorne,_Portland,_Oregon), [Belmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont,_Portland,_Oregon), [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn,_Portland,_Oregon), and [Mount Tabor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tabor,_Portland,_Oregon). [Reed College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_College), a private liberal arts college that was founded in 1908, is located within the confines of Southeast Portland as is Mount Tabor, a volcanic landform.&#x20;

South Portland includes the Lair Hill, Johns Landing and South Waterfront districts and Lewis & Clark College as well as the Riverdale area of unincorporated Multnomah County south of the Portland city limits.&#x20;

Portland's population has been and remains predominantly [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people). In 1940, Whites were over 98% of the city's population.[^cite_note-census1-128] In 2009, Portland had the fifth-highest percentage of White residents among the 40 largest U.S. metropolitan areas. A 2007 survey of the 40 largest cities in the U.S. concluded Portland's urban core has the highest percentage of White residents.[^cite_note-bh-130] Some scholars have noted the Pacific Northwest as a whole is "one of the last Caucasian bastions of the United States".[^cite_note-WilsonE-131] While Portland's diversity was historically comparable to metro Seattle and Salt Lake City, those areas grew more diverse in the late 1990s and 2000s. Portland not only remains White, but migration to Portland is disproportionately White.[^cite_note-bh-130][^cite_note-npr-132]&#x20;

The Oregon Territory banned African-American settlement in 1849. In the 19th century, certain laws allowed the immigration of Chinese laborers but prohibited them from owning property or bringing their families.[^cite_note-bh-130][^cite_note-insiders-133][^cite_note-frazier-134] The early 1920s saw the rapid growth of the [Ku Klux Klan](16779.md), which became very influential in Oregon politics, culminating in the election of [Walter M. Pierce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_M._Pierce) as governor.[^cite_note-insiders-133][^cite_note-frazier-134][^cite_note-Levitas2002-135]&#x20;

In 1940, Portland's African-American population was approximately 2,000 and largely consisted of railroad employees and their families. The largest influxes of populations of color occurred during World War II, as the city’s African-American population grew by a factor of ten for wartime work.[^cite_note-bh-130] After the war, the [Vanport flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanport_City,_Oregon) in 1948 displaced many African Americans. As they resettled, [redlining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redlining) directed the displaced workers from the wartime settlement to neighboring [Albina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albina,_Oregon).[^cite_note-WilsonE-131][^cite_note-frazier-134][^cite_note-136] There and elsewhere in Portland, they experienced police hostility, lack of employment, and [mortgage discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortgage_discrimination), leading to half the black population leaving after the war.[^cite_note-bh-130]&#x20;

Portland residents identifying solely as Asian Americans account for 7.1% of the population; an additional 1.8% is partially of Asian heritage. [Vietnamese Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_American) make up 2.2% of Portland's population, and make up the largest Asian ethnic group in the city, followed by [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_American) (1.7%), [Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_American) (0.6%), [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_American) (0.5%), [Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_American) (0.4%), [Laotians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laotian_American) (0.4%), [Hmong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_American) (0.2%), and [Cambodians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_American) (0.1%).[^cite_note-137] A small population of [Iu Mien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iu_Mien_Americans) live in Portland. Portland has two Chinatowns, with New Chinatown in the '[Jade District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jade_District)' along SE 82nd Avenue with Chinese supermarkets, Hong Kong-style noodle houses, [dim sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dim_sum), and Vietnamese [phở](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph%E1%BB%9F) restaurants.[^cite_note-138]&#x20;

With about 12,000 Vietnamese residing in the city proper, Portland has one of the largest Vietnamese populations in America per capita.[^cite_note-139] According to statistics, there are over 4,500 [Pacific Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_American) in Portland, making up 0.7% of the city's population.[^cite_note-140] There is a [Tongan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongan_American) community in Portland, who arrived in the area in the 1970s, and Tongans and Pacific Islanders as a whole are one of the fastest-growing ethnic groups in the Portland area.[^cite_note-141]&#x20;

In the 1980s and 1990s, radical skinhead groups flourished in Portland.[^cite_note-frazier-134] In 1988, [Mulugeta Seraw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulugeta_Seraw), an Ethiopian immigrant, was killed by three skinheads. The response to his murder involved a community-driven series of rallies, campaigns, nonprofits, and events designed to address Portland's racial history, leading to a city considered significantly more tolerant than in 1988 at Seraw's death.[^cite_note-142]&#x20;

76% of Latinos in Portland are of [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Americans) heritage.[^cite_note-143] Italians and Russian Jews had a very visible presence in Portland.[^cite_note-144] Portland also has a historic [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) population.[^cite_note-145]&#x20;

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), Portland had a population of 652,503. The median age was 37.6 years. 17.0% of residents were under the age of 18, and 13.9% of residents were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 97.8 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 96.4 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-146][^cite_note-Census2020PL-147] Whites not of Hispanic origin made up 66.4% of the total population.[^cite_note-Census2020PL-147]&#x20;

99.8% of residents lived in urban areas, while 0.2% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-148]&#x20;

There were 282,110 households in Portland, of which 22.5% had children under the age of 18 living in them. Of all households, 35.9% were married-couple households, 23.2% were households with a male householder and no spouse or partner present, and 29.5% were households with a female householder and no spouse or partner present. About 34.6% of all households were made up of individuals, and 10.1% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-146]&#x20;

There were 302,034 housing units, of which 6.6% were vacant. Among occupied housing units, 49.8% were owner-occupied, and 50.2% were renter-occupied. The homeowner vacancy rate was 1.3%, and the rental vacancy rate was 6.5%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-146]&#x20;

Whites not of Hispanic origin made up 72.2% of the total population.[^cite_note-quickfacts.census.gov-127]&#x20;

### Households

As of the [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census), there were 583,776 people living in the city, organized into 235,508 households. The population density was 4,375.2 people per square mile. There were 265,439 housing units at an average density of 1,989.4 per square mile (768.1/km<sup>2</sup>). Population growth in Portland increased 10.3% between 2000 and 2010.[^cite_note-149] Population growth in the [Portland metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_metropolitan_area) has outpaced the national average during the last decade, and this is expected to continue over the next 50 years.[^cite_note-pt_growth-150]&#x20;

Out of 223,737 households, 24.5% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 38.1% were married couples living together, 10.8% had a female householder with no husband present, and 47.1% were non-families. 34.6% of all households were made up of individuals, and 9% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.3, and the average family size was 3. The age distribution was 21.1% under the age of 18, 10.3% from 18 to 24, 34.7% from 25 to 44, 22.4% from 45 to 64, and 11.6% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 35 years. For every 100 females, there were 97.8 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 95.9 males.&#x20;

The median income for a household in the city was \$40,146, and the median income for a family was \$50,271. Males had a reported median income of \$35,279 versus \$29,344 reported for females. The per capita income for the city was \$22,643. 13.1% of the population and 8.5% of families were below the poverty line. Out of the total population, 15.7% of those under the age of 18 and 10.4% of those 65 and older were living below the poverty line. Figures delineating the income levels based on race are not available at this time. According to the [Modern Language Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Language_Association), in 2010, 80.9% (539,885) of Multnomah County residents ages 5 and over spoke English as their [primary language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_language) at home.[^cite_note-mod-151] 8.1% of the population spoke Spanish (54,036), with Vietnamese speakers making up 1.9%, and Russian 1.5%.[^cite_note-mod-151]&#x20;

### Sexual orientation and gender identity

The Portland metropolitan area has historically had a significant [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) population throughout the late 20th and early 21st century.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] In 2015, the city metro had the second highest percentage of LGBT residents in the United States with 5.4% of residents identifying as gay, lesbian, bisexual, or transgender, second only to San Francisco.[^cite_note-nylgbt-154] In 2006, it was reported to have the seventh highest LGB population in the country, with 8.8% of residents identifying as gay, lesbian, or bisexual, and the metro ranking fourth in the nation at 6.1%.[^cite_note-ACSGates-155] The city held its first [pride festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_festival) in 1975 on the [Portland State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_State_University) campus.[^cite_note-156]&#x20;

### Homelessness



Tent camps set up on the sidewalk in the Lloyd District neighborhood

A 2019 survey by the city's budget office showed that homelessness is perceived as the top challenge facing Portland, and was cited as a reason people move and do not participate in park programs.[^cite_note-159] Calls to 911 concerning "unwanted persons" significantly increased between 2013 and 2018, and since then, the police have increasingly dealt with the homeless population, many of whom are diagnosed as mentally ill.[^cite_note-160] Homelessness has taken a toll on the sense of safety among visitors, and residents and business owners are adversely impacted.[^cite_note-161] Even though homeless services and shelter beds have increased, as of 2020 homelessness is considered an intractable problem in Portland.[^cite_note-162]&#x20;

When encampments were being cleared in the [Springwater Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springwater_Corridor) in 2016, a community member from the nearby [Lents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lents,_Portland,_Oregon) neighborhood raised concerns that those campers were moving to their neighborhood. An aide, Lucas Hillier, from then-mayor [Charlie Hales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Hales)'s office publicly mocked the citizen on a Facebook post that it's nothing compared to the Springwater Corridor camp, referencing a [KPTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPTV) story, writing "You think that one is bad, check out this douchebag." While the aide issued an apology, the citizen commented that the mayor's office was very disconnected from issues faced by their constituents.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164] As of 2020, Lucas Hillier manages the campsite cleanup program for the city.[^cite_note-165]&#x20;

The proposed budget for 2022–23 includes \$5.8MM to buy land for affordable housing, and \$36MM to equip and operate "safe rest villages".[^cite_note-166] A 2022 initiative approved by the Portland city council makes homeless camping illegal, eventually requiring homeless individuals to move into mass shelters.[^cite_note-167]&#x20;

Portland voters passed the Supportive Housing Services tax in 2020. In 2019, the reported number of homeless people was around 4,000. That number rose to over 10,000, though some of that increase was due to a change in counting methods in 2022.[^cite_note-168]&#x20;

### Crime

According to the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI)'s [Uniform Crime Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Crime_Report) in 2009, Portland ranked 53rd in violent crime out of the top 75 U.S. cities with a population greater than 250,000.[^cite_note-169] The murder rate in Portland in 2013 averaged 2.3 murders per 100,000 people per year, which was lower than the national average. In 2011, 72% of arrested male subjects tested positive for illegal drugs, and the city was dubbed the "deadliest drug market in the Pacific Northwest" due to drug-related deaths.[^cite_note-170]&#x20;

In the Portland [Metropolitan statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area), which includes Clackamas, Columbia, Multnomah, Washington, and Yamhill Counties, OR, and Clark and Skamania Counties, WA, for 2017, the murder rate was 2.6, and violent crime was 283.2 per 100,000 people per year. In 2017, the population within the city of Portland was 649,408, and there were 24 murders and 3,349 violent crimes.[^cite_note-171]&#x20;

In the years following the COVID pandemic, the city saw an increase in crime, with car theft rates in Portland reaching the fifth-highest of any US metropolitan area in 2023.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] More than 11,000 vehicles were stolen in 2022, up from 6,500 in 2019.[^cite_note-174] Similarly, an increase in violent crime peaked in 2022 with a record 101 homicides.[^cite_note-175] In 2021, Portland recorded 90 homicides, compared with 20 in 2016, and 27 in 2017.[^cite_note-176]&#x20;

As of 2025, violent crime in the city saw a marked decline, representing the highest drop of any city covered by the Major City Chiefs Mid-Year Report at a 51% decrease from 2024.[^cite_note-portland.gov2025-15] However, Portland has the second highest property crime per 100,000 population in the United States.[^cite_note-177] The city saw just 17 homicides in the period from January 1, 2025, to June 30 as opposed to 35 during the same time frame in the previous year.[^cite_note-\:1-16] Car theft also saw a significant decrease starting in 2024, with the city seeing the lowest number of cars stolen since recording began in 2015.[^cite_note-\:3-17]&#x20;

## Economy

See also: [Companies based in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Companies_based_in_Portland,_Oregon)



[Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) has its North American headquarters in the [Overlook neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overlook,_Portland,_Oregon).

Portland's location is beneficial for several industries. Relatively low energy cost, accessible resources, north–south and east–west Interstates, international air terminals, large marine shipping facilities, and both West Coast intercontinental railroads are all economic advantages.[^cite_note-citydata_economy-178]&#x20;

The city's marine terminals alone handle over 13 million tons of cargo per year, and the port is home to one of the largest commercial dry docks in the country.[^cite_note-answers_cg-179][^cite_note-portofportland_report-180] The [Port of Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Portland_\(Oregon\)) is the third-largest export tonnage port on the west coast of the U.S., and being about 80 miles (130 km) upriver, it is the largest freshwater port.[^cite_note-citydata_economy-178]&#x20;

The scrap steel industry's history in Portland predates World War II. [Radius Recycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radius_Recycling) (formerly Schnitzer Steel), a major [scrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrap) recycler is headquartered in the [KOIN Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KOIN_Tower) in Portland.[^cite_note-181] Other heavy industry companies in Portland include [ESCO Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESCO_Group)[^cite_note-182] and [Oregon Steel Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Steel_Mills).[^cite_note-183]&#x20;

Technology is a major component of the city's economy, with more than 1,200 technology companies existing within the metro.[^cite_note-citydata_economy-178] This high density of technology companies has led to the nickname [Silicon Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Forest) being used to describe the Portland area, a reference to the abundance of trees in the region and to the [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley) region in Northern California.[^cite_note-name-184] The area also hosts facilities for software companies and online [startup companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Startup_companies), some supported by local [seed funding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_funding) organizations and [business incubators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_incubator).[^cite_note-185]&#x20;

[Providence Health & Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Health_%26_Services) became the largest employer in the Portland Metro area in 2023 with the second largest being Intel.[^cite_note-186]&#x20;

The Portland metro area has become a [business cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_cluster) for athletic/outdoor gear and footwear manufacturer's headquarters. The area is home to the global, North American or U.S. headquarters of [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) ### Housing

In 2016, home prices in Portland grew faster than in any other city in the United States.[^cite_note-194] Apartment rental costs in Portland reported in November 2019 were \$1,337 for two bedrooms and \$1,133 for one bedroom.[^cite_note-195]&#x20;

In 2017, developers projected an additional 6,500 apartments to be built in the Portland Metro Area over the next year.[^cite_note-196] However, as of December 2019, the number of homes available for rent or purchase in Portland continues to shrink. Over the past year, housing prices in Portland have risen 2.5%. Housing prices in Portland continue to rise, the median price rising from \$391,400 in November 2018 to \$415,000 in November 2019.[^cite_note-197] There has been a rise of people from out of state moving to Portland, which impacts housing availability. Because of the demand for affordable housing and influx of new residents, more Portlanders in their 20s and 30s are still living in their parents' homes.[^cite_note-198] There is a considerable amount of "[Airbnb](32058290.md) type" rentals in the city. An audit in 2018 located around 4,600 listings, of which 80% were illegally operated.[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200]&#x20;

In 2021, Portland became the largest city in the United States to end [single-family zoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-family_zoning), allowing four homes by right on virtually all residential lots and eliminating most off-street parking requirements.[^cite_note-201] In the three years since the law passed, approximately 1,400 new "[missing middle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missing_middle_housing)" homes were created, outpacing other types of homes, and these homes cost approximately \$300,000 less than comparable new single-family units.[^cite_note-202]&#x20;

### Tax policy

Portland residents face some of the highest taxes in the United States. The state levies the sixth highest income tax on individuals, the top begins in at \$125,000.[^cite_note-203] Including taxes administered by [Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_\(Oregon_regional_government\)) and [Multnomah County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multnomah_County,_Oregon), Portland Oregon has the 2nd highest top marginal rate in the United States at 14.7737%. The city rates ranks first for [S-Corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S_corporation), [Partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership) and [LLCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_liability_company) and first for [C-Corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_corporation).[^cite_note-204] In addition to state taxes, Portland has the nation's fifth-highest effective property tax rate on median-valued homes among major cities[^cite_note-205] and the 13th highest rate for commercial property taxes. Due to [Oregon Ballot Measure 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Ballot_Measures_47_and_50), property tax rates are related to the value of the land as of 1995, which has caused disparities in tax burden between two properties which sell for the same price but in different neighborhoods.[^cite_note-206] The city also imposes a 1% Clean Energy Surcharge on business with more than \$1 billion in worldwide receipts and more than \$500k in Portland revenue. All Portland residents over age 18 who earn more than \$1,000 and are in a household with income above the poverty level are also required to pay a \$35 [Portland Arts Tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Arts_Tax).[^cite_note-207]&#x20;

In 2020, local voters passed bills to impose a Supportive Housing Services tax and a Preschool for All Personal Income tax.[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-multco.us-209] The goal of the Supportive Housing Services tax was to raise \$250 million annually to address homelessness across the metro Portland region by levying a 1% tax on income above \$125,000 for individuals or \$200,000 for couples filing jointly, and a 1% business income tax on net income for business with receipts above \$5 million. The Preschool for All tax is 1.5% on \$125k individual / \$200k joint filers and an additional 1.5% on income over \$250k individuals / \$400k joint, to fund preschool programs in Multnomah County. The Preschool for All rates are expected to increase by 0.8% in 2027.[^cite_note-multco.us-209] All local taxes are administered by the City of Portland Revenue Division, including for those who live in portions of Metro or Multnomah County outside the city.[^cite_note-210]&#x20;

## Arts and culture

Main article: [Culture of Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Portland,_Oregon)

### Music, film, and performing arts

See also: [Music of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Oregon#Portland); [Cinemas in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinemas_in_Portland,_Oregon); and [List of films shot in Northwestern Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_shot_in_Oregon#Northwest)



The Sagebrush Symphony, an early incarnation of the [Portland Youth Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Youth_Philharmonic), performing in [Burns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burns,_Oregon) c. 1916

Portland is home to a range of classical performing arts institutions including the [Portland Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Opera), [Portland Baroque Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Baroque_Orchestra), [Oregon Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Symphony) and [Portland Youth Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Youth_Philharmonic); the last of these, established in 1924, was the first youth orchestra established in the United States.[^cite_note-211] The city is also home to several theaters and performing arts institutions including the [Oregon Ballet Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Ballet_Theatre), Northwest Children's Theatre, [Portland Center Stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Center_Stage), [Artists Repertory Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artists_Repertory_Theatre), Curious Comedy Theatre and [Miracle Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracle_Theatre).&#x20;

In 2013, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* named the city's music scene as one of the "most vibrant" in the United States.[^cite_note-212] Portland is home to famous bands such as the [Kingsmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kingsmen) and [Paul Revere & the Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Revere_%26_the_Raiders), both famous for their association with the song "[Louie Louie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louie_Louie)" (1963).[^cite_note-213] Other widely known musical groups include the [Dandy Warhols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dandy_Warhols), [Quarterflash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarterflash), [Everclear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everclear_\(band\)), [Pink Martini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Martini), [Sleater-Kinney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleater-Kinney), [Blitzen Trapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitzen_Trapper), the [Decemberists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Decemberists), and [Elliott Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Smith). More recently, [Portugal. The Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal._The_Man), [Modest Mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modest_Mouse), and [the Shins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shins) have made their home in Portland. In the 1980s, the city was home to a burgeoning punk scene, which included bands such as the [Wipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wipers_\(band\)) and [Dead Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Moon).[^cite_note-214] The city's now-demolished [Satyricon nightclub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satyricon_nightclub) was a punk venue where [Nirvana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nirvana_\(band\)) frontman [Kurt Cobain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Cobain) first encountered his future wife and [Hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hole_\(band\)) frontwoman [Courtney Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtney_Love) in 1990.[^cite_note-215] Love was then a resident of Portland and started several bands there with [Kat Bjelland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kat_Bjelland), later of [Babes in Toyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babes_in_Toyland_\(band\)).[^cite_note-e_weeklykennedy-216][^cite_note-e_true-217] Multi-[Grammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy) award-winning jazz artist [Esperanza Spalding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esperanza_Spalding) is from Portland and performed with the Chamber Music Society of Oregon at a young age.[^cite_note-218]&#x20;

A [wide range of films have been shot in Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_shot_in_Oregon), from various independent features to major big-budget productions. Director [Gus Van Sant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Van_Sant) has notably set and shot many of his films in the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFalsetto20151–29-219] The city has also been featured in various television programs, notably the [IFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFC_\(American_TV_channel\)) [sketch comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sketch_comedy) series *[Portlandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portlandia)*. The series, which ran for eight seasons from 2011 to 2018,[^cite_note-220] was shot on location in Portland, and satirized the city as a hub of liberal politics, organic food, alternative lifestyles, and anti-establishment attitudes.[^cite_note-221] [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV)'s long-time running reality show *[The Real World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Real_World_\(TV_series\))* was also shot in Portland for the show's 28th season: *[The Real World: Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Real_World:_Portland)* premiered on MTV in 2013.[^cite_note-222] Other television series shot in the city include *[Leverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverage_\(American_TV_series\))*, [*The Librarians*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Librarians_\(2014_TV_series\)),[^cite_note-223] *[Under Suspicion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Suspicion_\(TV_series\))*, *[Grimm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimm_\(TV_series\))*, and *[Nowhere Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowhere_Man_\(American_TV_series\))*.[^cite_note-224]&#x20;

An unusual feature of Portland entertainment is the large number of movie theaters serving beer, often with second-run or revival films.[^cite_note-225] Notable examples of these "brew and view" theaters include the [Bagdad Theater and Pub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bagdad_Theater_and_Pub), a former [vaudeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaudeville) theater built in 1927 by [Universal Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPalahniuk200363–64-226] [Cinema 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_21); and the [Laurelhurst Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurelhurst_Theater), in operation since 1923. Portland hosts the world's longest-running [H. P. Lovecraft Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._P._Lovecraft)[^cite_note-227] at the [Hollywood Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Theatre_\(Portland,_Oregon\)).[^cite_note-228]&#x20;

### Museums and recreation

See also: [List of museums in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Portland,_Oregon); [Tourism in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Portland,_Oregon); and [List of artists and art institutions in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_artists_and_art_institutions_in_Portland,_Oregon)



[Oregon Museum of Science and Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Museum_of_Science_and_Industry) (OMSI)

Portland is home to numerous museums and educational institutions, ranging from art museums to institutions devoted to science and wildlife. Among the science-oriented institutions are the [Oregon Museum of Science and Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Museum_of_Science_and_Industry) (OMSI), which consists of five main halls and other ticketed attractions, such as the [USS *Blueback*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Blueback_\(SS-581\)) submarine,[^cite_note-229] the ultra-large-screen Empirical Theater (which replaced an OMNIMAX theater in 2013),[^cite_note-230] and the Kendall [Planetarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetarium).[^cite_note-231] The [World Forestry Center Discovery Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Forestry_Center), located in the city's [Washington Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Park_\(Portland,_Oregon\)) area, offers educational exhibits on forests and forest-related subjects. Also located in Washington Park are the [Hoyt Arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoyt_Arboretum), the [International Rose Test Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Rose_Test_Garden), the [Japanese Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Japanese_Garden), and the [Oregon Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Zoo).[^cite_note-232]&#x20;



[Portland Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Art_Museum)

The [Portland Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Art_Museum) owns the city's largest art collection and presents a variety of touring exhibitions each year and, with the recent addition of the Modern and Contemporary Art wing, it became one of the United States' 25 largest museums. The [Oregon Historical Society Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Historical_Society_Museum), founded in 1898, which has a variety of books, film, pictures, artifacts, and maps dating back throughout Oregon's history. It houses permanent and temporary exhibits about Oregon history, and hosts traveling exhibits about the history of the United States.[^cite_note-233]&#x20;

[Oaks Amusement Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaks_Amusement_Park), in the Sellwood district of Southeast Portland, is the city's only [amusement park](137327.md) and is also one of the country's longest-running amusement parks. It has operated since 1905 and was known as the "[Coney Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coney_Island) of the Northwest" upon its opening.[^cite_note-beck-234]&#x20;

### Free speech and public nudity



[Protests against the Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protests_against_the_Iraq_War) on March 19, 2006

Strong free speech protections of the [Oregon Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Constitution) upheld by the [Oregon Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Supreme_Court) in *[State v. Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_v._Henry)*,[^cite_note-242] specifically found that full nudity and [lap dances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lap_dance) in [strip clubs](1380550.md) are [protected speech](21401843.md).[^cite_note-243] Portland has the highest number of strip clubs per-capita in a city in the United States, and Oregon ranks as the highest state for per-capita strip clubs.[^cite_note-244]&#x20;

In November 2008, a Multnomah County judge dismissed charges against a nude bicyclist arrested on June 26, 2008. The judge stated that the city's annual [World Naked Bike Ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Naked_Bike_Ride) – held each year in June since 2004 – has created a "well-established tradition" in Portland where cyclists may ride naked as a form of protest against cars and fossil fuel dependence.[^cite_note-245] The defendant was not riding in the official World Naked Bike Ride at the time of his arrest as it had occurred 12 days earlier that year, on June 14.[^cite_note-246]&#x20;

### Public art

Main articles: [List of artists and art institutions in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_artists_and_art_institutions_in_Portland,_Oregon) and [List of public art in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_public_art_in_Portland,_Oregon)

## Sports

Main article: [Sports in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Portland,_Oregon)

See also: [List of sports venues in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sports_venues_in_Portland,_Oregon)



The [Moda Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moda_Center), home of the [Portland Trail Blazers](72897.md) and the [Portland Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Fire)

Portland is home to four major league sports franchises: the [Portland Trail Blazers](72897.md) of the [NBA](22093.md), the [Portland Timbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Timbers) of [Major League Soccer](71802.md) (MLS), the [Portland Thorns FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Thorns_FC) of the [National Women's Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women%27s_Soccer_League), and the [Portland Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Fire) of the [WNBA](57721.md). In 2015, the Timbers won the [MLS Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Cup_2015), which was the first male professional sports championship for a team from Portland since the Trail Blazers won the [NBA championship in 1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_NBA_Finals).[^cite_note-251]&#x20;

Despite being the 26th most populated [metro area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_metropolitan_area) in the United States, Portland contains only one franchise from the four major sports leagues (NFL, NBA, NHL, MLB), making it the United States' third most populated metro area with that distinction, behind [San Antonio](53848.md), which also has only an NBA team (the [Spurs](72889.md)), and [San Diego](28504.md) which has only an MLB team (the [Padres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Padres)). The city has been often rumored to receive an additional franchise, although efforts to acquire a team have failed due to stadium funding issues.[^cite_note-252] An organization known as the [Portland Diamond Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Diamond_Project) (PDP)[^cite_note-253] has worked with MLB and local government, and there are plans to have an MLB stadium constructed at [Zidell Yards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zidell_Yards) in the [South Waterfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Waterfront,_Portland,_Oregon) neighborhood.[^cite_note-254] The PDP has not yet received the funding for this project.&#x20;



[Providence Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Park), home of the [Portland Timbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Timbers) and the [Portland Thorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Thorns_FC)

Portland sports fans are characterized by their passionate support. The Trail Blazers sold out every home game between 1977 and 1995, a span of 814 consecutive games, the second-longest streak in American sports history.[^cite_note-255] The Timbers joined MLS in 2011 and sold out 163 consecutive games through March 8, 2020, when they played their final home game prior to MLS suspending the season due to the COVID-19 pandemic.[^cite_note-256] The Timbers season ticket waiting list has reached over 10,000, the longest waiting list in MLS.[^cite_note-257] In 2015, they became the first team in the Northwest to win the [MLS Cup](483246.md). Player Diego Valeri marked a new record for fastest goal in MLS Cup history at 27 seconds into the game.[^cite_note-258]&#x20;

The annual [Cambia Portland Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Classic) women's golf tournament in September, now in its 50th year, is the longest-running non-major tournament on the [LPGA Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LPGA_Tour), plays in the southern suburb of [West Linn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Linn,_Oregon).[^cite_note-259]&#x20;



The Shamrock Run, held annually on [St. Patrick's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Patrick%27s_Day)

## Parks and recreation

Main article: [List of parks in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_parks_in_Portland,_Oregon)



[Forest Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Park_\(Portland,_Oregon\)) is the largest wilderness park in the United States that is within city limits.

Parks and greenspace planning date back to [John Charles Olmsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Charles_Olmsted)'s 1903 *Report to the Portland Park Board*. In 1995, voters in the Portland metropolitan region passed a regional bond measure to acquire valuable natural areas for fish, wildlife, and people.[^cite_note-267] Ten years later, more than 8,100 acres (33 km<sup>2</sup>) of ecologically valuable natural areas had been purchased and permanently protected from development.[^cite_note-268]&#x20;

Portland is one of only four cities in the U.S. with extinct volcanoes within its boundaries (along with [Pilot Butte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_Butte_\(Oregon\)) in [Bend, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bend,_Oregon), [Jackson Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_Volcano) in [Jackson, Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson,_Mississippi), and [Diamond Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_Head,_Hawaii) in [Honolulu](13887.md), Hawaii). [Mount Tabor Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tabor,_Portland,_Oregon) is known for its scenic views and historic reservoirs.[^cite_note-269]&#x20;

[Forest Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Park_\(Portland,_Oregon\)) is the largest wilderness park within city limits in the United States, covering more than 5,000 acres (2,023 ha).[^cite_note-270]&#x20;

Portland's downtown features two groups of contiguous city blocks dedicated for park space: the [North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Park_Blocks) and [South Park Blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park_Blocks).[^cite_note-271][^cite_note-272] The 37-acre (15 ha) [Tom McCall Waterfront Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_McCall_Waterfront_Park) was built in 1974 along the length of the downtown waterfront after [Harbor Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_Drive) was removed; it now hosts large events throughout the year.[^cite_note-273] The nearby historically significant [Burnside Skatepark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnside_Skatepark) and five indoor [skateparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skatepark) give Portland a reputation as possibly "the most skateboard-friendly town in America."[^cite_note-274]&#x20;

In February 2015, the City Council approved a total ban on smoking in all city parks and natural areas effective July 1, 2015. The ban includes cigarettes, vaping, as well as marijuana.[^cite_note-275]&#x20;

## Government

### City hall

See also: [Government of Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Portland,_Oregon); [Mayor of Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Portland,_Oregon); and [Portland City Council (Oregon)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_City_Council_\(Oregon\))



[Portland City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_City_Hall_\(Oregon\))

Since January 1, 2025, the city of Portland is governed by a [mayor–council government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor%E2%80%93council_government) system. Elected officials include the [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Portland,_Oregon), a 12-member city council, and a city auditor. The city council is responsible for legislative policy, while the mayor appoints a professional [city manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_manager) who oversees the various bureaus and day-to-day operations of the city. The mayor is elected [at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-large), while the council is elected in four geographic districts using [single transferable vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_transferable_vote), with 3 winning candidates per district. Portland's current form of government was approved by voters in a 2022 ballot measure, with the [first elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Portland,_Oregon_municipal_elections) under the new system [held in 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Portland,_Oregon_City_Council_election).[^cite_note-276]&#x20;

The [Portland Office of Community and Civic Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Office_of_Community_and_Civic_Life) (formerly Office of Neighborhood Involvement)[^cite_note-277] serves as a conduit between city government and Portland's [95 officially recognized neighborhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_of_Portland,_Oregon). Each neighborhood is represented by a volunteer-based [neighborhood association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood_association) which serves as a liaison between residents of the neighborhood and the city government. The city provides funding to neighborhood associations through seven district coalitions, each of which is a geographical grouping of several neighborhood associations. Most (but not all) neighborhood associations belong to one of these district coalitions.&#x20;

Portland and its surrounding metropolitan area are served by [Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_\(Oregon_regional_government\)), the United States' only directly elected [metropolitan planning organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_planning_organization). Metro's charter gives it responsibility for [land use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_use) and [transportation planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_planning), [solid waste management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_waste_management), and map development.&#x20;

The [Multnomah County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multnomah_County,_Oregon) government provides many services to the Portland area, as do [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_County,_Oregon) and [Clackamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clackamas_County,_Oregon) counties to the west and south.&#x20;

Fire and emergency services are provided by [Portland Fire & Rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Fire_%26_Rescue).[^cite_note-278]&#x20;

### Courts and law enforcement

See also: [Multnomah County Circuit Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multnomah_County_Circuit_Court) and [Portland Police Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Police_Bureau)

Law enforcement is primarily provided by the [Portland Police Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Police_Bureau) (PPB). The [Multnomah County Sheriff's Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multnomah_County_Sheriff%27s_Office) (MCSO) provides law enforcement on the city's [transit system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TriMet) through their Transit Police division which is supplemented from officers from all local law enforcement agencies that fall within the transit authority's boundaries.[^cite_note-279]&#x20;

### State and national politics



Built in 1869, the [Pioneer Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_Courthouse) is the oldest federal building in the Pacific Northwest.

Portland strongly favors the [Democratic Party](5043544.md); registered Democrats (51.2%) outnumber [Republicans](32070.md) (10.5%) nearly 5 to 1. Portland is split among three of Oregon's U.S. [congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_districts). A small portion of southeastern Portland is in the [5th District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon%27s_5th_congressional_district), currently represented by Democrat [Janelle Bynum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janelle_Bynum) (D-Happy Vslley). [Lori Chavez-DeRemer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lori_Chavez-DeRemer) was the first [Republican](32070.md) to represent a significant portion of the city in the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md) since 1975, from 2023 to 2025. Both of Oregon's senators, [Ron Wyden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Wyden) and [Jeff Merkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Merkley), are progressive Democrats from Portland.&#x20;

In the [2008 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_election_in_Oregon,_2008), Democratic candidate [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) easily carried Portland, winning 245,464 votes from city residents to 50,614 for his [Republican](32070.md) rival, [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain). In the [2012 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_election_in_Oregon,_2012), Democratic candidate Barack Obama again easily carried Portland, winning 256,925 votes from Multnomah county residents to 70,958 for his Republican rival, [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney).[^cite_note-282]&#x20;

[Sam Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Adams_\(Oregon_politician\)), the former mayor of Portland, became the city's first openly gay mayor in 2009.[^cite_note-283] In 2004, 59.7 percent of Multnomah County voters cast ballots against [Oregon Ballot Measure 36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Ballot_Measure_36_\(2004\)), which amended the [Oregon Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Constitution) to prohibit recognition of [same-sex marriages](92656.md). The measure passed with 56.6% of the statewide vote. Multnomah County is one of two counties where a majority voted against the initiative; the other is [Benton County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benton_County,_Oregon), which includes [Corvallis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvallis,_Oregon), home of [Oregon State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_University).[^cite_note-284] On April 28, 2005, Portland became the only city in the nation to withdraw from a [Joint Terrorism Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Terrorism_Task_Force).[^cite_note-ACLU20050428-285][^cite_note-WashExam20101128BY-286] As of February 19, 2015, the Portland city council approved permanently staffing the JTTF with two of its city's police officers.[^cite_note-287]&#x20;

### City planning and development

Video of Portland's urban growth boundary. The red dots indicate areas of growth between 1986 and 1996.

The city consulted with urban planners as far back as 1904, resulting in the development of [Washington Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Park_\(Portland,_Oregon\)) and the [40-Mile Loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40-Mile_Loop) greenway, which connects many of the city's parks.[^cite_note-289] Portland is often cited as an example of a city with strong [land use planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_use_planning) controls.[^cite_note-smartplan-290] This is largely the result of statewide land conservation policies adopted in 1973 under Governor [Tom McCall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_McCall), in particular the requirement for an [urban growth boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_growth_boundary) (UGB) for every city and metropolitan area. The opposite extreme, a city with few or no controls, is typically illustrated by [Houston](13774.md).[^cite_note-291][^cite_note-292][^cite_note-293][^cite_note-294]&#x20;



1966 photo shows sawdust-fired power plant on the edge of downtown that was removed to make way for dense residential development. High-rises to left in background were early projects of the Portland Development Commission.

Oregon's 1973 "[urban growth boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_growth_boundary)" law limits the boundaries for large-scale development in each metropolitan area in Oregon.[^cite_note-metro_ugb-295] This limits access to utilities such as sewage, water and telecommunications, as well as coverage by fire, police and schools.[^cite_note-metro_ugb-295] Portland's urban growth boundary, adopted in 1979, separates urban areas (where high-density development is encouraged and focused) from traditional farm land (where restrictions on non-agricultural development are very strict).[^cite_note-296] This was atypical in an era when automobile use led many areas to neglect their core cities in favor of development along [interstate highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_highway), in suburbs, and [satellite cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_city).&#x20;

The original state rules included a provision for expanding urban growth boundaries, but critics felt this was not being accomplished. In 1995, the State passed a law requiring cities to expand UGBs to provide enough undeveloped land for a 20-year supply of future housing at projected growth levels.[^cite_note-297] In 2007, the legislature changed the law to require the maintenance of an estimated 50 years of growth within the boundary, as well as the protection of accompanying farm and rural lands.[^cite_note-pt_growth-150] The growth boundary, along with efforts of the Portland Development Commission to create economic development zones, has led to the development of a large portion of downtown, a large number of mid- and high-rise developments, and an overall increase in housing and business density.[^cite_note-ssp_portland-298]&#x20;

[Prosper Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosper_Portland) (formerly the Portland Development Commission) is a semi-public agency that plays a major role in downtown development; city voters created it in 1958 to serve as the city's [urban renewal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_renewal) agency. It provides housing and economic development programs within the city and works behind the scenes with major local developers to create large projects. In the early 1960s, the Portland Development Commission led the razing of a large Italian-Jewish neighborhood downtown, bounded roughly by I-405, the Willamette River, 4th Avenue and Market street.[^cite_note-299] Mayor [Neil Goldschmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Goldschmidt) took office in 1972 as a proponent of bringing housing and the associated vitality back to the downtown area, which was seen as emptying out after 5 pm. The effort has had dramatic effects in the 30 years since, with many thousands of new housing units clustered in three areas: north of Portland State University (between I-405, SW Broadway, and SW Taylor St.); the RiverPlace development along the waterfront under the Marquam (I-5) bridge; and most notably in the Pearl District (between I-405, Burnside St., NW Northrup St., and NW 9th Ave.).&#x20;

Historically, [environmental consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism) has weighed significantly in the city's planning and development efforts.[^cite_note-topgreen-300] Portland was one of the first cities in the United States to promote and integrate alternative forms of transportation, such as the [MAX Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAX_Light_Rail) and extensive bike paths.[^cite_note-topgreen-300] The Urban Greenspaces Institute, housed in [Portland State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_State_University) Geography Department's Center for Mapping Research, promotes better integration of the built and natural environments. The institute works on urban park, trail, and natural areas planning issues, both at the local and regional levels.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlatt200643-301] In October 2009, the Portland City Council unanimously adopted a [climate action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_and_political_action_on_climate_change) plan that will cut the city's greenhouse gas emissions to 80% below 1990 levels by 2050.[^cite_note-302]&#x20;

As of 2012, Portland was the largest city in the United States that did not add [fluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluoride) to its public water supply,[^cite_note-303] and [fluoridation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_fluoridation) has historically been a subject of [controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_fluoridation_controversy) in the city.[^cite_note-304] Portland voters have four times voted against fluoridation, in 1956, 1962, 1980 (repealing a 1978 vote in favor), and 2013.[^cite_note-305] In 2012 the city council, responding to advocacy from public health organizations and others, voted unanimously to begin fluoridation by 2014. Fluoridation opponents forced a public vote on the issue,[^cite_note-306] and on May 21, 2013, city voters again rejected fluoridation.[^cite_note-307]&#x20;

## Education

Main article: [Education in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Portland,_Oregon)

### Primary and secondary education



[St. Mary's Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary%27s_Academy_\(Portland,_Oregon\)), a private [Roman Catholic](606848.md) girls' school established in 1859



Blanchard Education Service Center, [Portland Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Public_Schools_\(Oregon\))' headquarters

Nine public school districts include sections of Portland. [Portland Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Public_Schools_\(Oregon\)) is the largest school district, operating 86 public schools.[^cite_note-308] In addition to PPS, other school districts in Multnomah County that serve parts of the city include the [Beaverton School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaverton_School_District), [Centennial School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centennial_School_District_\(Oregon\)), [David Douglas School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Douglas_School_District), [Parkrose School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkrose_School_District), [Reynolds School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reynolds_School_District_\(Oregon\)), [Riverdale School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale_School_District_\(Oregon\)), and [Scappoose School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scappoose_School_District).[^cite_note-309] Portions in Clackamas County are in the [North Clackamas School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Clackamas_School_District) and Centennial School District.[^cite_note-310] Portions in Washington County are in Portland Public Schools.[^cite_note-311]&#x20;

[David Douglas High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Douglas_High_School), in the [Powellhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powellhurst-Gilbert,_Portland,_Oregon) neighborhood, has the largest enrollment of any public high school in the city.[^cite_note-312] Other high schools include [Benson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benson_Polytechnic_High_School), [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_High_School_\(Portland,_Oregon\)), [Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_High_School_\(Portland,_Oregon\)), [Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_High_School_\(Portland,_Oregon\)), [Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_High_School_\(Portland,_Oregon\)), [Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_High_School_\(Portland,_Oregon\)), [Parkrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkrose_High_School), [Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosevelt_High_School_\(Portland,_Oregon\)), and [Ida B Wells-Barnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ida_B._Wells-Barnett_High_School) (formerly Woodrow Wilson), and several suburban high schools which serve the city's outer areas. Established in 1869, [Lincoln High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_High_School_\(Portland,_Oregon\)) (formerly Portland High School) is the city's oldest public education institution, and is one of two of the oldest high schools west of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) (after San Francisco's [Lowell High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowell_High_School_\(San_Francisco\))).[^cite_note-Oregonian_2005-09-22-313]&#x20;

Former public schools in the city included [Washington High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_High_School_\(Portland,_Oregon\)), which operated from 1906 until 1981, as well as [Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adams_High_School_\(Portland,_Oregon\)) and [Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_High_School_\(Portland,_Oregon\)), which also closed the same year.&#x20;

There are nine private schools in Portland.&#x20;

### Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Portland,_Oregon)



[MAX Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAX_Light_Rail) is the centerpiece of the city's public transportation system.



[Portland Streetcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Streetcar) is a three-line system serving [downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Portland) and nearby areas.

The Portland metropolitan area has transportation services common to major U.S. cities, though Oregon's emphasis on proactive [land-use planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_use_forecasting) and [transit-oriented development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit-oriented_development) within the [urban growth boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_growth_boundary) means commuters have multiple well-developed options.&#x20;

In 2024, 7.1% of all commutes in Portland were on public transit.[^cite_note-\:4-322] [TriMet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TriMet) operates most of the region's buses and the [MAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAX_Light_Rail) (short for Metropolitan Area Express) [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) system, which connects the city and suburbs. Opened in 1986, the MAX system has expanded to five lines, with the latest being the [Orange Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAX_Orange_Line_\(TriMet\)) to [Milwaukie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukie,_Oregon), which began operating in September 2015.[^cite_note-323] [WES Commuter Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WES_Commuter_Rail) opened in February 2009 in Portland's western suburbs, linking [Beaverton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaverton,_Oregon) and [Wilsonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilsonville,_Oregon).&#x20;

The city-owned [Portland Streetcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Streetcar) serves two routes in the Central City – [downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Portland) and adjacent districts. The first line, which opened in 2001 and was extended in 2005–07, operates from the South Waterfront District through Portland State University and north through the West End of downtown, to shopping areas and dense residential districts north and northwest of downtown. The second line that opened in 2012 added 3.3 miles (5.3 km) of tracks on the east side of the Willamette River and across the [Broadway Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_Bridge_\(Portland,_Oregon\)) to a connection with the original line.[^cite_note-oreg-2012sep22-324] The east-side line completed a loop to the tracks on the west side of the river upon completion of the new [Tilikum Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilikum_Crossing) in 2015,[^cite_note-325] and, in anticipation of that, had been named the Central Loop line in 2012. However, it was renamed the [Loop Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loop_Service_\(Portland_Streetcar\)), with an A Loop (clockwise) and B Loop (counterclockwise), when it became a complete loop with the opening of the Tilikum Crossing bridge.&#x20;

Fifth and Sixth avenues within downtown comprise the [Portland Transit Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Transit_Mall), two streets devoted primarily to bus and light rail traffic with limited automobile access. Opened in 1977 for buses, the transit mall was renovated and rebuilt in 2007–09, with light rail added. Starting in 1975 and lasting nearly four decades, all transit service within downtown Portland was free, the area being known by TriMet as [Fareless Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fareless_Square), but a need for minor budget cuts and funding needed for expansion prompted the agency to limit free rides to rail service only in 2010,[^cite_note-oreg-2009aug12-326] and subsequently to discontinue the fare-free zone entirely in 2012.[^cite_note-oreg-2012aug31-327]&#x20;

TriMet provides real-time tracking of buses and trains with its TransitTracker, and makes the data available to software developers so they can create customized tools of their own.[^cite_note-transit_apps-328][^cite_note-google_adds_trimet-329]&#x20;



[Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Union_Station)

[I-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5_in_Oregon) connects Portland with the Willamette Valley, [Southern Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Oregon), and California to the south and with [Washington](13015878.md) to the north. [I-405](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_405_\(Oregon\)) forms a loop with I-5 around the central downtown area of the city and [I-205](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_205_\(Oregon-Washington\)) is a loop freeway route on the east side which connects to the [Portland International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_International_Airport). [U.S. 26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_26_in_Oregon) supports commuting within the metro area and continues to the Pacific Ocean westward and [Mount Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hood) and [Central Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Oregon) eastward. [U.S. 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_30_in_Oregon) has a main, bypass, and business route through the city extending to [Astoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astoria,_Oregon) to the west; through [Gresham, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gresham,_Oregon), and the eastern [exurbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter_town), and connects to [I-84](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_84_in_Oregon), traveling towards [Boise, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise,_Idaho).&#x20;



[Portland International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_International_Airport)

Portland's main airport is [Portland International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_International_Airport) (PDX), about 20 minutes by car (40 minutes by MAX) northeast of downtown. Portland is also home to [Oregon's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Oregon) only public use heliport, the [Portland Downtown Heliport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Downtown_Heliport).&#x20;

[Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak), the national passenger rail system, provides service to Portland at [Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Union_Station) on three routes. Long-haul train routes include the *[Coast Starlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Starlight)* (with service from Los Angeles to Seattle) and the *[Empire Builder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Builder)* (with service to Chicago). The *[Amtrak Cascades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_Cascades)* state-supported trains operate between [Vancouver, B.C.](32706.md), and [Eugene, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon), and serve Portland several times daily. The city is also served by [Greyhound Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines) intercity bus service, which also operates [BoltBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BoltBus), an express bus service. The city's first airport was the [Swan Island Municipal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Island_Municipal_Airport), which was closed in the 1940s.&#x20;



The [Portland Aerial Tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Aerial_Tram) connects the [South Waterfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Waterfront) district with [OHSU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Health_and_Science_University).

Portland is the only city in the United States that owns operating mainline [steam locomotives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_locomotive), donated to the city in 1958 by the railroads that ran them.[^cite_note-330] [Spokane, Portland & Seattle 700](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane,_Portland_%26_Seattle_700) and the world-famous [Southern Pacific 4449](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Pacific_4449) can be seen several times a year pulling a special [excursion train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excursion_train), either locally or on an extended trip. The "Holiday Express", pulled over the tracks of the [Oregon Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Pacific_Railroad_\(1997\)) on weekends in December, has become a Portland tradition over its several years running.[^cite_note-331] These trains and others are operated by volunteers of the [Oregon Rail Heritage Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Rail_Heritage_Foundation), an amalgamation of rail preservation groups which collaborated on the finance and construction of the [Oregon Rail Heritage Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Rail_Heritage_Center), a permanent and publicly accessible home for the locomotives, which opened in 2012 adjacent to OMSI.[^cite_note-oreg-2012sep-332]&#x20;

[In Portland, cycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Portland,_Oregon) is a significant mode of transportation. As the city has been particularly supportive of [urban bicycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_bicycling) it now ranks highly among the most [bicycle-friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle-friendly) cities in the world.[^cite_note-333] Bicycles accounted for 3.2% of commuting in 2024.[^cite_note-\:4-322] Bicycle commuting declined by 46% between 2016 and 2022 according to [Portland Bureau of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Bureau_of_Transportation) report.[^cite_note-334] For its achievements in promoting cycling as an everyday means of transportation, Portland has been recognized by the [League of American Bicyclists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_American_Bicyclists) and other [cycling organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_user_group) for its network of on-street bicycling facilities and other bicycle-friendly services, being one of only three U.S. cities to have earned a Platinum-level rating.[^cite_note-335] A new [bicycle-sharing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle-sharing_system), [Biketown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biketown), launched on July 19, 2016,[^cite_note-biketown_launches-336] with 100 stations in the city's central and eastside neighborhoods.[^cite_note-337]&#x20;

Portland completely abolished the requirement for parking minimums in 2023.[^cite_note-338]&#x20;

As of 2015, Portland is owed \$32.4 million in unpaid parking citations due to the [city government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Portland,_Oregon)'s refusal to collect more aggressively.[^cite_note-339]&#x20;

### Water

Portland's drinking water comes from two sources: The [Bull Run Watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull_Run_Watershed) and the Columbia South Shore Well Field. Most the city's supply of water comes from the Bull Run Watershed, which consists of roughly 102 square miles (260 km<sup>2</sup>) of forested land about 30 miles (48 km) west of Mount Hood.[^cite_note-\:5-340] The Columbia South Shore Well Field is a secondary water source that supplements the water supply during high-demand summer months and unexpected watershed events. The well field consists of 25 groundwater [wells](15105978.md) near the Columbia River in Northeast Portland.[^cite_note-341]&#x20;

Water from the Bull Run Watershed is [treated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_treatment) first at the Headworks Treatment Facility for disinfection, then at the Lusted Hill Treatment Facility for [chlorination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloramination) and [pH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PH) and [alkalinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkalinity) adjustment. Water from the Columbia South Short Well Field is treated onsite. All of the water is then sent to the Powell Butte reservoirs: Two large, concrete reservoirs buried underground at [Powell Butte Nature Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powell_Butte) with a capacity of 50 million gallons of water each. Powell Butte feeds two other smaller reservoirs in Portland: The [Washington Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Park_\(Portland,_Oregon\)) Reservoir and the [Kelly Butte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Butte_Natural_Area) Reservoir.[^cite_note-\:5-340]&#x20;
