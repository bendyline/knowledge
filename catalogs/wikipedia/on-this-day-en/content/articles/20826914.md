The **Iranian Red Crescent Society** (**IRCS**), officially the **Red Crescent Society of the Islamic Republic of Iran** (**RCSIRI**) ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): جمعیت هلال احمر جمهوری اسلامی ایران, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *Jamʿiyyat-e Helâl-e Ahmar-e Jomhuri-ye Eslâmi-ye Irân*) is a [non-governmental](46539.md) [humanitarian organization](1064581.md) in [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-Iranica-1] Founded as the [Red Lion and Sun Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Lion_and_Sun_Society) in 1922,[^cite_note-IRRC-4] it became affiliated with the [International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies](9720597.md) (IFRC) in 1924[^cite_note-6] and changed its name and emblem in 1980, informing the international community of *[Hilal Ahmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilal_Ahmar)* adoption[^cite_note-7] while assuming the right to adopt the former emblem in future.[^cite_note-Iranica-1]

The society is one of the world's largest national societies within IFRC[^cite_note-IRRC-4] and is noted for its special expertise in responding to [earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquakes).[^cite_note-BJMES-8]

Since inception, the IRCS has participated in a variety of public activities. Its core activity is to perform relief and rescue operations to help victims and the injured in natural disasters and accidents. It also engages a in wide range of humanitarian services in health and rehabilitation, training and research.[^cite_note-IRRC-4] The society had a therapeutic approach and was regarded a major [healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare) institution with thousands of hospital beds across the country until 1979, when all of its medical facilities were transferred to the [Ministry of Health](24234168.md).[^cite_note-Iranica-1]

IRCS is an example of strong national societies that play an important role domestically[^cite_note-BJMES-8] and is held in high esteem by the Iranian general public.[^cite_note-IRRC-4]

## Organization

[^cite_note-9]

IRCS has 30 governorate headquarters, one in each [province of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Iran), and 330 branches throughout the country, as of 2005.[^cite_note-IRRC-4] Based on the latest structure, it is made up of four departments: relief and rescue (*emdād wa najāt*), medical provisions (*tadārokāt-e pezeški*), volunteers (*dāvṭalabān*), and youth (*javānān*).[^cite_note-Iranica-1] The latter is largely made up of high school and university student members.[^cite_note-IRRC-4]

According to the law enacted by the [Iranian Parliament](383276.md), the society is run by a chairman appointed by the [President of Iran](233913.md). This procedure is in contrast with the elective nature of a society chairman as suggested by the IFRC.[^cite_note-Iranica-1] The secretary general is responsible for the administrative and executive affairs.[^cite_note-Iranica-1]

### Other subordinates

IRCS owns and runs Helal Iran Textile Industries Co., a producer of blankets and tents.[^cite_note-10] SOHA, a manufacturer of disposable medical devices is also owned by the society,[^cite_note-11] as well as *Shahrvand*, a daily newspaper.[^cite_note-12] The *Iranian Red Crescent Medical Journal*, an English language peer-reviewed journal on clinical and scientific medicine, is another publication of IRCS.[^cite_note-ircmj--13]

## Presidents

Heads of the society have been:[^cite_note-Iranica-1]

- 1922–1949: Amir Amir-A'lam (deputy director)&#x20;

  - 1922–1925: [Mohammad Hassan Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Hassan_Mirza) (honorary)

  - 1927–1931: [Mostowfi ol-Mamalek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostowfi_ol-Mamalek) (chairman)

  - 1931–1941: [Reza Pahlavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Shah) (symbolic)

  - 1941–1949: [Mohammad Reza Pahlavi](20065598.md)

- 1949–1979: Hossein Khatibi Nouri (managing director)&#x20;

  - 1949–1979: [Shams Pahlavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shams_Pahlavi) (chairwoman)

- 1979–1980: [Kazem Sami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazem_Sami)

- 1980–1981: Ali Behzadnia

- 1981–1983: [Hassan Firouzabadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Firouzabadi)

- 1983–1999: [Seifollah Vahid Dastjerdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seifollah_Vahid_Dastjerdi)

- 1999–2006: Ahmad-Ali Nourbala

- 2006–2010: Masoud Khatami

- 2010–2013: Abolhassan Faqih

- 2013–2014: [Mohammad Farhadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Farhadi)

- 2014–2017: Amir-Mohsen Ziayi

- 2017–2019: [Ali Asqar Peyvandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Asqar_Peyvandi)

- 2020–2021: Karim Hemmati

- 2021–present: [Pirhossein Kolivand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirhossein_Kolivand)
