# Hanging

"Death by hanging" redirects here. For the film, see [*Death by Hanging*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_by_Hanging).

For other uses, see [Hang (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_\(disambiguation\)).



Detail from a painting by [Pisanello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisanello), 1436–1438

**Hanging** is killing a person by suspending them from the neck with a [noose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noose) or [ligature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligature_strangulation). Hanging has been a standard method of [capital punishment](5902.md) since the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), and has been the primary execution method in numerous countries and regions. As a form of execution, it is commonly practiced at a structure called a [gallows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallows). The first known account of execution by hanging is in [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer)'s *[Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odyssey)*.[^cite_note-JSpine2011-02-1] Hanging is also a common [method of suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_by_hanging).

## Methods of judicial hanging

There are numerous methods of hanging in execution that induce death either by [cervical fracture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervical_fracture) or by [strangulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangling).

### Short drop

The short drop is a method of hanging in which the condemned prisoner stands on a raised support, such as a stool, ladder, cart, horse, or other vehicle, with the noose around the neck. The support is then moved away, leaving the person dangling from the rope.[^cite_note-Hughes2012-2][^cite_note-Potter1965-3] Suspended by the neck, the weight of the body tightens the noose around the neck, effecting [strangulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangulation) and death. Loss of consciousness is typically rapid and death ensues in a few minutes.[^cite_note-4]

&#x20;Before 1850, the short drop was the standard method of hanging, and it is still common in [suicides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicides) and extrajudicial hangings (such as [lynchings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynchings) and [summary executions](1227309.md)) which lack the specialised equipment and [drop-length calculation tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Table_of_Drops) used in the newer methods.



Execution of guards and [kapos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapo) of the [Stutthof concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stutthof_concentration_camp) on 4 July 1946 by short-drop hanging. In the foreground are the female overseers: [Jenny-Wanda Barkmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny-Wanda_Barkmann), [Ewa Paradies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewa_Paradies), [Elisabeth Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_Becker), [Wanda Klaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanda_Klaff), [Gerda Steinhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerda_Steinhoff) (left to right).

#### Pole method



Mass execution using "pole-hanging" of Serbs by the [Austro-Hungarian army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_army) in 1916

A short-drop variant is the [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian) "pole" method, called *Würgegalgen* (literally: strangling gallows), in which the following steps take place:

1. The condemned is made to stand before a specialized vertical pole or pillar, approximately 3 metres (9.8 ft) in height.

2. A rope is attached around the condemned's feet and routed through a pulley at the base of the pole.

3. The condemned is hoisted to the top of the pole by means of a sling running across the chest and under the arms.

4. A narrow-diameter noose is looped around the prisoner's neck, then secured to a hook mounted at the top of the pole.

5. The chest sling is released, and the prisoner is rapidly jerked downward by the assistant executioners via the foot rope, thus resulting in strangulation and death.

This method was later also adopted by the successor states, most notably by [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), where the "pole" method was used as the single type of execution from 1918 until 1954, when the prison hosting Czechoslovakia's executions, [Pankrác Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pankr%C3%A1c_Prison), constructed an indoor gallows that exclusively accommodated short-drop hangings to replace the pole method.[^cite_note-5] Nazi war criminal [Karl Hermann Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Hermann_Frank), executed in 1946 in [Prague](23844.md), was among approximately 1,000 condemned people executed by the pole hanging method in Czechoslovakia.[^cite_note-KHF-6]

### Standard drop



The execution of [Henry Wirz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Wirz) in 1865 with the U.S. Capitol dome in the background. Wirz was given a standard drop, which did not break his neck immediately.&#x20;

The standard drop involves a drop of between 4 and 6 feet (1.2–1.8 m) and came into use from 1866, when the scientific details were published by Irish doctor [Samuel Haughton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Haughton). Its use rapidly spread to English-speaking countries and those with judicial systems of English origin.

It was considered a humane improvement on the short drop because it was intended to be enough to [break the person's neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervical_fracture), causing immediate unconsciousness and rapid brain death.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Although considered more humane than the short drop, the standard drop was frequently insufficient to break the neck, and death often resulted from strangulation.[^cite_note-9]

This method was used to execute condemned [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) under United States jurisdiction after the [Nuremberg Trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Trials), including [Joachim von Ribbentrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_von_Ribbentrop) and [Ernst Kaltenbrunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Kaltenbrunner).[^cite_note-10] In the execution of Ribbentrop, historian Giles MacDonogh records that: "The hangman botched the execution and the rope throttled the former foreign minister for 20 minutes before he expired."[^cite_note-11] A *[Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_\(magazine\))* magazine report on the execution merely says: "The trap fell open and with a sound midway between a rumble and a crash, Ribbentrop disappeared. The rope quivered for a time, then stood tautly straight."[^cite_note-12]

The standard drop was also used to execute four of the conspirators involved in the [assassination of Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Abraham_Lincoln). Each of the four was given a drop of "almost" 5 ft (1.5 m) irrespective of their weight. Of the four, press accounts portray [George Atzerodt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Atzerodt) and [Mary Surratt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Surratt) as having fallen unconscious quickly and seemingly painlessly, while [David Herold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Herold) and [Lewis Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Powell_\(conspirator\)) struggled for several minutes after the drop, although none of the four experienced a broken neck as intended; surgeons found that all four strangled to death.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

### Long drop

See also: [Official Table of Drops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Table_of_Drops)



1901 postcard showing [Tom Ketchum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Ketchum)'s decapitated body following a botched execution by long-drop hanging. Caption reads "Body of Black Jack after the hanging showing head snapped off."



Execution of an unidentified Nazi war criminal after [World War II](32927.md)

The long-drop process, also known as the measured drop, was introduced to Britain in 1872 by [William Marwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Marwood) as a scientific advance on the standard drop, and further refined by his successor [James Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Berry_\(executioner\)). Instead of everyone falling the same standard distance, the person's height and weight[^cite_note-15] were used to determine how much slack would be provided in the rope so that the distance dropped would be enough to ensure that the neck was broken, but not so much that the person was decapitated. Careful placement of the eye or knot of the noose (so that the head was jerked back as the rope tightened) contributed to breaking the neck.

Prior to 1892, the drop was in the range of 4–10 ft (1.2–3.0 m), depending on the weight of the body, and was calculated to deliver an energy of 1,260 [foot-pounds force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot-pound_\(energy\)) (1,710 [J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joule)), which fractured the neck at either the 2nd and 3rd or 4th and 5th [cervical vertebrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervical_vertebrae). This force resulted in some decapitations, such as the infamous case of [Black Jack Ketchum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Ketchum) in [New Mexico Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico_Territory) in 1901, owing to a significant weight gain while in custody not having been factored into the drop calculations. Between 1892 and 1913, the length of the drop was shortened to avoid decapitation. After 1913, other factors were also taken into account, and the energy delivered was reduced to about 1,000 foot-pounds force (1,400 J). The record speed for a British long-drop hanging was seven seconds from the executioner entering the cell to the drop. Speed was considered to be important in the British system as it reduced the condemned's mental distress.[^cite_note-isbn0-340-21307-8-16]

Long-drop hanging is still practiced as the method of execution in a few countries, including [Japan](4967816.md) and Singapore.[^cite_note-\:0-17][^cite_note-18]

## As suicide

Main article: [Suicide by hanging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_by_hanging)

See also: [List of people who died by hanging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_who_died_by_hanging)



A sixteenth-century fresco from Tarzhishte Monastery, Strupets, Bulgaria, showing [Judas Iscariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judas_Iscariot) hanging himself

In [Canada](5042916.md), hanging is the most common method of suicide,[^cite_note-cansuicide-19] and in the United States, hanging is the second most common method, after self-inflicted [gunshot wounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunshot_wounds).[^cite_note-20] In 2024, hanging was the most common method in the [United Kingdom](31717.md), accounting for 56% of the 3,504 suicides.[^cite_note-21]

Those who survive a suicide-via-hanging attempt, whether due to breakage of the cord or [ligature point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangling#Ligature_strangulation), or being discovered and cut down, face a range of serious injuries, including [cerebral anoxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_anoxia) (which can lead to permanent brain damage), laryngeal fracture, cervical spine fracture (which may cause [paralysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralysis)), tracheal fracture, pharyngeal laceration, and carotid artery injury.[^cite_note-22]

## As human sacrifice

There are [some suggestions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norse_rituals#Human_sacrifice) that the [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings) practised hanging as human sacrifices to [Odin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odin), to honour Odin's own [sacrifice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrifice) of hanging himself from [Yggdrasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yggdrasil).[^cite_note-23] In Northern Europe, it is widely speculated that the [Iron Age bog bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bog_body#Iron_Age), many of which show signs of having been hanged, were examples of human sacrifice to the gods.[^cite_note-bog-24]

## Medical effects



[Anoxic brain injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_hypoxia) following a hanging—both the loss of visible differentiation between [grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_matter) and [white matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_matter) and the reduced [ventricle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventricular_system) size due to brain swelling are visible

A hanging may induce one or more of the following medical conditions, some leading to death:

- Closure of [carotid arteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carotid_arteries) causing [cerebral hypoxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_hypoxia)

  [^cite_note-JSpine2011-02-1]

- Closure of the [jugular veins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jugular_vein)

- Breaking of the [neck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neck) ([cervical fracture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervical_fracture)) causing traumatic [spinal cord injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervical_spine_injury) or even unintended [decapitation](70802.md)

  [^cite_note-25]

- Closure of the [airway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airway)

  [^cite_note-JSpine2011-02-1]

The cause of death in hanging depends on the conditions related to the event. When the body is released from a relatively high position, the major cause of death is severe trauma to the upper cervical spine. The injuries produced are highly variable. One study showed that only a small minority of a series of judicial hangings produced fractures to the cervical spine (6 out of 34 cases studied), with half of these fractures (3 out of 34) being the classic "[hangman's fracture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangman's_fracture)" (bilateral fractures of the pars interarticularis of the C2 vertebra).[^cite_note-26]

The side, or subaural knot, has been shown to produce other, more complex injuries, with one thoroughly studied case producing only ligamentous injuries to the cervical spine and bilateral vertebral artery disruptions, but no major vertebral fractures or crush injuries to the spinal cord.[^cite_note-27]



[John Ogilvie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ogilvie_\(saint\)), who in 1615 was hanged and disembowelled after torture for his refusal to give up the Catholic faith and convert to Protestantism

In the absence of fracture and dislocation, occlusion of blood vessels becomes the major cause of death, rather than [asphyxiation](98534.md). Obstruction of venous drainage of the brain via occlusion of the internal jugular veins leads to [cerebral oedema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_oedema) and then [cerebral ischemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_ischemia). The face will typically become engorged and [cyanotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanosis) (turned blue through lack of oxygen). Compromise of the cerebral blood flow may occur by obstruction of the carotid arteries, even though their obstruction requires far more force than the obstruction of jugular veins, since they are seated deeper and they contain blood in much higher pressure compared to the jugular veins.[^cite_note-28]

## Practices across the globe



*La Pendaison* (*The Hanging*), a plate from French artist [Jacques Callot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Callot)'s 1633 series *[The Great Miseries of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Grandes_Mis%C3%A8res_de_la_guerre)*.

Hanging has been a method of [capital punishment](5902.md) in many countries, and is still used by many countries. Long-drop hanging is mainly used by former British colonies, while short-drop and suspension hanging is common elsewhere, in countries including Iran and Afghanistan.&#x20;

### Afghanistan

Main article: [Capital punishment in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Afghanistan)

Hanging is the most used form of capital punishment in [Afghanistan](737.md). Following the [fall of the Taliban regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Kabul_\(2001\)) in 2001, the administration of the [Islamic Republic of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Afghanistan) under its first president [Hamid Karzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Karzai) was hesitant to use the death penalty.[^cite_note-afghkarzai-29] Karzai signed the first death warrant in April 2004, when the method of shooting was used to execute serial killer [Abdullah Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Shah).[^cite_note-30] In June 2011, Afghanistan resumed hanging as a method when Karzai ordered the execution of teenage terrorist [Zar Ajam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zar_Ajam) and an accomplice who were convicted of a terror attack in [Jalalabad](16274.md).[^cite_note-afghkarzai-29][^cite_note-31]

### Australia

Main article: [Capital punishment in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Australia)

Capital punishment was a part of the [legal system of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_system_of_Australia) from the establishment of [New South Wales](21654.md) as a British penal colony, until 1985, by which time all Australian states and territories had abolished the death penalty.[^cite_note-relTolerance-32] In practice, the last execution in Australia was the hanging of [Ronald Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Ryan) on 3 February 1967, in [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\)).[^cite_note-33]

During the 19th century, crimes that could carry a death sentence included [burglary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burglary), sheep theft, [forgery](62312.md), [sexual assaults](218407.md), murder and [manslaughter](7397035.md). During the 19th century, there were roughly eighty people hanged every year throughout the Australian colonies for these crimes.

### Bahamas

Main article: [Capital punishment in the Bahamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_the_Bahamas)

The Bahamas employs hanging to execute the condemned, but no executions have been conducted in the country since 2000.[^cite_note-34] As of 2023, there have been some inmates on death row but their sentences have been commuted.

### Bangladesh

Main article: [Capital punishment in Bangladesh](50059840.md)

Hanging is the only method of execution in [Bangladesh](3454.md), ever since its independence.

### Brazil

Main article: [Capital punishment in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Brazil)

Death by hanging was the customary method of capital punishment in Brazil throughout its history. Some important national heroes like [Tiradentes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiradentes) (1792) were killed by hanging. The last man executed in Brazil was the slave [Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_\(slave\)), in 1876.[^cite_note-35] The death penalty was abolished for all crimes, except for those committed under extraordinary circumstances such as war or military law, in 1890.[^cite_note-encarta-36]

### Bulgaria

Main article: [Capital punishment in Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Bulgaria)

Bulgaria's national hero, [Vasil Levski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasil_Levski), was executed by hanging by the [Ottoman](22278.md) court in [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia) in 1873. Every year since Bulgaria's liberation, thousands come with flowers on the date of his death, 19 February, to his monument where the gallows stood. The last execution was in 1989, and the death penalty was abolished for all crimes in 1998.[^cite_note-encarta-36]

### Canada

Main article: [Capital punishment in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Canada)

Historically, hanging was the only method of execution used in Canada and was in use as possible punishment for all murders until 1961, when murders were reclassified into capital and non-capital offences. The death penalty was restricted to apply only for certain offences to the National Defence Act in 1976 and was completely abolished in 1998.[^cite_note-37] The last hangings in Canada took place on 11 December 1962.[^cite_note-encarta-36]

### Egypt

Main article: [Capital punishment in Egypt](4542038.md)

In 1955, Egypt hanged three Israelis on charges of spying.[^cite_note-38] In 1982 Egypt hanged three civilians convicted of the [assassination of Anwar Sadat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Anwar_Sadat).[^cite_note-39] In 2004, Egypt hanged five militants on charges of trying to kill the Prime Minister.[^cite_note-40] To this day, hanging remains the standard method of capital punishment in Egypt, which executes more people each year than any other African country.

### Germany

Main article: [Capital punishment in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Germany)



[Public execution](4733727.md) of Polish civilians by the [Nazi Germans](21212.md) in German occupied [Kraków](16815.md) in 1942



Alleged Soviet partisans hanged by the Nazis in January 1943

In the territories occupied by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) from 1939 to 1945, strangulation hanging was a preferred means of public execution, although more criminal executions were performed by [guillotine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillotine) than hanging. The most commonly sentenced were [partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partisan_\(military\)) and [black marketeers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_market), whose bodies were usually left hanging for long periods. There are also numerous reports of concentration camp inmates being hanged. Hanging was continued in post-war Germany in the [British and US Occupation Zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany) under their jurisdiction, and for Nazi war criminals, until well after (western) Germany itself had abolished the death penalty by the [Basic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_for_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany) (constitution) as adopted in 1949. West Berlin was not subject to the Basic Law and abolished the death penalty in 1951. The [German Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Democratic_Republic) abolished the death penalty in 1987. The last execution ordered by a West German court was carried out by guillotine in Moabit prison in 1949. The last hanging in Germany was the one ordered of several war criminals in [Landsberg am Lech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landsberg_am_Lech) on 7 June 1951. The last known execution in East Germany was in 1981 by a pistol shot to the neck.[^cite_note-relTolerance-32]

### Hong Kong

Under British colonial rule, hanging was the form of capital punishment in Hong Kong. The last person who was executed was a Chinese Vietnamese man who attacked a security guard and another person. This execution occurred in 1966.[^cite_note-41]

### Hungary

Main article: [Capital punishment in Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Hungary)

The prime minister of Hungary, during the [1956 Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956), [Imre Nagy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imre_Nagy), was secretly tried, executed by hanging, and buried unceremoniously by the new [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)-backed Hungarian government, in 1958. Nagy was later publicly exonerated by Hungary.[^cite_note-42] Capital punishment was abolished for all crimes in 1990.[^cite_note-relTolerance-32]

### India

Main article: [Capital punishment in India](1494050.md)



The hanging of two participants of the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857) in British India.

Hanging was introduced by the British. All executions in India since independence have been carried out by hanging, although the law provides for military executions to be carried out by firing squad. In 1949, [Nathuram Godse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathuram_Godse), who had been sentenced to death for the assassination of [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi), was the first person to be executed by hanging in independent India.[^cite_note-TIE-43]

The [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) has suggested that [capital punishment](5902.md) should be given only in the "rarest of rare cases".[^cite_note-44]

Since 2001, eight people have been executed in India.

- [Dhananjoy Chatterjee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhananjoy_Chatterjee), convicted for rape and murder in 1991, was executed on 14 August 2004 in [Alipore Jail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alipore_Jail), Kolkata.

- [Ajmal Kasab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajmal_Kasab), the lone surviving terrorist of the [2008 Mumbai attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks), was executed on 21 November 2012 in [Yerwada Central Jail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerwada_Central_Jail), Pune. The Supreme Court of India had previously rejected his mercy plea, which was then rejected by the President of India. He was hanged one week later.

- [Afzal Guru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afzal_Guru), a terrorist found guilty of conspiracy in the [December 2001 attack on the Indian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Indian_Parliament_attack), was executed by hanging in [Tihar Jail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tihar_Jail), Delhi on 9 February 2013.

- [Yakub Memon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakub_Memon) was convicted over his involvement in the [1993 Bombay bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Bombay_bombings) by the Special Terrorist and Disruptive Activities court on 27 July 2007. His appeals and petitions for clemency were all rejected and he was finally executed by hanging on 30 July 2015 in Nagpur jail.

- On 20 March 2020, four prisoners named Pawan Gupta, Vinay Sharma, Mukesh Singh and Akshay Thakur who were convicted in the [2012 Delhi gang rape and murder case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Delhi_gang_rape_and_murder) were executed by hanging in Tihar Jail.

  [^cite_note-45]

### Iran

Main article: [Capital punishment in Iran](5170842.md)

Hanging is the most used form of capital punishment in Iran.[^cite_note-46]

### Iraq

Main article: [Capital punishment in Iraq](5732607.md)

See also: [Execution of Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Execution_of_Saddam_Hussein)

Hanging was used under the regime of [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein),[^cite_note-47] but was suspended along with capital punishment on 10 June 2003, when a coalition led by the United States [invaded](201936.md) and overthrew the previous regime. The death penalty was reinstated on 8 August 2004.[^cite_note-48]

In September 2005, three murderers were the first people to be executed since the restoration. Then on 9 March 2006, an official of Iraq's Supreme Judicial Council confirmed that Iraqi authorities had executed the first [insurgents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_insurgency_\(Iraq_War\)) by hanging.[^cite_note-49]

Saddam Hussein was sentenced to death by hanging for [crimes against humanity](61099.md)[^cite_note-50] on 5 November 2006, and was executed on 30 December 2006 at approximately 6\:00 a.m. local time. During the drop, there was an audible crack indicating that his neck was broken, a successful example of a long-drop hanging.[^cite_note-51]

[Barzan Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barzan_Ibrahim), the head of the Mukhabarat, Saddam's security agency, and [Awad Hamed al-Bandar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awad_Hamed_al-Bandar), former chief judge, were executed on 15 January 2007, also by the long-drop method, but Barzan was decapitated by the rope at the end of his fall.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

Former vice-president [Taha Yassin Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taha_Yassin_Ramadan) had been sentenced to life in prison on 5 November 2006, but the sentence was changed to death by hanging on 12 February 2007.[^cite_note-54] He was the fourth and final man to be executed for the 1982 crimes against humanity on 20 March 2007. The execution went smoothly.[^cite_note-55]

At the Anfal genocide trial, Saddam's cousin [Ali Hassan al-Majid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Hassan_al-Majid) (nicknamed Chemical Ali by Iraqis), former defence minister [Sultan Hashim Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Hashim_Ahmed) al-Tay, and former deputy Hussein Rashid Mohammed were sentenced to hang for their role in the [Al-Anfal Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Anfal_Campaign) against the Kurds on 24 June 2007.[^cite_note-56] Al-Majid was sentenced to death three more times: once for the 1991 suppression of a Shi'a uprising along with Abdul-Ghani Abdul Ghafur on 2 December 2008;[^cite_note-57] once for the 1999 crackdown in the assassination of [Grand Ayatollah Mohammad al-Sadr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Mohammad_Sadeq_al-Sadr) on 2 March 2009;[^cite_note-58] and once on 17 January 2010 for the gassing of the Kurds in 1988;[^cite_note-59] he was hanged on 25 January.[^cite_note-60]

On 26 October 2010, Saddam's top minister [Tariq Aziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariq_Aziz) was sentenced to hang for persecuting the members of rival Shi'a political parties.[^cite_note-61] His sentence was commuted to indefinite imprisonment after Iraqi president [Jalal Talabani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalal_Talabani) did not sign his execution order and he died in prison in 2015.

On 14 July 2011, US forces transferred condemned prisoners [Sultan Hashim Ahmad al-Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Hashim_Ahmad_al-Tai) and two of Saddam's half-brothers, [Sabawi Ibrahim al-Tikriti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabawi_Ibrahim_al-Tikriti) and [Watban Ibrahim al-Tikriti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watban_Ibrahim_al-Tikriti), to Iraqi authorities for execution.[^cite_note-62] The Iraqi High Tribunal had sentenced Saddam's half-brothers to death on 11 March 2009 for their roles in the executions of 42 traders who were accused of manipulating [food prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_prices).[^cite_note-63] None of the three men were executed.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

It is alleged that Iraq's government keeps the execution rate secret, and hundreds may be carried out every year. In 2007, Amnesty International stated that 900 people were at "imminent risk" of execution in Iraq.

### Israel

Main article: [Capital punishment in Israel](8951344.md)

Israel has provisions in its criminal law to use the death penalty for extraordinary crimes. It has been used only twice for Israelis, and only one of those executions was by hanging. On 31 May 1962, Nazi leader [Adolf Eichmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Eichmann) was executed by hanging after having been captured in Argentina in May 1960, taken to Israel and tried and sentenced to death.[^cite_note-encarta-36][^cite_note-67]

### Japan

Main article: [Capital punishment in Japan](4967816.md)

All executions in Japan are carried out by hanging.

On 23 December 1948, [Hideki Tojo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideki_Tojo), [Kenji Doihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenji_Doihara), [Akira Mutō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Mut%C5%8D), [Iwane Matsui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwane_Matsui), [Seishirō Itagaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seishir%C5%8D_Itagaki), [Kōki Hirota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dki_Hirota), and [Heitaro Kimura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heitaro_Kimura) were hanged at [Sugamo Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugamo_Prison) by the [U.S. occupation authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Commander_for_the_Allied_Powers) in [Ikebukuro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikebukuro) in [Allied-occupied Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Japan) for [war crimes](48626.md), [crimes against humanity](61099.md), and [crimes against peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimes_against_peace) during the Asian-Pacific theatre of [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

On 27 February 2004, the mastermind of the [Sarin gas attack on the Tokyo subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarin_gas_attack_on_the_Tokyo_subway), [Shoko Asahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoko_Asahara), was found guilty and sentenced to death by hanging. On 25 December 2006, serial killer [Hiroaki Hidaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroaki_Hidaka) and three others were hanged in Japan. Long-drop hanging is the method of carrying out judicial capital punishment on civilians in Japan, as in the cases of [Norio Nagayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norio_Nagayama),[^cite_note-70] [Mamoru Takuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamoru_Takuma),[^cite_note-71] and [Tsutomu Miyazaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsutomu_Miyazaki).[^cite_note-72] In 2018 [Shoko Asahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoko_Asahara) and several of his cult members were hanged for committing the 1995 sarin gas attack.

### Jordan

### Russia

Main article: [Capital punishment in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Russia)

Hanging was commonly practised in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) during the rule of the [Romanov dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanov_dynasty) as an alternative to [impalement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impalement), which was used in the 15th and 16th centuries.

Hanging was abolished in 1868 by [Alexander II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_II_of_Russia) after [serfdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serfdom), but was restored by the time of his death and his assassins were hanged. While those sentenced to death for murder were usually pardoned and sentences commuted to life imprisonment, those guilty of high treason were usually executed. This also included the [Grand Duchy of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Finland) and [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Poland) under the Russian crown. [Taavetti Lukkarinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taavetti_Lukkarinen) became the last Finn to be executed this way. He was hanged for espionage and high treason in 1916.

The hanging was usually performed by short drop in public. The gallows were usually either a stout nearby tree branch, as in the case of Lukkarinen, or a makeshift gallows constructed for the purpose.

After the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution) in 1917, capital punishment was, on paper, abolished, but continued to be used unabated against people perceived to be enemies of the regime. Under the Bolsheviks, most executions were performed by shooting, either by firing squad or by a single firearm. In 1943, hanging was restored primarily for German servicemen and native collaborators for atrocities committed against Soviet POWs and civilians. The last to be hanged were [Andrey Vlasov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Vlasov) and his companions in 1946.

### Singapore

Main article: [Capital punishment in Singapore](3278380.md)

In [Singapore](27318.md), long-drop hanging[^cite_note-\:0-17] is currently used as a mandatory punishment for crimes such as [drug trafficking](218449.md), [murder](20171.md) and some types of [kidnapping](169376.md). It was introduced by the British, when they occupied Singapore and neighbouring Malaysia. It has also been used for punishing those convicted of unauthorised discharging of firearms.[^cite_note-80]

### Sri Lanka

Main article: [Capital punishment in Sri Lanka](4125734.md)

Hanging was abolished in [Sri Lanka](26750.md) in 1956, but in 1959 it was brought back and later halted in 1978. In 1975, the day before the execution of [Maru Sira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maru_Sira), he had been overdosed by the prison guards to prevent him from escaping. On the day of his execution he was unconscious, so when he was brought to the gallows, he was slumped over on the trapdoor with a noose around his neck, and when the executioner pulled the lever, his execution was botched and he strangled.

### Syria

Main article: [Capital punishment in Syria](36765422.md)



[Eli Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Cohen), publicly hanged by Syria on 18 May 1965

Syria has publicly hanged people, such as two individuals in 1952, Israeli spy [Eli Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Cohen) in 1965, and a number of Jews accused of spying for Israel in 1969.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

According to a 19th-century report, members of the [Alawite](436616.md) sect centred on [Lattakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattakia) in Syria had a particular aversion towards being hanged, and the family of the condemned was willing to pay "considerable sums" to ensure its relations were [impaled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impalement), instead of being hanged. The [Islamic State](9087364.md) also used hanging post-mortem, after they [executed alleged spies for the western-backed coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_captives_by_the_Islamic_State) in [Deir ez-Zor](7445749.md) by [cutting their throats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exsanguination) in a [slaughterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slaughterhouse), during the Islamic holiday of [Eid al-Adha](358226.md) in 2016. They also used shooting, beheading, fire and other methods to execute people during their rule.

### United Kingdom

Main article: [Capital punishment in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_the_United_Kingdom)

As a form of [judicial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) execution in England, hanging is thought to date from the [Anglo-Saxon period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Anglo-Saxon_England).[^cite_note-85] Records of the names of British [hangmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executioner) begin with Thomas de Warblynton in the 1360s; complete records extend from the 16th century to the last hangmen, [Robert Leslie Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Leslie_Stewart) and [Harry Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Allen_\(executioner\)), who conducted the last British executions in 1964.

Until 1868 hangings were performed in public. In London, the traditional site was at [Tyburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyburn), a settlement west of the [City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London) on the main road to [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford), which was used on eight hanging days a year, though before 1865, executions had been transferred to the street outside [Newgate Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newgate_Prison), [Old Bailey](229046.md), now the site of the [Central Criminal Court](229046.md).

Three British subjects were hanged after [World War II](32927.md) after being convicted of having helped [Nazi Germany](21212.md) in its war against Britain. [John Amery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Amery), the son of prominent British politician [Leo Amery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Amery), became an [expatriate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate) in the 1930s, moving to France. He became involved in pre-war [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist) politics, remained in what became [Vichy France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France) following France's defeat by Germany in 1940 and eventually went to Germany and later the German puppet state in Italy headed by [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini). Captured by Italian [partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_resistance_movement) at the end of the war and handed over to British authorities, Amery was accused of having made [propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda) broadcasts for the Nazis and of having attempted to recruit British [prisoners of war](25008.md) for a [Waffen SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waffen_SS) regiment later known as the [British Free Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Free_Corps). Amery pleaded guilty to treason charges on 28 November 1945[^cite_note-86] and was hanged at [Wandsworth Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wandsworth_Prison) on 19 December 1945. [William Joyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Joyce), an American-born Irishman who had lived in Britain and possessed a British [passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passport), had been involved in pre-war fascist politics in the UK, fled to Nazi Germany just before the war began to avoid arrest by British authorities and became a naturalised German citizen. He made propaganda broadcasts for the Nazis, becoming infamous under the nickname [Lord Haw-Haw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Haw-Haw). Captured by British forces in May 1945, he was tried for treason later that year. Although Joyce's defence argued that he was by birth American and thus not subject to being tried for treason, the prosecution successfully argued that Joyce's pre-war British passport meant that he was a subject of the British Crown and he was convicted. After his appeals failed, he was hanged at Wandsworth Prison on 3 January 1946.[^cite_note-87] [Theodore Schurch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Schurch), a British soldier captured by the Nazis who then began working for the Italian and German intelligence services as a spy and informer who would be placed among other British prisoners, was arrested in Rome in March 1945 and tried under the [Treachery Act 1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treachery_Act_1940). After his conviction, he was hanged at [HM Prison Pentonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Prison_Pentonville) on 4 January 1946.

The [Homicide Act 1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homicide_Act_1957) created the new offence of [capital murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_murder), punishable by death, with all other murders being punishable by life imprisonment.

In 1965, Parliament passed the [Murder (Abolition of Death Penalty) Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_\(Abolition_of_Death_Penalty\)_Act), temporarily abolishing capital punishment for murder for five years. The Act was renewed in 1969, making the abolition permanent. With the passage of the [Crime and Disorder Act 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_and_Disorder_Act_1998) and the [Human Rights Act 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Act_1998), the death penalty was officially abolished for all crimes in both civilian and military cases. Following its complete abolition, the gallows were removed from [Wandsworth Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wandsworth_\(HM_Prison\)), where they remained in full working order until that year.

The last woman to be hanged was [Ruth Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Ellis) on 13 July 1955, by [Albert Pierrepoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Pierrepoint) who was a prominent hangman in the 20th century in England. The last hangings in Britain took place in 1964, when [Peter Anthony Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Anthony_Allen) was executed at [Walton Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_\(HM_Prison\)) in [Liverpool](18081.md), by [Robert Leslie Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Leslie_Stewart). [Gwynne Owen Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwynne_Owen_Evans) was executed by [Harry Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Allen_\(executioner\)) at [Strangeways Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strangeways_Prison) in [Manchester](20206.md). Both were executed for the [murder of John Alan West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_John_Alan_West).[^cite_note-88]

Hanging was also the method used in many colonies and overseas territories.[^cite_note-89]

### United States

Main article: [Hanging in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanging_in_the_United_States)



The execution of Mary Surratt, Lewis Powell, David Herold, and George Atzerodt, who were all convicted by a [military tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_tribunal) for being involved in the assassination of Abraham Lincoln, 7 July 1865

Hanging was one means by which [Puritans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puritans) of the [Massachusetts Bay Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Bay_Colony) enforced religious and intellectual conformity on the whole community.[^cite_note-94] The best known hanging carried out by the Puritans, [Mary Dyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Dyer), was one of the four executed [Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers) known as the [Boston martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_martyrs).[^cite_note-95]

Lethal injection is the sole method of execution for inmates sentenced to death under federal law in the United States. Of the 27 U.S. states with active death penalty statutes, none authorize hanging as a legal method of execution. Inmates sentenced to death in Delaware for crimes committed before 1986, and all condemned inmates in Washington were eligible to choose hanging as their method of execution prior to those states' abolition of capital punishment in 2016 and 2023, respectively.[^cite_note-96]

When [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) pastor [Denmark Vesey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_Vesey) of the [Emanuel African Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuel_African_Methodist_Episcopal_Church) was suspected of plotting to launch a [slave rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_rebellion) in Charleston, South Carolina in 1822, 35 people, including Vesey, were judged guilty by a city-appointed court and were subsequently hanged, and the church was burned down.[^cite_note-97]

The [Dakota War of 1862](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_War_of_1862), also known as the Dakota uprising, led to the largest mass execution in the United States when 38 Sioux Indians, who were facing starvation and displacement, attacked white settlers, for which they were sentenced to death via hanging in [Mankato, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mankato,_Minnesota) in December 1862.[^cite_note-Dakota-98] Originally, 303 had been sentenced to hang, but the convictions were reviewed by President [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) and the sentences of all but 38 were commuted.[^cite_note-99] In 2019, Governor [Tim Walz](2216593.md) issued an historic apology to the Dakota people for the mass hanging and the "trauma inflicted on [Native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) people at the hands of state government".[^cite_note-Dakota-98]

A total of 40 suspected [Unionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unionists_\(American\)) were hanged in [Gainesville, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gainesville,_Texas), in October 1862.[^cite_note-100] On 7 July 1865, four people involved in the [assassination of President Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Abraham_Lincoln)—[Mary Surratt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Surratt), [Lewis Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Powell_\(assassin\)), [David Herold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Herold), and [George Atzerodt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Atzerodt)—were hanged at [Fort McNair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_McNair) in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md)



Men pose for a photograph of the [1920 Duluth, Minnesota lynchings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Duluth_lynchings). Two of the black victims are still hanging while the third is on the ground.

While relatively uncommon, [hanging in chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbeting) has also been practiced (mainly during the colonial era), the first being a slave after the [New York Slave Revolt of 1712](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Slave_Revolt_of_1712). The last hanging in chains was in 1913, of John Marshall in [West Virginia](32905.md) for murder.[^cite_note-101] The last public hanging in the United States (not including [lynching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynching_in_the_United_States), one of the last of which was [Michael Donald in 1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynching_of_Michael_Donald)) took place on 14 August 1936, in [Owensboro, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owensboro,_Kentucky). [Rainey Bethea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainey_Bethea) was executed for the rape and murder of 70-year-old Lischa Edwards. The execution was presided over by the first female [sheriff](89516.md) in Kentucky, [Florence Shoemaker Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Shoemaker_Thompson).[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

In California, [Clinton Duffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Duffy), who served as warden of [San Quentin State Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Quentin_State_Prison) between 1940 and 1952, presided over ninety executions.[^cite_note-104] He began to oppose the death penalty, and after his retirement, wrote a memoir entitled *Eighty-Eight Men and Two Women* in support of the movement to abolish the death penalty. The book documents several hangings gone wrong and describes how they led his predecessor, Warden [James B. Holohan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_B._Holohan), to persuade the California Legislature to replace hanging with the [gas chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_chamber) in 1937.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

Various methods of capital punishment have been replaced by [lethal injection](62745.md) in most states and the federal government. Many states that offered hanging as an option have since eliminated the method. Condemned murderer [Victor Feguer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Feguer) became the last inmate to be executed by hanging in the state of [Iowa](26810748.md) on 15 March 1963. Hanging was the preferred method of execution for capital murder cases in Iowa until 1965, when the death penalty was abolished and replaced with [life imprisonment](148681.md) without [parole](342305.md). [Barton Kay Kirkham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton_Kay_Kirkham) was the last person to be hanged in Utah, preferring it over [execution by firing squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Execution_by_firing_squad). Laws in [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware) were changed in 1986 to specify lethal injection, except for those convicted before 1986 (who were still allowed to choose hanging). If a choice was not made, or the convict refused to choose injection, then hanging would become the default method. This was the case in the 1996 execution of [Billy Bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Bailey), the most recent hanging in American history; since then, no Delaware prisoner fit the category, and the state's gallows were later dismantled.

#### Upright jerker

Main article: [Upright jerker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upright_jerker)

The upright jerker is a method of hanging that originated in the United States in the late 19th century. The person to be hanged is jerked into the air by weights and pulleys. It proved to be ineffective at breaking the neck of the condemned, and death by asphyxiation often occurred instead. In the United States, use of the method ceased in the late 1930s.

## Inverted hanging



Woodcut by [Johann Stumpf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Stumpf_\(writer\)), who witnessed this type of execution in 1553

A completely different principle of hanging is to hang the convicted person from their legs, rather than from their neck, either as a form of torture, or as an execution method. In late medieval Germany, this came to be primarily associated with Jews accused of being thieves, called the *Judenstrafe* (lit. 'Jewish punishment'). The jurist Ulrich Tengler, in his *Layenspiegel* from 1509, describes the procedure as follows, in the section "Von Juden straff" ("On the punishment of Jews"):[^cite_note-107]

> To drag the Jew to the ordinary execution place between two angry or biting dogs. After dragging, to hang him from his feet by rope or chain at a designated gallows between the dogs, so that he is directed from life to death[^cite_note-108]

[Guido Kisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Kisch?action=edit\&redlink=1) showed that originally, this type of inverted hanging between two dogs was not a punishment specifically for Jews. Esther Cohen writes:[^cite_note-109]

> The inverted hanging with the accompaniment of two dogs, originally reserved for traitors, was identified from the fourteenth century as the "Jewish execution", being practised in the later Middle Ages in both northern and Mediterranean Europe. The Jewish execution in Germany has been thoroughly studied by G. Kisch, who has argued convincingly that neither the inverted hanging nor the stringing up of dogs or wolves beside the victim were particularly Jewish punishments during the High Middle Ages. They first appeared as Jewish punishments in Germany only towards the end of the thirteenth century, never being recognized as exclusively Jewish penalties.
>
> &#x20;In France the inverted, animal-associated hanging came to be connected with Jews by the later Middle Ages. The inverted hanging of Jews is specifically mentioned in the old customs of Burgundy in the context of animal hanging. The custom, dogs and all, was still in force in Paris shortly before the final expulsion of the Jews in 1394.

In Spain 1449, during a mob attack against the [Marranos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marranos) (Jews nominally converted to Christianity), the Jews resisted, but lost and several of them were hanged up by the feet.[^cite_note-110] The first attested German case for a Jew being hanged by the feet is from 1296, in present-day [Soultzmatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soultzmatt).[^cite_note-111] Some other historical examples of this type of hanging within the German context are one Jew in [Hennegau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Hainaut) 1326, two Jews hanged in [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main) in 1444,[^cite_note-Deutsches_bürgerthum_im_mittelalter-112] one in [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_\(region\)) in 1462,[^cite_note-113] one in [Dortmund](149349.md) in 1486,[^cite_note-114] one in [Hanau](442088.md) in 1499,[^cite_note-Deutsches_bürgerthum_im_mittelalter-112] one in [Breslau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breslau) in 1505,[^cite_note-115] one in [Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrttemberg) in 1553,[^cite_note-116] one in [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen,_Lower_Saxony) in 1588,[^cite_note-Deutsches_bürgerthum_im_mittelalter-112] one in [Öttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96ttingen) in 1611,[^cite_note-117] one in Frankfurt in 1615 and again in 1661,[^cite_note-Deutsches_bürgerthum_im_mittelalter-112] and one condemned to this punishment in [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) in 1637.[^cite_note-118]

The details of the cases vary widely: In the 1444 Frankfurt cases and the 1499 Hanau case, the dogs were dead prior to being hanged, and in the late 1615 and 1661 cases in Frankfurt, the Jews (and dogs) were merely kept in this manner for half an hour, before being [garroted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrote) from below. In the 1588 Bergen case, all three victims were left hanging till they were dead, ranging from 6 to 8 days after being hanged. In the Dortmund 1486 case, the dogs bit the Jew to death while hanging. In the 1611 Öttingen case, the Jew "Jacob the Tall" thought to blow up the Deutsche Ordenhaus with gunpowder after having burgled it. He was strung up between two dogs, and a large fire was made close by, and he expired after half an hour under this torture. In the 1553 Württemberg case, the Jew chose to convert to Christianity after hanging like this for 24 hours; he was then given the mercy to be hanged in the usual manner, from the neck, and without the dogs beside him. In the 1462 Halle case, the Jew Abraham also converted after 24 hours hanging upside down, and a priest ascended a ladder to baptise him. For two more days, Abraham was left hanging, while the priest argued with the city council that a true Christian should not be punished in this way. On the third day, Abraham was granted a reprieve, taken down, but died 20 days later in the local hospital having meanwhile suffered in extreme pain. In the 1637 case, where the Jew had murdered a Christian jeweller, the appeal to the [empress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Anna_of_Spain) was successful, and out of mercy, the Jew was condemned to be merely pinched with glowing pincers, have hot lead dripped into his wounds, and then be [broken alive on the wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_wheel).

Some of the reported cases may be myths, or wandering stories. The 1326 Hennegau case, for example, deviates from the others in that the Jew was not a thief, but was suspected (though he was a convert to Christianity) of having struck a [fresco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresco) of the [Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Mary), so that [blood seeped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weeping_statue) down the wall from the painting. Even under all degrees of judicial torture, the Jew denied performing this sacrilegious act, and was therefore exonerated. Then a brawny smith demanded from him a [trial by combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_by_combat), claiming he dreamt the Virgin herself had urged him to do so. The court accepted the smith's challenge, and he easily won the combat against the Jew, who was duly hanged up by the feet between two dogs. To add to the injury, one let him be slowly roasted as well as hanged.[^cite_note-119] This is a very similar story to one told in France, in which a young Jew threw a lance at the head of a statue of the Virgin, so that blood spurted out of it. There was inadequate evidence for a normal trial, but a frail old man asked for trial by combat, and bested the young Jew. The Jew confessed his crime, and was hanged by his feet between two mastiffs.[^cite_note-120]

The features of the earliest attested case, that of a Jewish thief hanged by the feet in Soultzmatt in 1296 are also rather divergent from the rest. The Jew managed somehow, after he had been left to die, to twitch his body in such a manner that he could hoist himself up on the gallows and free himself. At that time, his feet were so damaged that he was unable to escape, and when he was discovered 8 days after he had been hanged, he was strangled to death by the townspeople.[^cite_note-121]

As late as in 1699 in [Celle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celle), the courts were sufficiently horrified at how the Jewish leader of a robber gang (condemned to be hanged in the normal manner) declared [blasphemies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blasphemy) against Christianity, that they made a ruling on the *post mortem* treatment of Jonas Meyer. After three days, his corpse was cut down, his tongue cut out, and his body was hanged up again, but this time from its feet.[^cite_note-122]

### Punishment for traitors

Guido Kisch writes that the first instance he knows where a person in Germany was hanged up by his feet between two dogs until he died occurred about 1048, some 250 years earlier than the first attested Jewish case. This was a knight called Arnold, who had murdered his lord; the story is contained in [Adam of Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_of_Bremen)'s *History of the Archbishops of Hamburg-Bremen*.[^cite_note-123] Another example of a non-Jew who suffered this punishment as a torture, in 1196 [Richard, Count of Acerra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard,_Count_of_Acerra), was one of those executed by [Henry VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) in the suppression of the rebelling Sicilians:[^cite_note-124]

> He \[Henry VI] held a general court in Capua, at which he ordered that the count first be drawn behind a horse through the squares of Capua, and then hanged alive head downwards. The latter was still alive after two days when a certain German jester called Leather-Bag \[Follis], hoping to please the emperor, tied a large stone to his neck and shamefully put him to death

A couple of centuries earlier, in France in 991, a certain viscount Walter nominally owing his allegiance to the French King [Hugh Capet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Capet) chose, on instigation of his wife, to join the rebellion under [Odo I, Count of Blois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odo_I,_Count_of_Blois). When Odo found out he had to abandon [Melun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melun) after all, Walter was duly hanged before the gates, whereas his wife, the fomentor of treason, was hanged by her feet, causing much merriment and jeers from Hugh's soldiers as her clothes fell downwards revealing her naked body, although it is not wholly clear if she died in that manner.[^cite_note-125]

### Elizabethan maritime law

During Queen [Elizabeth I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_I)'s reign, the following was written concerning those who stole a ship from the [Royal Navy](26061.md):[^cite_note-126]

> If anye one practysed to steale awaye anye of her Majesty's shippes, the captaine was to cause him to be hanged by the heels until his braines were beaten out against the shippe's sides, and then to be cutt down and lett fall intoe the sea.&#x20;

Translation into modern English: *If anyone practised to steal away any of Her Majesty's ships, the captain was to cause him \[the thief] to be hanged by the heels until his brains were beaten out against the ship's sides, and then to be cut down and let fall into the sea.*

## Hanging by the ribs



*A Negro Hung Alive by the Ribs to a Gallows* by [William Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Blake). Originally published in Stedman's *Narrative*.

In 1713, [Juraj Jánošík](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juraj_J%C3%A1no%C5%A1%C3%ADk), a semi-legendary Slovak outlaw and [folk hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_hero), was sentenced to be hanged from his left rib. He was left to slowly die.[^cite_note-127]

The German physician Gottlob Schober (1670–1739),[^cite_note-128] who worked in Russia from 1712, noted that a person could hang from the ribs for about three days prior to dying, with his primary pain being that of extreme thirst. He thought this degree of insensitivity was something peculiar to the Russian mentality.[^cite_note-129]

The Dutch in [Suriname](26828.md) were also in the habit of hanging a slave from the ribs, a custom amongst the African tribes from whom they were originally purchased. [John Gabriel Stedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gabriel_Stedman) stayed in South America from 1772 to 1777 and described the method as told by a witness:[^cite_note-130]

> "Not long ago," (continued he) "I saw a black man suspended alive from a gallows by the ribs, between which, with a knife, was first made an incision, and then clinched an iron hook with a chain: in this manner he kept alive three days, hanging with his head and feet downwards, and catching with his tongue the drops of water (it being in the rainy season) that were flowing down his bloated breast. Notwithstanding all this, he never complained, and even upbraided a negro for crying while he was flogged below the gallows, by calling out to him: 'You man?—*Da boy fasy?* Are you a man? you behave like a boy.' Shortly after which he was knocked on the head by the commiserating sentry, who stood over him, with the butt end of his musket."

[William Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Blake) was specially commissioned to make illustrations to Stedman's narrative.[^cite_note-131]
