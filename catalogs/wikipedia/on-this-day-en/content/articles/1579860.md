The **Communist Party of Chile** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Partido Comunista de Chile*, **PCCh**) is a [Marxist-Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist-Leninist) party in [Chile](5489.md). It was founded in 1912 as the **Socialist Workers' Party** (*Partido Obrero Socialista*) and adopted its current name in 1922. The party established a youth wing, the [Communist Youth of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Youth_of_Chile) (*Juventudes Comunistas de Chile*, JJ.CC), in 1932.

## History

[Luis Corvalán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Corval%C3%A1n), Secretary-General of the PCCh (1958–1990)

The PCCh was founded on 4 June 1912 by [Luis Emilio Recabarren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Emilio_Recabarren), after he left the [Democrat Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrat_Party_\(Chile\)).[^cite_note-9] The party was initially known as the Socialist Workers' Party, before adopting its current name on 2 January 1922.

It achieved congressional representation shortly thereafter and played a leading role in the development of the Chilean labor movement.&#x20;

The party was founded as Chile began to [industrialize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialize). In the 1920s, the [nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrate) industry was booming, and many of its workers were among the first communists. The movement then gained momentum within the coal, textile, and port unions.[^cite_note-\:0-10]

Closely tied to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and the [Third International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_International), the PCCh participated in the [Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_\(Chile\)) (*Frente Popular*) government of 1938, growing rapidly among the unionized working class in the 1940s. It then participated to the Popular Front's successor, the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(Chile\)).

Concern over the PCCh's success at building a strong electoral base, combined with the onset of the [Cold War](325329.md), led to its being outlawed in 1948 by a [Radical government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Party_\(Chile\)), a status it had to endure for almost a decade until 1958 when it was again legalized. By the 1960s, the party had become a veritable political subculture, with its own symbols and organizations and the support of prominent artists and intellectuals such as [Pablo Neruda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Neruda), the [Nobel Prize](21201.md)-winning poet, and [Violeta Parra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violeta_Parra), the songwriter and folk artist.[^cite_note-11] At the time, the [U.S. State Department](31975.md) estimated the party membership to be approximately 27,500.[^cite_note-12]

It later came to power along with the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Chile) in the *[Unidad Popular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Unity_\(Chile\))* ("Popular Unity") coalition in 1970. Within the broad Unidad Popular alliance, the communists sided with Allende, a relative moderate from the Socialist Party, and other more moderate forces of that coalition, supporting more gradual reforms and urging to find a compromise with the Christian Democrats. This line was opposed by more radically leftist factions of the Socialist Party and smaller far-left groups. The party was outlawed after the 1973 [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_coup_of_1973) that deposed [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_Chile) [Salvador Allende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Allende). Much of the Communist leadership went underground, and for a while the party's moderation continued even after the coup had taken place. Also, it has been argued by [Mark Ensalaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ensalaco?action=edit\&redlink=1) that crushing the Communist Party was not a top priority for the military junta.[^cite_note-Chile_Under_Pinochet,_pg._7-13] In its first statement after the coup, the party leadership still argued that the coup could succeed because the Unidad Popular was too isolated, due to actions of the 'far-left'. Around 1977, the party changed direction.[^cite_note-Chile_Under_Pinochet,_pg._7-13] The Communist Party set up a [guerrilla](12720.md) organization, the [Manuel Rodríguez Patriotic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Patriotic_Front). With the restoration of democracy and the election of a new president in 1990, the Communist Party of Chile was legalized again.

As part of the Popular Unity coalition the PCCh advocated a broad alliance; however, it swung sharply to the left after the 1973 coup, regretting the failure to issue arms to the working class and pursuing an armed struggle against Pinochet's regime. Since the restoration of democracy it has acted independently of its previous partners. Between 1983 and 1987 it was a member of the [People's Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Democratic_Movement_\(Chile\)).

Under Pinochet's regime, around 500 of its members were murdered.[^cite_note-\:0-10]

In the 1999/2000 presidential elections, the party announced [Gladys Marín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gladys_Mar%C3%ADn) as their presidential candidate, becoming Marin as [the first female candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_female_Chilean_presidential_candidates) to contest the first round of a presidential election in Chile, alongside [Sara Larraín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Larra%C3%ADn). Marín won 3.2% of the vote in the first round.

At the [2005 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Chilean_parliamentary_election), 11 December 2005, the party won 5.1% of the popular vote, but as a result of Chile's [binomial electoral](23298.md) rules, no seats. The small but significant support of the PCCh is believed to have aided in the electoral victories of former socialist president [Ricardo Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Lagos) in the 2000 elections, and in the more recent victory of Chile's first female president, the socialist [Michelle Bachelet](986814.md) in January 2006, both of whom won in competitive second round runoffs.

From 2013 to 2018, the PCCh was a member of [New Majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Majority_\(Chile\)) ([Spanish](26825.md): *Nueva Mayoría*), a leftist coalition led by [Michelle Bachelet](986814.md).

## Political positioning

The Communist Party of Chile is considered a moderate and reformist party compared to some other communist parties. It has never sought to overthrow the Chilean democratic system, nor has it supported insurrectionary movements, nor has it been entirely subordinate to the Soviet Union. It embodies "Chilean-style communism: institutional, orderly, and open to dialogue."[^cite_note-\:0-10]

## Controversies

The PCCh faced criticism from several parties in Chile after congratulating Venezuelan president [Nicolás Maduro](7161476.md) on [his party's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Socialist_Party_of_Venezuela) victory in the [2020 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Venezuelan_parliamentary_election). Prominent members of the [Party for Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_for_Democracy_\(Chile\)), [Radical Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Party_of_Chile_\(2018\)), and [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Chile) questioned the PCCh's praise of the election as "flawless", echoing criticisms from opposition parties in Venezuela that the election was neither free nor fair.[^cite_note-14] However, some of its leaders have also publicly condemned the human rights abuses that have taken place in Venezuela under the government of Nicolás Maduro.[^cite_note-15]

### Presidential election

Keys

- RP = supported a candidate from the [Radical Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Party_\(Chile\))

- SP = supported a candidate from the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(Chile\))

- PU–SP = member of the [Popular Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Unity_\(Chile\)) coalition, supported the candidate from the Socialist Party

- PDC = supported a candidate from the [Christian Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Party_\(Chile\))

- Ind = supported an [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_\(politics\)) candidate

- HP = supported a candidate from the [Humanist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanist_Party_\(Chile\))

- NM–SP = member of the [New Majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Majority_\(Chile\)) coalition, supported the candidate from the Socialist Party

- NM–Ind = member of the [New Majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Majority_\(Chile\)) coalition, supported an independent candidate

- AD-SC = member of the [Apruebo Dignidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apruebo_Dignidad) coalition, supported the candidate from [Social Convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Convergence)
