**Volkel Air Base** ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Vliegbasis Volkel*) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **UDE**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **EHVK**) is a [military](843566.md) [airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_airbase) used by the [Royal Netherlands Air and Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Air_and_Space_Force) (RNLASF) - Dutch: *Koninklijke Luchtmacht (KLu)*, located near the village of [Volkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkel), [Netherlands](21148.md). Located in the north-east corner of the province of [North Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Brabant), the air base is home to two [F-35A](11812.md) squadrons, No. 312 and No. 313 and a maintenance, logistical, base squadron for the RNLASF.&#x20;

Volkel also houses the 703rd Munitions Support Squadron, part of the [52nd Fighter Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/52nd_Fighter_Wing) from the [United States Air Force](32090.md). After more than 50 years, former Prime Minister [Ruud Lubbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruud_Lubbers) in 2013 officially confirmed the presence of 22 [B61 nuclear bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B61_nuclear_bomb) at Volkel. In 2021 training with the latest B-61-12 modification began and in December 2023, 14 F-16 could be seen performing an [elephant walk (aeronautics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant_walk_\(aeronautics\)) before take off.

## Description

Volkel Air Base is located near the village of [Volkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkel). It is one of several [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) airfields in the Netherlands, and one of the three major operational bases of the [Royal Netherlands Air and Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Air_and_Space_Force) (RNLASF), the other two being [Leeuwarden Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeuwarden_Air_Base) and [Gilze-Rijen Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilze-Rijen_Air_Base). Together with these, it hosts the public viewing days of the RNLASF, held annually at one of these three airfields, having both an [airshow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airshow) and static display of various [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aircraft) and civilian [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft).

The airport has two parallel runways, both in the 06/24 direction, and both being just over 3,020 metres (9,900 ft) long. 06L/24R is 45 m (148 ft) wide, and is capable of handling larger aircraft. It is also equipped with an [instrument landing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_landing_system) (ILS). 06R/24L is narrower at only 23 m (75 ft) wide.

Besides military use, a [trauma helicopter](8286923.md) operated by [ANWB Medical Air Assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ANWB) on behalf of the [Radboud University Nijmegen Medical Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radboud_University_Nijmegen_Medical_Centre) is based here. The [Dienst Luchtvaart Politie](21357448.md) also makes use of Volkel Air Base.

## History



*Fliegerhorst Volkel* as seen from above in 1944 after having been bombed by allied forces

After the [occupation of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_the_Netherlands) by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) in 1940, the [Luftwaffe](17885.md) constructed a diversion airfield for night-fighter aircraft called *Nachtlandeplatz Volkel*. In 1943, the airfield was expanded into an operational Luftwaffe base, and renamed *Fliegerhorst Volkel*. It was home to [III./NJG 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nachtjagdgeschwader_2) operating [Junkers Ju 88](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Ju_88) night fighters, and [II & III./JG 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagdgeschwader_3) operating the [Messerschmitt Bf 109G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messerschmitt_Bf_109). The last German aircraft based at Volkel were jet-engined [Me 262](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messerschmitt_Me_262) fighters and [Ar 234](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arado_Ar_234) reconnaissance bombers.[^cite_note-2] To defend the airfield against aerial attacks, the Germans installed [flak](146640.md) guns, but it was still bombed extensively. Attacks in 1944 in support of [Operation Market Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Market_Garden) caused such extensive damage to the airfield that it could no longer be used by the Luftwaffe.

When the south of the Netherlands was liberated later that year, the British [Royal Air Force](25679.md) (RAF) took control of the airfield. Though the Germans had destroyed most of the remaining airport facilities, the RAF continued to use the airport for the remainder of the war, operating [Hawker Typhoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Typhoon) and [Hawker Tempest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Tempest) aircraft from Volkel in support of the allied advance into Germany. [French](5843419.md) ace [Pierre Clostermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Clostermann), at the time a flight commander in [No. 122 Wing RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._122_Wing_RAF), provides a detailed description of operations from Volkel in early 1945 in his book *The Big Show*.

The [Dutch Naval Aviation Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Naval_Aviation_Service) started flying from Volkel in 1949 for training purposes. In 1950, the Royal Netherlands Air Force took control of the airfield, restoring it to an operational fighter airfield. [Gloster Meteor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloster_Meteor) aircraft were the first [jet aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_aircraft) to be based at Volkel for the RNLAF. Later came the [Lockheed T-33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_T-33),[^cite_note-3] [Republic F-84 Thunderjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-84_Thunderjet) and [Thunderstreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-84F_Thunderstreak), which were eventually replaced by the [Lockheed F-104 Starfighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_F-104_Starfighter), the first [supersonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic) aircraft of the RNLAF. In the 1970s, airport facilities were improved, and 32 protective [Hardened Aircraft Shelters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardened_Aircraft_Shelter) (HAS) were constructed for the aircraft. Between 1982 and 1984, the Starfighters were slowly replaced by the F-16 Fighting Falcons that are situated at Volkel, which were manufactured under licence by [Fokker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker).[^cite_note-4] The F-16 jets have been replaced by the [Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II](11812.md).

In September 2012, a third squadron No 311 Sqn, was officially disbanded.[^cite_note-5]

## Nuclear weapons



Demonstration of a [B61 nuclear bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B61_nuclear_bomb) disarming procedure on a “dummy” in an underground Weapons Security and Storage System (WS3) vault at Volkel Air Base

As of 2023, Volkel is one of six active air bases in five European countries with [B61 nuclear bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B61_nuclear_bomb) in underground [WS3 Weapon Storage and Security System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapon_Storage_Security_System_\(WS3\)) inside aircraft shelters; [Ramstein Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramstein_Air_Base) has a vault but no nuclear weapons present and [RAF Lakenheath](856907.md)'s bunkers were being modernized.[^cite_note-6]

It is believed that since the early 1960s, [USAF](32090.md) nuclear weapons have been stored at Volkel Air Base, to be used by the host nation's aircraft.[^cite_note-7] Formerly, storage took place in a [weapon storage area](7034672.md) on the north side of the base, and in a heavily defended [quick reaction alert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quick_reaction_alert) (QRA) area; however, since 1991, eleven Weapons Storage and Security Systems (WS3) vaults have been operational in the floors of the aircraft shelters.[^cite_note-bell-8] The [USAF](32090.md) [703rd Munitions Support Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/703rd_Munitions_Support_Squadron?action=edit\&redlink=1) (703rd MUNSS) has been in charge of maintaining and securing the weapons.[^cite_note-fas2008-9][^cite_note-10] As of 2005, the [Dutch Ministry of Defence](9473113.md) had not officially acknowledged or denied the presence of nuclear weapons at Volkel.[^cite_note-11]

As of 2008, 22 [B61 nuclear bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B61_nuclear_bomb) were stored at Volkel, to be used by the Dutch 311 and 312 F-16 [squadrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squadron_\(aviation\)) at the base.[^cite_note-12] The F-16s based at Volkel can at times be seen with BDU-38 dummy bombs, which are used to simulate the B61.[^cite_note-13] In a book published by former air force pilot Steve Netto it is revealed that some fifty [B28 nuclear bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B28_nuclear_bomb) were in storage there around the time of the [Cuban Missile Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Missile_Crisis), which if needed were to be deployed by aircraft of the Royal Netherlands Air Force.[^cite_note-14] In a document leaked as a part of the 2010 [United States diplomatic cables leak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_diplomatic_cables_leak) the presence of nuclear weapons in the Netherlands was confirmed, though no specific location was given.[^cite_note-15] On 10 June 2013, former Prime Minister [Ruud Lubbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruud_Lubbers) confirmed the existence of 22 nuclear weapons at the airfield.[^cite_note-bbc-16] In a 2019 NATO draft report, Volkel was mentioned as one of six locations where, altogether, approximately 150 American B61 bombs are stored.[^cite_note-17]

According to a May 2021 article from [Bellingcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellingcat), many sensitive security details about the nuclear arsenal at Volkel, such as which vaults had nuclear weapons in them, were inadvertently exposed when journalists discovered that the soldiers tasked with overseeing these weapons had been using publicly available [flashcard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashcard) websites to assist them in learning these details.[^cite_note-bell-8]

In 2021, training with the new U.S. B61-12 bomb carried by the F-16 fighter began, per a video from Sandia National Laboratories. In December 2023, fourteen F-16s could be seen performing an [elephant walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant_walk_\(aeronautics\)) before take off.[^cite_note-fas2023-18]
