**Port Sudan** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): بور سودان, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Būr Sūdān*, [Beja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beja_language): *Bar'uut*) is a major port city on the [Red Sea](26191.md) in eastern [Sudan](27421.md), and the capital of [Red Sea State](767604.md). Port Sudan is Sudan's main seaport and the source of 90% of the country's international trade.[^cite_note-\:1-2] The population of Port Sudan was estimated in the 2008 Census of Sudan to be 394,561 people.[^cite_note-City_population-3]

Port Sudan has historically been a centre for commercial activity, particularly in the shipping industry.[^cite_note-\:2-4]

From April 2023 to January 2026, the Sudanese government largely operated from Port Sudan as a result of [intense fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khartoum_\(2023\)) in the capital city [Khartoum](17320.md) in the [ongoing civil war](73556699.md),[^cite_note-5] leading to it being described as a *de facto* capital of the country. Port Sudan also emerged as a refuge for [internally displaced persons](427408.md) in Sudan.

## History

### Founding and early history

Port Sudan was built between 1905 and 1909 by the administration of [Anglo-Egyptian Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_Sudan) to replace [Suakin](1124174.md)—the historic, coral-choked Arab port.[^cite_note-\:0-6] An [oil pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipeline_transport) was built between the port and [Khartoum](17320.md) in 1977.

### Early 21st century

In 2009, Israel allegedly used naval commandos to attack Iranian arms ships at Port Sudan as part of [Operation Birds of Prey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Sudan_airstrikes).[^cite_note-7] In 2020, Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) announced that the [Russian Navy](383258.md) would begin construction on a base with capacity for 300 personnel and four warships in Port Sudan, with an agreement to use the facility as a Russian naval base in Sudan for at least 25 years.[^cite_note-8] The plan was ultimately suspended, though Sudanese leadership indicated in 2022 that it was possible that the construction could again move forward in the future.[^cite_note-9]

In 2016, it was reported that residents of Port Sudan faced [water scarcity](15380061.md).[^cite_note-10] Following the [October–November 2021 Sudanese coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October%E2%80%93November_2021_Sudanese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), the [Beja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beja_people) tribal council initiated a weeklong blockade of the city's ports. Following negotiations with military officials, the blockade was lifted after a week.[^cite_note-11]

### Sudanese civil war (2023–present)

During the [Sudanese civil war (2023–present)](73556699.md), Port Sudan has emerged as a leading destination and refuge for [internally displaced persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internally_displaced_persons) fleeing war in other parts of the country.[^cite_note-\:2-4][^cite_note-12] Internally-displaced refugees in the city reportedly face [extreme heat](293270.md) and shortages of food and water.[^cite_note-13]

By late October 2023, [Reuters](18998750.md) reported that the [Rapid Support Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_Support_Forces_\(Sudan\)) (RSF) controlled most of [Khartoum](17320.md), causing the government led by Abdul Fattah el-Burhan to have largely relocated to Port Sudan.[^cite_note-14] General Abdul Fattah al-Burhan, leader of the Sudanese Armed Forces, "has threatened to establish a cabinet" in Port Sudan, "with the intention of creating an alternative or second capital." [Hemedti](60669608.md), leader of the RSF, in turn, warned that this would lead to him declaring his own rival government based in Khartoum or another city he controlled. Analysts have raised concerns of a lasting split similar to that of [Libya](17633.md) during its civil wars.[^cite_note-15] Port Sudan has been described as becoming a "*[de facto](43594.md)*" capital of Sudan.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

[Iran](14653.md) and Russia have been trying to negotiate with Sudan to build a naval base in the city.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

On 4 May 2025, the RSF [launched drone attacks](79888999.md) on Port Sudan for the first time since the beginning of the war, targeting the Osman Digna Air Base, "a goods warehouse and some civilian facilities" and causing "limited damage".[^cite_note-20] A day later, another RSF drone attack occurred targeting fuel depots, leading to a fire. Some [Arab nations](52625.md) condemned the actions.[^cite_note-21]

## Economy



Fish restaurant in Port Sudan

The city has an oil refinery and handles 90% of the country's international trade.[^cite_note-\:0-6][^cite_note-\:1-2] Major exports include [oilseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oilseed), [senna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senna_\(plant\)), and hides and skins. Imports include construction materials, heavy machinery, and vehicles.

## Transport

The city has a modern [container port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Container_port) to handle imports and exports.[^cite_note-\:0-6] The port is part of the [21st Century Maritime Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Century_Maritime_Silk_Road) that runs from the Chinese coast via the [Suez Canal](29323.md) to the Mediterranean, there to the Upper Adriatic region of [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) with its rail connections to [Central](5188.md) and [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

The main airport is [Port Sudan New International Airport](9265195.md). There is now a tarred road linking Port Sudan to Khartoum via Atbara. Port Sudan also has a 1,067 mm (3 ft 6 in) gauge [rail link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Sudan) with [Khartoum](17320.md). There is also an international ferry from [Jeddah](277718.md).

In 2023 a new seaport was proposed about 200 kilometres or 120 miles north of Port Sudan at [Abu Amama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Amama).[^cite_note-26] A 450-kilometre or 280-mile road to the farming hub at [Abu Hamad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Hamad) would also be provided. This new seaport would be opposite [Jeddah](277718.md) and thus shorten the ferry trip.

## Education



[Red Sea University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea_University) campus

The city is home to the [Red Sea University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea_University), established in 1994.[^cite_note-27]

## Places of worship



Mosque at Port Sudan

[Places of worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Places_of_worship) are predominantly [Muslim](6037917.md) mosques,[^cite_note-28] but there are also [Christian](5211.md) churches and temples including the [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Khartoum) ([Catholic Church](606848.md)), [Sudan Interior Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_Interior_Church) ([Baptist World Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist_World_Alliance)), and [Presbyterian Church in Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_in_Sudan) ([World Communion of Reformed Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Communion_of_Reformed_Churches)) and [Coptic Orthodox Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copts_in_Sudan).

## Climate

Port Sudan has a [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): BWh) with extremely hot summers and moderately hot winters, requiring the acquisition of fresh water from [Wadi Arba'at](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Arba'at?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the [Red Sea Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea_Hills) and from salt-evaporating pans. Temperatures can easily exceed 30 °C (86 °F) in winter and 45 °C (113 °F) in summer. Over 90% of the annual rainfall falls between October and January, mostly in November, with the wettest month on record being November 1947 with 182 millimetres (7.2 in), whilst the wettest year was from July 1923 to June 1924 with 231 millimetres (9.1 in). Average annual rainfall is 76 millimetres (3.0 in), and no rainfall occurred between January 1983 and June 1984.[^cite_note-29] The mean temperature year round (the average of all daily highs and nighttime lows) is 28.4 °C (83.1 °F).

## Sport

[Hay Al-Arab SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hay_Al-Arab_SC) founded in 1928, and [Hilal Alsahil SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilal_Alsahil_SC) founded in 1937, both play at the [Port Sudan Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Sudan_Stadium) in the [football](10568.md) [Sudan Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_Premier_League). The city's third team is [Al-Merreikh Al-Thagher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Merreikh_Al-Thagher).
