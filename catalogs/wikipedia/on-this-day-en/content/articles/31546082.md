# Coastal flooding



Coastal flooding during [Hurricane Lili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Lili) in 2002 on [Louisiana Highway 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Highway_1) (United States)

**Coastal flooding** occurs when dry and low-lying land is submerged ([flooded](50482.md)) by [seawater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawater).[^cite_note-ramsay-1] The range of a coastal [flooding](50482.md) is a result of the elevation of floodwater that penetrates the inland which is controlled by the [topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography) of the coastal land exposed to flooding.[^cite_note-ramsay-1][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDoornkamp1998-2] The seawater can flood the land via several different paths: direct flooding, overtopping or breaching of a barrier.[^cite_note-NatureComms_20210618-3] Coastal flooding is largely a natural event. Due to the [effects of climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change) (e.g. [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise) and an increase in [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather) events) and an increase in the population living in coastal areas, the damage caused by coastal flood events has intensified and more people are being affected.[^cite_note-4]

[Coastal areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_plain) are sometimes flooded by unusually high tides, such as [spring tides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_tide), especially when compounded by high winds and [storm surges](449744.md). This was the cause of the [North Sea flood of 1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_flood_of_1953) which flooded large swathes of the [Netherlands](21148.md) and the East coast of [England](9316.md).

When humans modify the coastal environment this can make coastal flooding worse.[^cite_note-ramsay-1][^cite_note-nicholls-5][^cite_note-griffis-6][^cite_note-dawson-7] Extraction of water from groundwater reservoirs in the coastal zone can instigate [subsidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidence) of the land, thus increasing the risk of flooding.[^cite_note-nicholls-5] Engineered protection structures along the coast, such as [sea walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_walls), alter the natural processes of the beach. This can lead to [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) on adjacent stretches of the coast which also increases the risk of flooding.[^cite_note-ramsay-1][^cite_note-dawson-7][^cite_note-pope-8]

Reduction and control of coastal flooding is carried out using structural methods to hold back or redirect flood waters. Non-structural methods include coastal management, behavioral and institutional response to adapt to the processes. Natural defenses include physical features like [gravel bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravel_bars) and [sand dune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_dune) systems, but also [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystems) such as [salt marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_marshes), [seagrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seagrass) and [mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) forests which have a buffering function. [Mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove), [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetlands) and [seagrass meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seagrass_meadow) are often considered to provide significant protection against storm waves, tsunamis, and shoreline [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) through their ability to attenuate wave energy.[^cite_note-griffis-6][^cite_note-alongi-9][^cite_note-\:1-10] To protect the coastal zone from flooding, the natural defenses should, therefore, be protected and maintained in for example [Marine Protected Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_protected_area) (MPAs).[^cite_note-\:2-11]

## Types



High tide flooding, also called [tidal flooding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_flooding), is one of the causes for coastal flooding. It has become much more common in the past seven decades.

[^cite_note-N0AA_HighTideFlooding_201802-12]

The [seawater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawater) can flood the land via several different paths:

- Direct flooding — where the sea height exceeds the elevation of the land, often where waves have not built up a natural barrier such as a dune

- Overtopping of a barrier — the barrier may be natural or human-engineered and overtopping occurs due to swelling conditions during storms or high tides often on open stretches of the coast.

  [^cite_note-NatureComms_20210618-3]

  &#x20;The height of the waves exceeds the height of the barrier and [water flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Flows_\(album\)) over the top of the barrier to flood the land behind it. Overtopping can result in high [velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velocity) flows that can erode significant amounts of the land surface which can undermine defense structures.

  [^cite_note-gallien-13]

- Breaching of a barrier — again the barrier may be natural (sand dune) or human-engineered (sea wall), and breaching occurs on open coasts exposed to large waves. Breaching occurs when the barrier is broken down or destroyed by waves allowing the seawater to extend inland and flood the areas

## Causes

&#x20;Coastal flooding can result from a variety of different causes including [storm surges](449744.md) created by storms like [hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane) and [tropical cyclones](8282374.md), rising sea levels due to climate change and [tsunamis](31161.md).



Storm surge from [Hurricane Carol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Carol) in 1954

### Storms and storm surges

[Storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storms), including [hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricanes) and [tropical cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclones), can cause flooding through [storm surges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_surges) which are waves significantly larger than normal.[^cite_note-ramsay-1][^cite_note-kurian-14] If a storm event coincides with the [high astronomical tide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_astronomical_tide), extensive flooding can occur.[^cite_note-benavente-15] Storm surges involve three processes:

1. wind setup

2. barometric setup

3. [wave setup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_setup)

Wind blowing in an onshore direction (from the sea towards the land) can cause the water to 'pile-up' against the coast; this is known as wind setup. Low [atmospheric pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_pressure) is associated with storm systems and this tends to increase the surface sea level; this is a barometric setup. Finally increased [wave breaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_breaking) height results in a higher water level in the [surf zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surf_zone), which is [wave setup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_setup). These three processes interact to create waves that can overtop natural and engineered coastal protection structures thus penetrating seawater further inland than normal.[^cite_note-benavente-15][^cite_note-link-16]

### Sea level rise

This section is an excerpt from [Sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Sea_level_rise\&action=edit)]

The [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level) has generally been rising since the end of the [Last Glacial Maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Maximum), which was around 20,000 years ago.[^cite_note-17] Between 1901 and 2018, the global average sea level rose by 15–25 cm (6–10 in), with an increase of 2.3 mm (0.091 in) per year since the 1970s.[^cite_note-Sea_level_rise_Fox-Kemper-2021-18]<sup>: 1216 </sup> This was faster than the sea level had ever risen over at least the past 3,000 years.[^cite_note-Sea_level_rise_Fox-Kemper-2021-18]<sup>: 1216 </sup> The rate accelerated to 4.62 mm (0.182 in)/yr for the decade 2013–2022.[^cite_note-Sea_level_rise_World_Meteorological_Organization-2023-19] [Climate change](5042951.md) due to human activities is the main cause of this persistent acceleration.[^cite_note-Sea_level_rise_IPCC-20]<sup>: 5, 8 </sup> Between 1993 and 2018, melting [ice sheets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_sheets) and [glaciers](12463.md) accounted for 44% of sea level rise, with another 42% resulting from [thermal expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_expansion) of [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water).[^cite_note-Sea_level_rise_WCRP_Global_Sea_Level_Budget_Group-2018-21]<sup>: 1576 </sup>

Sea level rise lags behind changes in the [Earth](9228.md)'s temperature by decades, and sea level rise will therefore continue to accelerate between now and 2050 in response to warming that has already happened.[^cite_note-Sea_level_rise_National_Academies_of_Sciences,_Engineering,_and_Medicine-2011-22] What happens after that depends on future human [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md). If there are very deep cuts in emissions, sea level rise would slow down between 2050 and 2100. It could then rise by between 30 cm (1 ft) and 1.0 m (3+1⁄3 ft) between the early 2020s and 2100, or by approximately 60 cm (2 ft) to 130 cm (4+1⁄2 ft) from the 19th century to 2100. With high emissions it would instead accelerate further, and could rise by 50 cm (1.6 ft) or even by 1.9 m (6.2 ft) by 2100.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-Sea_level_rise_IPCC-20][^cite_note-Sea_level_rise_Fox-Kemper-2021-18]<sup>: 1302 </sup> In the long run, sea level rise would amount to 2–3 m (7–10 ft) over the next 2000 years if warming stays to its current 1.5 °C (2.7 °F) over the pre-industrial past. It would be 19–22 metres (62–72 ft) if warming peaks at 5 °C (9.0 °F).[^cite_note-Sea_level_rise_IPCC-20]<sup>: 21 </sup> The expected increase in flood hazard potential is often exceedingly large even for modest sea-level rise scenarios, ranging from 10 to 1000 for rises of 0.5 m or less.[^cite_note-24]

### Tidal flooding

This section is an excerpt from [Tidal flooding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_flooding).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Tidal_flooding\&action=edit)]

[Tidal flooding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_flooding), also known as sunny day flooding[^cite_note-Tidal_flooding_TRDdev-25] or nuisance flooding,[^cite_note-Tidal_flooding_NOAAnuisance-26] is the temporary inundation of low-lying areas, especially streets, during exceptionally [high tide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_tide) events, such as at [full](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_moon) and [new moons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_moon). The highest tides of the year may be known as the [king tide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_tide), with the month varying by location. These kinds of floods tend not to be a high risk to property or human safety, but further stress coastal infrastructure in low-lying areas.[^cite_note-27]

This kind of flooding is becoming more common in cities and other human-occupied coastal areas as [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise) associated with [climate change](5042951.md) and other human-related environmental impacts such as [coastal erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_erosion) and [land subsidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_subsidence) increase the [vulnerability of infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_vulnerability).[^cite_note-Tidal_flooding_ReferenceA-28] Geographies faced with these issues can utilize [coastal management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_management) practices to mitigate the effects in some areas, but increasingly these kinds of floods may develop into coastal flooding that requires [managed retreat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managed_retreat) or other more extensive [climate change adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_adaptation) practices are needed for vulnerable areas.

### Tsunami waves

Coastal areas can be significantly flooded as the result of [tsunami](31161.md) waves[^cite_note-cochard-29] which propagate through the [ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean) as the result of the displacement of a significant body of water through [earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquakes), [landslides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslides), [volcanic eruptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_eruptions), and [glacier](12463.md) calvings. There is also evidence to suggest that significant tsunami have been caused in the past by [meteor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteor) impact into the ocean.[^cite_note-goff-30] Tsunami waves are so destructive due to the [velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velocity) of the approaching waves, the height of the waves when they reach land, and the [debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debris) the water entrains as it flows over land can cause further damage.[^cite_note-cochard-29][^cite_note-alongi-9]

Depending on the magnitude of the tsunami waves and floods, it could cause severe injuries which call for precautionary interventions that prevent overwhelming aftermaths. It was reported that more than 200,000 people were killed in the earthquake and subsequent tsunami that hit the Indian Ocean, on December 26, 2004.[^cite_note-\:0-31] Not to mention, several diseases are a result of floods ranging from hypertension to chronic obstructive pulmonary diseases.[^cite_note-\:0-31]

## Impacts

See also: [Flood § Negative impacts](50482.md#Negative_impacts)

### Social and economic impacts

The [coastal zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_zone) (the area both within 100 kilometres distance of the coast and 100 metres elevation of sea level) is home to a large and growing proportion of the global population.[^cite_note-nicholls-5][^cite_note-dawson-7] Over 50 percent of the global population and 65 percent of cities with populations over five million people are in the coastal zone.[^cite_note-hunt-32] In addition to the significant number of people at risk of coastal flooding, these coastal urban centres are producing a considerable amount of the global [Gross Domestic Product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_Domestic_Product) (GDP).[^cite_note-dawson-7]

People's lives, homes, businesses, and city infrastructure like roads, railways, and industrial plants are all at risk of coastal flooding with massive potential social and economic costs.[^cite_note-suarez-33][^cite_note-tomita-34][^cite_note-nadal-35] The recent [earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquakes) and [tsunami](31161.md) in [Indonesia](14579.md) in 2004 and in [Japan](15573.md) in March 2011 clearly illustrate the devastation coastal flooding can produce. Indirect economic costs can be incurred if economically important sandy [beaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaches) are eroded resulting in a loss of [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism) in areas dependent on the attractiveness of those beaches.[^cite_note-snoussi-36]

### Environmental impacts

Coastal flooding can result in a wide variety of environmental impacts on different spatial and temporal scales. Flooding can destroy coastal habitats such as coastal [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetlands) and [estuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuaries) and can erode dune systems.[^cite_note-gallien-13][^cite_note-nicholls-5][^cite_note-snoussi-36][^cite_note-hunt-32] These places are characterized by their high [biological diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_diversity) therefore coastal flooding can cause significant [biodiversity loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_loss) and potentially species [extinctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinctions).[^cite_note-cochard-29] In addition to this, these coastal features are the coasts natural buffering system against storm waves; consistent coastal flooding and sea-level rise can cause this natural protection to be reduced allowing waves to penetrate greater distances inland exacerbating erosion and furthering coastal flooding.[^cite_note-nicholls-5] "By 2050, "moderate" (typically damaging) flooding is expected to occur, on average, more than 10 times as often as it does today, and can be intensified by local factors."[^cite_note-37]

Prolonged [inundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inundation) of [seawater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawater) after flooding can also cause [salination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salination) of agriculturally productive soils thus resulting in a loss of productivity for long periods of time.[^cite_note-ramsay-1][^cite_note-snoussi-36] The effects of the soil salinization, which is brought on by sea levels rising and differed precipitation patterns, impacts agricultural production, ultimately leading towards food and water shortages. Food [crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crops) and [forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forests) can be completely killed off by salination of soils or wiped out by the movement of floodwaters.[^cite_note-nicholls-5] Coastal freshwater bodies including [lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake), [lagoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagoons), and coastal freshwater [aquifers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifers) can also be affected by [saltwater intrusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltwater_intrusion).[^cite_note-gallien-13][^cite_note-nicholls-5][^cite_note-hunt-32] This can destroy these water bodies as habitats for freshwater organisms and sources of drinking water for towns and cities.[^cite_note-nicholls-5][^cite_note-hunt-32]

## Reduction and control

Further information: [Climate change adaptation § Flooding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_adaptation#Flooding)

### Flood control

This section is an excerpt from [Flood management](20913655.md).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Flood_management\&action=edit)]



A [weir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weir) was built on the [Humber River (Ontario)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humber_River_\(Ontario\)) to prevent a recurrence of a catastrophic flood.

[Flood management](20913655.md) or flood control are methods used to reduce or prevent the detrimental effects of [flood](50482.md) waters. Flooding can be caused by a mix of both natural processes, such as [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather) upstream, and human changes to waterbodies and runoff. Flood management methods can be either of the *structural* type (i.e. flood control) and of the *non-structural* type. Structural methods hold back floodwaters physically, while non-structural methods do not. Building [hard infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_infrastructure) to prevent flooding, such as [flood walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_wall), is effective at managing flooding. However, it is best practice within [landscape engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_engineering) to rely more on [soft infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_infrastructure) and [natural systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature-based_solutions), such as [marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh) and [flood plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floodplain), for handling the increase in water.

Flood management can include *flood risk management,* which focuses on measures to reduce risk, vulnerability and exposure to flood disasters and providing risk analysis through, for example, [flood risk assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_risk_assessment).[^cite_note-Flood_management_\:62-38] *Flood mitigation* is a related but separate concept describing a broader set of strategies taken to reduce flood risk and potential impact while improving resilience against flood events.

As [climate change](5042951.md) has led to increased flood risk an intensity, flood management is an important part of [climate change adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_adaptation) and [climate resilience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_resilience).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-Flood_management_\:0-40] For example, to prevent or manage coastal flooding, [coastal management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_management) practices have to handle natural processes like [tides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tide) but also [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise) due to climate change. The prevention and mitigation of flooding can be studied on three levels: on individual properties, small communities, and whole towns or cities.

### Non-structural mechanisms

If human systems are affected by flooding, an adaption to how that system operates on the coast through behavioral and institutional changes is required, these changes are the so-called *non-structural* mechanisms of coastal flooding response.[^cite_note-dawson2011-41]

[Building regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_code), coastal [hazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard) zoning, urban development planning, spreading the risk through [insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance), and enhancing public awareness are some ways of achieving this.[^cite_note-nicholls-5][^cite_note-dawson2011-41][^cite_note-snoussi-36] [Adapting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_adaptation) to the risk of flood occurrence can be the best option if the cost of building defense structures outweighs any benefits or if the natural processes in that stretch of coastline add to its natural character and attractiveness.[^cite_note-pope-8]

A more extreme and often difficult to accept response to coastal flooding is abandoning the area (also known as [managed retreat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managed_retreat)) prone to flooding.[^cite_note-gallien-13] This however raises issues for where the people and [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure) affected would go and what sort of [compensation](8134.md) should/could be paid.

### Engineered defenses



[Groynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groynes) are engineered structures that aim to prevent [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) of the beach front

There are a variety of ways in which humans are trying to prevent the flooding of coastal environments, typically through so-called hard engineering structures such as [flood barriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_barrier), [seawalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawalls) and [levees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levees).[^cite_note-pope-8][^cite_note-short-42] That armouring of the coast is typical to protect towns and cities which have developed right up to the beachfront.[^cite_note-pope-8] Enhancing depositional processes along the [coast](5236.md) can also help prevent coastal flooding. Structures such as [groynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groynes), [breakwaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakwaters), and artificial headlands promote the deposition of sediment on the beach thus helping to buffer against storm waves and surges as the wave energy is spent on moving the sediments in the beach than on moving water inland.[^cite_note-short-42]

### Natural defenses



[Mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangroves) are one of the coasts natural defense systems against [storm surges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_surges) and flooding. Their high [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass) both above and below the water can help dissipate wave energy.

Coastal areas do provide natural protective structures to guard against coastal flooding. These include physical features like [gravel bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravel_bars) and [sand dune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_dune) systems, but also [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystems) such as [salt marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_marshes), [seagrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seagrass) and [mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) forests have a buffering function. [Mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove), [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetlands) and [seagrass meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seagrass_meadow) are often considered to provide significant protection against storm waves, tsunamis, and shoreline [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) through their ability to attenuate wave energy.[^cite_note-griffis-6][^cite_note-alongi-9][^cite_note-\:1-10] To protect the coastal zone from flooding, the natural defenses should, therefore, be protected and maintained in for example [Marine Protected Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_protected_area) (MPAs).[^cite_note-\:2-11]

### Longer term aspects and research

Reducing global sea level rise is one way to prevent significant flooding of coastal areas. This could be minimised by further reducing [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md). However, even if significant emission decreases are achieved, there is already a substantial amount of sea level rise into the future.[^cite_note-nicholls-5] International [climate change policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_policies) like the [Paris Agreement](30242372.md) are seeking to mitigate the future [effects of climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change), including sea level rise. In addition, more immediate measures of engineered and natural defenses are put in place to prevent coastal flooding.

### Hurricane Katrina in New Orleans

[Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina) made landfall as a category 3 [cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone) on the [Saffir–Simpson hurricane wind scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffir%E2%80%93Simpson_hurricane_wind_scale), indicating that it had become an only moderate level storm.[^cite_note-link-16] However, the catastrophic damage caused by the extensive flooding was the result of the highest recorded storm surges in [North America](21139.md).[^cite_note-link-16] For several days prior to the landfall of Katrina, wave setup was generated by the persistent winds of the [cyclonic rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclonic_rotation) of the system. This prolonged wave set up coupled with the very low central pressure level meant massive storm surges were generated.[^cite_note-ebersole-45] Storm surges overtopped and breached the [levees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levees) and floodwalls intended to protect the city from inundation.[^cite_note-griffis-6][^cite_note-link-16][^cite_note-ebersole-45] Unfortunately, [New Orleans](53842.md) is inherently prone to coastal flooding for a number of factors. Firstly, much of New Orleans is below sea level and is bordered by the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) therefore protection against flooding from both the sea and the river has become dependent on engineered structures. [Land-use change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-use_change) and modification to natural systems in the Mississippi River have rendered the natural defenses for the city less effective. [Wetland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland) loss has been calculated to be around 1,900 square miles (4,920 square kilometres) since 1930. This is a significant amount as four miles of wetland are estimated to reduce the height of a [storm surge](449744.md) by one foot (30 centimeters).[^cite_note-griffis-6]

### Indonesia and Japan earthquake-related tsunamis

Further information: [List of tsunamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tsunamis)

[2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake_and_tsunami): An [earthquake](10106.md) of approximately magnitude 9.0 struck off the coast of [Sumatra](28679.md), [Indonesia](14579.md) causing the propagation of a massive [tsunami](31161.md) throughout the [Indian Ocean](14580.md).[^cite_note-alongi-9] This tsunami caused significant loss of human life, an estimate of 280,000 – 300,000 people has been reported [^cite_note-cochard-29] and caused extensive damage to villages, towns, and cities and to the physical environment. The natural structures and habitats destroyed or damaged include [coral reefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_reefs), mangroves, beaches, and seagrass beds.[^cite_note-alongi-9] The more recent earthquake and tsunami in [Japan](15573.md) in March 2011 ([2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake_and_tsunami)) also clearly illustrates the destructive power of tsunamis and the turmoil of coastal flooding.
