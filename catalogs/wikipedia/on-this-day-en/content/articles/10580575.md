# Energy in Iran

In 2010, Iran held 10% of the world's proven [oil reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_reserves) and 15% of its [gas](22131.md). It is [OPEC](166346.md)'s second largest exporter and the world's fourth largest oil producer.

[^cite_note-EIA-1]

[^cite_note-2]



Total primary energy consumption in Iran, by fuel, 2015.

**Energy in Iran** is characterized by vast reserves of [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel), positioning the country as a global energy powerhouse. [Iran](14653.md) holds the world's third-largest proved oil reserves and the second-largest natural gas reserves as of 2021, accounting for 24% of the [Middle East](19323.md)'s oil reserves and 12% of the global total.[^cite_note-3]

In 2023, the Total Energy Supply (TES) in Iran was predominantly derived from natural gas (71.5%) and oil (26.5%), with nuclear power and renewable sources contributing only 0.5% and 1% each respectively.[^cite_note-4] Despite the heavy reliance on fossil fuels, Iran possesses significant potential for renewable energy. Due to its geographical location near the equator, 90% of the country's land is suitable for solar power generation for at least 300 days a year.[^cite_note-zawya.com-5][^cite_note-6]

While Iran's energy wealth provides considerable economic opportunities, it also poses challenges. Heavy dependence on oil and gas has resulted in widespread [air pollution](10934212.md) and high greenhouse gas emissions.[^cite_note-7]

In recent years, Iran has faced a significant [energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_energy_crisis) driven by a combination of aging infrastructure, mismanagement, and [international sanctions](25699056.md). This has resulted in frequent power outages, disruptions in industrial production, and challenges in meeting domestic energy demands. Additionally, the growth of cryptocurrency mining in the country has further strained the electricity grid, exacerbating the crisis.[^cite_note-8] Addressing these challenges is crucial for Iran's energy sector to meet the needs of its growing population and economic ambitions.[^cite_note-9] The crisis in Iran energy sector may indicate also a problem in its reported economic data.

## History

Iran is in a constant battle to use its energy resources more effectively in the face of [subsidization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_targeted_subsidy_plan) and the need for technological advances in energy exploration and production. In 2008, energy wastage in Iran amounted to six or seven billion dollars. The energy consumption in Iran is extraordinarily higher than international standards. Iran paid \$84 billion in subsidies for oil, gas and electricity in 2008.[^cite_note-10]

Iran is one of the most energy-intensive economies of the world, with [per capita energy consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_energy_consumption_per_capita) 15 times that of [Japan](15573.md) and 10 times that of [European Union](9317.md). Also due to [huge energy subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_subsidy_reform_plan), Iran is one of the most energy inefficient countries of the world, with the [energy intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_intensity) three times higher than global average and 2.5 times the middle eastern average.[^cite_note-11] In 2014, half of Iran's energy was wasted in the domestic sector, 3.4 of which is wasted through [single-occupancy vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-occupancy_vehicle) use. 2/3 parts of [Iranian power plant](26980995.md) energy is wasted.[^cite_note-12]

Iran is one of the leading members of [OPEC](166346.md) (Organization of Petroleum Exporting Countries) and the [Organization of Gas Exporting Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GECF) (GECF). In 2008, Iran received \$47 billion in oil export revenues, which accounts for about 50% of state revenues.[^cite_note-\:0-13] Natural gas and oil consumption both account for about half of Iran's [domestic energy consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_energy_consumption). With its heavy dependence on oil and gas revenues Iran continues to explore for new sources of natural gas and oil. Since 2009, Iran has focused its energy sector on the exploration of the [South Pars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pars) offshore natural gas fields in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-14]

Iran has become self-sufficient in designing, building and operating dams and power plants and it has won international bids, such as in Ecuador, in competition with foreign firms.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

In 2007, energy generation capacity of Iranian thermal power plants reached 173 terawatt hours, accounting for 17.9 percent of energy production in the Middle East and African region. In 2007, Natural gas was the main energy source in Iran, comprising over 55 percent of energy needs, with oil and hydroelectricity accounting for 42 and 2 percent respectively. The region's energy need will increase by 26.8 percent until 2012.[^cite_note-17] In 2017, the fuel consumption in Iran was equivalent to 5.5 million barrels of fuel per day (bpd), the rate of which is considered as the highest energy consumption rate in the world in terms of energy intensity.[^cite_note-18]

Energy plays an important role in [Iranian politics](64702.md). [Robert Baer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Baer) in his 2008 book *The Devil We Know: Dealing with the New Iranian Superpower* argued that Iran had attained the status of an [energy superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_superpower) and was on its way to become a military-political superpower.[^cite_note-19]

Iran plans to create an [energy saving company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_saving_company) in conjunction with the 2014 [Iranian subsidy reform plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_subsidy_reform_plan).[^cite_note-20]&#x20;

## Primary energy sources

Primary energy supply is around two thirds gas and one third oil, with tiny amounts from other sources.[^cite_note-22]

### Natural gas

Main article: [Natural gas reserves in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_reserves_in_Iran)

See also: [National Iranian Gas Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Gas_Company) and [South Pars Gas Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pars_Gas_Field)



Iran's gas production 2016, domestic consumption and exports

Iran is the world's third producer of [natural gas](22131.md) (5.1% of the world's total and 184 BCM); which is primarily used for domestic [electricity generation](9540.md) or heat production.[^cite_note-23] It contains an estimated 1,187.3 trillion cubic feet (33,620 km<sup>3</sup>) (Tcf) in proven natural gas reserves.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-24] In 2005 a large share of Iran's [natural gas](22131.md) reserves were believed to remain untapped. About 62% of these reserves are located in non-associated fields. Iranian production of natural gas is expected to increase over the next few years due to continuing discoveries in the [North Pars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pars) and [South Pars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pars) regions.[^cite_note-25]

Domestic consumption has matched production at 3.6×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>12</sup> cu ft (100 km<sup>3</sup>) in the year of 2005. Domestic consumption is expected to go up at about 7% per year for the following decade. The Iranian government has also subsidized natural gas prices along with [gasoline](23639.md) prices, and this is expected to sustain this high level of domestic consumption. In lieu of this increasing domestic demand, natural gas exports are expected to decrease in the following years.[^cite_note-26]

In 2021, Iran ranked as the world's fourth-largest consumer of natural gas, behind the United States, Russia, and China. A significant share of its natural gas production is utilized domestically in various sectors. Over the last ten years, consumption of natural gas in Iran has risen by approximately 50%, influenced by a range of factors. These include heavily subsidized prices, an extensive distribution network, enhanced domestic production, and government initiatives aimed at replacing oil with natural gas in residential, commercial, and [electric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power) sectors.[^cite_note-\:3-27]

### Oil

Main articles: [Petroleum industry in Iran](28205267.md), [National Iranian Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Company), and [Oil reserves in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_reserves_in_Iran)

See also: [Ministry of Petroleum of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Petroleum_of_Iran), [2007 Gas Rationing Plan in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Gas_Rationing_Plan_in_Iran), [National Iranian Petrochemical Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Petrochemical_Company), and [Asalouyeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asalouyeh)



Top oil-producing countries, 1973 to 2016, million barrels per day

Iran has the second largest oil reserves in the world, and the third largest exporter of it. By the end of 2009, Iranian oil [R/P ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserves-to-production_ratio) was 89.4 years which is the world's highest.[^cite_note-bp.com-29] By 2009, Iran had 52 active [rigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_platform) and 1,853 producing [oil wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_well).[^cite_note-30]

Iran possesses abundant [fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuels) from which to generate energy. Since 1913 [Iran](14653.md) has been a major oil-exporting country. Oil industry output averaged 4 million barrels (640,000 m<sup>3</sup>) per day in 2005, compared with the peak output of 6 million barrels per day (950,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d) reached in 1974. Following the 1979 [revolution](347268.md), however, the government reduced daily oil production in accordance with an oil conservation policy. Further production declines occurred as result of damage to oil facilities during the [war with Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War). In the early 2000s, industry infrastructure was increasingly inefficient because of technological lags. Few exploratory wells were drilled in 2005. Iranian oil was [nationalized in 1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization_of_the_Iranian_oil_industry) and thus is owned and operated by the [National Iranian Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Company) (NIOC).

Iran held 10.3% of the world's total proven [oil reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_reserves) and that figures out to be about 137.6 billion barrels (2.188×10<sup>10</sup> m<sup>3</sup>) of oil reserves at the end of 2009.[^cite_note-bp.com-29] Oil also is found in northern Iran and in the offshore waters of the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). Nevertheless, in 2005 Iran spent US\$4 billion on [gasoline](23639.md) imports, mainly because of [contraband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraband) and inefficient domestic use that result from [subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Petroleum_of_Iran#Gasoline_subsidies). Iran is one of the largest gasoline consumers in the world ranking second behind [United States](3434750.md) in consumption per car.[^cite_note-31]

There is a growing recognition that prices must rise faster to curb consumption and ease the burden on the public finances. Cheap energy has encouraged wasteful consumption in Iran, and a brisk business in smuggling petrol into [Iraq](7515928.md), [Turkey](11125639.md), [Pakistan](23235.md) and [Afghanistan](737.md). Demand has also been supported by rapid increases in car production in recent years. In the absence of imports, [the car industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_automobile_industry) has developed strongly (albeit from a low base) with output reaching over 1m vehicles in fiscal year 2006/07 (March 21 – March 20).[^cite_note-Iran-32]



Iran oil production, domestic consumption and exports

The growth in consumption of domestically produced oil has been modest, owing to [refining constraints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Refining_and_Distribution_Company). By contrast, fuel imports rose to 180,000 barrels per day (29,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d) in January 2005 from 30,000 barrels per day (4,800 m<sup>3</sup>/d) in 2000, and petrol consumption is estimated to have been around 1,800,000 barrels per day (290,000 m<sup>3</sup>/d) in 2007 (before [rationing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationing)), of which about one-third is imported. These imports are proving expensive, costing the government about US\$4bn in the first nine months of 2007/08, according to parliamentary sources.[^cite_note-Iran-32] Nearly 40% of refined oil consumed by Iran is imported from [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-LaFranchi-33]



A 2003 [USGS](23814944.md) map of countries where [oil](4207510.md) is located

Iran contains 27 onshore and 13 offshore oil-producing fields, largely concentrated in the southwestern [Khuzestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khuzestan) region near the [Iraqi](7515928.md) border.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-14] The Iranian government is heavily reliant on oil revenues and they have heavily subsidized the energy industries, which figures out to be about 12% of Iran's [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP). However, domestic oil consumption has decreased due to the alternative use of natural gas. Economic growth from these resources is uncertain and stunted in Iran due to these subsidies and population growth. Iran has been unable to reach it full production levels due to a combination of [sanctions](411315.md) and war plaguing the region. Iran's oil fields have a natural decline rate at 8 percent for onshore wells and 10% for offshore fields. The Iranian recovery rate is currently approximately 27 percent, which is well below the world average. Iran needs structural improvements made to coincide with their [enhanced oil recovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_oil_recovery) efforts.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-14]

### Nuclear energy

Main articles: [Nuclear program of Iran](721807.md), [Atomic Energy Organization of Iran](4667218.md), and [Nuclear facilities in Iran](15247782.md)



The [IR-40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IR-40) facility in [Arak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arak,_Iran)

Iran plans to generate 23,000 MW of electricity through nuclear technology to meet its increasing demand for energy.[^cite_note-\:1-36] The first of four 915 MW reactors of [Bushehr Nuclear Power Plant](18522794.md), built with help from [Russia](25391.md), came online in August 2010.[^cite_note-37] While nuclear power in the US costs a little over 10 cents per kilowatt hour, Iran, with domestic uranium enrichment capabilities, high interest rates, low energy output (a single 1,000 MW reactor), low efficiency, extremely slow reactor construction, and no reprocessing pays around 68 cents per kilowatt hour (this assumes a price of \$140 per separative working unit and of \$40 per kilogram of uranium).[^cite_note-38]

### Biofuel

In 2016, the Iranian Biofuel Society (IBS) in collaboration with the Vice Presidency for Science of Technology and Tehran and the Suburbs Bus Company executed the first urban pilot project for the consumption of waste cooking oil biodiesel in Tehran\`s bus fleet in an attempt to generate public awareness regarding *Global Climate Change*, wherein they recommend "reducing net [anthropogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_impact_on_the_environment) [CO2 emissions](9528025.md) to the atmosphere" and "minimizing anthropogenic disturbances of" atmospheric gasses by partial replacement of fossil fuels with waste-oriented biofuels.[^cite_note-42] The program was also supported by Small Grant Programme, [Global Environmental Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Environmental_Facility), United Nation Development Program (SGP/GEF/UNDP) Office in Iran.[^cite_note-43] Research into regional biofuel potential in Iran has identified arid regions like Kerman province as suitable for drought-resistant energy crops such as Jatropha and Sweet Sorghum.[^cite_note-44]

### Geothermal

Iran is the 9th largest [geothermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_energy) producer in the world.[^cite_note-51]

According to global tectonics Iran is impacted by subduction of the Arabian Plate under the Central Iranian Plate and four thrust faults: two that make the Southern Caspian Sea [Mazandaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran) Depression and two in the north of Iran, part of the Caucasian Range. Mt. [Sabalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabalan) and Mt. [Sahand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahand) are two Quaternary Volcanos in the area. Sabalan is a large stratovolcano consisting of 3 summits named Soltan (4811), Heram (4612m) and Kasra (4573m). The stratovolcano is situated on a possible horst trending northeast-southwest and erupted at latest in the Holocene. Caldera collapsing has caused a depression about 400 m in height and 12 km in diameter. The lava flows consists of trachyandesite, andesite and dacite and pyroclastic deposits. There are 9 hot springs with a temperature in the range of 25-85 °C.[^cite_note-52]

Iran's domestic consumption and production have steadily grown together since 1984 and it is still heavily reliant on traditional [thermal](2549543.md) energy sources of electricity, with a small fraction being produced by [hydroelectric](381399.md) plants.[^cite_note-56] As of 2023 a quarter of electricity is generated from oil and most of the rest is gas-fired.[^cite_note-57] In recent years, Iran has put greater emphasis on participation of domestic and foreign investors in electricity generation sector, with projects underway to add 40,000 MWh more capacity to the national grid.[^cite_note-58]

It is estimated that some 18.5 percent of electricity generated in Iran are wasted before it reaches consumers due to technical problems.[^cite_note-waste-53] Iran is among the top ten manufacturers of [gas turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_turbine) with a capacity of 160 megawatts.[^cite_note-59] Iran has acquired self-sufficiency of over 80 percent in constructing hydraulic turbines and over 90 percent in producing gas turbines. Within the next few years, Iran can join the list of countries that produce [power plant](212141.md) technology (2009).[^cite_note-60] Iran has achieved the technical expertise to set up hydroelectric, gas and combined cycle power plants. Iran is not only self-sufficient in power plant construction but has also concluded a number of contracts on implementing projects in neighboring states.[^cite_note-61]

The exploration efforts for sources of power generation are wide and diverse in Iran. Plans are being made to make oil efficient power plants as well as an emphasis on natural gas production in order to meet their growing electricity demand. [Nuclear power](22153.md) and hydroelectric power are not focused on for the time being, but they are part of an overall strategy to meet electricity demands. The electricity sector is also heavily subsidized and mostly state owned companies control power distribution, transmission and generation. In order to meet the demands of the electricity sector, however, Iran is beginning to look into private investment.[^cite_note-62] [A by-law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Direct_Investment_in_Iran#FIPPA) has been passed allowing the energy ministry to conclude rial or combined rial/foreign-currency contracts for the purchase of electricity from private companies.[^cite_note-Iran-32]

Iran has displayed a new approach to this sector. After the 1979 Islamic Revolution, the construction of hydroelectric power plants became both a popular private and public venture. Often, construction projects did not meet basic environmental standards, engineering standards, and technical requirements. As a result, many of these dams were destroyed or left in dilapidated conditions.[^cite_note-63]



The [Karun-3 dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun-3_dam) & [hydroelectric power plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_power_plant) was commissioned in 2005. Iran has emerged as one of the world's largest [dam](51518.md) builders in recent years.

[^cite_note-Elec-54]

By 2004, the addition of new hydroelectric stations and the streamlining of conventional [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal)- and [oil](4207510.md)-fired stations increased installed capacity to 33,000 megawatts (MW). Of that amount, about 75 percent was based on [natural gas](22131.md), 18 percent on [oil](4207510.md), and 7 percent on [hydroelectric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_power). However, in 2004 Iran opened its first [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind)-powered and [geothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_power) plants, and the first solar thermal plant was to come online in 2009.[^cite_note-Iran-32]

In 2021, Iran produced 341 terawatt-hours (TWh) of net electricity, 94% of which came from fossil fuels. Natural gas was the primary source, accounting for about 81% of electricity generation. Oil's contribution rose from 9% in 2018 to 14% in 2021, driven by [international sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions) that limited oil exports and impacted associated natural gas production, leading to increased domestic use and substitution with diesel and fuel oil in power generation. Additionally, condensate storage capacity constraints hindered non-associated natural gas production, prompting greater reliance on alternative fuels. In response, Iran is upgrading its older natural gas plants to more efficient [combined-cycle plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_cycle_plant) and expanding capacity to reduce hydropower dependency, improve generation efficiency, and increase oil availability for export.[^cite_note-\:3-27]

It has been estimated that Iran has the potential to produce at least 6,150 MWh of electricity by [wave power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_power) from its coastline on [Persian Gulf](24761.md) alone.[^cite_note-64] Iran is also experimenting with electricity generation from organic wastes and plans to build power plants using sewage and organic waste of domestic and industrial origin as fuel.[^cite_note-65] With about 300 clear sunny days a year and an average of 2,200 kilowatt-hour [solar radiation](27979.md) per square meter, Iran has significant potential to tap [solar energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_energy).[^cite_note-66]

In 2021, Iran's electricity generation was primarily sourced from natural gas, accounting for 81% of total production. Oil contributed 14%, followed by hydropower at 4%, and nuclear power at 1%. Both coal and non-hydro renewable sources each made up less than 1% of the electricity generation mix.[^cite_note-\:3-27]

According to a 2022 [U.S. Energy Information Administration](1704673.md) (EIA) report, Iran has experienced escalating electricity demand driven by factors including illegal [cryptocurrency mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_mining), population growth, highly subsidized electricity prices, and fuel supply shortages. This increase in demand has been particularly acute during the peak seasons of summer and winter, putting significant strain on the nation's electrical infrastructure. As a result, Iran encountered notable [power shortages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_shortage) in 2020 and 2021. During the summer of 2021, the situation intensified when electricity demand exceeded the available supply by nearly 12 gigawatts (GW).[^cite_note-\:3-27]

## Renewable energy

See also: [Iranian targeted subsidy plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_targeted_subsidy_plan)



Iran has established wind farms in several areas, this one near [Manjeel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjeel).

In 2022 less than 1% of electricity was generated by solar and wind.[^cite_note-71]

Iran plans to introduce 10,000 megawatts of renewable electricity into the electricity grid by the end of 2025.[^cite_note-72]
