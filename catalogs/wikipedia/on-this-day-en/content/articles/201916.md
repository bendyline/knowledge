**Lieutenant general** (**Lt Gen**, **LTG** and similar) is a [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) rank used in many countries. The rank traces its origins to the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), where the title of lieutenant general was held by the [second-in-command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-in-command) on the battlefield, who was normally subordinate to a [captain general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_general).

In modern armies, lieutenant general normally ranks immediately below [general](148537.md) (or [colonel general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_general)) and above [major general](36301471.md); it is equivalent to the navy rank of [vice admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_admiral), and in air forces with a separate rank structure, it is equivalent to [air marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_marshal). In the United States, a lieutenant general has a three star insignia and commands an [army corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_corps), typically made up of three [army divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_\(military\)), and consisting of around 60,000 to 70,000 soldiers.

The seeming incongruity that a lieutenant general outranks a major general (whereas a [major](201920.md) outranks a [lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant)) is due to the derivation of major general from [sergeant major general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergeant_major_general), which was a rank subordinate to lieutenant general (as a lieutenant outranks a [sergeant major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergeant_major)). Several countries (e.g. Balkan states) use the rank of [lieutenant colonel general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel_general) instead of lieutenant general, in an attempt to solve this apparent anomaly.[^cite_note-Albanian_Army-1]

In contrast, in [Russia](25391.md) and a number of other countries of the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), lieutenant general is a rank immediately below [colonel general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_general), and above [major general](36301471.md) – in these systems there is no use of the [brigadier general](206220.md) of many Western countries.

In addition, some countries use the lieutenant general as the rank of divisional commander, and some have designated them with [French revolutionary system](148537.md#French_Revolutionary_system). For example, some countries of South America use [divisional general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisional_general) as the equivalent of lieutenant general.
