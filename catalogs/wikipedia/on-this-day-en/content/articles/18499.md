**Left-wing politics**, **leftist politics**, and **leftism** are descriptions within the [left–right political spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left%E2%80%93right_political_spectrum) for a range of [political ideologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideology#Political_ideologies) that generally support and seek to achieve [social equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_equality) and [egalitarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egalitarianism).[^cite_note-Smith-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-Lukes-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-\:0-5] Left-wing politics typically involve a concern for those in society whom its adherents perceive as disadvantaged relative to others as well as a belief that there are unjustified inequalities that need to be reduced or abolished.[^cite_note-Lukes-3][^cite_note-\:0-5]

Ideologies considered to be *left-wing* vary greatly depending on the placement along the [political spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left%E2%80%93right_political_spectrum) in a given time and place. At the end of the 18th century, upon the founding of the first [liberal democracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracy#Origins), the term *Left* was used to describe [liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) in the United States and [republicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicanism) in France, supporting a lesser degree of [hierarchical decision-making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stratification) than the [*right-wing* politics](56522.md) of the [traditional conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_conservatism) and [monarchists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchists). In [modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism) politics, the term *Left* typically applies to ideologies and movements to the left of [classical liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_liberalism), supporting some degree of [democracy in the economic sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_democracy).

Today, ideologies such as [social liberalism](765430.md) and [social democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy) are considered to be [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left), while *the Left* is typically reserved for movements more [critical of capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criticism_of_capitalism), including the [labour movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_movement), [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism), [anarchism](12.md), [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism), [Marxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism), [Leninism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leninism) and [syndicalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndicalism), each of which rose to prominence in the 19th and 20th centuries.[^cite_note-6] In addition, the term *left-wing* has also been applied to a broad range of [culturally liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_liberalism) and [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism) social movements,[^cite_note-7] including the [civil rights movement](49001.md), [feminist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminist_movement), [LGBTQ rights movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_movements), [abortion-rights movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion-rights_movements), [multiculturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism), [anti-war movement](39766716.md), and [environmental movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_movement),[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] as well as a [wide range of political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_left-wing_political_parties).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-sarnold-12]‌

## Name

The terms *left* and *[right](56522.md)* that make up the [left–right political spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left%E2%80%93right_political_spectrum) were coined during the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), referring to the seating arrangement in the French [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Constituent_Assembly_\(France\)). The political groups opposed to the royal veto privilege ([Montagnard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mountain) and [Jacobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobin_\(politics\)) deputies from the [Third Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estates-General_of_1789)) generally sat to the left of the presiding member's chair in parliament, while the ones in favour of the royal veto privilege sat on its right.[^cite_note-Knapp-13][^cite_note-14]

Usage of the term *left* became more prominent after the [restoration of the French monarchy in 1815](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon_Restoration_in_France), when it was applied to the *Independents*.[^cite_note-15] The word *wing* was first appended to left and right factions in the late 19th century, usually with disparaging intent, and *left-wing* was applied to those who were unorthodox in their religious or political views.

## Positions

Political philosopher [Marius Ostrowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marius_Ostrowski) argues that, despite various differences among factions, the left is primarily concerned with who wields power in society and with fighting for "those without" against "those with" who harm "those without".[^cite_note-Ostrowski_p11-16] Ostrowski argues that the central value of the left is [social equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_equality), which is expressed in fighting for [empowerment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empowerment) and against [oppression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppression), for parity and against [hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchy), for [social recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_recognition) of marginalized groups and against [discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination), and for [cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperation) and against [competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition). Related concepts include [justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice), [solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity), [cultural pluralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_pluralism), [social progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_progress), and [freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom) from forceful control or coercion.[^cite_note-Ostrowski_p11-16]

### Economics

See also: [Anarchist economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist_economics), [Comparison of Marxian and Keynesian economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_Marxian_and_Keynesian_economics), and [Socialist economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_economics)



Members of the [All India Trade Union Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Trade_Union_Congress) demonstrating in [Alappuzha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alappuzha), [Kerala](4349459.md)&#x20;

Left-leaning economic beliefs range from [Keynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keynesian) economics and the [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state) through [industrial democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_democracy) and the [social market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_market) to the [nationalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization) of the economy and [central planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_economy),[^cite_note-17] to the [anarcho-syndicalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-syndicalist) advocacy of a council-based and self-managed [anarchist communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist_communism). Left-wing politics are typically associated with popular or state control of major political and economic institutions.[^cite_note-\:1-18] According to emeritus professor of economics Barry Clark, supporters of left-wing politics "claim that human development flourishes when individuals engage in cooperative, mutually respectful relations that can thrive only when excessive differences in status, power, and wealth are eliminated."[^cite_note-19] From the time of the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution), leftists have often supported [trade unions](17626.md).

Some leftists believe in [Marxian economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxian_economics), named after the economic theories of [Karl Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx), but drawing from a range of Marxist and non-Marxist sources. Some distinguish Marx's economic theories from his political philosophy, arguing that Marx's approach to understanding the economy is independent of his advocacy of [revolutionary socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_socialism) or his belief in the inevitability of a [proletarian revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletarian_revolution).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

The political relevance of farmers has divided the left. In *[Das Kapital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Kapital)*, Marx scarcely mentioned the subject,[^cite_note-22] though posthumous studies of his notebooks indicate that he was interested in studying the works of agricultural chemists to critique international agricultural trade and natural resource exploitation under capitalism.[^cite_note-23] In other works, including [The Communist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Communist_Manifesto), the [Critique of the Gotha Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critique_of_the_Gotha_Programme), and [The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Eighteenth_Brumaire_of_Louis_Bonaparte), Marx asserts the revolutionary potential of the peasant class through a transfer into the proletariat.[^cite_note-24] [Mikhail Bakunin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Bakunin) thought the [lumpenproletariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumpenproletariat) was a revolutionary class, while [Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong) believed that it would be rural peasants, not urban workers, who would bring about the [proletarian revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletarian_revolution).

At the beginning of the 20th century, many leftists advocated strong government intervention in the economy.[^cite_note-25] In contrast, [Left-libertarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-libertarians), [anarchists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchists) and [libertarian socialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_socialists) believe in a [decentralised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decentralized) economy run by [trade unions](17626.md), [workers' councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_councils), [cooperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperatives), [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities) and communes, opposing both [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_ownership) and [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_property) control of the economy, preferring [social ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_ownership) and local control in which a nation of decentralised regions is united in a [confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation).

Leftists continue to criticise the perceived exploitative nature of [globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization), the "[race to the bottom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_to_the_bottom)" and unjust lay-offs and exploitation of workers. In the last quarter of the 20th century, the belief that the government (ruling in accordance with the interests of the people) ought to be directly involved in the day-to-day workings of an economy declined in popularity amongst the [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left), especially [social democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democrats) who adopted the [Third Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Way).[^cite_note-\:1-18] From the 1990s, the [global justice movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_justice_movement), also known as the [anti-globalisation movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-globalisation_movement) and the [alter-globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alter-globalisation) movement, protests against [corporate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate) [economic globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_globalisation) due to its negative consequences for the poor, workers, the environment, and small businesses.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-Juris-28]

### Environment

See also: [Eco-socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco-socialism), [Green anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_anarchism), and [Green politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_politics)

One of the foremost left-wing advocates was [Thomas Paine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Paine), one of the first individuals since *left* and *right* became political terms to describe the collective human ownership of the world, which he speaks of in *Agrarian Justice*.[^cite_note-29] This principle is reflected in much of the historical left-wing thought and literature that came afterwards, although there were disagreements about what this entailed.

Karl Marx and the early socialist philosopher and scholar [William Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Morris) had a concern for environmental matters.[^cite_note-Foster2000-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] According to Marx, "\[e]ven an entire society, a nation, or all simultaneously existing societies taken together, are not the owners of the earth. They are simply its possessors, its beneficiaries, and have to bequeath it in an improved state to succeeding generations".[^cite_note-Foster2000-30][^cite_note-34] Following the Russian Revolution, environmental scientists such as revolutionary [Alexander Bogdanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Bogdanov) and the [Proletkult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletkult) organisation made efforts to incorporate environmentalism into Bolshevism and "integrate production with natural laws and limits" in the first decade of [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) rule, before [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) attacked ecologists and the science of ecology, purged environmentalists and promoted the [pseudoscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscience) of [Trofim Lysenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofim_Lysenko) during his rule up until his death in 1953.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] Similarly, [Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong) rejected environmentalism and believed that based on the laws of historical materialism, all of nature must be put into the service of revolution.[^cite_note-38]

From the 1970s onwards, environmentalism became an increasing concern of the left, with social movements and several unions campaigning on environmental issues and causes. In Australia, the left-wing [Builders Labourers Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Builders_Labourers_Federation), led by the communist Jack Mundy, united with environmentalists to place [green bans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_ban) on environmentally destructive development projects.[^cite_note-39] Several segments of the socialist and Marxist left consciously merged environmentalism and anti-capitalism into an [eco-socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco-socialist) ideology.[^cite_note-40] [Barry Commoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Commoner) articulated a left-wing response to *[The Limits to Growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Limits_to_Growth)* model that predicted catastrophic [resource depletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_depletion) and spurred environmentalism, postulating that capitalist technologies were the key cause responsible for environmental degradation, as opposed to human population pressures.[^cite_note-41] Environmental degradation can be seen as a class or equity issue, as environmental destruction disproportionately affects poorer communities and countries.[^cite_note-42]



Global warming was the cover story of this 2007 issue of the *[Ms.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ms._\(magazine\))* magazine.

Several left-wing or socialist groupings have an overt environmental concern and several green parties contain a strong socialist presence. For example, the [Green Party of England and Wales](79787.md) includes an eco-socialist group, the Green Left, founded in June 2005.[^cite_note-43] In Europe, several [green left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_left) political parties such as the [European United Left–Nordic Green Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_United_Left%E2%80%93Nordic_Green_Left) combine traditional social-democratic values such as a desire for greater economic equality and workers rights with demands for environmental protection. Democratic socialist [Bolivian president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivian_president) [Evo Morales](284163.md) has traced [environmental degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_degradation) to capitalist [consumerism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumerism),[^cite_note-44] stating that "\[t]he Earth does not have enough for the North to live better and better, but it does have enough for all of us to live well". [Noam Chomsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Chomsky) and [Naomi Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Klein) are among those who hold similar views.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

In [climate change mitigation](2119179.md), the Left is also divided over how to effectively and equitably reduce [carbon emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_emissions) as the center-left often advocates a reliance on market measures such as [emissions trading](37104.md) and a [carbon tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_tax) while those further to the left support direct government regulation and intervention in the form of a [Green New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_New_Deal), either alongside or instead of market mechanisms.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

### Nationalism and anti-nationalism

Main article: [Left-wing nationalism](16066160.md)

See also: [Anarchism and nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_and_nationalism), [Internationalism (politics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internationalism_\(politics\)), [Proletarian internationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletarian_internationalism), and [Anti-Stalinist left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Stalinist_left)

The question of [nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationality), [imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialism) and [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) has been a central feature of political debates on the Left. During the French Revolution, nationalism was a key policy of the Republican Left.[^cite_note-50] The Republican Left advocated for [civic nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_nationalism)[^cite_note-Knapp-13] and argued that the nation is a "daily plebiscite" formed by the subjective "will to live together". Related to [revanchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revanchism), the belligerent will to take revenge against Germany and retake control of [Alsace-Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace-Lorraine), nationalism was sometimes opposed to [imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialism). In the 1880s, there was a debate between leftists such as the [Radical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Party_\(France\)) [Georges Clemenceau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Clemenceau), the [Socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Section_of_the_Workers'_International) [Jean Jaurès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Jaur%C3%A8s) and the nationalist [Maurice Barrès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Barr%C3%A8s), who argued that colonialism diverted France from liberating the "blue line of the [Vosges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vosges)", in reference to Alsace-Lorraine; and the "[colonial lobby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire)" such as [Jules Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Ferry) of the [Moderate Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderate_Republicans_\(France,_1871%E2%80%931901\)), [Léon Gambetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9on_Gambetta) of the [Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Union_\(France\)) and [Eugène Etienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eug%C3%A8ne_Etienne), the president of the Parliamentary Colonial Group. After the [antisemitic](1078.md) [Dreyfus Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreyfus_Affair) in which officer [Alfred Dreyfus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Dreyfus) was falsely convicted of sedition and exiled to a penal colony in 1894 before being exonerated in 1906, nationalism in the form of [Boulangism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulangism) increasingly became associated with the far-right.[^cite_note-51]

The Marxist [social class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_class) theory of [proletarian internationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletarian_internationalism) asserts that members of the [working class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class) should act in solidarity with working people in other countries in pursuit of a common [class interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_interest), rather than only focusing on their own countries. Proletarian internationalism is summed up in the slogan: "[Workers of the world, unite!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers_of_the_world,_unite!)", the last line of *[The Communist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Communist_Manifesto)*. Union members had learned that more members meant more bargaining power. Taken to an international level, leftists argued that workers should act in solidarity with the international proletariat in order to further increase the power of the working class. Proletarian internationalism saw itself as a deterrent against war and international conflicts, because people with a common interest are less likely to take up arms against one another, instead focusing on fighting the [bourgeoisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeoisie) as the [ruling class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruling_class). According to Marxist theory, the [antonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonym) of proletarian internationalism is [bourgeois nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeois_nationalism). Some Marxists, together with others on the left, view [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism),[^cite_note-52] [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism)[^cite_note-53] (including antisemitism)[^cite_note-54] and [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion) as [divide and conquer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divide_and_rule) tactics used by the ruling classes to prevent the [working class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class) from uniting against them in solidarity with one another.

Anarchism has developed a critique of nationalism that focuses on nationalism's role in justifying and consolidating state power and domination. Through its unifying goal, nationalism strives for [centralisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralisation) (both in specific territories and in a ruling elite of individuals) while it prepares a population for capitalist exploitation. Within anarchism, this subject has been extensively discussed by [Rudolf Rocker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Rocker) in his book titled *[Nationalism and Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism_and_Culture)* and by the works of [Fredy Perlman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredy_Perlman) such as *[Against His-Story, Against Leviathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Against_His-Story,_Against_Leviathan)* and *The Continuing Appeal of Nationalism*.[^cite_note-55]

The failure of revolutions in [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Revolution) and [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Soviet_Republic) in the 1918–1920 years ended [Bolshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik) hopes for an imminent [world revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_revolution) and led to the promotion of the doctrine of [socialism in one country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism_in_one_country) by [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin). In the first edition of his book titled *Osnovy Leninizma* (*Foundations of Leninism*, 1924), Stalin argued that revolution in one country is insufficient. By the end of that year in the second edition of the book, he argued that the "[proletariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletariat) can and must build the socialist society in one country". In April 1925, [Nikolai Bukharin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Bukharin) elaborated on the issue in his brochure titled *Can We Build Socialism in One Country in the Absence of the Victory of the West-European Proletariat?*, whose position was adopted as state policy after Stalin's January 1926 article titled *On the Issues of Leninism* (К вопросам ленинизма) was published. This idea was opposed by [Leon Trotsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky) and his supporters, who declared the need for an international "[permanent revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_revolution)" and condemned Stalin for betraying the goals and ideals of the socialist revolution. Various [Fourth Internationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_International) groups around the world who describe themselves as [Trotskyist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trotskyist) see themselves as standing in this tradition while [Maoist China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maoist_China) formally supported the theory of socialism in one country.

European social democrats strongly support [Europeanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europeanism) and [supranational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supranational_union) integration within the [European Union](9317.md), although there is a minority of nationalists and [Eurosceptics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurosceptics) on the left. Several scholars have linked this form of left-wing nationalism to the pressure generated by economic integration with other countries, often encouraged by [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberal) [free trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade) agreements. This view is sometimes used to justify hostility towards supranational organisations.

[Left-wing nationalism](16066160.md) also can refer to any form of nationalism which emphasises a leftist working-class [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism) agenda that seeks to overcome exploitation or oppression by other nations. Many Third World [anti-colonialist movements](302084.md) have adopted leftist and socialist ideas. [Third-Worldism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third-Worldism) is a tendency within leftist thought that regards the division between [First World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World) and [Second World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World) [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country) and [Third World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_World) [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries) as being of high political importance. This tendency supports [decolonisation](302084.md) and [national liberation movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_liberation_movements). Third-Worldism is closely connected with [African socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_socialism), [Latin American socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_socialism), [Maoism](46636.md),[^cite_note-56] [pan-Africanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Africanism) and [pan-Arabism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Arabism). Several left-wing groups in the developing world such as the [Zapatista Army of National Liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapatista_Army_of_National_Liberation) in Mexico, the [Abahlali baseMjondolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abahlali_baseMjondolo) in [South Africa](17416221.md) and the [Naxalites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naxalites) in India have argued that the First World and the Second World Left takes a racist and paternalistic attitude towards liberation movements in the Third World.

### Religion

See also: [Anarchism and religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchism_and_religion), [Buddhist socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_socialism), [Christian anarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_anarchism), [Christian communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_communism), [Christian left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_left), [Christian socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_socialism), [Jewish left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_left), and [Islamic socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_socialism)

The original [French Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Left) was firmly [anti-clerical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-clerical), strongly opposing the influence of the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) and supporting [atheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism) and the [separation of church and state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_church_and_state), ushering in a policy known as *[laïcité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La%C3%AFcit%C3%A9)*.[^cite_note-Knapp-13] [Karl Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx) asserted that "religion is the sigh of the oppressed creature, the heart of a heartless world, and the soul of soulless conditions. It is the [opium of the people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_of_the_people)".[^cite_note-57] In Soviet Russia, the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks) under [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin) originally embraced an ideological principle which professed that all religion would eventually atrophy and resolved to eradicate organised [Christianity](5211.md) and other religious institutions. In 1918, 10 [Russian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox) hierarchs were summarily executed by a firing squad, and children were deprived of any religious education outside of the home.[^cite_note-58]

Today in the [Western world](21208200.md), those on the Left generally support [secularisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularization) and the separation of church and state. However, religious beliefs have also been associated with many left-wing movements such as the [progressive movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_movement), the [Social Gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Gospel) movement, the [civil rights movement](49001.md), the [anti-war movement](39766716.md), the anti-[capital punishment](5902.md) movement and [Liberation Theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Theology). Early [utopian socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopian_socialist) thinkers such as [Robert Owen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Owen), [Charles Fourier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Fourier) and the [Comte de Saint-Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comte_de_Saint-Simon) based their theories of socialism upon Christian principles. Other common leftist concerns such as [pacifism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifism), [social justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_justice), [racial equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_equality), [human rights](13831.md) and the rejection of capitalism and excessive [wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth) can be found in the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible).[^cite_note-59]

In the late 19th century, the [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) Social Gospel movement arose in the United States which integrated [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism) and [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist) thought with [Christianity](5211.md) through faith-based social activism. Other left-wing religious movements include Buddhist socialism, Jewish socialism and Islamic socialism. There have been alliances between the left and anti-war [Muslims](19541.md), such as the [Respect Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respect_Party) and the [Stop the War Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_the_War_Coalition) in Britain. In France, the left has been divided over moves to ban the [hijab](68301.md) from schools, with some leftists supporting a ban based on the separation of church and state in accordance with the principle of *[laïcité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La%C3%AFcit%C3%A9)* and other leftists opposing the prohibition based on personal and religious freedom.

Protestors in [Harlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem), New York City, express solidarity with the [Selma to Montgomery marches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selma_to_Montgomery_marches) against [racial segregation in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation_in_the_United_States), 1965

[Social progressivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_progress) is another common feature of modern leftism, particularly in the United States, where social progressives played an important role in the [abolition of slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_States),[^cite_note-60] the enshrinement of [women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage) in the [United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution),[^cite_note-61] and the protection of [civil rights](37071.md), [LGBTQ rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights), [women's rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_rights) and [multiculturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism). Progressives have both advocated for [alcohol prohibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_prohibition) legislation and worked towards its repeal in the mid to late 1920s and early 1930s. Current positions associated with social progressivism in the [Western world](21208200.md) include strong opposition to the [death penalty](5902.md), [torture](47702.md), [mass surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_surveillance), and the [war on drugs](1181646.md), and support for [abortion](765.md) rights, [cognitive liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_liberty), LGBTQ rights including legal recognition of [same-sex marriage](92656.md), [same-sex adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_adoption) of children, the [right to change one's legal gender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_reassignment), distribution of [contraceptives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraceptives), and public funding of embryonic [stem-cell research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem-cell_research). The desire for an expansion of social and civil liberties often overlaps that of the [libertarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian) movement. Public education was a subject of great interest to groundbreaking social progressives such as [Lester Frank Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Frank_Ward) and [John Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dewey), who believed that a democratic society and system of government was practically impossible without a universal and comprehensive nationwide system of education.

Various counterculture and anti-war movements in the 1960s and 1970s were associated with the New Left. Unlike the earlier leftist focus on [labour union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_union) activism and a proletarian revolution, the New Left instead adopted a broader definition of political activism commonly called [social activism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_activism). The New Left in the United States is associated with the [hippie movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippie_movement), mass protest movements on school campuses and a broadening of focus from protesting [class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_class)-based oppression to include issues such as [gender](38076.md), [race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(classification_of_human_beings\)) and [sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation). The British New Left was an intellectually driven movement which attempted to correct the perceived errors of the [Old Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Left). The New Left opposed prevailing authoritarian structures in society which it designated as "[The Establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Establishment)" and became known as the "Anti-Establishment". The New Left did not seek to recruit industrial workers en masse, but instead concentrated on a social activist approach to organization, convinced that they could be the source for a better kind of [social revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_revolution). This view has been criticised by several [Marxists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxists), especially [Trotskyists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trotskyists), who characterised this approach as "substitutionism" which they described as a misguided and non-Marxist belief that other groups in society could "substitute" for and "replace" the revolutionary agency of the working class.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

Many early [feminists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminists) and advocates of women's rights were considered a part of the Left by their contemporaries. Feminist pioneer [Mary Wollstonecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Wollstonecraft) was influenced by [Thomas Paine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Paine). Many notable leftists have been strong supporters of gender equality such as [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist) philosophers and activists [Rosa Luxemburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosa_Luxemburg), [Clara Zetkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Zetkin) and [Alexandra Kollontai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_Kollontai), [anarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist) philosophers and activists such as [Virginia Bolten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Bolten), [Emma Goldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Goldman) and [Lucía Sánchez Saornil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc%C3%ADa_S%C3%A1nchez_Saornil) and [democratic socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialist) philosophers and activists such as [Helen Keller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Keller) and [Annie Besant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Besant).[^cite_note-64] However, Marxists such as Rosa Luxemburg,[^cite_note-65] Clara Zetkin,[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] and Alexandra Kollontai,[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] who are supporters of radical social equality for women and have rejected and opposed [liberal feminism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_feminism) because they considered it to be a capitalist [bourgeois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeois) ideology. Marxists were responsible for organising the first [International Working Women's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Working_Women's_Day) events.[^cite_note-70]

The [women's liberation movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_liberation_movement) is closely connected to the New Left and other [new social movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_social_movements) which openly challenged the orthodoxies of the Old Left. Socialist feminism as exemplified by the [Freedom Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Socialist_Party) and [Radical Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Women) and [Marxist feminism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist_feminism), spearheaded by [Selma James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selma_James), saw themselves as a part of the Left that challenges male-dominated and [sexist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexist) structures within the Left. The connection between left-wing ideologies and the struggle for LGBTQ rights also has an important history. Prominent socialists who were involved in early struggles for LGBTQ rights include [Edward Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Carpenter), [Oscar Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Wilde), [Harry Hay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Hay), [Bayard Rustin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayard_Rustin) and [Daniel Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Gu%C3%A9rin), among others. The New Left is also strongly supportive of LGBTQ rights and liberation, having been instrumental in the founding of the [LGBTQ rights movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_movement) in the aftermath of the [Stonewall Riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonewall_riots) of 1969. Contemporary leftist activists and socialist countries such as Cuba are actively supportive of LGBTQ+ people and are involved in the struggle for LGBTQ+ rights and equality.

## History

See also: [American Left](28903916.md), [French Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Left), [Left–right political spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left%E2%80%93right_political_spectrum), and [History of political thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_political_thought)



The 5 May 1789 opening of the [Estates General of 1789](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estates_General_of_1789) in Versailles

Throughout the 19th century, the main line dividing [Left and Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_France) was between supporters of the French [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) and those of the [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy)'s privileges.[^cite_note-Knapp-13]<sup>: 2 </sup> The [June Days uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_Days_uprising) during the [Second Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Second_Republic) was an attempt by the Left to re-assert itself after the [1848 Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1848_Revolution_\(France\)), but only a small portion of the population supported this.

In the mid-19th century, [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism), [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism), [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) and [anti-clericalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-clericalism) became key features of the French Left. After [Napoleon III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_III)'s [1851 coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_coup_of_1851) and the subsequent establishment of the [Second Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Empire), [Marxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism) began to rival radical republicanism and [utopian socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopian_socialism) as a force within left-wing politics. The influential *[Communist Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Manifesto)* by [Karl Marx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Marx) and [Friedrich Engels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Engels), published amidst the wave of [revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848) across Europe, asserted that all of human history is defined by [class struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_struggle). They predicted that a [proletarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletarian) revolution would eventually overthrow [bourgeois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeois) [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism) and create a [stateless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stateless_society), [moneyless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-capitalism) and [classless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classless) [communist society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_society). It was in this period that the word *wing* was appended to both Left and Right.[^cite_note-oed.com-71]

&#x20;\




Depiction of the IWA's founding meeting at [St Martin's Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Martin's_Hall) in London, 1864

The [International Workingmen's Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Workingmen's_Association) (1864–1876), sometimes called the First International, brought together delegates from many different countries, with many different views about how to reach a classless and stateless society. Following a split between supporters of Marx and [Mikhail Bakunin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Bakunin), anarchists formed the [Saint-Imier International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Imier_International) and later the [International Workers' Association (IWA–AIT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Workers'_Association_%E2%80%93_Asociaci%C3%B3n_Internacional_de_los_Trabajadores).[^cite_note-72] The [Second International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_International) (1888–1916) became divided over the issue of [World War I](4764461.md). Those who opposed the war, among them [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin) and [Rosa Luxemburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosa_Luxemburg), saw themselves as further to the left.

In the United States, leftists such as [social liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_liberals), [progressives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States) and [trade unionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_unionists) were influenced by the works of [Thomas Paine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Paine), who introduced the concept of [asset-based egalitarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset-based_egalitarianism) which theorises that [social equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_equality) is possible by a redistribution of resources. After the [Reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era_of_the_United_States) era in the aftermath of the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), the phrase "the Left" was used to describe those who supported trade unions, the [civil rights movement](49001.md) and the [anti-war movement](39766716.md).[^cite_note-Van_Gosse_2005-73][^cite_note-74] More recently, *left-wing* and *right-wing* have often been used as synonyms for the [Democratic](5043544.md) and [Republican](32070.md) parties, or as synonyms for [liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_liberalism_in_the_United_States) and [conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States), respectively.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

During the 20th century, war resulted in a dramatic intensification of the pace of social changes, and was a crucial catalyst for the growth of left-wing politics.[^cite_note-Kolko_1994_xviitoxviii-79] Since the Right was populist, both in the [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Bloc) and the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc), anything viewed as avant-garde art was called leftist across Europe, thus the identification of Picasso's *[Guernica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guernica_\(Picasso\))* as "leftist" in Europe[^cite_note-80] and the condemnation of the Russian composer [Shostakovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shostakovich)'s opera (*[The Lady Macbeth of Mtsensk District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Macbeth_of_Mtsensk_\(opera\))*) in *[Pravda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pravda)* as follows: "Here we have 'leftist' confusion instead of natural, human music".[^cite_note-81]

## Types

The spectrum of left-wing politics ranges from [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left) to [far-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-left). The term *centre-left* describes a position within the political mainstream that accepts capitalism and a market economy. The term *far-left* is used for positions that are more [radical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_politics), strongly rejecting [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism) and mainstream [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy), instead advocating for a socialist society based on [economic democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_democracy) and [direct democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_democracy), representing economic, political and social democracy. The centre-left includes [social democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democrats), [social liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_liberals), [progressives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism) and [greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_politics). Centre-left supporters accept market allocation of resources in a [mixed economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_economy) with an empowered [public sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_sector) and a thriving [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector). Centre-left policies tend to favour limited [state intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_intervention) in matters pertaining to the [public interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_interest).

In several countries, the terms *far-left* and *radical left* have been associated with many varieties of [anarchism](12.md), [autonomism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomism) and [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism). They have been used to describe groups that advocate [anti-capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-capitalism) and [eco-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco-terrorism). In France, a distinction is made between the centre-left and the left represented by the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(France\)) and the [French Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Communist_Party) and the far-left as represented by [anarcho-communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-communists), [Maoists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maoists) and [Trotskyists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trotskyists).[^cite_note-82] The [United States Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) defines "left-wing extremism" as groups that "seek to bring about change through violent revolution, rather than through established political processes".[^cite_note-83] Similar to [far-right politics](221220.md), extreme far-left politics have motivated [political violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_violence), [radicalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radicalization), [genocide](12441.md), [terrorism](30636.md), sabotage and damage to property, the formation of [militant](310790.md) organisations, [political repression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_repression), [conspiracism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracism), [xenophobia](34141.md), and [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism).[^cite_note-Rossi_2021-84][^cite_note-Ojeili-Taylor_2018-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

In China, the term *[Chinese New Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_New_Left)* denotes those who oppose the [reform and opening up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up) enacted by [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping) in the 1980s and 1990s, favour instead the restoration of Maoist policies and the immediate transition to a socialist economy.[^cite_note-89] In the [Western world](21208200.md), the term *New Left* is used for social and cultural politics.

In the United Kingdom during the 1980s, the term *[hard left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_left)* was applied to supporters of [Tony Benn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Benn) such as the [Campaign Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaign_Group) and those involved in the *[London Labour Briefing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Briefing)* newspaper as well as Trotskyist groups such as [Militant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militant_\(Trotskyist_group\)) and the [Alliance for Workers' Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_Workers'_Liberty).[^cite_note-90] In the same period, the term *[soft left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_left)* was applied to supporters of the British [Labour Party](19279158.md) who were perceived to be more moderate and closer to the centre, accepting [Keynesianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keynesianism). Under the leadership of [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair) and [Gordon Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Brown), the Labour Party adopted the [Third Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Way) and rebranded itself as [New Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Labour) in order to promote the notion that it was less left-wing than it had been in the past to accommodate the [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberal) trend arising since the 1970s with the [displacement of Keynesianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_of_Keynesianism) and [post-war social democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-war_social_democracy). One of the first actions of [Ed Miliband](1290018.md), the Labour Party leader who succeeded Blair and Brown, was the rejection of the New Labour label and a promise to abandon the Third Way and turn back to the left. However, Labour's voting record in the [House of Commons](13828.md) from 2010 to 2015 indicated that the Labour Party under Miliband had maintained the same distance from the left as it did under Blair.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] In contrast, the election of [Jeremy Corbyn](415036.md) as the Labour Party leader was viewed by scholars and political commentators as Labour turning back toward its more classical socialist roots, rejecting neoliberalism and the Third Way whilst supporting a [democratic socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialist) society and an end to [austerity](684037.md) measures.
