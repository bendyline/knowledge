In a multicellular [organism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism), an **organ** is a collection of [tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_\(biology\)) joined in a structural unit to serve a common [function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_\(biology\)).[^cite_note-1] In the [hierarchy of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_organization), an organ lies between [tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_\(biology\)) and an [organ system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_system). Tissues are formed from same type [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_\(biology\)) to act together in a function. Tissues of different types combine to form an organ which has a specific function. The [intestinal wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrointestinal_tract), for example, is formed by [epithelial tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithelial_tissue) and [smooth muscle tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_muscle_tissue).[^cite_note-Kent-2] Two or more organs working together in the execution of a specific body function form an organ system, also called a [biological system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_system) or body system.

An organ's tissues are broadly classified into [parenchyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parenchyma), the functional tissue, and [stroma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroma_\(tissue\)), the structural tissue with supportive, connective, or [ancillary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancillary) functions. For example, the [gland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gland) tissue that produces [hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone) is the [parenchyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parenchyma), while the stroma includes the [nerves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_tissue) that innervate the parenchyma, the [blood vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_vessel) that oxygenate and nourish it and remove [metabolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism) wastes, and the [connective tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connective_tissue) that provide structure, placement, and anchoring. The primary tissues that form an organ generally have common [embryologic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryology) origins, often arising from the same [germ layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_layer). Organs are present in most multicellular [organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism). In [single-celled organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicellular_organism) such as [eukaryotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eukaryotes), the [functional analogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analogy_\(biology\)) of an organ is an [organelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organelle). In plants, there are three main organs.[^cite_note-Botany-3] The number of organs in any organism depends on the definition used. There are approximately 79 organs in the human body; the exact number remains debated.[^cite_note-4]

## Animals

See also: [List of organs of the human body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_organs_of_the_human_body) and [Biological system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_system)



The [liver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver) and [gallbladder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallbladder) of a [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep)

Except for [placozoans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placozoa), [multicellular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multicellular) [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal) including humans have a variety of [organ systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_system). These specific systems are widely studied in [human anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_anatomy). The functions of these organ systems often share significant overlap. For instance, the [nervous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervous_system) and [endocrine system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrine_system) both operate via a shared organ, the [hypothalamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalamus). For this reason, the two systems are combined and studied as the [neuroendocrine system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroendocrine_system). The same is true for the [musculoskeletal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musculoskeletal_system) because of the relationship between the [muscular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscular_system) and [skeletal systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeletal_system).

- [Cardiovascular system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_system): pumping and channeling [blood](3997.md) to and from the [body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_body) and [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung) with [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart), [blood](3997.md) and [blood vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_vessel).

- [Digestive system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digestion): [digestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digestion) and processing food with [salivary glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salivary_gland), [esophagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esophagus), [stomach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach), [liver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver), [gallbladder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallbladder), [pancreas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancreas), [intestines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intestine), [colon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colon_\(anatomy\)), [mesentery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesentery), [rectum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectum) and [anus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anus).

- [Endocrine system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrine_system): communication within the body using [hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone) made by [endocrine glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrine_gland) such as the [hypothalamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalamus), [pituitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituitary) gland, [pineal body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineal_body) or pineal gland, [thyroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroid), [parathyroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parathyroid) and [adrenals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenal), i.e., adrenal glands.

- [Excretory system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excretory_system): [kidneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_\(vertebrates\)), [ureters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ureter), [bladder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urinary_bladder) and [urethra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urethra) involved in fluid balance, [electrolyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrolyte) balance and excretion of [urine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urine).

- [Lymphatic system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphatic_system): structures involved in the transfer of [lymph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymph) between [tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_\(biology\)) and the [blood stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_stream), the lymph and the [nodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymph_node) and [vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymph_vessel) that transport it including the [immune system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_system): defending against [disease](8072.md)-causing agents with [leukocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukocyte), [tonsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonsil), [adenoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenoid), [thymus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thymus) and [spleen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spleen).

- [Integumentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integumentary_system): [skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin), [hair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hair) and [nails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nail_\(anatomy\)) of [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal). Also [scales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_\(anatomy\)) of [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish), [reptiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptile), and [birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird), and [feathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feather) of birds.

- [Muscular system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscular_system): movement with [muscles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle).

- [Nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervous_system): collecting, transferring and processing information with [brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain), [spinal cord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_cord) and [nerves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve).

- [Reproductive system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_system): the [sex organs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_organ), such as [ovaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovary), [oviducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oviduct), [uterus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterus), [vulva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulva), [vagina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vagina), [testicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testicle), [vasa deferentia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasa_deferentia), [seminal vesicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminal_vesicle), [prostate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostate) and [penis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penis).

- [Respiratory system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_system): the organs used for [breathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breathing), the [pharynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharynx), [larynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larynx), [trachea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertebrate_trachea), [bronchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchi), [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung) and [diaphragm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_diaphragm).

- [Skeletal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton): structural support and protection with [bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone), [cartilage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartilage), [ligaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligament) and [tendons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tendon).

In the study of [anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomy), **viscera** (sing.: **viscus**) refers to the **internal organs** of the [abdominal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdominal_cavity), [thoracic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_cavity), and [pelvic cavities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelvic_cavity).[^cite_note-Bell-5] The abdominal organs may be classified as **solid organs** or **hollow organs**. The solid organs are the [liver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver), [pancreas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancreas), [spleen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spleen), [kidneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney), and [adrenal glands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenal_gland). The hollow organs of the abdomen are the [stomach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach), [intestines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intestines), [gallbladder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallbladder), [bladder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bladder), and [rectum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectum).[^cite_note-6] In the [thoracic cavity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoracic_cavity), the [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart) is a hollow, muscular organ.[^cite_note-7] [Splanchnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splanchnology) is the study of the viscera.[^cite_note-MW-8] The term "visceral" is contrasted with the term "[parietal](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/parietal)", meaning "of or relating to the wall of a body part, [organ or cavity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_cavity)".[^cite_note-Inria-9] The two terms are often used in describing a membrane or piece of connective tissue, referring to the opposing sides.[^cite_note-Thoracic_cavity-10]

### Origin and evolution



Relationship of major animal lineages with indication of how long ago these animals shared a common ancestor. On the left, important organs are shown, which allows us to determine how long ago these may have evolved.

The organ level of organisation in [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal) can be first detected in [flatworms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatworm) and the more derived [phyla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylum), i.e. the [bilaterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilateria). The less-advanced [taxa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxon) (i.e. *[Placozoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placozoa)*, *[Porifera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porifera)*, *[Ctenophora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ctenophora)* and *[Cnidaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cnidaria)*) do not show unification of their tissues into organs.

More complex animals are composed of different organs, which have evolved over time. For example, the liver and heart evolved in the [chordates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chordate) about 550–500 million years ago, while the gut and brain are even more ancient, arising in the ancestor of vertebrates, insects, [molluscs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollusca), and worms about 700–650 million years ago.

Given the ancient origin of most vertebrate organs, researchers have looked for model systems, where organs have evolved more recently, and ideally have evolved multiple times independently. An outstanding model for this kind of research is the [placenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placenta), which has evolved more than 100 times independently in vertebrates, has evolved relatively recently in some lineages, and exists in intermediate forms in extant taxa.[^cite_note-Griffith-11] Studies on the evolution of the placenta have identified a variety of genetic and physiological processes that contribute to the origin and evolution of organs, these include the re-purposing of existing animal tissues, the acquisition of new functional properties by these tissues, and novel interactions of distinct tissue types.[^cite_note-Griffith-11]

## Plants

See also: [Plant morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_morphology), [Plant anatomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_anatomy), and [Plant physiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_physiology)



The flower is the angiosperm's reproductive organ. This *[Hibiscus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibiscus)* flower is [bisexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisexual_flower), and it contains [stamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamen) and [pistils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gynoecium).



Strobilus of *[Equisetum telmateia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equisetum_telmateia)*

The study of plant organs is covered in [plant morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_morphology). Organs of [plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant) can be divided into vegetative and reproductive structures. Vegetative plant organs include [roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root), [stems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_stem), and [leaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaf).[^cite_note-12] Reproductive organs are variable, and in [flowering plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flowering_plant), they are represented by the [flower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flower), [seed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed) and [fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit).[^cite_note-13] In [conifers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinophyta), the organ that bears the reproductive structures is called a [cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conifer_cone). In other divisions ([phyla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylum)) of plants, the reproductive organs are called [strobili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strobili), in *[Lycopodiophyta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycopodiophyta)*, or simply gametophores in [mosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss). Common organ system designations in plants include the differentiation of shoot and root. All parts of the plant above ground (in non-[epiphytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphyte)), including the functionally distinct leaf and flower organs, may be classified together as the shoot organ system.

The vegetative organs are essential for maintaining the life of a plant. While there can be 11 organ systems in animals, there are far fewer in plants, where some perform the vital functions, such as [photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthesis), while the reproductive organs are essential in [reproduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_sexuality). However, if there is [asexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asexual_reproduction) [vegetative reproduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetative_reproduction), the vegetative organs are those that create the new generation of plants (see [clonal colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clonal_colony)).

## Society and culture

Many societies have a system for [organ donation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_donation), in which a living or deceased donor's organ are [transplanted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_transplant) into a person with a failing organ. The transplantation of larger solid organs often requires [immunosuppression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunosuppression) to prevent [organ rejection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transplant_rejection) or [graft-versus-host disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graft-versus-host_disease).

There is considerable interest throughout the world in creating [laboratory-grown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_engineering) or [artificial organs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_organ).

### Organ transplants

Beginning in the 20th century,[^cite_note-Organ_Gov-14] [organ transplants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_transplantation) began to take place as scientists knew more about the anatomy of organs. These came later in time as procedures were often dangerous and difficult.[^cite_note-15] Both the source and method of obtaining the organ to transplant are major ethical issues to consider, and because organs as resources for transplant are always more limited than demand for them, various notions of justice, including [distributive justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributive_justice), are developed in the ethical analysis. This situation continues as long as transplantation relies upon organ donors rather than technological innovation, testing, and industrial manufacturing.

Animal donor organs and tissue have been subjects of study since the 1960s, and some [xenotransplant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenotransplant) tissues, particularly heart valves, have been commonly utilized. Xenotransplant has the potential to address the critical shortage in organ grafts. The science behind xenotransplant trials has advanced considerably and more human clinical trials utilizing porcine xenografts are quickly approaching.[^cite_note-Xenotransplantation-16]

The English word "organ" dates back to the twelfth century, referring to any musical instrument.[^cite_note-17] By the late 14th century, the musical term's meaning had narrowed to refer specifically to the [keyboard-based instrument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_\(music\)). At the same time, a second meaning arose, in reference to a "body part adapted to a certain function".[^cite_note-18]

Plant organs are made from tissue composed of different types of tissue. The three tissue types are ground, vascular, and dermal.[^cite_note-19] When three or more organs are present, it is called an organ system.[^cite_note-20]

The adjective *[visceral](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/visceral)*, also *[splanchnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splanchnic)*, is used for anything pertaining to the internal organs. Historically, viscera of animals were examined by [Roman](25458.md) pagan [priests](23707.md) like the [haruspices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haruspices) or the [augurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augur) in order to divine the future by their shape, dimensions or other factors.[^cite_note-21] This practice remains an important ritual in some remote, tribal societies.

The term "visceral" is contrasted with the term "[parietal](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/parietal)", meaning "of or relating to the wall of a body part, [organ or cavity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_cavity)"[^cite_note-Inria-9] The two terms are often used in describing a membrane or piece of connective tissue, referring to the opposing sides.[^cite_note-Thoracic_cavity-10]

### Antiquity

[Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle) used the word frequently in his philosophy, both to describe the organs of plants or animals (e.g. the roots of a tree, the heart or liver of an animal) because, in ancient Greek, the word '*organon*' means 'tool', and Aristotle believed that the organs of the body were tools for us by means of which we can do things. For similar reasons, his logical works, taken as a whole, are referred to as the *[Organon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organon)* because logic is a *tool* for philosophical thinking.[^cite_note-22] Earlier thinkers, such as those who wrote texts in the [Hippocratic corpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocratic_corpus), generally did not believe that there were organs of the body but only different *parts* of the body.[^cite_note-23]

Some alchemists (e.g. [Paracelsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracelsus)) adopted the [Hermetic Qabalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermetic_Qabalah) assignment between the seven vital organs and the seven [classical planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_planets) as follows: [^cite_note-24]

| Planet | Organ |
| --- | --- |
| Sun | Heart |
| Moon | Brain |
| Mercury | Lungs |
| Venus | Kidneys |
| Mars | Gall bladder |
| Jupiter | Liver |
| Saturn | Spleen |

[Chinese traditional medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_traditional_medicine) recognizes eleven organs, associated with the [five Chinese traditional elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxing_\(Chinese_philosophy\)) and with [yin and yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yin_and_yang), as follows:

| Element | Yin/yang | Organ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wood | yin | liver |
| yang | gall bladder |  |
| Fire | yin | heart |
| yang | small intestine / [san jiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_jiao) |  |
| Earth | yin | spleen |
| yang | stomach |  |
| Metal | yin | lungs |
| yang | large intestine |  |
| Water | yin | kidneys |
| yang | bladder |  |

The Chinese associated the five elements with the five planets (Jupiter, Mars, Venus, Saturn, and Mercury) similar to the way the classical planets were associated with different metals. The *yin* and *yang* distinction approximates the modern notion of solid and hollow organs.
