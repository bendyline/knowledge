**Safi**,[^cite_note-2] commonly known as **Asfi**, is a city in western [Morocco](19291.md) on the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md). It is the capital of [Safi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safi_Province). It recorded a population of 346,000 as of 2024.[^cite_note-census2014-1] The city was occupied by the [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) from 1488 to 1541, was the center of Morocco's [weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving) industry, and became a *fortaleza* of the Portuguese Crown in 1508.[^cite_note-3] Safi is the main fishing port for the country's [sardine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardine) industry, and also exports [phosphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate), textiles and [ceramics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery). During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), Safi was the site of [Operation Blackstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Blackstone), one of the landing sites for [Operation Torch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Torch).

## Etymology

The origin of the name Safi is likely derived from the [Berber language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_language). According to the scholar and historian Muhammad bin Ahmad al-Abdi al-Kanuni, the earliest mention of the city of Safi was in the book Geography by [Claudius Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius_Ptolemy) in the first section of the second part of the known world, referred to as "Taisapha." It appears that the name has Berber roots from the word "Asfo," pronounced by some as "Asfi," meaning light or bright. The city may have been named due to the lighthouses built on the shores to guide ships in their navigation. [^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

The first mention of the city in Islamic sources is found in the book Routes and Realms by the geographer Abu Ubayd al-Bakri. He explicitly states that the name of the city Safi is derived from the Berber word "Asif," meaning a temporary watercourse.[^cite_note-6] Indeed, a stream known as Wadi Shaaba still runs through the city and flows into it. Ahmad Toufiq similarly states that the name is Berber, meaning river mouth, and in the Mozabite dialect, a water basin filled by a waterwheel is called "Asfi."[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

In his book Nuzhat al-Mushtaq, Al-Idrisi connects the origin of the name to a story of young adventurers who set out from [Lisbon](18091.md) and sailed the dark sea until they reached the location of the city of Safi. When a group of [Berbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) saw them and learned of their ordeal, their leader exclaimed 'Wa Asfi', Arabic for regret, in sympathy for what they endured, and thus the place was named "Asfi."[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## History

According to historians Henri Basset and Robert Ricard, Safi was not a very ancient city.[^cite_note-\:0523-12] It was mentioned in the writings of [al-Bakri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bakri) in the 11th century and of [al-Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_al-Idrisi) in the 12th century.[^cite_note-\:0523-12] According to Moroccan historian Mohammed al-Kanuni, Safi can be identified with the ancient [Thymiaterium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thymiaterium) or Carcunticus that was founded by the [Carthaginian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthago) admiral [Hanno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanno_the_Navigator) during his [Periplus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periplus_of_Hanno), as related by [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder).[^cite_note-13]

Al-Idrisi mentions Safi as a busy port in the 12th century.[^cite_note-\:0523-12] At this time it served as a port for [Marrakesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marrakesh), the capital of the [Almoravids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravids) and the subsequent [Almohads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohads), replacing the port of Ribat Kuz (present-day Souira Kedima) that had served as the main port for [Aghmat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghmat) in the previous century.[^cite_note-14]

Safi was part of the [Barghawata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barghawata) state, a confederation of [ Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) tribes with a distinct religious and political structure. [^cite_note-15] This influential group in the region, resisted multiple invasions before eventually succumbing first to the [Almoravids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravids) and then being definitively eliminated by the [Almohads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohads) by 1149.[^cite_note-16]

The city came under [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) control in 1488 and remained under Portuguese rule until 1541.[^cite_note-17] The Portuguese military occupation of Safi began in 1508, and King [John III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_III_of_Portugal) decided to abandon the fortified strongholds of Safi and [Azemmour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azemmour) in 1541 because of the pressure of the [Saadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadi_dynasty) sharifs and the shortage of financial and human resources needed to defend Portuguese positions in Morocco.[^cite_note-18] Surviving Portuguese-era fortifications in Safi include the [Ksar el-Bahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Castle_\(Safi\)), also known as the Sea Castle, and the [Kechla Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kechla_Fortress).[^cite_note-19].

After the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) evacuation in 1541, Safi remained an important Moroccan Atlantic port. A modern historical study describes Safi in the following period as one of Morocco's safest and most easily usable ports.[^cite_note-20] Its role was linked to [Marrakesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marrakesh), for which Safi had long served as the main port and as a point of contact for European ambassadors and traders.[^cite_note-21]

Louis de Chénier, who resided in Safi in 1767 after the Franco-Moroccan peace treaty, wrote that the city had long been a centre of regular commerce with Europe and had a roadstead where ships could anchor safely, although vessels had to leave it in winter during southerly or south-westerly winds.[^cite_note-22]

[Bidé de Maurville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois-Joseph-Hippolyte_Bid%C3%A9_de_Maurville) a [French Navy](383256.md) later published his account of the 1765 [Larache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larache) expedition, *Relation de l'affaire de Larache*, in 1775.[^cite_note-23] Mid-eighteenth-century sources show Safi's continuing role in European trade: a French consular letter cited in *Archives marocaines* stated that English and Dutch merchants carried out almost all the trade of [Sainte-Croix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agadir) and much of that of Safi and [Salé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sal%C3%A9), while French merchants did little trade there.[^cite_note-24] The same survey states that the monopoly of trade in Safi and Sainte-Croix had been granted to the Danes in 1751.[^cite_note-25]

After Sultan [Mohammed ben Abdallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_ben_Abdallah) founded [Mogador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essaouira) in 1765 as a port intended to open Morocco to foreign commerce, the new city assumed a major commercial and fiscal role. To control maritime trade, the sultan closed the southern coast to European traders and obliged European consuls at [Safi](1560340.md), [Agadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agadir) and [Rabat](57400.md) to move to Mogador, where southern mercantile activities were concentrated.[^cite_note-26] This policy reduced Safi's former role as one of Morocco's leading Atlantic trade ports.[^cite_note-27]

Safi's patron saint is [Abu Mohammed Salih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Mohammed_Salih).

During [Operation Torch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Torch) in [World War II](32927.md), American forces landed at Safi on 8 November 1942 as part of [Operation Blackstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Blackstone). Safi was selected because its port could receive tank-carrying vessels for the advance on [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca).[^cite_note-28] The French garrison surrendered that afternoon, and American forces controlled the city and port eleven hours after landing.[^cite_note-29]

The [OCP Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OCP_Group), a cornerstone of Morocco's phosphate industry, established its Safi chemical complex in 1965.[^cite_note-30] This site, one of the earliest in OCP’s expansion into chemical processing, significantly contributes to the local economy by processing phosphate rock sourced from [Benguerir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguerir). Equipped with facilities for fertilizer production, phosphoric acid manufacturing, phosphate washing, and a [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid) plant, the Safi complex is a major industrial hub. In 2016, it achieved record production levels, producing 1.5 million tonnes of phosphoric acid and 832,600 tonnes of triple superphosphate [TSP fertilizer.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocalcium_phosphate), catering to both domestic and international markets. Founded in 1920, OCP is now the world's largest fertilizer manufacturer, controlling over 70% of global phosphate reserves. It employs 23,000 people and generated \$5.884 billion in 2018 revenue, emphasizing the Safi complex's significant role[^cite_note-31] The company holds a 31% market share of the world phosphate product market.[^cite_note-32]

## Infrastructure



Harbour access

Safi has seen significant advancements in its infrastructure aimed at supporting its economic growth and sustainability. The Safi Industrial Port, one of the key developments, has undergone substantial expansion as part of the national port strategy to enhance Morocco's infrastructure by 2030. This expansion, which began in 2014 and was completed in 2017 by SGTM-STFA, a construction firm, included enhancements to the port's dock facilities. These improvements are essential for the new [thermal power plant](2549543.md), which relies on coal to meet the energy demands of [OCP Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OCP_Group). Ciments du Maroc, a subsidiary of [Heidelberg Materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heidelberg_Materials), supplied approximately 210,000 tonnes of marine cement for this project, underscoring its scale and impact on the local economy, particularly in the energy and chemical sectors.[^cite_note-33] In addition to port development, the OCP Group is spearheading the construction of new modular seawater [desalination](156787.md) plants. These facilities are designed to significantly increase the supply of clean drinking water to Safi and the surrounding areas. Once operational, the plants will have a total annual capacity of 110 million cubic meters, with Safi benefiting from up to 75 million cubic meters. This project is critical in providing a sustainable source of unconventional water, catering to the growing demands of the area's industrial sites and improving water availability for over 1.5 million local residents and workers.[^cite_note-34]

According to the 2024 Moroccan census, 98.9% of the population of Safi spoke [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) as their native language, whereas 1% spoke [Berber languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages) natively.[^cite_note-39]

### Population

Historically, the population of the Safi region included both [Amazigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazigh_people) and [Arab](2185.md) tribal groups. Historian Yassir Benhima describes the medieval population of the region as originally Amazigh, and notes the gradual settlement of Arab tribal groups from the late 12th century. Early 16th-century Portuguese records also mention the Arabs of the [Abda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abda_\(Morocco\)) in Safi's hinterland.[^cite_note-40]

Safi also had an important [Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_Jews). On the eve of [World War I](4764461.md), the city had approximately 2,500 Jewish inhabitants out of no more than 25,000 people. The community numbered more than 3,600 in 1936 and about 4,500 in 1951, before declining after Moroccan independence because of emigration to Europe, Canada and [Israel](351513.md).[^cite_note-41]

## Economy



Pottery from Safi

In the early 20th century, the Moroccan potter [Boujemâa Lamali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boujem%C3%A2a_Lamali?action=edit\&redlink=1) established a pottery school in Safi, supported by the colonial administration. Since then pottery has been a mainstay of Safi's economy. Prior to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) there were 2,000 registered artisans working in the city's 212 workshops, and thousands more unregistered artisans.[^cite_note-42]

## Sport

[Football](10568.md), [rugby union](25405.md) and tennis are represented in Safi. The city's main football club is [Olympic Club Safi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Club_Safi), which returned to Morocco's top division for the 2004–05 season after winning promotion in 2004.[^cite_note-43] The city's rugby side, Olympique de Safi, has won the Moroccan rugby [Coupe du Trône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_du_Tr%C3%B4ne) more than once, including the 2005–06 title; *Le Matin* also noted that the club had won both the cup and the national championship the previous season.[^cite_note-44] Safi also has an affiliated tennis club, the Tennis Association de Safi, listed by the [Royal Moroccan Tennis Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Moroccan_Tennis_Federation).[^cite_note-45]

## European cemetery

There is an abandoned European cemetery in Safi. Some of the marble decorations have been stolen from the richest tombs, including: Russian, Portuguese, Spanish (e.g. the Do Carmo family), Italian (e.g. the Bormioli family), French (e.g., the Chanel family), German and other European nationals. Some engravings identifying or memorializing the deceased have also been stolen. Although there are 19th century tombs present, most are of pre-independence (1956) 20th century origin.
