**Club de Fútbol Monterrey Rayados, A.C.**, also known as **Rayados de Monterrey**, is a professional [football](10568.md) club based in the [Monterrey metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterrey_metropolitan_area), Nuevo León. The club competes in [Liga MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_MX), the top division of [Mexican football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_football_league_system), and plays its home matches at [Estadio BBVA](17130610.md).[^cite_note-2] Founded in 1945, it is the oldest active professional club from the [Northern Mexico](48157875.md), since 1999 the club has been owned by [FEMSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEMSA). Nicknamed *Rayados* stems from the club's traditional navy blue striped uniform, which is reflected in the club's crest.

Domestically, CF Monterrey has won five Liga MX titles and three [Copa MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_MX) titles. Internationally, it has won five [CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_Cup) titles and one [CONCACAF Cup Winners Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Cup_Winners_Cup). The club finished in third place twice in the [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup) ([2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_FIFA_Club_World_Cup) and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIFA_Club_World_Cup)). In 2020, the club became the second Mexican club to complete the [treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treble_\(association_football\)) after winning the Liga MX, Copa MX and CONCACAF Champions League titles.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-4]

The club's oldest and biggest rival is [Tigres UANL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigres_UANL) of the [Autonomous University of Nuevo León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_University_of_Nuevo_Le%C3%B3n). The derby between the two, known as the *[Clásico Regiomontano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cl%C3%A1sico_Regiomontano)*, is considered to be one of the most heated and intensely competed rivalries in Mexican football; both teams consistently rank among the highest in attendance[^cite_note-5] and regularly feature among the most expensively assembled squads in the country.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## History

### 1940s–50s



Team lineup in 1945.

Club de Football Monterrey was founded on 28 June 1945, near the end of [World War II](32927.md) by a group of industrial businessmen headed by Ramón Cárdenas Coronado, Enrique Ayala Medina, Paul C. Probert, Rogelio Cantú Gómez and Miguel Margáín Zozaya.

The team's nickname was popularly accepted, after the team's uniform, which is traditionally white with navy blue vertical stripes. Although the original uniform was white with a diagonal blue upper shoulder, the stripes were inspired in 1965, when the *Tampico Madero* (nicknamed "Jaibas Bravas", or Brave crabs) football team wore them, and the Monterrey team adopted them. Since then, the home uniform consists of vertical blue and white striped jerseys with blue shorts.

In its first professional game, played on 19 August 1945 against San Sebastián de León, Monterrey won 1–0, with José "Che" Gómez scoring the winner. That joy quickly came to an end, first by losing 6–0 to Montezuma, and then having the club's travelling bus involved in a tragic accident in the [San Juan de los Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_de_los_Lagos) roads that would take the lives of many of the club's players and had a big impact on the surviving players. The other Mexican clubs in solidarity loaned players to Monterrey to continue playing the tournament, but the club struggled nevertheless; they lost 21 games in a row and conceded 121 goals that year, finishing last in the league. Due to these events, the club decided to stop playing in the league in 1946 in honor of the players who died.

It was not until 1952 when the club resumed action thanks to Dr. Carlos Canseco, president of the Asociación de Fútbol de Nuevo León. The club enrolled in the second division and just 4 years later the club earned promotion to the top division. Once again the joy was short-lived, when the club finished last in their first year back and was relegated once again to the second division after finishing with a record of 4 wins, 7 draws and 13 losses for a total of 15 points, just 1 short of [Zacatepec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zacatepec) who earned their permanence in the category. The club would once again earn the promotion in the 1959–60 season, and haven't been relegated since then.&#x20;

### 1960s

The club started off the 1960s in bad shape, barely avoiding relegation with only 2 more points than Club Celaya, who had 19 points, in the 1960–61 season.

In 1960, C.F. Monterrey got into the first division. 20 March 1960, with a score of 2–0, Monterrey beat the Tigers. The lineup that had won that title was: Agustin "Chiras" Prieto, Humberto Gama, Pablo Thompson, Héctor Uzal, and Jesús "La Chuta" Medina; Eugenio Almirón and Angel Lama; Julián Briseño, Gustavo "Gato" Cuenca, José Antonio Juárez, and Nicolás "Pipo" Téllez. Once, while still in the second division, they also won a championship on 12 June 1960, after defeating Ovideo de Texcoco 1–0, the goal being scored by Agustin "Chiras" Prieto, who was one of the club's top 3 goal scorers during that year.&#x20;

In the 1963–64 season the club improved their performance and would finish 3rd in the league just 5 points behind champions [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.D._Guadalajara) who had 37 points. In the following two seasons the club would finish third and fourth respectively, narrowly missing out on clinching titles by only a few points. The 1966–67 season was mediocre, as the club finished tied for 8th in the league with [Irapuato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.D._Irapuato) on 30 points each. This season is also remembered for [Jabatos de Nuevo Leon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabatos_de_Nuevo_Leon)'s promotion to the first division, which meant 2 clubs from [Monterrey](40455.md) would be participating in the first division However, the following season was even worse, with the club finishing 14th, only four points from relegation.

### 1970s

In 1970 the tournament was split into 2 short tournaments, due to the [1970 World Cup](157227.md) that was taking part in Mexico. In the first stage, the club was placed in group 1 where they managed to finish 2nd with 17 points, one less than group leader [Toluca](1361541.md). In the second stage the club finished 7th of eight teams. The following year Monterrey finished runner up to [Club América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Am%C3%A9rica) who went on to win the league title that year against Toluca.



Portuguese football legend [Eusébio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eus%C3%A9bio) briefly played for the club in 1975

In the 1971–72 tournament the club qualified for the play-offs which had been introduced a few years back. The club would lose in quarterfinals to [Club América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Am%C3%A9rica) 2–1 on aggregate. The following year the club missed out on the playoffs, finishing tied for 5th with [Veracruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiburones_Rojos_de_Veracruz) and [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.D._Guadalajara) each with 32 points. The following season the club managed to qualify for the quarterfinals where they once again were eliminated, this time by [Atlético Español](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Espa%C3%B1ol) 5–6 on aggregate.

Monterrey played its first match in international tournaments on 5 July 1975 in the [1975 CONCACAF Champions' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_CONCACAF_Champions'_Cup) when they defeated Canadian club [Serbian White Eagles FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_White_Eagles_FC) by a score of 2–0.[^cite_note-8]

In the 1975–76 tournament the club finished in first place with a total of 44 points by means of 16 wins, 12 draws and 10 losses. In quarterfinals the club played [Cruz Azul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruz_Azul) and won 7–2 on aggregate. In the semi-finals the club played against Guadalajara who eliminated them, 2–3 on aggregate. During 1975, the Portuguese superstar [Eusébio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eus%C3%A9bio) played for the club.

In the 1976–77 and 1977–78 seasons, the club failed to qualify for the playoffs, finishing 4th both times. In the 1978–79 tournament the club once again qualified to the playoffs finishing 1st in group one with a total of 40 points by means of 14 wins 12 draws and 12 losses. This time a short tournament was played by the best 8 teams in the league who were then split into 2 groups. Monterrey was placed in group 2 along with [Pumas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumas_UNAM), [Tigres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigres_UANL) and [Zacatepec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Zacatepec). After 6 rounds of play the club finished in 3rd place with 6 points, just 2 points behind Pumas who went on to lose against Cruz Azul, the winners of the other group.&#x20;

In the following season the club finished 3rd in group 1 with 34 points but failed to qualify for the playoffs again. The decade came to an end with "Rayados" having shown great effort, as they qualified a couple times for the playoffs, but failed to win their first league title.

### 1980s

1986 was the first time that Monterrey won the [Liga MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_MX) title. Due to the [1986 FIFA World Cup](157231.md), the Mexican league was split in two short tournaments (as they are today): PRODE 86 and México 86. In México 86, the team was commanded by the coach Francisco Avilán and ended at the top spot of the Group 2 and qualified to the Liguilla with [Tampico Madero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampico_Madero), [Cruz Azul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruz_Azul) and [Chivas Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chivas_Guadalajara). In the Liguilla, thrashed 6–0 in aggregate against [Atlante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlante_F.C.) in the quarter-finals and eliminated Guadalajara by 2–0 aggregated at semi-finals. In the final, the Rayados won the title defeating Tampico Madero by 3–2 on agregate.

In the year 1989, the anthem of Rayados was created by composer [Luis Aguilé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Aguil%C3%A9). It is considered by Monterrey's fans as one of the symbols of identity of the team.[^cite_note-9]

### 1990s

At the beginning of the decade, Monterrey signed two notable players, [Carlos Hermosillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Hermosillo) and [Manuel Negrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Negrete_Arias), for the 1990–91 season. The next season, they won their first [Copa MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_MX) after defeating [Juarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.F._Cobras_de_Quer%C3%A9taro) 4–2 in the Estadio Tecnológico and then went on to reach the league final of the [1992–93 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_Mexican_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_season), losing to [Atlante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlante_F.C.).

While the early years of the decade seemed promising for Monterrey, the latter half of the decade would prove to be a disappointing one, as financial problems started to become a problem for the club. During this period, the club sold many players who would go on to have successful careers with other teams like [Ramón Morales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Morales) and [Sinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinha_\(footballer\)). Young talent [Jesus Arellano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jes%C3%BAs_Arellano) was sold to Guadalajara in 1997, though he returned to the club in 2000 and spent the next eleven years as captain before retiring in 2011.

One of the most memorable moments of this decade was the so-called "Clasico del Descenso" in the [1995–96 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_Mexican_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_season). On 24 March 1996, Monterrey defeated their arch-rivals Tigres 2–1 at [Estadio Universitario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Universitario_\(UANL\)). Tigres were facing a relegation battle after years of poor results, and needed a victory to avoid being relegated; the defeat meant that Tigres would be relegated for the first and so far only time in their history to the [Segunda División](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segunda_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico).[^cite_note-10]

In 1999, Monterrey was facing a fierce relegation battle of its own against [Puebla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puebla_F.C.), culminating on 9 May 1999, when the teams faced each other at the Estadio Tecnológico to decide which team would get relegated. Monterrey only needed a draw to stay in the Primera División, and with a memorable performance from [Francisco Javier "El Abuelo" Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Javier_Cruz) who had played a vital role in Monterrey's first title win thirteen years before, Monterrey managed to draw 1–1 and stay in Mexico's top flight. Also in 1999, Rayados made his first [CONMEBOL Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL_Libertadores) participation and had a decent campaign despite falling in the group stage.

In 2002, Monterrey hired Argentinian coach [Daniel Passarella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Passarella) and started to form a strong and competitive side featuring the likes of [Guillermo Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Franco), [Walter Erviti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Erviti), [Jesus Arellano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Arellano) and [Luis Perez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Ernesto_Perez). In the Clausura 2003 tournament, they won their second official title. In the semi-finals, they faced their arch-rivals Tigres for the first time ever in a Liguilla. In the first leg, they won 4–1 at the Estadio Universitario, and despite losing 2–1 at their home ground in the second leg, they managed to advance to the finals with an aggregate victory of 5–3. On 14 June 2003, they defeated [Monarcas Morelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarcas_Morelia) 3–1 on aggregate to claim their second league title after 17 years.

Pasarella left in 2004, and afterwards, Monterrey hired [Miguel Herrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Herrera) to be their head coach. He led them to the finals of the Apertura 2004, but the club lost against Pumas. He would lead them to the finals again in the Apertura 2005 tournament, this time losing to Toluca by an aggregate of 6–3. After the loss, Guillermo Franco left the club to play for Spanish club [Villarreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villarreal_CF). Herrera would remain their head coach until 2007, when he was fired after a poor string of results in the Apertura 2007. For the Clausura 2008, Monterrey hired [Ricardo La Volpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_La_Volpe) to be their head coach, and they managed to reach the semi-finals. This team featured new players such as [Humberto Suazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humberto_Suazo) and newly acquired league veteran striker [Jared Borgetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Borgetti). However, the next tournament was a poor one for Rayados, as they finished in 14th place.

#### The Vucetich Era (2009–13)

In 2009, Monterrey hired coach [Víctor Manuel Vucetich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Manuel_Vucetich) and formed a team that would become one of the strongest in the league, with an attack led by Humberto Suazo and new acquisition [Aldo de Nigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_de_Nigris), a midfield featuring veteran players Luis Ernesto Perez and Jesus Arellano along with [Walter Ayovi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Ayovi) and a defence led by [Jose Maria Basanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Basanta), [Duilio Davino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duilio_Davino) and goalkeeper [Jonathan Orozco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Orozco). They won their third league title, the Apertura 2009 tournament, with an aggregate 6–4 victory against [Cruz Azul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruz_Azul) in the finals.[^cite_note-11] The [first leg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico_Apertura_2009_Liguilla_Final#First_leg) was played at the Estadio Tecnológico, where Monterrey overcame a 3–1 deficit to win the game 4–3. [The second leg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_de_M%C3%A9xico_Apertura_2009_Liguilla_Final#Second_leg) was played at the Estadio Azul, with Monterrey winning 2–1. Thus, after a six-year wait Monterrey lifted their third league title.

They faced [Real Salt Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Salt_Lake) in the [Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_CONCACAF_Champions_League_Finals) and won 3–2 on aggregate to claim their first [CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_League) title and the third title in the Vucetich Era.[^cite_note-13]

The following year, they reached the finals of the Clausura 2012 league tournament and the [2011–12 CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_CONCACAF_Champions_League). Both finals were against the same opponent, [Santos Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_Laguna). Rayados were seeking to win their fifth league title and their second Champions League title. They lost the Clausura 2012 finals against Santos,[^cite_note-14] but won the [finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_CONCACAF_Champions_League_Finals) of the Champions League against them to claim their second consecutive CONCACAF title.[^cite_note-15]

Monterrey reached the [finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_CONCACAF_Champions_League_Finals) of the [2012–13 CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_CONCACAF_Champions_League) for the third consecutive time, and faced Santos Laguna in a repeat of the previous year's final. The first leg finished in a 0–0 draw. In the second leg, Santos built a 2–0 lead with goals from [Darwin Quintero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_Quintero) and former Rayados player [Felipe Baloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_Baloy). However, Monterrey managed to make a dramatic comeback and scored four goals within 30 minutes, starting with a brace from [Aldo de Nigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_de_Nigris) and a goal each from [Humberto Suazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humberto_Suazo) and [Neri Cardozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neri_Cardozo), which gave Monterrey their third consecutive CONCACAF Champions League title and the fifth overall title in the Vucetich Era. This solidified them as the best Mexican football team of all time. Although they enjoyed tremendous success in the CONCACAF Champions League, they did not return to the [following tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_CONCACAF_Champions_League) as they could not reach any league finals during the [2012–13 Liga MX season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Liga_MX_season) and thus could not get a chance to try to become the first team to win the tournament four times in a row.

Monterrey started the 2013–14 season with a lot of changes. [Aldo de Nigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_de_Nigris) left the club to join [Chivas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.D._Guadalajara), and [Walter Ayovi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Ayovi) joined [Pachuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.F._Pachuca). By now, players like [Jesus Arellano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Arellano) and [Duilio Davino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duilio_Davino) had retired, and long-time club players like [Luis Ernesto Perez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Ernesto_Perez) had left the club. With the arrival of new players like [Dorlan Pabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorlan_Pabon) and [Leobardo Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leobardo_Lopez), Monterrey was ready for the Apertura 2013 tournament. However, a string of poor results and the shock early departure of Pabon would prove to be a threat as Monterrey started the tournament poorly. On 25 August 2013, Rayados announced that Vucetich had stepped down as coach, and thus the Vucetich Era, which earned the club a total of five titles in four years, came to an end.[^cite_note-16]



Humberto Suazo is the club's second all-time top scorer with 121 goals across all competitions.

#### Post-Vucetich period (2013–15)

Rayados hired [José Guadalupe Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Guadalupe_Cruz_\(footballer\)) to become the new manager of the team, and although they managed to reach the semi-finals of the domestic cup, the [Copa MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_MX), they failed to qualify to the playoffs of the [Apertura 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Liga_MX_season) tournament. After a bad start to the Clausura 2014, they fired Cruz on 18 February 2014, after only 17 league games coached.[^cite_note-17]

Monterrey replaced Cruz with [Carlos Barra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Barra), who had worked as an assistant coach for Vucetich. Although they failed to reach the playoffs for the second consecutive tournament, the team kept Barra for the [2014–15 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Liga_MX_season). Colombian striker [Dorlan Pabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorlan_Pab%C3%B3n) re-joined the team, and with new signings such as [Stefan Medina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Medina) and [Pablo Barrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Barrera), Monterrey started the Apertura 2014 tournament with high expectations. They managed to secure 6th place and returned to the playoffs for the first time since Vucetich had managed the club. The club had a strike partnership of Dorlan Pabon and [Humberto Suazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humberto_Suazo), with Pabon scoring 11 goals. The club reached the semi-finals but lost 3–0 on aggregate against the eventual champions [Club América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Am%C3%A9rica) in what would prove to be Suazo's last games with the club before returning to [Colo-Colo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colo-Colo). Suazo had scored over 102 league goals in a seven-year span and became the all-time top scorer for the club at the time.[^cite_note-18]

The team started the Clausura 2015 tournament in poor form, losing four of their first six games, and on 15 February, the team fired Barra[^cite_note-19] and replaced him with two-time Liga MX champion [Antonio Mohamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Mohamed),[^cite_note-20] who had led Club América to the league championship the previous tournament. Mohamed had played for Rayados during his years as a player. He was part of the squad that managed to avoid relegation in 1999. Monterrey did not qualify to the playoffs under Mohamed, but the club were keen on keeping him for the [2015–16 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Liga_MX_season). That season would prove to be a special one as the team were moving to a new home ground, the [Estadio BBVA Bancomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_BBVA_Bancomer). In the summer, Rayados made new signings, including midfielder [Walter Gargano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Gargano), striker [Rogelio Funes Mori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogelio_Funes_Mori) and re-signed [Jose Maria Basanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Basanta), who had left for Italian side [Florentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACF_Fiorentina) after the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md). Former club veterans [Aldo de Nigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_de_Nigris) and [Luis Ernesto Pérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Ernesto_P%C3%A9rez) also returned to the club, along with the promotion to the first team of promising young center back [Cesar Montes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesar_Montes). Rayados had a formidable attacking trio of Dorlan Pabon, Rogelio Funes Mori and [Edwin Cardona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Cardona), who had signed with Monterrey during the winter of 2015. They narrowly missed the playoffs of the Apertura 2015 tournament, but their offense was lauded by many as one of the best in the league.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

#### Tenure of Antonio Mohamed (2015–2018)

After failing to qualify for the playoffs in 2015, the club loaned several players to other clubs in the league. Players such as [Stefan Medina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Medina) and [Severo Meza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severo_Meza) were loaned to [Pachuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.F._Pachuca) and [Sinaloa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorados_de_Sinaloa), respectively. In the winter of 2016, the team acquired club veteran [Walter Ayovi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Ayov%C3%AD) who had left the club in 2013 and [River Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_River_Plate) midfielder [Carlos Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Andr%C3%A9s_S%C3%A1nchez)[^cite_note-23] who had won the [Copa Libertadores](299730.md) a few months prior. The team began the [Clausura 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Liga_MX_season) tournament in great form by winning their first three games. They went on to have their best regular season in years by finishing on top of the league with 37 points, seven points ahead of second place [Pachuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.F._Pachuca).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The team's success was primarily due to their attacking trio composed of [Pabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorlan_Pab%C3%B3n), [Funes Mori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogelio_Funes_Mori) and [Cardona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Cardona), with midfielder Carlos Sanchez providing several goals and assists.



Antonio Mohamed was the club's manager from February 2015 to May 2018

The team entered the playoffs as favourites to win the title.[^cite_note-26] In the quarterfinals, they faced their arch-rivals and defending league champions [Tigres UANL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigres_UANL), the first leg was played at the [Estadio Universitario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Universitario_\(UANL\)) where Monterrey won 3–1.[^cite_note-27] The away leg was played at the [BBVA Bancomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_BBVA_Bancomer), where Tigres won 2–1 despite several penalties that were controversially awarded to Monterrey.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] Monterrey advanced to the semi-finals with a 4–3 aggregate victory and extended their record of never being eliminated by Tigres in the playoffs. In the semi-finals, they faced [América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Am%C3%A9rica), losing the first leg at the [Estadio Azteca](1834902.md) 1–0.[^cite_note-30] In the second leg, they faced them at home and pulled off a dramatic 4–2 victory, in what was described by some pundits as one of the best games in the history of the playoffs.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] They advanced to the league finals where they would face [Pachuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.F._Pachuca). Controversy arose when it was confirmed that Carlos Sanchez would not be able to play the finals as the [Uruguay national team](679782.md) had called him up for the upcoming [Copa América Centenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] In [the league finals against Pachuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clausura_2016_Liga_MX_championship_stage#Monterrey_v_Pachuca), Monterrey lost the first leg 1–0 at the [Estadio Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Hidalgo), with the sole goal coming from striker [Franco Jara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Jara). The second leg took place at the [Estadio BBVA Bancomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_BBVA_Bancomer) in what was its first ever final since it was inaugurated the year before. On 29 May, in front of 53,000 spectators, the final was played. Monterrey opened the scoring in the 39th minute with a shot from [Dorlan Pabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorlan_Pab%C3%B3n) to level the aggregate 1–1. Veteran goalkeeper [Óscar Pérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93scar_P%C3%A9rez_Rojas) had several key saves for Pachuca, and in the closing minutes of the game, a header from [Victor Guzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Guzm%C3%A1n_\(footballer,_born_1995\)) in the 93rd minute gave the title to Pachuca in what was described by the Mexican media as a heart-breaking defeat for Monterrey.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

The following tournament ended in disappointment as the team narrowly failed to qualify to the playoffs of the [Apertura 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Liga_MX_season#Apertura_2016).[^cite_note-39] However, it also saw the return of Rayados to the [CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_League) after a three-year absence, having won the tournament three times in a row under Vucetich. The team had high hopes to redeem itself after their league title loss; however, the team was shockingly eliminated in the group stage after finishing second in their group behind Panamanian club [Arabe Unido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.D._%C3%81rabe_Unido).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

In the Apertura 2017 season, Monterrey finished the regular season in first place with 37 points and advanced to playoffs. In quarterfinals, Monterrey beat [Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Atlas) 2–1 in the first leg and 4–1 in the second, a 6–2 aggregate. Monterrey faced [Morelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarcas_Morelia) in semi-finals, winning 1–0 in the away leg and 4–0 in the home leg, a 5–0 aggregate. Monterrey advanced to the final against arch rival Tigres, In the first leg, the teams tied 1–1 at the [Estadio Universitario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Universitario_\(UANL\)). In the second leg at the [Estadio BBVA Bancomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_BBVA_Bancomer), Tigres beat Monterrey 2–1 with goals from [Eduardo Vargas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Vargas) and [Francisco Meza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Meza). In May 2018, Mohamed resigned from his position shortly after being eliminated from the Clausura 2018 playoffs by [Club Tijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Tijuana). He left the club having won two titles and breaking the club record amount of points for short seasons, but was criticized for various playoff failures.[^cite_note-42]

#### Reappointment of Antonio Mohamed (2019–2020)

On 14 October, Antonio Mohamed was reappointed as the manager of Monterrey. The club would find themselves in a good run in the [Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup), advancing to the semi-finals after beating [Al Sadd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Sadd_SC) 3–2, but were eventually eliminated by losing to [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) after a stoppage time winner from [Roberto Firmino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Firmino). The club would beat [Al Hilal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hilal_SFC) on penalties, earning a third place medal in the [2019 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIFA_Club_World_Cup). That same month, despite the club finishing 8th on the [Apertura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apertura_and_Clausura) general table, they would go on to reach the championship finals against [América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Am%C3%A9rica) and defeat them in penalties, winning 4–2 and securing their 5th championship league trophy.[^cite_note-45]

#### A new decade (2020–present)

Monterrey entered a new decade under mounting pressure from the success of their local rivals. In the Clausura 2020, the club endured a ten-match winless streak before the tournament was abandoned due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). The following campaign saw Monterrey miss the playoffs, prompting Antonio Mohamed to step down from his role. [Javier Aguirre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Aguirre) took the reins ahead of the Clausura 2021, guiding the team to its fifth [CONCACAF Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_CONCACAF_Champions_League) title, Monterrey defeated Club América 1–0 in the final on 29 October 2021, with the decisive goal scored by Rogelio Funes Mori in the 8th minute. For a while, Monterrey was the "triplete" holder, having won Liga MX, CONCACHAMPIONS and Copa MX. In the modern apertura and clausura era, no other team had done this before or after. However, a string of poor results cost "el Vasco" his job midway through the Clausura 2022. Víctor Manuel Vucetich stepped in as his replacement, marking his return for a second stint at the helm.

In October 2022, [José Antonio Noriega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Antonio_Noriega) was appointed sporting director and oversaw an ambitious recruitment strategy focused on marquee signings. High-profile arrivals included [Sergio Canales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Canales), [Jesús Corona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jes%C3%BAs_Manuel_Corona), [Sergio Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Ramos), [Óliver Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93liver_Torres), and [Anthony Martial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Martial). Despite assembling a star-studded squad, Monterrey struggled to meet expectations, reaching just one final—the Apertura 2024—where, under [Martín Demichelis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mart%C3%ADn_Demichelis), they finished as runners-up after a narrow defeat to Club América. At the [2025 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup), Monterrey, now managed by [Domènec Torrent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom%C3%A8nec_Torrent), faced [Inter Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Milan), [River Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_River_Plate), and [Urawa Red Diamonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urawa_Red_Diamonds). They advanced to the round of 16 after finishing second in their group, where they were eliminated by [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund). Noriega left his post at the end of the Clausura 2026, after Rayados failed to reach the playoffs.

## Culture

### Colours

Since the club's founding in 1945, the colours used by the club have been white and blue, with varied use from the usual stripes. The shade of the blue itself has been in constant change, ranging from navy and cobalt to slightly lighter tones. The third colour has also been inconsistent, sometimes presented as being red, orange and cyan, and recently, violet, purple or green.

### Youth development

The club has constantly emphasized home-grown (*[cantera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantera)*) players and has produced international players such as [Francisco Javier Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Javier_Cruz), [Héctor Becerra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ctor_Becerra), [Missael Espinoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missael_Espinoza), [Jesús Arellano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jes%C3%BAs_Arellano), [Antonio de Nigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_de_Nigris), [Severo Meza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severo_Meza), [Jonathan Orozco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Orozco), [Jesús Zavala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jes%C3%BAs_Eduardo_Zavala), [Hiram Mier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiram_Mier), [Jesús Manuel Corona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jes%C3%BAs_Manuel_Corona), [César Montes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Montes), [Jonathan González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Gonz%C3%A1lez_\(footballer,_born_1999\)), and [Carlos Rodríguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Alberto_Rodr%C3%ADguez) among others.

### Rivalry

Rayados' biggest rival is [Tigres UANL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigres_de_la_UANL). This rivalry is called the [Clásico Regiomontano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cl%C3%A1sico_Regiomontano). Monterrey and Tigres are both known to sell out all of their home games regardless of weather conditions and the teams' status. For Clásico matches, the stadium is sold out as soon as tickets go on sale. The rivalry has been labeled as the most intensely competed rivalry in Mexican football.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

As of August 2022, there have been a total of 128 official Clásico games, Tigres has been victorious in 47 of them, while Monterrey has won 42, and a total of 39 games have been draws. Monterrey and Tigres played their first Clásico on 13 July 1974 in the [Estadio Universitario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Universitario_\(UANL\)), with the match ending in a 1–1 draw. Monterrey would be the first team to win the Clásico in their second confrontation, 2–1. Almost all their first encounters were played in the Estadio Universitario, with averages of 70,000 fans attending these games, which were before the stadium renovation. In addition, there were also another six "non-official" games before the first official Clásico, in five of which Rayados were victorious.&#x20;

Monterrey has recently held somewhat of a regional rivalry with state neighbors [Santos Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_Laguna), whom they have played a total of four finals, with Monterrey winning three of them and Santos one. It is known by many as La Nortena.

### Children's web series

In 2017, the club launched its [YouTube](3524766.md) and [YouTube Kids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube_Kids) original series, *[Rayados Kids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayados_Kids?action=edit\&redlink=1)*,[^cite_note-50] dedicated to creating content for children who love the team, in interview style format with the team players and original stories revolving around the teams actual positioning on football leagues. The web garnered around six thousand subscriptions and one million views in its first year.

### Rayados Christmas Campaign

Since 2010 Rayados teammates have joined to wish a Merry Christmas to its fans, by filming their annual holiday campaign.[^cite_note-51] It has become a tradition to include a new rendition of their famous crowd chant song "El Corrido de Monterrey" each year on their holiday video, showing their fans how their passion lives "En la Vida y en la Cancha" (In Life and on the Field).[^cite_note-52]

## Grounds

Main articles: [Estadio Tecnológico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Tecnol%C3%B3gico) and [Estadio BBVA](17130610.md)

Monterrey played their home matches at the [Estadio Tecnológico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Tecnol%C3%B3gico) from 1950 to 2015, though for a period of time from 1973 to 1980 they played at the [Estadio Universitario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Universitario_\(UANL\)), the stadium was opened on 17 July 1950, by Mexican president [Miguel Alemán Valdés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Alem%C3%A1n_Vald%C3%A9s), it was the second oldest football stadium in Mexico, after Estadio Azul. The stadium was a part of the [1986 Mexico FIFA World Cup](157231.md) which could hold 38,000 people, and served 4 matches within the tournament but, in July 2017 the stadium began to be demolished.



Monterrey played at the Estadio Tecnológico from 1950 to 2015

In [1977–78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977%E2%80%9378_Mexican_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_season), with preparations for the [1977 CONCACAF Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_CONCACAF_Championship) underway at the Estadio Universitario, both Tigres and Rayados played at the Estadio Tecnológico, including the first *[Clásico Regio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cl%C3%A1sico_Regiomontano)* derby held in the stadium; it would be the first of 42 meetings between the two clubs venue, with the final fixture being a 2–2 draw in October 2014.

Monterrey won league titles in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Mexican_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_season#Mexico-1986) (Mexico 1986) and [Apertura 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Mexican_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_season) in the venue, as well as the [Copa MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_MX) in 1991 and the [CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_League) in 2012–13.



The interior of Estadio BBVA

In July 2015, Monterrey moved to a new stadium called [Estadio BBVA](17130610.md), located in Guadalupe, Nuevo León, in [Greater Monterrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterrey_Metropolitan_area). The new stadium currently has a capacity of 53,500 people. The stadium has similar features of those incorporated within the design of England's [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md) and the [Aviva Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviva_Stadium) in Ireland. The stadium was inaugurated on 2 August 2015 in a friendly match for the eighth edition of the [Eusébio Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eus%C3%A9bio_Cup), where Monterrey defeated [Benfica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.L._Benfica) 3–0 hosting in front of a sold-out crowd.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]



Estadio BBVA has been Monterrey's current home since Apertura 2015

Estadio BBVA, designed by Christopher Lee of [Populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populous_\(company\)), started its development in October 2011, and included plans for reforestation and environmental healing for the decaying area that surrounds the construction site. The stadium was developed by [FEMSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FEMSA) which costed around US\$200 million. It is seen as one of the most beautiful stadiums in Mexico, it has the fourth largest capacity crowd in Mexico. It has an authentic grass surface, suites, a club-themed Restaurant, a club lounge, and high-end interior and exterior design. The inclination of the grandstand is 34 degrees and with the minimum distance allowed by FIFA to provide unsurpassed closeness to the action.

On 29 May 2016, Monterrey played their first final in their new stadium in front of 50,000 fans against [Pachuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.F._Pachuca) for the [Clausura 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Liga_MX_season) championship, which they tied 1–1, but lost 2–1 on aggregate.

### Kit evolution

Wikimedia Commons logo

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [C.F. Monterrey kits](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/C.F.%20Monterrey%20kits).

The original uniform was a shirt that was split diagonally across the chest with blue and white at each side, with white shorts and navy blue socks. In 1955 after winning the second division the club used a white shirt with two horizontal blue lines across the chest. In the 1960s the club wore a different kit inspired by the one used by [Jaibos Tampico Madero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaibos_Tampico_Madero) with vertical baby blue lines with white shorts and socks. It was in 1962 when **D. José Ramón Ballina** introduced the kit that the club still uses to date, inspired by [Asturias FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asturias_F.C.), a club he had played in Mexico City.

In the 1970s, many models emerged, some with broad, thicker stripes, and blue and black combinations, but the most significant change occurred in the mid-1980s when the color of the T-shirt changed from royal blue to navy blue, a colour that is still in use today.

[Atletica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atletica) was the kit manufacturer from 1999 to 2007, followed by American company [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) which manufactured the kits from 2007 to 2014. Afterwards, Monterrey signed a contract with German sportswear manufacturer [Puma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puma_\(brand\)) which has been making the team's kit ever since.
