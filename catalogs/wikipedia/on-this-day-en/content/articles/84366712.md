A series of widespread protests began in [Spain](26667.md) following the high-profile [eviction](538394.md) on 23 September 2026 of an 87-year-old, Maricarmen Abascal, which brought the country's ongoing [affordable housing crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_housing_crisis) back into the spotlight. Public discontent on the issue of housing had already been building up over a scandal involving the controversial purchase of a luxury penthouse by Madrid premier [Isabel Díaz Ayuso](59693424.md) and the suicide of an evicted woman in [L'Hospitalet de Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Hospitalet_de_Llobregat), Olga Matute, earlier in September. On 26 September, the protests turned into an organised [protest camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protest_camp) at the [Puerta del Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerta_del_Sol) square in [Madrid](41188263.md), in an action widely compared to the [15-M Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15-M_Movement) in 2011, forcing the [Spanish government](1306113.md) under [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md) to agree to an emergency relief package including a temporary ban on evictions and stronger protection for [tenants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenant) amid soaring rents.

Protesters have called for the enforcement of the [right to housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_housing), access to [affordable housing](3531662.md) and increased protection of tenants against [landlord abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlord_harassment) and price hikes. While they have been very critical of the housing policies of Spanish administrations—both at the national, regional and local level, and regardless of political affiliation—the protests have met with understanding and sympathy from left-wing political parties (such as the ruling [Spanish Socialist Workers' Party](272596.md) and [Sumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumar_\(electoral_platform\))), and with open hostility from the right-wing [People's Party](404727.md) and [Vox](41928488.md). These parties, jointly with [Together for Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Together_for_Catalonia_\(2020\)), blocked the parliamentary ratification of the two [decree-laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Decree-Law_\(Spain\)) containing the Spanish government's relief measures on 2 October, further fueling the protests, which spread to cities across the country amid calls for a [general strike](41138909.md) in defence of housing rights and against [property speculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_speculation). The parliamentary defeat of the decrees has been suggested as a potential trigger for Sánchez to advance the [next Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Spanish_general_election) to 29 November 2026.[^cite_note-ED021026-1-1][^cite_note-ABC021026-2][^cite_note-Politico021026-3][^cite_note-EP031026-4]

## Background

### Housing crisis in Spain

[Housing costs](3531662.md) and [rental prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rental_value) had been soaring for many years in [Spain](26667.md), particularly in major urban centers,[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] as wages have not increased to the same extent.[^cite_note-7] Housing construction in the country collapsed following the bursting of the [Spanish property bubble](5259909.md) in 2008 and the [subsequent real estate crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%932014_Spanish_real_estate_crisis), but population kept growing in larger city areas (such as [Madrid](41188263.md)), causing a housing shortage in places where it was needed the most; the proliferation of [short-term rents](9590858.md) for [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Spain) has been another factor, by significantly reducing long-term rental stock and driving up rent prices.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] In June 2026, the [Bank of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Spain) warned that 750,000 new homes would be needed to close the gap between newly-formed households and the available housing stock.[^cite_note-10]

Housing policy has been a heavily disputed battleground between the Spanish government and [regional administrations](55002.md), which hold primary constitutional powers over [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning) and [public housing](177912.md).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The central government adopted a Housing Law in 2023 that allowed regions to declare "stressed residential market zones" ([Spanish](26825.md): *zonas tensionadas*) to enforce [state-backed rent caps](41258426.md),[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] but it failed to ease pressure on the country's housing crisis as most regions ruled by the [People's Party](404727.md) (PP) fundamentally resisted or blocked these measures, arguing that price controls hurt the market supply.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

According to opinion polls, the issue of housing has emerged as one of Spain's major problems since 2025,[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] with those surveyed also frequently showing support for increased government intervention to lower rents.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-TG021026-22]

### Scandals and incidents

A scandal broke out in early 2026 after it transpired that [public housing](177912.md) in [Alicante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicante)—a PP-ruled municipality—had been allocated to high-ranking party and city council officials (including the local official responsible for urban planning), their relatives, and friends.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] A judicial probe was opened to investigate possible crimes of [malfeasance in office](2289190.md), [insider trading](15368.md), [conflict of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest) and [political corruption](66220.md).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

Throughout the summer of 2026, it was revealed that the [Madrid regional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_regional_government) had purchased a luxury penthouse valued at €6.3 million earlier that year; while the official purpose of the purchase remained unclear amid changing versions, it was suggested for use as a personal residence for regional premier [Isabel Díaz Ayuso](59693424.md).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The scandal inflamed public outrage amid the ongoing housing crisis, while legal proceedings were initiated regarding a possible [misappropriation of public funds](209411.md).[^cite_note-30] During *The District* real estate fair in Madrid on 22 September 2026, three housing activists stormed the venue to protest against the policies of Ayuso, [José Luis Martínez-Almeida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Luis_Mart%C3%ADnez-Almeida) ([Madrid's mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Madrid)) and speculation, scattering leaflets and setting off stink bombs.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

A 54-year-old woman from [L'Hospitalet de Llobregat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Hospitalet_de_Llobregat), Olga Matute, committed suicide in early September 2026 after being evicted from her home.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] Her eviction procedure had resumed after the Congress —with the votes of PP, [Vox](41928488.md) and [Together for Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Together_for_Catalonia_\(2020\)) (Junts)—struck down a [decree-law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Decree-Law_\(Spain\)) in April 2026 extending a suspension of evictions approved by Sánchez's government in the context of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) in 2020.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

## Developments

### Maricarmen's eviction and initial protests

On 23 September, María del Carmen "Maricarmen" Abascal, an 87-year-old woman with a disability, was forcefully [evicted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evicted) from her home in Madrid after a long judicial struggle with a [real estate company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate_company), Urbagestión.[^cite_note-Reut230926-37][^cite_note-CNN270926-38][^cite_note-39] The case gained notoriety due to Maricarmen's vulnerable condition and Urbagestión's refusal to suspend the eviction despite pending court filings, humanitarian appeals, a volunteer's offer to cover her rent payments and attempts by public authorities ​to secure an alternative accommodation for her;[^cite_note-Reut230926-37] as well as by the violent police response—seeing the use of teargas—at protesters attempting to block the eviction, among whom were actor [Carlos Bardem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Bardem) and the singer and actor [Ana Belén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Bel%C3%A9n).[^cite_note-40]

Tent in Madrid with a sign reading "Being a rentier is not a job"

Tents in the [Puerta del Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerta_del_Sol) ([Madrid](41188263.md)) protest camp on 29 September 2026, with signs reading "Being a [rentier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rentier_capitalism) is not a job".

Maricarmen's case served as a catalyst for public discontent at the ongoing housing crisis in Spain, as well as a symbol for the pro-housing movement internationally.[^cite_note-CNN270926-38][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Following the eviction, a 10,000-strong spontaneous protest started in front of the Madrilenian government's luxury penthouse, with attendees calling for "Ayuso's penthouse for Maricarmen".[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] The Madrid Tenants' Union (Spanish: *Sindicato de Inquilinas de Madrid*), which had organised [eviction resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eviction_resistance) in support of Maricarmen,[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] called for a demonstration on 26 September which saw the participation of between 25,000 (according to authorities) and 300,000 people (according to organisers).[^cite_note-48] This demonstration resulted in protesters spontaneously [camping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protest_camp) in the middle of the [Puerta del Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerta_del_Sol) square,[^cite_note-49] mirroring the [15-M Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15-M_Movement) in 2011,[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] to demand the [Spanish government](1306113.md) under [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md) to ban evictions and push for stronger protection for tenants amid the housing shortage and soaring rents.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] Protests subsequently spread to other major Spanish cities, such as [Barcelona](4443.md), [Valencia](63861.md), [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville), [Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaragoza), [Málaga](184763.md), [Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcia), [Vigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigo), [A Coruña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Coru%C3%B1a), [Palma de Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca), [Oviedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oviedo), [Cádiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1diz), [Santiago de Compostela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Compostela) or [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara,_Spain).[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-BBC290926-56]

On 29 September, the [City Council of Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Council_of_Madrid) under Almeida demanded the immediate end of the Puerta del Sol camp, while the regional government in Madrid sent a formal request to the Spanish government to dismantle the camp within 48 hours or face legal action.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] These calls by PP-controlled administrations in Madrid came as [Sánchez's government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_government_of_Pedro_S%C3%A1nchez)—formed by the [Spanish Socialist Workers' Party](272596.md) (PSOE) and [Sumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumar_\(electoral_platform\))—openly rejected forcefully clearing the encampment,[^cite_note-59] and was joined by protests from campers that the clock chimes at the [Royal House of the Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_House_of_the_Post_Office) (Ayuso's headquarters as [president of the Community of Madrid](608649.md)) rang every 15 minutes throughout the night and into the early morning, continuously interrupting their sleep.[^cite_note-60] That same day, far-right groups such as [Desokupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desokupa) threatened on social media to storm Sol and evict the activists.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] By 30 September, the number of tents in Puerta del Sol had risen to over 1,200,[^cite_note-63] with new encampments in support of the Madrid one spreading to major squares in cities such as Barcelona ([Plaça de Catalunya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pla%C3%A7a_de_Catalunya)) and Málaga ([Plaza de la Constitución](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_de_la_Constituci%C3%B3n_\(M%C3%A1laga\))).[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-TA300930-65]

### Government's response and reactions

Mounting public pressure forced Urbagestión—the investment fund that had triggered Maricarmen's eviction—to backtrack and allow her to move back into the apartment,[^cite_note-66] while prompting Sánchez's government to agree to emergency relief measures on housing.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] These measures, distributed in two decree-laws, included:[^cite_note-BBC290926-56][^cite_note-TG290926-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

- A ban on evictions of vulnerable tenants until 2030;

- Bans on so-called "[vulture funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture_fund)" buying property until 2028;

- Regulating temporary and room rentings:

- A two-year extension on rental agreements due to expire before the end of 2028;

- A [VAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VAT) rate increase to 10% for [tourist apartments](1822425.md), as well as a hike in the [property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) (IBI) for such dwellings;

- Zero-interest loans of up to €50,000 for the purchase of a first home;

- [Tax breaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_break) for landlords and tenants to encourage a drop in rental prices;

- A VAT rate reduction to 4% for housing construction;

- The automatic renovation of rental agreements (with a 12-month penalty for landlords seeking to terminate the contract without just cause).

While [tenant unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenant_union) criticised the government's move to split the measures into two bills and regarded them as not ambitious enough, they also viewed them as a starting point and demanded their passage in parliament, calling for pressure actions on still-doubting parties.[^cite_note-ED300926-73][^cite_note-74] The [Catalan government](294461.md) under [Salvador Illa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Illa) praised the central government's measures and announced an additional €125 million shared-ownership plan for families unable to secure a mortgage.[^cite_note-TA300930-65][^cite_note-75]

Sánchez's plans were dismissed by the right-wing opposition PP and Vox parties, with its approval relying on Junts's position.[^cite_note-TG290926-70][^cite_note-76] PP leader [Alberto Núñez Feijóo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_N%C3%BA%C3%B1ez_Feij%C3%B3o) announced his party's outright opposition to the decrees even before the specific measures were unveiled,[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] while Vox blamed the housing crisis on [immigration](10499603.md) and called for [mass deportations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_deportation).[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] [Republican Left of Catalonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Left_of_Catalonia), [EH Bildu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EH_Bildu), [Podemos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podemos_\(Spanish_political_party\)), [Més–Compromís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9s%E2%80%93Comprom%C3%ADs) and the [Galician Nationalist Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_Nationalist_Bloc) all announced their support to the two bills,[^cite_note-81] while the [Basque Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_Nationalist_Party) and [Canarian Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_Coalition) announced their support for the first and their opposition to the second.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

Tenant unions called for new protest actions across the country in support of the decrees' approval, including [pot-banging protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacerolazo) on 1 October in front of town halls and further demonstrations on 3 and 4 October,[^cite_note-ED300926-73][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] while urging for a [general strike](41138909.md) against the housing crisis.[^cite_note-86] An online initiative calling for the [Congress of Deputies](352254.md) to ratify the measures adopted by the government (in a vote scheduled for 2 October) overwhelmed legislators' inboxes with over 300,000 e-mails within 24 hours.[^cite_note-87] In the night previous to the vote, Junts regarded the decrees as having "negative and irreversible effects on housing" and called for their withdrawal or else it would oppose them, all but ensuring they would be brought down by parliament.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] Sánchez's government replied by announcing it would not withdraw the housing bills and dared Junts, together with PP and Vox, to "show their true colours".[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] Public outcry at a possible parliamentary defeat of the bills was speculated by media as a potential trigger for Sánchez to advance the [next Spanish general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Spanish_general_election)—scheduled for no later than August 2027,[^cite_note-92] and with a tentative date of February or March 2027[^cite_note-93]—to 29 November 2026.[^cite_note-ED021026-1-1][^cite_note-Politico021026-3][^cite_note-EP031026-4][^cite_note-94]

### Vote on the government's plan

The tenant unions announced protests in front of the Congress ahead of the 2 October vote, calls which intensified after Junts confirmed its opposition to the government's plans.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] Junts' decision was received with outrage by the government parties, as the first decree in particular included many concessions and proposals to appease them,[^cite_note-ED021026-1-1][^cite_note-97] while the unions accused Junts of "constantly boycotting" all talks.[^cite_note-98] Negotiations continued in the last few hours before the vote, with Junts presenting a new housing package and the Spanish government unwilling to withdraw its own plans.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] Through his [X](9988187.md) account, Sánchez himself accused PP, Vox and Junts of siding with speculators and stated that "their vote will define them in the eyes of entire generations".[^cite_note-TG021026-22][^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

PSOE and Sumar requested a [roll-call vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_methods_in_deliberative_assemblies#Recorded_vote) on the decrees to force Junts's legislators to publicly record their votes,[^cite_note-103] while the government simultaneously hinted at a [snap election](462915.md) in the event of a parliamentary defeat.[^cite_note-ED021026-1-1][^cite_note-ABC021026-2][^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] The PP dubbed the decrees' ratification ballot as a "veiled [vote of confidence](208101.md)" on the government.[^cite_note-106] Protesters gathered in the vicinity of Congress in Madrid and in front of Junts's headquarters in Barcelona while the parliamentary debate was taking place.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] Sánchez made an unscheduled, last-ditch effort to convince parliamentary groups opposing his plan,[^cite_note-109] warning that "history will judge them sooner than they think and with more force than they imagine".[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

The defeat of the two decrees was hailed as a victory by the PP and Vox, whose deputies visibly cheered at the result of the vote,[^cite_note-117] as a group of activists threw leaflets from the visitors' gallery into the chamber while shouting that "vulture funds rule Congress".[^cite_note-118] The Spanish government announced it would reflect on the situation during the weekend, without ruling out an early electoral call.[^cite_note-ED021026-1-1][^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] PP leader Feijóo dared Sánchez to set a date for an early election, as tenant unions vowed to continue their mobilization and called for people to take to the streets across the country in new protests scheduled for between 2 and 5 October.[^cite_note-TG021026-22][^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123]

### 2–3 October protests

Spain's two largest trade unions, [Workers' Commissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Commissions) and the [General Union of Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Union_of_Workers), urged citizens to participate in the countrywide protests in response to the decrees' defeat and considered whether to support a general strike.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125] During the afternoon and into the night of 2 October, thousands protested in Seville, [Bilbao](68029.md), [Vitoria-Gasteiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitoria-Gasteiz), [San Sebastián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n), [Pamplona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamplona) and [Oviedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oviedo) in defence of the right to housing and against the political class.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127]

Subsequent demonstrations were held on 3 October in more than 50 Spanish cities, stepping up demands for action against the ongoing housing crisis following the parliamentary repeal of the government's decrees.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130] In Madrid, between 70,000 (according to authorities) and 500,000 people (according to organisers) marched in four columns that converged at [Plaza de Cibeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_de_Cibeles).[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133] In Valencia—where the demonstration gathered between 6,000 and 50,000—protesters attempted to storm a real estate investment conference at the [Palau de les Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_de_les_Arts), leading to police charges and resulting in nine police officers injured and five people detained;[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] after the protests, dozens of young people set up a camp in Valencia's [Plaza del Ayuntamiento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernisme_Plaza_of_the_City_Hall_of_Valencia).[^cite_note-136] Other major demonstrations took place in Málaga (9,000–50,000),[^cite_note-137] (3,500–8,000),[^cite_note-ED031026-142] or Vigo (5,000).[^cite_note-144] The demonstration in Barcelona had to be postponed due to weather warnings and the potential risk of torrential rains caused by a [Mediterranean mini-hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_tropical-like_cyclone).[^cite_note-145] Riots took place in Madrid during the afternoon and evening of that day, incited by far-right organizations (such as [National Nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Nucleus) and [National Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democracy_\(Spain\))) attempting to assault the Puerta del Sol encampment, with three detained.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

As the protests unfolded and in anticipation on an early electoral call, Junts offered to negotiate its housing proposals with the Spanish government to reach an agreement, while the PSOE responded skeptically that the recently rejected decrees already included those very proposals.[^cite_note-148]
