**Perth** ([Nyungar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyungar_language): *Boorloo*) is the [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Australian_capital_cities) and largest city of the state of [Western Australia](33613.md). It is the [fourth-most-populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Australia_by_population) in [Australia](4689264.md), with a population of over 2.2 million in the city and 2.3 million within [Greater Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Perth) as of 2023. The [world's most isolated major city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremes_on_Earth#Other_places_considered_the_most_remote) by certain criteria, Perth is part of the [South West Land Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_Land_Division) of Western Australia, with most of [Perth's metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_metropolitan_region) on the [Swan Coastal Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Coastal_Plain) between the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) and the [Darling Scarp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darling_Scarp). The city has expanded outward from the original British settlements on the [Swan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_River_\(Western_Australia\)), upon which its [central business district](#Central_business_district) and port of [Fremantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle) are situated.

Perth was founded by [Captain James Stirling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Stirling_\(Royal_Navy_officer\)) in 1829 as the administrative centre of the [Swan River Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_River_Colony). The city is situated on the traditional lands of the [Whadjuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whadjuk) [Noongar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noongar) people, where [Aboriginal Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_Australian) have lived for at least 48,000 years. Perth was named after the city of [Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth,_Scotland) in [Scotland](26994.md).[^cite_note-NMA-7] Initially established as a free settlement, the colony accepted [transported convicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convicts_in_Australia) from 1850 to supply labour for public works and construction. Perth was proclaimed as a city by [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria) in 1856.[^cite_note-8] Substantial population growth occurred during the late 19th-century [Western Australian gold rushes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian_gold_rushes), and the city has continued to expand, particularly after [World War II](32927.md) due to a high [net migration rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_migration_rate). [Post-war immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-war_immigration_to_Australia) were predominantly from the British Isles and Southern Europe, while more recent arrivals see a growing population of Asian descent. During the late 20th and early 21st centuries, a series of mining booms in various regions of Western Australia propelled Perth into the role of the regional headquarters for significant [mining operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_in_Western_Australia). It became Australia's fourth-most populated city in 1984, overtaking [Adelaide](1148.md).[^cite_note-ABSAdelaide1984-9]

Ranked as one of the world's [most liveable cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_liveable_cities), Perth was classified by the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network) as a Beta [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) in 2020. As of 2021, Perth is divided into [30 local government areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_metropolitan_region#Subregions_and_local_government_areas), comprising over [350 suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Perth_suburbs). The metropolitan contours span 125 kilometres (78 mi) from [Two Rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Rocks) in the north to [Singleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singleton,_Western_Australia) in the south, and 45 kilometres (28 mi) from the west coast to [Sawyers Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawyers_Valley,_Western_Australia) in the east. Beyond the central business district, predominant urban centres within the metropolitan area include [Armadale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armadale,_Western_Australia), [Fremantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle), [Joondalup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joondalup), [Midland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland,_Western_Australia) and [Rockingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockingham,_Western_Australia). Most of those were originally established as separate settlements and retained a distinct identity after being subsumed into the wider metropolitan area. [Mandurah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandurah), Western Australia's second-largest city, forms a [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation) with Perth along the coastline. Despite this, it is generally regarded as an independent city.

Perth is home to many parkland areas and nature reserves, the most-visited being [Kings Park and Botanic Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Park,_Western_Australia), one of the world's largest inner-city parks. Other popular natural features include [Cottesloe Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottesloe_Beach) and [Rottnest Island](216061.md). Notable [heritage buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_heritage_buildings_in_Perth,_Western_Australia) and cultural sites include [Perth Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Mint), [WA Museum Boola Bardip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian_Museum) and the World Heritage-listed [Fremantle Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle_Prison). All five of Western Australia's universities are based in Perth. The city is served by [Fremantle Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle_Harbour) and [Perth Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Airport).

## Toponymy

The name of the city is taken from [Perth, Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth,_Scotland), in honour of the [Secretary of State for War and the Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_War_and_the_Colonies), and Member for [Perthshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perthshire_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) in the [British House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_House_of_Commons), [George Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Murray_\(British_Army_officer\)).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Murray's association with the city was included in Stirling's proclamation of the colony, read in Fremantle on 18 June 1829, which concluded with the statement, "Given under my hand and Seal at Perth this 18th Day of June 1829. James Stirling Lieutenant Governor".[^cite_note-Stirling_1829-12] The only contemporary information on the source of the name comes from [Charles Fremantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Fremantle)'s diary entry for 12 August 1829, which records that they "named the town Perth according to the wishes of Sir George Murray".[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

Similarly to the English name *Perth*, the [Noongar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noongar_language) name *Boorloo* is sometimes used to denote the [Perth central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_central_business_district) area,[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] the [City of Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Perth) local government area,[^cite_note-17] or the capital city Perth in general.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Use of this term has gained traction in the arts,[^cite_note-21] by [Tourism Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_Western_Australia) (the statutory authority responsible for promoting Western Australia as a tourist destination),[^cite_note-22] the [University of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Australia),[^cite_note-23] [Curtin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtin_University),[^cite_note-24] the City of Perth,[^cite_note-25] journalists for the [Australian Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Broadcasting_Corporation) and [National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic),[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] and has been promoted by state politicians such as [Roger Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Cook_\(politician\)) and two of his ministers, [deputy premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_premier_of_Western_Australia) [Rita Saffioti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Saffioti) and [John Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carey_\(Australian_politician\)).[^cite_note-csc24-28][^cite_note-29] The name *Boorloo* was initially recorded by [Robert Menli Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Menli_Lyon) as *Boorlo* in 1833,[^cite_note-30] which was interpreted as "Perth, properly Point Fraser"[^cite_note-31] (a location in what was established in 1954 as [East Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Perth,_Western_Australia)), with the area around Point Fraser traditionally known to the Whadjuk Noongar people as *Boodjargabbeelup*.[^cite_note-CityPerth-32]

## History

Main article: [History of Perth, Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Perth,_Western_Australia)

### Prehistory



Perth is located on the traditional land of the [Whadjuk people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whadjuk), one of several groups in south-western Western Australia that make up the [Noongar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noongar) people.

Archaeological evidence attests to human habitation in the Perth area for at least 48,000 years;[^cite_note-men22-33]<sup>: 9 </sup> according to [Noongar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noongar) tradition, they have occupied the area since "time immemorial".[^cite_note-auto-34] Noongar country encompasses the south-west corner of Western Australia, with particular significance attached to the [wetlands on the Swan Coastal Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Wetlands), both spiritually (featuring in [local mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreamtime)) and as a source of food.[^cite_note-35]

The current central business district location is within the traditional territory of the [Mooro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mooro), a Noongar clan, led by [Yellagonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellagonga) at the time of the British settlement. The Mooro was one of several Noongar clans based around the Swan River, known collectively as the [Whadjuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whadjuk). The Whadjuk themselves were one of a larger group of fourteen tribes that formed the south-west socio-linguistic block known as the Noongar (meaning 'the people' in [their language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noongar_language)), also sometimes called the Bibbulmun.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

On 19 September 2006, the [Federal Court of Australia](4688076.md) ruled in the case of *Bennell v State of Western Australia* \[2006] FCA 1243 that [Noongar native title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_title_in_Australia#2005_%E2%80%93_Noongar) persisted over Perth metropolitan area.[^cite_note-Bennell-39] An appeal was subsequently filed, and in 2008, the Full Court of the Federal Court upheld parts of the appeal by the Western Australian and Commonwealth governments.[^cite_note-40] Following this appeal, the [Western Australian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Western_Australia) and the [South West Aboriginal Land and Sea Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_Aboriginal_Land_and_Sea_Council) negotiated the South West Native Title Settlement. This settlement, including the Whadjuk Indigenous Land Use Agreement over the Perth region, was finalised by the Federal Court on 1 December 2021.[^cite_note-41] As part of this agreement, the *Noongar (Koorah, Nitja, Boordahwan) (Past, Present, Future) Recognition Act* was passed in 2016, officially recognising the Noongar people as the [traditional owners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_owners) of the south-west region of Western Australia.[^cite_note-42]

### European exploration



[Willem de Vlamingh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_de_Vlamingh)'s ships and [black swans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_swan) at the entrance to the Swan River, 1697

In 1619 the Dutch explorer [Frederick de Houtman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_de_Houtman), sailing in the [VOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) ship *Dordrecht*, with Councillor of the Indies Jacob Dedel in the VOC ship *Amsterdam*, sighted the Australian coast near present-day Perth, which they called *Dedelsland*.[^cite_note-43] Many more boats followed in the ensuing years (an estimated 54 European ships prior to 1770 from a range of nations).

On 10 January 1697, Dutch Captain [Willem de Vlamingh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_de_Vlamingh) conducted the first documented exploration of the present-day Perth region. His crew initially explored the area on foot, leading them to what is now central Perth.[^cite_note-44] Vlamingh's expedition also ventured far up the Swan River, in search of native inhabitants.[^cite_note-HeritageInName-45] They named the river *[Swarte Swaene-Revier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_River_\(Western_Australia\))*, a reference to the [black swans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_swan) prevalent in the region.[^cite_note-HeritageInName-45] After Vlamingh's expedition, other Europeans conducted further voyages of exploration in the period between 1697 and 1829. However, as with Vlamingh's assessments, they judged the area inhospitable and unsuitable for the agriculture necessary to sustain a European-style settlement.[^cite_note-46]

### Swan River Colony

Main article: [Swan River Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_River_Colony)

Despite the [Colony of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_New_South_Wales) establishing a convict-supported settlement at [King George's Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George's_Sound) (called *Frederick Town*, renamed to *[Albany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany,_Western_Australia)* upon becoming part of Western Australia) on the south coast of the continent in 1826, responding to rumours of potential [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France_\(1814-1830\)) annexation, Perth marked the first comprehensive European settlement in the western portion of the continent in 1829. Officially designated as *Western Australia* in 1832, the colony retained the informal moniker "Swan River Colony" for many years, after the area's major watercourse.[^cite_note-srnsw-47]



*[The Foundation of Perth 1829](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Foundation_of_Perth_1829)* by [George Pitt Morison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pitt_Morison) is a historical reconstruction of the official ceremony by which Perth was founded, although not everyone depicted may have actually been present.

On 4 June 1829, newly arriving British colonists had their first view of the mainland. Captain James Stirling, aboard [*Parmelia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parmelia_\(barque\)), noted that the site was "as beautiful as anything of this kind I had ever witnessed".[^cite_note-48] On 12 August that year, Helen Dance, wife of the captain of the second ship, *Sulphur*, felled a tree to commemorate the town's founding.[^cite_note-49] From 1831 onward, confrontations between British settlers and the Noongar people escalated due to conflicting land-value systems and increased land use as the colony expanded. These confrontations resulted in multiple events, including the murder of settlers (such as [Thomas Peel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Peel)'s servant Hugh Nesbitt[^cite_note-50]), the execution without trial of Whadjuk elder [Midgegooroo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midgegooroo),[^cite_note-Fforde2002Yagan-51]<sup>: 230 </sup> the killing of his son [Yagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yagan) in 1833,[^cite_note-Fforde2002Yagan-51]<sup>: 231 </sup> and the [Pinjarra massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinjarra_massacre) in 1834.[^cite_note-men22-33]<sup>: 114 </sup>[^cite_note-inherit3957-52]

The strained relations between the Noongar people and the Europeans arose due to these events. Agricultural development on the land restricted the traditional [hunter-gatherer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer) practices of the native Whadjuk Noongar, compelling them to camp in designated areas, including swamps and lakes north of the European settlement. [Third Swamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park_\(Western_Australia\)), known to them as *Boodjamooling*, remained a primary campsite for the remaining Noongar people in the Perth region, also accommodating travellers, itinerants, and homeless individuals. During the gold rush in the 1890s, miners on their way to the goldfields joined this community.[^cite_note-53]

### Convict era and gold rushes



Built by convicts in the early 1850s, [Fremantle Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle_Prison) is a UNESCO [World Heritage Site](44940.md).



[Perth Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Mint), built in 1899 to refine gold from the gold rushes

In 1850, at a time when [penal transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penal_transportation) to Australia's eastern colonies had ceased, Western Australia was [opened to convicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convict_era_of_Western_Australia) at the request of farming and business people due to a shortage of labour.[^cite_note-54] Over the next eighteen years, 9,721 convicts arrived in Western Australia aboard [43 ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_convict_ship_voyages_to_Western_Australia),[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] outnumbering the approximately 7,300 free settlers.[^cite_note-NMA-7]

The designation of Perth as a city was formally announced by [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria) in 1856. However, despite this recognition, Perth remained a tranquil town. A description from 1870 by a Melbourne journalist depicted it as:[^cite_note-historyofCOP-57][^cite_note-58]

> a quiet little town of some 3000 inhabitants spread out in straggling allotments down to the water's edge, intermingled with gardens and shrubberies and half rural in its aspect ... The main streets are macadamised, but the outlying ones and most of the footpaths retain their native state from the loose sand—the all pervading element of Western Australia—productive of intense glare or much dust in the summer and dissolving into slush during the rainy season.

With the [discovery of gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian_gold_rushes) at [Kalgoorlie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalgoorlie) and [Coolgardie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolgardie) in the late 19th century, Western Australia experienced a mining boom.[^cite_note-59] Perth became a key hub for supplying the goldfields, and the newfound prosperity helped finance the construction of important public buildings, roads and railways. Perth's population grew from approximately 8,500 in 1881 to 61,000 in 1901.[^cite_note-60]

### Federation and beyond



Like many of Perth's colonial-era buildings, Moir Chambers (left) at the southwest corner of the [St Georges Terrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Georges_Terrace) and [Barrack Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrack_Street,_Perth) intersection was demolished to make way for [Citibank House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citibank_House) during a period of substantial modernisation in the 1960s-70s.

After a referendum in 1900,[^cite_note-naaCiP-61] Western Australia joined the [Federation of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Australia) in 1901,[^cite_note-historyofCOP-57] and "became a founding state of Australia".[^cite_note-men22-33] It was the last of the Australian colonies to agree to join the Federation, and it did so only after the other colonies had offered several concessions, including the construction of a [transcontinental railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Australian_Railway) from [Port Augusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Augusta) in [South Australia](26716.md) to [Kalgoorlie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalgoorlie) to link Perth with the eastern states.[^cite_note-62]

In 1927, [Indigenous people](12598742.md) were [prohibited from entering large swathes of Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Prohibited_Area) under penalty of imprisonment, a ban that lasted until 1954.[^cite_note-63]

In 1933, two-thirds of Western Australians [voted in a referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933_Western_Australian_secession_referendum) to [secede from](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secessionism_in_Western_Australia) the rest of Australia. However, the state general election held at the same time as the referendum had voted out the pro-independence government, replacing it with a government that did not support the independence movement. Respecting the result of the referendum, the new government nonetheless petitioned the Imperial Parliament at Westminster. The [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_House_of_Commons) established a [select committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_select_committees_of_the_United_Kingdom) to consider the issue but after 18 months of negotiations and lobbying, finally refused to consider the matter, declaring that it could not legally grant secession.[^cite_note-naaCiP-61][^cite_note-64]

Perth entered the post-war period with a population of approximately 280,000 and an economy that had not experienced sustained growth since the 1920s. Successive state governments, beginning with the [Willcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Willcock) Labor Government (1936–1945), determined to change this. Planning for post-war economic development was initially driven by [Russell Dumas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Dumas), who as Director of Public Works (1941–1953) drew up plans for Western Australia's major post-war public-works projects, including the raising of the [Mundaring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundaring_Weir) and [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_Dam_Hydro_Power_Station) dams, the development of the new Perth Airport, and the development of a new industrial zone centred on Kwinana. The advent of the [McLarty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLarty%E2%80%93Watts_Ministry) Liberal Government (1947–1953) saw the emergence of something of a consensus on the need for continuing economic development. Economic growth was fuelled by large-scale public works, the post-war immigration program, and the success that various state governments had in attracting substantial foreign investment into the state, beginning with the construction of the [Anglo-Iranian Oil Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwinana_Oil_Refinery) at [Kwinana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwinana_Beach) in 1951–52.[^cite_note-65]



Looking across [Perth railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_railway_station) c. 1955



[Elizabeth Quay Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Quay_Bridge)

The result of this economic activity was the rapid growth of the population of Perth and a marked change in its urban design. Commencing in the 1950s, Perth began to expand along an extensive highway network laid out in the [Stephenson-Hepburn Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_for_the_Metropolitan_Region,_Perth_and_Fremantle), which noted that Perth was beginning to resemble a pattern of development less in line with the British experience and more in line with North America.[^cite_note-66] This was encouraged by the opening of the [Narrows Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrows_Bridge_\(Perth\)) and the gradual closure of the [Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Perth) and [Fremantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Fremantle) tram systems. The mining-pastoral boom of the 1960s only accelerated the pace of urban growth in Perth.

In 1962, Perth received global media attention when city residents lit their house lights and streetlights as American astronaut [John Glenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Glenn) passed overhead while orbiting the Earth on *[Friendship 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendship_7)*. This led to its being nicknamed the "City of Light".[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] The city repeated the act as Glenn passed overhead on the [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle) in 1998 on [STS-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-95).[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

Perth's development and relative prosperity, especially since the mid-1960s,[^cite_note-73] has resulted from its role as the main service centre for the state's resource industries, which extract gold, iron ore, nickel, alumina, diamonds, mineral sands, coal, oil and natural gas.[^cite_note-74] Whilst most mineral and petroleum production takes place elsewhere in the state, the non-base services provide most of the employment and income to the people of Perth.[^cite_note-GreaterPerth-75]

Perth saw significant population growth in the 2000s, as well as the commencement of several major urban infrastructure projects, bolstered in part by the state's mining boom. These include the [Perth Convention and Exhibition Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Convention_and_Exhibition_Centre) (2004) and the redevelopment of the city's waterfront, giving rise to the mixed-use [Elizabeth Quay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Quay) precinct.

## Geography

### Central business district

See also: [Perth (suburb)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_\(suburb\))



[City of Perth Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Perth_Library) and [State Buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_Buildings,_Perth), Hay Street

The [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district) of Perth is bounded by the [Swan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_River_\(Western_Australia\)) to the south and east, with [Kings Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Park,_Western_Australia) and Thomas Street on the western end, and Newcastle Street to the north.[^cite_note-76] A state and federally funded project named [Perth City Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_City_Link) sank a section of the railway line to allow easy pedestrian access between [Northbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northbridge,_Western_Australia) and the CBD. The [Perth Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Arena) is an entertainment and sporting arena in the city link area that has received several architectural awards from institutions such as the [Design Institute of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_Institute_of_Australia), the [Australian Institute of Architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Institute_of_Architects), and [Colorbond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlueScope).[^cite_note-77] [St Georges Terrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Georges_Terrace) is the area's prominent street, with a large amount of office space in the CBD. [Hay Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hay_Street,_Perth) and [Murray Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Street,_Perth) have most of the retail and entertainment facilities. The city's tallest building is [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park_\(skyscraper\)), the [twelfth tallest building in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Australia).[^cite_note-78] The CBD until 2012 was the centre of a mining-induced boom, with several commercial and residential projects being built, including [Brookfield Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookfield_Place_\(Perth\)), a 244-metre (801 ft) office building for Anglo-Australian mining company [BHP](200825.md).[^cite_note-79]

### Metropolitan area

Main article: [Perth metropolitan region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_metropolitan_region)



Area of the Perth Metropolitan Region Scheme

Perth's metropolitan area extends along the coast to [Two Rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Rocks,_Western_Australia) in the north and [Singleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singleton,_Western_Australia) to the south,[^cite_note-landgate_map-80] a distance of approximately 125 kilometres (80 mi).[^cite_note-81] From the coast in the west to [Mundaring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundaring,_Western_Australia) in the east is a distance of approximately 50 kilometres (30 mi). The Perth metropolitan area covers 6,418 square kilometres (2,478 sq mi).[^cite_note-ABSGCCSAXLS-4] The built-up urban area of Perth is 1,722 square kilometres (665 sq mi), the same as [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan) or [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City) and slightly smaller than [London](17867.md), making Perth the 67th-largest urban area in the world. Perth is also the 50th-least densely populated out of the 990 urban areas in the world with a population above 500,000.[^cite_note-82]

The metropolitan region is defined by the *Planning and Development Act 2005* to include 30 [local government areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_areas_of_Western_Australia), with the outer extent being the [City of Wanneroo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Wanneroo) and the [City of Swan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Swan) to the north, the [Shire of Mundaring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire_of_Mundaring), [City of Kalamunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Kalamunda) and the [City of Armadale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Armadale) to the east, the [Shire of Serpentine-Jarrahdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire_of_Serpentine-Jarrahdale) to the south-east and the [City of Rockingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Rockingham) to the south-west, and including [Rottnest Island](216061.md) and [Garden Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_Island_\(Western_Australia\)) off the west coast.[^cite_note-MLGRP2011-83] This extent correlates with the [Metropolitan Region Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Region_Scheme), and the [Australian Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Bureau_of_Statistics)' Perth (Major Statistical Division).[^cite_note-MLGRP2011-83]

The metropolitan extent of Perth can be defined in other ways—the Australian Bureau of Statistics Greater Capital City Statistical Area, or [Greater Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Perth) in short, consists of that area, plus the [City of Mandurah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Mandurah) and the Pinjarra Level 2 Statistical Area[^cite_note-84] of the [Shire of Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire_of_Murray),[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] while the *Regional Development Commissions Act 1993* includes the [Shire of Serpentine-Jarrahdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire_of_Serpentine-Jarrahdale) in the [Peel region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peel_\(Western_Australia\)).[^cite_note-87]

### Geology and landforms

See also: [List of islands of Perth, Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Perth,_Western_Australia)

The largest river flowing through Perth is the Swan River, named for the native [black swans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_swan) by [Willem de Vlamingh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_de_Vlamingh), captain of a Dutch expedition and namer of Western Australia's [Rottnest Island](216061.md), who discovered the birds while exploring the area in 1697.[^cite_note-88] This water body is also known as *Derbarl Yerrigan*.[^cite_note-det-derbarlyerrigan-89] The city centre and most of the suburbs are on the sandy and relatively flat [Swan Coastal Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Coastal_Plain), which lies between the [Darling Scarp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darling_Scarp) and the [Indian Ocean](14580.md). The soils of this area are quite infertile.

Much of colonial Perth was built on the [Perth Wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Wetlands), a series of freshwater wetlands running from [Herdsman Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herdsman_Lake) in the west through to [Claisebrook Cove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claisebrook_Cove) in the east.[^cite_note-90]

To the east, the city is bordered by a low escarpment called the Darling Scarp. Perth is on generally flat, rolling land, largely due to the high amount of sandy soils and deep [bedrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedrock). The Perth metropolitan area has two major river systems, one made up of the Swan and [Canning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canning_River_\(Western_Australia\)) Rivers, and one of the [Serpentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpentine_River_\(Western_Australia\)) and [Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_River_\(Western_Australia\)) Rivers, which discharge into the [Peel Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peel_Inlet) at [Mandurah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandurah). The [Perth-Gingin Shrublands and Woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth-Gingin_Shrublands_and_Woodlands) and [Banksia Woodlands of the Swan Coastal Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banksia_Woodlands_of_the_Swan_Coastal_Plain) straddle the metropolitan area.

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Perth)



Sunset over the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) at [City Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Beach,_Western_Australia)

Perth receives moderate, though highly seasonal, winter-based rainfall. Summers are generally hot, sunny and dry, lasting from December to March, with February generally the hottest month. Winters are relatively mild and wet, giving Perth a [hot-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Csa*).[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] Perth has an average of 8.8 hours of sunshine per day, which equates to around 3,200 hours of sunshine and 138.7 clear days annually, making it Australia's sunniest capital city.[^cite_note-PerthAP-93]

Summers are typically hot and dry but not completely devoid of rain, with sporadic rainfall in the form of short-lived thunderstorms, weak [cold fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_front) and on occasions decaying [tropical cyclones](8282374.md) from Western Australia's north-west, which can bring heavy rain. Temperatures above 35 °C (95 °F) occur, on average, 26 days per year and rise above 40 °C (104 °F) on 5 days per year. The highest temperature recorded in Perth was 46.2 °C (115.2 °F) on 23 February 1991, although [Perth Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Airport) recorded 46.7 °C (116.1 °F) on the same day.[^cite_note-PerthAP-93][^cite_note-Perthmedia2008-94] On most summer afternoons a [sea breeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_breeze), known locally as the *[Fremantle Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle_Doctor)*, blows from the south-west, providing relief from the hot north-easterly winds. Temperatures often fall below 30 °C (86 °F) a few hours after the arrival of the wind change.[^cite_note-hazards-95] In the summer, the 3 p.m. dewpoint averages at around 12 °C (54 °F).[^cite_note-PerthAP-93]

Winters are mild and wet, with most of Perth's annual rainfall between May and September. Winters see significant rainfall as [frontal systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_front) move across the region, interspersed with clear and sunny days where minimum temperatures tend to drop below 5 °C (41 °F). The lowest temperature recorded in Perth was −0.7 °C (30.7 °F) on 17 June 2006.[^cite_note-Perthmedia2008-94] The lowest temperature within the [Perth metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_metropolitan_area) was −3.4 °C (25.9 °F) on the same day at [Jandakot Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jandakot_Airport), although temperatures at or below zero are rare occurrences. The lowest maximum temperature recorded in Perth is 8.8 °C (47.8 °F) on 26 June 1956. It occasionally gets cold enough for frost to form.[^cite_note-JandakotAP-96] While [snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_in_Australia) has never been recorded in the Perth CBD, light snowfalls have been reported in outer suburbs of Perth in the [Perth Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Hills) around [Kalamunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalamunda), [Roleystone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roleystone) and [Mundaring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundaring). The most recent snowfall was in 1968.

The rainfall pattern has changed in Perth and [south-west Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_drainage_division) since the mid-1970s. A significant reduction in winter rainfall has been observed with a greater number of extreme rainfall events in the summer,[^cite_note-97] such as the slow-moving storms on 8 February 1992 that brought 120.6 millimetres (4.75 in) of rain,[^cite_note-Perthmedia2008-94][^cite_note-hazards-95] heavy rainfall associated with a [tropical low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016-17_Australian_region_cyclone_season#Tropical_Low_15U) on 10 February 2017, which brought 114.4 millimetres (4.50 in) of rain,[^cite_note-98] and the remnants of [ex-Tropical Cyclone Joyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Australian_region_cyclone_season#Tropical_Cyclone_Joyce) on 15 January 2018 with 96.2 millimetres (3.79 in).[^cite_note-99] Perth was also hit by a [severe thunderstorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Western_Australian_storms) on 22 March 2010, which brought 40.2 mm (1.58 in) of rain, and large hail and caused significant damage in the metropolitan area.[^cite_note-100]

The average sea temperature ranges from 18.9 °C (66.0 °F) in October to 23.4 °C (74.1 °F) in March.[^cite_note-101]

### Isolation

With more than two million residents, Perth is one of the [most isolated major cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extremes_on_Earth#Other_places_considered_the_most_remote) in the world. The nearest city with a population of more than 100,000 is [Adelaide](1148.md), over 2,100 km (1,305 mi) away.[^cite_note-gill-103] Perth is geographically closer to both [Timor-Leste](19159399.md) (2,800 km or 1,700 mi), and [Jakarta](16275.md), Indonesia (3,000 km or 1,900 mi), than to [Sydney](27862.md) (3,300 km or 2,100 mi).[^cite_note-gill-103]

Perth's population is notable for the high proportion of British- and Irish-born residents. At the 2021 Census, 169,938 England-born Perth residents were counted,[^cite_note-auto1-107] ahead of even Sydney (151,614),[^cite_note-112] despite the latter having well over twice the population.



[Russell Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Square,_Perth), Northbridge—historically the favoured meeting place of the Italian community of "[Little Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Italy)"

[^cite_note-113]

The ethnic make-up of Perth changed in the second part of the 20th century when significant numbers of continental European immigrants arrived in the city. Prior to this, Perth's population had been almost completely [Anglo-Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Celtic_Australian) in ethnic origin. As [Fremantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle) was the first landfall in Australia for many migrant ships coming from Europe in the 1950s and 1960s, Perth started to experience a diverse influx of people, including [Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Australians), [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_people), [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people), [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_people), [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people), [Croats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_people) and [Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonians_\(ethnic_group\)). The Italian influence in the Perth and Fremantle area has been substantial, evident in places like the "Cappuccino strip" in Fremantle featuring many Italian eateries and shops. In Fremantle, the traditional Italian [blessing of the fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blessing_of_the_fleet) festival is held every year at the start of the fishing season. In Northbridge every December is the San Nicola (Saint Nicholas) Festival, which involves a pageant followed by a concert, predominantly in Italian. Suburbs surrounding the Fremantle area, such as [Spearwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spearwood,_Western_Australia) and [Hamilton Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Hill,_Western_Australia), also contain high concentrations of Italians, Croatians and Portuguese. Perth has also been home to a small [Jewish](25955086.md) community since 1829[^cite_note-114]—numbering 6,331 in 2021—who have emigrated primarily from Eastern Europe and more recently from South Africa.[^cite_note-censusj-115]



[Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Perth) entry on Roe Street

A more recent wave of arrivals includes [white South Africans](4734206.md). South Africans overtook those born in Italy as the fourth-largest foreign group in 2001. By 2016, there were 35,262 South Africans residing in Perth.[^cite_note-quickstats.censusdata.abs.gov.au-116] Many [Afrikaners](18947965.md) and [Anglo-Africans](1472509.md) emigrated to Perth during the 1980s and 1990s, with the phrase "packing for Perth" becoming associated with South Africans who choose to emigrate abroad, sometimes regardless of the destination.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] As a result, the city has been described as "the Australian capital of South Africans in exile".[^cite_note-119]

Since the end of the [White Australia policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Australia_policy) in 1973, [Asia](689.md) has become an increasingly significant source of migrants, with communities from [Vietnam](202354.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Indonesia](14579.md), [Thailand](30128.md), [Singapore](27318.md), [Hong Kong](13404.md), [Mainland China](55079.md) and [India](14533.md) all now well established. There were 112,293 persons of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Australian) descent in Perth in 2016—5.3% of the city's population.[^cite_note-auto1-107] These are supported by the Australian Eurasian Association of Western Australia,[^cite_note-120] which also serves a community of Portuguese-Malacca Eurasian or [Kristang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristang_people) immigrants.[^cite_note-121]

Middle Eastern immigrants have a presence in Perth. They come from a variety of countries, including [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), [Syria](7515849.md), [Iran](14653.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), [Israel](9282173.md), [Lebanon](17771.md), [The United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_United_Arab_Emirates), [Oman](22316.md), [Yemen](350939.md) and [Afghanistan](737.md).

The Indian community includes a substantial number of [Parsees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsi_people) who emigrated from [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay)—Perth being the closest Australian city to India—in 2021 those with Indian ancestry accounted for 3.5% of Perth's population[^cite_note-auto1-107] Perth is also home to the largest population of [Anglo-Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Burmese) in the world; many settled here following the independence of Burma in 1948 with immigration taking off after 1962. The city is now the cultural hub for Anglo-Burmese worldwide.[^cite_note-122] There is also a substantial [Anglo-Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Indian) population in Perth, who also settled in the city following the independence of India.

At the 2021 census, 2% of Perth's population identified as being [Aboriginal](2912594.md) and/or [Torres Strait Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_Strait_Islander).[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-quickstats_2021-124]

### Language

At the 2021 census, 74% of inhabitants spoke only English at home, with the next most common languages being [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese) (2.3%), [Italian](14708.md) (1.1%), [Vietnamese](32511.md) (1.0%), [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) (0.9%) and [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese) (0.9%).[^cite_note-quickstats_2021-124]

### Religion



[St Mary's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's_Cathedral,_Perth)

41.8% of the 2021 census respondents in Perth had no religion,[^cite_note-quickstats_2021-124] as against 38.4% of national population.[^cite_note-quickstats_2021-124] In 1911, the national figure was 0.4%.[^cite_note-Losing-125]

[Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholics) are the largest single Christian denomination in the Greater Perth area at 19.5%.[^cite_note-quickstats_2021-124] Perth is the seat of the [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Perth).[^cite_note-126] The [Personal Ordinariate of Our Lady of the Southern Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_Ordinariate_of_Our_Lady_of_the_Southern_Cross) claims over 2,000 members.[^cite_note-127] [Anglicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican) are 9.9% of the population.[^cite_note-quickstats_2021-124] Perth is the seat of the [Anglican Diocese of Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Perth).[^cite_note-128]

[Buddhism](3267529.md) and [Islam](6037917.md) each have more than 50,000 adherents.[^cite_note-profile.id.com.au-129] The suburb of [Gidgengannup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gidgegannup,_Western_Australia) in the [Perth Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Hills) is home to the Dhammasara Nuns Monastery of the Buddhist [Thai Forest Tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_Forest_Tradition).[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] Over 31,000 members of the [Uniting Church in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniting_Church_in_Australia) live in Perth.[^cite_note-profile.id.com.au-129]

Perth has the third largest [Jewish](25955086.md) population in Australia, numbering approximately 6,331 in the 2021 census.[^cite_note-censusj-115] Perth's [Jewish Day School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Day_School), [Carmel School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmel_School_\(Perth\)) claims a city Jewish population closer to 10,000.[^cite_note-132] The city is home to both Orthodox and Progressive synagogues, most notably [Perth Hebrew Congregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Hebrew_Congregation) and [Temple David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_David_\(Perth,_Western_Australia\)). There is also a [Chabad house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabad_house) in Perth.

The [Baháʼí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith) community in Perth numbers around 2,178.[^cite_note-profile.id.com.au-129] [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) has over 49,000 adherents in Perth;[^cite_note-profile.id.com.au-129] the [Diwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diwali) (festival of lights) celebration in 2009 attracted over 20,000 visitors. There are Hindu temples in [Canning Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canning_Vale), [Anketell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anketell) and a [Swaminarayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaminarayan_\(spiritual_tradition\)) temple in [Bennett Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bennett_Springs,_Western_Australia).[^cite_note-133] Hinduism is the fastest growing religion in Australia.[^cite_note-134] Perth is also home to 4,719 [Mormons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormons)[^cite_note-profile.id.com.au-129] and the [Perth Australia Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Australia_Temple) of [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints).

## Governance

Perth, like the rest of Australia, is governed by three levels of government: local, state and federal.[^cite_note-135]

### Local

[Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_House,_Perth)

The Perth metropolitan area is divided into thirty [local government bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Australia), including the [City of Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Perth) which administers Perth's central business district. The outer extent of the [administrative region of Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_metropolitan_region) comprises the [City of Wanneroo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Wanneroo) and the [City of Swan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Swan) to the north, the [Shire of Mundaring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire_of_Mundaring), [City of Kalamunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Kalamunda) and the [City of Armadale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Armadale) to the east, the [Shire of Serpentine-Jarrahdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire_of_Serpentine-Jarrahdale) to the south-east and the [City of Rockingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Rockingham) to the south-west, and including the islands of [Rottnest Island](216061.md) and [Garden Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_Island,_Western_Australia) off the west coast.[^cite_note-MLGRP2011-83]

### State

Further information: [Government of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Western_Australia)



[Supreme Court of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Western_Australia)

The state's highest court, the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Western_Australia), is located in Perth,[^cite_note-136] along with the [District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Court_of_Western_Australia)[^cite_note-137] and [Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Court_of_Western_Australia)[^cite_note-138] Courts. ## Economy

See also: [Economy of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Western_Australia)



[Brookfield Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookfield_Place_\(Perth\)) towering above heritage buildings in the CBD. Built during the late 2000s mining boom, it is tenanted by the mining company [BHP](200825.md).

By virtue of its population and role as the administrative centre for business and government, Perth dominates the [Western Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian) economy, despite the major mining, petroleum and agricultural export industries being located elsewhere in the state.[^cite_note-GreaterPerth-75] Perth's function as the state's capital city, its economic base and population size have also created development opportunities for many other businesses oriented to local or more diversified markets. Perth's economy has been changing in favour of the service industries since the 1950s. Although one of the major sets of services it provides is related to the resources industry and, to a lesser extent, agriculture, most people in Perth are not connected to either; they have jobs that provide services to other people in Perth.[^cite_note-Structure2005-144]

As a result of Perth's relative geographical isolation, it has never had the necessary conditions to develop significant manufacturing industries other than those serving the immediate needs of its residents, mining, agriculture and some specialised areas, such as, in recent times, niche shipbuilding and maintenance. It was simply cheaper to import all the needed manufactured goods from either the [eastern states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_states_of_Australia) or overseas.

Industrial employment influenced the economic geography of Perth. After WWII, Perth experienced suburban expansion aided by high levels of car ownership. Workforce decentralisation and transport improvements made it possible for the establishment of small-scale manufacturing in the suburbs. Many firms took advantage of relatively cheap land to build spacious, single-storey plants in suburban locations with plentiful parking, easy access and minimal traffic congestion. "The former close ties of manufacturing with near-central and/or rail-side locations were loosened."[^cite_note-GreaterPerth-75]



Bulk carrier at the [CBH Grain Jetty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwinana_Grain_Terminal) in [East Rockingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Rockingham,_Western_Australia)

Industrial estates such as [Kwinana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwinana_Beach), [Welshpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welshpool,_Western_Australia) and [Kewdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kewdale) were post-war additions contributing to the growth of manufacturing south of the river. The establishment of the Kwinana industrial area was supported by standardisation of the east–west rail gauge linking Perth with eastern Australia. Since the 1950s the area has been dominated by heavy industry, including an oil refinery, steel-rolling mill with a blast furnace, alumina refinery, power station and a nickel refinery. Another development, also linked with rail standardisation, was in 1968 when the [Kewdale Freight Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kewdale_Freight_Terminal) was developed adjacent to the Welshpool industrial area, replacing the former Perth railway yards.[^cite_note-GreaterPerth-75]

With significant population growth post-WWII,[^cite_note-145] employment growth occurred not in manufacturing but in retail and wholesale trade, business services, health, education, community and personal services, and in public administration. Increasingly it was these services sectors, concentrated around the Perth metropolitan area, that provided jobs.[^cite_note-GreaterPerth-75]

Perth has also become a hub of technology-focused startups since the early 2000s that provide jobs to the Perth community. Companies such as [Canva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canva), VGW, Appbot, Agworld and Healthengine all hail from Perth and have made headlines globally. Organisations like StartupWA, Spacecubed and Perth Angels, and programs like Meshpoints, Curtin Accelerate and Plus Eight are all focused on creating a startup culture in Perth and growing the next generation of Perth-based employers.[^cite_note-146]

## Education

See also: [Education in Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Western_Australia)

Education is compulsory in [Western Australia](33613.md) between the ages of six and seventeen, corresponding to primary and secondary school.[^cite_note-147] Tertiary education is available through several universities and [technical and further education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_and_further_education) (TAFE) colleges.

### Primary and secondary



[Perth Modern School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Modern_School), Perth's first [public high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_school)

Students may attend either public schools, run by the state government's [Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Education_\(Western_Australia\)), or private schools, usually associated with a religion, or engage in home schooling.

The [Western Australian Certificate of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian_Certificate_of_Education) (WACE) is the credential given to students who have completed Years 11 and 12 of their secondary schooling.[^cite_note-148]

In 2012 the minimum requirements for students to receive their WACE changed.[^cite_note-149]

### Tertiary



The [University of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Australia), located in [Crawley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crawley,_Western_Australia)

Perth is home to four public universities: the [University of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Australia), [Curtin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtin_University), [Murdoch University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murdoch_University) and [Edith Cowan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edith_Cowan_University). There are also two private universities, the [University of Notre Dame Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame_Australia), and a local campus of the [University of Divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Divinity).

[The University of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_University_of_Western_Australia), which was founded in 1911,[^cite_note-150] is renowned as one of Australia's leading research institutions.[^cite_note-151] The university's monumental neo-classical architecture, most of which is carved from white limestone, is a notable tourist destination in the city. It is the only university in the state to be a member of the [Group of Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_Eight_\(Australian_universities\)), as well as the [Sandstone universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone_universities). It is also the state's only university to have produced a [Nobel Laureate](21201.md):[^cite_note-152] [Barry Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Marshall), who graduated with a Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery in 1975 and was awarded a joint Nobel Prize in physiology or medicine in 2005 with [Robin Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Warren).

[Curtin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtin_University), previously known as Western Australian Institute of Technology (1966–1986) and Curtin University of Technology (1986–2010), is Western Australia's largest university by student population.[^cite_note-153]

[Murdoch University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murdoch_University) was founded in 1973 and incorporates Western Australia's only veterinary school and, until its controversial closure in 2020, Australia's only [theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology) program to be completely integrated into a secular university.

[Edith Cowan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edith_Cowan_University) was established in 1991 from the existing [Western Australian College of Advanced Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Advanced_Education) which itself was formed on 11 December 1981 from the existing Teachers Colleges at Claremont, Nedlands, Churchlands and Mount Lawley after Graylands had merged into Claremont, Churchlands and Mount Lawley in 1979. It incorporates the [Western Australian Academy of Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian_Academy_of_Performing_Arts).

The [University of Notre Dame Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame_Australia) was established in 1990. Notre Dame was established as a [Catholic university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_university) with its lead campus in [Fremantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle) and a large campus in Sydney, and a campus in Broome. Its lead campus is in the west end of Fremantle, using historic port buildings built in the 1890s, giving Notre Dame a distinct European university atmosphere.

The Melbourne-based [University of Divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Divinity) established a campus in Perth in 2022 through its admission of [Wollaston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollaston_College), the theological college of the [Anglican Diocese of Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Perth), as a collegiate college of the university.

Colleges of TAFE provide trade and vocational training, including certificate- and diploma-level courses. TAFE began as a system of technical colleges and schools under the Education Department, from which they were separated in the 1980s and ultimately formed into regional colleges. Two are in the Perth metropolitan area: [North Metropolitan TAFE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Metropolitan_TAFE) (formerly [Central Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Institute_of_Technology) and [West Coast Institute of Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_Institute_of_Training)); and [South Metropolitan TAFE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Metropolitan_TAFE) (formerly [Polytechnic West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytechnic_West) and [Challenger Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenger_Institute_of_Technology)).

### Arts and entertainment

Scene from the inauguration of the 2015 [Perth Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Festival), Australia's oldest continuously running cultural festival

A number of cultural events are held in Perth. Held annually since 1953, [Perth Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Festival) is Australia's longest running annual cultural festival and includes the [Perth Writers Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Writers_Festival) and the Winter Arts Festival. The [Fringe World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fringe_World) Festival has been held annually across January and February in Perth since 2012.[^cite_note-159] Perth also hosts annual music festivals including Listen Out, Origin and [St Jerome's Laneway Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Jerome's_Laneway_Festival). The [Perth International Comedy Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_International_Comedy_Festival) features a variety of local and international comedic talent, with performances held at the Astor Theatre and nearby venues in Mount Lawley. Regular night food market events are held during the summer months throughout the Perth CBD and surrounding suburbs. [Sculpture by the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculpture_by_the_Sea) showcases a range of local and international sculptors' creations along [Cottesloe Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottesloe,_Western_Australia). There is also a wide variety of [public art and sculptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_public_art_in_Western_Australia) on permanent display across the city.

The [Perth Cultural Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Cultural_Centre) is home to many of the city's major arts, cultural and educational institutions, including the [Art Gallery of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Gallery_of_Western_Australia), [Western Australian Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian_Museum), [State Library of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Library_of_Western_Australia), [State Records Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Records_Office_of_Western_Australia) and [Perth Institute of Contemporary Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Institute_of_Contemporary_Arts) (PICA).[^cite_note-pcc-160] The [State Theatre Centre of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Theatre_Centre_of_Western_Australia) is also located there,[^cite_note-pcc-160] and is the home of the [Black Swan State Theatre Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Swan_State_Theatre_Company)[^cite_note-161]. Other performing arts companies based in Perth include the [West Australian Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Australian_Ballet), the [West Australian Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Australian_Opera) and the [West Australian Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Australian_Symphony_Orchestra), all of which present regular programs.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164] The [Western Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian) Youth Orchestras provide young musicians with performance opportunities in orchestral and other musical ensembles.[^cite_note-165]



[His Majesty's Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Majesty's_Theatre,_Perth)

Perth is also home to the [Western Australian Academy of Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian_Academy_of_Performing_Arts) at [Edith Cowan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edith_Cowan_University), from which many actors and broadcasters have launched their careers.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-waa17-167] The city's main performance venues include the Riverside Theatre within the [Perth Convention & Exhibition Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Convention_&_Exhibition_Centre),[^cite_note-168] the [Perth Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Concert_Hall_\(Western_Australia\)),[^cite_note-169] the historic [His Majesty's Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Majesty's_Theatre,_Western_Australia),[^cite_note-HMT_DCA-170] the [Regal Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regal_Theatre,_Perth) in Subiaco[^cite_note-171] and the [Astor Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astor_Theatre,_Perth) in [Mount Lawley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lawley,_Western_Australia).[^cite_note-172] [Perth Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Arena) can be configured as an entertainment or sporting arena, and concerts are also hosted at other sporting venues, including [Perth Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Stadium), [Perth High Performance Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_High_Performance_Centre) and [Perth Rectangular Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Rectangular_Stadium). Outdoor concert venues include [Quarry Amphitheatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarry_Amphitheatre), [Supreme Court Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_Gardens), [Kings Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Park,_Western_Australia) and [Russell Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Square,_Perth).

The largest performance area within the [State Theatre Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Theatre_Centre_of_Western_Australia), the Heath Ledger Theatre, is named in honour of Perth-born film actor [Heath Ledger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath_Ledger). Other performers from Perth include [Judy Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judy_Davis),[^cite_note-mra15-173] [Melissa George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_George),[^cite_note-alb18-174][^cite_note-bar17-175] [Tim Minchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Minchin),[^cite_note-tmc-176] [Lisa McCune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_McCune),[^cite_note-tho13-177] [Troye Sivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troye_Sivan), [Sam Worthington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Worthington) and [Isla Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isla_Fisher).[^cite_note-mit18-178] Performers that studied in Perth at the [Western Australian Academy of Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian_Academy_of_Performing_Arts) include [Hugh Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Jackman) and Lisa McCune.[^cite_note-waa17-167]



[Perth Concert Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Concert_Hall_\(Western_Australia\))

Due to Perth's relative isolation from other Australian cities, overseas performing artists sometimes exclude it from their Australian tour schedules. This isolation, however, is considered a key factor in the development of a distinct and tight-knit music scene in Perth, with [many bands and artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Musical_groups_from_Perth,_Western_Australia) hailing from the city.[^cite_note-179] Famous musical performers from Perth include the late [AC/DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC/DC) frontman [Bon Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Scott), whose heritage-listed grave at [Fremantle Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle_Cemetery) is reportedly the most visited grave in Australia.[^cite_note-180] Further notable music acts from Perth include [The Triffids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Triffids),[^cite_note-181] [The Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scientists),[^cite_note-182] [The Drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Drones_\(Australian_band\)),[^cite_note-183] [Tame Impala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tame_Impala),[^cite_note-mos17-184] [Karnivool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnivool),[^cite_note-mgr18-185] [Spacey Jane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacey_Jane),[^cite_note-186] and [Pendulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendulum_\(drum_and_bass_band\)).[^cite_note-Pendulum-187]

Perth has inspired various artistic and cultural works. [John Boyle O'Reilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Boyle_O'Reilly), a [Fenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenian) convict transported to Western Australia, published *[Moondyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moondyne)* in 1879, the most famous early novel about the Swan River Colony. Perth is also the setting for various works by novelist [Tim Winton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Winton), most notably *[Cloudstreet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloudstreet)* (1991). Songs that refer to the city include "I Love Perth" (1996) by [Pavement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavement_\(band\)), "Perth" (2011) by [Bon Iver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Iver), and "Perth" (2015) by [Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut_\(band\)). Films shot or set in Perth include *[Japanese Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Story)* (2003), *[These Final Hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/These_Final_Hours)* (2013), *[Kill Me Three Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_Me_Three_Times)* (2014) and *[Paper Planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_Planes_\(film\))* (2015). A dedicated screen production facility, [Perth Film Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Film_Studios), opened in January 2026.[^cite_note-188]

### Tourism and recreation

Main article: [Tourism in Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Perth)



The [Fremantle West End Heritage area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle_West_End_Heritage_area) is home to hundreds of Victorian and Edwardian era buildings.

Tourism is an important part of Perth's economy, with approximately 2.8 million domestic visitors and 0.7 million international visitors in the year ending March 2012.[^cite_note-QVS-march12-189] Tourist attractions are generally focused around the city centre, Fremantle, the coast and the Swan River. In addition to the Perth Cultural Centre, there are [dozens of museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Western_Australia) across the city. The [Scitech Discovery Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scitech) in [West Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Perth,_Western_Australia) is an interactive science museum, with regularly changing exhibitions on a large range of science and technology-based subjects. Scitech also conducts live science demonstration shows and operates the adjacent *Horizon* planetarium. The [WA Maritime Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WA_Maritime_Museum) in Fremantle displays maritime objects from all eras. It houses *[Australia II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_II)*, the yacht that won the [1983 America's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_America's_Cup), as well as a former [Royal Australian Navy](26327.md) submarine. Also in Fremantle is the [Army Museum of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Museum_of_Western_Australia), situated within a historic artillery barracks. The museum consists of several galleries that reflect the Army's involvement in Western Australia and the military service of Western Australians.[^cite_note-190] The museum holds numerous items of significance, including three Victoria Crosses.[^cite_note-191] Aviation history is represented by the [Aviation Heritage Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_Heritage_Museum_\(Western_Australia\)) in [Bull Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull_Creek,_Western_Australia), with its significant collection of aircraft, including a [Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_Lancaster) bomber and a [Catalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_PBY_Catalina) of the type operated from the Swan River during WWII.[^cite_note-192]



The "Wirin" sculpture at [Yagan Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yagan_Square)

There are many heritage sites in Perth's CBD, Fremantle and other parts of the metropolitan areas. Some of the oldest remaining buildings, dating back to the 1830s, include the [Round House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_House_\(Western_Australia\)) in Fremantle, the [Old Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Mill,_Perth) in South Perth, and the [Old Court House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Court_House,_Perth) in the city centre. Registers of important buildings are maintained by the [Heritage Council of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_Council_of_Western_Australia) and local governments. A late heritage building is the [Perth Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Mint).[^cite_note-193] [Yagan Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yagan_Square) connects [Northbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northbridge,_Western_Australia) and the [Perth CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_\(suburb\)), with a 45-metre-high digital tower and the 9-metre statue *Wirin* designed by [Noongar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noongar) artist [Tjyllyungoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tjyllyungoo). [Elizabeth Quay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Quay) is also a notable attraction in Perth, featuring [Swan Bells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Bells), a panoramic view of Swan River, and the sculpture *[Spanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanda_\(Australian_art_work\))* by artist [Christian de Vietri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_de_Vietri).



Forrest Place, a major pedestrian thoroughfare

Retail shopping in the Perth CBD is focused around Murray Street and Hay Street. Both these streets are pedestrian malls between [William Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Street,_Perth) and Barrack Street. [Forrest Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forrest_Place) is another pedestrian mall, connecting the Murray Street mall to Wellington Street and the [Perth railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_railway_station). A number of arcades run between Hay Street and Murray Street, including the [Piccadilly Arcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piccadilly_Theatre_and_Arcade), which housed the Piccadilly Cinema until it closed in late 2013. Other shopping precincts include [Watertown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour_Town) in West Perth, featuring factory outlets for major brands, the historically significant [Fremantle Markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle_Markets), which date to 1897, and the Midland townsite on [Great Eastern Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Eastern_Highway), combining historic development around the Town Hall and Post Office buildings with the modern [Midland Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Gate) shopping centre further east. [Joondalup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joondalup)'s central business district is largely a shopping and retail area lined with townhouses and apartments, and also features [Lakeside Joondalup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakeside_Joondalup). Joondalup was granted the status of "tourism precinct" by the State Government in 2009, allowing for extended retail trading hours.



Riverbank Estate Winery, Caversham in the [Swan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Valley_\(Western_Australia\))

Restaurants, bars and nightclubs can be found in the entertainment hubs of [Northbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northbridge,_Western_Australia) (just north of the Perth CBD), the west end of the CBD itself, [Elizabeth Quay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Quay), [Leederville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leederville,_Western_Australia), [Beaufort Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaufort_Street), [Scarborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough,_Western_Australia) and [Fremantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle). The [Crown casino and resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Perth) is located at [Burswood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burswood,_Western_Australia).

The [Swan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Valley_\(Western_Australia\)), with fertile soil, uncommon in the Perth region, features numerous wineries, such as the large complex at [Houghtons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houghton_Winery), the state's biggest producer, Sandalfords and many smaller operators, including microbreweries and rum distilleries. The Swan Valley also contains specialised food producers, many restaurants and cafes, and roadside local produce stalls that sell seasonal fruit throughout the year. [Tourist Drive 203](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_Drive_203_\(Western_Australia\)) is a circular route in the Swan Valley, passing by many attractions on [West Swan Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Swan_Road) and [Great Northern Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_Highway).



[Cottesloe Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottesloe_Beach) is a notable attraction.&#x20;

[Kings Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Park,_Western_Australia), in central Perth between the CBD and the [University of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Western_Australia), is one of the world's largest inner-city parks,[^cite_note-194] at 400.6 hectares (990 acres).[^cite_note-195] It has many landmarks and attractions, including the State War Memorial Precinct on Mount Eliza, Western Australian Botanic Garden and children's playgrounds. Other features include [DNA Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Park,_Western_Australia#DNA_Tower), a 15 m (49 ft) high [double helix staircase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_helix_staircase) that resembles the [deoxyribonucleic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deoxyribonucleic_acid) (DNA) molecule,[^cite_note-196] and [Jacob's Ladder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob's_Ladder_\(Perth\)), comprising 242 steps that lead down to [Mounts Bay Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mounts_Bay_Road).

[Hyde Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park,_Perth) is another inner-city park 2 km (1.2 mi) north of the CBD. It was gazetted as a public park in 1897, created from 15 ha (37 acres) of a chain of wetlands known as Third Swamp.[^cite_note-197] [Avon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avon_Valley_National_Park), [John Forrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Forrest_National_Park) and [Yanchep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanchep_National_Park) national parks are areas of protected bushland at the northern and eastern edges of the metropolitan area. Within the city's northern suburbs is Whiteman Park, a 4,000-hectare (9,900-acre) bushland area, with bushwalking trails, bike paths, sports facilities, playgrounds, a vintage tramway, a light railway on a 6-kilometre (3.7 mi) track, motor and tractor museums, and [Caversham Wildlife Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caversham_Wildlife_Park).



[Hyde Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park,_Perth)

[Perth Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Zoo), in South Perth, houses a variety of Australian and exotic animals from around the globe. The zoo is home to highly successful breeding programs for [orangutans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orangutans) and [giraffes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giraffes), and participates in captive breeding and reintroduction efforts for a number of Western Australian species, including the [numbat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbat), the [dibbler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dibbler), the [chuditch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Quoll) and the [western swamp tortoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_swamp_tortoise).[^cite_note-198]

More wildlife can be observed at the [Aquarium of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquarium_of_Western_Australia) in [Hillarys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillarys,_Western_Australia), Australia's largest aquarium, specialising in marine animals that inhabit the 12,000-kilometre-long (7,500 mi) western coast of Australia. The northern Perth section of the coastline is known as [Sunset Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset_Coast); it includes numerous beaches and the [Marmion Marine Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmion_Marine_Park), a protected area inhabited by tropical fish, [Australian sea lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_sea_lion) and [bottlenose dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottlenose_dolphin), and traversed by [humpback whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humpback_whales). [Tourist Drive 204](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_Drive_204_\(Western_Australia\)), also known as Sunset Coast Tourist Drive, is a designated route from [North Fremantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Fremantle) to [Iluka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iluka,_Western_Australia) along coastal roads.

## Sport

Main article: [Sport in Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Western_Australia)

The climate of Perth allows for extensive outdoor sporting activity, and this is reflected in the wide variety of sports available to residents of the city. Perth was host to the [1962 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_British_Empire_and_Commonwealth_Games) and the [1987 America's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_America's_Cup) defence (based at [Fremantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle)). [Australian rules football](2403.md) is the most popular spectator sport in Perth—nearly 23% of Western Australians attended a match at least once in 2009–2010.[^cite_note-199] The two [Australian Football League](4732785.md) teams located in Perth, the [West Coast Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_Eagles) and the [Fremantle Football Club](312661.md), have two of the largest fan bases in the country. The Eagles, the older club, was until recently, one of the most successful teams in the league, and one of the largest sporting clubs in Australia. The next level of football is the [Western Australian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian_Football_League), comprising nine clubs each having a League, Reserves and Colts team. Each of these clubs has a junior football system for ages 7 to 17. The next level of Australian rules football is the [Perth Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Football_League), comprising 68 clubs servicing senior footballers within the metropolitan area. Other popular sports include [cricket](25675557.md), [basketball](3921.md), [soccer](10568.md), [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league), [motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsport) and [rugby union](25405.md).[^cite_note-200]

Perth has hosted numerous state and international sporting events. Ongoing international events include the [ATP Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Cup) (replacing the [Hopman Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopman_Cup) in 2020) during the first week of January at the [Perth Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Arena), and the [Perth International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_International) golf tournament at [Lake Karrinyup Country Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Karrinyup_Country_Club). In addition to these Perth has hosted the [Rally Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rally_Australia) of the [World Rally Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Rally_Championships) from 1989 to 2006, international [rugby union](25405.md) games, including qualifying and pool stage matches for the [2003 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Rugby_World_Cup) and the [Bledisloe Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bledisloe_Cup) in 2019. The 1991 and 1998 [FINA World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FINA_World_Championships_-_Long_Course) were held in Perth.[^cite_note-FINAWorldChampionships-201] Four races (2006, 2007, 2008 and 2010) in the [Red Bull Air Race World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Air_Race_World_Championship) have been held on a stretch of the Swan River called [Perth Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Water), using Langley Park as a temporary airfield.[^cite_note-redbullairrace-202] Several [motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsport) facilities exist in Perth including [Perth Motorplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Motorplex), catering to drag racing and speedway, and [Wanneroo Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanneroo_Raceway) for circuit racing and drifting, which hosts a [V8 Supercars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V8_Supercars) round. Perth also has two [thoroughbred racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoroughbred_racing_in_Australia) facilities: [Ascot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascot_Racecourse,_Western_Australia), home of the [Railway Stakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_Stakes_\(Perth_racing\)) and [Perth Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Cup); and [Belmont Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont_Park,_Western_Australia). [Daniel Ricciardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Ricciardo) is a Perth-born [Formula 1](10854.md) driver who most recently raced for the [Visa Cash App RB Formula One Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RB_Formula_One_Team) from 2023 to 2024 and previously from 2012 to 2013 when the team was known as [Scuderia Toro Rosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Toro_Rosso), having also raced for [Red Bull Racing](1173193.md), [Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_in_Formula_One) and [McLaren](20994.md), respectively.

The [WACA Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WACA_Ground) opened in the 1890s and has hosted [Test cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket) since 1970. The [Western Australian Athletics Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian_Athletics_Stadium) opened in 2009.

## Infrastructure

### Health

See also: [List of hospitals in Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_Western_Australia)



[Perth Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Children's_Hospital)

Perth has ten large hospitals with emergency departments. As of 2013, [Royal Perth Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Perth_Hospital) in the city centre is the largest, with others spread around the metropolitan area: [Armadale Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armadale_Health_Service), [Joondalup Health Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joondalup_Health_Campus), [King Edward Memorial Hospital for Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_Memorial_Hospital_for_Women) in [Subiaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subiaco,_Western_Australia), [Rockingham General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockingham_General_Hospital), [Sir Charles Gairdner Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Charles_Gairdner_Hospital) in [Nedlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nedlands,_Western_Australia), [St John of God Murdoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John_of_God_Murdoch_Hospital) and [Subiaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_John_of_God_Subiaco_Hospital) Hospitals, [Midland Health Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Health_Campus) in [Midland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland,_Western_Australia), and [Fiona Stanley Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiona_Stanley_Hospital) in [Murdoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murdoch,_Western_Australia). [Perth Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Children's_Hospital) is the state's only specialist children's hospital, and [Graylands Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graylands_Hospital) is the only public stand-alone psychiatric teaching hospital. Most of these are public hospitals, with some operating under public-private partnerships. St John of God Murdoch and Subiaco Hospitals, and Hollywood Hospital are large privately owned and operated hospitals.

A number of other public and private hospitals operate in Perth.[^cite_note-203]

### Transport

Main article: [Transport in Perth, Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Perth,_Western_Australia)



[Transperth B-Series train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transperth_B-series_train) at [Perth railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_railway_station)

#### Air

Perth is served by [Perth Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Airport) in the city's east for regional, domestic and international flights and [Jandakot Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jandakot_Airport) in the city's southern suburbs for general aviation and charter flights.

#### Cycling

[Cycling in Perth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Perth) is common on the roads and paths for [recreation](5931.md#Recreational), [commuting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_commuting) and [sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_racing).

#### Driving

Perth has a road network with three freeways—[Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchell_Freeway), [Kwinana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwinana_Freeway) and [Graham Farmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Farmer_Freeway)—and nine metropolitan highways. The [Northbridge Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northbridge_Tunnel), part of the Graham Farmer Freeway, is the only significant road tunnel in Perth.

#### Public transport (trains, buses, ferries)

Perth metropolitan public transport is known as [Transperth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transperth), and includes [trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railways_in_Perth), [buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buses_in_Perth) and ferries, which are provided by the [Public Transport Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Transport_Authority_\(Western_Australia\)). Links to rural areas provided by [Transwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transwa). There are [74 railway stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Transperth_railway_stations) and [14 bus-only stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Transperth_bus_stations) on the Transperth network.[^cite_note-204]

Perth provides [zero-fare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-fare) bus and train trips around the city centre (the "Free Transit Zone"), including four high-frequency [CAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Central_Area_Transit) bus routes.

The *[Indian Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Pacific)* passenger rail service connects Perth with [Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide_Parklands_Terminal) and [Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_railway_station,_Sydney) once per week in each direction. The *[Prospector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transwa_Prospector)* passenger rail service connects Perth with [Kalgoorlie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalgoorlie_railway_station) via several [Wheatbelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheatbelt_\(Western_Australia\)) towns, while the *[Australind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transwa_Australind)* connects to [Bunbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunbury_railway_station), the [MerredinLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MerredinLink) connects to [Merredin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merredin,_Western_Australia) and the *[AvonLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transwa_AvonLink)* connects to [Northam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northam_railway_station,_Western_Australia).

Rail freight terminates at the [Kewdale Rail Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kewdale_Rail_Terminal), 15 km (9 mi) south-east of the city centre.

#### Sea

Perth's main container and passenger port is at Fremantle, 19 km (12 mi) south-west at the mouth of the Swan River.[^cite_note-205] The [Fremantle Outer Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle_Outer_Harbour) at [Cockburn Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockburn_Sound) is one of Australia's major bulk cargo ports.[^cite_note-206]

### Utilities



[Mundaring Weir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundaring_Weir)

Perth's electricity is predominantly generated, supplied and retailed by three [Western Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian) Government corporations. [Verve Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verve_Energy) operates coal and gas power generation stations, as well as wind farms and other power sources.[^cite_note-207] The physical network is maintained by [Western Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Power_\(networks_corporation\)),[^cite_note-208] while [Synergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synergy_\(electricity_corporation\)), the state's largest energy retailer, sells electricity to residential and business customers.[^cite_note-209]

[Alinta Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alinta_Energy), which was previously a government owned company, had a monopoly in the domestic gas market since the 1990s. However, in 2013 [Kleenheat Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesfarmers#Chemicals,_Energy_and_Fertilisers) began operating in the market, allowing consumers to choose their gas retailer.[^cite_note-210]

The [Water Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Corporation) is the dominant supplier of water, as well as wastewater and drainage services, in Perth and throughout [Western Australia](33613.md). It is also owned by the state government.[^cite_note-211]

Perth's water supply has traditionally relied on both groundwater and rain-fed dams. Reduced rainfall in the region over recent decades had greatly lowered inflow to reservoirs and affected groundwater levels. Coupled with the city's relatively high growth rate, this led to concerns that Perth could run out of water in the near future.[^cite_note-RunOutOfWater-212] The [Western Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Australian) Government responded by building desalination plants, and introducing mandatory household sprinkler [restrictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_restrictions_in_Australia). The [Kwinana Desalination Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwinana_Desalination_Plant) was opened in 2006,[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214] and [Southern Seawater Desalination Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Seawater_Desalination_Plant) at [Binningup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binningup,_Western_Australia) (on the coast between Mandurah and Bunbury) began operating in 2011. A trial winter (1 June – 31 August) sprinkler ban was introduced in 2009 by the State Government, a move which the Government later announced would be made permanent.[^cite_note-215]
