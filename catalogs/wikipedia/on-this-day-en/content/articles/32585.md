**Volgograd**,[^cite_note-11] [formerly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_renaming) **Tsaritsyn**[^cite_note-12] (1589–1925) and **Stalingrad**[^cite_note-13] (1925–1961), is the [administrative centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_centre) and largest city of [Volgograd Oblast](478748.md), Russia. The city lies on the western bank of the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga), covering an area of 859.4 square kilometres (331.8 square miles), with a population of slightly over one million residents.[^cite_note-est.2020-14] Volgograd is the [16th-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Russia_by_population) by population size in Russia,[^cite_note-2021Census-6] the third-largest city of the [Southern Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Federal_District), and the [fourth-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga#Biggest_cities_on_the_shores_of_the_Volga) on the Volga.

The city was founded as the fortress of *Tsaritsyn* in 1589. By the 19th century, Tsaritsyn had become an important river-port and commercial centre, leading to its rapid population growth. In November 1917, at the start of the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), Tsaritsyn came under [Bolshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik) control. It fell briefly to the [White Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Army) in mid-1919 but [returned to Bolshevik control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tsaritsyn) in January 1920. In 1925, the city was renamed *Stalingrad* in honor of [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin), who took part in defending the city against the White Army. During [World War II](32927.md), [Axis forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) attacked the city, leading to the [Battle of Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad), the [largest and bloodiest battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_battles_by_casualties) in World War II,[^cite_note-15] from which it received the title of [Hero City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_City_\(Soviet_Union\)). The Soviet victory at Stalingrad is widely held to be the turning point of World War II.[^cite_note-Corrigan_2011-16][^cite_note-Bell_2011-17] In 1961, [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev)'s administration renamed the city to *Volgograd* as part of [de-Stalinization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De-Stalinization).

Volgograd today is the site of *[The Motherland Calls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Motherland_Calls)*, an 85-metre (279 ft) high statue dedicated to the heroes of the Battle of Stalingrad, which is the tallest statue in Europe, as well as the second tallest statue of a woman in the world. The city has many tourist attractions, such as museums, sandy beaches, and a self-propelled floating church. Volgograd was one of the host cities of the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md).[^cite_note-18]

## Etymology

Tsaritsyn was established in 1555 and was named after the [Tsaritsa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsaritsa_\(river\)?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] It was written as Царицынъ, with the [hard sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_sign).

When [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin) died in 1924, [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) was the [General Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union); Tsaritsyn was renamed Stalingrad in honour of his role in the defence of the city.[^cite_note-21] The name is derived from the compound of Stalin (Сталин; his name) and [grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grad_\(toponymy\)) ([град](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B4): name for a settlement in Russian).

In the aftermath of [Stalin's death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Joseph_Stalin), [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev) announced the policy of [de-Stalinization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De-Stalinization). The name was changed to Volgograd in 1961, derived from the name of the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga) river, on whose bank the city is situated.

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Volgograd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Volgograd).

### Tsaritsyn



Tsaritsyn Coat of Arms (1857) which was the arms of [Tsaritsynsky Uezd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsaritsynsky_Uyezd), in the [Saratov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratov_Governorate).

Although the city may have originated in 1555, documented evidence of *Tsaritsyn* at the [confluence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence) of the [Tsaritsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsaritsa_River?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga) rivers dates from 1589.[^cite_note-gr-3] The structure stood slightly above the mouth of the Tsaritsa River on the right bank. It soon became the nucleus of a trading settlement.

At the beginning of the 17th century, the garrison consisted of 350 to 400 people. In 1607 the fortress garrison rebelled for six months against the troops of Tsar [Vasili Shuisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasili_Shuisky). The following year saw the construction of the first stone church in the city, dedicated to St. John the Baptist.

From Streltsy in Dimitrovsk ([Kamyshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamyshin)) and [Tsaritsynye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsaritsyn), voivodes, were often appointed to Kazan and Kazan-Meshchersk orders from the cities of Sviyazhsk, Cheboksary, Alatyr, Kurmysh, Yadrin, Ottudova and Streltsov were hired. The Streltsy were paid a salary, and they tried to defend the Volga trade route from the Cossacks. At the same time, some of the Streltsy even joined the Cossacks.

In 1670, troops of [Stepan Razin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepan_Razin) captured the fortress; they left after a month. In 1708 the insurgent Cossack [Kondraty Bulavin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kondraty_Bulavin) (died July 1708) held the fortress. In 1717 in the [Kuban pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuban_pogrom?action=edit\&redlink=1), raiders from the [Kuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuban_River) under the command of the [Crimean Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars) [Bakhti Gerai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhti_Gerai?action=edit\&redlink=1) blockaded, the town and enslaved thousands, in the area. In August 1774 [Cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack) leader [Yemelyan Pugachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemelyan_Pugachev) unsuccessfully attempted to storm the city.

In 1691, Moscow established a [customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs)-post at Tsaritsyn.[^cite_note-22] In 1708 Tsaritsyn was assigned to the [Kazan Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_Governorate); in 1719 to the [Astrakhan Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrakhan_Governorate). According to the census in 1720, the city had a population of 408 people. In 1773 the settlement was designated as a provincial and district town. From 1779 it belonged to the [Saratov Viceroyalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratov_Viceroyalty?action=edit\&redlink=1). In 1780 the city came under the newly established [Saratov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratov_Governorate).

In the 19th century, Tsaritsyn became an important river-port and commercial center. As a result, it also became a hub for migrant workers; in 1895 alone, over 50,000 peasant migrants came to Tsaritsyn in search of work.[^cite_note-Henze-23] The population expanded rapidly, increasing from fewer than 3,000 people in 1807 to about 84,000 in 1900. By 1914, the population had again jumped and was estimated at 130,000.[^cite_note-Dixon-24] Sources show 893 Jews registered as living there in 1897, with the number exceeding 2,000 by the middle of the 1920s.[^cite_note-Krapivensky-25] At the turn of the nineteenth century, Tsaritsyn was essentially a frontier town; almost all of the structures were wooden, with neither paved roads nor utilities.[^cite_note-Dixon-24] The first [railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway) reached the town in 1862. The first [theatre](20913771.md) opened in 1872, the first [cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movie_theater) in 1907. In 1913 Tsaritsyn got its first tram-line, and the city's first electric lights were installed in the city center.

Between 1903 and 1907, the area was one of the least healthy in Europe, with a mortality rate of 33.6 for every 1000 persons. Untreated sewage spilled into the river, causing several [cholera](7591.md) epidemics between 1907 and 1910.[^cite_note-Dixon-24] Although the region had an active Sanitary Executive Commission that sent out instructions on the best ways to prevent outbreaks and dispatched a delegate from the Anti-Plague Commission to Tsaritsyn in 1907, local municipal officials did not put any precautions into place, citing economic considerations. The city's drinking water came directly from the river, the intake pipe dangerously close to both the port and the sewage drain. There were neither funds nor political will to close the port (the main hub of economic activity) or move the intake pipes. As a result, in the three years spanning 1908 to 1910, Tsaritsyn lost 1,045 people to cholera. With a population of only 102,452 at the time, that amounted to a 1.01% loss of the population.[^cite_note-Henze-23]

Between 1908 and 1911, Tsaritsyn was home to [Sergei Trufanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Trufanov), also known as the 'mad monk' Iliodor. He spent most of his time causing infighting and power struggles within the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church), fomenting [anti-semitic](1078.md) zeal and violence in local populations, attacking the press, denouncing local municipal officials and causing unrest wherever he went. The most permanent mark he left on the city was the Holy Spirit Monastery ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Свято-Духовский монастырь), built in 1909, parts of which still stand today.[^cite_note-Dixon-24]

In light of the explosive population growth, the lack of political action on sanitation and housing, the multiple epidemics and the presence of volatile personalities, it is no surprise that the lower Volga region was a hotbed of revolutionary activity and civil unrest. The inability of the Tsarist government to provide basic protections from cholera on the one hand and subjecting the populace to strict but ineffective health measures on the other, caused [multiple riots in 1829](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1826%E2%80%931837_cholera_pandemic), in the [1890s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1881%E2%80%931896_cholera_pandemic) and throughout the [first decade of the 1900s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1899%E2%80%931923_cholera_pandemic), setting the stage for multiple Russian revolutions and adding fuel to the political fire.[^cite_note-Henze-23] During the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War) of 1917–1923, Tsaritsyn came under Soviet control from November 1917. In 1918, [White Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Movement) troops under [Pyotr Krasnov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Krasnov), the [Ataman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ataman) of the [Don Cossack Host](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Cossack_Host), besieged Tsaritsyn. The Reds repulsed three assaults by the Whites. However, in June 1919 the White [Armed Forces of South Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_South_Russia), under the command of General [Denikin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Denikin), captured Tsaritsyn, and held it until January 1920. The fighting from July 1918 to January 1920 became known as the [Battle for Tsaritsyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_for_Tsaritsyn).

### Stalingrad

On April 10, 1925, the city was renamed Stalingrad, in honor of [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin), General Secretary of the Communist Party.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] This was officially to recognize the city and Stalin's role in its defense against the [Whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement) between 1918 and 1920.[^cite_note-28]

Once the Soviets established control, ethnic and religious minorities were targeted. The only Jewish school in the area was closed down in 1926.[^cite_note-Krapivensky-25] In 1928, a campaign was launched by the Regional Executive Council to close down the synagogue in Stalingrad. Due to local resistance, they were not successful until 1929, when the council convened a Special Commission. The Commission convinced local municipal authorities that the building was in need of major repairs, was unsafe and much too small for the over 800 worshippers who regularly attended for high holidays.[^cite_note-Krapivensky-25]

In 1931, the German settlement-colony [Old Sarepta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Sarepta) (founded in 1765) became a district of Stalingrad. Renamed *Krasnoarmeysky Rayon* (or "Red Army District"), it was the largest area of the city. The first higher education institute was opened in 1930. A year later, the Stalingrad Industrial Pedagogical Institute, now [Volgograd State Pedagogical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgograd_State_Pedagogical_University), was opened. Under Stalin, the city became a center of heavy industry and [transshipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transshipment) by rail and river.

#### Battle of Stalingrad

During [World War II](32927.md), German and Axis forces attacked the city, which, in 1942, became the site of one of the war's pivotal battles. The Battle of Stalingrad was the [deadliest single battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_battles_by_casualties) in the history of warfare (casualties estimates vary between 1,250,000[^cite_note-29] and 2,500,000[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]).

The battle began on August 23, 1942, and on the same day, the city suffered heavy aerial bombardment that reduced most of it to rubble. [Martial law](50477.md) had already been declared in the city on July 14. By September, the fighting reached the city center. It was of unprecedented intensity; the city's central railway station changed hands thirteen times, and the [Mamayev Kurgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamayev_Kurgan) (one of the highest points of the city) was captured and recaptured eight times.

By early November, the German forces controlled 90 percent of the city and had cornered the Soviets in two narrow pockets, but they were unable to eliminate the last pockets of Soviet resistance before Soviet forces launched a [huge counterattack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Uranus) on November 19. This resulted in the Soviet encirclement of the [German Sixth Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Army_\(Wehrmacht\)) and other Axis units. On January 31, 1943, [Field Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Marshal) [Friedrich Paulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Paulus), the Sixth Army's commander, surrendered; by February 2, with the elimination of straggling German troops, the Battle of Stalingrad was over.

The bombing campaign and five months of fighting destroyed 99% of the city.[^cite_note-\:0-32] Of the population of more than half a million before the battle, only 1,515 remained following the battle's conclusion.[^cite_note-\:0-32]

In 1945, the Soviet Union awarded Stalingrad the title [Hero City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_City_\(Soviet_Union\)) for its resistance. Great Britain's [King George VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_VI_of_the_United_Kingdom) awarded the citizens of Stalingrad the jewelled "[Sword of Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sword_of_Stalingrad)" in recognition of their bravery.[^cite_note-33]

A number of cities around the world (especially those that had suffered similar wartime devastation) established sister, friendship, and [twinning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_twinning) links (see list below) in the spirit of solidarity or reconciliation. One of the first "sister city" projects was that established during [World War II](32927.md) between Stalingrad and [Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md); both had suffered extensive devastation from aerial bombardment. In March 2022, this twinning link was suspended because of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-34]

### Volgograd

On 10 November 1961, [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev)'s administration changed the name of the city to Volgograd ("Volga City") as part of his programme of [de-Stalinization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De-Stalinization) following [Stalin's death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalin's_death). This action was and remains somewhat controversial, because Stalingrad has such importance as a symbol of resistance during World War II.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

During [Konstantin Chernenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Chernenko)'s brief rule in 1984, proposals were floated to revive the city's Stalinist name for that reason. There was a strong degree of local support for a reversion, but the [Russian Soviet government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_People's_Commissars_of_the_Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic) did not accept such proposals.[^cite_note-37]

On May 21, 2007, [Roman Grebennikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Grebennikov) of the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Russian_Federation) was elected as mayor with 32.47% of the vote, a plurality. Grebennikov became Russia's youngest mayor of a [federal subject](701438.md) administrative center at the time.[^cite_note-38]

On January 30, 2013, the Volgograd City Council passed a measure to use the title "[Hero City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_City_\(Soviet_Union\)) Stalingrad" in city statements on nine specific dates annually.[^cite_note-StalingradName-39][^cite_note-torygraf-40][^cite_note-41] On the following dates, the title "Hero City Stalingrad" can officially be used in celebrations:

- February 2 (end of the Battle of Stalingrad);

- February 23 ([Defender of the Fatherland Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defender_of_the_Fatherland_Day));

- May 9 ([Victory Day](337670.md));

- June 22 (start of [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa));

- August 23 (start of the [Battle of Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad));

- September 2 ([Victory over Japan Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_over_Japan_Day));

- November 19 (start of [Operation Uranus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Uranus));

## Governance

In 2011, the City Duma canceled direct election of the mayor and confirmed the position of City Manager. This was short-lived, as in March 2012, Volgograd residents voted for relevant [amendments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amendments) to the [city charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_charter) to reinstate the direct mayoral elections.[^cite_note-44]

### Administrative and municipal status



View of Voroshilovsky City District of Volgograd

The [Administrative divisions of Volgograd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Volgograd?action=edit\&redlink=1) consists of 8 districts; [Traktorozavodsky district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traktorozavodsky_district,_Volgograd?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Krasnooktyabrsky district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnooktyabrsky_district,_Volgograd?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Dzerzhinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzerzhinsky_district,_Volgograd?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Tsentraly district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsentraly_district,_Volgograd?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Voroshilovsky district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voroshilovsky_district,_Volgograd?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Kirovsky district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirovsky_district,_Volgograd?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Sovietsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovietsky_District,_Volgograd?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Krasnoarmeysky district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnoarmeysky_distract?action=edit\&redlink=1).

Volgograd is the [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center) of [Volgograd Oblast](478748.md).[^cite_note-45] Within the [framework of administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Administrative_divisions), it is incorporated as the **[city of oblast significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_federal_subject_significance) of Volgograd**—an administrative unit with the status equal to that of the [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Volgograd_Oblast).[^cite_note-Ref151-2] As a [municipal division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Municipal_divisions), the city of oblast significance of Volgograd is incorporated as **Volgograd Urban Okrug**.[^cite_note-Ref921-8]

## Economy

Further information on the private Russian bank: [Volgoprombank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgoprombank)

Although the city was on an important trade route for moving timber, grain, cotton, cast iron, fish, salt and linseed oil, the economic reach of the Volga was relatively small. When the first rail lines were linked up to Moscow in 1871, this isolated area was suddenly and efficiently connected to the rest of the empire. Thanks to that connection, the province became a major producer, processor and exporter of grain, supplying most of Russia. By the 1890s, the economy of Volgograd (then Tsaritsyn), relied mainly on the trade of grain, [naphtha](184342.md), fish and salt.[^cite_note-Henze-23] Modern Volgograd remains an important industrial city. Industries include [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding), [oil refining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_refining), [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel) and [aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminum) production, manufacture of heavy machinery and vehicles at the [Volgograd Tractor Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgograd_Tractor_Plant) and [Titan-Barrikady](54624831.md) plant, and chemical production. The large [Volgograd Hydroelectric Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgograd_Hydroelectric_Plant) is a short distance to the north of Volgograd. South of the city near the river [Lukoil](1027881.md) operates the large [Volgograd Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgograd_Refinery).

## Transportation

Volgograd is a major railway junction served by the [Privolzhskaya Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privolzhskaya_Railway). Rail links from the [Volgograd railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgograd_railway_station) include Moscow; Saratov; Astrakhan; the [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas) region of [Ukraine](31750.md); the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) and [Siberia](29639.md). It stands at the east end of the [Volga–Don Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Don_Canal), opened in 1952 to link the two great rivers of [Southern Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Federal_District). [European route E40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E40), the longest European route connecting [Calais](85468.md) in [France](5843419.md) with [Ridder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridder,_Kazakhstan) in [Kazakhstan](16642.md), passes through Volgograd. The [M6 highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M6_highway_\(Russia\)) between Moscow and the Caspian Sea also passes through the city. The [Volgograd Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgograd_Bridge), under construction since 1995, was inaugurated in October 2009.[^cite_note-46] The city river terminal is the center for local passenger shipping along the Volga River.

The [Stalingrad International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalingrad_International_Airport) provides air links to major Russian cities as well as [Antalya](361527.md), [Yerevan](34352.md) and [Aktau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aktau).

Volgograd's public transport system includes a [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) service known as the [Volgograd Metrotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgograd_metrotram). Local public transport is provided by buses, trolleybuses and trams.

The [Volga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_River) still is a very important communication channel.

## Culture

### Mamayev Kurgan Memorial Complex



Hall of Military Glory

A memorial complex commemorating the battle of Stalingrad, dominated by an immense allegorical sculpture *[The Motherland Calls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Motherland_Calls)*, was erected on the [Mamayev Kurgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamayev_Kurgan) (Russian: Мамаев Курган), the hill that saw some of the most intense fighting during the battle. This complex includes the Hall of Military Glory, a circular building housing an eternal flame and bearing plaques with the names of the fallen heroes of the Battle of Stalingrad. This memorial features an hourly changing of the guard that draws many tourists during the warmer months. Across from this Hall, there is a statue called Mother's Sorrow, which depicts a grieving woman holding a fallen soldier in her arms. During the summer months, this statue is surrounded by a small water feature, called the Lake of Tears. Further down the hill of this complex, there is a Plaza of Heroes (also known as Heroes' Square), featuring multiple allegorical sculptures of heroic deeds. This plaza is sometimes referred to by the title of the most famous of these sculptures, called "Having withstood, we conquered death".

### Panorama Museum



Panorama Museum of the Battle of Stalingrad, including Gerhardt's Mill

The Panorama Museum of the Battle of Stalingrad is a large cultural complex that sits on the shore of the [Volga river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga). It is located on the site of the "Penza Defense Junction", a group of buildings along Penzenskaya Street (now Sovetskaya Street), which was defended by the 13th Guards Rifle Division. The complex includes [Gerhardt's Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhardt's_Mill), which is preserved in its bombed out state. The museum on the complex grounds houses the largest painting in Russia, a [panoramic painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panoramic_painting) of the battlefield as seen from [Mamayev Kurgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamayev_Kurgan), where "The Motherland Calls" statue now stands. This museum also features Soviet military equipment from the 1940s, numerous exhibits of weapons (including a rifle of the famous sniper [Vasily Zaytsev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Zaitsev_\(sniper\))), uniforms, personal belongings of generals and soldiers involved in the battle and detailed maps and timelines of the battle.

### Planetarium



Volgograd Planetarium

The Volgograd Planetarium was a gift from [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) in honor of what would have been Stalin's 70th birthday.[^cite_note-48] Neoclassical in style, the building facade is designed like a Roman temple, with six [Tuscan columns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_order) topped by capitals decorated with stars. Designed by [Vera Ignatyevna Mukhina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Mukhina), the dome is crowned by a female personification of Peace, holding an astrolabe with a dove. Opened in 1954, it was only the second purpose-built planetarium in the Soviet Union. The entryway interior features a mural of Stalin in the white uniform of a naval admiral, surrounded by lilies and doves, more symbols of peace. On either side of the mural, are busts of [Konstantin Tsiolkovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Tsiolkovsky), a Soviet rocket scientist, and [Yuri Gagarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Gagarin), a Soviet pilot and cosmonaut and the first human to venture into outer space. On the second floor, there are large stained glass windows, featuring images related to Soviet space exploration. The planetarium was outfitted with a [Zeiss projector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeiss_projector), the first produced by the [Carl Zeiss Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Zeiss_AG) in their [Jena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jena) plant after the end of World War 2.[^cite_note-49] The projector supplied was the UPP-23/1s model, which was produced between 1954 and 1964; it is still operational and in regular use at the Volgograd Planetarium. The projector was supplemented by a digital system in 2019; the [Fulldome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulldome) Pro model LDX12. Zeiss also provided the 365mm [refractor telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refractor_telescope) for the observatory, which is still in operation today.[^cite_note-50] The planetarium hosts scientific and educational lectures, provides [Fulldome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulldome) shows, has scheduled tours, features daytime and nighttime observations and runs an astronomy club for children.[^cite_note-51]

### Other

Across the street from the Panorama Museum, stands [Pavlov's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlov's_House), another surviving monument to the Battle of Stalingrad. Several monuments and memorials can be found nearby, including a statue of Lenin, a statue in honor of children who survived war and another to the Pavlov's House defenders.

The [Musical Instrument Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_Instrument_Museum_\(Volgograd\)) is a branch of the Volgograd regional Museum of local lore.[^cite_note-52]

## Religion

As a port city along an important and busy trading route, Volgograd has long been a diverse place. An 1897 survey reveals 893 Jews (512 men and 381 women), 1,729 Muslims (938 men and 791 women), and 193 Catholics (116 men and 77 women).[^cite_note-53]

### Holy Spirit Monastery



Holy Spirit Monastery, before 1923

Land for the Holy Spirit Monastery was originally allocated in 1904, but construction did not begin until 1909 and was not complete until 1911. [Sergei Trufanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Trufanov), also known as the 'mad monk' of Tsaritsyn, was the driving force behind fundraising and getting the project off the ground.[^cite_note-Dixon-24] The original complex had a church that could accommodate 6,000 people, the monastery itself could house 500 and an auditorium that held 1,000. There was a school, space for workshops, a printing office and an [almshouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almshouse). The land the monastery stood on also hosted multiple gardens, a fountain and several inner yards.[^cite_note-54]

In 1912, the monastery was divided to a male and female section, housing both monks and nuns. In 1914, the school on the grounds of the Holy Spirit Monastery became part of the city school system and in 1915, housed 53 girls whose fathers were on the front lines. During the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), an infirmary was set up and the complex was alternately used by both the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks) and the [Whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement). In 1923, once the area was under firm Bolshevik control, the monastery was closed. During the following decades, the complex was used as an orphanage, a library, a cinema and a student hostel. Eventually, many of the buildings fell into disuse and became dilapidated. At the onset of the Second World War, the complex was given to the military and many of the original buildings were demolished.[^cite_note-55]

After the collapse of the USSR in 1991, the [Diocese of Volgograd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Volgograd?action=edit\&redlink=1) was established and the military began the process of transferring what was left of the Holy Spirit Monastery back to the church. A theological school was established in 1992 and restoration of the site continues today.[^cite_note-56]

### Alexander Nevsky Cathedral

Main article: [Cathedral of Alexander Nevsky (Volgograd)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Alexander_Nevsky_\(Volgograd\))



Original Alexander Nevsky Cathedral in Tsaritsyn, before 1932

Construction of the cathedral began on April 22, 1901, with the laying of the foundation stone by [Bishop Hermogenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermogenes_\(Dolganyov\)). The domes were installed in 1915 and consecration took place on May 19, 1918. Almost as soon as it was built, the cathedral fell out of use. The Soviet powers closed it down officially in 1929, with the crosses and bells removed and the liturgical objects confiscated. The cathedral was then used as a motor depot and eventually demolished in 1932. In 2001, the long project of rebuilding the cathedral was begun. The first foundation stone was laid in 2016 and the finished replica was finally consecrated in 2021 by [Patriarch Kirill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_Kirill_of_Moscow).[^cite_note-57]

The new church stands in central Volgograd, bounded by Communist Street ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Коммунистическая Улица, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *Kommunisticheskaya Ulitsa*) and Mir Street (Улица Мира, *Ulitsa Mira*) on the north and south and Volodarsk Street (Улица Володарского, *Ulitsa Volodarskovo*) and Gogol Street (Улица Гоголя, *Ulitsa Gogolya*) on the west and east, respectively. This area is also a park, called Alexander's Garden (Александровский Сад, *Aleksandrovskiy Sad*). The cathedral stands across the street from a World War 2 monument, and a statue of and chapel for, the eponymous [Alexander Nevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Nevsky).

### Floating churches



Original St.Nicholas floating church, consecrated in 1910

Volgograd, hosts one of the few self-propelled floating churches in the world: the chapel boat of Saint Vladimir of Volgograd. Spearheaded by Vladimir Koretsky and assisted by a [Dutch Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Orthodox) priest who was part of the organization [Aid to the Church in Need](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid_to_the_Church_in_Need) (ACN), the Saint Vladimir was consecrated in October 2004 on the shore of the Volga. Originally a decommissioned landing craft found in a shipyard outside St. Petersburg, it took two years to convert it into a floating church. The boat chapel sports three shining domes and was decorated with icons and religious motifs by a local Volgograd artist. On its maiden voyage, the Saint Vladimir reached [Astrakhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrakhan) in the south and [Saratov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saratov) in the north; traveling an 800 kilometer (\~500 mile) span of the Volga River.[^cite_note-BarbaLata-58]

In addition to this self propelled church, Vladimir Koretsky first built two other floating churches in Volgograd, both of which must be towed by another craft. The Saint Innocent was originally a repair vessel and was located in a shipyard in Volgograd. Despite it being in poor condition, the boat had good sized cabins and a kitchen unit; the hull was restored, the largest cabins were merged and a single shining dome was added. Icons and sacred relics were donated by parishes from all over the country and the floating church was consecrated on 22 May 1998. During its first year in operation, it visited 28 villages, where 446 people were baptised and 1,500 received communion. The Saint Innocent was mobile for four months of the year, operating mostly on the Don River, and spent the rest of the time moored in [Pyatimorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyatimorsk), providing a semi permanent church for that rural locality.[^cite_note-BarbaLata-58]

Due to the success of the Saint Innocent, the ACN launched the creation of a second floating church, this time built atop an old barge. Christened the Saint Nicholas, in honor of the original floating church built in 1910, it was moored at a yacht club in Volgograd for several years, serving as a place of worship for passing ships crews. It was later towed to [Oktyabrsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktyabrsky,_Russia#Volgograd_Oblast), a remote southern village of the Volgograd Oblast, to serve as a semi-permanent church.[^cite_note-BarbaLata-58]

All of these floating churches were inspired by the original; a retrofitted tug-passenger steamer, which ran between Kazan and Astrakhan, named the Saint Nicholas. Commissioned in 1858, it was first christened the Kriushi, then the Pirate, until it was purchased by the [Diocese of Astrakhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Astrakhan) in 1910 and converted into a church. It served for 8 years, traveling up and down the Volga River, sometimes clocking 4,000 miles a year. Much like every other church in Russia, it was decommissioned in 1918 by the Soviets. It made such an impact on the local population however, that almost 80 years later, it was the inspiration for a new "flotilla of God".[^cite_note-BarbaLata-58]

### Volgograd Synagogue



First Volgograd Synagogue

Also known as Beit David Synagogue, it was named after David Kolotilin, a Jewish leader during the Soviet period. Although some sources claim that this was the first synagogue to serve the Jews of Volgograd, was constructed in 1888, and its original purpose was exclusively that of a synagogue, there is little evidence to support this. What little documentation exists suggests that it was indeed built at the turn of the century, but its original purpose is unknown.[^cite_note-CJA-59] In fact, a 1903 tourist guide to Tsaritsyn warns that almost all of the buildings in the town are wooden and makes no mention of this structure, so an 1888 construction date is highly unlikely.[^cite_note-Dixon-24] It is a two-story, rectangular building, made of brick and richly decorated. The architectural style is typical of residential buildings constructed in Tsaritsyn after the turn of the century.[^cite_note-IOP-60] The original building barely survived the Battle of Stalingrad; it was in ruins as late as 1997, with broken windows and gaping holes made by Nazi bombs. Some sources suggest that the building was reconstructed, but not restored, by 1999.[^cite_note-CJA-59] Emissaries of the [Chabad-Lubavitch organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabad) launched a campaign to return the building to the Jewish community and were finally successful in 2003. With the help of multiple fundraising campaigns and generous donors, including Edward Shifrin and Alex Schneider, the synagogue was restored. An annex was constructed in 2005 to mimic the original style and the building was rededicated in 2007.[^cite_note-61] The prayer hall can be found on the first floor, with communal offices on the second.[^cite_note-CJA-59] Located at 2 Balachninskaya Street in the center of Volgograd. In addition to regular religious services, it also hosts a soup kitchen, a Jewish day school and an overnight children's camp. As of 2022, the community was led by Rabbi Zalman Yoffe.[^cite_note-62]

## Climate

Volgograd has a [hot-summer humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dfa*) with hot summers and cold winters, which borders with [cold semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_semi-arid_climate) (Köppen climate classification *BSk*). Precipitation is low and spread more or less evenly throughout the year.[^cite_note-77]
