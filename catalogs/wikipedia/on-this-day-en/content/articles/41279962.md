**Joshua Booth Green** (born February 11, 1970) is an American politician and physician serving since 2022 as the ninth [governor of Hawaii](253068.md). A member of the [Democratic Party](5043544.md), he served from 2018 to 2022 as the 15th [lieutenant governor of Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Hawaii). From 2004 to 2018, he was a member of the [Hawaii State Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_State_Legislature).

## Early life and education

Green was born on February 11, 1970, in [Kingston, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_New_York), to a [Jewish](25955086.md) family.[^cite_note-PVS-1] He was raised in [Pittsburgh](25101.md), Pennsylvania.[^cite_note-Lieutenant_Governor’s_Biography-2] He attended [Quaker Valley High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker_Valley_High_School), where he graduated as one of four [valedictorians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valedictorian) in 1988; as a Quaker Valley student, he was president of the Key Club and played on the school's soccer and tennis teams.[^cite_note-DiVittorio-3]

Green received a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) in [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology) from [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College) in 1992 and his [Doctor of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine) from the [Penn State Milton S. Hershey Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_State_Milton_S._Hershey_Medical_Center) at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University) in 1997.[^cite_note-PVS-1][^cite_note-HL-4] He completed a residency in family medicine at [UPMC Shadyside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPMC_Shadyside) in 2000.[^cite_note-5] In 2022, [Swarthmore College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarthmore_College) awarded Green an honorary [Doctorate of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Science).[^cite_note-6]

## Medical career

After completing his residency in 2000, Green joined the [National Health Service Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Service_Corps) and was stationed in Hawaii as a physician for the [Big Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_\(island\)). He practiced [family medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_medicine) and worked in emergency rooms. At times, he was the only physician in the island's rural areas.[^cite_note-Lieutenant_Governor’s_Biography-2] As of 2012, he remained a physician in the Big Island's rural emergency departments on weekends while he was a state senator.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

Green has been awarded Physician of the Year by the Hawaiʻi Medical Association twice in his career, first in 2009, and again in 2022 for his leadership and service during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-8]

## Early political career

### Hawaii House of Representatives

Green was elected to the [Hawaii House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_House_of_Representatives) in 2004. He represented the 6th district, based in a rural area of the western portion of the Big Island. Green served two terms before being elected to the [Hawaii Senate](798044.md) in 2008.

### Hawaii Senate

Green was first elected to the Hawaii Senate in 2008. He represented the 3rd district, which encompassed the southwestern portion of the Big Island. He was reelected in 2012 and 2014. As a state senator, Green served as majority leader and chaired the Committee on Health and Human Services.

In 2013, Green was honored as "Hawaii Legislator of the Year".[^cite_note-9] He championed the initiative to create an insurance mandate for children with [autism](29113700.md) via legislation known as Luke's Law. The legislation went into effect on January 1, 2016.

In 2018, Green fought to establish a legal safeguard so that parents with disabilities would no longer have their children taken away from them because of their disabilities. He also led the charge to raise the legal age to obtain [tobacco](30942.md) products and [electronic cigarettes](11996885.md) from 18 to 21, making Hawaii the first U.S. state to do so.

Green opted not to run for reelection to the Senate in 2018. He was succeeded by [Dru Kanuha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dru_Kanuha), who now serves as majority leader.

### Lieutenant Governor of Hawaii

See also: [2018 Hawaii gubernatorial election § Lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Hawaii_gubernatorial_election#Lieutenant_governor)



Green's official portrait as Hawaii Lieutenant Governor

In 2018, Green won the Democratic primary for [lieutenant governor of Hawaii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Hawaii) and was the running mate of incumbent Democratic Governor [David Ige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ige), who was running for a second term. In Hawaii, gubernatorial and lieutenant gubernatorial candidates run in separate primaries but on the same ticket in the general election. Ige and Green won the [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Hawaii_gubernatorial_election) on November 6, 2018.[^cite_note-10]

Ige tasked Green with addressing Hawaii's chronic homelessness crisis and called on him to use his background as a physician to address how mental illness and addiction affect Hawaii's homeless population.[^cite_note-11]

In 2019, shortly before the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), Green led a team of over 75 doctors, nurses and other Hawaii health care workers on an emergency medical mission to [Samoa](27238.md). They aided in vaccination efforts against a measles epidemic across the region.[^cite_note-12]

On March 3, 2020, Ige appointed Green as the administration's liaison between the state and healthcare community as it pertains to COVID-19 preparedness and response.[^cite_note-13]

A poll conducted in April 2021 by [Hawaii News Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_News_Now) found that Green had a 63% approval rating, with only 17% of voters disapproving of his work as lieutenant governor, while Ige held an approval rating of 22%.[^cite_note-14] It is speculated that Green's visibility throughout the COVID-19 pandemic and background as an emergency room doctor contributed to the difference.[^cite_note-15]

## Governor of Hawaii



Green in Tokyo Japan 2024.

### 2022 gubernatorial campaign

Main article: [2022 Hawaii gubernatorial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Hawaii_gubernatorial_election)

In August 2019, Green announced he was considering a run for [governor of Hawaii](253068.md) in the [2022 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Hawaii_gubernatorial_election).[^cite_note-16] He launched his campaign on February 10, 2022.[^cite_note-17]

Green won the Democratic primary on August 13, 2022; his running mate was Democratic state representative [Sylvia Luke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvia_Luke). On November 8, 2022, Green won the general election, defeating Republican nominee and former Hawaii Lieutenant Governor [Duke Aiona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Aiona) in the general election.[^cite_note-18] He became the first governor born after Hawaii became a state in 1959.

### Tenure

Green was inaugurated as the ninth [Governor of Hawaii](253068.md) on December 5, 2022, succeeding [David Ige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ige). In his inaugural address, Green emphasized unity and addressed Hawaii's rising cost of living, pledging to combat it through affordable housing initiatives and tax reforms. He said, "When we come together, we can meet any challenge, and accomplish anything we set our minds to", highlighting values of *‘[ohana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohana)* and *[aloha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aloha)*.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

#### 2023: early legislative actions

On March 22, 2023, Green signed legislation expanding access to [abortion](765.md) and protecting healthcare providers from out-of-state prosecution. The laws allowed minors to obtain abortions without parental consent in certain cases and repealed hospital requirements for procedures, aligning with national shifts following the [U.S. Supreme Court](31737.md)'s *[Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobbs_v._Jackson_Women's_Health_Organization)* decision.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] On June 2, coinciding with National Gun Violence Awareness Day, Green signed two firearms bills in response to the Supreme Court's *[New York State Rifle & Pistol Association, Inc. v. Bruen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Rifle_&_Pistol_Association,_Inc._v._Bruen)* ruling. Act 52 (SB1230) expanded concealed carry rights but prohibited firearms in sensitive locations such as hospitals, beaches, and theaters, strengthening Hawaii's strict gun laws. Act 53 (HB1239) mandated active shooter training in schools.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] These measures followed similar laws in New York and New Jersey.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] On July 17, Green issued an emergency proclamation addressing Hawaii's housing crisis, citing high costs and outmigration. The order aimed to streamline construction of 50,000 housing units by suspending state and local land use regulations. The move faced legal challenges for potential environmental and cultural impacts but was praised for its ambition.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

#### 2024: comprehensive legislative agenda

In the 2024 legislative session, Green signed 253 bills, addressing healthcare, education, culture, and economic issues. On June 3, he enacted Senate Bill 1035, exempting medical services under [Medicare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_\(United_States\)), [Medicaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicaid), and [Tricare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricare) from Hawaii's General Excise Tax to reduce healthcare costs and support providers.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] On June 26, he signed 22 healthcare bills, including SB 3139, which expanded mental health crisis intervention, strengthened the healthcare workforce, and promoted [Native Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiian) resources.[^cite_note-33] Green advanced [Hawaiian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_language) education by signing bills to enhance access in early education and universities. On June 28, Senate Bill 2659 mandated sustainable tourism strategies incorporating Native Hawaiian cultural values, aligning with regenerative tourism goals.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] Additional legislation addressed child and elderly safety, local agriculture, veteran support, and commercial ocean activities. On July 3 and 5, Green signed 11 agriculture bills for biosecurity and fair labeling and 16 bills for fire safety, emergency management, and veteran services.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

#### Natural gas imports

Despite the state's mandate for 100% renewable electricity by 2045, Green has proposed building a [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) import facility in [Oahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O%CA%BBahu) with Japanese power company [JERA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JERA). The proposal was backed by an initial report that inflated the benefits of LNG while omitting the cost of natural gas itself, which has increased in the wake of the [2026 Iran war fuel crisis](82827398.md). JERA's estimates for household savings assume the facility operates into the 2070s.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

## Personal life

Green is [Jewish](1131183.md).[^cite_note-41] He married [Jaime Ushiroda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_Green) in 2006. The couple met when Ushiroda, a family law expert, was clerking for [Suzanne Chun Oakland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Chun_Oakland), who was chair of the state's Human Services committee.[^cite_note-42] They have two children.[^cite_note-43]

Green is a fan of the [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers).[^cite_note-DiVittorio-3]
