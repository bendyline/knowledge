**Pape Bouna Thiaw** (born 5 February 1981) is a Senegalese [football manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manager_\(association_football\)) and a former player who played as a [forward](428681.md). He most recently served as the head coach of the [Senegal national football team](1017436.md).

## Playing career

He played for several clubs in France, Switzerland and Spain, as well as [Dynamo Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Moscow) in Russia on a six-month loan from [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC_Strasbourg_Alsace).

He played for [Senegal national football team](1017436.md) and was a participant at the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup). He scored 5 goals for his country.

In 2008, while playing for US Créteil in France, Thiaw was reported to have been placed under a *mandat de dépôt* (detention warrant) in connection with a domestic violence case. French media reported that he was tried in absentia and accused of breaking his wife’s nose, facing a potential one year prison sentence. He did not appear at the hearing, resulting in an arrest warrant and a firm one year sentence.[^cite_note-2] [^cite_note-3]

## Coaching career

Thiaw was the manager of the [Senegal A' national team](1017436.md) that won the [2022 African Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_African_Nations_Championship).[^cite_note-4] African Nations Championship limits the squads to players from the domestic league. The main Senegal squad that participates in the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) and [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) and includes players from the foreign clubs was managed by [Aliou Cissé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliou_Ciss%C3%A9). Thiaw was appointed coach of the Senegal national football team on 13 December 2024, following the latter dismissal two month before.[^cite_note-5]

In October 2025, he led Senegal to qualify to the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-6] In January 2026, Thiaw coached Senegal to at the [2025 Africa Cup of Nations](57061770.md).[^cite_note-7] During the [AFCON final](81129500.md), he urged his players to leave the field in protest after the referee awarded Morocco a penalty in stoppage time, which prompted disciplinary proceedings against him after the match.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] This led to him being banned for five games and fined \$100,000 for "unsporting conduct" and "bringing the game into disrepute" by the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md).[^cite_note-10] He led Senegal to reach the knockout stage of the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) before they were eliminated by [Belgium](679716.md) in a 3–2 defeat after extra time.[^cite_note-11] He was subsequently dismissed along with his coaching staff following the tournament.[^cite_note-12] He was replaced by [Patrick Vieira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Vieira) in August 2026.
