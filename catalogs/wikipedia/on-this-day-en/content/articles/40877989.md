# United States at the FIFA World Cup

For the women's team, see [United States at the FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_at_the_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup).



The United States during the national anthem at [Lumen Field](206812.md) in the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).

The [United States men's national soccer team](582488.md) (USMNT) has participated in twelve editions of the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), an international [soccer](10568.md) competition contested by [men's national teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_men's_national_association_football_teams) representing members of [FIFA](11049.md). The tournament is held every four years by the top qualifying teams from the continental confederations under FIFA. The [United States](3434750.md) is a member of [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF), which governs the sport in [North America](21139.md), [Central America](6121.md), and the [Caribbean](18956035.md), and has the second-most World Cup appearances from the confederation behind [Mexico](32352129.md).[^cite_note-1]

The United States participated in the [inaugural World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup) in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup) and finished in the semifinals, which was later declared a third-place finish, their best result to date and the best-ever result for a non-European and non-South American nation.[^cite_note-2] The tournament also featured the first [hat-trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_World_Cup_hat-tricks) scored at a World Cup, awarded to American striker [Bert Patenaude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bert_Patenaude) following recognition by FIFA in 2006.[^cite_note-3] After the [1950 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup), in which the United States upset [England](9904.md) in group play 1–0, the U.S. was absent from the tournament until 1990. The United States participated in every World Cup from 1990 through 2014, but did not qualify in 2018, marking the first time the team had missed a World Cup since [1986](157231.md). They returned to the World Cup by qualifying for the [2022 edition](17742072.md) and hosted the World Cup for the second time in [2026](36581929.md).[^cite_note-4]

#### Tim Howard world record

On July 1, 2014, [Tim Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Howard) was named man of the match, despite the United States losing 2–1 to [Belgium](679716.md) after [extra time](633242.md#Association_football) in the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Round_of_16).[^cite_note-18] During the match, he broke the record for most saves in a World Cup match with 16.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] After breaking this record, his performance was celebrated worldwide on the internet, with the hashtag #ThingsTimHowardCouldSave trending on [Twitter](9988187.md), and the [Wikipedia](5043734.md) article for the [United States Secretary of Defense](43998.md) was briefly [vandalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandalism_on_Wikipedia) to indicate that he was the [incumbent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incumbent).[^cite_note-23]

## Media coverage

See also: [FIFA World Cup on ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_on_ABC)

### ABC

***FIFA World Cup on ABC*** is the branding used for presentations of the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) produced by the [American Broadcasting Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) television network in the United States. ABC first broadcast World Cup matches in [1970](157227.md), when they aired week-old filmed highlights shown on [ABC's *Wide World of Sports*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_World_of_Sports_\(US_TV_series\)). ABC next broadcast the [1982 FIFA World Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup_Final). Beginning in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup), ABC was the official American network broadcaster of the World Cup up through [2014](656933.md). ABC also broadcast the [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) and [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup); [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_on_Fox_Sports) took over the American World Cup TV broadcasts in 2011, which took effect in 2015.[^cite_note-24]

#### 1970

See also: [List of events broadcast on Wide World of Sports (American TV program)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_events_broadcast_on_Wide_World_of_Sports_\(American_TV_program\)) and [List of Wide World of Sports (American TV series) announcers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Wide_World_of_Sports_\(American_TV_series\)_announcers)

The first American telecast of a World Cup match was when [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_on_NBC) aired the final between [England](9904.md) and [West Germany](250204.md) from [four years prior](61629.md). NBC there, aired the contest on a same-day [tape delay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tape_delay_\(broadcasting\)) using the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Sport)’s [black-and-white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_and_white#Media) feed.

In [1970](157227.md), it was ABC's turn to broadcast the World Cup final. While ABC aired the contest between [Italy](362466.md) and [Brazil](149286.md) in [color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_television) unlike what NBC did in 1966, ABC decided to wait until [Christmas](6237.md), six months after Brazil won, to show it as part of an episode of *[Wide World of Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_World_of_Sports_\(American_TV_program\))*.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

#### 1982

In [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup), [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS) and [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) provided the first thorough American television coverage of the FIFA World Cup. [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company)[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] aired the first [live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_television) telecast of the final. ABC aired commercials during the live action. Meanwhile, PBS aired [same day highlights of the top game of the day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_Made_in_Germany).

#### 1994

The [1994 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) marked the return of the World Cup on [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) and [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_World_of_Sports_\(U.S._TV_series\))[^cite_note-33] and the first time they used their own commentary teams for all matches. [Roger Twibell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Twibell) and [Seamus Malin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seamus_Malin)[^cite_note-34] were the lead broadcast team.[^cite_note-35] [Al Trautwig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Trautwig) and [Rick Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Davis) were the secondary broadcast team.[^cite_note-\:2-36][^cite_note-\:0-37][^cite_note-\:1-38] Other play-by-play announcers were: [Bob Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Carpenter_\(sportscaster\)) [Bob Ley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Ley), [Ian Darke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Darke), [Randy Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Hahn), and [Jim Donovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Donovan_\(sportscaster\)).[^cite_note-\:0-37][^cite_note-\:1-38] Other color commentators were: [Clive Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Charles), [Ty Keough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ty_Keough), [Peter Vermes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Vermes),[^cite_note-39] [Ron Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Newman_\(footballer\)), and Bill McDermott. [Jim McKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_McKay) was the studio host alongside studio analyst [Desmond Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Armstrong) only for games on ABC.

The 1994 American coverage had many firsts: The first with all of the matches televised, the first with no commercial interruptions during live action, and the first to feature an [on-screen score & time box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-screen_score_&_time_box).

#### 1998

See also: [1998 FIFA World Cup broadcasting rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_broadcasting_rights)

In [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), all 64 matches were televised in the United States live for the first time. [Bob Ley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Ley)[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] and [Seamus Malin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seamus_Malin) was the lead broadcast team with other broadcast teams include: [Roger Twibell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Twibell) and Mike Hill, [JP Dellacamera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JP_Dellacamera) and [Bill McDermott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_McDermott), [Derek Rae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Rae) and [Ty Keough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ty_Keough), and Phil Schoen and [Tommy Smyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Smyth). [Brent Musburger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brent_Musburger) and [Eric Wynalda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Wynalda) worked in the studio.

#### 2002

See also: [2002 FIFA World Cup broadcasting rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_broadcasting_rights)

Unlike in 1998, when ESPN and ABC paid \$20 million for the broadcast rights to the World Cup, the English-language rights for the 2002 and 2006 editions were sold instead to [Major League Soccer](71802.md) for \$40–50 million. Through an agreement with the [Walt Disney Company](37398.md), ESPN and ABC would air both tournaments at no cost, while MLS would cover production costs and sell advertisements via its newly-created marketing arm, [Soccer United Marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_United_Marketing).[^cite_note-43]

In [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), 59 matches were broadcast live, along with 5 rebroadcasts on ABC,[^cite_note-44] with coverage from [Japan](15573.md) and [South Korea](27019.md) carried live in the American late night [graveyard slot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graveyard_slot).

Hockey play-by-play announcer [Jack Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Edwards_\(sportscaster\))[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] and [Ty Keough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ty_Keough) were the lead broadcast team and called the games live in [South Korea](27019.md) and [Japan](15573.md). Other broadcast teams were: [JP Dellacamera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JP_Dellacamera) and [Tommy Smyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Smyth), [Glenn Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Davis_\(sportscaster\)) and [Shep Messing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shep_Messing), and [Mike Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Hill_\(sportscaster\)) and [Shep Messing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shep_Messing), and [Seamus Malin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seamus_Malin), however, they were based at the [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) headquarters in [Bristol, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol,_Connecticut). [Terry Gannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Gannon) hosted in the studio alongside studio analysts [Eric Wynalda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Wynalda) and [Giorgio Chinaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Chinaglia).

#### 2006

See also: [2006 FIFA World Cup broadcasting rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_broadcasting_rights)

The [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) coverage from [Germany](11867.md) was fully live as well. [Dave O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_O'Brien_\(sportscaster\))[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] joined [Marcelo Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Balboa) on the lead broadcast team for the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) coverage on ESPN and [ABC Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_on_ABC), despite having no experience calling [soccer](10568.md) matches prior to that year. Because [The Walt Disney Company](37398.md), owner of both television outlets, retained control over on-air talent, the appointment of O'Brien as the main play-by-play voice was made over the objections of [Soccer United Marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_United_Marketing), who wanted [JP Dellacamera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JP_Dellacamera) to continue in that role. Disney stated that their broadcast strategy was intended, in voice and style, to target the vast majority of Americans who do not follow the sport on a regular basis. [Mispronunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mispronunciation) and incorrect addressing of names, misuse of soccer terminology, and lack of insight into tactics and history plagued the telecasts, resulting in heavy criticism from [English-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) soccer fans, many of whom ended up watching the games on [Univision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univision) instead.[^cite_note-53]

Other broadcast teams included: [JP Dellacamera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JP_Dellacamera) and [John Harkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Harkes), [Glenn Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Davis_\(sportscaster\)) and [Shep Messing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shep_Messing), [Adrian Healey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Healey) and [Tommy Smyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Smyth), and [Rob Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Stone_\(sportscaster\)) and [Robin Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Fraser). [Brent Musburger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brent_Musburger) returned for his 2nd World Cup as lead studio host with other hosts [Rece Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rece_Davis), and [Dave Revsine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Revsine). [Alexi Lalas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexi_Lalas), [Eric Wynalda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Wynalda), [Julie Foudy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Foudy), and [Heather Mitts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heather_Mitts) were the studio analysts.

### NBC

***FIFA World Cup on NBC*** is the branding used for presentations of the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) produced by the [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) television network in the United States. NBC[^cite_note-58] was the official American network television broadcaster for the international [soccer](10568.md) competition in [1966](61629.md) and [1986](157231.md).[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

#### 1966

The first American coverage[^cite_note-61] of the World Cup consisted only of a [previously filmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinescope) telecast of the [1966 Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) on [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] The Final was aired before their coverage of the Saturday *[Major League Baseball Game of the Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_Game_of_the_Week)*. NBC used the [black & white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_and_white_television) [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Broadcasting_Corporation) feed and aired it on a two-hour film delay. This was the first time soccer had been shown in the United States as a stand-alone broadcast. Previously, [ABC's *Wide World of Sports*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_World_of_Sports_\(US_TV_series\)) had shown [England's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association) [Football Association Cup](11237.md) on as long as a two-week delay.

#### 1986

On October 6, 1984. NBC's [anthology series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthology_series), *[SportsWorld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsworld_\(American_TV_series\))*[^cite_note-65] provided World Cup soccer qualifying coverage featuring the [United States](582488.md) and the [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles_national_football_team).

[1986](157231.md) marked the first time that the World Cup had extensive live [cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television) and [network television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_television) coverage in the United States. [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) carried most of the weekday matches while [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC)[^cite_note-66] did weekend games. NBC aired seven[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] matches, including the ["Hand of God" quarterfinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_v_England_\(1986_FIFA_World_Cup\)), with broadcasters on-site. NBC's theme music[^cite_note-69] for their 1986 coverage was [Herb Alpert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herb_Alpert)'s[^cite_note-70] "1980", from his 1979 album *[Rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rise_\(Herb_Alpert_album\))*. It was originally a cue meant for the ill-fated [1980 Moscow Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics) [broadcasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Olympic_broadcasts). Meanwhile, ESPN aired about 25 matches that year, all with broadcasters in studio.

NBC's producers were forced to run the games' audio feed through [telephone lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_line) rather than through [satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_television). This was because the [International Broadcast Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Broadcast_Centre) in [Mexico City](18987.md) crossed up many communication lines. Consequently, various countries received commentary from others (or no sound or video at all). NBC in this case, received commentary from somewhere in [Southeast Asia](28741.md) and so were forced to have [Charlie Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Jones_\(sportscaster\)) [call collect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collect_call) and broadcast the [Italy](362466.md)-[Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team) opener via a handset telephone receiver. NBC lost the sound but still had video so Charlie Jones dialed collect again.

### Telemundo Deportes'

See also: [Telemundo Deportes § Notable personalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemundo_Deportes#Notable_personalities)

On October 22, 2011, Deportes Telemundo acquired the Spanish language rights to broadcast the [FIFA Men's](11370.md) and [Women's World Cup](244862.md) for around \$600 million, replacing [Univision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univision) as the tournament's Spanish language broadcaster, which began carrying the World Cup tournaments in [1970](157227.md) ([Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company) acquired the English language U.S. broadcast rights through a separate agreement). The deal, which began with the [2015 Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) and runs through 2026, includes rights to associated [FIFA](11049.md)-sanctioned tournaments (including the Men's Under 20 and Under 17 World Cups, and the Men's Beach Soccer World Cup), which will be telecast on Telemundo and NBC Universo; the deal was extended on February 12, 2015, to include rights to the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

On May 16, 2015, during Telemundo's 2015–16 [upfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upfronts) presentation in [New York City](645042.md), it was announced that Deportes Telemundo would be replaced by a new division initially known as **NBC Deportes**; the new division was formed as a branch of the English-language [NBC Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Sports) division, and be responsible for sports content for Telemundo, NBC Universo and related digital platforms. While it retained all existing sports telecast rights and programs aired by both Telemundo and NBC Universo, the latter network also began to expand its sports coverage, primarily in preparation for the [2016 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics) and the start of the division's contract with [FIFA](11049.md)—whose first events included the [2015 U-20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_U-20_World_Cup) and [Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup).[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-mcn-nbcdeportes-85][^cite_note-variety-universo-86]

### Fox Sports

The English television rights to the FIFA World Cup have been held by [Fox Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Sports_\(United_States\)) since the 2018 edition and are set to run through 2026. The rights were originally set to expire in 2022, but that tournament's move to a November–December schedule prompted FIFA to award the 2026 rights to appease Fox, who had prior commitments to air other sporting events during the period.[^cite_note-NYT-Contract-87] The 2022 World Cup broadcast was criticized for ignoring Qatar's human rights issues.[^cite_note-88] [Telemundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemundo) holds the Spanish television rights to broadcast the FIFA World Cup in the United States; their contract was also renewed through 2026.[^cite_note-NYT-Contract-87]
