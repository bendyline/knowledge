**Ghana**,[^cite_note-8] officially the **Republic of Ghana**, is a country in [West Africa](67393.md). It is situated with the [Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Guinea) and the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) to the south, and shares borders with [Ivory Coast](57744.md) to the west, [Burkina Faso](3470.md) to the north, and [Togo](30138.md) to the east.[^cite_note-9] Ghana covers an area of 239,567 km<sup>2</sup> (92,497 sq mi), spanning various [ecologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biome), from [coastal](5236.md) [savannas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna) to [tropical rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest). With over 35 million inhabitants,[^cite_note-pop-4] Ghana is the [thirteenth-most populous country in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_population), and the second-most populous country in West Africa. The [capital](181337.md) and most populous city is [Accra](56824.md).[^cite_note-10]

The earliest kingdoms to emerge in Ghana were [Bonoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bono_state) in the south and the [Kingdom of Dagbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Dagbon) in the north, with Bonoman existing in the area during the 11th century.[^cite_note-NP-11] The [Asante Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asante_Empire) and other [Akan kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_people) in the south emerged over the centuries.[^cite_note-12] Beginning in the 15th century, the [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire), followed by other [European powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Powers), contested the area for trading rights, until the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) ultimately established control of the coast by the 19th [century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century). Following more than a century of colonial resistance, the later borders of the country took shape, encompassing four separate British colonial territories: [Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(British_colony\)), [Ashanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashanti_\(Crown_Colony\)), the [Northern Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territories_of_the_Gold_Coast), and [British Togoland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Togoland). These were unified as an independent dominion within the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations). On 6 March 1957, Ghana became the first [colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony) in [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md) to achieve sovereignty.[^cite_note-Universal_Newsreel-13][^cite_note-First_For_Sub-Saharan_Africa-14][^cite_note-Exploring_Africa_–_Decolonization-15] Under President [Kwame Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah), it became influential in [decolonisation efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Africa) and the [Pan-African movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Africanism).[^cite_note-Ateku-16][^cite_note-17]

Ghana is a [multi-ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-ethnic) country with various linguistic and religious groups;[^cite_note-popest-18] while the [Akan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_people) are the largest ethnic group, they constitute a plurality. Most [Ghanaians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_people) are [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Ghana) (71.3%); almost a fifth are [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Ghana); a tenth practise traditional faiths or report no religion.[^cite_note-statsghana1-2] Ghana is a unitary [constitutional democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracy) led by a [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ghana) who is [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and [head of government](37570.md).[^cite_note-Ghana:_CIA_World_FactBook-19] For political stability in [Africa](5334607.md), it ranked seventh in the 2022 [Ibrahim Index of African Governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Index_of_African_Governance) and fifth in the 2024 [Fragile States Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragile_States_Index).[^cite_note-Ateku-16][^cite_note-20] Ghana is a founding member of the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_and_the_Non-Aligned_Movement) and the [African Union](10634933.md), and a member of the [United Nations](31769.md), [South Atlantic Peace and Cooperation Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_Peace_and_Cooperation_Zone), [Economic Community of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Community_of_West_African_States), the [Group of 24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_24) and the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations).[^cite_note-21]

## Etymology

### Empire of Ghana

Further information: [Ghana Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Empire)

The name *Ghana* comes from [Wagadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Empire), an empire in west Africa from the 3rd to 12th centuries; Wagadu was termed *Ghana* by [Arab](2185.md) traders involved in the [trans-Saharan trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_trade). *Ghana* is thought to originate from the title *[Kaya Maghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaya-Magha)* of the rulers of Wagadu, which translates as *ruler of gold*.[^cite_note-Gestrich-22]

### Adoption in precolonial scholarship

The earliest attempt to associate the people of the [Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(British_colony\)) with ancient Ghana had been made by Rev. J. B. Anaman around the turn of the 20th century. Anaman drew on [W.D. Cooley's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Desborough_Cooley) 19th-century interpretation of Arabic geographical sources to make an argument for a historical connection, suggesting an alternative derivation connecting the name to the [Wangara people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wangara_people). [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKimble1963xvi–xvii-23] [Lady Flora Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_Shaw,_Baroness_Lugard) later compiled both Arabic and European writings to create detailed narratives of the kingdom. She presented it as a major African power comparable to contemporary Western European states.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKimble1963xvi-24]

According to [Jack Goody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Goody), the theory that the [Akan peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_people) originated from the medieval [Empire of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Empire) was continuously promoted through the teachings of Rev. W. T. Balmer between 1907 and 1911, who instructed students that the Akan had migrated from the ancient kingdom located near the [Upper Niger](21437.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoody1968461–462-25] Goody states that Balmer's hypothesis lacked linguistic and historical evidence, yet it later became influential among educated elites and nationalist intellectuals.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoody1968462–463-26]

The hypothesis gained more popularity when it was introduced into educational institutions, primarily in [Achimota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achimota_School), during the 1920s where it later spread to other schools.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKimble1963xvii-27] The theory was later publicized by [J. B. Danquah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._Danquah)'s academic writings; he used [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) translations to claim that the Akan migrated from the Upper Niger region. Danquah proposed that the term Ghana was a corruption of Akane or Akana and associated it with the ancient region of [Akkad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_Empire).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoody1968468-28] Eva L. R. Meyerowitz expanded this rhetoric through a series of publications that sought to reconstruct early history. She claimed that Akan origins and culture came from areas in the [Sahara](325363.md) and the Near East, and argued that Akan culture was not mainly black African, but could instead be considered [Libya](17633.md)-[Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) or connected to [Mediterranean](19006.md) or [Near Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East) cultures.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoody1968468–470-29]

### National acceptance and symbolic meaning

By the period leading to independence, the name "Ghana" was accepted and adopted as a symbol of precolonial prestige, cultural unity, and national legitimacy.[^cite_note-Gestrich-22]

## Geography

Main articles: [Geography of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Ghana) and [Geology of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Ghana)

See also: [Biodiversity of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_of_Ghana)



[Topographic map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topographic_map)



[Satellite image](1184557.md)

Ghana lies on the [Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Guinea) in [West Africa](67393.md), a few degrees north of the [Equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator). It covers 238,540 km<sup>2</sup> (92,101 sq mi) and has an [Atlantic](698.md) coastline of about 560 kilometres (350 miles).[^cite_note-Geography_Physical-30] It is situated between latitudes 4°45′N and 11°N, and longitudes 1°15′E and 3°15′W. The [Prime Meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Meridian) passes through [Tema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tema), near Accra, making Ghana the country closest to the intersection of the Prime Meridian and the Equator (0°, 0°), located offshore in the Atlantic.[^cite_note-Geography_Physical-30]

Grasslands mixed with south coastal shrublands and forests dominate Ghana, with forest extending northward from the coast 320 kilometres (200 miles) and eastward for a maximum of about 270 kilometres (170 miles) with locations for mining of industrial minerals and timber.[^cite_note-Geography_Physical-30] Ghana is home to five terrestrial ecoregions: [Eastern Guinean forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Guinean_forests), [Guinean forest–savanna mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinean_forest%E2%80%93savanna_mosaic), [West Sudanian savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Sudanian_savanna), [Central African mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_mangroves), and [Guinean mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinean_mangroves).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-31]

The [White Volta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Volta) River and its tributary [Black Volta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Volta), flow south through Ghana to [Lake Volta](1477772.md), the world's [third-largest reservoir by volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reservoirs_by_volume) and largest by surface area, formed by the hydroelectric [Akosombo Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akosombo_Dam),[^cite_note-32] completed in 1965.[^cite_note-auto2-33] The Volta flows out of Lake Volta into the [Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Guinea).[^cite_note-auto1-34] The northernmost part of Ghana is Pulmakong and the southernmost part of Ghana is [Cape Three Points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Three_Points).[^cite_note-Geography_Physical-30]

Ghana consists of low coastal plains, forested hills in the centre, and savanna in the north. Its main geographical units are: the [Coastal Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Plain) along the Gulf of Guinea, the [Ashanti uplands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashanti_uplands?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the centre, the [Volta Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volta_Basin) in the east, which is dominated by [Lake Volta](1477772.md), and the [Northern Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Plains) stretching to Burkina Faso.[^cite_note-Geography_Physical-30] The highest point is [Mount Afadja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Afadja) (885 m) in the Akwapim-Togo Range.[^cite_note-Geography_Physical-30] The northernmost settlement in Ghana is Pulmakong near the Burkina Faso border, while the southernmost point is [Cape Three Points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Three_Points).[^cite_note-Geography_Physical-30]

### Climate

Main articles: [Climate of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Ghana) and [Climate change in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Ghana)

The [climate of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Ghana) is [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate), and there is [wet season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season) and [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season).[^cite_note-UNDP_Climate-35] Ghana sits at the intersection of three hydro-climatic zones.[^cite_note-36] The [eastern coastal belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahomey_Gap) is warm and comparatively dry, the south-west corner of Ghana is hot and [humid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humidity), and the north of Ghana is hot and dry.[^cite_note-Ghana_high_plains-37]

[Climate change in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Ghana) has impacts on the people of Ghana. Increasing temperatures and changes in [rainfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainfall), [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather), [drought](37335.md), [wild fires](56106.md), [floods](50482.md) and [sea-level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise)[^cite_note-38] are expected to negatively affect the country's infrastructure, hydropower production, [food security](216361.md), [water supply](684928.md), and coastal and agricultural [livelihoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livelihood) such as farming and fisheries.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-climatelinks-40][^cite_note-41] Ghana's economy will be impacted by [climate change](5042951.md), due to its dependence on [climate-sensitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_sensitivity) sectors such as [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy), and [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry). Diseases like [malaria](20423.md), [dengue fever](39669.md) and [cholera](7591.md) are predicted to increase due to changes in water conditions.[^cite_note-42] Ghana signed the [Paris Agreement](30242372.md) in 2016. It aims to avoid 64 million metric tons of [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md) by 2030, compared to a business-as-usual scenario for 2020–2030. Ghana has committed to [net zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net-zero_emissions) by 2060.[^cite_note-43]

## History

Main article: [History of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ghana)

### Medieval kingdoms

Main articles: [Ashanti Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashanti_Empire), [Kingdom of Dagbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Dagbon), and [Bono state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bono_state)



An 1850 map showing the [Akan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_people) [Kingdom of Ashanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Ashanti) within the [Guinea region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_\(region\)) and surrounding regions in West Africa

The earliest kingdoms to emerge in Ghana were [Bonoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bono_state) in the south and the [Kingdom of Dagbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Dagbon) in the north, with Bonoman existing in the area during the 11th century.[^cite_note-NP-11][^cite_note-col-44] From the 17th century, Akan states began to emerge from what is believed to have been the Bonoman area, mainly based on gold trading.[^cite_note-45] These states included Bonoman ([Brong-Ahafo region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brong-Ahafo_region)), [Adansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adansi) and [Asante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asante_Empire) ([Ashanti Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashanti_Region)), [Denkyira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denkyira) ([Western North region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Region_\(Ghana\))), [Mankessim Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mankessim_Kingdom) ([Central region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Region_\(Ghana\))), [Akyem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akyem) and [Akwamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akwamu) [(Eastern region)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Region_\(Ghana\)).[^cite_note-col-44][^cite_note-46] By the 19th century, the territory of the southern part of Ghana was included in the [Asante Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashanti_Empire).[^cite_note-col-44] The government of the Ashanti Empire operated first as a loose network and eventually as a centralised kingdom with a specialised bureaucracy centred in the capital city of [Kumasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumasi).[^cite_note-col-44] Prior to Akan contact with Europeans, the Akan people created an economy based on principally gold and [gold bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_bar) precious metals, which were traded with other states in Africa.[^cite_note-col-44][^cite_note-47]

The Ga-Dangme migrated westward from south-western [Nigeria](21383.md). The Ewe, formerly known as Dogbo, migrated from Ketu (Benin) area with their Gbe-speaking kinsmen (Adja, Fon, Phla/Phera and Ogun/Gun) and, in transition, settled at Tado in Togo, and Dogbo Nyigbo in Benin Republic, with Nortsie (a walled town in what later is Togo) as their final dispersal point. Their dispersal from Nortsie was necessitated by the high-handed rule of King Agorkorli (Agɔ Akɔli), who was the reigning monarch of the tribe at that time. The Ewe in Ghana speak three principal dialects: Anlo (along the coast), Tongu (along the [Volta River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volta_River)) and Ewedome (in the hill country side). The [Ga-Dangme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ga-Adangbe_people) occupy the Greater Accra Region and parts of the Eastern Region, while the Ewe are found in the [Volta Region](2227703.md) and the neighbouring Togo, Benin Republic and Nigeria (around Badagry area).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

### European contact and colonialism

See also: [Slave Coast of West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_Coast_of_West_Africa) and [Dutch Slave Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Slave_Coast)



The [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) established the [Portuguese Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Gold_Coast) with the construction of [Elmina Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmina_Castle) (*Castelo da Mina*) by [Diogo de Azambuja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogo_de_Azambuja) in 1482, making it the oldest [European building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_colonial_architecture) in West Africa.

Akan trade with European states began after contact with the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) in the 15th century.[^cite_note-51] European contact was by the [Portuguese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people), who came to the [Gold Coast region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(region\)) in the 15th century to trade. The Portuguese then established the [Portuguese Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Gold_Coast) (Costa do Ouro), focused on the availability of gold.[^cite_note-History-52] The Portuguese built a trading lodge at a coastal settlement called Anomansah ("the perpetual drink"), which they renamed [São Jorge da Mina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmina).[^cite_note-History-52] In 1481, King [John II of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_II_of_Portugal) commissioned [Diogo de Azambuja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogo_de_Azambuja) to build [Elmina Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmina_Castle), which was completed in three years.[^cite_note-History-52] By 1598, the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) had joined the Portuguese in the gold trade, establishing the [Dutch Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Gold_Coast) (*Nederlandse Bezittingen ter Kuste van Guinea* – "Dutch properties at the Guinea coast") and building forts at [Fort Komenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Komenda) and Kormantsi.[^cite_note-Ghana_book-53] In 1617, the Dutch captured Elmina Castle from the Portuguese and [Axim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axim) in 1642 ([Fort St Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Saint_Anthony)).[^cite_note-Ghana_book-53]

European traders had joined in gold trading by the 17th century, including the [Swedes](29380512.md), establishing the [Swedish Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Gold_Coast) (*Svenska Guldkusten*), and [Denmark–Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark%E2%80%93Norway), establishing the [Danish Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Gold_Coast) (*Danske Guldkyst* or *Dansk Guinea*).[^cite_note-History_of_Ghana-54] European traders participated in the [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md) in this area.[^cite_note-Emmer-55] More than 30 forts and castles were built by the merchants. The Germans established the [Brandenburger Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburger_Gold_Coast) or Groß Friedrichsburg.[^cite_note-56] In 1874, Great Britain established control over some parts of the country, assigning these areas the status of the [British Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(British_colony\)).[^cite_note-colestablish-57] [Military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) engagements occurred between the British colonial powers and Akan nation-states. The Kingdom of [Ashanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashanti_Empire) defeated the British some times in the 100-year-long [Anglo-Ashanti wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Ashanti_wars) and eventually lost with the [War of the Golden Stool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Golden_Stool) in 1900.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-Chronology_of_world_history-59][^cite_note-60]

### Transition to independence

See also: [Dominion of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Ghana) and [Ghana Independence Act 1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Independence_Act_1957)



A Gold Coast postage stamp overprinted for Ghanaian independence in 1957

Celebrations marking Ghana's independence on 6 March 1957

In 1947, the [United Gold Coast Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Gold_Coast_Convention) led by ["The Big Six"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Six_\(Ghana\)) called for "self-government within the shortest possible time." Following the [1946 Gold Coast legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_Gold_Coast_general_election),[^cite_note-History_of_Ghana-54][^cite_note-61] [Kwame Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah), a Ghanaian nationalist who led Ghana from 1957 to 1966 as the country's first [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Ghana) and [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ghana), formed the [Convention People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_People's_Party) in 1949 with the motto "self-government now".[^cite_note-History_of_Ghana-54] The party initiated a "positive action" campaign involving non-violent protests, strikes and non-cooperation with the British authorities. Nkrumah was arrested and sentenced to one year imprisonment during this time. In the Gold Coast's [1951 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Gold_Coast_general_election), he was elected to Parliament and was released from prison.[^cite_note-History_of_Ghana-54]

At midnight on 6 March 1957, the Gold Coast, Ashanti, the Northern Territories, and [British Togoland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Togoland) were unified as one single independent dominion within the [British Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) under the name Ghana. This was done under the [Ghana Independence Act 1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Independence_Act_1957). The [flag of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Ghana), consisting of the colours red, gold, green, and a black star, dates back to this unification.[^cite_note-Ghana_flag_and_description-62] On 1 July 1960, following the [Ghanaian constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Ghanaian_constitutional_referendum) and [Ghanaian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Ghanaian_presidential_election), Nkrumah declared Ghana a republic and assumed the presidency.[^cite_note-Universal_Newsreel-13][^cite_note-First_For_Sub-Saharan_Africa-14][^cite_note-Exploring_Africa_–_Decolonization-15][^cite_note-History_of_Ghana-54] The nation's [Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Ghana\)) is 6 March, and 1 July is celebrated as [Republic Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Day).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

[Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah) led an [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) regime in Ghana, repressing political opposition and conducting elections that were not [free and fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_fair_election).[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] In 1964, a [constitutional amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Ghanaian_constitutional_referendum) made Ghana a [one-party state](102459.md), with Nkrumah as [president for life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_for_life) of the nation and its party.[^cite_note-70] Nkrumah was the first African head of state to promote the concept of [Pan-Africanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Africanism), which he had been introduced to during his studies at [Lincoln University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_University_\(Pennsylvania\)), Pennsylvania in the United States, at the time when [Marcus Garvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Garvey) was known for his "Back to Africa Movement".[^cite_note-History_of_Ghana-54] He merged the teachings of Garvey, [Martin Luther King Jr.](20076.md) and the naturalised Ghanaian scholar [W. E. B. Du Bois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._E._B._Du_Bois) into the formation of 1960s Ghana.[^cite_note-History_of_Ghana-54] Osagyefo Dr. Kwame Nkrumah, as he became known, played a part in the founding of the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement), and in establishing the [Kwame Nkrumah Ideological Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah_Ideological_Institute) to teach his ideologies of [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) and [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism).[^cite_note-71] His life achievements were recognised by Ghanaians during his centenary birthday celebration, and the day was instituted as a [public holiday in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Ghana) ([Founders' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founders'_Day_\(Ghana\))).[^cite_note-72]

### Operation Cold Chop and aftermath

Main article: [History of Ghana (1966–1979)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ghana_\(1966%E2%80%931979\))

The government of Nkrumah was subsequently overthrown in a coup by the [Ghana Armed Forces](12075.md), codenamed "Operation Cold Chop". This occurred while Nkrumah was abroad with [Zhou Enlai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_Enlai) in the People's Republic of China, on a mission to [Hanoi](56667.md), Vietnam, to help end the [Vietnam War](32611.md). The coup took place on 24 February 1966, led by Colonel [Emmanuel Kwasi Kotoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Kwasi_Kotoka) and Brigadier Akwasi Afrifa. The [National Liberation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberation_Council) was formed, chaired by Lieutenant General [Joseph A. Ankrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Arthur_Ankrah).[^cite_note-political_&_social_thought_of_Kwame_Nkrumah-73][^cite_note-74]

A series of alternating military and civilian governments, mostly affected by economic instabilities,[^cite_note-75] ruled Ghana from 1966, ending with the ascent to power of [Flight Lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_lieutenant) [Jerry John Rawlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Rawlings) of the [Provisional National Defence Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_National_Defence_Council) in 1981.[^cite_note-Flight_Lieutenant_Jerry_John_Rawlings_(J.J_Rawlings)-76] These changes resulted in the suspension of the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ghana) in 1981 and the banning of [political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Ghana).[^cite_note-Rawlings:_The_legacy-77] The economy declined, so Rawlings negotiated a structural adjustment plan, changing some economic policies, and growth recovered during the 1980s.[^cite_note-Rawlings:_The_legacy-77] A new constitution restoring [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system) politics was promulgated in the [presidential election of 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Ghanaian_presidential_election), in which Rawlings was elected, and again in the [general election of 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Ghanaian_general_election).[^cite_note-Elections_in_Ghana-78]

In a [tribal war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkomba%E2%80%93Nanumba_conflict) in [Northern Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Region_\(Ghana\)) in 1994, between the [Konkomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkomba_people) and other ethnic groups, including the [Nanumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanumba_people), [Dagomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagomba_people) and [Gonja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonja_people), between 1,000 and 2,000 people were killed and 150,000 people were displaced.[^cite_note-79]



Traditional [chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chieftaincy) in 2015

After the [2000 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Ghanaian_general_election), [John Kufuor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kufuor) of the [New Patriotic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Patriotic_Party) became president of Ghana on 7 January 2001 and was [re-elected in 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Ghanaian_general_election), thus also serving two terms (the term limit) as president of Ghana and marking the first time under the fourth republic that power was transferred from one legitimately elected head of state and head of government to another.[^cite_note-Elections_in_Ghana-78]

[Nana Akufo-Addo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nana_Akufo-Addo), the ruling party candidate, was defeated in the [2008 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Ghanaian_general_election) by [John Atta Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Atta_Mills) of the [National Democratic Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Congress_\(Ghana\)).[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] Mills died of natural causes and was succeeded by Vice President [John Mahama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mahama) on 24 July 2012.[^cite_note-82] Following the [2012 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Ghanaian_general_election), Mahama became president in his own right,[^cite_note-Ghanaian_President_John_Dramani_Mahama_sworn_in-83] and Ghana was described as a "stable democracy".[^cite_note-OBG2012-84][^cite_note-85] As a result of the [2016 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Ghanaian_general_election),[^cite_note-86] Nana Akufo-Addo became president on 7 January 2017.[^cite_note-87] He was re-elected after the [2020 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Ghanaian_general_election).[^cite_note-88] John Mahama is later the president after being elected again in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Ghanaian_general_election), by beating [Mahamudu Bawumia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahamudu_Bawumia) by the 4th largest margin since 1992.[^cite_note-89]

To combat deforestation, on 11 June 2021, Ghana inaugurated Green Ghana Day, with the aim of planting five million trees.[^cite_note-90]

## Politics

Main articles: [Government of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Ghana) and [Politics of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Ghana)

See also: [Regions of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Ghana)



[Parliament House of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_House_of_Ghana), the Supreme Court of Ghana, and [Judiciary of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Ghana) buildings. [Jubilee House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubilee_House) is the [presidential palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_palace).

Ghana is a [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_executive_theory) [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) [constitutional democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracy) with a parliamentary [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system) that is dominated by two parties—the [National Democratic Congress (NDC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Congress_\(Ghana\)) and the [New Patriotic Party (NPP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Patriotic_Party). Ghana alternated between civilian and military governments until January 1993, when the military government gave way to the Fourth Republic of Ghana after [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Ghanaian_presidential_election) and [parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Ghanaian_parliamentary_election) in late 1992. The 1992 [constitution of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ghana) divides powers among a [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the [Ghana Armed Forces](12075.md) ([President of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Ghana)), parliament ([Parliament of Ghana](3166296.md)), cabinet ([Cabinet of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Ghana)), council of state ([Ghanaian Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_\(Ghana\))), and an independent judiciary ([Judiciary of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Ghana)). The government is elected by [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage) after every four years.[^cite_note-cs-91]

The 2012 [Fragile States Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragile_States_Index) indicated that Ghana is ranked the 67th-least fragile state in the world and the fifth-least fragile state in Africa. Ghana ranked 112th out of 177 countries on the index.[^cite_note-92] Ghana ranked as the 64th-least corrupt and politically corrupt country in the world out of all 174 countries ranked and ranked as the fifth-least politically corrupt country in Africa out of 53 countries in the 2012 [Transparency International Corruption Perception Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index).[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] Ghana was ranked seventh in Africa out of 53 countries in the 2012 [Ibrahim Index of African Governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Index_of_African_Governance). The Ibrahim Index is a comprehensive measure of African government, based on variables that reflect the success with which governments deliver essential political goods to its citizens.[^cite_note-95] According to 2023 [V-Dem Democracy indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-Dem_Democracy_indices) Ghana is ranked 67th electoral democracy worldwide and 10th [electoral democracy in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_in_Africa).[^cite_note-vdem_dataset-96] Ghana is a country in West Africa that is known for its cultural diversity, democratic government, and growing economy. The country has more than 30 million people and is home to many ethnic groups, languages, and traditions. Ghana is one of the world's leading producers of cocoa and is also rich on natural resources such as oil and gold. Besides economic importance, Ghana is recognized for its peaceful elections, historic landmarks, national parks and contributions to regional development in West Africa.

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Ghana)



[Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan), Ghanaian diplomat and United Nations Secretary-General 1997–2006

Since independence, Ghana has been devoted to ideals of nonalignment and is a founding member of the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement). Ghana favours international and regional political and economic co-operation, and is an active member of the [United Nations](31769.md) and the [African Union](10634933.md).[^cite_note-H._E._Mr._Ken_Kanda-97]

Three U.S. presidents have made diplomatic trips to Ghana ([Bill Clinton](3356.md), [George W. Bush](3414021.md) and [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama)), along with a Vice President ([Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris)).[^cite_note-98] Some Ghanaian diplomats and politicians hold positions in international organisations, including Ghanaian diplomat and former [Secretary-General of the United Nations](162415.md) [Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan), [International Criminal Court](14880.md) Judge [Akua Kuenyehia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akua_Kuenyehia), and former President [Jerry John Rawlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Rawlings) and former President [John Agyekum Kufuor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kufuor), who both served as diplomats of the United Nations.[^cite_note-cs-91]

In September 2010, President [John Atta Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Atta_Mills) visited China on an official visit. Mills and then-[general secretary of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Hu Jintao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hu_Jintao) marked the 50th anniversary of [diplomatic ties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Ghana_relations) between the two nations, at the [Great Hall of the People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Hall_of_the_People).[^cite_note-Hu_Jintao-John_Atta_Mills-99] China reciprocated with an official visit in November 2011, by the vice-chairman of the [Standing Committee of the National People's Congress of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Committee_of_the_National_People's_Congress), [Zhou Tienong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_Tienong) who visited Ghana and met with Ghana's president [John Mahama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mahama).[^cite_note-Xinhua2011-100] China became one of the top investing countries of Ghana, which predominantly focus on infrastructure, natural resources, and the [manufacturing sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector_of_the_economy), have promoted economic growth, job creation, and technology transfer in Ghana. Concerns regarding the sustainability of Chinese-financed projects, environmental impacts, and the lack of transparency in their investments call for a careful assessment of these collaborations.[^cite_note-101] Iranian President [Mahmoud Ahmadinejad](7907317.md) met with Mahama in 2013 to hold discussions on strengthening the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement) and also co–chair a bilateral meeting between Ghana and Iran at the Ghanaian [presidential palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_palace) [Flagstaff House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubilee_House).[^cite_note-Ahmadinejad-102][^cite_note-Ahmadinejad2-103][^cite_note-CPP_welcomes_President_Ahmadinejad_visit_to_Ghana-104][^cite_note-Ghana_welcomed_Iran's_President_Mahmoud_Ahmadinejad-105][^cite_note-President_Mahmoud_Ahmadinejad-106]

The [Sustainable Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals_and_Ghana) (SDG) were integrated into Ghana's development agenda and the budget. According to reports, the SDGs were implemented through a decentralized planning approach. This allows for stakeholders' participation, such as in UN agencies, traditional leaders, civil society organizations, academia, and others.[^cite_note-107] The 17 SDGs are a global call to action to end poverty among others, and the UN and its partners in the country are working towards achieving them.[^cite_note-108] According to the President [Nana Akufo-Addo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nana_Akufo-Addo), Ghana was "the first Sub-Saharan African country to achieve the goal of halving poverty, as contained in Goal 1 of the [Millennium Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Development_Goals)".[^cite_note-109]

### Military

Main article: [Ghana Armed Forces](12075.md)

In 1957, the [Ghana Armed Forces](12075.md) (GAF) consisted of its headquarters, support services, three battalions of infantry and a reconnaissance squadron with armoured vehicles.[^cite_note-Canada's_Military_Assistance-110] President Nkrumah aimed at rapidly expanding the GAF to support the [United States of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_Africa) ambitions. Thus, in 1961, 4th and 5th Battalions were established, and in 1964 6th Battalion was established, from a [parachute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paratrooper) [airborne unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_forces) originally raised in 1963.[^cite_note-111] Today, Ghana is a [regional power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_power) and [regional hegemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_hegemony).[^cite_note-South_America_and_West_Africa-112] In his book *[Shake Hands with the Devil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shake_Hands_with_the_Devil_\(book\))*, [Canadian Forces](182792.md) commander [Roméo Dallaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rom%C3%A9o_Dallaire) highly rated the GAF soldiers and military personnel.[^cite_note-Canada's_Military_Assistance-110]

The [military operations](666301.md) and [military doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_doctrine) of the GAF are conceptualised in the constitution, Ghana's Law on Armed Force Military Strategy, and [Kofi Annan International Peacekeeping Training Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan_International_Peacekeeping_Training_Centre) agreements to which GAF is attestator.[^cite_note-Defence-113][^cite_note-Ghana's_Regional_Security_Policy-114][^cite_note-KAIPTC-115] GAF military operations are executed under the auspices and imperium of the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Ghana\)).[^cite_note-Defence-113][^cite_note-Vision_&_Mission-116] Ghana has experienced political violence in the past and 2017 has thus far seen an upward trend in incidents motivated by political grievances.[^cite_note-117]

### Law enforcement

Further information: [Law enforcement in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_Ghana) and [Crime in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_Ghana)



[Militarized police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militarized_police) Unit of the [Ghana Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Police_Service)

The [Ghana Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Police_Service) and the [Criminal Investigation Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Investigation_Department) are the main law enforcement agencies, responsible for the detection of crime, maintenance of law and order and the maintenance of internal peace and security.[^cite_note-MINT-118] The Ghana Police Service has eleven specialised police units, including a [Militarized police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militarized_police) [Rapid deployment force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_deployment_force) and [Marine Police Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Police_Service#Marine_Police_Unit).[^cite_note-UNIT-119][^cite_note-POLICE-120] The Ghana Police Service operates in 12 divisions: ten covering the regions of Ghana, one assigned specifically to the seaport and industrial hub of [Tema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tema), and the twelfth being the Railways, Ports and Harbours Division.[^cite_note-POLICE-120] The Ghana Police Service's Marine Police Unit and Division handles issues that arise from the country's offshore [oil and gas industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_and_gas_industry).[^cite_note-POLICE-120]

&#x20;The [Ghana Prisons Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Prisons_Service) and the sub-division [Borstal Institute for Juveniles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borstal_Institute_for_Juveniles) administers incarceration.[^cite_note-Ghana_Prisons_Service_General_Information-121] The new sustainable development goals adopted by the United Nations call for the international community to come together to promote the rule of law; support equal access to justice for all; reduce corruption; and develop effective, accountable, and transparent institutions at all levels.[^cite_note-DIPNote:_Promoting_Peace,_Justice,_and_Strong_Institutions_in_the_Great_Lakes-122]



Ghana is among the sovereign states of West Africa used by [drug cartels](197015.md) and drug traffickers (shown in orange).

Ghana is used as a key narcotics industry transshipment point by traffickers, usually from South America and some from other African nations.[^cite_note-Ghana_hit_by_illegal_drug_trade-123] In 2013, the UN chief of the Office on Drugs and Crime stated that "West Africa is completely weak in terms of border control and the big drug cartels from [Colombia](5222.md) and [Latin America](18524.md) have chosen Africa as a way to reach Europe."[^cite_note-DW_Made_for_minds:_Illegal_drug_use_on_the_rise_in_Africa-124] The social context within which narcotic trafficking, storage, transportation, and repacking systems exist in Ghana and the state's location along the Gulf of Guinea makes Ghana a more attractive country for the narcotics business.[^cite_note-Ghana_hit_by_illegal_drug_trade-123][^cite_note-Ghana_could_be_taken_over_by_drug_barons_if-125] The [Narcotics Control Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcotics_Control_Board_\(Ghana\)) has impounded container ships at the Sekondi Naval Base in the [Takoradi Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takoradi_Harbour). These ships were carrying thousands of kilograms of cocaine, with a street value running into billions of [Ghana cedis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_cedi). Drug seizures saw a decline in 2011.[^cite_note-Ghana_hit_by_illegal_drug_trade-123][^cite_note-Ghana_could_be_taken_over_by_drug_barons_if-125] [Drug cartels](197015.md) are using new methods in narcotics production and narcotics exportation, to avoid Ghanaian security agencies.[^cite_note-Ghana_hit_by_illegal_drug_trade-123][^cite_note-Ghana_could_be_taken_over_by_drug_barons_if-125] Underdeveloped institutions, porous open borders, and the existence of established smuggling organisations contribute to Ghana's position in the narcotics industry.[^cite_note-Ghana_hit_by_illegal_drug_trade-123][^cite_note-Ghana_could_be_taken_over_by_drug_barons_if-125] President Mills initiated ongoing efforts to reduce the role of airports in Ghana's drug trade.[^cite_note-Ghana_hit_by_illegal_drug_trade-123]

### Human rights

See also: [Human rights in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Ghana) and [LGBT rights in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Ghana)

[Homosexual acts](5488304.md) are prohibited by law in Ghana.[^cite_note-126] According to a 2013 survey by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center), 96% of Ghanaians believe that homosexuality should not be accepted by society.[^cite_note-pewglobal.org-127] In 2026 further laws were introduced creating a duty to report homosexual acts and to criminalise "supporters" of LGBTQ+ people.[^cite_note-128] Sometimes elderly [women in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_Ghana) are accused of [witchcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witchcraft), particularly in rural Ghana. Issues of witchcraft mainly remain as speculations based on superstitions within families. In some parts of northern Ghana [witch camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witch_camp) exist. These are said to house a total of around 1,000 people accused of and ostracised for witchcraft.[^cite_note-Camps-129] The Ghanaian government announced that it intended to close the camps.[^cite_note-Camps-129] Over a decade later, in 2023, the Anti-Witchcraft bill passed in parliament, to address accusations and the social impacts of being accused of being a witch, and the president refused to sign it into law. The legislation was reintroduced in 2025.[^cite_note-130]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Ghana)

See also: [Agriculture in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Ghana) and [Manufacturing in Ghana § Automobile manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_in_Ghana#Automobile_manufacturing)



Change in per capita GDP, 1870–2018. Figures are inflation-adjusted to 2011 International dollars.



Ghana's [petroleum](23195.md), gold, and [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean); exports in [percentage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percentage)

Ghana possesses [industrial minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_mineral), [hydrocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon) and [precious metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precious_metal). It is an emerging designated [digital economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_economy) with [mixed economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_economy) hybridisation and an [emerging market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_market). It has an economic plan target known as the "Ghana Vision 2020". This plan envisions Ghana as the first African country to become a [developed country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country) between 2020 and 2029 and a [newly industrialised country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newly_industrialized_country) between 2030 and 2039.[^cite_note-131] This excludes fellow [Group of 24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_24) member and Sub-Saharan African country [South Africa](17416221.md), which is a newly industrialised country.[^cite_note-132]

Ghana's economy has ties to the [Chinese yuan renminbi](66958.md) along with Ghana's gold reserves. In 2013, the [Bank of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Ghana) began circulating the renminbi throughout Ghanaian state-owned banks and to the Ghana public as [hard currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_currency) along with the national [Ghanaian cedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_cedi) for second national trade currency.[^cite_note-BoG_introduce_Chinese_Yuan_onto_the_FX_market-133]

Between 2012 and 2013, 38% of rural dwellers were experiencing poverty whereas 11% of urban dwellers were.[^cite_note-134] Urban areas hold greater opportunity for employment, particularly in informal trade, while 94% of "rural poor households" participate in the agricultural sector.[^cite_note-135]

The [Volta River Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volta_River_Authority) and the [Ghana National Petroleum Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_National_Petroleum_Corporation), both state-owned, are the two major electricity producers.[^cite_note-Ghana_–_Gross_Domestic_Product-136] The [Akosombo Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akosombo_Dam), built on the Volta River in 1965, along with the [Bui Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bui_Dam), the [Kpong Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kpong_Dam) and other hydroelectric dams, provide [hydropower](14073.md).[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-New_fuel_for_faster_development-138]

The [Ghana Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Stock_Exchange) is the fifth largest on continental Africa and third largest in sub-Saharan Africa, having a [market capitalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization) of [GH¢](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Cedi) 57.2 billion or [CN¥](66958.md)180.4 billion in 2012, with the South Africa [JSE Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JSE_Limited) ranked first.[^cite_note-139] The Ghana Stock Exchange was the second best performing [stock exchange](28168.md) in Sub-Saharan Africa in 2013.[^cite_note-140]

Ghana produces [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean).[^cite_note-Ghana_Entering_A_Sweet,_Golden_Era-141] It is the second largest producer of cocoa globally and its [ICCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cocoa_Organization) membership helps in its international cocoa trade.[^cite_note-142] Ghana is classified as a middle-income country.[^cite_note-IMFWEO.GH-3][^cite_note-143] [Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy) account for 50% of GDP, followed by manufacturing (24.1%), [extractive industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_sector_of_the_economy) (5%), and taxes (20.9%).[^cite_note-Ghana_–_Gross_Domestic_Product-136]

Ghana's economy is characterized by a growing manufacturing sector and the export of digital technology products. The country is also engaged in the assembly and export of automobiles and ships. Ghana's economy benefits from a range of resource-rich exports, including industrial minerals and agricultural products, with cocoa being a primary commodity. The nation is a producer and exporter of [petroleum](23195.md) and [natural gas](22131.md).[^cite_note-Ghana's_Jubilee_oil_field_nears_output_plateau_-operator-144]

The [information and communications technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_communications_technology) (ICT) sector plays a role in Ghana's industrial landscape, with companies such as [Rlg Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rlg_Communications), a state-affiliated digital technology corporation, leading in the production of tablet computers, smartphones, and consumer electronics.[^cite_note-The_Top_5_Countries_for_ICT_in_Africa-145]

Urban [electric cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_car) have been manufactured in Ghana since 2014.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

Ghana announced plans to issue government debt by way of social and green bonds in autumn of 2021, making it the first African country to do so.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] The country, which was planning to borrow up to \$5 billion in international markets, would use the proceeds from these sustainable bonds to refinance debt used for social and environmental projects and pay for educational or health. The country will use the proceeds to forge ahead with a free secondary-school initiative started in 2017 among other programs, while having recorded its lowest economic growth rate in 37 years in 2020.[^cite_note-150]



[Jubilee Oil Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubilee_Oil_Field) of the [Ghana National Petroleum Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_National_Petroleum_Corporation) and [National Petroleum Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Petroleum_Authority), located off the coast of the [Western Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Region_\(Ghana\))

Ghana produces and exports [hydrocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon) such as [sweet crude oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_crude_oil) and natural gas.[^cite_note-Five_Countries_to_Watch-151][^cite_note-152] The 100%-state-owned [filling station](61260.md) company, [Ghana Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Oil_Company), is the number one petroleum and gas filling station, and the 100%-state-owned state oil company Ghana National Petroleum Corporation oversees [hydrocarbon exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon_exploration) and production of petroleum and natural gas reserves. Ghana aims to further increase the output of oil to 2.2 million barrels (350,000 m<sup>3</sup>) per day and gas to 34,000,000 cubic metres (1.2×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>9</sup> cu ft) per day.[^cite_note-cs2-153] The [Jubilee Oil Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubilee_Oil_Field), which contains up to 3 billion barrels (480,000,000 m<sup>3</sup>) of sweet crude oil, was discovered in 2007.[^cite_note-154] Ghana is believed to have up to 5 billion barrels (790,000,000 m<sup>3</sup>) to 7 billion barrels (1.1×10<sup>9</sup> m<sup>3</sup>) of petroleum in reserves,[^cite_note-155] which is the fifth-largest in Africa and the 21st-to-25th-[largest proven reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_proven_oil_reserves) in the world. It also has up to 1.7×10<sup>11</sup> cubic metres (6×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>12</sup> cu ft) of natural gas in reserves.[^cite_note-Atuabo_gas_project_to_propel_more_growth-156] The government has drawn up plans to [nationalise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization) petroleum and natural gas reserves to increase government revenue.[^cite_note-157]

In 2015, Ghana produced 88 metric tonnes of gold as per the *Our World in Data* report.[^cite_note-158] As of 2019, Ghana was the 7th largest producer of gold in the world, producing \~140 [tonnes](31185.md) that year.[^cite_note-CEIC-159] This record saw Ghana surpass South Africa in output for the first time, making Ghana the largest gold producer in Africa.[^cite_note-160] In addition to gold, Ghana exports [silver](27119.md), timber, [diamonds](8082.md), [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite), and [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese), and has other mineral deposits.[^cite_note-Ghana_Mineral_and_Mining_Sector_Investment_and_Business_Guide-161] Ghana ranks 9th in the world in diamond export and reserve size.[^cite_note-162] The government has drawn up plans to [nationalize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization) mining industry to increase government revenue.[^cite_note-Ghana_Mineral_and_Mining_Sector_Investment_and_Business_Guide-161][^cite_note-163]

"Shortages" of electricity in 2015 and 2016 led to [dumsor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumsor) ("persistent, irregular and unpredictable" electric power outages),[^cite_note-germany-164] increasing the interest in renewables.[^cite_note-graphic1-165] As of 2019, there is a surplus of electricity.[^cite_note-166]

The [judicial system of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Ghana) deals with corruption, economic malpractice and lack of economic transparency. According to Transparency International's [Corruption Perceptions Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index) of 2018, out of 180 countries, Ghana was ranked 78th, with a score of 41 on a scale where a 0–9 score means highly corrupt, and a 90–100 score means very clean. This was based on perceived levels of public sector corruption.[^cite_note-167]

### Science and technology

Ghana launched a cellular mobile network in 1992. It was later connected to the Internet and introduced [ADSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADSL) broadband services.[^cite_note-Ghanaweb-168]

Ghana was ranked 101st in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2025.[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170]

The [Ghana Space Science and Technology Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Space_Science_and_Technology_Centre) (GSSTC) and Ghana Space Agency (GhsA) oversee [space exploration](28431.md) and space programmes. GSSTC and GhsA worked to have a [national security](240468.md) [observational satellite](46597.md) launched into orbit in 2015.[^cite_note-BBCSpace-171][^cite_note-172] Ghana's annual space exploration expenditure has been 1% of its GDP, to support research in science and technology. In 2012, Ghana was elected to chair the [Commission on Science and Technology for Sustainable Development in the South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Science_and_Technology_for_Sustainable_Development_in_the_South) (Comsats); Ghana has a joint effort in space exploration with the [South African National Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Space_Agency).[^cite_note-BBCSpace-171]

### Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Ghana)



[Surfers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfer) at [Busua Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busua_Beach) in the Western Region

[^cite_note-11_of_the_world's_most_unusual_surf_spots-173]

In 2011, tourists visiting Ghana numbered 1,087,000,[^cite_note-WAO-174] with arrivals including South Americans, Asians, Europeans, and North Americans.[^cite_note-Trade_Expo_International_Ghana-175] Among the attractions and tourist destinations are waterfalls such as [Kintampo waterfalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintampo_waterfalls) and the largest waterfall in west Africa, [Wli waterfalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wli_waterfalls), the coastal palm-lined sandy beaches, caves, mountains, rivers, and reservoirs and lakes such as [Lake Bosumtwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Bosumtwi) and the largest human-made lake in the world by surface area, [Lake Volta](1477772.md), dozens of [forts and castles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_castles_in_Ghana), [World Heritage Sites](44940.md), nature reserves and national parks.[^cite_note-Trade_Expo_International_Ghana-175] Castles include [Cape Coast Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Coast_Castle_Museum) and the [Elmina Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmina_Castle).[^cite_note-176] Castles mark where blood was shed in the slave trade and preserve and promote the African heritage stolen and destroyed through the slave trade.[^cite_note-Centre-177] The World Heritage Convention of UNESCO named Ghana's castles and forts as World Heritage Monuments: "The Castles and Forts of Ghana shaped not only Ghana's history but that of the world over four centuries as the focus of first the gold trade and then the slave trade. They are a significant and emotive symbol of European–African encounters and of the starting point of the African Diaspora."[^cite_note-Centre-177]

The [World Economic Forum](172185.md) statistics in 2010 showed that out of the world's favourite tourist destinations, Ghana was ranked 108th out of 139 countries.[^cite_note-RANK-178] The country had moved two places up from the 2009 rankings. In 2011, *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* magazine published that Ghana was ranked the 11th most friendly country in the world. The assertion was based on a survey in 2010 of a cross-section of travellers. Of all the African countries that were included in the survey, Ghana ranked highest.[^cite_note-RANK-178] Tourism is the fourth highest earner of foreign exchange for the country.[^cite_note-RANK-178] In 2024, Ghana ranked as the [55th most peaceful country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Peace_Index) in the world.[^cite_note-179]

Up and down the coastline, surfing spots have been identified and cultivated by locals and internationals. Surfers have made trips to the country to sample the waves. Surfers carried their boards amid [traditional fishing vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_fishing_boat).[^cite_note-180]

According to Destination Pride,[^cite_note-181] Ghana's Pride score is 22 (out of 100).[^cite_note-182]

### Languages

Main article: [Languages of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Ghana)

English is the [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) of Ghana.[^cite_note-USA-192][^cite_note-193][^cite_note-The_Ghanaian_Government_states_that_English_is_the_official_language_for_the_nation-194][^cite_note-Ghana_–_2010_Population_and_Housing_Census-195] There are 11 languages that have the status of government-sponsored languages:

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Ghana)

[Christianity](5211.md) is the largest religion in Ghana, with 71.3% of the population being members of various [Christian denominations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denomination) as of the 2021 census.[^cite_note-205] [Islam](6037917.md) is practiced by 16.8% of the total population. According to a 2012 report by [Pew Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research), 60.71% of Muslims are followers of [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam), while around 9.52% identify with [Shia](26961.md) Islam, while the remainder 29.76% are [non-denominational Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-denominational_Muslim).[^cite_note-TheWorld'sMuslims\:UnityandDiversity-206][^cite_note-rs-207] There is "no significant link between ethnicity and religion in Ghana". Approximately 16% belong to the [Ahmadiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadiyya) religion. Rest 8.7% are others.[^cite_note-stte.gov-208]

Ghana has around 150,000 [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses).[^cite_note-209]

The [Grand Orange Lodge of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Order_in_Africa) is a [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) [fraternal organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraternity) within the [Orange Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Order).[^cite_note-210] The one governed by the [Grand Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Lodge) is The Loyal Leopold Lodge No. 907. It was founded in 1894 by a British [colonial](7299.md) official, Dr. A. D. MacDonald. The lodge initially served as a [social club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_club) for European administrators, [military officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_officers), and merchants.[^cite_note-211] Over time, it began to admit local African members, which was a departure from the lodges in Ireland and Britain that were overwhelmingly white and Protestant. Its members participate in events and [parades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parade), including [The Twelfth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Twelfth).[^cite_note-212] They also are involved in community and charitable work.[^cite_note-213]

### Health

Main article: [Health in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Ghana)

Further information: [Eye care in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_care_in_Ghana) and [Optometry in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optometry_in_Ghana)



Development of life expectancy, 1921 to 2019

Ghana has a [universal health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care) system, [National Health Insurance Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Insurance_Scheme_\(Ghana\)) (NHIS), which is strictly designated for [Ghanaian nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_people).[^cite_note-214] Health care is variable throughout Ghana and in 2012, more than 12 million Ghanaian nationals were covered by the NHIS.[^cite_note-Ghana:_National_Health_Insurance_Scheme_(NHIS)-215] Urban centres are served and contain most of the hospitals, clinics, and pharmacies. There are more than 200 hospitals, and Ghana is a destination for [medical tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_tourism).[^cite_note-216] In 2010, there were 0.1 physicians per 1,000 people and as of 2011, 0.9 hospital beds per 1,000 people.[^cite_note-databank.worldbank.org-184] In 2010, 5.2% of Ghana's GDP was spent on health.[^cite_note-Field_Listing:_Health_expenditures-217] In 2020, the WHO announced Ghana became the second country in the WHO African Region to attain regulatory system "maturity level 3", the second-highest in the four-tiered WHO classification of [National medicines regulatory systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_medicines_regulatory_systems?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-218]

Life expectancy at birth in 2021 was 68.6 for a female and 63.7 for a male.[^cite_note-219] In 2013, [infant mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality) was to 39 per 1,000 live births.[^cite_note-Listing:_Infant_mortality_rate-220] Sources vary on life expectancy at birth; the [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO) estimated 64 years for women and 62 years for men born in 2016.[^cite_note-221] The fertility rate declined from 3.99 (2000) to 3.28 (2010) with 2.78 in urban region and 3.94 in rural region.[^cite_note-statsghana.gov.gh-185] The United Nations reports a fertility decline from 6.95 (1970) to 4.82 (2000) to 3.93 live births per woman in 2017.[^cite_note-222]

As of 2012, the [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS) prevalence was estimated at 1.40% among adults aged 15–49.[^cite_note-223]

### Education

Main article: [Education in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Ghana)



Education system's implementation of [information and communications technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_communications_technology) at the [University of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ghana)

The education system is divided into three parts: basic education, secondary cycle, and tertiary education. "Basic education" lasts 11 years (ages 4‒15).[^cite_note-GEScurr-224] It is divided into kindergarten (two years), primary school (two modules of three years) and junior high (three years). Junior high school ends with the [Basic Education Certificate Examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Education_Certificate_Examination).[^cite_note-GEScurr-224][^cite_note-GEScurr2-225] Once certified, the pupil can proceed to the secondary cycle.[^cite_note-BECE-226] Hence, the pupil has the choice between general education (offered by the senior high school) and vocational education (offered by the technical senior high school or the technical and vocational institutes). Senior high school lasts three years and leads to the [West African Senior School Certificate Examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Senior_School_Certificate_Examination), which is a prerequisite for enrollment in a university bachelor's degree programme.[^cite_note-nab.gov.gh-227]<sup>: 7 </sup> Polytechnics are open to vocational students.[^cite_note-UNEVOC-228]

A bachelor's degree requires four years of study. It can be followed by a one- or two-year master's degree programme, which can be followed by a PhD programme of at least three years.[^cite_note-nab.gov.gh-227]<sup>: 9 </sup> A polytechnic programme lasts two or three years.[^cite_note-UNEVOC-228] Ghana possesses colleges of education.[^cite_note-229] Some of the universities are the [University of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ghana), [Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah_University_of_Science_and_Technology), and [University of Cape Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cape_Coast).[^cite_note-230]

There are more than 95% of children in school.[^cite_note-UNICEF_–_Basic_Education_and_Gender_Equality-231][^cite_note-232] The literacy rate of youth ages 15 to 24 years old was 81% in 2010, with males at 82%,[^cite_note-233] and females at 80%.[^cite_note-234] The education system annually attracts [foreign students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_student), particularly in the university sector.[^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236]

Ghana has a [free education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_education) six-year primary school education system beginning at age 6.[^cite_note-Ghana_Lauded_for_Free_Primary_School_Program-237] The government largely funds basic education comprising public primary schools and public junior high schools. Senior high schools were subsidised by the government until September 2017/2018 academic year that senior high education became free.[^cite_note-238] At the higher education level, the government funds more than 80% of resources provided to public universities, polytechnics and teacher training colleges. As part of the Free Compulsory Universal Basic Education, Fcube, the government supplies all basic education schools with all their textbooks and other educational supplies, like exercise books. Senior high schools are provided with all their textbook requirements by the government. Private schools acquire their educational material from private suppliers.[^cite_note-KoinzerNikolai2017-239]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Ghana)



Jollof rice.

### Cuisine

[Ghanaian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghanaian_cuisine) includes an assortment of soups and stews with varied seafoods; most Ghanaian soups are prepared with vegetables, meat, poultry or fish.[^cite_note-Ghanaian_cuisine,_banku,_okra_and_soup-240] Fish is in the diet with tilapia, roasted and fried [whitebait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitebait), smoked fish and crayfish, all being components of Ghanaian dishes.[^cite_note-Ghanaian_cuisine,_banku,_okra_and_soup-240] [Banku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banku_\(dish\)) (akple) is a starchy food made from ground corn (maize),[^cite_note-Ghanaian_cuisine,_banku,_okra_and_soup-240] and cornmeal based staples kɔmi ([kenkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenkey)) and banku (akple) may be accompanied by some form of fried fish (chinam) or grilled tilapia and a spicy condiment made from raw red and green chillies, onions and tomatoes (pepper sauce).[^cite_note-Ghanaian_cuisine,_banku,_okra_and_soup-240] Banku and tilapia is a combo served in some restaurants.[^cite_note-Ghanaian_cuisine,_banku,_okra_and_soup-240] [Fufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fufu) is the most common exported Ghanaian dish and is a delicacy across the [African diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_diaspora).[^cite_note-Ghanaian_cuisine,_banku,_okra_and_soup-240] Rice is an established staple meal across the country, with various rice-based dishes serving as breakfast, lunch and dinner, the main variants are waakye, plain rice and stew (either kontomire or tomato gravy), fried rice and [jollof rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jollof_rice).[^cite_note-241]

### Clothing



[Adinkra symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adinkra_symbols) by [Robert Sutherland Rattray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Sutherland_Rattray)

During the 13th century, Ghanaians developed their art of *[adinkra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adinkra_symbols)* printing. Hand-printed and hand-embroidered adinkra clothes were made and used exclusively by royalty for devotional ceremonies. Each of the motifs that make up the [corpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text_corpus) of adinkra symbolism has a name and meaning derived from a proverb, a historical event, human attitude, [ethology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethology), [plant life-form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_life-form), or shapes of inanimate and man-made objects. The meanings of the motifs may be categorised into aesthetics, ethics, human relations, and concepts.[^cite_note-LIT-242] The Adinkra symbols have a decorative function as tattoos and also represent objects that encapsulate evocative messages that convey traditional wisdom, aspects of life, or the environment. There are symbols with meanings, with some linked with proverbs. In the words of [Anthony Appiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Anthony_Appiah), they were one of the means in a pre-literate society for "supporting the transmission of a complex and nuanced body of practice and belief".[^cite_note-243] [Kente cloth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kente_cloth), the traditional or national cloth of Ghana, is worn by most southern Ghanaian ethnic groups, especially, the [Akan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_people) Along with the *adinkra cloth,* Ghanaians use cloth fabrics for their traditional attire.[^cite_note-Ghanaian_Kente_Cloth-244] The different ethnic groups have their own individual cloth. The most well known is the Kente cloth.[^cite_note-Ghanaian_Kente_Cloth-244] Kente is a national costume and clothing, and these clothes are used to make traditional and modern Kente attire.[^cite_note-Ghanaian_Kente_Cloth-244] Different symbols and colours mean different things.[^cite_note-Ghanaian_Kente_Cloth-244] Kente is a ceremonial cloth hand-woven on a horizontal [treadle loom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loom) and strips measuring about 4 inches wide are sewn together into larger pieces of cloths.[^cite_note-Ghanaian_Kente_Cloth-244] Cloths come in various colours, sizes and designs and are worn during social and religious occasions.[^cite_note-Ghanaian_Kente_Cloth-244] In a cultural context, kente is more important than just a cloth as it is a visual representation of history and also a form of written language through weaving.[^cite_note-Ghanaian_Kente_Cloth-244] The term "kente" has its roots in the Akan word *kɛntɛn*, which means a basket, and the first kente weavers used raffia fibres to weave cloths that looked like *kenten* (a basket); and thus were referred to as *kenten ntoma*; meaning "basket cloth".[^cite_note-Ghanaian_Kente_Cloth-244] Kente is also woven by the Ewe people (Ewe Kente) in the Volta Region. The main weaving centres are Agortime area and Agbozume. Agbozume has a kente market that attracts patrons from all over West Africa and the diaspora.[^cite_note-Ghanaian_Kente_Cloth-244]



Ghanaian women's fashion with African print/Ankara and other fabrics



Ghanaian men's fashion with Kente and other traditional styles

Later Ghanaian fashion includes traditional and modern styles and fabrics and has made its way into the African and global fashion scene. The cloth known as [African print fabric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_wax_prints) was created out of Dutch wax textiles. It is believed that in the 19th century, Dutch ships on their way to Asia stocked with machine-made textiles that mimicked Indonesian [batik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batik) stopped at West African ports on the way. In West Africa—mainly Ghana where there was an already established market for cloths and textiles—the client base grew and it was changed to include local and traditional designs, colours and patterns to cater to the taste of the new consumers.[^cite_note-245] Outside of Africa, it is called "Ankara", and has a client base beyond Ghana and Africa as a whole. It is present among Caribbean peoples and [African Americans](2154.md); celebrities such as [Solange Knowles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solange_Knowles) and her sister [Beyoncé](83688.md) have been seen wearing African print attire.[^cite_note-246] Designers from countries in North America and Europe use African prints.[^cite_note-247] British luxury fashion house [Burberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burberry) created a collection around Ghanaian styles.[^cite_note-248] Ghanaian-British designer [Ozwald Boateng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozwald_Boateng) introduced African print suits in his 2012 collection.[^cite_note-249]

### Music and dance

Main articles: [Azonto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azonto) and [Kpanlogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kpanlogo)

[Adowa dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adowa_dance) form and music performance

Music incorporates types of musical instruments such as the talking drum ensembles, [Akan Drum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_Drum), [goje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goje) fiddle and koloko lute, court music, including the Akan [Seperewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seperewa), the Akan atumpan, the Ga kpanlogo styles, and log [xylophones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xylophone) used in asonko music.[^cite_note-Ghana:_From_Highlife_to_Hiplife-250] Afro-jazz — "the reuniting of African-American jazz with its African roots"[^cite_note-251] — was created by [Kofi Ghanaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Warren).[^cite_note-252] A form of secular music called [highlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highlife) originated in the 19th and 20th centuries and spread throughout West Africa.[^cite_note-Ghana:_From_Highlife_to_Hiplife-250]

In the 1990s, a genre of music was created incorporating the influences of highlife, Afro-reggae, [dancehall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancehall) and [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop).[^cite_note-Ghana:_From_Highlife_to_Hiplife-250] This hybrid was called [hiplife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiplife).[^cite_note-Ghana:_From_Highlife_to_Hiplife-250]

There are dances for occasions.[^cite_note-Dance,_Ghana-253] Dances for celebrations include the [Adowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adowa_dance), [Kpanlogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kpanlogo), [Azonto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azonto), Klama, [Agbadza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agbadza), [Borborbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borborbor) and Bamaya.[^cite_note-Dance,_Ghana-253] The Nana Otafrija Pallbearing Services, also known as the [Dancing Pallbearers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_Pallbearers), were featured in a BBC feature story in 2017, and footage from the story became part of an Internet meme in the wake of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-254]

### Media

Main article: [Mass media in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media_in_Ghana)



Mass media, news and information provided by television

Chapter 12 of the 1992 Constitution of Ghana guarantees [freedom of the press](105323.md) and independence of the media, while Chapter 2 prohibits censorship.[^cite_note-gov-255] Post-independence, private outlets closed during the military governments, and media laws prevented criticism of government.[^cite_note-Anokwa-256] Press freedoms were restored in 1992, and after the election in 2000 of Kufuor, the tensions between the private media and government decreased. Kufuor supported press freedom and repealed a [libel](28661.md) law, and maintained that the media had to act responsibly.[^cite_note-pr-257] The media have been described as "one of the most unfettered" in Africa.[^cite_note-bbc-258]

### Architecture

Further information: [Architecture of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Ghana)

See also: [Ghana's material cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana's_material_cultural_heritage) and [Ghanaian museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Ghana)



[High-rise buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-rise_building) in [Accra](56824.md), the capital

There are two types of construction: the series of adjacent buildings in an enclosure around a common, and the round huts with grass roof.[^cite_note-ARC-259] The round huts with grass roof architecture are situated in the northern regions, while the series of adjacent buildings are in the southern regions. [Postmodern architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodern_architecture) and [high-tech architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-tech_architecture) buildings are in the southern regions, while heritage sites are evident in the more than 30 forts and castles in the country, such as [Fort William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_William,_Ghana) and [Fort Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Amsterdam,_Ghana). Ghana has museums that are situated inside castles, and two are situated inside a fort.[^cite_note-Museums_and_Monuments_Board-260] The [Military Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Museum_\(Ghana\)) and the [National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Ghana) organise temporary exhibitions.[^cite_note-Museums_and_Monuments_Board-260]

Ghana has museums that allow an in-depth look at specific regions, with a number of museums providing insight into the traditions and history of the geographical areas.[^cite_note-Museums_and_Monuments_Board-260] The [Cape Coast Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Coast_Castle) Museum and St. George's Castle ([Elmina Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmina_Castle)) Museum offer guided tours. The [Museum of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah_University_of_Science_and_Technology) provides its visitors with a look into the domain of scientific development.[^cite_note-Museums_and_Monuments_Board-260]
