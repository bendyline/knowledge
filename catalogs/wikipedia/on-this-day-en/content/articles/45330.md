**Orange County**, officially the **County of Orange**, often initialized: **O.C.**, is located in the [Greater Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles) area in [Southern California](62520.md), United States. As of the [2020 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the population was 3,186,989,[^cite_note-cedsci-4] making it the third most populous county in California, the [sixth most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_statistics_of_the_United_States) in the [United States](3434750.md), and more populous than 19 U.S. states and [Washington, D.C.](108956.md)[^cite_note-6] Although mostly [suburban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburb), it is the second most densely populated county in the state behind [San Francisco County](49728.md).[^cite_note-7] The county's three most populous cities are [Anaheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim,_California), [Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_California) and [Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine,_California), each of which has a population surpassing 300,000.[^cite_note-GR6-8] Santa Ana is also the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat). Six cities in Orange County lie along the [Pacific Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States): [Seal Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_Beach), [Huntington Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_Beach), [Newport Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Beach), [Laguna Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Beach), [Dana Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Point) and [San Clemente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Clemente).

Orange County is included in the Los Angeles–Long Beach–Anaheim Metropolitan Statistical Area. The county has 34 [incorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incorporated_place) cities. Older cities such as [Tustin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tustin), [Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_California), [Anaheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim), [Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange,_California) and [Fullerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fullerton,_California) have traditional downtowns dating back to the 19th century, while newer commercial development or "[edge cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_cities)" stretch along the [Interstate 5 (Santa Ana) Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5_in_California) between Disneyland and Santa Ana and [between South Coast Plaza and the Irvine Business Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Coast_Plaza%E2%80%93John_Wayne_Airport_edge_city) and cluster at [Irvine Spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine_Spectrum).[^cite_note-EdgeCity-9]

## History

Main articles: [History of Orange County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Orange_County,_California); [Ranchos of Los Angeles County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranchos_of_Los_Angeles_County); and [Ranchos of Orange County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranchos_of_Orange_County)



Mission San Juan Capistrano, [photochrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photochrom) print by [William Henry Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Jackson) c. 1899

The history of Orange County, [California](5407.md), spans thousands of years, beginning with [Acjachemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acjachemen) indigenous peoples who lived off the land through hunting, fishing, and gathering. Later, Shoshonean-speaking tribes, including the [Juaneño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juane%C3%B1o) and [Gabrielino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabrielino), settled in the area. [Spanish colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas) began in 1769 with expeditions and the establishment of missions like [Mission San Juan Capistrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_San_Juan_Capistrano). Under Spanish and later Mexican rule, large [land grants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranchos_of_California) formed ranchos, which dominated the economy through cattle ranching.

One of the first land grants in Orange County under [Spanish Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain) was [Rancho Santiago de Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Santiago_de_Santa_Ana) which was given to [José Antonio Yorba and Juan Pablo Peralta (nephew)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Antonio_Yorba) in 1810, the year of the commencement of the war of [Mexican Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_independence).[^cite_note-12]

After [California became part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Statehood_Act) of the [United States](3434750.md) in 1850, the [Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Gold_Rush) spurred demand for beef,[^cite_note-13] boosting the local economy. However, environmental challenges and legal disputes over land ownership led many rancheros to sell their lands, often to American settlers.[^cite_note-14] The first American-founded town, [Anaheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim), was established in 1857 by German immigrants.[^cite_note-15] Other towns like [Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_California)[^cite_note-16] and [Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange,_California) soon followed, supported by agriculture and improved irrigation.[^cite_note-OrangeFounding-17]

The arrival of [railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_rail_transportation_in_California) in the 1870s and a real estate boom in the 1880s fueled rapid growth. In 1889, Orange County officially separated from [Los Angeles County](79734.md), with Santa Ana as its seat. Agriculture, especially citrus,[^cite_note-OrangeFounding-17] dominated until the mid-20th century, alongside oil discoveries and transportation developments like highways and railways.[^cite_note-18]

[World War II](32927.md) brought military bases and postwar population growth. By the 1950s, suburban development replaced farmland, and cities rapidly incorporated. [The Disneyland Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyland) 1955 opening marked the rise of tourism.[^cite_note-19]

In 1994, [Robert Citron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Citron) was serving as Treasurer-Tax Collector and chose leveraging strategies with county investments that ended in [Chapter 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapter_9,_Title_11,_United_States_Code) bankruptcy for the County, costing 3,000 jobs at the county and \$1.64 billion in losses.[^cite_note-20]

## Geography

See also: [List of rivers of Orange County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Orange_County,_California)



The Orange County watershed

According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Census_Bureau), the county has a total area of 948 sq mi (2,460 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 791 sq mi (2,050 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 157 sq mi (410 km<sup>2</sup>) (16.6%) is water.[^cite_note-GR1-21] It is the smallest county by area in [Southern California](62520.md), being just over 40% the size of the region's next smallest county, [Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County,_California). The average annual temperature is about 68 °F (20 °C).

Orange County is bordered on the southwest by the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md), on the north by [Los Angeles County](79734.md), on the northeast by [San Bernardino County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino_County), on the east by [Riverside County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_County), and on the southeast by [San Diego County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County).

The northwestern part of the county lies on the [coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_plain) of the [Los Angeles Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Basin), while the southeastern end rises into the foothills of the [Santa Ana Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana_Mountains). Most of Orange County's population reside in one of two shallow coastal valleys that lie in the basin, the [Santa Ana Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana_Valley) and the [Saddleback Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddleback_Valley). The Santa Ana Mountains lie within the eastern boundaries of the county and of the [Cleveland National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_National_Forest). The high point is [Santiago Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Peak) (5,689 ft (1,734 m)[^cite_note-ngs-22]), about 20 mi (32 km) east of Santa Ana. Santiago Peak and nearby [Modjeska Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modjeska_Peak), just 200 ft (60 m) shorter, form a ridge known as [Saddleback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddleback_\(Orange_County,_California\)), visible from almost everywhere in the county. The [Peralta Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peralta_Hills) extend westward from the Santa Ana Mountains through the communities of [Anaheim Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Hills), [Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange,_California), and ending in [Olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive,_California). The Loma Ridge is another prominent feature, running parallel to the Santa Ana Mountains through the central part of the county, separated from the taller mountains to the east by [Santiago Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Creek).

The [Santa Ana River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana_River) is the county's principal watercourse, flowing through the middle of the county from northeast to southwest. Its major tributary to the south and east is [Santiago Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Creek). Other watercourses within the county include [Aliso Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliso_Creek_\(Orange_County\)), [San Juan Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_Creek), and Horsethief Creek. In the North, the [San Gabriel River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gabriel_River_\(California\)) also briefly crosses into Orange County and exits into the Pacific on the Los Angeles-Orange County line between the cities of [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach) and [Seal Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_Beach). [Laguna Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Beach) is home to the county's only natural lakes, Laguna Lakes, which are formed by water rising up against an underground fault.

### Regions of Orange County



Aerial view of an [edge city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_city): [Irvine Business Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine_Business_Complex) (top), [John Wayne Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wayne_Airport) runway (upper center), [South Coast Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Coast_Metro) buildings (lower center) and below, the [South Coast Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Coast_Plaza) mall

Orange County is sometimes divided into northern and southern regions. There are significant political, demographic, economic and cultural distinctions between North and South Orange County.[^cite_note-uci-23] A popular dividing line between the two regions is the [Costa Mesa Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Mesa_Freeway).

Northern Orange County, including [Anaheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim), [Fullerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fullerton,_California), [Garden Grove](107814.md) and [Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_California), was the first part of the county to be developed and is culturally closer to neighboring [Los Angeles County](79734.md). This region is more Hispanic (mostly Mexican) and Asian (predominantly Vietnamese and Korean),[^cite_note-24] more densely populated (Santa Ana is [among the most densely populated cities in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population_density)), younger, less wealthy and with higher unemployment. It has more renters and fewer homeowners and generally votes Democratic. There are notable exceptions to these general trends, such as strongly Republican [Yorba Linda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorba_Linda) and affluent [Anaheim Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Hills), [North Tustin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Tustin), and [Villa Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Park,_California).[^cite_note-uci-23] Northern Orange County is predominantly flat, giving way to the [Santa Ana Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana_Mountains) in the Northeast.

Southern Orange County is wealthier, more residential, more Republican, predominantly non-Hispanic white, and more recently developed. [Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine,_California), the largest city in the region, is an exception to some of these trends, being not only a major employment center, but also a major tech hub and education center with UCI. Furthermore, the city is an Asian plurality (both South and East Asian), and votes reliably Democratic in recent years. Southern Orange County almost always includes [Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine,_California),[^cite_note-25] [Newport Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Beach), and the cities to their southeast, including [Lake Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Forest,_California), [Laguna Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Hills,_California), [Laguna Niguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Niguel,_California), [Laguna Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Beach), [Laguna Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Woods,_California), [Mission Viejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Viejo), [San Juan Capistrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_Capistrano), [Aliso Viejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliso_Viejo), and [San Clemente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Clemente).

Another region of Orange County is the [Orange Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Coast), which includes the six cities bordering the Pacific Ocean.

### Commercial districts and edge cities



[Three Arch Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Arch_Bay) in Laguna Beach, Southern Orange County

See also: [Irvine Spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine_Spectrum) and [South Coast Plaza–John Wayne Airport edge city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Coast_Plaza%E2%80%93John_Wayne_Airport_edge_city)

Older cities in North Orange County like Santa Ana, Anaheim, Orange and [Fullerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fullerton,_California) have traditional downtowns dating to the late 19th century, with Downtown Santa Ana being the home of the county, state and federal institutions. However, far more commercial activity is concentrated in clusters of newer commercial development located further south in the county's [edge cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_city) which include:

- Anaheim–Santa Ana, running along [Interstate 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5) between [Disneyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyland) and Downtown Santa Ana,

- The [South Coast Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Coast_Metro), located along [Interstate 405](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_405_\(California\)) and including [South Coast Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Coast_Plaza), [John Wayne Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wayne_Airport) and the [Irvine Business Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine_Business_Complex); and

- [Irvine Spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine_Spectrum) in eastern Irvine, at [the interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Toro_Y) where I-5 and I-405 meet.

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the county had a population of 3,186,989. The median age was 39.0 years, 20.9% of residents were under the age of 18, and 15.7% were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 96.3 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 94.1 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-32]

The racial makeup of the county was 1,383,257 (43.4%) [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._Census\)), 53,842 (1.7%) [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American_\(U.S._census\)), 38,322 (1.2%) [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 706,813 (22.2%) [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._Census\)), 9,035 (0.3%) [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_\(U.S._Census\)), 548,539 (17.2%) from [other races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), and 447,181 (14.0%) from two or more races. [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._Census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_\(U.S._Census\)) residents of any race numbered 1,086,834 persons (34.1%).[^cite_note-Census2020PL-33]

99.8% of residents lived in urban areas, while 0.2% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-34]

There were 1,074,105 households in the county, of which 33.4% had children under the age of 18 living with them and 25.2% had a female householder with no spouse or partner present. About 20.6% of all households were made up of individuals and 9.2% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-32]

There were 1,129,785 housing units, of which 4.9% were vacant. Among occupied housing units, 56.1% were owner-occupied and 43.9% were renter-occupied. The homeowner vacancy rate was 0.9% and the rental vacancy rate was 4.8%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-32]

The racial makeup of Orange County was 1,830,758 (60.8%) [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._Census\)) (44.0% non-Hispanic white), 50,744 (1.7%) [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American_\(U.S._census\)), 18,132 (0.6%) [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 537,804 (17.9%) [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._Census\)), 9,354 (0.3%) [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_\(U.S._Census\)), 435,641 (14.5%) from [other races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), and 127,799 (4.2%) from two or more races. [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._Census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_\(U.S._Census\)) of any race were 1,012,973 persons (33.7%).[^cite_note-90]

The Hispanic and Latino population is predominantly of [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_American) origin; this group accounts for 28.5% of the county's population, followed by Salvadorans (0.8%), Guatemalans (0.5%), Puerto Ricans (0.4%), Cubans (0.3%), Colombians (0.3%), and Peruvians (0.3%).[^cite_note-factfinder2010-91] [Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_California) with its population reportedly 75 percent Hispanic/Latino, is among the most Hispanic/Latino percentage cities in both [California](5407.md) and the U.S., esp. of [Mexican-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican-American) descent.[^cite_note-92]

Among the Asian population, 6.1% are Vietnamese, followed by Koreans (2.9%), Chinese (2.7%), Filipinos (2.4%), Indians (1.4%), Japanese (1.1%), Cambodians (0.2%), Pakistanis (0.2%), Thais (0.1%), Indonesians (0.1%), and Laotians (0.1%).[^cite_note-factfinder2010-91] According to [KPCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPCC_\(radio_station\)) in 2014, Orange County has the largest proportion of Asian Americans in Southern California, where one in five residents is an Asian American.[^cite_note-93] There is also a significant [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_United_States) population in the county.[^cite_note-94]

### 2000 census

As of the [census](6889.md)[^cite_note-GR8-95] of 2000, there were 2,846,289 people, 935,287 households, and 667,794 families living in the county, making Orange County the second most populous county in [California](5407.md). The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 1,392/km<sup>2</sup> (3,606/sq mi). There were 969,484 housing units at an average density of 474/km<sup>2</sup> (1,228/sq mi). The racial makeup of the county was 64.8% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._Census\)), 13.6% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._Census\)), 1.7% [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American_\(U.S._census\)), 0.7% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.3% [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_\(U.S._Census\)), 14.8% from [other races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), and 4.1% from two or more races. 30.8% were [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._Census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_\(U.S._Census\)) of any race. 8.9% were of [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_people), 6.9% [English](18803164.md) and 6.0% [Irish](775859.md) ancestry according to [Census 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_2000). 58.6% spoke only [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) at home; 25.3% spoke [Spanish](26825.md), 4.7% [Vietnamese](32511.md), 1.9% [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language), 1.5% [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language) ([Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese) or [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Mandarin)) and 1.2% [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language).

In 1990, still according to the [census](6889.md)[^cite_note-GR2-96] there were 2,410,556 people living in the county. The racial makeup of the county was 78.6% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._Census\)), 10.3% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._Census\)) or [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_\(U.S._Census\)), 1.8% [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American_\(U.S._census\)), 0.5% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._Census\)), and 8.8% from [other races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)). 23.4% were [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._Census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_\(U.S._Census\)) of any race.

Out of 935,287 households, 37.0% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 55.9% [married couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage) were living together, 10.7% had a female householder with no husband present, and 28.6% were non-families. 21.1% of all households were made up of individuals, and 7.2% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 3.00 and the average family size was 3.48.

Ethnic change has been transforming the population. By 2009, nearly 45 percent of the residents spoke a language other than English at home. Whites now comprise only 45 percent of the population, while the numbers of Hispanics grow steadily, along with Vietnamese, Korean and Chinese families. The percentage of foreign-born residents jumped to 30 percent in 2008 from 6 percent in 1970. The mayor of Irvine, Sukhee Kang, was born in Korea, making him the first Korean-American to run a major American city. "We have 35 languages spoken in our city," Kang observed.[^cite_note-NYT_Nagourney2010-97] The population is diverse age-wise, with 27.0% under the age of 18, 9.4% from 18 to 24, 33.2% from 25 to 44, 20.6% from 45 to 64, and 9.9% 65 years of age or older. The median age is 33 years. For every 100 females, there were 99.0 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 96.7 males.

The median income for a household in the county was \$61,899, and the median income for a family was \$75,700 (these figures had risen to \$71,601 and \$81,260 respectively as of a 2007 estimate[^cite_note-98]). Males had a median income of \$45,059 versus \$34,026 for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the county was \$25,826. About 7.0% of families and 10.3% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 13.2% of those under age 18 and 6.2% of those age 65 or over.

Residents of Orange County are known as "Orange Countians".[^cite_note-99]

In 2016, the top countries of origin for Orange County's immigrants were [Mexico](3966054.md), [Vietnam](202354.md), [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea), the [Philippines](23440.md), [China](5405.md), [India](14533.md), [Iran](14653.md), [Japan](15573.md) and [El Salvador](9356.md).[^cite_note-100]

## Government

Main article: [Government of Orange County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Orange_County,_California)



Charter of the County of Orange, with amendments through June 2016

The [Government of Orange County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Orange_County,_California) operates as a [charter county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_county), defined and authorized under the [California Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Constitution), [California law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_law) and the Charter of the County of Orange.[^cite_note-101] Much of the [Government of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_California) is in practice the responsibility of local governments such as the Government of Orange County. [Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_California) serves as the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of Orange County.

The elected offices of the county government consist of the five-member [board of supervisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County_Board_of_Supervisors), [assessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Assessor), [auditor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audit)-[controller](965040.md), [clerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_clerk)-[recorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorder_of_deeds), [district attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_attorney)-[public administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_administrator), [sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheriffs_in_the_United_States)-[coroner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coroner), and [treasurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer)-[tax collector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_collector). Except for the board of supervisors, each of these elected officers are elected by the voters of the entire county and oversee their own county departments.[^cite_note-CountyElected-102]

A seventh countywide elected officer, the [County Superintendent of Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintendent_\(education\)) (jointly with an independently elected County Board of Education) oversees the independent [Orange County Department of Education](#Department_of_Education).[^cite_note-AboutOCBE-103]

As of 2025, the Board of Supervisors oversees a \$10.8 billion annual budget and over 16,000 employees.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] The county government is managed on a day-to-day basis by a county executive officer and is organized into many departments. Outside of the elected official departments (named above), the board of supervisors manage the following departments: Clerk of the Board, County Counsel, Orange County Ethics Commission, Office of Independent Review and Internal Audit. ### Department of Education

The County Department of Education is wholly separate from the County government and is jointly overseen by the elected County Superintendent of Schools and the five-member Orange County Board of Education, whose trustees are popularly elected from five separate trustee areas.[^cite_note-AboutOCBE-103]

### County-level scandals

On July 12, 2010, it was revealed that former Sheriff [Mike Carona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Carona) received over \$215,000 in [pension](43613.md) checks in 2009, despite his felony conviction for attempting to obstruct a grand jury investigation.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] A 2005 state law denied a public pension to public officials convicted of wrongdoing in office, however, that law only applied to benefits accrued after December 2005. Carona became eligible for his pension at age 50, and is also entitled, by law, to medical and dental benefits.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] The county pension program continues to pay out his pension through 2023.[^cite_note-111]

In October 2024, Supervisor [Andrew Do](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Do) submitted a guilty plea to conspiracy to commit bribery as a result of taking more than \$550,000 in bribes for directing and voting in favor of more than \$10 million in COVID funds to his daughter's charity. His plea deal included paying back bribes that he and his daughters had received. He was sentenced to five years in federal prison.[^cite_note-112]

### Political history

From the mid-20th century until the 2010s, Orange County was known as a [Republican](32070.md) stronghold and consistently sent Republican representatives to the state and federal legislatures—so strongly that [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) described it as the place that "all the good Republicans go to die."[^cite_note-ARELLANO-114] It was one of five counties in the state that voted for [Barry Goldwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Goldwater) in 1964. During most of the 20th century and up until 2016, Orange County was known for its political [conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States) and for being a bastion for the [Republican Party](32070.md), with a 2005 academic study listing three Orange County cities as among America's 25 most conservative.[^cite_note-115] Orange County's Republican registration reached its apex in 1991, 55.6% of registered voters.[^cite_note-will-116] But with the 2008 election it began trending Democratic until Hillary Clinton won the county with an eight-point majority in 2016.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

[Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) became the first Democrat since 1936 to carry Orange County in a presidential election and in the [2018 midterm elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_California) the [Democratic Party](5043544.md) gained control of every Congressional seat in the county.[^cite_note-ARELLANO-114][^cite_note-Wildermuth-119][^cite_note-LAT_Thornton-120][^cite_note-NYT_Nagourney-121] Although Democrats controlled all congressional districts in Orange County at the time, Republicans maintained a lead in voter registration numbers (although it shrunk to less than a percentage point as of February 10, 2019,[^cite_note-voterstats-122] as compared with over 10% on February 10, 2013).[^cite_note-CA-SS-123] The number of registered Democrats surpassed the number of registered Republicans in the county in August 2019. As the number of Democrats increased, the number of voters not aligned with a political party increased to comprise 27.4% of the county's voters in 2019.[^cite_note-LAT_2019-08-07-124] Republicans held a majority on the county [Board of Supervisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County_Board_of_Supervisors) until 2022, when Democrats established a 3–2 control of the body. Seven out of the 12 state legislators from Orange County are also Republicans.

In 2020, [Joe Biden](145422.md) further improved slightly on Clinton's 2016 margin of victory.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-Politico.Silver-126] In 2023, the Republican party's registration was 33%, while the Democratic party's registration was 37.5%.[^cite_note-will-116] In [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_California), Orange County again voted for the Democratic nominee and California native [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris), though Harris only won a plurality by a smaller margin than Biden or Hillary Clinton. Orange County is one of six "reverse pivot counties", counties that voted Republican in 2008 and 2012 before voting Democratic in 2016 onward.[^cite_note-127]

The Republican margin began to narrow in the 1990s and 2000s as the state trended Democratic until the mid- to late-2010s when it voted for the [Democratic Party](5043544.md) in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election_in_California) and in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_California), when the Democratic party won every United States House District anchored in the county, including four that had previously been held by Republicans.[^cite_note-129] This prompted media outlets to declare Orange County's Republican leanings "dead", with the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* running an [op-ed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-ed) titled, "An obituary to old Orange County, dead at age 129."[^cite_note-ARELLANO-114][^cite_note-Wildermuth-119][^cite_note-LAT_Thornton-120][^cite_note-NYT_Nagourney-121][^cite_note-130] While Republicans were able to recapture two of the seven U.S. House seats in Orange County in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_California), Democrats continued to hold the other five, Biden won the county by a slightly greater margin than Clinton had, and Democrats received a majority of the votes in each of the seven congressional districts.[^cite_note-Politico.Silver-126] Republicans still carry more weight at the local level, and in 2020 for the [State Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Assembly) elections, they won 50.2% of the vote and four out of seven seats of the county.[^cite_note-131] In the [2022 midterm elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_California_elections), no congressional districts flipped, though Republicans performed strongly in Orange County, with every statewide GOP candidate carrying it.

### Overview

According to the California Secretary of State, as of February 10, 2019, Orange County has 1,591,543 registered voters. Of these, 34% (541,711) are registered Republicans, and 33.3% (529,651) are registered Democrats. An additional 28.5% (453,343) declined to state a political party.[^cite_note-voterstats-122]

Orange County has produced notable Republicans, such as President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) (born in [Yorba Linda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorba_Linda,_California) and lived in [Fullerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fullerton,_California) and [San Clemente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Clemente,_California)), U.S. Senator [John F. Seymour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Seymour) (previously [mayor of Anaheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Anaheim,_California)), and U.S. Senator [Thomas Kuchel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Kuchel) (of Anaheim). Former Congressman [Christopher Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Cox) (of Newport Beach), a White House counsel for President Reagan, is also a former chairman of the [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Securities_and_Exchange_Commission). Orange County was also home to former Republican Congressman [John G. Schmitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_G._Schmitz), a presidential candidate in 1972 from the ultra-conservative [American Independent Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Independent_Party), [John Birch Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Birch_Society) member, and the father of [Mary Kay Letourneau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Kay_Letourneau). In 1996, [Curt Pringle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curt_Pringle) (later mayor of Anaheim) became the first Republican Speaker of the [California State Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Assembly) in decades.

While the growth of the county's [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic) and [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_people) populations in recent decades has significantly influenced Orange County's culture, its conservative reputation has remained largely intact. Partisan voter registration patterns of Hispanics, Asians and other ethnic minorities in the county have tended to reflect the surrounding demographics, with resultant Republican majorities in all but the central portion of the county. When [Loretta Sanchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loretta_Sanchez), a [Blue Dog Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Dog_Coalition), defeated veteran Republican [Bob Dornan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dornan) in 1996, she was continuing a trend of Democratic representation of that district that had been interrupted by Dornan's 1984 upset of former Congressman [Jerry Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_M._Patterson). Until 1992, Sanchez herself was a moderate Republican, and she is viewed as somewhat more moderate than other Democrats from Southern California.

The county is featured prominently in Lisa McGirr's book *Suburban Warriors: The Origins of the New American Right*. She argues that its conservative political orientation in the 20th century owed much to its settlement by farmers from the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains), who reacted strongly to communist sympathies, the [civil rights movement](49001.md), and the turmoil of the 1960s in nearby Los Angeles — across the "[Orange Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Curtain)". In the 1970s and 1980s, Orange County was one of California's leading Republican voting blocs and a subculture of residents with "Middle American" values that emphasized capitalist religious morality in contrast to [West coast liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_values).

Orange County has many Republican voters from culturally conservative [Asian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans), [Middle Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Eastern_Americans) and Latino immigrant groups. The large [Vietnamese-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_Americans) communities in [Garden Grove](107814.md) and [Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster,_California) are predominantly Republican; Vietnamese Americans registered Republicans outnumber those registered as Democrats, 55% to 22% as of 2007, while as of 2017 that figure is 42% to 36%. Republican Assemblyman [Van Tran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Tran) was the first Vietnamese-American elected to a state legislature and joined with Texan [Hubert Vo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Vo) as the highest-ranking elected Vietnamese-American in the United States until the 2008 election of [Joseph Cao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Cao) in [Louisiana's 2nd congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana's_2nd_congressional_district). In the 2007 special election for the vacant county supervisor seat following Democrat [Lou Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Correa)'s election to the state senate, two Vietnamese-American Republican candidates topped the list of 10 candidates, separated from each other by only seven votes, making the [Orange County Board of Supervisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County_Board_of_Supervisors) entirely Republican; Correa is first of only two Democrats to have served on the Board since 1987 and only the fifth since 1963.

Even with the Democratic sweep of Orange County's congressional seats in 2018, as well as a steady trend of Democratic gains in voter registration, the county remains very Republican downballot. Generally, larger cities–those with a population over 100,000, such as Anaheim, Santa Ana, and Irvine – feature a registration advantage for Democrats, while the other municipalities still have a Republican voter registration advantage. This is especially true in Newport Beach, Yorba Linda, and Villa Park, the three cities where the Republican advantage is largest. As of February 10, 2019, the only exceptions to the former are Huntington Beach and Orange, while exceptions to the latter include Buena Park, Laguna Beach and Stanton.[^cite_note-voterstats-122]

Similarly, despite Orange County supporting Democratic candidates for president in 2016, and 2020, there are still several smaller municipalities in the county that have continued to vote Republican for president. In addition to the aforementioned Newport Beach, Yorba Linda, and Villa Park, the cities of Huntington Beach, Dana Point, San Juan Capistrano, and San Clemente also supported Republican nominee [Donald Trump](4848272.md) for president twice.[^cite_note-133]

### Tourism

Tourism remains a vital aspect of Orange County's economy. [Anaheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim) is the main tourist hub, with the [Disneyland Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyland_Resort)'s [Disneyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyland) being the second most visited theme park in the world. Also, [Knott's Berry Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knott's_Berry_Farm) gets about 7 million visitors annually and is located in the city of [Buena Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buena_Park). The [Anaheim Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Convention_Center) holds many major conventions throughout the year. Resorts within the Beach Cities receive visitors throughout the year due to their close proximity to the beach, biking paths, mountain hiking trails, golf courses, shopping and dining.

## Arts and culture



1965 aerial photo of Anaheim [Disneyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyland), [Disneyland Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disneyland_Hotel_\(California\)) with its Monorail Station. The Disneyland Heliport, surrounding orange groves, Santa Ana Freeway (now I-5) and the Melodyland Theater "in the round," and part of the City of Anaheim. [Anaheim Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Stadium) can be seen under construction near the upper left.

The area's warm [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) and 42 mi (68 km) of year-round beaches attract millions of tourists annually. [Huntington Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_Beach) is a hot spot for sunbathing and [surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfing); nicknamed [Surf City USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surf_City_USA), it is home to many surfing competitions. "[The Wedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wedge_\(surfing\))", at the tip of The Balboa Peninsula in Newport Beach, is one of the most famous body surfing spots in the world. Southern California [surf culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surf_culture) is prominent in Orange County's beach cities. Another one of these beach cities being Laguna Beach, just south of Newport Beach. A few popular beaches include A Thousand Steps on 9th Street, Main Street Beach, and The Montage.

Other tourist destinations include the [theme parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_park) Disneyland and [Disney California Adventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_California_Adventure) in Anaheim and Knott's Berry Farm in Buena Park. Due to the 2022 reopening of [Wild Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Rivers_\(water_park\)) in Irvine, the county is home to three [water parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_parks) along with [Soak City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knott's_Soak_City) in Buena Park and [Great Wolf Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wolf_Lodge) in Anaheim.[^cite_note-150] The Anaheim Convention Center is the largest such facility on the West Coast. The [Old Towne, Orange Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Towne,_Orange_Historic_District) in the City of Orange (the traffic circle at the middle of Chapman Avenue at Glassell Street) still maintains its 1950s image, and appeared in the movie *[That Thing You Do!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That_Thing_You_Do!)*.

[Little Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Saigon,_Orange_County) is another tourist destination, home to the largest concentration of [Vietnamese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people) outside [Vietnam](202354.md). There are also sizable Taiwanese, Filipino, Chinese, and Korean communities, particularly in western Orange County. This is evident in several Asian-influenced shopping centers in Asian American hubs like Irvine. Popular food festival [626 Night Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/626_Night_Market) has a location at [OC Fair & Event Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OC_Fair_&_Event_Center) in Costa Mesa and is a popular attraction for Asian and fusion food, as well as an Art Walk and live entertainment.[^cite_note-151]

Historical points of interest include [Mission San Juan Capistrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_San_Juan_Capistrano), the destination of migrating swallows. The [Richard Nixon Presidential Library and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon_Presidential_Library_and_Museum) is in [Yorba Linda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorba_Linda) and the [Richard Nixon Birthplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birthplace_of_Richard_Nixon), on the grounds of the Library, is a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark). [John Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wayne)'s yacht, the *Wild Goose* or [USS YMS-328](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_YMS-328), is in [Newport Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Beach). Other notable structures include the home of Madame [Helena Modjeska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_Modjeska), in [Modjeska Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modjeska_Canyon) on [Santiago Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Creek); [Ronald Reagan Federal Building and Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan_Federal_Building_and_Courthouse_\(California\)) in [Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_California), the largest building in the county; the historic [Balboa Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balboa_Pavilion) and [Balboa Fun Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balboa_Fun_Zone) in [Newport Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Beach,_California), and the [Huntington Beach Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_Beach_Pier). The county has nationally known centers of worship, such as [Crystal Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Cathedral) in [Garden Grove](107814.md), the largest house of worship in California; [Saddleback Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddleback_Church) in [Lake Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Forest,_California), one of the largest churches in the United States; and the [Calvary Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvary_Chapel).

## Sports

Main article: [Sports in Orange County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Orange_County,_California)

[Huntington Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_Beach) annually plays host to the [U.S. Open of Surfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Open_of_Surfing), [AVP Pro Beach Volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AVP_Pro_Beach_Volleyball) and *Vans World Championship of Skateboarding*.[^cite_note-163] It was also the shooting location for [Pro Beach Hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Beach_Hockey).[^cite_note-164] [USA Water Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women's_national_water_polo_team), Inc. has moved its headquarters to [Irvine, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine,_California).[^cite_note-165] Orange County's active outdoor culture is home to many surfers, skateboarders, mountain bikers, cyclists, climbers, hikers, kayaking, sailing and sand volleyball.



Street banners promoting the county's two major league teams, the Ducks and the Angels

The [Major League Baseball](38776.md) team in Orange County is the [Los Angeles Angels](1360083.md). The team won the [World Series](23489161.md) under manager [Mike Scioscia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Scioscia) in 2002. In 2005, new owner [Arte Moreno](229691.md) wanted to change the name to "Los Angeles Angels" in order to better tap into the Los Angeles media market, the second largest in the country. However, the standing agreement with the city of Anaheim demanded that they have "Anaheim" in the name, so they became the Los Angeles Angels of Anaheim. This name change was hotly disputed by the city of Anaheim, but the change stood, which [prompted a lawsuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Anaheim_v._Angels_Baseball_LP) by the city of Anaheim against Arte Moreno, won by the latter. Prior to the 2016 season Moreno officially dropped the Anaheim moniker, and the club is now simply called the Los Angeles Angels.

The county's [National Hockey League](21809.md) team, the [Anaheim Ducks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Ducks), beat the [Ottawa Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Senators) to win the 2007 [Stanley Cup](66968.md). They also came close to winning the 2003 Stanley Cup, but lost in Game 7 of the finals against the [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils).

The Toshiba Classic, the only PGA Champions Tour event in the area, is held each March at The Newport Beach Country Club. Past champions include Fred Couples (2010), Hale Irwin (1998 and 2002), Nick Price (2011), Bernhard Langer (2008) and Jay Haas (2007). The tournament benefits the Hoag Hospital Foundation and has raised over \$16 million in its first 16 years.

[Orange County SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County_SC) is a [United Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Soccer_League) team and are the only professional soccer club in Orange County. In 2011, the team's first season, the then-Los Angeles Blues made it to the quarter-finals of the playoffs under head coach [Charlie Naimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Naimo). With home games played at [Championship Soccer Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Championship_Soccer_Stadium) in [Orange County Great Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County_Great_Park), the team looks to grow in the Orange County community and reach continued success. Former and current Orange County SC players include [Richard Chaplow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Chaplow), [Bright Dike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Dike), [Maykel Galindo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maykel_Galindo), [Carlos Borja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Borja_\(American_soccer\)), and goalkeeper [Amir Abedzadeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Abedzadeh).

Professional football made its debut in the Orange County area in 1960 when the [Los Angeles Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Chargers) of the [American Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Football_League) held their inaugural training camp at [Chapman University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapman_University), then known as Chapman College. In 1966, the AFL considered expansion into the Southern California market and staged a doubleader exhibition at the newly-opened [Anaheim Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Stadium). A year later, the [National Football League](21211.md)'s [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams) began holding one preseason game at The Big A for the next three seasons before making a permanent move in time for the 1980 season. Though the team had some success, the Rams stayed in California for only 15 years before relocating to [St. Louis](27687.md) in 1995. The Rams later returned to Los Angeles in 2016, but did not return to Orange County, playing home games in [Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Memorial_Coliseum) from 2016 to 2019 and in [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md) from 2020 onwards.&#x20;

The [National Basketball Association](22093.md)'s [Los Angeles Clippers](72892.md) played some home games at The Arrowhead Pond, now known as the [Honda Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_Center), from 1994 to 1999, before moving to Staples Center (now [Crypto.com Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crypto.com_Arena)), which they shared with the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers). In 2011, a relocation bid was launched by the [Sacramento Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_Kings) to move to Anaheim, but the effort was denied in a vote by the NBA Board of Governors and the franchise ultimately remained in Sacramento.

## Media

Orange County is served by [media in Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_in_Los_Angeles), including its TV and radio stations. Two television stations—[KOCE-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KOCE-TV), the main [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS) member station in the Southland and [KDOC-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDOC-TV), a [Tri-State Christian Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tri-State_Christian_Television) (TCT) owned-and-operated station—are located in Orange County.

## Transportation

Transit in Orange County is offered primarily by the [Orange County Transportation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County_Transportation_Authority) (OCTA). OCTA manages the county's bus network and funds the construction and maintenance of local streets, highways, and [freeways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_California_freeways); regulates taxicab services; maintains express toll lanes through the median of [California State Route 91](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_91); and works with Southern California's [Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\)) to provide [commuter rail](6286.md) service along three lines: the [Orange County Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County_Line), the [91/Perris Valley Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/91/Perris_Valley_Line), and the [Inland Empire–Orange County Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Empire%E2%80%93Orange_County_Line), along with owning the land on which the [Surf Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surf_Line) rests upon from the county line just north of [Trestles Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trestles_Bridge) until the wye adjacent to [Fullerton Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fullerton_Station).

### Major highways



[State Route 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_1) winds down the coast over Corona Del Mar State Beach.

Ground transportation in Orange County relies heavily on three major interstate highways: the [Santa Ana Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana_Freeway) (I-5), the [San Diego Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_405_\(California\)) (I-405 and [I-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5_in_California) south of Irvine), and the [San Gabriel River Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gabriel_River_Freeway) ([I-605](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_605_\(California\))), which briefly passes through northwestern Orange County. The other freeways in the county are state highways, and include the [Riverside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_Freeway) and [Artesia Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artesia_Freeway) ([SR 91](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_91)) and the [Garden Grove Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_Grove_Freeway) ([SR 22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_22)) running east–west, and the [Orange Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Freeway) ([SR 57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_57)), the [Costa Mesa Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Mesa_Freeway) ([SR 55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_55)), the Laguna Freeway ([SR 133](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_133)), the [San Joaquin Transportation Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Joaquin_Hills_Toll_Road) ([SR 73](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_73)), the [Eastern Transportation Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Toll_Road_\(California\)) ([SR 261](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_261), [SR 133](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_133), [SR 241](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_241)), and the [Foothill Transportation Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foothill_Toll_Road) ([SR 241](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_241)) running north–south. Minor stub freeways include the Richard M. Nixon Freeway (SR 90), also known as Imperial Highway, and the southern terminus of Pacific Coast Highway (SR 1). There are no [U.S. Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Numbered_Highways) in Orange County, though two existed in the county until the mid-1960s: [91](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_91) and [101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_101). US 91 went through what is now [the state route of the same number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CA-91), and US 101 was replaced by Interstate 5. SR 1 was once a bypass of US 101 (Route 101A).

### Bus

Further information: [Orange County Transportation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County_Transportation_Authority)

The bus network comprises 6,542 stops on 51 lines, running along most major streets, and accounts for 210,000 boardings a day. The fleet of 508 buses is gradually being replaced by [CNG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNG) (Compressed natural gas)-powered vehicles and electric powered buses, which already represent over 70% of the total fleet. Service is operated by OCTA employees and First Transit under contract. OCTA operates four [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) service, Rapid, on [Harbor Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_Boulevard), [Beach Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_39), Main St and 17th/Westminister. In addition, OCTA offers paratransit service for the disabled (OC ACCESS), also operated by MV. OCTA offers OC flex for the rural areas of Orange County.

### Rail



[Anaheim Regional Transportation Intermodal Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Regional_Transportation_Intermodal_Center) (ARTIC)



[Santa Ana Regional Transportation Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana_Regional_Transportation_Center)

Since 1992, [Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\)) has operated three commuter rail lines through Orange County, and has also maintained Rail-to-Rail service with parallel [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) service. On a typical weekday, over 40 trains run along the [Orange County Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_County_Line), the [91/Perris Valley Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/91/Perris_Valley_Line) and the [Inland Empire–Orange County Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Empire%E2%80%93Orange_County_Line). Along with [Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\)) riders on parallel [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) lines, these lines generate approximately 15,000 boardings per weekday. [Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\)) also began offering weekend service on the Orange County Line and the Inland Empire-Orange County line in the summer of 2006. As ridership has steadily increased in the region, new stations have opened at [Anaheim Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Canyon_station), [Buena Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buena_Park_station), [Tustin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tustin_station), and [Laguna Niguel/Mission Viejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Niguel/Mission_Viejo_station). Plans for a future station in [Placentia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placentia_station) are underway and is expected to be completed by 2020.

Since 1938, the [Atchison, Topeka, and Santa Fe Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atchison,_Topeka,_and_Santa_Fe_Railroad) and later Amtrak, has operated the *[Pacific Surfliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Surfliner)* regional [passenger train](15461460.md) route (previously named the *[San Diegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diegan_\(train\))* until 2000)[^cite_note-170] through Orange County. The route includes stops at eight stations in Orange County including, in northbound order, [San Clemente Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Clemente_Pier_station) (selected trips), [San Juan Capistrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_Capistrano_station), [Laguna Niguel/Mission Viejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Niguel/Mission_Viejo_station) (formerly), [Irvine Transportation Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine_Transportation_Center), [Santa Ana Regional Transportation Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana_Regional_Transportation_Center), [Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_station_\(California\)) (formerly), [Anaheim Regional Transportation Intermodal Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Regional_Transportation_Intermodal_Center) (ARTIC), and [Fullerton Transportation Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fullerton_Transportation_Center).

[OC Streetcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OC_Streetcar), formerly known as the Santa Ana/Garden Grove Fixed Guideway Project, is a [streetcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streetcar) line connecting Downtown Santa Ana to the Depot at Santa Ana which is currently under construction and expected to open in 2026.[^cite_note-171] OCTA has also proposed connecting the two systems via [Harbor Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_Boulevard) and the [West Santa Ana Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Santa_Ana_Branch) corridor.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] Plans for a streetcar for Harbor Boulevard in Fullerton, Anaheim, and Garden Grove — the [Anaheim Rapid Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Rapid_Connection) — were shelved in 2018.

### Sea

A car and passenger ferry service, the [Balboa Island Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balboa_Island_Ferry), comprising three ferries running every five minutes, operates within Newport Harbor between Balboa Peninsula and [Balboa Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balboa_Island) in [Newport Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Beach). The [Catalina Flyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalina_Flyer) connects the Balboa Peninsula to [Avalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalon,_California) with daily round-trip passage through about nine months of the year. The [Catalina Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalina_Express) connects [Dana Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Point) to Avalon (with departures from two greater [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach) ports also connecting to [Two Harbors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Harbors,_California)).

### Air

Orange County's only major airport is [John Wayne Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wayne_Airport); its abbreviation (SNA) refers to Santa Ana, the closest large town in the early 20th century.[^cite_note-174] The airport is located in unincorporated territory surrounded by Newport Beach, Costa Mesa, and Irvine. On destination monitors with flights to SNA, the airport is usually described as "Orange County, CA" or "Santa Ana/Orange County". In 2014, its [Thomas F. Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_F._Riley) Terminal handled over 9 million passengers annually and as of 2019, seven airline brands provide scheduled service. [Los Angeles International Airport](18131.md), is frequently used by Orange County residents which provides more domestic and international destinations.

### Planned communities

Orange County has a history of large [planned communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_community). Nearly 30 percent of the county was created as master planned communities, the most notable being the [City of Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine,_California), [Coto de Caza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coto_de_Caza), [Anaheim Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Hills), [Tustin Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tustin_Ranch), [Tustin Legacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tustin_Legacy), [Ladera Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladera_Ranch), [Talega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talega), [Rancho Santa Margarita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Santa_Margarita,_California), and [Mission Viejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Viejo). [Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine,_California) is often referred to as a model master-planned city because its original seven villages (College Park, The Colony, The Ranch, Culverdale, The Racquet Club, University Park, and Turtle Rock) were laid out by the [Irvine Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irvine_Company) of the mid-1960s before it was bought by a group of investors including [Donald Bren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Bren).

## In culture

Orange County has been the setting for numerous written works and motion pictures, as well as a popular location for shooting motion pictures.

The city of [San Juan Capistrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_Capistrano) is where writer [Johnston McCulley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnston_McCulley) set the first novella about [Zorro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zorro), *[The Curse of Capistrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Curse_of_Capistrano)*. It was published in 1919 and later renamed *The Mark of Zorro*. Science fiction novels set in Orange County include *[A Scanner Darkly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Scanner_Darkly)* (1977) by [Philip K. Dick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_K._Dick) and the *[Three Californias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Californias_Trilogy)* trilogy by [Kim Stanley Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Stanley_Robinson) (1984–1990). Many novels by suspense thriller writer [Dean Koontz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Koontz) are set in Orange County; Koontz is a resident of [Newport Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_Beach).

Orange County was featured by [Huell Howser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huell_Howser) in *Road Trip* Episode 109.[^cite_note-176]

Popular television series set in Orange County include the [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting) drama *[The O.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_O.C.)* (2003–2007), the Fox sitcom *[Arrested Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrested_Development)* (2003–2006), and the [Bravo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bravo_\(US_TV_network\)) reality show *[The Real Housewives of Orange County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Real_Housewives_of_Orange_County)* (2006–present). The three programs share a common focus on the extravagant lifestyles of the county's upper class.
