**Malnutrition** occurs when an [organism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organism) gets too few or too many [nutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutrient), resulting in health problems.[^cite_note-Clinical_Nutrition_in_Practice_(2011)-11][^cite_note-12] Specifically, it is a [deficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deficiency_\(medicine\)), excess, or imbalance of [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_energy), [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein) and [other nutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_deficiency) which adversely affects the body's [tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_\(biology\)) and form.[^cite_note-Hickson,_M_2018._p.3-13]

Malnutrition is a category of diseases that includes [undernutrition](#undernutrition) and [overnutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overnutrition).[^cite_note-14] Undernutrition is a lack of nutrients, which can result in [stunted growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stunted_growth), [wasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasting), and being [underweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underweight).[^cite_note-15] A surplus of nutrients causes overnutrition, which can result in [obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity) or toxic levels of micronutrients. In some [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries), overnutrition in the form of obesity is beginning to appear within the same communities as undernutrition.[^cite_note-16] Malnutrition is reported to occur in US healthcare[^cite_note-\:9-17] and worldwide up to 40% of hospitalized patients are estimated to be affected by disease-related malnutrition.[^cite_note-\:9-17]

Most clinical studies use the term 'malnutrition' to refer to undernutrition. However, the use of 'malnutrition' instead of 'undernutrition' makes it impossible to distinguish between undernutrition and overnutrition, a less acknowledged form of malnutrition.[^cite_note-Hickson,_M_2018._p.3-13][^cite_note-18] Accordingly, a 2019 report by [The Lancet](463899.md) Commission suggested expanding the definition of malnutrition to include "all its forms, including obesity, undernutrition, and other [dietary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_\(nutrition\)) risks."[^cite_note-19] The [World Health Organization](33583.md)[^cite_note-20] and The Lancet Commission have also identified "\[t]he double burden of malnutrition", which occurs from "the coexistence of overnutrition (overweight and obesity) alongside undernutrition (stunted growth and wasting)".[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-Ghattas,_H._2020-22]

### Undernutrition



Prevalence of undernourishment (2022–2024 average)

Number of undernourished people (2022–2024 average)

[^cite_note-24]



The prevalence of undernourishment (PoU) was still higher in 2022 than before the pandemic in 58% of countries, and the situation is worse in low-income countries (77%).

Undernutrition can occur either due to protein-energy wasting or as a result of [micronutrient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronutrient) deficiencies.[^cite_note-Young2012-2][^cite_note-Jones2011-25][^cite_note-\:3-26][^cite_note-FFL2010-1][^cite_note-Dor2017-3][^cite_note-\:4-27][^cite_note-Morley,_J._2012-28] It adversely affects physical and mental functioning, and causes changes in body composition and body cell mass.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] Undernutrition is a major health problem, causing the highest [mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortality_rate) in children, particularly in those under 5 years, and is responsible for long-lasting [physiologic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiology) effects.[^cite_note-31] It is a barrier to the complete physical and mental development of children.[^cite_note-Morley,_J._2012-28]

Undernutrition can manifest as stunting, wasting, and being underweight. If undernutrition occurs during pregnancy, or before two years of age, it may result in permanent problems with physical and mental development.[^cite_note-FFL2010-1][^cite_note-\:4-27] Extreme undernutrition can cause [starvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starvation), chronic hunger, [Severe Acute Malnutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_Acute_Malnutrition) (SAM), or Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM).[^cite_note-32]

The [signs and symptoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signs_and_symptoms) of [micronutrient deficiencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronutrient_deficiencies) depend on which micronutrient is lacking.[^cite_note-Young2012-2] However, undernourished people are often thin and short, with very poor energy levels; and [swelling in the legs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedal_edema) [and abdomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascites) is also common.[^cite_note-FFL2010-1][^cite_note-Young2012-2][^cite_note-\:4-27] People who are undernourished often get infections and frequently feel cold.[^cite_note-Young2012-2]

#### Micronutrient undernutrition

Micronutrient undernutrition results from insufficient intake of vitamins and minerals.[^cite_note-\:3-26] Worldwide, deficiencies in [iodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine), [Vitamin A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_A), and iron are the most common. Children and pregnant women in [low-income countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-income_countries) are at especially high risk for micronutrient deficiencies.[^cite_note-\:3-26][^cite_note-\:4-27]

[Anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemia) is most commonly [caused by iron deficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron-deficiency_anemia), but can also result from other micronutrient deficiencies and diseases. Vitamin B<sub>12</sub> deficiency may cause anemia, but not in all cases, in which may result in changes in consciousness or thinking, or even irreversible neurological damage.[^cite_note-33] This condition can have major health consequences.[^cite_note-34]

It is possible to have overnutrition simultaneously with micronutrient deficiencies; this condition is termed the **double burden of malnutrition**.

#### Protein-energy malnutrition

'Undernutrition' sometimes refers specifically to [protein–energy malnutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein%E2%80%93energy_malnutrition) (PEM).[^cite_note-Young2012-2][^cite_note-Nutrition_in_pediatrics-35] This condition involves both micronutrient deficiencies and an imbalance of protein intake and energy expenditure.[^cite_note-Jones2011-25] It differs from [calorie restriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calorie_restriction) in that calorie restriction may not result in negative health effects. Hypoalimentation (underfeeding) is one cause of undernutrition.[^cite_note-36]

Two forms of PEM are [kwashiorkor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwashiorkor) and [marasmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marasmus); both commonly coexist.[^cite_note-Clinical_Nutrition_in_Practice_(2011)-11]



Child in the United States with signs of [kwashiorkor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwashiorkor), a dietary protein deficiency

*Kwashiorkor* is primarily caused by inadequate protein intake.[^cite_note-Clinical_Nutrition_in_Practice_(2011)-11] Its symptoms include [edema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edema), wasting, [liver enlargement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatomegaly), [hypoalbuminaemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoalbuminaemia), and [steatosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steatosis); the condition may also cause [depigmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depigmentation) of skin and hair.[^cite_note-Clinical_Nutrition_in_Practice_(2011)-11] The disorder is further identified by a characteristic [swelling of the belly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascites), and [extremities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limb_\(anatomy\)) which disguises the patient's undernourished condition.[^cite_note-Chowdhury_2008-37] 'Kwashiorkor' means 'displaced child' and is derived from the [Ga language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ga_language) of coastal Ghana in West Africa. It means "the sickness the baby gets when the next baby is born," as it often occurs when the older child is deprived of breastfeeding and [weaned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaning) to a diet composed largely of carbohydrates.[^cite_note-listeningga-38]

*Marasmus* (meaning 'to waste away') can result from a sustained diet that is deficient in both protein and energy. This causes their [metabolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism) to adapt to prolong survival.[^cite_note-Clinical_Nutrition_in_Practice_(2011)-11] The primary symptoms are severe wasting, leaving little or no [edema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edema); minimal [subcutaneous fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcutaneous_tissue); and abnormal [serum albumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serum_albumin) levels.[^cite_note-Clinical_Nutrition_in_Practice_(2011)-11] It is traditionally seen in cases of [famine](51329.md), significant food restriction, or severe [anorexia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anorexia_nervosa).[^cite_note-Clinical_Nutrition_in_Practice_(2011)-11] Conditions are characterized by extreme wasting of the muscles and a gaunt expression.[^cite_note-Chowdhury_2008-37]

### Overnutrition

Excessive consumption of energy-dense foods and drinks and limited physical activity causes overnutrition.[^cite_note-39] It causes overweight, defined as a [body mass index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_mass_index) (BMI) of 25 or more, and can lead to obesity (a BMI of 30 or more).[^cite_note-\:3-26][^cite_note-Young2012-2] Obesity has become a major health issue worldwide.[^cite_note-40] Overnutrition is linked to [chronic non-communicable diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_condition) like [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes), certain cancers, and [cardiovascular diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_diseases). Hence identifying and addressing the immediate risk factors has become a major health priority.[^cite_note-41] The recent evidence on the impact of diet-induced obesity in fathers and mothers around the time of conception is identified to negatively program the health outcomes of multiple generations.[^cite_note-42]

According to UNICEF, at least 1 in every 10 children under five is overweight in 33 countries.[^cite_note-43]

## Effects

See also: [Stunted growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stunted_growth) and [Wasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasting)



Child of a [sharecropper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharecropper) with undernutrition and [rickets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickets), 1935

Undernutrition weakens every part of the [immune system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_system).[^cite_note-Stillwaggon_2008-44] Protein and energy undernutrition increases susceptibility to infection; so do deficiencies of specific micronutrients (including iron, [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), and vitamins).[^cite_note-Stillwaggon_2008-44] In communities or areas that lack access to [safe drinking water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potable), these additional health risks present a critical problem.[^cite_note-45]

Undernutrition plays a major role in the onset of active [tuberculosis](30653.md).[^cite_note-46] It also raises the risk of HIV transmission from mother to child, and increases [replication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_replication) of [the virus](14170.md).[^cite_note-Stillwaggon_2008-44] Undernutrition can cause [vitamin-deficiency-related diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avitaminosis) like [scurvy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scurvy) and [rickets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickets). As undernutrition worsens, those affected have less energy and experience impairment in brain functions.[^cite_note-47]

Undernutrition can also cause acute problems, like [hypoglycemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoglycemia) (low [blood sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_sugar_level)). This condition can cause lethargy, limpness, [seizures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seizure), and [loss of consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconsciousness). Children are particularly at risk and can become hypoglycemic after 4 to 6 hours without food. [Dehydration](155056.md) can also occur in malnourished people, and can be life-threatening, especially in babies and small children.[^cite_note-48]

### Cognitive development

[Protein-calorie malnutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein-calorie_malnutrition) can cause [cognitive impairments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_impairment). This most commonly occurs in people who were malnourished during a "critical period ... from the final third of [gestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestation) to the first 2 years of life".[^cite_note-50] For example, in children under two years of age, iron deficiency anemia is likely to affect brain function acutely, and probably also chronically. Similarly, [folate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folate) deficiency has been linked to [neural tube defects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_tube_defect).[^cite_note-51]

Iodine deficiency is "the most common preventable cause of mental impairment worldwide."[^cite_note-salt-52][^cite_note-53] "Even moderate \[iodine] deficiency, especially in pregnant women and [infants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant), lowers intelligence by 10 to 15 [I.Q. points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_quotient), shaving incalculable potential off a nation's development."[^cite_note-salt-52] Among those affected, very few people experience the most visible and severe effects: disabling [goiters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goitre), [cretinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretinism) and [dwarfism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarfism). These effects occur most commonly in mountain villages. However, 16 percent of the world's people have at least mild goiter (a swollen [thyroid gland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroid) in the neck)."[^cite_note-salt-52][^cite_note-54]

### Social and political



A child with extreme malnutrition

Social conditions have a significant influence on the health of people.[^cite_note-55] The social determinants of undernutrition mainly include poor education, poverty, disease burden and lack of women's empowerment.[^cite_note-Bhutta,_Z._2008._pp.417-440-56] Identifying and addressing these determinants can eliminate undernutrition in the long term.[^cite_note-Bhutta,_Z._2008._pp.417-440-56] Identification of the social conditions that causes malnutrition in children under five has received significant research attention as it is a major public health problem.

Undernutrition most commonly results from a lack of access to high-quality, nutritious food.[^cite_note-WHO2014-5] High [food prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_prices) are a major factor preventing low income households from getting nutritious food.[^cite_note-FFL2010-1][^cite_note-WHO2014-5] The household income is a socio-economic variable that influences the access to nutritious food and the probability of under and overnutrition in a community.[^cite_note-57] For example, in Bangladesh, low [socioeconomic status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socioeconomic_status) was associated with chronic malnutrition since it inhibited purchase of nutritious foods (like milk, meat, poultry, and fruits).[^cite_note-Factors_in_Bangladesh-58] The probability of overnutrition is significantly higher in higher-income families than in disadvantaged families.[^cite_note-Ghattas,_H._2020-22]

[Food shortages](51329.md) may also contribute to malnutritions in countries which lack technology. However, in the developing world, eighty percent of malnourished children live in countries that produce food surpluses, according to estimates from the [Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAO).[^cite_note-Gardner00-59] The economist [Amartya Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amartya_Sen) observes that, in recent decades, famine has always been a problem of [food distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_distribution), [purchasing power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power) or poverty, since there has always been enough food for everyone in the world.[^cite_note-Sen81-60]

There are also [sociopolitical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_sociology) causes of malnutrition. For example, the population of a community might be at increased risk for malnutrition if government is poor and the area lacks health-related services. On a smaller scale, certain households or individuals may be at an even higher risk due to [differences in income levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality), access to land, or levels of education.[^cite_note-61] Community plays a crucial role in addressing the social causes of malnutrition.[^cite_note-62] For example, communities with high social support and knowledge sharing about social protection programs can enable better public service demands.[^cite_note-63] Better public service demands and social protection programs minimise the risk of malnutrition in these communities.

It is argued that [commodity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodity_\(type_of_good\)) [speculators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculation) are increasing the cost of food. As the [real-estate bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-estate_bubble) in the United States was collapsing, it is said that trillions of dollars moved to [invest](63121.md) in food and primary commodities, causing the 2007–2008 [food price crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_price_crisis).[^cite_note-64]

The use of [biofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel) as a replacement for traditional fuels raises the price of food.[^cite_note-65] The United Nations special [rapporteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapporteur) on the [right to food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_food), [Jean Ziegler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Ziegler) proposes that [agricultural waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_waste), such as [corn cobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corncob) and [banana leaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_leaves), should be used as fuel instead of crops.[^cite_note-66]

In some developing countries, overnutrition (in the form of obesity) is beginning to appear in the same communities where malnutrition occurs.[^cite_note-67] Overnutrition increases with urbanisation, food commercialisation and technological developments and increases physical inactivity.[^cite_note-68] Variations in the health status of individuals in the same society are associated with the societal structure and an individual's socioeconomic status which leads to income inequality, racism, educational differences and lack of opportunities.[^cite_note-69]

### Diseases and conditions

[Infectious diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_diseases) which increase nutrient requirements, such as [gastroenteritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastroenteritis),[^cite_note-70] [pneumonia](52135.md), [malaria](20423.md), and [measles](58911.md), can cause malnutrition.[^cite_note-WHO2014-5] So can some chronic illnesses, especially [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS).[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

Malnutrition can also result from abnormal nutrient loss due to [diarrhea](53951.md)[^cite_note-73] or chronic [small bowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_intestine) illnesses, like [Crohn's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crohn's_disease) or untreated [coeliac disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coeliac_disease).[^cite_note-PapadiaDiSabatino2014-4][^cite_note-KastinBuchman2002-8][^cite_note-Newnham2017-74] "Secondary malnutrition" can result from increased energy expenditure.[^cite_note-Grover_2009-49][^cite_note-WHO_2001-75]

In infants, a lack of breastfeeding may contribute to undernourishment.[^cite_note-Grover_2009-49][^cite_note-WHO_2001-75] Anorexia nervosa and [bariatric surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bariatric_surgery) can also cause malnutrition.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

### Dietary practices

#### Undernutrition

Undernutrition due to lack of adequate breastfeeding is associated with the deaths of an estimated one million children annually. Illegal advertising of [breast-milk substitutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast-milk_substitute) contributed to malnutrition and continued three decades after its 1981 prohibition under the *WHO International Code of Marketing Breast Milk Substitutes*.[^cite_note-78]

Maternal malnutrition can also factor into the poor health or death of a baby. Over 800,000 [neonatal deaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neonatal_deaths) have occurred because of deficient growth of the [fetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetus) in the [mother's womb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterus).[^cite_note-79]

Deriving too much of one's diet from a single source, such as eating almost exclusively potato, maize or rice, can cause malnutrition. This may either be from a lack of education about proper nutrition, only having access to a single food source, or from poor healthcare access and unhealthy environments.[^cite_note-Burchi-80][^cite_note-81]

It is not just the total amount of calories that matters but specific nutritional deficiencies such as vitamin A deficiency, [iron deficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_deficiency) or [zinc deficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc_deficiency) can also increase risk of death.[^cite_note-\:1-82]

#### Overnutrition



Chart showing a trend between obesity and diabetes over the years

Overnutrition caused by [overeating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overeating) is also a form of malnutrition. In the United States, more than half of all adults are now overweight—a condition that, like hunger, increases susceptibility to disease and disability, reduces worker [productivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Productivity), and lowers [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy).[^cite_note-Gardner00-59] Overeating is much more common in the United States, since most people have adequate access to food. Many parts of the world have access to a [surplus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surplus_product) of non-nutritious food. Increased [sedentary lifestyles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedentary_lifestyle) also contribute to overnutrition. [Yale University](34273.md) psychologist Kelly Brownell calls this a "[toxic food environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxic_food_environment)", where fat- and sugar-laden foods have taken precedence over healthy nutritious foods.[^cite_note-Gardner00-59]

In these developed countries, overnutrition can be prevented by choosing the right kind of food. More fast food is consumed per capita in the United States than in any other country. This mass consumption of fast food results from its affordability and accessibility. Fast food, which is low in cost and nutrition, is high in calories. Due to increasing urbanization and [automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation), people are living more sedentary lifestyles. These factors combine to make weight gain difficult to avoid.[^cite_note-Gardner00_5-83]

Overnutrition also occurs in developing countries. It has appeared in parts of developing countries where income is on the rise.[^cite_note-Gardner00-59] It is also a problem in countries where hunger and poverty persist. Economic development, rapid urbanisation and shifting dietary patterns have increased the burden of overnutrition in the cities of low and middle-income countries.[^cite_note-84] In China, consumption of high-fat foods has increased, while consumption of rice and other goods has decreased.[^cite_note-Gardner00-59] Overeating leads to many diseases, such as [heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_disease) and diabetes, that may be fatal.

### Agricultural productivity



Most of the people unable to afford a healthy diet in 2021 lived in southern Asia, and in eastern and western Africa.

Local food shortages can be caused by a lack of [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land), adverse weather or poorer farming skills (like inadequate [crop rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop_rotation)), compared to the population. They can also occur in areas which lack the technology or resources needed for the higher yields found in modern agriculture. These resources include [fertilizers](37401.md), [pesticides](48340.md), [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation), [machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_machinery), and storage facilities. As a result of widespread poverty, farmers and governments cannot provide enough of these resources to improve local yields.

Additionally, the [World Bank](36752.md) and some wealthy donor countries have pressured developing countries to use [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market) policies. Even as the United States and Europe extensively [subsidized](146719.md) their own farmers, they urged developing countries to cut or eliminate subsidized agricultural inputs, like fertilizer.[^cite_note-newyorktimes-85][^cite_note-86] Without subsidies, few (if any) farmers in developing countries can afford fertilizer at [market prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_prices). This leads to low agricultural production, low wages, and high, unaffordable food prices.[^cite_note-newyorktimes-85] Fertilizer is also increasingly unavailable because Western [environmental groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_movement) have fought to end its use due to environmental concerns. The [Green Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution) pioneers [Norman Borlaug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Borlaug) and Keith Rosenberg cited as the obstacle to feeding Africa by .[^cite_note-atlantic-87]

### Future threats



Projected world population by 2100 based on the UN's medium population scenario.

In the future, a variety of factors could potentially disrupt global food supply and cause widespread malnutrition. According to UNICEF's projections, it is projected that almost 600 million people will be chronically undernourished in 2030.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

[Global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) is of [importance to food security](216361.md#Climate_change). Almost all malnourished people (95%) live in the tropics and [subtropics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics), where the climate is relatively stable. According to the [Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change](15030.md) report in 2007, temperature increases in these regions are "very likely."[^cite_note-ipcc-90] Even small changes in temperatures can make [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather) conditions occur more frequently.[^cite_note-ipcc-90] Extreme weather events, like drought, have a major impact on agricultural production, and hence nutrition. For example, the 1998–2001 Central Asian drought killed about 80 percent of livestock in Iran and caused a 50% reduction in wheat and [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley) crops there.[^cite_note-91] Other central Asian nations experienced similar losses. An increase in extreme weather such as drought in regions such as [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md) would have even greater consequences in terms of malnutrition. Even without an increase of extreme weather events, a simple increase in temperature reduces the productivity of many crop [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species), and decreases food security in these regions.[^cite_note-ipcc-90][^cite_note-92]

Another threat is [colony collapse disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_collapse_disorder), a phenomenon where [bees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bee) die in large numbers.[^cite_note-93] Since [many agricultural crops worldwide are pollinated by bees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_crop_plants_pollinated_by_bees), colony collapse disorder represents a threat to the global food supply.[^cite_note-94]

## Diagnosis

### Definition by Gomez and Galvan

In 1956, Gómez and Galvan studied factors associated with death in a group of undernourished children in a hospital in [Mexico City](18987.md), Mexico. They defined three categories of malnutrition: first, second, and third degree.[^cite_note-Stevenson_2011-95] The degree of malnutrition is calculated based on a child's body size compared to the [median](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median) weight for their age.[^cite_note-Grover_2009-49] The risk of death increases with increasing degrees of malnutrition.[^cite_note-Stevenson_2011-95]

An adaptation of Gomez's original classification is still used today. While it provides a way to compare malnutrition within and between populations, this classification system has been criticized for being "[arbitrary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitrariness)" and for not considering overweight as a form of malnutrition. Also, height alone may not be the best indicator of malnutrition; children who are [born prematurely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preterm_birth) may be considered short for their age even if they have good nutrition.[^cite_note-Gueri_1980-96]

| Degree of PEM | % of desired body weight for age and sex |
| --- | --- |
| Normal | 90–100% |
| Mild: Grade I (1st degree) | 75–89% |
| Moderate: Grade II (2nd degree) | 60–74% |
| Severe: Grade III (3rd degree) | <60% |
| SOURCE:"Serum Total Protein and Albumin Levels in Different Grades of Protein Energy Malnutrition"[^cite_note-Chowdhury_2008-37] |  |

### Definition by Waterlow

In the 1970s, [John Conrad Waterlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Waterlow) established a new classification system for malnutrition.[^cite_note-Waterlow_1972-97] Instead of using just weight for age measurements, Waterlow's system combines weight-for-height (indicating acute episodes of malnutrition) with height-for-age to show the stunting that results from chronic malnutrition.[^cite_note-J._C._Waterlow-98] One advantage of the Waterlow classification is that weight for height can be calculated even if a child's age is unknown.[^cite_note-Waterlow_1972-97]

| Degree of PEM | Stunting (%) Height for age | Wasting (%) Weight for height |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal: Grade 0 | >95% | >90% |
| Mild: Grade I | 87.5–95% | 80–90% |
| Moderate: Grade II | 80–87.5% | 70–80% |
| Severe: Grade III | <80% | <70% |
| SOURCE: "Classification and definition of protein-calorie malnutrition." by Waterlow, 1972[^cite_note-Waterlow_1972-97] |  |  |

The World Health Organization frequently uses these classifications of malnutrition, with some modifications.[^cite_note-Grover_2009-49]

## Prevention

[Irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) canals have opened dry desert areas of Egypt to agriculture.

Reducing malnutrition is key part of the United Nations' [Sustainable Development Goal 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goal_2) ([SDG2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SDG2)), "Zero Hunger", which aims to reduce malnutrition, undernutrition, and stunted child growth.[^cite_note-99] Managing severe acute undernutrition in a community setting has received significant research attention.[^cite_note-Bhutta,_Z._2008._pp.417-440-56][^cite_note-Morley,_J._2012-28]

### Food security

Main article: [Food security](216361.md)

In the 1950s and 1960s, the [Green Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution) aimed to bring modern Western agricultural techniques (like [nitrogen fertilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_fertilizers) and [pesticides](48340.md)) to Asia. Investments in agriculture, such as fund fertilizers and seeds, increased food [harvests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvest) and thus [food production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_production). Consequently, food prices and malnutrition decreased (as they had earlier in Western nations).[^cite_note-newyorktimes-85][^cite_note-100]



[World population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population) supported with and without synthetic nitrogen [fertilizers](37401.md).

The Green Revolution was possible in Asia because of existing infrastructure and institutions, such as a system of roads and public [seed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed) companies that made seeds available.[^cite_note-101] These resources were in short supply in Africa, decreasing the Green Revolution's impact on the continent.

For example, almost five million of the 13 million people in Malawi used to need emergency [food aid](434679.md). However, in the early 2000s, the Malawian government changed its agricultural policies, and implemented subsidies for fertilizer and seed introduced against World Bank strictures. By 2007, farmers were producing record-breaking corn harvests. Corn production leaped to 3.4 million in 2007 compared to 1.2 million in 2005, making Malawi a major food exporter.[^cite_note-newyorktimes-85] Consequently, food prices lowered and wages for [farmworkers](10721543.md) rose.[^cite_note-newyorktimes-85] Such investments in agriculture are still needed in other African countries like the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC). Despite the country's great agricultural potential, the prevalence of malnutrition in the DRC is among the highest in the world.[^cite_note-102] Proponents for investing in agriculture include [Jeffrey Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Sachs), who argues that [wealthy countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World) should [invest](63121.md) in fertilizer and seed for Africa's farmers.[^cite_note-newyorktimes-85][^cite_note-obama-103]

Imported [Ready to Use Therapeutic Food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ready-to-use_therapeutic_food) (RUTF) has been used to treat malnutrition in northern Nigeria. Some Nigerians also use [*soy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean) *[kunu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunu),* a [locally sourced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_purchasing) and prepared blend consisting of peanut, [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet) and soybeans.[^cite_note-Soykunu-104]

New technology in agricultural production has great potential to combat undernutrition. It makes farming easier, thus improving agricultural yields.[^cite_note-105] By increasing farmers' incomes, this could reduce poverty. It would also open up area which farmers could use to [diversify crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_diversification) for household use.

The [World Bank](36752.md) claims to be part of the solution to malnutrition, asserting that countries can best break the [cycle of poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_of_poverty) and malnutrition by building export-led economies, which give them the financial means to buy [foodstuffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foodstuff) on the world market.

Child malnutrition can worsen with food price inflation. The 2021 to 2023 food price surge is associated with higher rates of wasting among children under five years of age. A 10% increase in food prices is associated with a 2.7% to 4.3% rise in overall wasting and a 4.8 to 6.1 percent increase in severe wasting among children under five years of age.[^cite_note-106]

### Economics

Many aid groups have found that giving cash assistance (or cash vouchers) is more effective than donating food. Particularly in areas where food is available but unaffordable, giving cash assistance is a cheaper, faster, and more efficient way to deliver help to the hungry.[^cite_note-csmonitor-107] In 2008, the UN's [World Food Programme](33582.md), the biggest non-governmental distributor of food, announced that it would begin distributing cash and vouchers instead of food in some areas, which [Josette Sheeran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josette_Sheeran), the WFP's executive director, described as a "revolution" in food aid.[^cite_note-csmonitor-107][^cite_note-wfp-108] The aid agency [Concern Worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concern_Worldwide) piloted a method of giving cash assistance using a [mobile phone operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone_operator), [Safaricom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safaricom), which runs a money transfer program that allows cash to be sent from one part of a country to another.[^cite_note-csmonitor-107]

However, during a drought, delivering food might be the most appropriate way to help people, especially those who live far from markets and thus have limited access to them.[^cite_note-csmonitor-107] [Fred Cuny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Cuny) stated that "the chances of saving lives at the outset of a relief operation are greatly reduced when food is imported. By the time it arrives in the country and gets to people, many will have died."[^cite_note-109] U.S. law requires food aid to be purchased at home rather than in the countries where the hungry live; this is inefficient because approximately half of the money spent goes for transport.[^cite_note-Let_them_eat_micronutrients-110] Cuny further pointed out that "studies of every recent famine have shown that food was available in-country—though not always in the immediate food deficit area" and "even though by local standards the prices are too high for the poor to purchase it, it would usually be cheaper for a donor to buy the [hoarded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoarding) food at the [inflated price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_level) than to import it from abroad."[^cite_note-111]



A soup kitchen in [Montreal](7954681.md), Quebec, Canada in 1931

[Food banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_bank) and [soup kitchens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soup_kitchen) address malnutrition in places where people lack money to buy food. A [basic income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_income) has been proposed as a way to ensure that everyone has enough money to buy food and other basic needs. This is a form of [social security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_security) in which all citizens or residents of a country regularly receive an unconditional sum of money, either from a government or some other public institution, in addition to any income received from elsewhere.[^cite_note-112]

#### Successful initiatives

Ethiopia pioneered a program that later became part of the World Bank's prescribed method for coping with a food crisis. Through the country's main food assistance program, the Productive Safety Net Program, Ethiopia provided rural residents who were chronically short of food a chance to work for food or cash. Foreign aid organizations like the World Food Programme were then able to buy food locally from surplus areas to distribute in areas with a shortage of food.[^cite_note-113] Aid organizations now view the Ethiopian program as a model of how to best help hungry nations.

Successful initiatives also include Brazil's recycling program for [organic waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodegradable_waste), which benefits farmers, the urban poor, and the city in general. City residents separate organic waste from their garbage, bag it, and then exchange it for fresh fruit and vegetables from local farmers. This reduces the country's waste while giving the urban poor a steady supply of nutritious food.[^cite_note-Gardner00_5-83]

### World population



World population since 1800 in billions. Data from the United Nations projections in 2019.

[^cite_note-114]

Restricting population size is a proposed solution to malnutrition. [Thomas Malthus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Malthus) argues that [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) can be controlled by [natural disasters](58921.md) and by voluntary limits through "moral restraint."[^cite_note-115] Robert Chapman suggests that government policies are a necessary ingredient for curtailing global population growth.[^cite_note-116] The United Nations recognizes that poverty and malnutrition (as well as the environment) are interdependent and complementary with population growth.[^cite_note-117] According to the World Health Organization, "[Family planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_planning) is key to slowing unsustainable population growth and the resulting negative impacts on the economy, environment, and national and regional development efforts".[^cite_note-118] However, more than 200 million women worldwide lack adequate access to family planning services.

There are [different theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theories_of_famines) about what causes famine. Some theorists, like the Indian economist Amartya Sen, believe that the world has more than enough resources to sustain its population. In this view, malnutrition is caused by unequal distribution of resources and under- or unused arable land.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] For example, Sen argues that "no matter how a famine is caused, methods of breaking it call for a large supply of food in the [Public Distribution System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Distribution_System). This applies not only to organizing [rationing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationing) and control, but also to undertaking work programmes and other methods of increasing [purchasing power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power) for those hit by [shifts in exchange entitlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theories_of_famines#Failure_of_exchange_entitlements) in a general inflationary situation."[^cite_note-Sen81-60]

### Food sovereignty

Main article: [Food sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_sovereignty)

Food sovereignty is one suggested policy framework to resolve access issues. In this framework, people (rather than international market forces) have the right to define their own food, agricultural, livestock, and [fishery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery) systems. Food First is one of the primary [think tanks](37101.md) working to build support for food sovereignty. [Neoliberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism) advocate for an increasing role of the [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market).

### Health facilities

Another possible long-term solution to malnutrition is to increase access to health facilities in rural parts of the world. These facilities could monitor undernourished children, act as supplemental food distribution centers, and provide education on dietary needs. Similar facilities have already proven very successful in countries such as Peru and Ghana.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]

### Breastfeeding

In 2016, estimates suggested that more widespread breastfeeding could prevent about 823,000 deaths annually of children under age 5.[^cite_note-Lancet2016-123] In addition to reducing [infant deaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality), [breast milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_milk) provides an important source of micronutrients - which are clinically proven to bolster children's immune systems – and provides long-term defenses against [non-communicable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-communicable_disease) and [allergic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allergy) diseases.[^cite_note-\:7-124] Breastfeeding may improve cognitive abilities in children, and correlates strongly with individual educational achievements.[^cite_note-\:7-124][^cite_note-\:03-125] As previously noted, lack of proper breastfeeding is a major factor in [child mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_mortality) rates, and is a primary determinant of disease development for children. The medical community recommends exclusively breastfeeding infants for 6 months, with nutritional whole [food supplementation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_supplement) and continued breastfeeding up to 2 years or older for overall optimal health outcomes.[^cite_note-\:03-125][^cite_note-\:13-126][^cite_note-\:62-127] Exclusive breastfeeding is defined as giving an infant only breast milk for six months as a source of food and nutrition.[^cite_note-\:03-125][^cite_note-\:62-127] This means no other liquids, including water or semi-solid foods.[^cite_note-\:62-127]

#### Barriers to breastfeeding

Breastfeeding is noted as one of the most [cost-effective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost-effectiveness_analysis) medical interventions benefiting child health.[^cite_note-\:13-126] While there are considerable differences among developed and [developing countries](78449.md), there are universal determinants of whether a mother breastfeeds or uses [formula](65330.md); these include income, employment, [social norms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_norm), and access to healthcare.[^cite_note-\:03-125][^cite_note-\:13-126] Many newly made mothers face financial barriers; community-based healthcare workers have helped to alleviate these barriers, while also providing a viable alternative to traditional and expensive hospital-based medical care.[^cite_note-\:03-125] Recent studies, based upon surveys conducted from 1995 to 2010, show that exclusive breastfeeding rates have risen globally, from 33% to 39%.[^cite_note-\:62-127] Despite the growth rates, medical professionals acknowledge the need for improvement given the importance of exclusive breastfeeding.[^cite_note-\:62-127]

### 21st century global initiatives

Starting around 2009, there was renewed international media and political attention focused on malnutrition. This resulted in part from spikes in food prices and the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis). Additionally, there was an emerging consensus that combating malnutrition is one of the most cost-effective ways to contribute to development. This led to the 2010 launch of the UN's *Scaling up Nutrition* movement (SUN).[^cite_note-128]

In April 2012, a number of countries signed the [Food Assistance Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_Assistance_Convention), the world's first [legally binding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legally_binding) international agreement on food aid. The following month, the [Copenhagen Consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Consensus) recommended that politicians and [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector) [philanthropists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philanthropy) should prioritize interventions against hunger and malnutrition to maximize the effectiveness of aid spending. The Consensus recommended prioritizing these interventions ahead of any others, including the fights against [malaria](20423.md) and AIDS.[^cite_note-129]

In June 2015, the European Union and the [Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_&_Melinda_Gates_Foundation) launched a partnership to combat undernutrition, especially in children. The program was first implemented in Bangladesh, Burundi, Ethiopia, Kenya, Laos and Niger. It aimed to help these countries improve information and analysis about nutrition, enabling them to develop effective national nutrition policies.[^cite_note-130]

Also in 2015, the UN's [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization) created a partnership aimed at ending hunger in Africa by 2025. The [African Union](10634933.md)'s Comprehensive Africa Agriculture Development Programme (CAADP) provided the framework for the partnership. It includes a variety of interventions, including support for improved food production, a strengthening of social protection, and integration of the [right to food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_food) into national legislation.[^cite_note-131]

The [EndingHunger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization_of_the_United_Nations#Online_campaign_against_hunger) campaign is an online communication campaign whose goal is to raise awareness about hunger. The campaign has created [viral videos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_video) depicting [celebrities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebrity) voicing their anger about the large number of hungry people in the world.

After the [Millennium Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Development_Goals) expired in 2015, the [Sustainable Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals) became the main global policy focus to reduce hunger and poverty. In particular, Goal 2: Zero Hunger sets globally agreed-upon targets to wipe out hunger, end all forms of malnutrition, and make [agriculture sustainable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_agriculture).[^cite_note-132] The partnership Compact2025 develops and disseminates evidence-based advice to politicians and other decision-makers, with the goal of ending hunger and undernutrition by 2025.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] The [International Food Policy Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Food_Policy_Research_Institute) (IFPRI) led the partnership, with the involvement of UN organisations, non-governmental organizations (NGOs), and [private foundations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_foundation).

## Treatment



A malnourished Afghan child being treated by a medical team



A Somali boy receiving treatment for malnourishment at a health facility

### Improving nutrition

Efforts such as infant and young child feeding practices to improve nutrition are some of the common forms of [development aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_aid).[^cite_note-UK2012-6][^cite_note-WHO2021-136] Interventions often promote breastfeeding to reduce [rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rate_\(mathematics\)) of malnutrition and death in children.[^cite_note-FFL2010-1] Some of these interventions have been successful.[^cite_note-Bh2013-7] For example, interventions with commodities such as ready to use therapeutic foods, ready to use supplementary foods, micronutrient intervention and vitamin supplementation were identified to significantly improve nutrition, reduce stunting and prevent diseases in communities with severe acute malnutrition.[^cite_note-Bhutta,_Z._2008._pp.417-440-56] In young children, outcomes improve when children between six months and two years of age receive complementary food (in addition to breast milk).[^cite_note-Bh2013-7] There is also good evidence that supports giving [supplemental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietary_supplements) micronutrients to pregnant women and young children in the developing world.[^cite_note-Bh2013-7]

The United Nations has reported on the importance of nutritional [counselling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counselling) and support, for example in the care of HIV-infected persons, especially in "resource-constrained settings where malnutrition and food insecurity are endemic".[^cite_note-137] [UNICEF](19698110.md) provides nutritional counselling services for malnourished children in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-138]

Sending food and money is a common form of development aid, aimed at feeding hungry people. Some strategies help people buy food within local markets.[^cite_note-UK2012-6][^cite_note-139] Simply [feeding students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_meal) at school is insufficient.[^cite_note-UK2012-6]

Longer-term measures include improving agricultural practices,[^cite_note-solcultivateplanet-140] reducing poverty, and improving [sanitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitation).

### Identifying malnourishment

Measuring children is crucial to identifying malnourishment. In 2000, the United States [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](6811.md) (CDC) established the International Micronutrient Malnutrition Prevention and Control (IMMPaCt) program. It tested children for malnutrition by conducting a three-dimensional scan, using an [iPad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPad) or a [tablet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tablet_computer). Its objective was to help doctors provide more efficient treatments.[^cite_note-\:2-141] There may be some chance of error when using this method.[^cite_note-\:2-141] The Screening Tool for the Assessment of Malnutrition in Paediatrics (STAMPa) is another method for the identification and evaluation of malnutrition in young children.[^cite_note-doi.org-142] The assessment tool has fair to medium reliability in the identification of children at risk of malnutrition.[^cite_note-doi.org-142]

A [systematic review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systematic_review) of 42 studies found that many approaches to mitigating acute malnutrition are equally effective; thus, intervention decisions can be based on cost-related factors. Overall, evidence for the effectiveness of acute malnutrition interventions is not robust. The limited evidence related to cost indicates that community and outpatient management of children with uncomplicated malnutrition may be the most cost-effective strategy.[^cite_note-143]

Regularly measuring and charting children's growth and including activities to promote health (an intervention called growth monitoring and promotion, also known as GPM) is often considered by policy makers and is recommended by the [World Health Organization](33583.md).[^cite_note-144] This program is often performed at the same time as a child has their regular [immunizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunization).[^cite_note-\:8-145] Despite widespread use of this type of program, further studies are needed to understand the impact of these programs on overall child health and how to better address faltering growth in a child and improve practices related to feeding children in lower to middle income countries.[^cite_note-\:8-145]

[UNICEF](19698110.md) has supported an initiative in [Madagascar](18964.md) assisting parents to recognise the signs of severe malnutrition among their children.[^cite_note-146]

### Medical management

It is often possible to manage severe malnutrition within a person's home, using [ready-to-use therapeutic foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Therapeutic_food#Ready-to-Use_Therapeutic_Food).[^cite_note-Bh2013-7] In people with severe malnutrition complicated by other health problems, treatment in a hospital setting is recommended.[^cite_note-Bh2013-7] In-hospital treatment often involves managing [low blood sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoglycemia), maintaining adequate [body temperature](146879.md), addressing [dehydration](155056.md), and gradual feeding.[^cite_note-Bh2013-7][^cite_note-WHO2003-147]

Routine [antibiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotic) are usually recommended because malnutrition weakens the immune system, causing a high risk of infection.[^cite_note-WHO2003-147] Additionally, [broad spectrum antibiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_spectrum_antibiotic) are recommended in all severely undernourished children with diarrhea requiring admission to hospital.[^cite_note-WHO2005Chp8-148]

A severely malnourished child who appears to have dehydration, but has not had diarrhea, should be treated as if they have an infection.[^cite_note-WHO2005Chp8-148]

Among malnourished people who are hospitalized, nutritional support improves protein intake, calorie intake, and weight.[^cite_note-149]

#### Bangladeshi model



Baby with protein malnutrition due to insufficient amount of nutrients

In response to child malnutrition, the Bangladeshi government recommends ten steps for treating severe malnutrition:[^cite_note-Bangladeshi-Ministry-of-Health-and-Family-Welfare-2008-150]

1. Prevent or treat [dehydration](155056.md)

2. Prevent or treat low blood sugar

3. Prevent or treat [low body temperature](146879.md)

4. Prevent or treat infection;

5. Correct [electrolyte imbalances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrolyte_imbalance)

6. Correct micronutrient deficiencies

7. Start feeding cautiously

8. Achieve catch-up growth

9. Provide psychological support

10. Prepare for discharge and follow-up after recovery

### Therapeutic foods

Due in part to limited research on supplementary feeding, there is little evidence that this strategy is beneficial.[^cite_note-151] A 2015 systematic review of 32 studies found that there are limited benefits when children under 5 receive supplementary feeding, especially among younger, poorer, and more undernourished children.[^cite_note-152]

However, specially formulated foods do appear to be useful in treating moderate acute malnutrition in the developing world.[^cite_note-Lazzer2013-153] These foods may have additional benefits in humanitarian emergencies, since they can be stored for years, can be eaten directly from the packet, and do not have to be mixed with clean water or refrigerated.[^cite_note-BBC-154] In young children with severe acute malnutrition, it is unclear if ready-to-use therapeutic food differs from a normal diet.[^cite_note-155]

Severely malnourished individuals can experience [refeeding syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refeeding_syndrome) if fed too quickly.[^cite_note-Via2012-156] Refeeding syndrome can result regardless of whether food is taken orally, [enterally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enteral_nutrition) or [parenterally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parenteral_nutrition).[^cite_note-Via2012-156] It can present several days after eating with potentially fatal heart failure, [dysrhythmias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_dysrhythmia), and confusion.[^cite_note-Via2012-156][^cite_note-157]

Some manufacturers have [fortified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_fortification) everyday foods with micronutrients before selling them to consumers. For example, flour has been fortified with iron, zinc, folic acid, and other B vitamins like thiamine, riboflavin, niacin and vitamin [B12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B12).[^cite_note-\:1-82] Baladi bread (Egyptian [flatbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatbread)) is made with fortified wheat flour. Other fortified products include [fish sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_sauce) in Vietnam and [iodized salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodised_salt).[^cite_note-BBC-154]

### Micronutrient supplementation

According to the World Bank, treating malnutrition – mostly by fortifying foods with micronutrients – improves lives more quickly than other forms of aid, and at a lower cost.[^cite_note-IQ-158] After reviewing a variety of development proposals, The [Copenhagen Consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Consensus), a group of economists who reviewed a variety of development proposals, ranked micronutrient supplementation as its number-one treatment strategy.[^cite_note-hiddenhunger-159][^cite_note-Let_them_eat_micronutrients-110]

In malnourished people with [diarrhea](53951.md), [zinc supplementation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc_deficiency) is recommended following an initial four-hour rehydration period. Daily zinc supplementation can help reduce the severity and duration of the diarrhea. Additionally, continuing daily zinc supplementation for ten to fourteen days makes diarrhea less likely to recur in the next two to three months.[^cite_note-WHO_(2005)-160]

Malnourished children also need both [potassium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium) and [magnesium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesium).[^cite_note-Bangladeshi-Ministry-of-Health-and-Family-Welfare-2008-150] Within two to three hours of starting rehydration, children should be encouraged to take food, particularly foods rich in potassium[^cite_note-Bangladeshi-Ministry-of-Health-and-Family-Welfare-2008-150][^cite_note-WHO_(2005)-160] like bananas, green [coconut water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut_water), and unsweetened fresh fruit juice.[^cite_note-WHO_(2005)-160] Along with continued eating, many homemade products can also help restore normal electrolyte levels. For example, early during the course of a child's diarrhea, it can be beneficial to provide cereal water (salted or unsalted) or vegetable broth (salted or unsalted).[^cite_note-WHO_(2005)-160] If available, vitamin A, potassium, magnesium, and zinc supplements should be added, along with other vitamins and minerals.[^cite_note-Bangladeshi-Ministry-of-Health-and-Family-Welfare-2008-150]

Giving base (as in [Ringer's lactate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringer's_lactate_solution)) to treat [acidosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acidosis) without simultaneously supplementing potassium worsens [low blood potassium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypokalemia).[^cite_note-WHO_(2005)-160]

### Treating diarrhea

#### Preventing dehydration

Food and drink can help prevent dehydration in malnourished people with diarrhea. Eating (or breastfeeding, among infants) should resume as soon as possible.[^cite_note-WHO2005Chp8-148] Sugary beverages like soft drinks, fruit juices, and sweetened teas are not recommended as they may worsen diarrhea.[^cite_note-WHO2005Chp4-161]

Malnourished people with diarrhea (especially children) should be encouraged to drink fluids; the best choices are fluids with modest amounts of sugar and salt, like [vegetable broth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broth) or salted [rice water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_water). If clean water is available, they should be encouraged to drink that too. Malnourished people should be allowed to drink as much as they want, unless signs of swelling emerge.

Babies can be given small amounts of fluids via an eyedropper or a syringe without the needle. Children under two should receive a teaspoon of fluid every one to two minutes; older children and adults should take frequent sips of fluids directly from a cup.[^cite_note-WHO_(2005)-160] After the first two hours, fluids and foods should be alternated, rehydration should be continued at the same rate or more slowly, depending on how much fluid the child wants and whether they are having ongoing diarrhea.[^cite_note-Bangladeshi-Ministry-of-Health-and-Family-Welfare-2008-150]

If vomiting occurs, fluids can be paused for 5–10 minutes and then restarted more slowly. Vomiting rarely prevents rehydration, since fluids are still absorbed and vomiting is usually short-term.[^cite_note-WHO2005Chp4-161]

#### Oral rehydration therapy

If prevention has failed and dehydration develops, the preferred treatment is rehydration through [oral rehydration therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_rehydration_therapy) (ORT). In severely undernourished children with diarrhea, rehydration should be done slowly, according to the World Health Organization.

Oral rehydration solutions consist of clean water mixed with small amounts of sugars and salts. These solutions help restore normal electrolyte levels, provide a source of [carbohydrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbohydrate), and help with fluid replacement.[^cite_note-162]

Reduced-[osmolarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmotic_concentration) ORS is the current standard of care for oral rehydration therapy, with reasonably wide availability.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164] Introduced in 2003 by WHO and UNICEF, reduced-osmolarity solutions contain lower concentrations of sodium and glucose than original ORS preparations. Reduced-osmolarity ORS has the added benefit of reducing stool volume and vomiting while simultaneously preventing dehydration. Packets of reduced-osmolarity ORS include glucose, table salt, [potassium chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_chloride), and [trisodium citrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trisodium_citrate). For general use, each packet should be mixed with a liter of water. However, for malnourished children, experts recommend adding a packet of ORS to two liters of water, along with an extra 50 grams of [sucrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucrose) and some stock potassium solution.[^cite_note-165]

People who have no access to commercially available ORS can make a homemade version using water, sugar, and table salt. Experts agree that homemade ORS preparations should include one liter (34 [oz.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_ounce)) of clean water and 6 tsp (30 mL) of sugar; however, they disagree about whether they should contain half a teaspoon of table salt or a full teaspoon. Most sources recommend using half a teaspoon of salt per liter of water.[^cite_note-WHO_(2005)-160][^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167][^cite_note-Family-Practice-Notebook-Feb-2014-168] However, people with malnutrition have an excess of body sodium.[^cite_note-Bangladeshi-Ministry-of-Health-and-Family-Welfare-2008-150] To avoid worsening this symptom, ORS for people with severe undernutrition should contain half the usual amount of sodium and more potassium.

&#x20;Patients who do not drink may require fluids by [nasogastric tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasogastric_tube). [Intravenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intravenous) fluids are recommended only in those who have [significant dehydration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_\(circulatory\)) due to their potential complications, including [congestive heart failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congestive_heart_failure).[^cite_note-WHO2005Chp8-148]&#x20;



Examples of commercially available oral rehydration salts (Nepal on left, Peru on right)

### Low blood sugar

[Hypoglycemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoglycemia), whether known or suspected, can be treated with a mixture of sugar and water. If the patient is conscious, the initial dose of sugar and water can be given by mouth.[^cite_note-169] Otherwise, they should receive glucose by [intravenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intravenous) or [nasogastric tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasogastric_tube). If seizures occur (and continue after glucose is given), rectal [diazepam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diazepam) may be helpful. Blood sugar levels should be re-checked on two-hour intervals.[^cite_note-Bangladeshi-Ministry-of-Health-and-Family-Welfare-2008-150]

### Hypothermia

[Hypothermia](146879.md) (dangerously low [core body temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_body_temperature)) can occur in malnutrition, particularly in children. Mild hypothermia causes confusion, trembling, and clumsiness; more severe cases can be fatal. Keeping malnourished children warm can prevent or treat hypothermia. Covering the child (including their head) in blankets is one method. Another method is to warm the child through direct skin-to-skin contact with their mother or father, then covering both parent and child.

Warming methods are usually most important at night.[^cite_note-Bangladeshi-Ministry-of-Health-and-Family-Welfare-2008-150] Prolonged bathing or prolonged medical exams can further lower body temperature and are not recommended for malnourished children at high risk of hypothermia.

### Mortality



Number child deaths malnutrition

In 2010 [protein-energy malnutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein-energy_malnutrition) resulted in 600,000 deaths down from 883,000 deaths in 1990.[^cite_note-Loz2012-179] Other nutritional deficiencies, which include [iodine deficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine_deficiency) and [iron deficiency anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_deficiency_anemia), result in another 84,000 deaths.[^cite_note-Loz2012-179] In 2010 malnutrition caused about 1.5 million deaths in women and children.[^cite_note-Lim2012-180]

According to the World Health Organization, malnutrition is the biggest contributor to [child mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_mortality), present in half of all cases.[^cite_note-economist-181] Six million children die of hunger every year.[^cite_note-182] [Underweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underweight) births and intrauterine growth restrictions cause 2.2 million child deaths a year. Poor or non-existent breastfeeding causes another 1.4 million. Other deficiencies, such as lack of [vitamin A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_A) or [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), for example, account for 1 million. Malnutrition in the first two years is irreversible. Malnourished children grow up with worse health and lower education achievement. Their own children tend to be smaller. Malnutrition was previously seen as something that exacerbates the problems of diseases such as measles, pneumonia and diarrhea, but malnutrition actually causes diseases, and can be fatal in its own right.[^cite_note-economist-181]



Number of people undernourished by region

[^cite_note-183]

It was estimated in 2017 that nearly one in three persons globally had at least one form of malnutrition: [wasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasting), [stunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stunting_\(Medical\)), [vitamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin) or [mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_\(nutrient\)) deficiency, overweight, obesity, or diet-related noncommunicable diseases.[^cite_note-184] Undernutrition is more common in [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries).[^cite_note-185] Stunting is more prevalent in urban slums than in rural areas.[^cite_note-Murarkar,_S._2020-186]

Studies on malnutrition have the population categorised into different groups including infants, under-five children, children, adolescents, pregnant women, adults and the elderly population. The use of different growth references in different studies leads to variances in the undernutrition prevalence reported in different studies. Some of the growth references used in studies include the [National Center for Health Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Health_Statistics) (NCHS) growth charts, WHO reference 2007, [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](6811.md) (CDC) growth charts, National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), WHO reference 1995, Obesity Task Force (IOTF) criteria and [Indian Academy of Pediatrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Academy_of_Pediatrics) (IAP) growth charts.[^cite_note-187] In 2023, an estimated 28.9 percent of the [global population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population) – 2.33 billion people – were moderately or severely food insecure.[^cite_note-188]

### Children



Prevalence of undernourishment by region

[^cite_note-189]

The prevalence of undernutrition is highest among children under five.[^cite_note-Murarkar,_S._2020-186] In 2024, 150.2 million children under five years old were stunted, 42.8 million were wasted, and 35.5 million were overweight or obese.[^cite_note-190] In 2021, an estimated 45% of deaths in children were linked to undernutrition.[^cite_note-\:3-26][^cite_note-WHO2014-5] As of 2020, the prevalence of wasting among children under five in South Asia was reported to be 16% moderately or severely wasted.[^cite_note-Murarkar,_S._2020-186] As of 2022, [UNICEF](19698110.md) reported this prevalence as having slightly improved, but still being at 14.8%.[^cite_note-191] India has one of the highest burdens of wasting in Asia with over 20% wasted children.[^cite_note-192] However, the burden of undernutrition among under-five children in African countries is much higher. A pooled analysis of the prevalence of chronic undernutrition among under-five children in East Africa was identified to be 33.3%. This prevalence of undernutrition among under-five children ranged from 21.9% in Kenya to 53% in Burundi.[^cite_note-193]

In Tanzania, the prevalence of stunting, among children under five varied from 41% in lowland and 64.5% in highland areas. Undernutrition by underweight and wasting was 11.5% and 2.5% in lowland and 22.% and 1.4% in the highland areas of Tanzania respectively.[^cite_note-194] In South Sudan, the prevalence of undernutrition explained by stunting, underweight and wasting in under-five children were 23.8%, 4.8% and 2.3% respectively.[^cite_note-195] In 28 countries, at least 30% of children were still affected by stunting in 2022.[^cite_note-196]

[Vitamin A deficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_A_deficiency) affects one third of children under age 5 around the world,[^cite_note-\:5-197] leading to 670,000 deaths and 250,000–500,000 cases of [blindness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_impairment).[^cite_note-198] Vitamin A supplementation has been shown to reduce all-cause mortality by 12 to 24%.[^cite_note-199]

### Adults

Main article: [Undernutrition in older adults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undernutrition_in_older_adults)



Most countries either do not have sufficient data or are off track to achieve the 2030 global nutrition targets

As of June 2021, 1.9 billion adults were overweight or obese, and 462 million adults were underweight.[^cite_note-\:3-26] Globally, two billion people had [iodine deficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine_deficiency) in 2017.[^cite_note-200] In 2020, 900 million women and children had anemia, which is often caused by iron deficiency.[^cite_note-201] More than 3.1 billion people in the world – 42% – were unable to afford a healthy diet in 2021.[^cite_note-202]

Certain groups have higher rates of undernutrition, including [elderly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undernutrition_in_older_adults) people and women (in particular while pregnant or [breastfeeding](19347033.md) [children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undernutrition_in_children) under five years of age). Undernutrition is an increasing health problem in people aged over 65 years, even in developed countries, especially among nursing home residents and in acute care hospitals.[^cite_note-203] In the elderly, undernutrition is more commonly due to physical, psychological, and social factors, not a lack of food.[^cite_note-204] Age-related reduced dietary intake due to chewing and swallowing problems, sensory decline, depression, imbalanced gut microbiome, poverty and loneliness are major contributors to undernutrition in the elderly population. Malnutrition is also attributed due to wrong diet plan adopted by people who aim to reduce their weight without medical practitioners or nutritionist advice.[^cite_note-205]

### Increase



Four year old Palestinian girl starved to death, during [Gaza Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Famine)

There has been a global increase in food insecurity and hunger between 2011 and 2020. In 2015, 795 million people (about one in ten people on earth) had undernutrition.[^cite_note-UNFAO2018-172][^cite_note-FAO2010-206] It is estimated that between 691 and 783 million people in the world faced hunger in 2022.[^cite_note-207] According to UNICEF, 2.4 billion people were moderately or severely food insecure in 2022, 391 million more than in 2019.[^cite_note-208]



Projected numbers of undernourished people by FAO shows global malnutrition rates

These increases are partially related to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), which continues to highlight the weaknesses of current food and health systems. It has contributed to [food insecurity](216361.md), increasing hunger worldwide; meanwhile, lower physical activity during [lockdowns](2530301.md) has contributed to increases in overweight and obesity.[^cite_note-\:6-209] In 2020, experts estimated that by the end of the year, the pandemic could have double the number of people at risk of suffering acute hunger, around 130 million more undernourished people.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211] Similarly, experts estimated that the [prevalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prevalence) of moderate and severe wasting could increase by 14% due to COVID-19; coupled with reductions in nutrition and health services coverage, this could result in over 128,000 additional deaths among children under 5 in 2020 alone.[^cite_note-\:6-209] Although COVID-19 is less severe in children than in adults, the risk of severe disease increases with undernutrition.[^cite_note-212]

Other major causes of hunger include manmade conflicts, [climate changes](5042951.md), and economic downturns.[^cite_note-213]

## History

See also: [Hunger § The fight against hunger](149422.md#The_fight_against_hunger)

Hunger has been a perennial human problem. However, until the early 20th century, there was relatively little awareness of the qualitative aspects of malnutrition.

Throughout history, various peoples have known the importance of eating certain foods to prevent symptoms now associated with malnutrition. Yet such knowledge appears to have been repeatedly lost and then re-discovered. For example, the [ancient Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) reportedly knew the symptoms of [scurvy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scurvy). Much later, in the 14th century, [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) sometimes used anti-scurvy measures – for example, ensuring that [citrus fruits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus) were planted on Mediterranean islands, for use on sea journeys. However, for several centuries, Europeans appear to have forgotten the importance of these measures. They rediscovered this knowledge in the 18th century, and by the early 19th century, the [Royal Navy](26061.md) was issuing frequent rations of [lemon juice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemon) to every crewman on their ships. This massively reduced scurvy deaths among British sailors, which in turn gave the British a significant advantage in the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars). Later on in the 19th century, the Royal Navy replaced lemons with limes (unaware at the time that lemons are far more effective at preventing scurvy).[^cite_note-Ruxin-214][^cite_note-HungerModHist-215]



Starving children during the [Russian famine of 1921–1922](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_famine_of_1921%E2%80%931922)

According to historian Michael Worboys, malnutrition was essentially discovered, and the [science of nutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutritional_science) established, between [World War I](4764461.md) and [World War II](32927.md). Advances built on prior works like [Casimir Funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_Funk)'s 1912 formulisation of the concept of vitamins. Scientific study of malnutrition increased in the 1920s and 1930s, and grew even more common after World War II.

Non-governmental organizations and United Nations agencies began to devote considerable energy to alleviating malnutrition around the world. The exact methods and priorities for doing this tended to fluctuate over the years, with varying levels of focus on different types of malnutrition like [Kwashiorkor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwashiorkor) or [Marasmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marasmus); varying levels of concern on protein deficiency compared to vitamins, minerals and lack of raw calories; and varying priorities given to the problem of malnutrition in general compared to other health and development concerns. The [green Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution) of the 1950s and 1960s saw considerable improvement in capability to prevent malnutrition.[^cite_note-HungerModHist-215][^cite_note-Ruxin-214][^cite_note-Grigg-216]

One of the first official global documents addressing Food security and global malnutrition was the 1948 [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights)(UDHR). Within this document it stated that access to food was part of an adequate right to a standard of living.[^cite_note-217] The [Right to food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_food) was asserted in the [International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Covenant_on_Economic,_Social_and_Cultural_Rights), a [treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty) adopted by the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) on December 16, 1966. The Right to food is a [human right](13831.md) for people to feed themselves in dignity, be free from hunger, food insecurity, and malnutrition.[^cite_note-218] As of 2018, the treaty has been signed by 166 countries, by signing states agreed to take steps to the maximum of their available resources to achieve the right to adequate food.

However, after the 1966 International Covenant the global concern for the access to sufficient food only became more present, leading to the first ever World Food Conference that was held in 1974 in Rome, Italy. The [Universal Declaration on the Eradication of Hunger and Malnutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_on_the_Eradication_of_Hunger_and_Malnutrition) was a UN resolution adopted November 16, 1974, by all 135 countries that attended the 1974 World Food Conference.[^cite_note-219] This non-legally binding document set forth certain aspirations for countries to follow to sufficiently take action on the global food problem. Ultimately this document outline and provided guidance as to how the international community as one could work towards fighting and solving the growing global issue of malnutrition and hunger.

Adoption of the right to food was included in the [Additional Protocol to the American Convention on Human Rights in the area of Economic, Social, and Cultural Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Convention_on_Human_Rights#Additional_Protocols), this 1978 document was adopted by many countries in the Americas, the purpose of the document is, "to consolidate in this hemisphere, within the framework of democratic institutions, a system of personal liberty and [social justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_justice) based on respect for the essential rights of man."[^cite_note-220]

A later document in the timeline of global initiatives for malnutrition was the 1996 [Rome Declaration on World Food Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_Declaration_on_World_Food_Security), organized by the [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization). This document reaffirmed the right to have access to safe and nutritious food by everyone, also considering that everyone gets sufficient food, and set the goals for all nations to improve their commitment to food security by halving their number of undernourished people by 2015.[^cite_note-221] In 2004 the Food and Agriculture Organization adopted the [Right to Food Guidelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_Food_Guidelines), which offered states a framework of how to increase the right to food on a national basis.

## Special populations

Undernutrition is an important determinant of maternal and child health, accounting for more than a third of child deaths and more than 10 percent of the total global [disease burden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease_burden) according to 2008 studies.[^cite_note-Black2008-222]

### Children

Main article: [Undernutrition in children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undernutrition_in_children)



Malnourished children in Niger, during the 2005 famine

Undernutrition adversely affects the cognitive development of children, contributing to poor earning capacity and poverty in adulthood.[^cite_note-223] The development of childhood undernutrition coincides with the introduction of complementary weaning foods which are usually nutrient deficient.[^cite_note-224] The World Health Organization estimated in 2008 that malnutrition accounted for 54 percent of child mortality worldwide,[^cite_note-Nutrition_in_pediatrics-35] about 1 million children.[^cite_note-ENJM013113-225] There is a strong association between undernutrition and child mortality.[^cite_note-226]

Another estimate in 2008 also by WHO stated that childhood underweight was the cause for about 35% of all deaths of children under the age of five years worldwide.[^cite_note-\:0-227] Over 90% of the stunted children below five years of age live in sub-Saharan Africa and South Central Asia.[^cite_note-Bhutta,_Z._2008._pp.417-440-56] Although access to adequate food and improving nutritional intake is an obvious solution to tackling undernutrition in children, the progress in reducing children undernutrition has been disappointing.[^cite_note-228]

### Women

Main article: [Food security § Gender and food security](216361.md#Gender_and_food_security)



*[Migrant Mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migrant_Mother)* by [Dorothea Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothea_Lange) (1936)



Starved girl

In 2022, more than 1 billion adolescent girls and women suffered from undernutrition, according to UNICEF's 2023 report "Undernourished and Overlooked: A Global Nutrition Crisis in Adolescent Girls and Women".[^cite_note-\:10-229] The gender gap in food insecurity more than doubled between 2019 (49 million) and 2021 (126 million). The report shows that globally, 30% of women aged 15–49 years are living with anaemia while 10 per cent of women aged 20–49 years suffer from underweight. South Asia, West and Central Africa and Eastern and Southern Africa are home to 60% of women with anaemia and 65% of women being underweight. In contrast, overweight is affecting more than 35% of women aged 20–49 years, of which 13% are living with obesity.[^cite_note-\:10-229]

The Middle East and North Africa has the highest prevalence of overweight with 61% affected. North America closely follows at 60%.[^cite_note-\:10-229] Fewer than 1 in 3 adolescent girls and women have diets meeting the minimum dietary diversity in the Sudan (10%), Burundi (12%), Burkina Faso (17%) and Afghanistan (26%).[^cite_note-\:10-229] In Niger, the percentage of women accessing a minimally diverse diet fell from 53% to 37% between 2020 and 2022.[^cite_note-\:10-229]

Researchers from the Centre for World Food Studies in 2003 found that the gap between levels of undernutrition in men and women is generally small, but that the gap varies from region to region and from country to country.[^cite_note-Nube_2003-230] These small-scale studies showed that female undernutrition prevalence rates exceeded male undernutrition prevalence rates in South/Southeast Asia and Latin America and were lower in Sub-Saharan Africa.[^cite_note-Nube_2003-230] Datasets for Ethiopia and Zimbabwe reported undernutrition rates between 1.5 and 2 times higher in men than in women; however, in India and Pakistan, datasets rates of undernutrition were 1.5–2 times higher in women than in men. Intra-country variation also occurs, with frequent high gaps between regional undernutrition rates.[^cite_note-Nube_2003-230] [Gender](38076.md) inequality in nutrition in some countries such as India is present in all stages of life.[^cite_note-Women_2008-231]

Studies on nutrition concerning gender bias within households look at patterns of food allocation, and one study from 2003 suggested that women often receive a lower share of food requirements than men.[^cite_note-Nube_2003-230] Gender discrimination, gender roles, and social norms affecting women can lead to early marriage and childbearing, close birth spacing, and undernutrition, all of which contribute to malnourished mothers.[^cite_note-Factors_in_Bangladesh-58]

Within the household, there may be differences in levels of malnutrition between men and women, and these differences have been shown to vary significantly from one region to another, with problem areas showing relative deprivation of women.[^cite_note-Nube_2003-230] Samples of 1000 women in India in 2008 demonstrated that malnutrition in women is associated with poverty, lack of development and awareness, and illiteracy.[^cite_note-Women_2008-231] The same study showed that gender discrimination in households can prevent a woman's access to sufficient food and healthcare.[^cite_note-Women_2008-231] How socialization affects the health of women in Bangladesh, Najma Rivzi explains in an article about a research program on this topic.[^cite_note-232] In some cases, such as in parts of Kenya in 2006, rates of malnutrition in pregnant women were even higher than rates in children.[^cite_note-233]

Women in some societies are traditionally given less food than men since men are perceived to have heavier workloads.[^cite_note-Gender_and_Nutrition_2001-234] Household chores and agricultural tasks can in fact be very arduous and require additional energy and nutrients; however, physical activity, which largely determines energy requirements, is difficult to estimate.[^cite_note-Nube_2003-230]

#### Physiology

Women have unique nutritional requirements, and in some cases need more nutrients than men; for example, women need twice as much calcium as men.[^cite_note-Gender_and_Nutrition_2001-234]

#### Pregnancy and breastfeeding

During pregnancy and breastfeeding, women must ingest enough nutrients for themselves and their child, so they need significantly more protein and calories during these periods, as well as more vitamins and minerals (especially iron, iodine, calcium, folic acid, and vitamins A, C, and K).[^cite_note-Gender_and_Nutrition_2001-234] In 2001 the FAO of the UN reported that iron deficiency affected 43 percent of women in [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries) and increased the risk of death during childbirth.[^cite_note-Gender_and_Nutrition_2001-234] A 2008 review of interventions estimated that universal supplementation with calcium, iron, and folic acid during pregnancy could prevent 105,000 maternal deaths (23.6 percent of all maternal deaths).[^cite_note-Bhutta_2008-235] Malnutrition has been found to affect three-quarters of UK women aged 16–49 indicated by them having less folic acid than the WHO recommended levels.[^cite_note-236]

Frequent pregnancies with short intervals between them and long periods of breastfeeding add an additional nutritional burden.[^cite_note-Nube_2003-230]

#### Educating children

"[Action for Healthy Kids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_for_Healthy_Kids)" has created several methods to teach children about nutrition. They introduce 2 different topics, self-awareness which teaches children about taking care of their own health and social awareness, which is how culinary arts vary from culture to culture. As well as its importance when it comes to nutrition. They include eBooks, tips, cooking clubs. including facts about vegetables and fruits.[^cite_note-237]

Team Nutrition has created "[MyPlate eBooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MyPlate)" this includes 8 different eBooks to download for free. These eBooks contain drawings to color, audio narration, and a large number of characters to make nutrition lessons entertaining for children.[^cite_note-238]

According to the FAO, women are often responsible for preparing food and have the chance to educate their children about beneficial food and health habits, giving mothers another chance to improve the nutrition of their children.[^cite_note-Gender_and_Nutrition_2001-234]

### Elderly



[Essential nutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_nutrient) are one of the main requirements of [elderly care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elderly_care).

Malnutrition and being underweight are more common in the elderly than in adults of other ages.[^cite_note-quality_of_life_2011-239] If elderly people are healthy and active, the aging process alone does not usually cause malnutrition.[^cite_note-Elder_Insecurities-240] However, changes in body composition, organ functions, adequate energy intake and ability to eat or access food are associated with aging, and may contribute to malnutrition.[^cite_note-geriatric_syndromes-241] Sadness or depression can play a role, causing changes in appetite, digestion, energy level, weight, and well-being.[^cite_note-Elder_Insecurities-240] A study on the relationship between malnutrition and other conditions in the elderly found that malnutrition in the elderly can result from gastrointestinal and endocrine system disorders, loss of taste and smell, decreased appetite and inadequate dietary intake.[^cite_note-geriatric_syndromes-241] Poor dental health, ill-fitting dentures, or chewing and swallowing problems can make eating difficult.[^cite_note-Elder_Insecurities-240] As a result of these factors, malnutrition is seen to develop more easily in the elderly.[^cite_note-Volkert_2002-242]

Rates of malnutrition tend to increase with age with less than 10 percent of the "young" elderly (up to age 75) malnourished, while 30 to 65 percent of the elderly in home care, long-term care facilities, or acute hospitals are malnourished.[^cite_note-243] Many elderly people require assistance in eating, which may contribute to malnutrition.[^cite_note-Volkert_2002-242] However, the mortality rate due to undernourishment may be reduced.[^cite_note-ener.2009-244] Because of this, one of the main requirements of [elderly care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elderly_care) is to provide an adequate diet and all [essential nutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_nutrients).[^cite_note-245] Providing the different nutrients such as protein and energy keeps even small but consistent weight gain.[^cite_note-ener.2009-244] Hospital admissions for malnutrition in the United Kingdom have been related to insufficient social care, where vulnerable people at home or in care homes are not helped to eat.[^cite_note-246]

In Australia malnutrition or risk of malnutrition occurs in 80 percent of elderly people presented to hospitals for admission.[^cite_note-247] Malnutrition and weight loss can contribute to [sarcopenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcopenia) with loss of lean body mass and muscle function.[^cite_note-quality_of_life_2011-239] Abdominal obesity or weight loss coupled with sarcopenia lead to immobility, skeletal disorders, insulin resistance, hypertension, atherosclerosis, and [metabolic disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolic_disorders).[^cite_note-geriatric_syndromes-241] A paper from the *Journal of the American Dietetic Association* noted that routine nutrition screenings represent one way to detect and therefore decrease the prevalence of malnutrition in the elderly.[^cite_note-Elder_Insecurities-240]
