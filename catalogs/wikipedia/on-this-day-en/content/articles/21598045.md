**Bunkering** is the supplying of [fuel](21188370.md) for use by [ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship) ([such fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_diesel_oil) is referred to as **bunker**),[^cite_note-Mana-1] including the logistics of loading and distributing the fuel among available shipboard tanks.[^cite_note-2] A person dealing in trade of bunker (fuel) is called a bunker trader.

The term bunkering originated in the days of [steamships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamship), when [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) was stored in bunkers.[^cite_note-Mana-1] Nowadays, the term bunker is generally applied to the [petroleum](23195.md) products stored in [tanks](4723661.md), and bunkering to the practice and business of refueling ships. Bunkering operations take place at [seaports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) and include the storage and provision of the bunker (ship fuels) to vessels.[^cite_note-3]

The [Port of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Singapore) is currently the largest bunkering port in the world.[^cite_note-4] In 2023, Singapore recorded bunker fuel[^cite_note-5] sales volume totaling 51,824,000 tonnes, setting a new industry standard.

The [Island of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_of_Malta) is known to host bunkering, in six locations including [Hurd's bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurd's_bank). In 2015 the [Malta-Sicily Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta-Sicily_Channel) was noted as "one of the most important oil transit routes in the world," and in 2011, around eight vessels per day bunkered in Maltese waters.[^cite_note-tom2-6]

## Two types of bunkering

The two most common types of bunkering procedure at sea are "ship to ship bunkering" (STSB), in which one ship acts as a terminal, while the other moors. The second type is "stern line bunkering" (SLB), which is the easiest method of transferring oil but can be risky during bad weather.[^cite_note-7]

## Bunkering in maritime law

Main article: [Admiralty law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_law)

In many maritime contracts, such as [charter parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charterparty), [contracts for carriage of goods by sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carriage_of_Goods_by_Sea_Act_1971),[^cite_note-8] and [marine insurance policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_insurance),[^cite_note-9] the [ship-owner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship-owner) or [ship operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_operator) is required to ensure that the ship is [seaworthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaworthiness_\(law\)). Seaworthiness requires not only that the ship be sound and properly crewed, but also that it be fully fuelled (or "bunkered") at the start of the voyage.[^cite_note-10] If the ship operator wishes to bunker en route, this must be provided for in a written agreement, or the interruption of the voyage may be deemed to be [deviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deviation_\(law\)) (a serious [breach of contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breach_of_contract)). If the vessel runs out of fuel in mid-ocean, this also constitutes serious breach, allowing the insurer to cancel a policy[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] and allowing a [consignee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consignee) to make a cargo claim. It may also lead to a [salvage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_salvage) operation.

The [International Maritime Organisation](14986.md) is an agency of the [United Nations](31769.md) responsible for the prevention of [marine pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_pollution) by ships. On 1 January 2020, the agency began enforcing the IMO 2020 regulation of [MARPOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MARPOL_73/78) [Annex VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MARPOL_73/78#Annex_VI) to minimise bunkering's environmental impact.[^cite_note-13]
