**Lomé** ([UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English): [/ˈloʊmeɪ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*LOH-may*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/loʊˈmeɪ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*loh-MAY*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is the [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(political\)) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Togo) of [Togo](30138.md). It has an urban population of 837,437[^cite_note-Census-2] while there were 2,188,376 permanent residents in its [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) as of the 2022 census.[^cite_note-3] Located on the [Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Guinea) at the southwest corner of the country, with its entire western border along the easternmost edge of [Ghana](12067.md)'s [Volta Region](2227703.md), Lomé is the country's administrative and industrial center, which includes an oil refinery. It is also the country's chief port, from where it exports [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean), [copra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copra), and [oil palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaeis_guineensis) kernels.

Its city limits extends to the border with Ghana, located a few hundred meters west of the city center, to the Ghanaian city of [Aflao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aflao) and the South Ketu district where the city is situated, had 160,756 inhabitants in 2010. The cross-border agglomeration of which Lomé is the centre has about 2 million inhabitants as of 2020.

## Etymology

The name 'Lomé' comes from *Alo(ti)mé* or (shorter) *Alomé*, which in the [Ewe language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewe_language), literally, means "in the alo trees", or "within the alo trees", to designate simply the forest of alo.

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Lomé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Lom%C3%A9).



Lomé in 1903.

The city was founded by the [Ewes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewe_people) and expanded in the 19th century by [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_colonial_empire), British and African traders,[^cite_note-4] becoming the capital of [Togoland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togoland) in 1897.[^cite_note-britannica-5] At the end of the 19th century, British [customs duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs) on imported products (especially on alcohol and tobacco) weighed very heavily. The traders, mainly maritime Ewe or [Anlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anlo) from the area between [Aflao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aflao) and [Keta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keta) in the east of the British colony of [Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(region\)), then looking for an alternative place to unload goods while being out of reach of British customs officers, naturally targeted the coastal site of Lomé, nearby. This commercial dynamic of customs circumvention and tax evasion then favored the expansion of Lomé around 1880. The calm and sparsely inhabited Loméen coastline began to be populated rapidly. The Ewes were soon joined by European, British and especially German companies, as well as itinerant merchants from the interior, such as the [Hausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa_people) caravans from the cola roads. Many people were attracted by the new economic hub that Lomé represented. The rapid growth of the city was reinforced, and Lomé quickly earned a reputation as a place where good business was done.

It was the threats of the British present in the neighboring Gold Coast (now [Ghana](12067.md)) that put an end to the competition that Lomé provoked for their colony, which then provoked a call for the protection of [Germany](11867.md). Togoland was thus created as an entity of international law within the German colonial empire on 5 July 1884, by the [Treaty of Togoville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Togoville?action=edit\&redlink=1), signed by [Gustav Nachtigal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Nachtigal) and King [Mlapa III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mlapa_III?action=edit\&redlink=1).

Lomé continued to prosper freely as a centre of import, thus becoming the main gateway to the North, whose major axis of penetration was then the Volta Valley; it was to access it that the construction of the first real road in the country, [Lomé-Kpalimé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kpalim%C3%A9), was undertaken from 1892. It was this major economic role that led the German administration to transfer the capital of the territory to a city that already had more than 2,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-britannica-5]



Stamp of the German colony Togogebiet cancelled in Lomé in 1902.



Alley leading to [the Governor's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Governors,_Togo) (1904).

Lomé benefited from 1904 from a port that made it the only maritime contact point of Togo, ruining its rival, [Aného](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An%C3%A9ho), until then much more important. From this development, a network of railways could be deployed: to Aného in 1905, to Kpalimé in 1907 and to [Atakpamé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atakpam%C3%A9) in 1909. All the "useful Togo" was now organized in a funnel around Lomé, whose preponderance on the Togolese urban network was definitively established and growth assured. The city reached a population of 8,000 in 1914. But, if the infrastructure set up by the Germans - which consisted of a post office in 1890, the [telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone) in 1894, the cathedral in 1904, a bank in 1906 and the intercontinental telegraph in 1913 - could benefit everyone, a system of discriminatory patents and licenses gradually ousted African traders from the most lucrative activities, that is the trade or import-export business.

Apart from the rich [Octaviano Olympio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octaviano_Olympio), with his large Cocoterais, the first in the city, his herds, his [brickyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brickyard) and his construction company, most [Togolese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togolese) merchants had to put themselves at the service of foreign firms as managers of their agencies in other cities, or enjoying more autonomy as buyers of agricultural export products in the interior. The smallest had been hired in large numbers as clerks in the main factories (headquarters of the offices of a trading company abroad). Firms in other African territories looked with envy at Togo, which had an abundant skilled workforce, while elsewhere, it was necessary to entrust all positions to expatriates, much more expensive for the employer.

The [First World War](4764461.md) completely spared the city. However, in 1916, it led to the eviction of German companies, gradually replaced by British and French firms. Many Togolese traders returned to Lomé. Their flourishing businesses, their vast coconut groves, and their large land holdings made them a bourgeoisie with which the new French colonial authorities had to reckon, prompting the creation of the council of notables in 1922 (elective from 1925), which gave Lomé a remarkably early political life in French-speaking Africa. It is also quite exceptional that an African capital has been so marked by its indigenous [bourgeoisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeoisie), both in the production practices of urban space, so original in Lomé, and in the singularities of its popular architecture.

The French renewed the infrastructure left by the Germans, repairing railways, building more roads, constructing a new quay, etc. They added electrification in 1926 and drinking water supply in 1940. However, they took years to fill the void left in the schools by the [German verbist missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_the_Divine_Word) when they left. The level of students enrolled in 1945, at the death of [Jean-Marie Cassou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Cassou?action=edit\&redlink=1), reached that of 1914.

In the 1920s, a policy of systematic low taxation ushered in a long period of prosperity. In January 1923, a [Lomé Women's Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lom%C3%A9_Women's_Revolt?action=edit\&redlink=1) took place against the arrest of two Duawo leaders and forced their release.[^cite_note-6]

Lomé reached 15,000 inhabitants around 1930. But the [global economic crisis of the early 1930s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) led to a brutal recession. Many businesses closed or had to consolidate. Investments stopped, like the Northern Railway, definitively stopped in [Blitta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitta) in 1934. A plan for a sharp tax increase, while the people's resources were falling, provoked the riots of January 1933, which were undoubtedly a major political break in the history of Togo. It was only after [World War II](32927.md), following a decade of postwar stagnation, that boom times resumed in Lomé.

The population of the city increased rapidly in the second half of the 20th century. It had only a population of 30,000 in 1950, which increased to 80,000 in 1960, the year when Togo became independent. A decade later, it stood at 200,000 people, in 1970. Within two decades, the population of Lomé increased by seven-fold. The city, as throughout the country, the very high prices of export products operate the markets, the significant investments of the colonial administration (the "FIDES" plan created a large number of roads, bridges, schools, hospitals) ensured full employment. The constructions were rapidly expanding at the expense of the old coconut groves, the hope animated each of an imminent take-off.

From the mid-1970s, investments became more and more gigantic, but not always in well-targeted areas, Togo, a small open country and hub of trade between its powerful neighbors, did not have the protected market that would have been needed for the large industries that were built for it, nor the stable tourism potential for the luxurious hotels that were coming up.[^cite_note-Roman_Adrian_Cybriwsky_2013,_p._162-7] At the same time, the railways were allowed to deteriorate, even though they had an important role, especially for serving the outlying districts of the city.

But, the economic activity of an African city is not just an accumulation of large companies, banks and factories. There is also the very vast field of the popular economy, these innumerable activities of production, exchange, service, repair, which are in fact the livelihood of the majority of the population, and the only way for it to access services commensurate with its modest resources. Difficult to grasp in the statistical tools of economists, the [informal sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_economy) is increasingly affecting the real economic life of African city dwellers. In addition, the development of market gardening around the city, stimulated by growing unemployment, arose rural exodus and demand for vegetables. Market gardening, first extended to the north, is mainly on the beach (as the sand is too less salty), planting protective hedges. The various studies of the city's land market indicate that the neighborhoods are relatively heterogeneous, mixing opulent villas and modest housing, without social and spatial division of the city. This is explained by the fact that Lomeans are very attached to their plot of land and what they call their "home" (their home). This, therefore, led to a land freeze. However, if the city is not a socially divided city, the fact remains that Lomé is experiencing more and more problems related to the collection of household waste, the fight against urban insalubrity has become one of the priorities of the city and its inhabitants.

## Climate

Owing to its location in the [Dahomey Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahomey_Gap), Lomé has a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Aw*/*As*) despite its latitude close to the equator. The capital of Togo is relatively dry with an annual average rainfall of 800 to 900 millimetres (31 to 35 in) and on average 59 rainy days per year. Despite this, the city experiences heavy fog most of the year from the northern extension of the [Benguela Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguela_Current) and receives a total of 2,330 bright sunshine hours annually. Comparably dry inland West African cities like [Bamako](56606.md) or [Kano](508782.md) expect between 2,900 and 3,000 hours of sunshine annually.

The annual mean temperature is about 26.9 °C or 80.4 °F but heat is constant as monthly mean temperatures range from 24.9 °C or 76.8 °F in July, the least hot month of the year to 29.6 °C or 85.3 °F in February and in April, the hottest months of the year.

### Climate change

A 2019 paper published in [PLOS One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLOS_One) estimated that under [Representative Concentration Pathway 4.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_Concentration_Pathway#4.5), a "moderate" scenario of [climate change](5042951.md) where global warming reaches \~2.5–3 °C (4.5–5.4 °F) by 2100, the climate of Lomé in the year 2050 would most closely resemble the current climate of [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua) in [Nicaragua](21362.md). The annual temperature would increase by 1 °C (1.8 °F), the temperature of the warmest month by 1.6 °C (2.9 °F), and of the coldest month by 2.1 °C (3.8 °F).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] According to [Climate Action Tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Action_Tracker), the current warming trajectory appears consistent with 2.7 °C (4.9 °F), which closely matches RCP 4.5.[^cite_note-13]

Moreover, according to the 2022 [IPCC Sixth Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPCC_Sixth_Assessment_Report), Lomé is one of 12 major African cities ([Abidjan](21371767.md), [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), [Algiers](1644.md), [Cape Town](6653.md), [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca), [Dakar](44251.md), [Dar es Salaam](8500.md), [Durban](313650.md), [Lagos](85232.md), Lomé, [Luanda](18402.md) and [Maputo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maputo)) which would be the most severely affected by the future [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise). It estimates that they would collectively sustain cumulative damages of US\$65 billion under RCP 4.5 and US\$86.5 billion for the high-emission scenario RCP 8.5 by the year 2050. Additionally, RCP 8.5 combined with the hypothetical impact from [marine ice sheet instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_ice_sheet_instability) at high levels of warming would involve up to US\$137.5 billion in damages, while the additional accounting for the "low-probability, high-damage events" may increase aggregate risks to US\$187 billion for the "moderate" RCP4.5, US\$206 billion for RCP8.5 and US\$397 billion under the high-end ice sheet instability scenario.[^cite_note-14] Since sea level rise would continue for about 10,000 years under every scenario of climate change, future costs of sea level rise would only increase, especially without adaptation measures.[^cite_note-15]

## Geography

At its creation, the commune of Lomé was wedged between the [lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagoon) to the north, the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) to the south, the village of Bè to the east and the border of [Aflao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aflao) to the west.

Today, it has experienced a vertiginous extension, and is bounded by the Togolese Insurance Group (GTA) to the north, the Atlantic Ocean to the south, the oil refinery to the east, and the [Togo-Ghana border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togo-Ghana_border) to the west. The agglomeration spreads over an area of 333 km<sup>2</sup>. including 30 km<sup>2</sup>. in the lagoon area.

The services of the municipality of Lomé go far beyond the limits of the gulf and the municipality to the north and east of the city.

## Districts and cities of Lomé

The city of Lomé is subdivided into 5 arrondissements grouping 69 administrative districts [Catégorie\:Quartier de Lomé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cat%C3%A9gorie\:Quartier_de_Lom%C3%A9?action=edit\&redlink=1):

**2nd arrondissement:** From the north to northeast and bordering the maritime region, it includes the 18 districts of:\

## International agreements

### Lomé Convention

The [Lomé Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lom%C3%A9_Convention) is a trade and aid agreement between the [European Union](9317.md) (EU) and 71 [African, Caribbean, and Pacific (ACP) countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African,_Caribbean_and_Pacific_Group_of_States). It was first signed on 28 February 1975, in Lomé.[^cite_note-Roman_Adrian_Cybriwsky_2013,_p._162-7]

### Lomé Peace Accord

The [Lomé Peace Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lom%C3%A9_Peace_Accord) between the warring parties in the civil war in [Sierra Leone](27308.md) was signed in Lomé. With the assistance of the international community, Sierra Leone President [Ahmad Tejan Kabbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Tejan_Kabbah) and [Revolutionary United Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_United_Front) leader [Foday Sankoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foday_Sankoh) signed the Peace Accord on 7 July 1999. However, the agreement did not last and the [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone_Civil_War) continued for two more years.

## Economy



Grand Market of Lomé

Located 200 km from [Accra](56824.md) and 150 km from [Cotonou](56647.md), Lomé has an important port, including a free zone opened in 1968. Phosphates, coffee, cocoa, cotton and palm oil are exported and much of the transit is on behalf of [Ghana](12067.md), [Mali](19127.md), [Niger](21373.md) and [Burkina Faso](3470.md). The port is also home to an oil refinery and, since 1989, a shipyard.[^cite_note-16] The concession of two container terminals to the Bolloré group gave rise to police custody[^cite_note-17] and indictment of Vincent Bolloré in France in April 2018.[^cite_note-18]

The city produces building materials, including cement from the German group, [HeidelbergCement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HeidelbergCement).

Inbound tourism to Togo has seen a steady increase since 2008, from 74,000 arrivals to a peak of 876,000 arrivals in 2019,[^cite_note-19] with Lomé's airport serving as the main point of entry.

## Culture

The [Togo National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togo_National_Museum) is in the Palais de Congrès. The museum contains collections such as jewellery, [musical instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_instrument), [dolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doll), [pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery), weapons and many other materials showing arts and traditions.

### Orange Order

LOL 867, also known as "Defenders of Lome," is a historic [Orange Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Order) that was formed in September 1915 in the city. It holds a unique place in the history of the Orange Order as it was the first lodge to be established in that country.[^cite_note-20] Its founder was a local man named John Amate Atayi.[^cite_note-21] They focus on positive [evangelism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelism), [youth development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_youth_development), and community [philanthropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philanthropy). Members view the victory of [William of Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_III_of_England) not as a political symbol but as one of civil and [religious freedom](46006.md).[^cite_note-22]

The [Grand Orange Lodge of Togo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Orange_Lodge_of_Togo) was established in 1983, and is headquartered at Victoria Hall, also known as Orange House.[^cite_note-23] [Orange walks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_walk) have taken place in the city.[^cite_note-24]

The city center dates from the early twentieth century. There are some remains of colonial architecture, such as the [Palace of the Governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Governors,_Togo) or the [Sacred Heart Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Heart_Cathedral,_Lom%C3%A9), in German neo-Gothic style.

There are also many modern buildings such as the headquarters of the [Central Bank of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_West_African_States) (BCEAO), the [West African Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African_Development_Bank) (BOAD), the Togolese Bank for Trade and Industry (BTCI), the Economic Community of West African States (ECoWAS) or hotel buildings such as the Hotel de la Paix, the Mercure Sarakawa Hotel, the Palm Beach Hotel or the famous [Hotel du 2 Février](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_du_2_F%C3%A9vrier), a modernist building mixing concrete and glass panels, culminating at 102 meters with 36 floors, and is the tallest building in Togo.

Not far away is the [Grand Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lom%C3%A9_Grand_Market), with a three-storey hall. There are red peppers, limes, dried fish, combs, travel bags, traditional medicinal remedies. On the first floor is the kingdom of the famous "[Nana Benz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nana_Benz)", sellers of multiple loincloths made locally, in [Europe](9239.md) or [India](14533.md). To the west of the city is a residential area which, facing the sea, unfolds long arteries, punctuated by official buildings such as the [Palace of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Justice_\(Togo\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and various embassies and consulates.

Further north, next to the Independence Monument, is the headquarters of the [Rally of the Togolese People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rally_of_the_Togolese_People) (RPT) and an important convention center.

More eccentric, compared to the center of the city, we find in [Akodesséwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akodess%C3%A9wa?action=edit\&redlink=1), a much more specialized market than the large market and for good reason, it is the [fetish market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akodessawa_Fetish_Market). Fetishes, gongons, gray-gray are found here. The port of Lomé serves most of the landlocked countries of the Sahel, especially since the political problems experienced by [Côte d'Ivoire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4te_d'Ivoire) and which deprives [Abidjan](21371767.md) of an economic outlet for countries such as [Mali](19127.md) or [Burkina Faso](3470.md). Lomé is therefore taking advantage of the difficult political situation in Côte d'Ivoire.

## Transport

### Road

For urban transport, there are a few taxis; however, [motorcycle taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcycle_taxis?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([zémidjans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%A9midjans)) are more commonly used.

### Rail

The city, which had no rail service since 1997, has seen since 2014, the return of trains, with a new station: those of Blueline Togo, constructed by the French international firm, Bolloré.[^cite_note-27] The inaugural train on 26 April 2014 ran on the short distance Lomé to [Cacavéli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacav%C3%A9li?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-28] The under-construction of the Lomé-Cotonou link, as part of a rail loop project from Lomé, Cotonou, [Niamey](57061.md), [Ouagadougou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouagadougou) and to [Abidjan](21371767.md), is expected to be completed by 2024.[^cite_note-29]

### Air

The city is served by [Lomé-Tokoin International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lom%C3%A9-Tokoin_International_Airport), located 5 km (3.1 mi) northeast from the city centre. [ASKY Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASKY_Airlines), a subsidiary of [Ethiopian Airlines](394924.md), and itself, operates at the airport.

### Water

The city has the Port of Lomé, which is the only commercial port of Togo that carries out trade and export-import activities in the country. It also has a cruise terminal where passengers are also served.

It is one of the few natural deep-water ports on the West African coast and, through its Lomé Container Terminal (LCT) — operated under concession by Terminal Investment Limited (TiL), the terminal-operating arm of [MSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Shipping_Company) — serves as a container transshipment hub for the region and a transit gateway for the landlocked countries of [Mali](19127.md), [Niger](21373.md) and [Burkina Faso](3470.md).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]
