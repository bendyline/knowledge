**Daishō-in** or **Daisyō-in** (大聖院; lit. 'Great Holy Temple'), also called **Suishō-ji** (水精寺; lit. 'Crystal Temple'), is a Japanese Buddhist temple complex with many temples and statues on [Mount Misen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Misen), the holy mountain on the island of [Itsukushima](1067163.md), off the coast of [Hatsukaichi, Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatsukaichi,_Hiroshima), [Japan](15573.md). It is the 14th temple in the [Chūgoku 33 Kannon Pilgrimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%ABgoku_33_Kannon_Pilgrimage) and famous for the maple trees and their [autumn colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_change_in_leaves). Including Mt. Misen, Daishō-in is within the [World Heritage](44940.md) Area of [Itsukushima Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itsukushima_Shrine).

The temple's Reikado Hall (霊火堂) housed a flame which is said to have been burning since its foundation in the 9th century CE.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] The hall collapsed in a fire in 2026.[^cite_note-destroyed-3] The administrators of the temple stated they had "salvaged" the fire and would work to rebuild the hall.[^cite_note-4]

- According to tradition, Daishō-in was founded by the monk [Kūkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%ABkai), also known posthumously as Kōbō-Daishi (弘法大師), in 806 CE, the 1st year of the [Daidō era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daid%C5%8D).

Kūkai was one of the most famous [monks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhikshu) in Japan and the founder of [Shingon Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shingon_Buddhism).

Made head temple of one of denomination of Shingon Buddhism and the oldest temple on Itsukushima.

- By an Imperial order of [Emperor Toba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Toba), it was the place to pray for the peace and security of the nation.

- [Emperor Meiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Meiji) stayed there on 31 July 1885, for his visit to [Itsukushima Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itsukushima_Shrine).

- Repair work for the trail to [Mount Misen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Misen) was finished in 1905 through a donation of [Itō Hirobumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%C5%8D_Hirobumi).

- Heavy damage by 19th typhoon on 27 September 1991.

- Held a memorial service to congratulate the restoration of the damage on 10 October 1998.

- The [14th Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Dalai_Lama) visited Itsukushima to celebrate the 1200th year of Daishō-in from 3 to 8 November 2006.

  [^cite_note-5]

- The Sacred Fire Hall of the temple was completely burned in a fire at around 8\:30 a.m. [Japan Standard Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Standard_Time) on 20 May 2026 (23\:30 p.m. UTC, 19 May).

  [^cite_note-destroyed-3]

There are also many buildings, gates of the temple and statues of [Kannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuan_Yin), [Jūichimen Kannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%ABichimen_Kannon), [Fudō-myōō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acala) (Acala) and [Seven Lucky Gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Lucky_Gods) on and around Mt. Misen.

The temple was the administrator of the Itsukushima shrine before the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration) forbade syncretism ([Shinbutsu-shūgō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinbutsu-sh%C5%ABg%C5%8D)) between [Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto) and Buddhism in 1868 as part of the [Shinbutsu bunri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinbutsu_bunri) policy.[^cite_note-6]
