# Tennis in Spain



Spanish tennis legend [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) playing at [Roland-Garros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Roland_Garros) in 2011.

Tennis is highly popular in Spain, and many professional Spanish tennis players have achieved international success. After a slow start throughout the early 20th century, Spanish tennis began its rise to prominence in the 1960s, when [Manuel Santana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Santana) became the first Spaniard to win a [major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_\(tennis\)) singles title.[^cite_note-1]

[Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal), who was active throughout the 2000s, 2010s and 2020s, is widely considered the greatest Spanish tennis player of all time: he was ranked world No. 1 for 209 weeks, and won 22 major singles titles. Active player [Carlos Alcaraz](63121147.md) has also been ranked world No. 1, and has won 7 major singles titles. Other successful Spanish players include [Arantxa Sánchez Vicario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arantxa_S%C3%A1nchez_Vicario), [Conchita Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conchita_Mart%C3%ADnez), [Carlos Moyá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Moy%C3%A1), [Juan Carlos Ferrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Ferrero), and [Garbiñe Muguruza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbi%C3%B1e_Muguruza).&#x20;

Spanish tennis is particularly characterised by success on [clay courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_court), which many Spaniards train on in their youth.[^cite_note-2] Spanish players have won 28 major singles titles on clay at [Roland-Garros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland-Garros). Spain has won the [Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_Cup) six times ([2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Davis_Cup), [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Davis_Cup), [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Davis_Cup), [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Davis_Cup), [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Davis_Cup) and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Davis_Cup)) and the [Billie Jean King Cup](1108893.md) (formerly Fed Cup) five times ([1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Federation_Cup_\(tennis\)), [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Federation_Cup_\(tennis\)), [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Federation_Cup_\(tennis\)), [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Fed_Cup) and [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Fed_Cup)).&#x20;

## Governance

The roots of Spanish tennis can be traced to the 19th century, when clubs such as the [Real Club de Tenis Barcelona 1899](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Club_de_Tenis_Barcelona) were founded.[^cite_note-3] In September 1909, the Lawn-Tennis Association of Spain ([Spanish](26825.md): *Asociación General de Lawn Tennis, AGLT*) was founded to act as the official governing body of Spanish tennis.[^cite_note-4] It became known as the [Royal Spanish Tennis Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Tennis_Federation) ([Spanish](26825.md): *Real Federación Española de Tenis, RFET*) in 1940.

## Notable players

### 1960s

&#x20;The first Spanish tennis player to reach a major singles final was [Manuel Santana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Santana), who defeated [Nicola Pietrangeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Pietrangeli) in five sets to win the [French Championships](147724.md) in [1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_French_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-10] Santana went on to claim a further three major singles titles, and was ranked as amateur [world No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_number_1_male_tennis_player_rankings) for the first time in 1965 by Ned Potter.[^cite_note-11] [Juan Gisbert Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Gisbert_Sr.) and [Andrés Gimeno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Gimeno) achieved further success for Spanish men's tennis in the 1960s, finishing as runners-up in the [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Australian_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) and [1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) [Australian Open](97851.md) singles finals.



[Manuel Santana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Santana), the first Spanish tennis player to reach a major singles final.

### 1970s

Spanish men continued to find some success in the 1970s, primarily on clay courts. Gimeno claimed the title at the [1972 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-12] At 34 years, 301 days old, he remained the oldest French Open champion in men's singles until compatriot [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) surpassed him in 2022.[^cite_note-13] [Manuel Orantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Orantes) made the final of the [1974 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), where he won the first two sets but lost the next three to hand [Björn Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rn_Borg) his first major title.[^cite_note-14] Orantes rebounded to win the [1975 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), which was played on clay, in straight sets.[^cite_note-15]

### 1980s

The 1980s were comparatively less successful for Spain, with no Spanish men in singles slam finals throughout the decade. However, at the [1989 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), [Arantxa Sánchez Vicario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arantxa_S%C3%A1nchez_Vicario) defeated [Steffi Graf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steffi_Graf), champion at the previous five majors, to become the first Spanish woman to win a major singles title.[^cite_note-16]

### 1990s

The 1990s oversaw an explosion of Spanish talent on both the men's and women's tours. Spanish women claimed three of tour singles majors in 1994: Sánchez Vicario won both the French Open and the US Open,[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] while [Conchita Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conchita_Mart%C3%ADnez) became the first Spanish woman to win Wimbledon.[^cite_note-19] In 1995, Sánchez Vicario became the first Spanish woman to secure the world No. 1 ranking.[^cite_note-20] After a heartbreaking streak of five slam final losses in 1995 and 1996, she picked up her fourth and final major singles title at the [1998 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-21] Spanish women also won the [Fed Cup](1108893.md) five times, in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Federation_Cup_\(tennis\)), [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Federation_Cup_\(tennis\)), [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Federation_Cup_\(tennis\)), [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Fed_Cup) and [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Fed_Cup).[^cite_note-22]

Meanwhile, Spanish men's 18-year slam drought ended with [Sergi Bruguera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergi_Bruguera)'s victory at the [1993 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), a title he defended in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-23] [Carlos Moyá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Moy%C3%A1) claimed the title at the [1998 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), where he defeated countryman [Àlex Corretja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%80lex_Corretja) in the final.[^cite_note-24] Moyá would win 20 ATP Tour titles across his career, and was ranked as the [ATP world No. 1](13480983.md) in 1999.[^cite_note-25]

### 2000s

Spanish men dominated the ATP Tour in the 2000s. Spaniard [Albert Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Costa) won the [2002 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) singles title, upsetting countryman [Juan Carlos Ferrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_Ferrero) in the final.[^cite_note-26] Ferrero rebounded from this loss in 2003, as winner of the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), runner-up at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), and world No. 1 for a total of eight weeks.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] Spanish men also claimed four [Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_Cup) titles during this decade, in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Davis_Cup), [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Davis_Cup), [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Davis_Cup), and [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Davis_Cup).[^cite_note-29]

The most significant development in Spanish tennis in the 2000s was the arrival of [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal), who won six of his 22 major singles titles in that decade: one Australian Open ([2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles)), one Wimbledon ([2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles)), and four French Opens ([2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), and [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles)). He clinched the world No. 1 ranking for the first time on 18 August 2008, one month after his [defeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles_final) of [Roger Federer](262376.md) at Wimbledon in what is now widely considered one of the greatest matches of all time.[^cite_note-30]

Spanish women's tennis saw slightly less success in this decade, after Martínez reached her third and last slam final at the [2000 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-31]

### 2010s



[Carlos Alcaraz](63121147.md) poses with his first French Open trophy.

Nadal continued to lead Spanish tennis throughout the 2010s, claiming a further thirteen major singles titles: one Wimbledon ([2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles)), four US Opens ([2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles)), and a stunning eight French Opens ([2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles)). Spanish men picked up a further two Davis Cups in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Davis_Cup) and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Davis_Cup), led by Nadal and aided by players such as 2013 French Open runner-up [David Ferrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ferrer).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

[Garbiñe Muguruza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbi%C3%B1e_Muguruza) was the most successful Spanish woman of the decade, claiming major singles titles at the French Open in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) and Wimbledon in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), finishing runner-up at Wimbledon in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), and reaching the world No. 1 ranking in 2017.[^cite_note-34]

### 2020s

Nadal secured a final three major titles, at the [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) and [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) French Opens, and the [2022 Australian Open](69399815.md), dubbed "the miracle in Melbourne".[^cite_note-35] Plagued by injuries, he retired aged thirty-eight at the Davis Cup in 2024. Spanish men's tennis underwent a seamless transition to its next champion: [Carlos Alcaraz](63121147.md), who in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) won his first major title at the US Open and became the first teenage ATP world No. 1.[^cite_note-36] Alcaraz won further major singles titles at Wimbledon in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), the French Open in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), the US Open in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), and the Australian Open in [2026](81715568.md). With his first Australian Open title he became the youngest man in history to complete the [Career Grand Slam](197638.md#Career_Grand_Slam).[^cite_note-37]

In [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Davis_Cup), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_Davis_Cup_team) returned to the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Davis_Cup_Finals) of the Davis Cup for the first time in six years despite the non-participation of their two top-ranked singles players, Alcaraz and [Alejandro Davidovich Fokina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Davidovich_Fokina). They lost the final to defending champions [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_Davis_Cup_team).[^cite_note-38]

Muguruza reached her fourth and last major final at the Australian Open in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-39] [Paula Badosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Badosa) reached a career-high ranking of world No. 2 in 2022.
