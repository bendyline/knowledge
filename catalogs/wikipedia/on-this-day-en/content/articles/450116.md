This article lists the [heads of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) of [Sudan](27421.md) since the establishment of the [First Sovereignty Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_Sovereignty_Council_\(1955%E2%80%931958\)) in 1955, prior to the country's [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Sudan#Independent_Sudan_\(1956_to_present\)).

## History of the office

Since independence was proclaimed on 1 January 1956, six individuals (and three multi-member [sovereignty councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regency_\(government\))) have served as [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) of [Sudan](27421.md), currently under the title **President of the Republic of Sudan**. Prior to independence, Sudan was governed as a [condominium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condominium_\(international_law\)) by [Egypt](8087628.md) and the [United Kingdom](31717.md), under the name [Anglo-Egyptian Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_Sudan). As such, executive power was vested in a [dyarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyarchy) consisting of both countries' heads of state – at the time of independence, the [Queen of the United Kingdom](47063.md) ([Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II)) and the [Egyptian Revolutionary Command Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Revolutionary_Command_Council) (headed by [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser)). Immediately following independence, the role of head of state was filled by a five-member Sovereignty Council, with rival [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) factions unable to agree on a single candidate. In November 1958, General [Ibrahim Abboud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Abboud) led a [military coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Sudanese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), assuming the role of head of state as Chairman of the Supreme Council. Assuming the title of president in 1964, he resigned later that year due to general discontent around the rule of the military regime. Abboud was succeeded by a senior civil servant, [Sirr Al-Khatim Al-Khalifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirr_Al-Khatim_Al-Khalifa), who served as acting president for 18 days before transferring executive authority to a Committee of Sovereignty.

[Ismail al-Azhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_al-Azhari), the leader of the [National Unionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Unionist_Party_\(Sudan\)), was made president in July 1965; he ruled with limited power until he was deposed in a [1969 military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Sudanese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat). The military officers responsible for the coup established the [National Revolutionary Command Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Revolutionary_Command_Council_\(Sudan\)), chaired by [Jaafar Nimeiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaafar_Nimeiry). Nimeiry, the leader of the newly formed [Sudanese Socialist Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_Socialist_Union), assumed the position of president in 1971, and subsequently established a [one-party state](102459.md), which existed until 1985, when a group of military officers [overthrew his government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Sudanese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) and established the [1985 Transitional Military Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Military_Council_\(1985\)), led by Field Marshal [Abdel Rahman Swar al-Dahab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdel_Rahman_Swar_al-Dahab). [Ahmed al-Mirghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_al-Mirghani) succeeded to the relatively powerless position of Chairman of the Supreme Council in 1986, after [multi-party election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Sudanese_parliamentary_election) held that year. He was deposed in a [1989 military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Sudanese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) led by Lieutenant-General [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md). Al-Bashir served as head of state, under the title of Chairman of the [Revolutionary Command Council for National Salvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Command_Council_for_National_Salvation) from 1989 to 1993 and as president from 1993 to 2019 (and from 1996 as the leader of the [National Congress Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_Party_\(Sudan\))).

Al-Bashir was [removed from power](60469131.md) by the [Sudanese Armed Forces](27429.md) on 11 April 2019, amid the [Sudanese revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_revolution) after holding the office for nearly 30 years. Lieutenant-General [Ahmed Awad Ibn Auf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Awad_Ibn_Auf) took control of Sudan without becoming head of state, established the [2019 Transitional Military Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Military_Council_\(2019\)), but resigned the following day in favor of Lieutenant-General [Abdel Fattah al-Burhan](60480545.md).[^cite_note-\:0-3] The Transitional Military Council was replaced with the [Transitional Sovereignty Council](61549039.md) on 21 August 2019, under the chairmanship of al-Burhan.[^cite_note-4] The Sovereignty Council, an 11-member civilian-military [collective head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state#Multiple_or_collective_heads_of_state), is designed to lead the country for 39 months in the [transition to democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_transition_to_democracy_\(2019%E2%80%932021\)), which is supposed to end with the [next general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Sudanese_general_election).[^cite_note-5] The Transitional Sovereignty Council was dissolved by al-Burhan on 25 October 2021, following a [coup d'état](69110231.md).[^cite_note-6] Al-Burhan reinstated it on 11 November 2021, with some members replaced.[^cite_note-al_Jazeera_11/2021-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## Term limits

As of 2021, there is a two-term limit for the president in the [Constitution of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Sudan). The term limit has not been met by any president yet.[^cite_note-termlimits-10]

- **1971**: Chairman of the National Revolutionary Command Council

- **1989–1993**: Chairman of the [Revolutionary Command Council for National Salvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Command_Council_for_National_Salvation)

- **1993–2019**: President

- **2019**: President/Chairman of the [Transitional Military Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Military_Council_\(2019\))
