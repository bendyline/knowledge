The **Chinese people**, or simply **Chinese**, are people or [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity) identified with [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_China). Understandings of who is considered Chinese have shifted across time through [social construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_constructionism).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

People of Chinese ethnicity or ancestry who live outside [Greater China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_China) may consider themselves [overseas Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] In some areas throughout the world, [ethnic enclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_enclave) known as [Chinatowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown) are home to populations of overseas Chinese.

## Terminology

The term *Zhōngguó zhī rén* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 中國之人; lit. 'people of China'; [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_language): ᡩᡠᠯᡳᠮᠪᠠᡳ\
ᡤᡠᡵᡠᠨ ‍ᡳ\
ᠨᡳᠶᠠᠯᠮᠠ, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Manchu): *Dulimbai gurun-i niyalma*) was used by the Qing government to refer to all traditionally native subjects of the empire, including Han, Manchu, and Mongols.[^cite_note-6] In modern [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language), there is a distinction between *Huaren* and *Zhongguoren*. The former term refers to ethnic Chinese, and is often used for those who reside overseas or those who are non-citizens. The latter term refers to people who have lived in China or are Chinese citizens.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] The term *Zhongguoren* has a more political or ideological aspect in its use, and while many in China may use *Zhongguoren* to mean the Chinese ethnicity, some in Taiwan would refuse to be called *Zhongguoren*.[^cite_note-chang-10]

People from Taiwan, which is officially called the Republic of China (ROC), may also be referred to as "Chinese" in various contexts, though they are usually referred to as "[Taiwanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_people)". The territory of Taiwan is [disputed](66555.md) and the ROC has [limited recognition of its sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Taiwan).

## Ethnicities

Further information: [Ethnic minorities in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_China), [List of ethnic groups in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ethnic_groups_in_China), and [Unrecognized ethnic groups in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unrecognized_ethnic_groups_in_China)

A number of ethnic groups as well as other racial minorities of China are referred to as Chinese people.[^cite_note-MWD-11] *[Zhonghua minzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhonghua_minzu)* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 中华民族), the "Chinese nation", is a [supra-ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supraethnicity) concept which includes all 56 ethnic groups living in China that are officially recognized by the government of the People's Republic of China. It includes established ethnic groups who have lived within the borders of premodern China.[^cite_note-12] The term *zhonghua minzu* was used during the Republic of China from 1911 to 1949 to refer to [five primary ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Races_Under_One_Union)[^cite_note-13] in China.[^cite_note-14] The term *zhongguo renmin* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 中国人民), "Chinese people", was the government's preferred term during the early communist era; *zhonghua minzu* is more common in recent decades.[^cite_note-15]

### Han Chinese

This section is an excerpt from [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Han_Chinese\&action=edit)]

The [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), alternatively Han people[^cite_note-Han_people-16] or Chinese people,[^cite_note-Han_Chinese_\:6-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] are an [East Asian ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_people) native to [Greater China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_China). With a global population of over 1.4 billion, the Han Chinese are the [world's largest ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_contemporary_ethnic_groups), making up about 17% of the [world population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_population). The Han Chinese represent 91.11% of the population in [China](5405.md) and 97% of the population in [Taiwan](25734.md).[^cite_note-Han_Chinese_中華民國國情簡介2016-20][^cite_note-Han_Chinese_Yearbook2014-21] They are a significant [diasporic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese) in Southeast Asian countries such as [Singapore](27318.md),[^cite_note-22] [Thailand](30128.md), [Malaysia](3607937.md), [Myanmar](19457.md), [Vietnam](202354.md), the [Philippines](23440.md) and [Indonesia](14579.md).

Han Chinese have exerted the primary formative influence in [Chinese culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_culture) and [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-Han_Chinese_hack-24][^cite_note-Han_Chinese_MA-25] Han Chinese trace their ancestry and culture to the [Huaxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaxia) people, a confederation of agricultural tribes that lived along the middle and lower reaches of the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River)[^cite_note-Han_Chinese_\:4-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-Han_Chinese_CH-28][^cite_note-29] in the north central plains of China.[^cite_note-Han_Chinese_CA-30][^cite_note-31]

Han Chinese people and culture later spread southwards in the Chinese mainland, driven by large and sustained waves of migration during successive periods of Chinese history, for example the [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty) (221–206 BC) and [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) (202 BC – 220 AD) dynasties, leading to a demographic and economic tilt towards the south, and the absorption of various [non-Han ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Chinese_history#List_of_ethnic_groups) over the centuries at various points in Chinese history.[^cite_note-Han_Chinese_CH-28][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] The Han Chinese became the main inhabitants of the fertile lowland areas and cities of Southern China by the time of the [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) and [Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) dynasties,[^cite_note-Han_Chinese_PK-34] with minority tribes occupying the highlands.

### Ethnic minorities

The [Chinese government](23239.md) recognizes 56 ethnic groups in China. Other ethnic groups in China include the [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people), [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people), [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people), [Uyghurs](31783.md), and [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people), who make up the five largest [ethnic minorities in mainland China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_China), with populations of approximately 10 million or more. In addition, the [Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people), [Tujia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tujia_people), [Tibetans](249458.md) and [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols_in_China) each have populations between five and ten million. Ethnic minorities may consider themselves *Zhongguoren.*

## Diaspora

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Overseas Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Overseas_Chinese\&action=edit)]

An [Overseas Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese) person is a person of Chinese ancestry or is a person of full or partial Chinese origin whose ancestry is traced back to [Greater China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_China) but are living and working outside of Greater China ([mainland China](55079.md), [Hong Kong](13404.md), [Macau](19068.md), and [Taiwan](25734.md)).[^cite_note-35] This term generally applies to both people of Chinese ancestry and citizens abroad.

As of 2026, approximately 40 million ethnic Chinese live abroad.[^cite_note-Overseas_Chinese_\:023-36]<sup>: 61 </sup> As of 2023, there were 10.5 million people living outside mainland China who were born in mainland China, corresponding to 0.7 percent of China's population.[^cite_note-Overseas_Chinese_\:1-37] Overall, China has a low percent of its population [living overseas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_by_immigrant_and_emigrant_population).
