# Secession

For other uses, see [Secession (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secession_\(disambiguation\)).

**Secession**[^cite_note-1] is the unilateral withdrawal of an integral part of a state's territory. Secession generally aims to establish a separate [political entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_entity), typically a [sovereign state](23604120.md), although it may instead seek accession to another existing state.[^cite_note-2]

Secession often begins with an act such as a [declaration of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_independence), and may be pursued peacefully or through armed conflict.[^cite_note-\:5-3][^cite_note-\:1-4] Scholars distinguish secession from related concepts such as [separatism](286527.md).[^cite_note-\:6-5] Theories of secession differ over its causes, moral justification, and legal status, including whether groups possess a general right of secession or only a remedial right in cases of serious injustice. International law generally neither recognizes a broad right of unilateral secession nor absolutely prohibits it, while the constitutional law of individual states varies considerably.

Secession has occurred throughout history through [decolonization](302084.md), the breakup of federations and multinational states, and regional [independence movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_movement). Historical and contemporary secessionist movements have sought [independence](62450.md) or greater [autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division) in many parts of the world. Threats of secession may be used to obtain greater autonomy or other political concessions short of independence.[^cite_note-Buchanan2-6]

## Secession theory

According to the 2017 book *Secession and Security,* by political scientist [Ahsan Butt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahsan_I._Butt), states respond violently to secessionist movements if the potential state poses a greater threat than the would-be secessionist movement.[^cite_note-\:0-7] States perceive a future war with a potential new state as likely if the ethnic group driving the secessionist struggle has deep identity division with the central state, and if the regional neighborhood is violent and unstable.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

### Explanations for the 20th century increase in secessionism

According to political scientist Bridget L. Coggins, the academic literature contains four potential explanations for the drastic increase in secessions during the 20th century:[^cite_note-\:7-8]

- Ethnonational mobilization, where ethnic minorities have been increasingly mobilized to pursue states of their own.

- Institutional empowerment, where the growing inability of empires and ethnic federations to maintain colonies and member states increases the likelihood of success.

- Relative strength, where increasingly powerful secessionist movements are more likely to achieve statehood.

- Negotiated consent, where home states and the international community increasingly consent to secessionist demands.

Other scholars have linked secession to resource discoveries and extraction.[^cite_note-9] David B. Carter, H. E. Goemans, and Ryan Griffiths find that border changes among states tend to conform to the borders of previous administrative units.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

Several scholars argue that changes in the international system have made it easier for small states to survive and prosper.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-\:2-14][^cite_note-\:3-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] [Tanisha Fazal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanisha_Fazal) and Ryan Griffiths link increased numbers of secessions to an international system that is more favorable for new states. For example, new states can obtain assistance from international organizations such as the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), [World Bank](36752.md), and the [United Nations](31769.md).[^cite_note-\:2-14] [Alberto Alesina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Alesina) and Enrico Spolaore argue that greater levels of free trade and peace have reduced the benefits of being part of a larger state, thus motivating nations within larger states to seek secession.[^cite_note-\:3-15]

[Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson)'s [proclamations on self-determination in 1918](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteen_Points) created a surge in secessionist demands.[^cite_note-\:2-14]

## Philosophy of secession

The [political philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_philosophy) of the rights and moral justification for secession began to develop as recently as the 1980s.[^cite_note-18] American philosopher [Allen Buchanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Buchanan) offered the first systematic account of the subject in the 1990s and contributed to the [normative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normative) classification of the literature on secession. In his 1991 book *Secession: The Morality of Political Divorce From Fort Sumter to Lithuania and Quebec*, Buchanan outlined limited rights to secession under certain circumstances, mostly related to oppression by people of other ethnic or racial groups, and especially those previously conquered by other people.[^cite_note-BuchananBook-19] In his collection of essays from secession scholars, *Secession, State, and Liberty*,[^cite_note-20] professor [David Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gordon_\(philosopher\)) challenges Buchanan, making a case that the moral status of the seceding state is unrelated to the issue of secession itself.[^cite_note-21]

### Justifications for secession

Some theories of secession emphasize a general right of secession for any reason ("Choice Theory") while others emphasize that secession should be considered only to rectify grave injustices ("Just Cause Theory").[^cite_note-22] Some theories do both. A list of justifications may be presented supporting the right to secede, as described by Allen Buchanan, [Robert McGee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_McGee), [Anthony Birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Birch),[^cite_note-23] [Jane Jacobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Jacobs),[^cite_note-24] [Frances Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Kendall?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Leon Louw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Louw),[^cite_note-25] [Leopold Kohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Kohr),[^cite_note-26] [Kirkpatrick Sale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkpatrick_Sale),[^cite_note-27] [Donald W. Livingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_W._Livingston)[^cite_note-28] and various authors in David Gordon's "Secession, State and Liberty", includes:

- United States [President](24113.md) [James Buchanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Buchanan), Fourth [Annual Message to Congress on the State of the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Union) December 3, 1860: "The fact is that our Union rests upon public opinion, and can never be cemented by the blood of its citizens shed in civil war. If it cannot live in the affections of the people, it must one day perish. Congress possesses many means of preserving it by conciliation, but the sword was not placed in their hand to preserve it by force."

- Former President [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson), in a letter to [William H. Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_H._Crawford), [Secretary of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Secretary_of_War) under President [James Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Madison), on June 20, 1816: "In your letter to Fisk, you have fairly stated the alternatives between which we are to choose: 1, licentious commerce and gambling speculations for a few, with eternal war for the many; or, 2, restricted commerce, peace, and steady occupations for all. If any State in the Union will declare that it prefers separation with the first alternative, to a continuance in union without it, I have no hesitation in saying, 'let us separate.' I would rather the States should withdraw, which are for unlimited commerce and war, and confederate with those alone which are for peace and agriculture."

  [^cite_note-29]

- Economic enfranchisement of an economically oppressed class that is regionally concentrated within the scope of a larger national territory.

- The right to [liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty), [freedom of association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_association) and [private property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_property)

- Recognition of the will of the majority to secede, in keeping with consent as an important democratic principle

- Increased ease for states to join with others in an experimental union

- Dissolution of such a union when goals for which it was constituted are not achieved

- [Self-defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-defense) when larger group presents lethal threat to minority or the government cannot adequately defend an area

- [Self-determination](29269.md) of peoples

- Preservation of culture, language, etc. from assimilation or destruction by a larger or more powerful group

- Furtherance of diversity by allowing diverse cultures to keep their [identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_politics)

- Rectification of past injustices, especially past conquest by a larger power

- Escape from "discriminatory redistribution", i.e. tax schemes, regulatory policies, economic programs, and similar policies that distribute resources away to another area, especially in an undemocratic fashion

- Enhanced efficiency when the state or empire becomes too large to administer efficiently

- Preservation of "liberal purity" (or "conservative purity") by allowing less (or more) liberal regions to secede

- Provision of superior constitutional systems which allow flexibility of secession

- Minimizing the size of political entities and the [human scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_scale) through right to secession

Political scientist Aleksander Pavkovic describes five justifications for a general right of secession within liberal political theory:[^cite_note-Pavkovic-Questions-30]

- [Anarcho-Capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarcho-capitalism): individual liberty to form political associations and private property rights together justify right to secede and to create a "viable political order" with like-minded individuals.

- Democratic Secessionism: the right of secession, as a variant of the right of self-determination, is vested in a "territorial community" which wishes to secede from "their existing political community"; the group wishing to secede then proceeds to delimit "its" territory by the majority.

- Communitarian Secessionism: any group with a particular "participation-enhancing" identity, concentrated in a particular territory, which desires to improve its members' political participation has a *[prima facie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prima_facie)* right to secede.

- Cultural Secessionism: any group which was previously in a minority has a right to protect and develop its own culture and distinct national identity through seceding into an independent state.

- The Secessionism of Threatened Cultures: if a minority culture is threatened within a state that has a majority culture, the minority needs a right to form a state of its own which would protect its culture.

### Arguments against secession

[Allen Buchanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Buchanan), who supports secession under limited circumstances, lists arguments that might be used against secession:[^cite_note-31]

- "Protecting legitimate expectations" of those who now occupy territory claimed by secessionists, even in cases where that land was stolen

- "[Self defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self_defense)" if losing part of the state would make it difficult to defend the rest of it

- "Protecting [majority rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_rule)" and the principle that minorities must abide by them

- "Minimization of strategic bargaining" by making it difficult to secede, such as by imposing an [exit tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exit_tax)

- "[Soft paternalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_paternalism)" because secession will be bad for secessionists or others

- "Threat of [anarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchy)" because smaller and smaller entities may choose to secede until there is chaos, although this is not the true meaning of the political and philosophical concept

- "Preventing wrongful taking" such as the state's previous investment in infrastructure

- "[Distributive justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributive_justice)" arguments posit that wealthier areas cannot secede from poorer ones

## Types of secession



[Hashim Thaçi](3626980.md) (left) and then-[US Vice President](32759.md) [Joe Biden](145422.md) with the [Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Kosovo_declaration_of_independence) of [Kosovo](17391.md)

Secession theorists have described a number of ways in which a political entity ([city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City), [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County), [canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_\(administrative_division\)), [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)), etc.) can secede from the larger or original state:[^cite_note-Buchanan2-6][^cite_note-Pavkovic-Questions-30][^cite_note-32]

- Secession from [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation) or [confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation) (political entities with substantial reserved powers which have agreed to join) versus secession from a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state) (a state governed as a single unit with few powers reserved to sub-units)

- [Colonial wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_national_liberation) from an [imperial state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire) although this is decolonisation rather than secession.

- Recursive secession, such as [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) decolonising from the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), then [Pakistan](23235.md) seceding from [India](14533.md), or [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) seceding from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), then [South Ossetia](318381.md) seceding from [Georgia](48768.md).

- National secession (seceding entirely from the national state) versus local secession (seceding from one entity of the national state into another entity of the same state)

- Central or [enclave](4848945.md) secession (seceding entity is completely surrounded by the original state) versus peripheral secession (along a border of the original state)

- Secession by contiguous units versus secession by non-contiguous units ([exclaves](4848945.md))

- [Separation](286527.md) or [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_\(politics\)) (although an entity secedes, the rest of the state retains its structure) versus dissolution (all political entities dissolve their ties and create several new states)

- [Irredentism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irredentism) where secession is sought in order to annex the territory to another state because of common ethnicity or prior historical links

- Minority secession (a minority of the population or territory secedes) versus majority secession (a majority of the population or territory secedes)

- Secession of better-off regions versus secession of worse-off regions

- The threat of secession is sometimes used as a strategy to gain greater autonomy within the original state

## &#x20;Rights to secession

See also: [Self-determination](29269.md)

Most [sovereign states](23604120.md) do not recognize the right to self-determination through secession in their constitutions. Many expressly forbid it. However, there are several existing models of self-determination through greater autonomy and through secession.[^cite_note-Kreptul-33]

In liberal constitutional democracies the principle of [majority rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_rule) has dictated whether a minority can secede. In the United States [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) acknowledged that secession might be possible through [amending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_amendments_to_the_United_States_Constitution) the [United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution). The [Supreme Court](31737.md) in *[Texas v. White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_v._White)* held secession could occur "through revolution, or through consent of the States".[^cite_note-books.google.com-34][^cite_note-Texas_v._White-35] The [British Parliament](13964.md) in 1933 held that [Western Australia could secede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secessionism_in_Western_Australia) from the [Commonwealth of Australia](4689264.md) only upon vote of a majority of the country as a whole; the previous two-thirds majority vote for secession via [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933_Western_Australian_secession_referendum) in [Western Australia](33613.md) was insufficient.[^cite_note-Pavkovic-36]

The [Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_China) followed the Soviet Union in including the right of secession in its 1931 constitution in order to entice [ethnic nationalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_China) and [Tibet](31516.md) into joining. However, the Party eliminated the right to secession in later years, and had anti-secession clause written into the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_China) before and after the founding the [People's Republic of China](5405.md). The 1947 Constitution of the [Union of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) contained an express state right to secede from the union under a number of procedural conditions. It was eliminated in the 1974 constitution of the [Socialist Republic of the Union of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_the_Union_of_Burma) (officially the "[Union of Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Myanmar)"). Burma still allows "local autonomy under central leadership".[^cite_note-Kreptul-33]

As of 1996, the [constitutions of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Austria), [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Constitution_of_Ethiopia), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_France), and [Saint Kitts and Nevis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Saint_Kitts_and_Nevis) have express or implied rights to secession. Switzerland allows for the secession from current and the creation of new [cantons](180752.md). In the case of proposed [Quebec separation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_sovereignty_movement) from [Canada](5042916.md), the [Supreme Court of Canada](123468.md) in 1998 ruled that only both a clear majority of the province and a constitutional amendment confirmed by all participants in the Canadian federation could allow secession.[^cite_note-Kreptul-33]

The [European Union](9317.md) is not a sovereign state but an association of sovereign states formed by treaty; as such, leaving it, which is possible by simply denouncing the treaty, is not secession. Nonetheless, the 2003 draft of the [European Union Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_establishing_a_Constitution_for_Europe) allowed for the [voluntary withdrawal of member states from the union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_from_the_European_Union), although the representatives of the [member-state](10890716.md) which wanted to leave could not participate in the withdrawal discussions of the European Council or of the Council of Ministers.[^cite_note-Kreptul-33] There was much discussion about such self-determination by minorities[^cite_note-37] before the final document underwent the unsuccessful ratification process in 2005. In 2007, [Article 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_50_of_the_Treaty_on_European_Union) of the [Treaty on European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_European_Union) established a mechanism for withdrawal from the EU.

As a result of the successful [constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Liechtenstein_constitutional_referendum) held in 2003, every municipality in the [Principality of Liechtenstein](17810.md) has the right to secede from the Principality by a vote of a majority of the citizens residing in that municipality.[^cite_note-38]

[Indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) have a range of different forms of [indigenous sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_sovereignty) and have the right of [self-determination](29269.md), but under current understanding of international law they have a mere "remedial" right to secession in extreme cases of abuse of their rights, because [independence](62450.md) and [sovereign statehood](23604120.md) is a territorial and diplomatic claim and not one of self-determination and [self-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-government), respectively, generally leaving rights to secession to the internal legislation of sovereign states.

## Secession movements

Main articles: [Lists of active separatist movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lists_of_active_separatist_movements) and [List of historical separatist movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_historical_separatist_movements)

See also the categories [Separatism by country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Separatism_by_country) and [Secessionist organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Secessionist_organizations)

National secessionist movements advocate that a population has the right to form its own nation-state.[^cite_note-39] Movements that work towards political secession may describe themselves as being [autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_entity), [separatist](286527.md), [independence](62450.md), [self-determination](29269.md), [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_\(politics\)), [devolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution), [decentralization](49139.md), [sovereignty](28791.md), [self-governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance) or [decolonization](302084.md) movements instead of, or in addition to, being secession movements.

Notable examples of secession, and secession attempts, include:

- The [United Provinces of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) breaking away from the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) during the [Eighty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighty_Years'_War) (1566–1648):

- The [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) (the later [United States](3434750.md)) revolting from the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) during the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) (1775–83);

- [Hispanic America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_America) gaining independence from the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) during [Spanish American wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence);

- [Texas](29810.md) leaving Mexico, during the [Texas Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Revolution) (1835–36);

- the [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) seceding from the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)), setting off the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War);

- [Panama](22997.md) [seceding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secession_of_Panama_from_Colombia) from [Colombia](5222.md) in 1903, during [United States](3434750.md) acquisition of the [Panama Canal](24849.md);

- the [Irish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Republic) leaving the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland);

- [Finland voting to leave Soviet Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Finland) in 1917, setting off the [Finnish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Civil_War);

- [Biafra](44431.md) leaving Nigeria (and returning, after losing the [Nigerian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Civil_War));

- the former [Soviet republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_the_Soviet_Union) leaving the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in 1991, causing its [dissolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union);

- the former [republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Yugoslavia) leaving [Yugoslavia](34244.md) during the 1990s, causing its [dissolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_of_Yugoslavia).

### Australia

Main article: [Proposals for new Australian States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposals_for_new_Australian_States)

During the 19th century, the single [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) [colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony) in eastern mainland Australia, [New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_New_South_Wales) (NSW) was progressively divided up by the British government as new settlements were formed and spread. [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Victoria) (Vic) was formed in 1851 and [Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Queensland) (Qld) in 1859.

However, settlers agitated to divide the colonies throughout the later part of the century; particularly in [central Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Queensland) (centered in [Rockhampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockhampton)) in the 1860s and 1890s, and in [North Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Queensland) (with [Bowen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowen,_Queensland) as a potential colonial capital) in the 1870s. Other secession (or territorial separation) movements arose and these advocated the secession of [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_\(New_South_Wales\)) in northern central [New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_New_South_Wales), [Deniliquin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deniliquin) in the [Riverina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverina) district also in NSW, and [Mount Gambier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Gambier) in the eastern part of [South Australia](26716.md).

Western Australia

Main article: [Secessionism in Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secessionism_in_Western_Australia)

Secession movements have surfaced several times in Western Australia (WA), where a [1933 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933_Western_Australian_secession_referendum) for secession from the [Federation of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Australia) passed with a two-thirds majority. The referendum had to be ratified by the [British Parliament](13964.md), which declined to act, on the grounds that it would contravene the [Australian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Constitution).

The [Principality of Hutt River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Hutt_River) claimed to have seceded from Australia in 1970, although its status was not recognised by Australia or any other country.

### Azerbaijan

Main article: [Karabakh movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh_movement)

The Karabakh movement, also known as the Artsakh movement[^cite_note-\:5-3][^cite_note-\:6-5] was a national liberation movement[^cite_note-Vicken_Cheterian-40][^cite_note-\:02-41][^cite_note-\:12-42] in [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) and [Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md) between 1988 and 1991 that advocated for the reunification ("[miatsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miatsum)") of the [Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorno-Karabakh_Autonomous_Oblast) – formally an autonomous enclave in [Soviet Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) – with [Soviet Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). The movement was motivated by fears of cultural and physical erasure under [government policies from Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Armenian_sentiment_in_Azerbaijan#Suppression_of_Armenian_culture_in_Nagorno-Karabakh).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalkasian199627–28-43][^cite_note-\:14-44][^cite_note-Eduard_Abrahamyan2-45] Throughout the Soviet period, [Azerbaijani authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) implemented policies aimed at suppressing Armenian culture and diluting the Armenian majority in Nagorno-Karabakh through various means, including border manipulations,[^cite_note-\:02-41][^cite_note-Levon_Chorbajian,_Patrick_Donabédian,_Claude_Mutafian-46][^cite_note-Christopher_J._Walker-47] encouraging the exodus of Armenians, and settling Azerbaijanis in the region.[^cite_note-\:13-48][^cite_note-Patrick_Wilson_Gore-49] In the 1960s, 1970s, and 1980s, Armenians protested against Azerbaijan's cultural and economic marginalization[^cite_note-\:7-8][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuny1993192–194-50][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZürcher2007154-51] A referendum in 1988 was held to transfer the region to [Soviet Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Armenia), citing [self-determination](29269.md) laws in the [Soviet constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Constitution_of_the_Soviet_Union).[^cite_note-53] In 1991, both Armenia and [Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Nagorno-Karabakh_independence_referendum) declared independence from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). The Karabakh movement was met with a [series of pogroms against Armenians across Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Armenian_sentiment_in_Azerbaijan#During_the_Karabakh_Movement_\(1988-1992\)), and in November 1991, the Azerbaijani government [passed a motion aimed at abolishing the autonomy of the NKAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_on_Abolishment_of_Nagorno-Karabakh_Autonomous_Oblast) and prohibiting the use of Armenian placenames in the region.[^cite_note-\:4-54]

### Brazil

In 1825, soon after the [Empire of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Brazil) managed to defeat the [Cortes-Gerais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Cortes) and the [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) in an [Independence War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_War_of_Independence), the Platinean nationalists in [Cisplatina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisplatina) declared independence and joined the [United Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_the_R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata), which led to a [stagnated war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisplatine_War) between both, as they were both weakened, lacking manpower and politically fragile. The peace treaty accepted [Uruguay](31843.md)'s independence, reasserted the rule of both nations over their land and some important points like free navigation in the Silver River.

Three rather disorganized secessionist rebellions happened in [Grão-Pará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%A3o-Par%C3%A1_Province), Bahia, and Maranhão, where the people were unhappy with the Empire (these provinces were Portuguese bastions in the Independence War). The [Malê Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mal%C3%AA_Revolt), in Bahia, was an Islamic slave revolt. These three rebellions were bloodily crushed by the Empire of Brazil.

The [Pernambuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Pernambuco) was one of the most [nativist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativism_\(politics\)) of all Brazilian regions. Over a series of five revolts (1645–1654, 1710, 1817, 1824, 1848), the province [ousted the Dutch West India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernambucan_Revolt) and tried to secede from the Portuguese and Brazilian Empires. In each attempt, the rebels were crushed, the leaders shot and their territory divided. Nevertheless, they kept revolting until Pernambuco's territory was a little fraction of what it was before.

In the [Ragamuffin War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragamuffin_War), the [Province of Rio Grande do Sul](256268.md#History) was undergoing a (at that time common) [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism_in_Brazil) vs [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_Brazil) "cold" war. After Emperor [Pedro II of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_II_of_Brazil) favoured the conservatives, the liberals took the Capital and declared an [independent Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riograndense_Republic), fighting their way to the [Province of Santa Catarina](256305.md#History) and declaring the [Juliana Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliana_Republic). Eventually they were slowly forced back, and made a reunification peace with the Empire. This was not considered a secessionist war, even if it could have resulted in an independent republic if the Empire had been defeated. After the Empire agreed to aid Santa Catarina's economy by taxing [Argentina](18951905.md)'s products (like dry meat), the rebels reunited with the Empire and joined its military ranks.

In modern times, the [South Region of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Region,_Brazil) has been the centre of a secessionist movement led by an organization called [The South is My Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_South_is_My_Country) since the 1990s. Reasons cited for Southern Brazil's secession movement are taxation, due to it being one of the wealthiest regions in the country; political disputes with the northernmost states of Brazil; [2016 scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_protests_in_Brazil) revolving around the [Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers_Party_\(Brazil\))'s involvement in a [kickback scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickback_\(bribery\)) with state-owned oil company [Petrobras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrobras);[^cite_note-57] and the impeachment of then-[President](273638.md) [Dilma Rousseff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilma_Rousseff). Additionally, there is an ethnic divide as the South Region is predominately [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Brazilian), populated primarily by [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Brazilians), [Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Brazilians), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Brazilians) and other European groups. In contrast, the rest of Brazil is a [multicultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_democracy) [melting pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_pot). The South Region in 2016 voted in an unofficial referendum called "Plebisul" in which 95% of voters supported secession and the creation of an independent South Region.

There is also a push for secession movement in the state of [São Paulo](229422.md), which seeks to become a country independent from the rest of Brazil.

### Cameroon

Main article: [Anglophone Crisis](57170868.md)

In October 2017, [Ambazonia](841514.md) declared its independence from [Cameroon](5447.md). Less than a month beforehand, tensions had escalated into [open warfare](57170868.md) between separatists and the [Cameroon Armed Forces](5455.md). The conflict, known as the "Anglophone Crisis", is deeply rooted in the October 1, 1961 incomplete decolonization of the former [British Southern Cameroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cameroons) (UNGA Resolution 1608). On January 1, 1960, [French Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Cameroon) was granted independence from France as the Republic of Cameroon and was admitted into the United Nations. The more advanced democratic and self-ruling people of [British Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Cameroon) were instead limited to two choices. Through a UN [plebiscite](51582.md), they were directed to either join the federation of [Nigeria](21383.md) or the independent Republic of Cameroon as a federation of two equal states. While the Northern Cameroons voted to join Nigeria, the Southern Cameroons voted to integrate into the Republic of Cameroon, but they did so without a formal Treaty of Union on record at the UN. In 1972, Cameroon used its majority population to abolish the federation and implement a system which resulted in the occupation of the former South Cameroons territory by French-speaking Cameroon administrators. In 1984, Cameroon heightened tensions by returning to its name at independence, "Republic of Cameroun", which did not include the territory of the former British Southern Cameroons or Ambazonia. For more than fifty years the English-speaking people of the Former British Southern Cameroons made multiple attempts both nationally and internationally to get the Cameroon government to address these issues and possibly return to the previously agreed federation at independence. In 2016, after all these attempts failed, Cameroon engaged in a military crackdown, including cutting the internet in the English-speaking regions. In response, the people of Southern Cameroon declared on October 1, 2017, the restoration of their UN state of Southern Cameroons, which they called the "[Federal Republic of Ambazonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Ambazonia)".

### Canada

Main article: [Secessionist movements of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secessionist_movements_of_Canada)

Throughout Canada's history, there has been tension between [English-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Canada) and [French-speaking Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francophone_Canadians). Under the [Constitutional Act of 1791](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Act_of_1791), the [Province of Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Quebec_\(1763%E2%80%931791\)) (including parts of what are today [Quebec](7954867.md), [Ontario](22218.md) and [Newfoundland and Labrador](21980.md)) was divided in two: [Lower Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Canada) (which retained [French law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_France) and institutions and is now part of the provinces of Quebec and [Newfoundland and Labrador](21980.md)) and [Upper Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Canada) (a new colony intended to accommodate the many new English-speaking settlers, including the [United Empire Loyalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Empire_Loyalists), and now part of [Ontario](22218.md)). The intent was to provide each group with its own colony. In 1841, the two Canadas were merged into the [Province of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Canada). The union proved contentious, however, resulting in a legislative deadlock between English and French legislators. The difficulties of the union, among other factors, led in 1867 to the formation of the [Canadian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation), a [federal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_federalism) that united the [Province of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Canada), [Nova Scotia](21184.md) and [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick) (later joined by other [British colonies in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America)). The federal framework did not eliminate all tensions, however, leading to the [Quebec sovereignty movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_sovereignty_movement) in the latter half of the 20th century.

Other occasional secessionist movements have included anti-Confederation movements in the 19th century [Atlantic Canada](71095.md) (see [Anti-Confederation Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Confederation_Party)), the [North-West Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-West_Rebellion) of 1885, and various small separatist movements in Alberta particularly (see [Alberta separatism](2040033.md)) and [Western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada) generally (see, for example, [Western Canada Concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada_Concept)).

### China

The [People's Republic of China](5405.md) government claims control over [Taiwan](25734.md) and describes the [political status of Taiwan](66555.md) as an issue of secession, despite having never governed the territory. The [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China) (Taiwan) government administers control over Taiwan and outlying islands but lacks widespread official international recognition. The *[Anti-Secession Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Secession_Law),* passed in 2005, formalized the long-standing policy of the People's Republic of China to use military means against [Taiwan independence](31156.md) in the event peaceful means become otherwise impossible.

Western regions of [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) ([East Turkistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Turkistan)) and [Tibet](31516.md) are the focus of secessionist calls by the [Tibetan independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_independence_movement) and [East Turkestan Independence Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Turkestan_Independence_Movement). The [East Turkistan Government in Exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Turkistan_Government_in_Exile) does not view East Turkistan as a part of China but rather an occupied country, so it does not view independence from China as "secession" but rather "decolonization".

The [Special Administrative Region of Hong Kong](13404.md) has a secessionist movement in the city that the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) has placed on the national security agenda in 2017 which is called the [Hong Kong independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_independence_movement).

### European Union

Main article: [Withdrawal from the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_from_the_European_Union)

See also: [2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum)

Before the [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon) entered into force on 1 December 2009, no provision in the [treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaties_of_the_European_Union) or [law of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_law) outlined the ability of a state to voluntarily withdraw from the EU. The European Constitution did propose such a provision and, after the failure to ratify the [Treaty establishing a Constitution for Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_establishing_a_Constitution_for_Europe), that provision was then included in the Lisbon Treaty.

The treaty introduced an exit clause for members who wish to withdraw from the Union. This formalised the procedure by stating that a member state may notify the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) that it wishes to withdraw, upon which withdrawal negotiations begin; if no other agreement is reached, the treaty ceases to apply to the withdrawing state two years after such notification.[^cite_note-62]

On June 23, 2016, the United Kingdom voted to leave the [European Union](9317.md) in a binding referendum voted for by parliament, and finally left the European Union on January 31, 2020.[^cite_note-63] This is informally known as [Brexit](41688778.md).

### Finland

Finland successfully and peacefully seceded from the newly-formed and unstable [Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic) in 1917. The latter was led by [Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin), who had sought refuge in Finland during the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution_of_1905). Unsuccessful attempts at greater autonomy or peaceful secession had already been made during the preceding [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) but had been denied by the Russian emperor. However, with the country still at war and under great pressure, Lenin allowed Finland to secede. Its peripheral location made it difficult to defend and less strategically important than Russia's other territories, so he conceded sovereignty to the Finns rather than try to defend it.[^cite_note-64]

### India

### Italy

The [Movement for the Independence of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_the_Independence_of_Sicily) (Movimento Indipendentista Siciliano, MIS) has its roots in the [Sicilian Independence Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Independence_Movement) of the late 1940s; it was active for around 60 years. Today, the MIS no longer exists, though many other parties have emerged. One is [Nation Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_Sicily?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Sicilia Nazione), which still believes in the idea that Sicily, due to its deeply personal and ancient history, should be a sovereign country. Moreover, a common ideology shared by all the [Sicilian independentist movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Independentist_Movement) is to fight against [Cosa Nostra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosa_Nostra) and all the other [Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia) organizations, which have a very deep influence over Sicily's public and private institutions. The Sicilian branch of the [Five Star Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Star_Movement), which polls show is Sicily's most popular party, has also publicly expressed the intention to start working for a possible secession from Italy if the [central government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Italy) would not collaborate in shifting the nation's administrative organization from a unitary country to a [federal state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_state).

In Southern Italy, several movements have expressed a will to secede from Italy. This newborn ideology is called [neo-Bourbonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Bourbonism), because the [Kingdom of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Two_Sicilies) was under the control of the House of Bourbon. The Kingdom of the Two Sicilies was created in 1816 after the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna), and it comprised both Sicily and continental Southern Italy. The Kingdom came to an end in 1861, being annexed to the newborn Kingdom of Italy. However, the patriotic feelings shared among the southern Italian population is more ancient, starting in 1130 with the Kingdom of Sicily, which was composed by both the island and south Italy. According to the neo-Bourbonic movements the Italian regions which should secede are [Sicily](27619.md), [Calabria](44772.md), [Basilicata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilicata), [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia), [Molise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise), [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campania), [Abruzzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abruzzo), and [Latio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latio)'s provinces of [Rieti](1448595.md), [Latina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Latina) and [Frosinone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Frosinone). The major movements and parties which believe in this ideology are [Unione Mediterranea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unione_Mediterranea?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Mo!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo!?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Briganti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigandage_in_Southern_Italy_after_1861).

[Lega Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lega_Nord) has been seeking the independence of the region known to separatists as [Padania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padania), which includes lands along the [Po Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Valley) in [northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy). Some organizations separately work for the [independence of Venetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_nationalism) or Veneto and the [secession or reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Tyrolean_secessionist_movement) of [South Tyrol](169611.md) with Austria. Lega Nord governing [Lombardy](43807.md) has expressed a will to turn the region into a sovereign country. Also, the island of [Sardinia](29376.md) is home to a notable [nationalist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardinian_nationalism).

### New Zealand

The [NZ South Island Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NZ_South_Island_Party), with a pro-South agenda, fielded only five candidates (4.20% of electoral seats) candidates in the [1999 General Election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_New_Zealand_general_election) but achieved only 0.14% (2622 votes) of the general vote. The reality today is that although South Islanders have a strong identity rooted in their geographic region, secession does not carry any real constituency; the party was not able to field any candidates in the 2008 election, as they had less than 500 paying members, a requirement by the New Zealand Electoral commission. The party is treated more as a "joke" party than any real political force.

### South Africa

Main articles: [Cape Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Independence) and [Volkstaat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkstaat)

In 1910, following the [Boer Republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boer_Republics) defeat by the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) in the [Boer Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boer_War), four self-governing colonies in the south of Africa were merged into the [Union of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa). The four regions were the [Cape Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Colony), [Orange Free State](180208.md), [Natal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Natal) and [Transvaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_Colony). Three other territories, [High Commission Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Commissioner_for_Southern_Africa) of [Bechuanaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bechuanaland) (now [Botswana](3464.md)), [Basutoland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basutoland) (now [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho)) and [Swaziland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaziland) (now Eswatini) later became independent states in the 1960s. Following the election of the [Nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_\(South_Africa\)) government in 1948, some [English-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) whites in Natal advocated either secession or a loose federation.[^cite_note-74] There were also calls for secession, with Natal and the eastern part of the [Cape Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Province) breaking away[^cite_note-75] following the [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_South_African_republic_referendum) in 1960 on establishing a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic). In 1993, prior to South Africa's first elections under universal suffrage and the end of apartheid, some [Zulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_people) leaders in [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md)[^cite_note-76] again considered secession as did some politicians in the Cape Province.[^cite_note-77]

In 2008, a political movement calling for the return to independence of the Cape resurged in the shape of the political organisation, the [Cape Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Party). The Cape Party contested their first elections on 22 April 2009.[^cite_note-78] They finished the Western Cape provincial elections in 2019 with 9,331 votes, or 0.45% of votes, gaining no seats[^cite_note-dash-79]

The idea gained popularity in the early half of the 2020s, with polling suggesting that 58% of Western Cape Voters want a referendum on independence in July 2021.[^cite_note-80]

### South Sudan

A [referendum took place in Southern Sudan](13265409.md) from 9 to 15 January 2011, on whether the region should remain a part of Sudan or become independent. The referendum was one of the consequences of the [2005 Naivasha Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Peace_Agreement) between the [Khartoum central government](5698926.md) and the [Sudan People's Liberation Army/Movement](19196675.md) (SPLA/M).[^cite_note-81]

On 7 February 2011, the referendum commission published the final results, with 98.83% voting in favour of independence. While the ballots were suspended in 10 of the 79 counties for exceeding 100% of the voter turnout, the number of votes was still well over the requirement of 60% turnout, and the majority vote for secession is not in question.[^cite_note-82]

A simultaneous referendum was supposed to be held in Abyei on whether to join Southern Sudan but it has been postponed because of conflict over demarcation and residency rights. In October 2013, a symbolic referendum was held in which 99.9% of voters in Abyei voted to join Southern Sudan. However, this resolution was non-binding.[^cite_note-83] As of February 2024, an official referendum still has not taken place. Abyei currently holds "special administrative status".[^cite_note-84]

The predetermined date for the creation of an independent state was 9 July 2011.

The Irish republicans attempted to withdraw [Ireland](147575.md) from the [United Kingdom](31717.md) during the [Easter Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Rebellion) of 1916. Ireland gained independence as the [Irish Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Free_State) in 1922, except for six [Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Ulster) counties which chose to remain in the United Kingdom as [Northern Ireland](21265.md). - In [Northern Ireland](21265.md), [Irish republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_republicanism) and [nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_nationalism) have long called for the secession of Northern Ireland to join the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md). This is opposed by [Unionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unionism_in_Ireland). A minority have supported the [independence of Northern Ireland from the United Kingdom without joining the Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_nationalism).

- In [Scotland](26994.md), the [Scottish National Party](28887.md) (SNP) campaigns for [Scottish independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_independence) and direct Scottish membership in the [European Union](9317.md). It has representation at all levels of [Scottish politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_in_Scotland) and forms the devolved [Scottish government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_government). Later pro-independence parties have had lesser electoral success. The [Scottish Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Greens) and the [Scottish Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Socialist_Party) are most widely publicised. However, all independence movements/parties are opposed by [unionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unionism_in_Scotland). A [referendum on independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Scottish_independence_referendum) in which voters were asked "Should Scotland be an independent country?" took place in September 2014. It saw "no" win, as 55.3% of voters voted against independence.

On June 25, 1991, [Croatia](5573.md) and [Slovenia](27338.md) seceded from the [Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia). [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md) and [North Macedonia](23564616.md) also declared independence, after which the federation broke up, causing the separation of the remaining two countries [Serbia](29265.md) and [Montenegro](20760.md). Several [wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars) ensued between the [Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro) and seceding entities and among other ethnic groups in [Slovenia](27338.md), [Croatia](5573.md), [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), and later, [Kosovo](17391.md). Montenegro peacefully separated from its union with Serbia in 2006.

[Kosovo unilaterally declared independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Kosovo_declaration_of_independence) from Serbia on February 17, 2008, and was recognized by around 100 countries, with the rest considering it remaining under [United Nations administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Interim_Administration_Mission_in_Kosovo).
