The **Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences**, often referred to as the **Nobel Prize in Economics**, is an award in the field of [economic sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) administered by the [Nobel Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Foundation). Officially, it is titled **The Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel**[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] ([Swedish](26689.md): *Sveriges riksbanks pris i ekonomisk vetenskap till Alfred Nobels minne*), established in 1968 by Sweden's central bank, [Sveriges Riksbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sveriges_Riksbank), to celebrate its 300th anniversary and in memory of [Alfred Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Nobel).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-EB-NP2-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-bank-estab2-9]

Although the prize was not one of the original five [Nobel Prizes](21201.md) established by Alfred Nobel's will,[^cite_note-\:1-10] it is considered part of the Nobel Prize framework[^cite_note-11] and is administered and referred to along with Nobel Prizes by the Nobel Foundation.[^cite_note-12] Laureates of the Prize in Economic Sciences are chosen in a similar manner to and announced alongside the Nobel Prize recipients, and receive the Prize in Economic Sciences at the Nobel Prize Award Ceremony.[^cite_note-\:1-10][^cite_note-13]

The laureates of the Prize in Economic Sciences are selected by the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences), which also selects the laureates of the [prizes in Physics](52497.md) and [Chemistry](25416893.md).[^cite_note-statutes-econ-14][^cite_note-nomaw-15] The Prize was first awarded in 1969 to Dutch economist [Jan Tinbergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Tinbergen) and Norwegian economist [Ragnar Frisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragnar_Frisch) "for having developed and applied dynamic models for the analysis of economic processes".[^cite_note-bank-estab-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Creation and funding

Since 1968 an endowment "in perpetuity" from [Sveriges Riksbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sveriges_Riksbank) has been paying the Nobel Foundation's administrative expenses associated with the award and funds the monetary component of the award.[^cite_note-statutes-econ-14] The amount of the Prize in Economic Sciences remains the same as other prizes, at 11 million Swedish kronor in 2023 and 2024.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-Lindbeck-21][^cite_note-22] Since 2006, Sveriges Riksbank has given the Nobel Foundation an annual grant of 6.5 million Swedish kronor (in January 2008, approx. [US\$](18717338.md)1 million; [€](9472.md)0.7 million) for its administrative expenses associated with the award as well as 1 million Swedish kronor (until the end of 2008) to include information about the award on the Nobel Foundation's official websites.[^cite_note-23]

### Relation to the Nobel Prizes

The Prize in Economic Sciences is not one of the original five Nobel Prizes endowed by [Alfred Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Nobel) in his will.[^cite_note-EB-NP-24][^cite_note-25] However, the nomination process, selection criteria, and awards presentation of the Prize in Economic Sciences are performed in a manner similar to that of the original Nobel Prizes.[^cite_note-\:1-10][^cite_note-statutes-econ-14][^cite_note-Lindbeck-21][^cite_note-26]

Laureates are announced with the other Nobel Prize laureates, and receive the award at the same ceremony.[^cite_note-EB-NP-24] The [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) awards the prize "in accordance with the rules governing the award of the Nobel Prizes instituted through his \[Alfred Nobel's] will",[^cite_note-statutes-econ-14] which stipulate that the prize be awarded annually to "those who ... shall have conferred the greatest benefit on mankind".[^cite_note-27]

## Award nomination and selection process

Main articles: [Committee for the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_for_the_Sveriges_Riksbank_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences_in_Memory_of_Alfred_Nobel) and [List of Nobel Memorial Prize laureates in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_Memorial_Prize_laureates_in_Economic_Sciences)



The Prize in Economic Sciences being announced at the Nobel Prize press conference by the Prize Committee in 2008

According to its official website, the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) "administers a researcher exchange with academies in other countries and publishes six scientific journals. Every year the Academy awards the [Nobel Prizes in Physics](52497.md) and [in Chemistry](25416893.md), the Prize in Economic Sciences, the [Crafoord Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crafoord_Prize) and a number of other large prizes".[^cite_note-nomaw-15]

Each September the Academy's Economics Prize Committee, which consists of five elected members, "sends invitations to thousands of scientists, members of academies and university professors in numerous countries, asking them to nominate candidates for the Prize in Economics for the coming year. Members of the Academy and former laureates are also authorised to nominate candidates."[^cite_note-statutes-econ-14][^cite_note-nomaw-15][^cite_note-28] All proposals and their supporting evidence must be received before February 1.[^cite_note-selectionProcess-29] The proposals are reviewed by the Prize Committee and specially appointed experts. Before the end of September, the committee chooses potential laureates. If there is a tie, the chairman of the committee casts the deciding vote.

Members of the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences vote in mid-October to determine the next laureate or laureates of the Prize in Economics.[^cite_note-statutes-econ-14][^cite_note-30] As with the Nobel Prizes, no more than three people can share the prize for a given year; they must still be living at the time of the Prize announcement in October; and information about nominations, including the names of nominees and nominators and related investigations and opinions, cannot be disclosed publicly or privately for 50 years.[^cite_note-31]

Like the Nobel laureates in [physics](52497.md), [chemistry](25416893.md), [physiology or medicine](52502.md), and [literature](23385442.md), each laureate in Economics receives a diploma, gold medal, and monetary grant award document from the [King of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch_of_Sweden) at the annual Nobel Prize Award Ceremony in [Stockholm](26741.md), on the anniversary of Nobel's death (December 10).[^cite_note-EB-NP-24][^cite_note-32]

## Laureates

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of Nobel Memorial Prize laureates in Economic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_Memorial_Prize_laureates_in_Economic_Sciences).



The Laureates of the Prize in economics, physics and chemistry appeared together at an event of the [Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Swedish_Academy_of_Sciences) in 2013.

The first prize in economics was awarded in 1969 to [Ragnar Frisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragnar_Frisch) and [Jan Tinbergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Tinbergen) "for having developed and applied dynamic models for the analysis of economic processes".[^cite_note-33] Three women have received the prize: [Elinor Ostrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elinor_Ostrom), who won in 2009, [Esther Duflo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Duflo), who won in 2019,[^cite_note-34] and [Claudia Goldin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Goldin), who won in 2023. Goldin was the first woman to win the award solo.[^cite_note-35]

### Awards to non-economists

In February 1995, following acrimony within the selection committee pertaining to the awarding of the 1994 Prize in Economics to [John Forbes Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Forbes_Nash), the Prize in Economics was redefined as a prize in social sciences. This made it available to researchers in such topics as political science, psychology, and sociology.[^cite_note-nasarmind372-36][^cite_note-notnoble-37]

Moreover, the composition of the Economics Prize Committee changed to include two non-economists. This has not been confirmed by the Economics Prize Committee. The members of the 2007 Economics Prize Committee are still dominated by economists, as the secretary and four of the five members are professors of economics.[^cite_note-38]

In 1978, [Herbert A. Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_A._Simon), whose [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) was in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science), became the first non-economist to win the prize,[^cite_note-39] for his work in the fields of economics and organizational decision making. [Elinor Ostrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elinor_Ostrom) and [James Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_A._Robinson), who are also political scientists, won the prize in 2009 and 2024, respectively. Similarly, [Daniel Kahneman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Kahneman), a professor of psychology and public affairs at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), won the prize for work in the field of [behavioral economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioral_economics).[^cite_note-40]

## Controversies and criticisms

### Alleged misuse of the Nobel name

Critics argue that the prestige of the Prize in Economic Sciences derives in part from its association with the Nobel Prizes. Among them is [Peter Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Nobel), a great-grandnephew of [Alfred Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Nobel) and a Swedish human rights lawyer.[^cite_note-\:0-41] Nobel accuses the awarding institution of misusing his family's name, and states that no member of the Nobel family has ever had the intention of establishing a prize in economics.[^cite_note-42] He explained that "Nobel despised people who cared more about profits than society's well-being", saying that "There is nothing to indicate that he would have wanted such a prize", and that the association with the Nobel prizes is "a PR coup by economists to improve their reputation".[^cite_note-\:0-41]

### Accusations of bias

Critics cite the apparent snub of [Joan Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Robinson) as evidence of the [committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_for_the_Prize_in_Economic_Sciences_in_Memory_of_Alfred_Nobel)'s bias towards [mainstream economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainstream_economics).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

### Undue authority

In his speech at the 1974 Nobel Prize banquet, [Friedrich Hayek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Hayek) stated that had he been consulted on the establishment of a Nobel Prize in economics, he would have "decidedly advised against it",[^cite_note-notnoble-37][^cite_note-hayekbanquet-45] primarily because "The Nobel Prize confers on an individual an authority which in economics no man ought to possess. This does not matter in the [natural sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_sciences). Here the influence exercised by an individual is chiefly an influence on his fellow experts; and they will soon cut him down to size if he exceeds his competence. But the influence of the economist that mainly matters is an influence over laymen: politicians, journalists, civil servants and the public generally."[^cite_note-hayekbanquet-45] Nevertheless, Hayek accepted the award.

### Expansion of the scope

The 1994 prize to mathematician [John Forbes Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Forbes_Nash) caused controversy within the selection committee.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] This resulted in a change to the rules governing the committee during 1994: the prize's scope was redefined as one of social sciences, and Prize Committee members were limited to serve for three years.[^cite_note-nasarmind372-36]

## Alternative names

The prize's official name is the **Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel**[^cite_note-\:2-57][^cite_note-\:3-58][^cite_note-\:4-59] ([Swedish](26689.md): *Sveriges riksbanks pris i ekonomisk vetenskap till Alfred Nobels minne*).
