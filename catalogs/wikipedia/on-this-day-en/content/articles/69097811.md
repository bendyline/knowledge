**Boris Ludwig Pistorius** (German: [\[ˈbɔʁɪs pɪsˈtoːʁiʊs\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/0d/De-Boris_Pistorius.ogg/De-Boris_Pistorius.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/0d/De-Boris_Pistorius.ogg/De-Boris_Pistorius.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; born 14 March 1960) is a German lawyer and politician of the [Social Democratic Party of Germany](60585.md) (SPD) who has been serving as [Federal Minister of Defence](2563857.md) in the governments of successive Chancellors [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz) and [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md) since 2023, making him the only minister of the [Scholz cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholz_cabinet) to retain his position. He is considered to be amongst the most popular politicians in Germany, receiving significant popular approval ever since his appointment to the Defence Ministry.[^cite_note-2]

Since the [2025 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_German_federal_election), he is a member of the 21st [Bundestag](3768.md). Earlier in his career, Pistorius was a member of the [State Parliament of Lower Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Parliament_of_Lower_Saxony), and served as State Minister for Interior and Sports in the state government of [Lower Saxony](18435.md) ([Cabinet Weil II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Weil_II)) from 2013 to 2017.[^cite_note-3] From 2006 to 2013, Pistorius was Lord Mayor of [Osnabrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osnabr%C3%BCck).[^cite_note-\:0-4]

## Early life and education

Pistorius was born in [Osnabrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osnabr%C3%BCck), the second son of [Ursula Pistorius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursula_Pistorius) (née Raabe; 1933–2015) and Ludwig Pistorius (1923–2009).[^cite_note-5] His mother was a member of the Osnabrück city council from 1972 to 1996 and also served as a member of the [Landtag of Lower Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag_of_Lower_Saxony) from 1978 to 1990.[^cite_note-6]

After taking his [abitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abitur) at the Ernst-Moritz-Arndt Gymnasium[^cite_note-7] in Osnabrück, he was [conscripted](5735.md) to [military service](1285284.md) in the [Bundeswehr](288188.md) in 1980[^cite_note-8] before taking up law studies at the [University of Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_M%C3%BCnster) and [Osnabrück University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osnabr%C3%BCck_University), and also, for a brief period, at the [Catholic University of the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_the_West).[^cite_note-b-9]

## Early career

Pistorius worked as the personal advisor to [State Minister for Interior of Lower Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_for_Interior_and_Sports_of_Lower_Saxony) [Gerhard Glogowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Glogowski) in [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Schr%C3%B6der_I_\(Lower_Saxony\)) led by [Minister-President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister-President_of_Lower_Saxony) [Gerhard Schröder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Schr%C3%B6der) from 1991 to 1995, and was the deputy head of his office from 1995 to 1996.[^cite_note-b-9]

## Political career

### Career in local politics

Pistorius joined the [SPD](60585.md) in 1976.[^cite_note-10]

Pistorius was part of the city council from 1996 until 2013 and from 1999 to 2002 he served as second mayor of [Osnabrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osnabr%C3%BCck).[^cite_note-11]

Pistorius served as [Mayor of Osnabrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osnabr%C3%BCck),[^cite_note-\:0-4] starting his mayoral term on 7 November 2006 winning with 55.5% against Wolfgang Griesert[^cite_note-12] who would later go on to become the Mayor after Pistorius's resignation due to his position at the [Lower Saxony government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Lower_Saxony) in 2013.[^cite_note-13]

### State Minister of the Interior and Sports (2013–2022)

After the [Lower Saxony state elections in 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Lower_Saxony_state_election), Pistorius was sworn in as [State Minister of the Interior and Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_for_Interior_and_Sports_of_Lower_Saxony) at the constituent session of the 17th [State Parliament of Lower Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag_of_Lower_Saxony) on 19 February 2013.

From 2013 to 2017, Pistorius was one of the state's representatives on the [German Bundesrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Bundesrat); from 2017, he was an alternate member.[^cite_note-14] In this capacity, he was a member of the German-Russian Friendship Group set up in cooperation with the [Russian Federation Council](1268578.md). He was also an alternate member of the German delegation to the [NATO Parliamentary Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Parliamentary_Assembly), where he was part of the Political Committee, its Sub-Committee on NATO Partnerships and its Sub-Committee on Transatlantic Relations.[^cite_note-15]

During his time in office in state government, Pistorius was widely seen as standing out in his state for his tough stance[^cite_note-hansen-16] on Islamist radicalism, terror threats, [organized crime](22625.md) and far-right extremism.[^cite_note-17]

### Role in national politics

Main article: [2019 Social Democratic Party of Germany leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Social_Democratic_Party_of_Germany_leadership_election)

In the negotiations to form a [fourth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Merkel_cabinet) [coalition government](6036.md) under [Chancellor](20890626.md) [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel)'s leadership following the [2017 federal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_German_federal_election), Pistorius was part of the working group on internal and legal affairs, led by [Thomas de Maizière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_de_Maizi%C3%A8re), [Stephan Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephan_Ernst_Johann_Mayer) and [Heiko Maas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiko_Maas).

In the [2019 SPD leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Social_Democratic_Party_of_Germany_leadership_election), Pistorius was a candidate for the position as the party's co-chair, together with Saxony State Minister [Petra Köpping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra_K%C3%B6pping).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Köpping and Pistorius came in fifth place, receiving only 14.41% of the vote.

In the negotiations to form a so-called [traffic light coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_light_coalition) of the SPD, the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_90/The_Greens) and the [Free Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party_\(Germany\)) (FDP) following the [2021 German elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_German_federal_election), Pistorius led his party's delegation in the working group on migration and integration; the co-chairs from the other parties were [Luise Amtsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luise_Amtsberg) and [Joachim Stamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_Stamp).[^cite_note-20]

### Federal Minister of Defence (2023–present)

On 17 January 2023, [Chancellor](20890626.md) [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz) announced that Pistorius would succeed [Christine Lambrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Lambrecht), who resigned on 16 January after numerous political blunders, as [Minister of Defence](2563857.md) in [Chancellor Olaf Scholz's cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholz_cabinet).[^cite_note-kcgbp-21][^cite_note-22] This came as a surprise to many political observers, with [SPD co-leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Social_Democratic_Party_of_Germany) [Lars Klingbeil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Klingbeil) and Bundestag [Armed Forces](288188.md) Commissioner [Eva Högl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_H%C3%B6gl) being floated most often as replacements.[^cite_note-23] The appointment was criticized,[^cite_note-24] as it would mean breaking the gender parity Scholz had promised upon the cabinet's formation.[^cite_note-25] A plausible explanation is that, in the acute European military crisis – many people, including people in the Bundeswehr – thought it irresponsible to again appoint somebody without any military experience,[^cite_note-26] and to instead appoint based on competency rather than gender.

He was retained by [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md) following the formation of [his cabinet](79691794.md) following the [2025 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_German_federal_election).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

Pistorius was formally appointed by German president [Frank-Walter Steinmeier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank-Walter_Steinmeier) and eventually took the oath of office at the [Bundestag](3768.md) on 19 January 2023.

During his first year in office, Pistorius announced [military aid](70196881.md) worth €2.7 billion (US\$3.0 billion) to [Ukraine](31750.md) both in May 2023[^cite_note-29] and €1 billion (US\$1.1 billion) in October 2023.[^cite_note-30]

In March 2023, Pistorius participated in the first joint cabinet meeting of the governments of Germany and Japan in [Tokyo](30057.md), chaired by Chancellor Scholz and Prime Minister [Fumio Kishida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumio_Kishida). In October 2023, he joined the first joint cabinet retreat of the German and French governments in Hamburg, chaired by Scholz and President [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

In September 2023, Pistorius and his Israeli counterpart, [Yoav Gallant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoav_Gallant), signed a "historic" agreement for Germany to purchase the Israeli-made [Arrow 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_3) missile defense system for approximately €3.3 billion (\$3.5 billion), making it the largest defense deal in [Israel](9282173.md)'s history.[^cite_note-33]

In July 2024, [Christian Lindner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Lindner) warned Boris Pistorius that no additional funds would be provided for military modernization beyond what the coalition government had already agreed upon. This followed a budget decision that left Pistorius, responsible for revitalizing Germany's defenses amid increased security concerns following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, feeling disappointed. Lindner defended the funding arrangement, highlighting a special 100-billion-euro fund that had been made available to Pistorius for military upgrades, a resource unprecedented for previous defense ministers, and urged efficient use of these resources.[^cite_note-34] This budgetary restraint came as the IMF noted that Germany's low public infrastructure investment was affecting its economic growth, despite having the lowest debt-to-GDP ratio in the G7.[^cite_note-35]

Under Pistorius' leadership, Germany joined the US-led [United Nations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Command) (UNC) in South Korea in August 2024, becoming the 18th nation to do so.[^cite_note-36] Also in August 2024, Pistorius and his Philippine counterpart [Gilberto Teodoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilberto_Teodoro) committed to establishing long-term relations between their armed forces to expand training and bilateral exchanges, explore opportunities to expand bilateral armaments cooperation and engage in joint projects.[^cite_note-37]

In July 2024, the United States announced its intention to deploy [long-range missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missiles) in Germany beginning in 2026.[^cite_note-38] US weapons in Germany would include [SM-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-174_Standard_ERAM) and [Tomahawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_\(missile_family\)) cruise missiles and [hypersonic weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-Range_Hypersonic_Weapon).[^cite_note-guardian-missiles-39] The United States' decision to deploy missiles in Germany has been compared to the deployment of [Pershing II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pershing_II) launchers in Western Europe in the 1980s.[^cite_note-aljazeera-cold_war-40][^cite_note-guardian-missiles-39] The decision was supported by Boris Pistorius and German Chancellor [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz). Critics say the move would trigger a new [arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_race).[^cite_note-41] According to Russian military analysts, it would be extremely difficult to distinguish between a conventionally armed missile and a missile carrying a [nuclear warhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_warhead), and Russia could respond by deploying longer-range nuclear systems targeting Germany.[^cite_note-42]

Polling conducted in 2024 found Pistorius to be the most popular politician in Germany. He declined to be the SPD's Chancellor-candidate in the [2025 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_German_federal_election).[^cite_note-43]

## Political positions

In 2018, Pistorius suggested that [sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) against [Russia](25391.md) should be reviewed.[^cite_note-die_zeit_pistorius_review_sanctions-50] In 2021, he demanded that Germany order the messaging program [Telegram](41501305.md) removed from [Apple Inc.](856.md)'s and Google's [app stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/App_store) if it continues to ignore requests to help track down extremist content.[^cite_note-51] Amid the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), Pistorius condemned what he has called Russia's "brutal attacks" on Ukraine.[^cite_note-hansen-16] In May 2022, he said that Russian sympathizers must not glorify Russia's war against Ukraine on German streets.[^cite_note-hansen-16] In October 2023, following the outbreak of the [Gaza war](74998519.md), Pistorius expressed full support for [Israel](9282173.md). He told Israeli Defence Minister [Yoav Gallant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoav_Gallant) that "whatever we can do to support you, with material support, we will do this."[^cite_note-52] In January 2024, he warned that Russia could attack [NATO](21133.md) in 5 to 8 years.[^cite_note-53] In April 2024, Pistorius compared Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) to [Nazi Germany](21212.md)'s leader [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md) and urged Europe to prepare for a full-scale war with Russia, saying that "Putin will not stop once the war against Ukraine is over."[^cite_note-54]

## Personal life

Pistorius has two daughters with his wife Sabine, who died in 2015 due to cancer.[^cite_note-55] He was in a relationship with [Doris Schröder-Köpf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doris_Schr%C3%B6der-K%C3%B6pf) from 2016 until spring 2022.[^cite_note-56] He has been in a relationship with Julia Schwanholz, a professor at the [University of Duisburg-Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Duisburg-Essen), since 2022.[^cite_note-57]
