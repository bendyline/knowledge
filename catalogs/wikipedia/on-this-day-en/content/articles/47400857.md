The U.S. is the third-largest producer of raw [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel) worldwide, after China and India, and is ranked sixth in [pig iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_iron) production. In 2024, the industry produced over 79 million net tons of crude steel.[^cite_note-1] Approximately 25% of the steel used in the U.S. is imported.[^cite_note-2]

Major steel-makers in the United States include [Cleveland-Cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland-Cliffs), [Commercial Metals Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_Metals_Company), [Nucor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucor), [Steel Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Dynamics), [Nippon Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Steel), and [Carpenter Technology Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpenter_Technology_Corporation).

Employment as of 2014 was 149,000 people employed in iron and steel mills, and 69,000 in foundries. The value of iron and steel produced in 2014 was \$113 billion.[^cite_note-3] As of 2020, about 0.3% of the US population is employed by the steel industry,[^cite_note-4] and by 2025 steel mills were only employing 83,600 people, making the industry a relatively small portion of [US manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_in_the_United_States) despite outsize political influence.[^cite_note-SupplyLinesSteel-5]

## Types of steel mills

Steel mills in the U.S. fall into two categories: traditional integrated mills that process iron ore into steel and mini-mills that melt steel scrap to produce new steel. Mini-mills now dominate U.S. steel production.

### Integrated steel mills

In 2017, there were 9 operating integrated steel mills in the United States (plus one idled), down from 13 in 2000. Integrated mills produced 31% of the steel produced in the US.

In an integrated steel mill, iron ore is reduced to metallic iron. In the US, this is done in [blast furnaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_furnaces) and since 2014 again using [direct reduced iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_reduced_iron) furnaces in [Nucor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucor#Divisions)'s plant in [Louisiana](18130.md) as well as another DRI plant (producing a compactified version of DRI called hot briquetted iron, or HBI) in Texas by [Voestalpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voestalpine#North_American_operations). Some of the iron from the blast furnaces is converted to steel; today this is done in basic oxygen furnaces. Iron ore, coke, and flux are fed into the blast furnace and heated. The coke reduces the iron oxide in the ore to metallic iron, and the molten mass separates into slag and iron. Some of the iron from the blast furnace is cooled, and marketed as pig iron; the rest flows into basic oxygen furnaces, where it is converted into steel. Iron and steel scrap may be added to both the blast furnace and the basic iron furnace.

US Steel operates a number of integrated steel mills, including the [Gary Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Works) in [Gary, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary,_Indiana). They also operate the [Edgar Thomson Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Thomson_Works), which is the iron- and steel-making unit of the Mon Valley Works, which includes three other related plants. The company operates the Great Lakes Works,[^cite_note-LAT_2019-12-20-6] and Granite City Works.[^cite_note-7]

[Cleveland-Cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland-Cliffs) operates every integrated steel mill: in [East Chicago, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Chicago,_Indiana), [Burns Harbor, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burns_Harbor,_Indiana), and [Cleveland, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland,_Ohio).[^cite_note-8]

The Ashland Works was demolished.

### Specialty steel mills / minimills

There were about 112 minimills or specialty mills in the US, which in 2013 produced 59% of US total steel production. The specialty mills use iron and steel scrap, rather than iron ore, as feedstock, and melt the scrap in electric furnaces.

### Iron ore

Main article: [Iron mining in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_mining_in_the_United_States)

### Iron and steel scrap

Two-thirds of the iron and steel produced in the US is made from recycled scrap, rather than from iron ore. In 2014, 81 million mt of iron and steel were produced from scrap.[^cite_note-16] Most steel from scrap is produced using electric arc furnaces.

### Coke

Coke, derived from [coking coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgical_coal), reduces iron ore (iron oxides) to metallic iron.

### Flux

Flux materials, like lime, are added to the furnace to lower the melting point and remove impurities as slag. Other common fluxes include dolomite, soda ash, and fluorspar.

### Alloy metals

Other metals are commonly added to steel to produce [alloy steels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloy_steel) of various types. Common alloy metals are manganese, nickel, molybdenum, chromium, and vanadium. [Stainless steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stainless_steel) commonly contains a minimum of 10.5% chromium, and may also contain significant amounts of nickel or molybdenum.

### Slag

Main article: [Slag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slag)

Slag, a byproduct of iron and steel-making composed primarily of highly impure glass, would normally be a waste product. However, it is in demand as an aggregate in concrete, asphalt paving, and construction fill. In 2014, the industry produced and marketed about 16.0 million mt of slag, worth an estimated \$270 million.[^cite_note-17]

Since the 1800s, the US steel industry has generally had a history of supporting [tariffs](55551.md) on foreign steel imports, which [Bloomberg](432006.md) has described as a "the reward delivered...in a [patronage system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patronage_system)" tied to a general tendency for [heavy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_industry) to favor [economic protectionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_protectionism). Presidents from both the [Republican](32070.md) and [Democratic](5043544.md) parties have been known to cooperate with these demands. This has been cited as a contributing factor in [Biden administration](65732174.md)'s skepticism towards the 2023-2024 [proposed acquisition of U.S. Steel by Nippon Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposed_acquisition_of_U.S._Steel_by_Nippon_Steel).[^cite_note-SupplyLinesSteel-5]

[Tariffs instituted during the first term of President Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Trump_tariffs) starting in 2019, which some of the biggest corporate steel buyers in the country criticized for their inclusion of exemptions, succeeded at increasing investment in the country's steel industry, but did not fare well in terms of [job creation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_creation). Bloomberg has also argued that evidence suggests those tariffs led to a [recession](25382.md) in the [US manufacturing industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_in_the_United_States) as a whole via rising prices.[^cite_note-SupplyLinesSteel-5]

Following the 2024 U.S. presidential election, the U.S. steel industry actively advocated for stronger trade protections, including reimposing tariffs on steel imports from the United Kingdom, European Union, and Japan to address trade-distorting practices. The industry argued that steel tariffs imposed in 2018 boosted steel prices, profits, and employment. They have also publicly called for stricter enforcement of trade laws and increased tariffs on steel-intensive products from China and Southeast Asia.[^cite_note-22]

## History of US iron- and steel-making

Main article: [History of the iron and steel industry in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_iron_and_steel_industry_in_the_United_States)



Graph of US iron and steel production, 1900–2014, data from USGS

The US iron and steel industry has paralleled the industry in other countries in technological developments. In the 1800s, the US switched from charcoal to coke in ore smelting, adopted the Bessemer process, and saw the rise of very large integrated steel mills. In the 20th century, the US industry successively adopted the open hearth process, then the basic oxygen furnace. Since the American industry peaked in the 1940s and 1950s, the US industry has shifted to small mini-mills and specialty mills, using iron and steel scrap as feedstock, rather than iron ore.
