**Mecklenburg-Vorpommern** (**MV**; German: [\[ˌmeːklənbʊʁkˈfoːɐ̯pɔmɐn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) or [\[ˌmɛk-\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/80/De-Mecklenburg-Vorpommern2.ogg/De-Mecklenburg-Vorpommern2.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/80/De-Mecklenburg-Vorpommern2.ogg/De-Mecklenburg-Vorpommern2.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>;[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] [Low German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_German_language): *Mäkelborg-Vörpommern*), often known by its [anglicised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicisation) name **Mecklenburg–West Pomerania**, is a coastal [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) in the north-east of Germany. Of the country's sixteen states, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern [ranks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Germany#States) 14th in population; it covers an area of 23,300 km<sup>2</sup> (9,000 sq mi),[^cite_note-8] making it the sixth largest German state in area; and it is 16th in population density. [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin) is the state capital and [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock) is the largest city. Other major [cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Mecklenburg-Vorpommern) include [Neubrandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neubrandenburg), [Stralsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stralsund), [Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greifswald), [Wismar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wismar), and [Güstrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCstrow). It was named after the two regions of Mecklenburg and [Western Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Pomerania) (German: *Vorpommern*).

The [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Mecklenburg_\(1945%E2%80%931952\)) was established in 1945 after [World War II](32927.md) through the merger of the historic regions of [Mecklenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg) and Prussian [Western Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Pomerania) by the [Soviet military administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Military_Administration_in_Germany) in [Allied-occupied Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany). It became part of the [German Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Democratic_Republic) in 1949, but was dissolved in 1952 during [administrative reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_East_Germany) and its territory divided into the districts of [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bezirk_Rostock), [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bezirk_Schwerin), and [Neubrandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bezirk_Neubrandenburg). Mecklenburg-Vorpommern was re-established in 1990 following [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) and became one of the [new states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_states_of_Germany) of the Federal Republic of Germany.[^cite_note-9] Mecklenburg-Vorpommern has land borders with [Poland](22936.md) and the German states of [Brandenburg](3765.md), [Lower Saxony](18435.md), and [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md).

On the state's coastline on the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) are many holiday resorts and much unspoilt nature, including the islands of [Rügen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCgen), [Usedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usedom), and others, as well as the [Mecklenburg Lake District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg_Lake_District), making the state one of [Germany's leading tourist destinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Germany). Three of Germany's fourteen [national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_park), as well as several hundred nature [conservation areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_area), are in the state. The [University of Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Rostock), founded in 1419, and the [University of Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Greifswald), established in 1456, are among the [oldest universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_universities_in_continuous_operation) in Europe. In 2007, the [33rd G8 summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/33rd_G8_summit) took place at the [Grand Hotel Heiligendamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Hotel_Heiligendamm) on the Mecklenburg Baltic coast during the chancellorship of [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel), who represented various constituencies of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern in the national legislature, the [Bundestag](3768.md).

## Name

Due to its lengthy name, the state is often abbreviated as *MV* or (colloquially) shortened to *MeckPomm*.[^cite_note-10] In English, it is usually called "Mecklenburg–West Pomerania" or less frequently "Mecklenburg–Cispomerania." Inhabitants are called either *[Mecklenburger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg)* or *[Pomeranians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Pomerania)*; the combined form is never used.

The place name *Vorpommern* derives from [West Slavic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Slavic_languages). The prefix "po-" means *along* or *at*, and the base word "more" means *sea*, so that together "po more" means *Land at the Sea*. The German prefix "vor-" denotes a closer location; from the German point of view, this is the near part of Pomerania.[^cite_note-11]

The full name in German is pronounced [\[ˈmeːklənbʊʁk ˈfoːɐ̯pɔmɐn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German). Sometimes, *Mecklenburg* is pronounced [\[ˈmɛklənbʊʁk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German), because the digraph ⟨ck⟩ marks a preceding short vowel in [High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_German). However, Mecklenburg is within the historical [Low German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_German) language area, and the ⟨c⟩ appeared in its name during the period of transition to [Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_German), High German usage (Low German authors wrote the name *Meklenborg* or *Męklenborg*, depicting proper Low German pronunciation, which itself was a [syncope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncope_\(phonetics\)) of [Middle Low German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Low_German) *Mekelenborg*). The introduction of the ⟨c⟩ is explained as follows: Either the ⟨c⟩ signals the stretched pronunciation of the preceding ⟨e⟩ (*Dehnungs-c*), or it signals the pronunciation of the subsequent ⟨k⟩ as an [occlusive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plosive) \[[k](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_velar_plosive)] to prevent it from falsely being rendered as a [fricative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fricative_consonant) \[[χ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voiceless_uvular_fricative)] following a Low German trend.[^cite_note-12] Another explanation is that the ⟨c⟩ comes from a mannerism in High German [officialese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officialese) of writing unnecessary letters, a so-called *Letternhäufelung* (lit. 'letter accumulation', as was done sometimes in English with words such as "doubt").

## History



One of more than 1000 [megalith sites in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megaliths_in_Mecklenburg-Vorpommern), the [Lancken-Granitz dolmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancken-Granitz_dolmen)

In the aftermath of the [Second World War](32927.md) and [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) in 1990, the state was constituted from the historic region of [Mecklenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg) and [Western Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Pomerania), both of which had long and rich independent histories.[^cite_note-13]

### Prehistory

See also: [Megaliths in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megaliths_in_Mecklenburg-Vorpommern)



Slavic ring fortress at [Cape Arkona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Arkona), [Rügen Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCgen)

Human settlement in the area of modern Mecklenburg and Vorpommern began after the [Ice Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period), about 10,000 BC. About two thousand years ago, [Germanic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_peoples) were recorded in the area. Most of them left during the [Migration Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Period), heading towards Spain, Italy, and France, leaving the area relatively deserted. In the sixth century [Polabian Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polabian_Slavs) populated the area. While Mecklenburg was settled by the [Obotrites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obotrites), Pomerania was settled by the [Veleti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veleti) (later *Liuticians*) and the [Rani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rani_\(Slavic_tribe\)).[^cite_note-MN-14]

Along the coast, [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking) and [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) established trade posts like [Reric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reric), [Ralswiek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralswiek) and [Menzlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altes_Lager_Menzlin). In the early 12th century, Hither Pomerania passed under Polish suzerainty under [Bolesław III Wrymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_III_Wrymouth), who initiated [Christianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Pomerania), entrusting this task to [Otto of Bamberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_of_Bamberg).[^cite_note-15] In the late 12th century, Mecklenburg and Hither Pomerania were conquered by [Henry the Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_the_Lion) and briefly incorporated into the [Duchy of Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Saxony), joining the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) in the 1180s. Parts of Mecklenburg and Pomerania were settled with [Germans](152735.md) in the *[Ostsiedlung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostsiedlung)* process, starting in the 12th century.[^cite_note-MN-14]

### Mecklenburg



Mecklenburg from 1866 to 1934, with [Mecklenburg-Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Mecklenburg-Schwerin) and the small [Mecklenburg-Strelitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Mecklenburg-Strelitz)

Main article: [Mecklenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg)

In the late 12th century, [Henry the Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_the_Lion), Duke of the Saxons, conquered the [Obotrites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obotrites), subjugated its [Nikloting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Mecklenburg) dynasty, and Christianized its people. In the course of time, German monks, nobility, peasants and traders arrived to settle here. After the 12th century, the territory remained stable and relatively independent of its neighbours; one of the few German territories for which this is true. Mecklenburg first became a [duchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy) of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) in 1348. Though later partitioned and re-partitioned within the same dynasty, Mecklenburg always shared a common history and identity. The states of [Mecklenburg-Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Mecklenburg-Schwerin) and [Mecklenburg-Strelitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Mecklenburg-Strelitz) became [Grand Duchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_duchy) in 1815, and in 1870 they voluntarily joined the new [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), while retaining their own internal autonomy. After the [First World War](4764461.md) and the [abdication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdication) of the German [Kaiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser), the monarchies of the duchies were abolished and republican governments of both Mecklenburg states were established, until the Nazi government merged the two states into a unified [state of Mecklenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Mecklenburg?action=edit\&redlink=1), a virtually meaningless administrative decision under the centralised regime.

### Western Pomerania

Main article: [History of Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Pomerania)



Late medieval [Brick Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick_Gothic) architecture in [Stralsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stralsund), nowadays a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md)

[Vorpommern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Pomerania), literally Fore-Pomerania, is the smaller, western part of the former [Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia) [Province of Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pomerania_\(1815%E2%80%931945\)); the eastern part became part of [Poland](22936.md) after the end of [World War II](32927.md).

Since the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), the area was ruled by the [Pomeranian dukes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pomeranian_duchies_and_dukes) as part of the [Duchy of Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Pomerania). The duchy was established as a [vassal state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal_state) of [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland_\(1025%E2%80%931385\)) in 1121, which it remained until 1138. Afterwards the [Dukes of Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukes_of_Pomerania) were independent, and later were vassals of [Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Saxony) from 1164 to 1181, of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) from 1181 to 1185, of [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Denmark#Middle_Ages) from 1185 to 1227 and finally, from 1227 on, staying with the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). Fore Pomerania was under [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire) rule after the [Peace of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Westphalia) from 1648 until 1815 as [Swedish Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Pomerania). It became a province of Prussia after the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) in 1815 and remained so until 1945.

### Mecklenburg-Vorpommern

#### Wartime

In May 1945, the armies of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and the Western allies met east of [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin). Following the [Potsdam Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Agreement), the Western allies handed over [Mecklenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg) to the Soviets. Mecklenburg-West Pomerania was established on 9 July 1945, by order No. 5 of [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) Marshal [Georgy Zhukov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgy_Zhukov), head of the [Soviet Military Administration in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Military_Administration_in_Germany) (SMAD), as the *Province of Mecklenburg and West Pomerania (zapadnoi Pomeranii)*.[^cite_note-16]



Division of Pomerania

During the war, the make-up of Mecklenburg and Vorpommern's population changed, due to wartime losses and the influx of evacuees (mainly from the Berlin and Hamburg metropolitan areas that were subject to air raids). After the war, people who [fled and were expelled from](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Germans_after_World_War_II) the [former eastern territories of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_eastern_territories_of_Germany) east of the [Oder-Neisse line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder-Neisse_line) settled in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern (and elsewhere in Germany), increasing the population by 40%. Before the war, [Mecklenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg) and [Western Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Pomerania) had a population of 1,278,700, of whom many perished during the war and others moved west in the course of the Red Army's advance. In 1947, some 1,426,000 refugees from the former eastern parts of Germany were counted. Most of them settled in rural communities, but the urban population also increased, most notably in [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin) from 65,000 (1939) to 99,518 (January 1947), in [Wismar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wismar) from 29,463 to 44,173, and in [Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greifswald) from 29,488 to 43,897.[^cite_note-17] Western Pomerania was additionally stripped of [the area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_County) around the Pomeranian regional capital Stettin/Szczecin as well as the city itself, despite its location to the west of the river Oder.

#### German Democratic Republic



[Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock) was the major overseas port of [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany), and is one of the most important [Baltic Sea](3335.md) ports today. Pictured is [Hanse Sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanse_Sail), one of the world's largest maritime events.

On 5 June 1946, a law enacted by the Soviets constituted a provisional German administration (*Beratende Versammlung*, English: "Consulting assembly") under Soviet supervision on 29 June 1946. After elections on 20 October 1946, a *[Landtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag)* replaced the *Beratende Versammlung* and created the constitution of 16 January 1947, for the *[Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) Mecklenburg-Vorpommern*. On 18 April 1947, the state's name was shortened to *Land Mecklenburg*. Mecklenburg became a constituent state ("*Land*") of the [German Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) (GDR) upon its formation in 1949.

Among the states of the GDR, Mecklenburg absorbed by far the largest share of [German expellees from Central and Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_and_expulsion_of_Germans_\(1944%E2%80%931950\)) relative to its population size.[^cite_note-amos15-18] The vast majority of expellees from [Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pomerania_\(1815%E2%80%931945\)) who arrived in the GDR settled in Mecklenburg.[^cite_note-amos16-19] In October 1949, expellees made up 43.3% of the population.[^cite_note-amos15-18]

In 1952, the East German government abandoned the term *Land* in this context and redesignated its administrative territorial divisions as "districts" (German: *[Bezirke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division_of_the_German_Democratic_Republic#Division_into_Bezirke#Division_into_Bezirke)*). The territory of Mecklenburg and Vorpommern was divided into three districts that covered roughly the same area: *[Bezirk Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock_\(Bezirk\))*, *[Bezirk Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin_\(Bezirk\))* and *[Bezirk Neubrandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neubrandenburg_\(Bezirk\))*. These were commonly known as the *Nordbezirke* (northern districts) under the highly centralised GDR government. The East German government developed the [shipyards](213654.md) in the old [Hanseatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) ports (the largest being in [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock) and [Stralsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stralsund)), and also established the [Greifswald Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greifswald_Nuclear_Power_Plant) in [Lubmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubmin) near [Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greifswald).

#### Reunification

At the time of [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) in 1990, the eastern states were reconstituted along their postwar boundaries (with minor adjustments) as they had existed until 1952, and the historic name Mecklenburg-Vorpommern was restored. Since 1990, the state has undergone dramatic changes. What had been largely an industrial and agricultural economy is increasingly driven by the [service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy), [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Germany), and [high-tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_tech) sectors. The [old towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Mecklenburg-Vorpommern), [hundreds of castles and manors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_castles_and_palaces_in_Mecklenburg-Vorpommern), [resort buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort_architecture), [windmills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_windmills_in_Mecklenburg-Vorpommern), churches, and various other cultural monuments of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern have been renovated in recent years. Since 2013, net migration into the state has been positive again.

Sixth-largest in area and fourteenth in overall population among Germany's sixteen [Bundesländer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) (federal states), Mecklenburg-Vorpommern is bounded to the north by the [Baltic Sea](3335.md), to the west by [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md), to the southwest by [Lower Saxony](18435.md), to the south by [Brandenburg](3765.md), and to the east by the [West Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pomeranian_Voivodeship) in Poland.

Mecklenburg-Vorpommern's state capital is [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin). The largest city is [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock) with approximately 205,000 people, followed by [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin). Other major cities include [Neubrandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neubrandenburg), [Stralsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stralsund), [Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greifswald), [Wismar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wismar) and [Güstrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCstrow).

### Landscape

The state's [Baltic Sea](3335.md) coast is about 2,000 km (1,200 mi) long and features several islands, most notably Germany's two biggest islands [Rügen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCgen) and [Usedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usedom), but also a number of smaller islands such as [Hiddensee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiddensee) and [Poel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poel). Mecklenburg-Vorpommern's varied coastline also has many peninsulas such as [Fischland-Darß-Zingst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischland-Dar%C3%9F-Zingst) and various [lagoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagoons) (also known as *Bodden* or *Haff*).

A total of 283 nature reserves, 110 landscape reserves and three of Germany's fourteen [national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Germany) are scattered all over the state.

#### Lakes

See also: [List of lakes in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lakes_in_Mecklenburg-Vorpommern)

The southern part of the state is characterized by a multitude of lakes within the [Mecklenburg Lakeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg_Lake_District), the largest of which is Lake [Müritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCritz) (also the largest [German lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lakes_of_Germany)), followed by [Lake Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Schwerin), [Plauer See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plauer_See_\(Mecklenburg-Vorpommern\)) and [Lake Kummerow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Kummerow). The "land of a thousand lakes" (German: *Land der tausend Seen*) is hallmarked by its unspoilt nature. Due to its clean air and idyllic setting, [medical tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_tourism) has become a notable tourism sector in the region.[^cite_note-Welt-20]

## Culture

Over the centuries, Mecklenburg and Vorpommern have developed and maintained strong regional cultures. It can generally be described as [North German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Germany) and has similar linguistic and historic characteristics to other north German states, such as [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md). People in Vorpommern, as a result of that territory being a former province of [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia), tend to look slightly more towards [Berlin](3354.md) and [Brandenburg](3765.md) than people in Mecklenburg would.

### Architecture

The cities are characterised by a certain "[Hanseatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League)" style also found in other parts of northern Germany (e.g. [Lübeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCbeck)) as well as in countries bordering the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) like [Estonia](28222445.md) (e.g. [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn)) or [Latvia](17514.md) (e.g. [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga)). A common feature of many towns in Mecklenburg and Vorpommern are red [Brick Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick_Gothic) churches and houses dating back to the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages). Also [stepped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crow-stepped_gable) and [tailed gables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_gable) are a typical feature of the [Hanseatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) old towns, such as [Stralsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stralsund), [Wismar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wismar) and [Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greifswald).

The old towns are usually built around one or several market places with a church or the town hall. Often towns were founded at the [Baltic Sea](3335.md), one of the many [lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg_Lake_District) or a river for logistical and trade motives.

Rural areas of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern are often characterized by Brick Gothic village [churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Churches_in_Mecklenburg-Vorpommern) and [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural) heritage, like brick [homesteads](2752506.md), [thatched roof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thatched_roof) houses, [windmills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_windmills_in_Mecklenburg-Vorpommern), [manor houses and castles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_castles_and_palaces_in_Mecklenburg-Vorpommern).

### Museums, art and theatres

See also: [List of museums in Germany § Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Germany#Mecklenburg-Vorpommern)



The [Mecklenburg State Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburgisches_Staatstheater_Schwerin) in [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin)



The [Ozeaneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozeaneum) in [Stralsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stralsund), Europe's museum of the year 2010 and Northern Germany's most popular museum (as part of the [German Oceanographic Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Oceanographic_Museum))

The largest publicly funded theatres in the state are the [Mecklenburg State Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg_State_Theatre), the [Rostock People's Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock_People's_Theatre), the [Theatre of West Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_West_Pomerania), with venues in [Stralsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stralsund), [Putbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putbus) and [Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greifswald), and the Mecklenburg State Theatre of Neustrelitz with venues in [Neubrandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neubrandenburg) and [Neustrelitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neustrelitz). All four theatres offer both drama and musical theatre as well as orchestral music. Other important theatres are the Ernst Barlach Theatre of [Güstrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCstrow), the Theatre of [Parchim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parchim), the [Anklam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anklam) Theatre and the [Wismar Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wismar_Theatre). There are also many small theatres on the Baltic coast and in individual artist's villages and resorts (e.g. the popular concert pavilions at the [Baltic Sea](3335.md)). Since its growing importance for summer tourism, [open-air theatres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-air_theatre) and festivals become more common again as well, such as the [Störtebeker Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%B6rtebeker_Festival) on the island of [Rügen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCgen), and the *Vineta Festival* on [Usedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usedom).

Since 1993, the [Störtebeker Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%B6rtebeker_Festival) has taken place in [Ralswiek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralswiek) on the island of Rügen. It is Germany's most successful open-air theatre.

Notable museums include, for example, the [Schwerin State Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatliches_Museum_Schwerin) and the [Pomeranian State Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeranian_State_Museum) at Greifswald. The [German Oceanographic Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Oceanographic_Museum) with its [Ozeaneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozeaneum) in Stralsund is the most popular museum in northern Germany. Furthermore, the [German Amber Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Amber_Museum?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Ribnitz-Damgarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribnitz-Damgarten), Rostock's [Abbey of the Holy Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_of_the_Holy_Cross,_Rostock) and [Rostock Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock_Art_Gallery) are of national importance. The oldest museum in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania is Stralsund's [Cultural History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Cultural_History,_Stralsund?action=edit\&redlink=1), the smallest is the [Professor Wandschneider Sculpture Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Wandschneider) in Plau am See.

Mecklenburg-Vorpommern is home to many cultural events throughout the year. During summer, many open-air concerts and operas are open to visitors. The Mecklenburg-Vorpommern Festival (*Festspiele Mecklenburg-Vorpommern*) attracts a sizeable audience by performing classical concerts in parks, churches and castles.

[Caspar David Friedrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspar_David_Friedrich), a famous [romanticist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism) [painter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painting) born in [Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greifswald), immortalised parts of the state in several of his paintings.

### Language



Low German dialects

Today the vast majority of people speak [Standard German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_German); a few centuries ago, most people spoke [Low German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_German) (German: *Plattdeutsch or Niederdeutsch*), a language that is still kept alive within various communities and cultural events.

### Food and drinks

See also: [Pomeranian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeranian_cuisine)

Like most German regions, Mecklenburg and Vorpommern have their own traditional dishes, often including fish, beef and pork. Rostock has its own type of [bratwurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratwurst) called *Rostocker Bratwurst*. A famous dish from [Western Pomerania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Pomerania) is [Bismarck Herring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickled_herring). [Rote Grütze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B8dgr%C3%B8d) is a popular dessert. The largest [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_beer) breweries are *[Mecklenburgische Brauerei Lübz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holsten_Brewery)* ([Lübzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCbz) Pils), *Hanseatische Brauerei [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock)*, *[Darguner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dargun) Brauerei* and *Störtebeker Braumanufaktur* ([Stralsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stralsund), multiple winner of the [World Beer Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Beer_Cup)). Besides, there are many smaller breweries and [craft beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craft_beer) variations, such as the *[Mellenthin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellenthin) Castle Beer* from [Usedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usedom) Island.

As of 2020, the majority (82.4%) of the citizens of Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania are not religious or adhere to other religions. 14.2% are members of the [Protestant Church in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Church_in_Germany) and 3.4% of the [Catholic Church](606848.md).[^cite_note-22]

Following the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation), led in Germany by [Martin Luther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther), as well as a period of [Swedish](5058739.md) rule, the traditional faith in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern is [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), specifically [Lutheranism](23371382.md). There are also a number of [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) and people of other faiths.

In May 2012 the [Evangelical Lutheran Church of Mecklenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_of_Mecklenburg) merged with [North Elbian Evangelical Lutheran Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Elbian_Evangelical_Lutheran_Church) and [Pomeranian Evangelical Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeranian_Evangelical_Church) into the new [Evangelical Lutheran Church in Northern Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran_Church_in_Northern_Germany).[^cite_note-23] Some parishes of the state belong to [Evangelical Church of Berlin and Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Church_Berlin-Brandenburg-Silesian_Upper_Lusatia).

There are also [Jewish](15624.md) communities, in the state capital of [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin) (including [Wismar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wismar)) and in the city of [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock). Historically, there were also synagogues in smaller towns, of which some are still preserved (like [Röbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6bel), [Krakow am See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krakow_am_See) and [Boizenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boizenburg)). The state's Jewish organisation is part of the [Central Council of Jews in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Council_of_Jews_in_Germany).

### Schools

The state's school system is centralised. There are two main types of schools, *Regionalschule* (for the majority of pupils) and *Gymnasium* (for the top 30% of each year's students, leading to the university entrance qualification "[Abitur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abitur)"). Besides, there are also independent schools, comprehensive schools and trade schools.

## Politics

Article 20 of the State Constitution states that the [Landtag](17706695.md) is the "site of political decision-making".[^cite_note-State_Landtag-33] The Mecklenburg-Vorpommern Landtag is elected democratically by the citizens of the state and sits for a 5-year legislative period.[^cite_note-State_Landtag-33] The seat of the Landtag is located at [Schwerin Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin_Palace) in [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin).[^cite_note-State_Landtag-33] The essential functions of the Landtag are to elect the [Minister-President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister-President) of the state; to discuss and decide on laws which have been proposed by the government, by any four members of the Landtag, or a people's initiative or petition for a referendum initiated directly by the people; and to control the state government.[^cite_note-State_Landtag-33]

### Minister-President

See also: [2026 Mecklenburg-Vorpommern state election](70944924.md)



The [Schwerin Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin_Palace), seat of the [Landtag of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](17706695.md), is one of more than 2,000 [palaces and castles in the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_castles_and_palaces_in_Mecklenburg-Vorpommern).

The last election of the [Landtag of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](17706695.md) took place on 21 September 2026. The [Alternative for Germany](38708229.md) (AfD) more than doubled its vote share to 38%, though is not expected to form a government due to the [firewall against the far-right policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firewall_against_the_far-right_in_Germany). The governing SPD suffered only minor losses, dropping to 35% and second place. All of the other four represented parties dropped close to or under the 5% threshold for representation, with the co-governing [The Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Linke) and the [Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_90/The_Greens) winning 5 seats each and likely joining a [red–red–green coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red%E2%80%93red%E2%80%93green_coalition).&#x20;

While the FDP was expected to [lose representation also in this state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party#State_parliaments_\(L%C3%A4nder\)), the [Christian Democratic Union of Germany](60584.md) (CDU) unexpectedly was reported close to 5% in exit pools. Soon, federal CDU leader and chancellor [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md) called it a disaster and vowed to stay on.[^cite_note-34] A few hours later, CDU was confirmed at 4.9% and failed for the first time ever to win seats in a German state election, adding to the [2026 Merz government crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Merz_government_crisis). The [Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahra_Wagenknecht_Alliance) (BSW) also remained below 5%, as it did in Berlin.

The Landtag has been led by Minister President [Manuela Schwesig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuela_Schwesig) since 2017.

## Economy

The [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of the state was 44.5 billion euros in 2018, accounting for 1.3% of German economic output. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was 25,400 euros or 84% of the EU27 average in the same year. The GDP per employee was 83% of the EU average. The GDP per capita was the lowest of all German states.[^cite_note-35]

### Labour market

Mecklenburg-Vorpommern is the least densely populated and least industrial [German state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany), being the sixth largest in area, but only the 14th in population. Formerly, unemployment has been negatively affected by the breakdown of non-competitive former [GDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDR) industries after the [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) in the 1990s. As of 2018 unemployment is the lowest in more than 15 years while the economy is growing and the number of jobs is increasing continually. Growing sectors are [biotechnology](4502.md), [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology), [life sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_sciences), [maritime industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_industry) and [tourist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Germany) services.

In Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, approximately 732,200 people were gainfully employed in 2008 with 657,100 of them were white and blue collar workers. About 4,200 new jobs were created in 2007. Employees worked an average of 1,455 hours a year. The number of self-employed did not change in 2008. Three out of every four of all workers are employed in the service sector.[^cite_note-Employment_and_Labour_Market-36] In October 2018 the unemployment rate stood at 7.1% and was the third highest rate in Germany.[^cite_note-37]

### Tourism

Mecklenburg-Vorpommern is the top destination for intra-German tourism and is gaining importance for [international tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_tourism). The main tourist regions are:

- Islands: [Rugia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCgen) and [Usedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usedom) (Germany's two largest islands), [Poel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poel) and [Hiddensee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiddensee);

- Peninsula: [Fischland-Darß-Zingst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fischland-Dar%C3%9F-Zingst) (with [Ahrenshoop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrenshoop) and [Zingst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zingst));

- Seaside resorts: [Binz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binz), [Boltenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltenhagen), [Graal-Müritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graal-M%C3%BCritz), [Heringsdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heringsdorf) (including [Bansin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bansin) and [Ahlbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahlbeck)), [Heiligendamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiligendamm), [Kühlungsborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BChlungsborn), [Warnemünde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warnem%C3%BCnde) and [Zinnowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinnowitz);

- [Stettin Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szczecin_Lagoon): [Ueckermünde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ueckerm%C3%BCnde);

- Hinterland: particularly the [Mecklenburg Lakeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg_Lake_District);

- Cities: [Stralsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stralsund) and [Wismar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wismar) (both listed as [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Sites](44940.md)), [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin), [Güstrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCstrow), [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock) and [Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greifswald), which have a diverse cultural heritage.

- Night sky: Mecklenburg-Vorpommern is home to some of the most [pristine night skies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyglow) in Germany, especially in the area near Nationalpark Müritz. It is a potential site for a [Dark Sky Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark-sky_preserve).

As a reminder of its rich history, nearly 2,000 castles, palaces and [manor houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manor_house) exist in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, many of which are used as venues for public events like concerts and festivals, such as Festspiele MV[^cite_note-39] (a classical music festival).

[Medical tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_tourism)[^cite_note-Welt-20] based on the clean air and idyllic settings by the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) has a growing importance to the regional tourism industry.[^cite_note-Welt-20]

## Transport



Airports in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania

There are two airports that are currently operating in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern which are [Rostock–Laage Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock%E2%80%93Laage_Airport) and [Heringsdorf Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heringsdorf_Airport). However, residents of the state also use [Berlin Brandenburg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Brandenburg_Airport) and [Hamburg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburg_Airport), which both can be easily reached by train and car.

## Sport

Main sporting attractions include the [German football league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) games of [F.C. Hansa Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F.C._Hansa_Rostock) and the international sailing event [Hanse Sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanse_Sail). If the bid for the 2012 summer Olympics in [Leipzig](17955.md) had been successful, the sailing competitions would have taken place off the coast of [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock).[^cite_note-40]

## Miscellaneous

- Mecklenburg-Vorpommern is Germany's number-one tourist location, the main destinations being the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) coastline with islands such as [Rügen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCgen) or [Usedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usedom), spa towns like [Heiligendamm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiligendamm), [Kühlungsborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BChlungsborn), [Boltenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltenhagen) or [Warnemünde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warnem%C3%BCnde) and the [Mecklenburg Lake District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg_Lake_District). It also offers important historical cities, such as [Stralsund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stralsund), [Wismar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wismar), [Greifswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greifswald) and [Rostock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostock) as former [Hanseatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_league) cities − or [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin), [Güstrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCstrow), [Ludwigslust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwigslust) and [Neustrelitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neustrelitz) as former residences.

- The first [V2 Rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V2_Rocket) which was the first explosive missile was launched in 1944 during World War II in [Peenemünde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peenem%C3%BCnde) on the present-day territory of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern.
