[Hungary](13275.md) wields considerable influence in [Central and Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_and_Eastern_Europe) and is a [middle power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_power) in international affairs.[^cite_note-Solomon-1][^cite_note-Higott-Cooper-2] The [foreign policy](233404.md) of Hungary includes commitments to [international development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_development), [international law](8195726.md), [European integration](683704.md), [Atlantic co-operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanticism) and increased co-operation within the [Global East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_East).[^cite_note-\:6-3] The [Hungarian economy](13428.md) is fairly open and relies strongly on [international trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade).

Hungary has been a member of the [United Nations](31769.md) since December 1955 and holds current membership with the [European Union](9317.md), [NATO](21133.md), the [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development), the [Visegrád Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visegr%C3%A1d_Group), the [WTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTO), the [World Bank](36752.md), the [AIIB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Infrastructure_Investment_Bank) and the [IMF](15251.md). Hungary took on the presidency of the [Council of the European Union](5866.md) for half a year in 2011 and the next will be in 2024. In 2015, Hungary was the fifth largest [OECD](33853117.md) Non-[DAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_Assistance_Committee) donor of [development aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_aid) in the world, which represents [0.13% of its Gross National Income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_development_aid_country_donors). In this regard, Hungary stands before [Spain](26667.md), [Israel](9282173.md) or [Russia](25391.md).

Hungary's capital city, [Budapest](36787.md) is home to more than 100 [embassies](8970.md) and foreign representative bodies.[^cite_note-4] Hungary hosts the main and regional headquarters of many international organizations as well, including [European Institute of Innovation and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Institute_of_Innovation_and_Technology), [European Police College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Police_College), [United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees](31964.md), [Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization_of_the_United_Nations), [International Centre for Democratic Transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_Democratic_Transition), [Institute of International Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_International_Education), [International Labour Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Labour_Organization), [International Organization for Migration](36893.md), [International Red Cross](9720597.md), Regional Environmental Center for Central and Eastern Europe, [Danube Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Commission_\(1948\)) and even others.[^cite_note-5]

From 1989, Hungary's top foreign policy goal was achieving integration into Western economic and security organizations. It joined the [Partnership for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_for_Peace) program in 1994 and has actively supported the [IFOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFOR) and [SFOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SFOR) missions in Bosnia. It also improved its often frosty neighborly relations by signing basic treaties with [Ukraine](31750.md), [Slovakia](26830.md), and [Romania](25445.md). These renounce all outstanding territorial claims and lay the foundation for constructive relations. However, the issue of [ethnic Hungarian minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_diaspora) rights in Romania, Slovakia and Ukraine periodically causes bilateral tensions to flare up. Hungary since 1989 has signed all of the [OSCE](22796.md) documents, and served as the OSCE's Chairman-in-Office in 1997. Hungary's record of implementing [CSCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Security_and_Cooperation_in_Europe) *Helsinki Final Act* provisions, including those on the reunification of divided families, remains among the best in Central and Eastern Europe.

Except for the short-lived [neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_country) declared by the [anti-Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Soviet) leader [Imre Nagy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imre_Nagy) in November 1956, [Hungary](13275.md)'s foreign policy generally followed the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) lead from 1947 to 1989. During the [Communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) period, Hungary maintained treaties of friendship, cooperation, and mutual assistance with the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Poland), [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), the [German Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Democratic_Republic), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Romania), and [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Bulgaria). It was one of the founding members of the Soviet-led [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact) and [Comecon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comecon), and it was the first country to withdraw from those organizations. After 1989, it began to orient more towards the West, joining [NATO](21133.md) in 1999 and the [European Union](9317.md) in 2004. In 2010, Hungary initiated its Eastern Opening Policy, marking a commitment to enhancing its relations with other nations within the Global East.[^cite_note-\:6-3]

## Overview

As with any country, Hungarian security attitudes are shaped largely by history and geography. For Hungary, this is a history of more than 400 years of domination by great powers—the [Ottomans](22278.md), the [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg) dynasty, the [Germans](21212.md) during [World War II](32927.md), and the Soviets during the [Cold War](325329.md)—and a geography of regional instability and separation from Hungarian minorities living in neighboring countries. Hungary's foreign policy priorities, largely consistent since 1990, represent a direct response to these factors. From 1990, Hungary's top foreign policy goal was achieving integration into Western economic and security organizations. Hungary joined the [Partnership for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_for_Peace) program in 1994 and has actively supported the [IFOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFOR) and [SFOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SFOR) missions in [Bosnia](3463.md). The [Horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyula_Horn) government achieved Hungary's most important foreign policy successes of the post-communist era by securing invitations to join both [NATO](21133.md) and the [European Union](9317.md) in 1997. Hungary became a member of NATO in 1999,[^cite_note-6] and a member of the [EU](9317.md) in 2004.[^cite_note-7]

Hungary also has improved its often frosty neighborly relations by signing basic treaties with [Romania](25445.md), [Slovakia](26830.md), and [Ukraine](31750.md). These renounce all outstanding territorial claims and lay the foundation for constructive relations. However, the issue of ethnic Hungarian minority rights in Slovakia and Romania periodically causes bilateral tensions to flare up. Hungary was a signatory to the [Helsinki Final Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_Final_Act) in 1975, has signed all of the CSCE/[OSCE](22796.md) follow-on documents since 1989, and served as the OSCE's Chairman-in-Office in 1997. Hungary's record of implementing CSCE **Helsinki Final Act** provisions, including those on the reunification of divided families, remains among the best in eastern Europe. Hungary has been a member of the [United Nations](31769.md) since December 1955.

The [Gabčíkovo - Nagymaros Dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gab%C4%8D%C3%ADkovo_-_Nagymaros_Dams) project

This involves Hungary and Czechoslovakia, and was agreed on September 16, 1977 ("Budapest Treaty"). The treaty envisioned a [cross-border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-border) barrage system between the towns Gabčíkovo, [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) and Nagymaros, Hungary. After an intensive campaign, the project became widely hated as a symbol of the old [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist) regime. In 1989 the [Hungarian](13275.md) government decided to suspend it. In its sentence from September 1997, the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) stated that both sides breached their obligation and that the 1977 Budapest Treaty is still valid. In 1998 the Slovak government turned to the International Court, demanding the Nagymaros part to be built. The international dispute was partially solved in 2017.

On March 19, 2008, Hungary recognized [Kosovo](17391.md) as an independent country.[^cite_note-8]

Relations between Hungary and its Western partners have strained, because Orban's government has maintained relations with Russia despite [sanctions against Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_against_Russia_for_the_invasion_of_Ukraine) after the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-9]

**Illicit drugs:** Major trans-shipment point for [Southwest Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Asia) [heroin](14034.md) and [cannabis](1481886.md) and transit point for [South American](26769.md) [cocaine](7701.md) destined for [Western Europe](33800.md); limited producer of precursor [chemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_compound), particularly for [amphetamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphetamine) and [methamphetamines](11038318.md)

**Refugee protection:** The [Hungarian border barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_border_barrier) was built in 2015, and Hungary was criticized by other European countries for using tear gas and water cannons on [refugees of the Syrian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_of_the_Syrian_Civil_War) as they were trying to pass the country.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

Since 2017, [Hungary–Ukraine relations](20544439.md) have rapidly deteriorated over the issue of the [Hungarian minority in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarians_in_Ukraine).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

The U.S. election on 5 November 2024 resulted in a landslide Republican victory. Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán loudly and consistently proclaimed that he not only expected Trump to take back the presidency but heartfully wished him to do so. Thanks to Orbán's unwavering support towards Trump, the personal relationship between the two statesmen has become impeccable, especially during the Biden administration when U.S.-Hungarian relations hit rock bottom.[^cite_note-14]

## Hungary and Turkic States



Viktor Orbán at the 8th Summit of the Organization of Turkic States

A number of Hungarian anthropologists and linguists have long had an interest in the [Turkic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples), fueled by the eastern origin of the Hungarians' ancestors.[^cite_note-15] The Hungarian ethnomusicologist [Bence Szabolcsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bence_Szabolcsi) explained this motivation as follows: "Hungarians are the outermost branch leaning this way from age-old tree of the great Asian musical culture rooted in the souls of a variety of peoples living from China through Central Asia to the Black Sea".[^cite_note-16]

Since the political transformation in 1990, Hungary has prioritized its diplomatic engagements with the Turkic world.[^cite_note-17] In 2018, Hungary became an observer within the [Organisation of Turkic States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_Turkic_States), underscoring a deepened engagement in regional cooperation.[^cite_note-18] Today, [Türkiye](11125639.md) and [Azerbaijan](746.md) constitute two of Hungary’s key strategic partners.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

## Foreign criticism

In December 2010, the [Fidesz](404647.md) government adopted a press and media law which threatens fines on media that engage in "unbalanced coverage".[^cite_note-ifla-258] The law aroused criticism in the [European Union](9317.md) as possibly "a direct threat to democracy".[^cite_note-ifla-258]

In 2013, the government adopted a [new constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Constitution) that modified several aspects of the institutional and legal framework in Hungary. These changes have been criticized by the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe), the [European Union](9317.md) and [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) as possibly undermining the rule of law and human rights protection.[^cite_note-259]
