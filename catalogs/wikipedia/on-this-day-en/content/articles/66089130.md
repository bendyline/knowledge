Fulani people

**Fulani extremism** refers to the use of [terrorism tactics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactics_of_terrorism) by [Islamic extremist](8734632.md) elements within the [Fula people](102383.md) against non-Muslims as part of the [Herder–farmer conflicts in Nigeria](54964174.md). Unlike other manifestations of [Islamic extremism in Northern Nigeria](42571861.md), the terror tactics used in these conflicts have not been orchestrated by an organised terrorist group.[^cite_note-\:0-1] [Nigeria](21383.md) is considered a “melting pot” of different cultural and ethnic groups. Ethnic identification in the country is a complicated [amalgamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalgamation_\(names\)) of [primordial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primordialism) and [constructivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_constructivism) approaches.[^cite_note-\:4-2]

## Background

The number of Fulani in Nigeria is estimated to be around 13 million.[^cite_note-\:0-1] The primary ethnic groups that the Fulani come into conflict with are the [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_culture) and the [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_people), although a total of 33 known groups participate in the [herder–farmer conflicts](54964174.md) in the country.[^cite_note-\:4-2][^cite_note-3]

## General history

The Fulani are largely [nomadic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad)/semi-nomadic group of approximately 100 million individuals who live in the [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) of [West Africa](67393.md).[^cite_note-\:0-1] The Fulani are a pastoralist group and their livelihood depends on herding cattle, and occasionally goats and sheep, along grazing routes.[^cite_note-4] In recent years, as [climate change](5042951.md) has brought about increased [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification) and a scarcity of resources, herder-farmer conflicts have increased in frequency.[^cite_note-5] As Fulani nomads move southward into more fertile lands, there has been greater competition for grazing routes with local farmers, prompting violence.[^cite_note-6]

While there are other kinds of [herder-farmer conflicts in Nigeria](54964174.md), Herder-Farmer conflicts have been categorized as frequent conflicts and tactics are used to settle the disputes.[^cite_note-\:0-1] In some parts of [Africa](5334607.md), such as in [Mali](19127.md), formal terrorist groups have been established. The [Macina Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macina_Liberation_Front), or the Front de Libération du Macina (FLM) in Mali is an official jihadist group that has become intertwined with the [Fulani pastoralists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulani_pastoralists).[^cite_note-\:5-7] While there is currently no formal organization in Nigeria, terrorist tactics are still common.[^cite_note-\:0-1] These tactics include, but are not limited to, destroying crops, deadly riots, blocking traffic, raping women, beating up farmers, and instigating armed attacks on villages.[^cite_note-\:6-8] The battle for fertile farming land and grazing routes has resulted in a significant amount of violence. These crises also occur throughout [Guinea](67393.md), [Senegal](27278.md), [Mali](19127.md), and [Cameroon](5447.md).[^cite_note-\:0-1] The religious conflicts arise with other non-[Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) tribes, even against other Fulani, such as the [Fula Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_Christians).[^cite_note-9]

While the specific details of some Fulani migration into Nigeria are largely unknown, it is generally assumed that some of the Fulani moved into Northern Nigeria from the [Senegambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegambia) region in the nineteenth century.[^cite_note-\:2-10] Since this initial migration, the Fulani have come into conflict with farmers in Nigeria.[^cite_note-11] The Herder-Farmer conflicts are most prominent in the [Kaduna](2227637.md), [Plateau](1723458.md), and [Benue states](1556364.md).[^cite_note-\:0-1]

Over the course of several centuries, these conflicts have fluctuated in intensity based on a variety of social, political, economic, and environmental factors.[^cite_note-12] Specifically, droughts, erratic rainfall, and the degradation of land in Nigeria have intensified the conflict.[^cite_note-\:0-1] The Sultan of Sokoto has emphasized that "not all Fulani herdsmen engage in terrorism" noting that while some bandits exist within the community, this is not representative of the whole group.[^cite_note-13]

## Statistics

- In 2018, the Herder-Farmer conflicts were responsible for 72% of deaths in Nigeria. The total death toll was 1,159 deaths.

  [^cite_note-\:0-1]

- In 2019, just one year later, Herder-Farmer conflicts were responsible for only 26% of deaths in Nigeria, accounting for 325 deaths.

  [^cite_note-\:0-1]

- Between the years 2010 and 2016, these conflicts were responsible for 3,068 deaths across several West-African countries.

  [^cite_note-\:0-1]

- In 2014, more than 1,200 people lost their lives. This made the Herder-Farmer conflicts the world's fourth deadliest conflicts group.

  [^cite_note-18]

### Nigerian Grazing Reserve Act of 1964

In 1964, the government passed this act, hoping that it would encourage the Fulani to adopt sedentary lifestyles and graze on these reserved lands.[^cite_note-\:3-19][^cite_note-20]

### Land Use Act of 1978

In 1978, the government implemented the Land Use Act. This piece of [legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislation) empowered the federal government to allocate land to different groups. Additionally, indigenous groups were granted the right to claim ownership of ancestral territories. The passage of the Land Use Act exacerbated the Fulani-farmer conflict, as the nomadic Fulani were largely excluded from the right to claim ownership of ancestral land.[^cite_note-\:2-10]

### Nigerian Agricultural Policy of 1988

In an attempt to resolve the issues presented by the Land Use Act, the government has demarcated specific grazing reserves with the Nigerian Agricultural Policy. This law set aside a minimum 10% of the total territory of the country to be reserved for [grazing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazing). To date, this mandate has not been enforced to its fullest capacity.[^cite_note-\:3-19]

## Effects of conflict

### Reduced crop yield

The constant conflict between the pastoralists and the farmers has had a negative effect on both the herder and the farmer output. Both indiscriminately destroy the crops and the cattle negatively affecting agricultural production.[^cite_note-\:7-21]

### Displacement of farmers

Both the Farmers and the Herders are displaced by this conflict, exacerbating poverty and disorder in the agricultural regions.[^cite_note-\:7-21]
