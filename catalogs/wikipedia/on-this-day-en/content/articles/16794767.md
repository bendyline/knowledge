**JSC Ukrposhta** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Укрпошта) is the national postal service of [Ukraine](31750.md) with 100% state ownership, which provides universal postal, logistics, financial and courier services throughout the country. Located in the [Central Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Post_Office_\(Kyiv\)) in the center of [Kyiv](585629.md) on [Khreshchatyk street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khreshchatyk) in the [Maidan Nezalezhnosti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maidan_Nezalezhnosti), Ukrposhta includes 24 regional branches, the Mail Processing and Transportation Directorate and the Autotransposhta Directorate, 4,273 stationary and 1,867 mobile post offices, which serve 20,204 settlements in Ukraine. From 1999–2015 it was a [unitary enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_enterprise) of the [government of Ukraine](1372585.md). Ukrposhta has been a member of [Universal Postal Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Postal_Union) since 1947.

Ukrposhta operated within the [Soviet Ministry of Telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Communications_\(Soviet_Union\)) (Administration of Postal Communication) as its republican branch on territory of the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) and was centered in [Moscow](19004.md). The Ukrainian SSR did not have a separate system of telecommunications and was completely integrated within the Soviet system of telecommunications. As part of the Gorbachev reforms ([perestroika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perestroika) and decentralization, see [Union of Sovereign States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Union_Treaty)), in 1991, [Ukrtelecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrtelecom) was created in [Soviet Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) which took over administration of all means of communication such as postal and signal.

In 1994 Ukrposhta started to operate as a separate business entity following the restructuring of Ukrtelecom, after which Ukrposhta has been providing [postal service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_service), while Ukrtelecom has been providing telephone and telegraph services.

In July 1998, Ukrposhta was reformed again on a [government](1372585.md) request.

It is currently managed by the [Ministry of Infrastructure of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Infrastructure_\(Ukraine\)) and the [Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine](1372585.md) decrees.

Ukrposhta's activities are regulated by the [Law of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Ukraine) "On Postal Service" (in force since 4 October 2001) and other laws including regulations of the [UPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Postal_Union).

In 2022, JSC Ukrposhta directed over 150 million UAH to humanitarian and charitable needs: support for business relocation and business support, delivery of important humanitarian cargoes (over a thousand 20-ton trucks), joint with [Ukrzaliznytsia](3149341.md) payments to passengers of evacuation trains, transferring [AFU](42799055.md) equipment, military gear, generators, medications, etc., supporting educational projects and helping animals.[^cite_note-8]

In July 2023, the [European Bank for Reconstruction and Development (EBRD)](37963.md) reallocated part of a previously allocated loan to enhance the operational resilience of "Ukrposhta." €22.7 million will go towards the purchase of over 5000 electric bicycles and about 350 trucks.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

In April 2024, it was reported that Ukrposhta is selling 531 units of outdated transport — cars, trucks, trailers, semi-trailers, tractors — on the [ProZorro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prozorro) platform.[^cite_note-11]

On May 9, 2024, the [Verkhovna Rada of Ukraine](844993.md) supported bill No. 8423, which allows Ukrposhta's employees to carry and use [firearms](11966.md) for the protection of postal funds and facilities from attacks.[^cite_note-12]

## Impact of events in 2014

Main article: [2014 Russian annexation of Crimea](42228673.md)

The events of 2014 narrowed the scope of the company's geographical activities: in April of 2014, Ukrposhta ceased operations in Crimea and [Sevastopol](51587.md), and in December left the territories of the unrecognized [DPR](42425747.md) and [LPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_People's_Republic). On the basis of its infrastructure in these regions, the “Post of Crimea” (from April 21), “Post of Donbass” (from December 2) and “LNR Post” appeared respectively. For reference, as of the beginning of 2014, Ukrposhta had 553 post offices and 221 vehicles to transport mail to Crimea. About 4.7 thousand people, or about 5% of its workforce, were involved in the directorates of Ukrposhta on the peninsula.

Starting from March 27, 2014, postal items sent by Ukrposhta from the mainland of Ukraine are not accepted by the postal service of Crimea and Sevastopol and are returned. Under these circumstances, Ukrposhta is not able to forward postal items to the Crimean Peninsula. Given the current situation, Ukrposhta has suspended the acceptance of postal items to Crimea.

## Violation

Based on the supervisory actions of the [National Bank of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Ukraine) regarding the activities of Ukrposhta in 2023–2026, during which the NBU issued several written warnings and fines against Ukrposhta for violations of payment services, financial monitoring, and deficiencies in risk management and internal control, on June 23, 2026, the Committee on Supervision and Regulation of Banking Activities, Supervision of Payment Systems of the NBU ruled that the General Director of Ukrposhta, Ihor Smilyansky, does not meet the professional suitability requirements established for the head of a financial payment services provider, and decided to remove him from his duties and appoint a new head.[^cite_note-13]
