**Velyka Pysarivka** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Велика Писарівка [\[ʋeˈɫɪkɐ ˈpɪsɐrʲiu̯kɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian); [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Великая Писаревка) is a [rural settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populated_places_in_Ukraine#Rural_settlements) in [Sumy Oblast](1567618.md), northeastern [Ukraine](31750.md). It was formerly the administrative center of [Velyka Pysarivka Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velyka_Pysarivka_Raion), but is now administered within [Okhtyrka Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okhtyrka_Raion). It is located close to the border with Russia, on the left bank of the [Vorskla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorskla), a tributary of the [Dnieper](380138.md). Population: 3,928 (2022 estimate),[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-1] 90 (2026 estimate).[^cite_note-2]

## History

During [World War II](32927.md), Velyka Pisarivka was occupied by Nazi troops in October 1941. During the occupation, the Nazis executed 36 villagers while 176 were deported to Germany for forced labour. After a failed attempt by Soviet soldiers in early 1943, the village was finally liberated in August 1943.

Until 26 January 2024, Velyka Pysarivka was designated [urban-type settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban-type_settlement). On this day, a new law entered into force which abolished this status, and Velyka Pysarivka became a rural settlement.[^cite_note-glavnoe-3]

### 2022 Russian invasion

On 24 February, as part of the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), two Russian tank columns crossed the international border at [Grayvoron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grayvoron) and [Popivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popivka,_Okhtyrka_Raion,_Sumy_Oblast), reaching Velyka Pysarivka by 08\:00.[^cite_note-SuspilneSumy24Feb-4] The next day, according to the Sumy Oblast governor [Dmytro Zhyvytskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmytro_Zhyvytskyi), fighting began at 07\:30 on the outskirts of the Okhtyrka in the direction of Velyka Pysarivka.[^cite_note-5] According to Ukrainian state employee [Anton Herashchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Herashchenko), Russian forces could not occupy Okhtyrka and retreated, leaving tanks and equipment.[^cite_note-6]

The Russian military repeatedly bombarded Velyka Pysarivka throughout March 2022. Shelling was reported in the settlement on 5 March[^cite_note-7] and 13 March.[^cite_note-8]

On 17 March 2024, according to the head of local community, the center of Velyka Pysarivka was destroyed by Russian strikes. The citizens were asked to evacuate from the settlement.[^cite_note-9]

## Economy

### Transportation

The settlement is connected by roads with [Bohodukhiv](866121.md), with [Sumy](561837.md) via [Krasnopillia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnopillia,_Sumy_Oblast), and across the border with [Grayvoron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grayvoron) in Russia.

The closest railway station is in Bohodukhiv, on the railway connecting [Kharkiv](2788558.md) and Sumy.
