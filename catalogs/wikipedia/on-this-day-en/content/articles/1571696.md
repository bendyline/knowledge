**Somalis**[^cite_note-43] or **Somali people**, are a [Cushitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic_peoples) [ethnic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) and [nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation) native to the [Somali Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Peninsula).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-2009factbook-46] The [Somali language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language) is the shared mother tongue of ethnic Somalis, which is part of the [Cushitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic_languages) branch of the [Afroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afroasiatic_languages) language family. They are predominantly [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam).[^cite_note-Britannica-39][^cite_note-Mohamed_Diriye_Abdullahi2-47] Forming one of the largest ethnic groups on the continent, they cover one of the most expansive landmasses by a single ethnic group in [Africa](5334607.md).[^cite_note-48]

According to some scholars, the ancient [Land of Punt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Punt) and its native inhabitants formed part of the ethnogenesis of the Somali people. This ancient historical kingdom is where a great portion of their cultural traditions and ancestry are said to derive from.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-49][^cite_note-ReferenceB-50][^cite_note-ReferenceC-51][^cite_note-ReferenceD-52] Somalis and their country have long been identified with the term *Barbar* (or *Al-Barbar*). The 12th century geographer [al-Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Idrisi), for example, identified the Somali Peninsula as *Barbara*,[^cite_note-53] and classical sources from the Greeks and Romans similarly refer to the region as the second [Barbaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbaria_\(region\)).[^cite_note-54]

Somalis share many historical and cultural traits with other [Cushitic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic_peoples),[^cite_note-Joireman-42] especially with [Lowland East Cushitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowland_East_Cushitic) people, specifically the [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_people) and the [Saho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saho_people).[^cite_note-55] Ethnic Somalis are principally concentrated in [Somalia](27358.md) (around 17.6 million),[^cite_note-UNFPA_Somali_Population_Survey_2014-56] [Somaliland](51425.md) (5.7 million),[^cite_note-somalilandchronicle1-57] [Ethiopia](187749.md) (4.6 million),[^cite_note-CSA-5] [Kenya](188171.md) (2.8 million),[^cite_note-Census2019-6] and [Djibouti](17207794.md) (586,000).[^cite_note-Djibouti-7] [Somali diasporas](9662371.md) are also found in parts of the [Middle East](19323.md), [North America](21139.md), [Western Europe](33800.md), [African Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Great_Lakes) region, [Southern Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Africa) and [Oceania](22621.md).[^cite_note-58]

## Etymology

Main article: [proto-Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Somali)

[Samaale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaale), the legendary common ancestor of several [Somali clans](#clan), is generally regarded as the source of the [ethnonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnonym) *Somali*. One other theory is that the name is held to be derived from the words *soo* and *maal*, which together mean literally "go and [milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk)". This interpretation differs depending on region, with northern Somalis implying it refers to [camel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel)'s milk,[^cite_note-\:0-59] while southern Somalis use the transliteration *sa' maal* which refers to [cow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cow)'s milk.[^cite_note-60] This is a reference to the ubiquitous [pastoralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoralism) of the Somali people.[^cite_note-61]

Another plausible [etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology) proposes that the term *Somali* is derived from the [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) word for *wealthy* (*zāwamāl*), referring to Somali riches in livestock.[^cite_note-Lewis-62][^cite_note-63] Historian Mohamed A. Rirash maintains that the etymology of the ethnonym derives from the compound term *soofmaal*—with *soof* meaning 'to herd' and *maal* referring to 'livestock'—initially serving as an occupational label for Somali pastoralists before evolving into the collective name for all ethnic Somalis.[^cite_note-64]

Alternatively, the ethnonym is believed to have been derived from the Automoli (Asmach), a group of warriors from [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) described by [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus). *Asmach* is thought to have been their [Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_language) name, with *Automoli* being a [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) derivative of the [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) word *S'mali* (meaning 'on the left hand side').[^cite_note-65]

A [Tang Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) document from the 9th century CE referred to the northern Somalia coast—which was then part of a broader region in [Northeast Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Africa) known as [Barbaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbaria_\(region\)), in reference to the area's Barbar ([Cushitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic-speaking_peoples)) inhabitants[^cite_note-Laitin-66]—as *Po-pa-li*.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-Singh-68]

The first clear written reference of the [sobriquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobriquet) *Somali* dates back to the early 15th century CE during the reign of Ethiopian Emperor [Yeshaq I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshaq_I) who had one of his court officials compose a [hymn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hymn) celebrating a military victory over the [Sultanate of Ifat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Ifat).[^cite_note-69] *Simur* was also an ancient [Harari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harari_people) alias for the Somali people.[^cite_note-70]

Somalis overwhelmingly prefer the demonym *Somali* over *Somalian,* which may be viewed as incorrect[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] since the former is an endonym, while the latter is an exonym with double suffixes.[^cite_note-73] The [hypernym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypernym) of the term *Somali* from a geopolitical sense is *[Horner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horner_\(demography\))* and from an ethnic sense, it is *[Cushite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic_peoples)*.[^cite_note-74] The constitution of Somalia defines every citizen as a *Somali*.[^cite_note-75]

## History

Main articles: [History of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Somalia), [History of Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Somaliland), and [Maritime history of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_history_of_Somalia)



[Puntite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Punt) King Parahu and Queen Ati receiving the [Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) delegation. Modern scholars widely associate Punt with the [Horn of Africa](62142.md), considering its ancient Cushitic inhabitants ancestral to modern Somalis

[^cite_note-76]

[^cite_note-77]

The origin of the Somali people which was previously theorized to have been from Southern [Ethiopia](187749.md) since 1000 BC or from the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md) in the eleventh century has now been overturned by newer archeological and linguistic studies which puts the original homeland of the Somali people in [Somaliland](51425.md#Prehistory) region, which concludes that the Somalis are the indigenous inhabitants of the [Horn of Africa](62142.md) for the last 7000 years.[^cite_note-78] Ancient [rock paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_painting), which date back 5000 years (estimated), have been found in [Somaliland](51425.md) region. These engravings depict early life in the territory.[^cite_note-Dawn-79] The most famous of these is the [Laas Geel complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laas_Geel). It contains some of the earliest known [rock art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_art) on the [African continent](5334607.md) and features many elaborate pastoralist sketches of animal and human figures. In other places, such as the [Dhambalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhambalin) region, a depiction of a man on a horse is postulated as being one of the earliest known examples of a mounted huntsman.[^cite_note-Dawn-79] [Inscriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigraphy) have been found beneath many of the rock paintings, but [archaeologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeologist) have so far been unable to decipher this form of ancient writing.[^cite_note-80] During the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age), the [Doian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buur_Heybe) and [Hargeisan](864284.md) cultures flourished here with their respective [industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_\(archaeology\)) and factories.[^cite_note-81] The oldest evidence of burial customs in the Horn of Africa comes from [cemeteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cemeteries) in Somalia dating back to [4th millennium BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_millennium_BC).[^cite_note-82] The [stone implements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_tool) from the *Jalelo* [site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_site) in Somalia are said to be the most important link in evidence of the universality in [palaeolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeolithic) times between the [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East) and the [West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West).[^cite_note-83] In [antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history), the ancestors of the Somali people were an important link in the Horn of Africa connecting the region's commerce with the rest of the ancient world. Somali sailors and merchants were the main suppliers of [frankincense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankincense), [myrrh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrrh) and [spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice), items which were considered valuable luxuries by the [Ancient Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt), [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia), [Mycenaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece) and [Babylonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon).[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] According to most scholars, the ancient [Land of Punt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Punt) and its native inhabitants formed part of the [ethnogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnogenesis) of the Somali people.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-49][^cite_note-ReferenceB-50][^cite_note-ReferenceC-51][^cite_note-ReferenceD-52] The ancient Puntites were a nation of people that had close relations with [Pharaonic Egypt](23294.md) during the times of [Pharaoh](23294.md) [Sahure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahure) and [Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_regnant) [Hatshepsut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatshepsut). The [pyramidal structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Somalia), temples and ancient houses of [dressed stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonry) littered around Somalia may date from this period.[^cite_note-Man,_God_and_Civilization_pg_216-86]

In [classical antiquity](252905.md), the [Macrobians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrobians), who may have been ancestral to the Automoli or ancient Somalis, established a powerful tribal kingdom that ruled large parts of modern [Somalia](27358.md). They were reputed for their longevity and wealth, and were said to be the "tallest and handsomest of all men".[^cite_note-Wheeler_pg_526-87] The Macrobians were warrior herders and seafarers. According to Herodotus' account, the [Achaemenid emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) [Cambyses II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambyses_II), upon [his conquest of Egypt in 525 BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Achaemenid_conquest_of_Egypt), sent ambassadors to Macrobia, bringing luxury gifts for the Macrobian king to entice his submission. The Macrobian ruler, who was elected based on his stature and beauty, replied instead with a challenge for his Persian counterpart in the form of an unstrung bow: if the Persians could manage to draw it, they would have the right to invade his country; but until then, they should thank the gods that the Macrobians never decided to invade their empire.[^cite_note-Wheeler_pg_526-87][^cite_note-Kitto2-88] The Macrobians were a regional power reputed for their advanced architecture and [gold](12240.md) wealth, which was so plentiful that they shackled their prisoners in golden chains.[^cite_note-Kitto2-88]







Somali sultanates and historical sites. Clockwise from upper left: ruins of the [Adal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adal_Sultanate) in [Zeila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeila), the [Citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel) of [Gondershe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondershe) from the [Ajuran Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajuran_Sultanate), and the [Ifat Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Ifat) realm in the 14th century

Several ancient city-states, such as [Opone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opone), [Essina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essina), [Sarapion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarapion), [Nikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikon_\(Somalia\)), [Malao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malao_\(ancient\)), [Damo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damo,_Somalia) and [Mosylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosylon) near [Cape Guardafui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Guardafui), which competed with the [Sabaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabaeans), [Parthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthia) and [Axumites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksumite_Empire) for the wealthy [Indo](21566765.md)-[Greco-Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_world) trade, also flourished in Somalia.[^cite_note-89]

[Islam](6037917.md) was introduced to the area early on by the first Muslims of [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) fleeing prosecution during the first [Hejira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_to_Abyssinia) with [Masjid al-Qiblatayn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masjid_al-Qiblatayn_\(Somalia\)) being built before the [Qiblah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qibla) faced towards [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca). The town of [Zeila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeila)'s two-[mihrab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihrab) Masjid al-Qiblatayn dates to the 7th century, and is one of the oldest [mosques](19894.md) in Africa.[^cite_note-Btgpb-90]

Consequently, the Somalis were some of the earliest non-Arabs that converted to Islam.[^cite_note-91] The peaceful conversion of the Somali population by [Somali Muslim scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Somalia) in the following centuries, the ancient city-states eventually transformed into Islamic [Mogadishu](60154.md), [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera), [Zeila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeila), [Barawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barawa), [Hafun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafun) and [Merca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merca), which were part of the Berberi civilization. The city of Mogadishu came to be known as the *City of Islam*,[^cite_note-92] and controlled the East African gold trade for several centuries.[^cite_note-93]

The [Sultanate of Ifat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Ifat), led by the [Walashma dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walashma_dynasty) with its capital at [Zeila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeila), ruled over parts of what is now eastern Ethiopia, Djibouti and Somaliland. The historian [al-Umari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Fadlallah_al-Umari) records that Ifat was situated near the [Red Sea](26191.md) coast, and states its size as 15 days travel by 20 days travel. Its army numbered 15,000 horsemen and 20,000 foot soldiers. Al-Umari also credits Ifat with seven "mother cities": Belqulzar, Kuljura, Shimi, [Shewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shewa), Adal, Jamme and Laboo.[^cite_note-94]

In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), several powerful Somali empires dominated the regional trade including the [Ajuran Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajuran_Sultanate), which excelled in [hydraulic engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_engineering) and [fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress) building,[^cite_note-95] the [Adal Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adal_Sultanate), whose general [Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_ibn_Ibrahim_al-Ghazi) (Ahmed Gurey) was the first [commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander) to use cannon warfare on the continent during Adal's conquest of the [Ethiopian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire),[^cite_note-96] and the [Sultanate of the Geledi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_the_Geledi), whose military dominance forced governors of the [Omani empire](22316.md) north of the city of [Lamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamu) to pay tribute to the Somali Sultan [Ahmed Yusuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Yusuf_\(Gobroon\)).[^cite_note-97] The [Harla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harla_people), an early group who inhabited parts of Somalia, Tchertcher and other areas in the Horn, also erected various [tumuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumulus).[^cite_note-98] These masons are believed to have been ancestral to the Somalis ("proto-Somali").[^cite_note-99]

[Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) was the most important port in the [Horn of Africa](62142.md) between the 18th–19th centuries.[^cite_note-100] For centuries, [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) had extensive trade relations with several historic ports in the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md). Additionally, the Somali and Ethiopian interiors were very dependent on [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) for trade, where most of the goods for export arrived from. During the 1833 trading season, the port town swelled to over 70,000 people, and upwards of 6,000 camels laden with goods arrived from the interior within a single day. [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) was the main marketplace in the entire Somali seaboard for various goods procured from the interior, such as [livestock](25160767.md), [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), [frankincense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankincense), [myrrh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrrh), [acacia gum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia_gum), [saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron), [feathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feathers), [ghee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee), [hide (skin)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hide_\(skin\)), [gold](12240.md) and [ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory).[^cite_note-101] Historically, the port of [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) was controlled indigenously between the [mercantile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercantile) Reer Ahmed Nur and Reer Yunis Nuh sub-clans of the [Habar Awal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habar_Awal).[^cite_note-102] According to a trade journal published in 1856, [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) was described as "the freest port in the world, and the most important trading place on the whole Arabian Gulf.":

> "The only seaports of importance on this coast are Feyla \[Zeila] and Berbera; the former is an Arabian colony, dependent of Mocha, but Berbera is independent of any foreign power. It is, without having the name, the freest port in the world, and the most important trading place on the whole Arabian Gulf. From the beginning of November to the end of April, a large fair assembles in Berbera, and caravans of 6,000 camels at a time come from the interior loaded with coffee, (considered superior to Mocha in Bombay), gum, ivory, hides, skins, grain, cattle, and sour milk, the substitute of fermented drinks in these regions; also much cattle is brought there for the Aden market."[^cite_note-103]

As a tributary of [Mocha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mocha,_Yemen), which in turn was part of the Ottoman possessions in Western Arabia, the port of [Zeila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeila) had seen several men placed as governors over the years. The Ottomans based in Yemen held nominal authority of Zeila when [Sharmarke Ali Saleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharmarke_Ali_Saleh), who was a successful and ambitious Somali merchant, purchased the rights of the town from the Ottoman governor of Mocha and Hodeida.[^cite_note-104]

> Allee Shurmalkee \[Ali Sharmarke] has since my visit either seized or purchased this town, and hoisted independent colours upon its walls; but as I know little or nothing save the mere fact of its possession by that Soumaulee chief, and as this change occurred whilst I was in Abyssinia, I shall not say anything more upon the subject.[^cite_note-105]

However, the previous governor was not eager to relinquish his control of Zeila. Hence in 1841, Sharmarke chartered two dhows (ships) along with fifty Somali [Matchlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matchlock) men and two [cannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannons) to target [Zeila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeila) and depose its Arab Governor, Syed Mohammed Al Barr. Sharmarke initially directed his cannons at the city walls which frightened Al Barr's followers and caused them to abandon their posts and succeeded Al Barr as the ruler of Zeila. Sharmarke's governorship had an instant effect on the city, as he maneuvered to monopolize as much of the regional trade as possible, with his sights set as far as [Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar) and the [Ogaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Region).[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

[Ali Yusuf Kenadid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Yusuf_Kenadid), 2nd Sultan of the [Hobyo Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobyo_Sultanate)

In 1845, Sharmarke deployed a few matchlock men to wrest control of neighboring Berbera from that town's then feuding Somali local authorities.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] Sharmarke's influence was not limited to the Somali coast as he had allies and influence in the interior of the Somali country, the Danakil coast and even further afield in Abyssinia. Among his allies were the Kings of Shewa. When there was tension between the Amir of Harar [Abu Bakr II ibn \`Abd al-Munan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr_II_ibn_%60Abd_al-Munan) and Sharmarke, as a result of the Amir arresting one of his agents in [Harar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harar), Sharmarke persuaded the son of [Sahle Selassie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahle_Selassie), ruler of [Shewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shewa), to imprison on his behalf about 300 citizens of Harar then resident in Shewa, for a length of two years.[^cite_note-111]

In the late 19th century, after the [Berlin Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Conference) had ended, the [Scramble for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa) reached the Horn of Africa. Increasing foreign influence in the region culminated in the creation of the first *Darawiish,* an armed resistance movement calling for the independence from European powers.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-AngusHamilton-113] The [Dervish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dervish_movement_\(Nugaal\)) had their leaders, [Mohammed Abdullah Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Abdullah_Hassan), [Haji Sudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haji_Sudi) and [Sultan Nur Ahmed Aman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nur_Ahmed_Aman), who sought a state in the Nugaal[^cite_note-dervishletter-114] and began one of the longest African conflicts in modern history.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]

The news of the incident that sparked the 21 year long [Dervish rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliland_Campaign), according to the consul-general [James Hayes Sadler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hayes_Sadler_\(colonial_administrator\)), was spread or as he claimed was concocted by Sultan Nur of the [Habr Yunis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habr_Yunis_Sultanate). The incident in question was that of a group of Somali children who were converted to Christianity and adopted by the French Catholic Mission at [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera) in 1899. Whether Sultan Nur experienced the incident first hand or whether he was told of it is not clear but what is known is that he propagated the incident in June 1899, precipitating the religious rebellion of the Dervishes.[^cite_note-117]

The [Dervish movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dervish_State) successfully stymied [British forces](31730.md) four times and forced them to retreat to the coastal region.[^cite_note-118] As a result of its successes against the British, the Dervish movement received support from the [Ottomans](22278.md) and [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire). The [Ottoman government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sublime_Porte) also named Hassan [Emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) of the Somali nation,[^cite_note-119] and the [German government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Germany) promised to officially recognise any territories the Dervishes were to acquire.[^cite_note-120]

After a quarter of a century of military successes against the British, the Dervishes were finally defeated by Britain in 1920 in part due to the successful deployment of the newly-formed [Royal Air Force](25679.md) by the [British government](25318118.md).[^cite_note-121]

[Majeerteen Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majeerteen_Sultanate) was founded in the early-1700s and rose to prominence in the following century, under the reign of the resourceful Boqor (King) [Osman Mahamuud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osman_Mahamuud).[^cite_note-Metz-122] His Kingdom controlled Bari Karkaar, Nugaaal, and also central Somalia in the 19th and early 20th centuries. The Majeerteen Sultanate maintained a robust trading network, entered into treaties with foreign powers, and exerted strong centralized authority on the domestic front.[^cite_note-HOA-123][^cite_note-WSP-124]

The Majeerteen Sultanate was nearly dismantled in the late-1800s by a power struggle between Boqor [Osman Mahamuud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osman_Mahamuud) and his ambitious cousin, [Yusuf Ali Kenadid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_Ali_Kenadid) who founded the [Sultanate of Hobyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Hobyo) in 1878. Initially Kenadid wanted to seize control of the neighbouring Sultanate. However, he was unsuccessful in this endeavour, and was eventually forced into exile in [Yemen](350939.md).[^cite_note-125] Both sultanates maintained written records of their activities, which still exist.[^cite_note-Ssarif-126]

In 1888, Sultan Yusuf Ali Kenadid entered into a treaty with the Italian government, making his Sultanate of Hobyo an Italian [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate). His rival Boqor Osman Mahamuud was to sign a similar agreement vis-a-vis his own Majeerteen Sultanate the following year. In signing the agreements, both rulers also hoped to exploit the rival objectives of the European imperial powers so as to more effectively assure the continued independence of their territories.[^cite_note-Issa-Salwe-127]



Ahmed Abokob, a Somali soldier from [French Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Somaliland) (present-day [Djibouti](17207794.md)), who participated in [World War I](4764461.md), 1917

The Italians, for their part, were interested in the territories mainly because of its [ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) specifically Port of [Bosaso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosaso) which could grant them access to the strategically important [Suez Canal](29323.md) and the [Gulf of Aden](58716.md).[^cite_note-Fitz-33-128] The terms of the treaties the rulers signed specified that Italy was to steer clear of any interference in the Sultanates' respective administrations.[^cite_note-Issa-Salwe-127] In return for Italian arms and an annual subsidy, the Sultans conceded to a minimum of oversight and economic concessions.[^cite_note-Hess-129] The Italians also agreed to dispatch a few ambassadors to promote both the Sultanates' and their own interests.[^cite_note-Issa-Salwe-127]

The new protectorates were thereafter managed by [Vincenzo Filonardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Filonardi) through a [chartered company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartered_company).[^cite_note-Hess-129] An Anglo-Italian border protocol was later signed on 5 May 1894, followed by an agreement in 1906 between Cavalier Pestalozza and General Swaine acknowledging that [Baran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buraan) fell under the Majeerteen Sultanate's administration.[^cite_note-Issa-Salwe-127] With the gradual extension into northern Somalia of Italian colonial rule, both Kingdoms were eventually annexed in the early 20th century.[^cite_note-Majsult-130] However, unlike the southern territories, the northern sultanates were not subject to direct rule due to the earlier treaties they had signed with the Italians.[^cite_note-Gtsahos-131] By the end of 1927, following a two-year military campaign, [Rome](25458.md) finally asserted full authority over the entirety of Italian Somalia.[^cite_note-132] In 1936, the region was integrated into [Italian East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_East_Africa) as the [Somalia Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia_Governorate).[^cite_note-133] In urban areas, the colony was one of the most developed on the continent in terms of standard of living.[^cite_note-134] In the late 1930s the triangle area between Mogadishu, [Merca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merca) and [Villabruzzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villabruzzi) was fully developed in agriculture with a growing export of bananas to [Western Europe](33800.md).[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] During this period, the Mogadishu area was transformed into a colonial capital through the imposition of a new European-style city upon the old historic centre.[^cite_note-Cauli_2019_217–241-137] The British conquest of Italian Somalia in 1941 however removed parts of the infrastructure the [Italians](385155.md) had built, such as the [Mogadishu–Villabruzzi Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogadishu%E2%80%93Villabruzzi_Railway).[^cite_note-Cauli_2019_217–241-137] Following [World War II](32927.md), Britain retained control of both [British Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Somaliland) and [Italian Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Somalia) as [protectorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate). In 1945, during the [Potsdam Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Conference), the United Nations granted Italy trusteeship of Italian Somalia, but only under close supervision and on the condition — first proposed by the [Somali Youth League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Youth_League) (SYL) and other nascent Somali political organizations, such as Hizbia Digil Mirifle Somali (HDMS) and the Somali National League (SNL) — that Somalia achieve independence within ten years.[^cite_note-Zolberg-138][^cite_note-Gates1999-139] British Somalia remained a protectorate of Britain until 1960.[^cite_note-Tripodi1999-140]

To the extent that Italy held the territory by UN mandate, the trusteeship provisions gave the Somalis the opportunity to gain experience in political education and self-government. These were advantages that British Somaliland, which was to be incorporated into the new [Somali Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Republic) state, did not have. Although in the 1950s British colonial officials attempted, through various administrative development efforts, to make up for past neglect, the protectorate stagnated. The disparity between the two territories in economic development and political experience would cause serious difficulties when it came time to integrate the two parts.[^cite_note-ChapinMetz-141]

Meanwhile, in 1948, under pressure from their [World War II allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) and to the dismay of the Somalis,[^cite_note-Federal-142] the British ceded official control of the [Haud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haud) (an important Somali grazing area that was brought under British protection via treaties with the Somalis in 1884 and 1886) and the [Ogaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogaden) to Ethiopia, based on a treaty they signed in 1897 in which the British ceded Somali territory to the Ethiopian Emperor [Menelik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menelek_II_of_Ethiopia) in exchange for his help against raids by Somali clans.[^cite_note-Laitin1977-143] Britain included the proviso that the Somali nomads would retain their autonomy, but Ethiopia immediately claimed sovereignty over them.[^cite_note-Zolberg-138] This prompted an unsuccessful bid by Britain in 1956 to purchase back the Somali lands it had turned over.[^cite_note-Zolberg-138] The British government also granted administration of the almost exclusively Somali-inhabited[^cite_note-144] [Northern Frontier District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Frontier_District) (NFD) to the [Kenyan government](20171676.md) despite an informal [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite) demonstrating the overwhelming desire of the region's population to join the newly formed Somali Republic.[^cite_note-145]

A [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_French_Somaliland_constitutional_referendum) was held in neighboring [French Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Somaliland) in 1958, on the eve of Somalia's independence in 1960, to decide whether or not to join the Somali Republic or to remain with France. The referendum turned out in favour of a continued association with France, largely due to a combined yes vote by the sizable [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_people) ethnic group and resident Europeans.[^cite_note-Barrington2006-146] There was also widespread [vote rigging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_rigging), with the French expelling thousands of Somalis before the referendum reached the polls.[^cite_note-Kseoah-147] The majority of those who voted no were Somalis who were strongly in favour of joining a united Somalia, as had been proposed by [Mahmoud Harbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Harbi), Vice President of the Government Council. Harbi was killed in a plane crash two years later.[^cite_note-Barrington2006-146] Djibouti finally gained its independence from [France](5843419.md) in 1977, and [Hassan Gouled Aptidon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Gouled_Aptidon), a Somali who had campaigned for a yes vote in the referendum of 1958, eventually wound up as Djibouti's first president (1977–1991).[^cite_note-Barrington2006-146]



Sultan [Abdillahi Deria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdillahi_Deria), a prominent [Grand Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq_Sultanate) of British Somaliland of the delegation sent from [British Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Somaliland) Protectorate to the British government in London to appeal for the return of [Haud Reserve Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haud), a territory ceded by the British to [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire) in 1954

British Somaliland became independent on 26 June 1960 as the [State of Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Somaliland), and the [Trust Territory of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_Territory_of_Somalia) (the former Italian Somalia) followed suit five days later.[^cite_note-148] On 1 July 1960, the two territories united to form the [Somali Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Republic), albeit within boundaries drawn up by Italy and Britain.[^cite_note-buluugleey.com-149][^cite_note-150] A government was formed by [Abdullahi Issa Mohamud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullahi_Issa_Mohamud) and [Muhammad Haji Ibrahim Egal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Haji_Ibrahim_Egal) other members of the trusteeship and protectorate governments, with [Haji Bashir Ismail Yusuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haji_Bashir_Ismail_Yusuf) as president of the Somali National Assembly, [Aden Abdullah Osman Daar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden_Abdullah_Osman_Daar) as the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_Somalia) of the Somali Republic and [Abdirashid Ali Shermarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdirashid_Ali_Shermarke) as [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Somalia) (later to become president from 1967 to 1969). On 20 July 1961 and through a popular [referendum](51582.md), the people of Somalia ratified a new [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution), which was first drafted in 1960. The constitution was rejected by the people of Somaliland.[^cite_note-151] In 1967, [Muhammad Haji Ibrahim Egal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Haji_Ibrahim_Egal) became Prime Minister, a position to which he was appointed by Shermarke.

On 15 October 1969, while paying a visit to the northern town of [Las Anod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Anod), Somalia's then President Abdirashid Ali Shermarke was shot dead by one of his own bodyguards. His assassination was quickly followed by a military [coup d'état](25914154.md) on 21 October 1969 (the day after his funeral), in which the [Somali Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Somalia) seized power without encountering armed opposition — essentially a bloodless takeover. The putsch was spearheaded by Major General [Mohamed Siad Barre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siad_Barre), who at the time commanded the army.[^cite_note-Myswenvwp-152]

Alongside Barre, the [Supreme Revolutionary Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Revolutionary_Council_\(Somalia\)) (SRC) that assumed power after President Sharmarke's assassination was led by Lieutenant Colonel [Salaad Gabeyre Kediye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salaad_Gabeyre_Kediye) and Chief of Police [Jama Korshel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jama_Ali_Korshel). The SRC subsequently renamed the country the [Somali Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Democratic_Republic),[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-Grolierenc-154] dissolved the parliament and the Supreme Court, and suspended the constitution.[^cite_note-Pjdlfw-155]

The revolutionary army established large-scale public works programs and successfully implemented an urban and rural [literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy) campaign, which helped dramatically increase the literacy rate. In addition to a nationalization program of industry and land, the new regime's foreign policy placed an emphasis on Somalia's traditional and religious links with the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), eventually joining the [Arab League](52625.md) (AL) in 1974.[^cite_note-Frankel-156] That same year, Barre also served as chairman of the [Organization of African Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_African_Unity) (OAU), the predecessor of the [African Union](10634933.md) (AU).[^cite_note-Yang-157]

Somali clans are patrilineal kinship groups based on agnatic descent of the Somali people.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewisSamatar199911-159][^cite_note-AppiahGates2010p39522-160] Tradition and folklore connects the origin of the Somali population by language and way of life, and societal organisations, by customs, and by a feeling of belonging to a broader family among individuals from the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md).[^cite_note-161]

The Somali people are mainly divided among five patrilineal clans, the [Hawiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawiye), [Darod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darod), [Isaaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq), [Rahanweyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahanweyn) and [Dir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dir_\(clan\)).[^cite_note-162] The average person is able to trace their ancestry generations back. Somali clans in contemporary times have an established official structure in the country's political system, acknowledged by a mathematical formula for equitably distributing seats between the clans in the [Federal Parliament of Somalia](36817385.md).[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] Minor Somali clans include [Asharaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asharaf).[^cite_note-Abdullahi8-166]

The clan represents the highest degree of familial affiliation. It holds territorial properties and is typically overseen by a Sultan. Clans possess ancestral lands, which are associated with the migratory patterns of the Somali people throughout their historical narrative. Each clan is administered by its designated leader and supported by its council of elders, with land being communally owned and overseen.[^cite_note-\:422-167] Various Somali clans utilise [distinct titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_aristocratic_and_court_titles) for their leaders, including [Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewisSamatar1999203–204-168] [Emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir), [Imam](15240.md),[^cite_note-169] [Ughaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ughaz),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewisSamatar1999203–204-168] and [Garaad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_aristocratic_and_court_titles).[^cite_note-170] Clan leadership may be [hereditary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditary_rule), or leaders may be [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election) by the council of elders composed of representatives from diverse clan lineages. The leaders of these clans fulfill both religious and political responsibilities.[^cite_note-\:422-167]

Certain clans are traditionally classed as noble clans, referring to their [pastoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoralism) lifestyle in contrast to the sedentary "Sab".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewisSamatar199911–14-171] The noble clans are the [Dir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dir_\(clan\)), [Darod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darod), [Hawiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawiye) and [Isaaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq).[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] Out of these clans, [Dir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dir_\(clan\)) and [Hawiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawiye) are regarded as descended from [Samaale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irir_Samaale), the likely source of the ethnonym *Somali (soomaali)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewisSamatar199911–13-174]The [Hawiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawiye) are descended from [Samaale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaale) through his grandson [Ahmed Bin Abdulrahman Bin Uthman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Bin_Abdulrahman_Bin_Uthman).[^cite_note-\:02-175][^cite_note-176] [Darod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darod) and [Isaaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq) have separate agnatic traditions of descent from [Abdirahman bin Isma'il al-Jabarti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdirahman_bin_Isma'il_al-Jabarti) and [Ishaaq bin Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishaaq_bin_Ahmed) respectively.[^cite_note-Lewismhs22-177] Sheikh Darod and Sheikh Ishaaq are asserted to have married women from the Dir clan, thus establishing [matrilateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrilateral) ties with the Samaale main stem.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewisSamatar199911–13-174] "Sab" is a term used to refer to the [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) clans such as the [Rahanweyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahanweyn), in contrast to "Samaale".[^cite_note-L\&S22-178]

The traditions of descent from noble elite forefathers who settled on the littoral are debated, although they are based on early Arab documents and Somali folklore.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-AppiahGates2010p395-180][^cite_note-181] The [tombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb) of the founders of [Darod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdirahman_bin_Isma'il_al-Jabarti), [Dir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dir_\(clan\)), [Isaaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaaq) as well as the [Abgaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abgaal) subclan of [Hawiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawiye) are all located in northern Somalia. Tradition holds this area as the ancestral homeland of the Somali people.[^cite_note-Abdullahi8-166]

## Religion



13th century [Fakr ad-Din mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakr_ad-Din_mosque) built by Fakr ad-Din, the first Sultan of the [Mogadishu Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Mogadishu)

With very few exceptions, Somalis are entirely Muslims, the vast majority belonging to the [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) branch of Islam and the [Shafi'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi'i) school of [Islamic jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_jurisprudence).[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-Mohamed_Diriye_Abdullahi2-47] There are two theories about when Somalis began adopting Islam.[^cite_note-locsomalia-183] One states that the religion entered the region very early on, as a group of persecuted Muslims had, at Prophet [Muhummad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhummad)'s urging, sought refuge across the [Red Sea](26191.md) in the [Horn of Africa](62142.md). Islam may thus have been introduced into Somalia well before the faith even took root in its place of origin.[^cite_note-locsomalia-183] An alternate theory states that Islam was brought to the coastal settlements of Somalia between the 7th and the 10th century by seafaring Arab and Persian merchants.[^cite_note-Lapidus2014p480-184][^cite_note-locsomalia-183]

At present, scholarly dates for the Islamicization of the country are uncertain and may be as late as the eleventh and twelfth centuries, though others suggest a possibly earlier date.[^cite_note-185][^cite_note-186] Islam entered the region very early on, shortly after the [hijra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hijra_\(Islam\)). The two-mihrab Masjid al-Qiblatayn dates to the 7th century, and is the oldest [mosque](19894.md) in the country. In the late 9th century, [Al-Yaqubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Yaqubi) wrote that [Muslims](19541.md) were living along the northern Somali seaboard.[^cite_note-Encyamer-187]

The [constitution of Somalia](37022532.md) defines Islam as the state religion of the Federal Republic of Somalia, and Islamic [sharia](28840.md) law as the basic source for national legislation. It also stipulates that no law that is inconsistent with the basic tenets of Shari'a can be enacted.[^cite_note-188] There are some Somalis who genuinely believe with great pride that they are of Arabian ancestry, and trace their stirp to the Islamic prophet Muhammad's lineage of [Quraysh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quraysh) and those of his companions. Although they do not consider themselves culturally Arabs, except for the shared religion, their presumed noble Arabian origins genealogically unite them.[^cite_note-pastoral_dem-189] The purpose behind claiming genealogical traditions of descent from the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md) is used to reinforce one's lineage and the various associated patriarchs with the spread of Islam.[^cite_note-James_Currey_Publishers-190]

According to data from the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center), the creed breakdown of Muslims in the Somali-majority [Djibouti](17207794.md) is as follows: 77% adhere to [Sunnism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunnism), 8% are [non-denominational Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-denominational_Muslim), 2% are [Shia](26961.md) and 13% declined to answer, and a further report inclusive of the [Somali Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Region) in [Ethiopia](187749.md) stipulating 2% adherence to a minority sect (e.g. [Ibadism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadism), [Quranism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quranism) etc.).[^cite_note-191] In the neighboring country of [Somalia](27358.md), 99.8% of the population is [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) according to the Pew Research Center.[^cite_note-192] The majority belong to the [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) branch of [Islam](6037917.md) and the [Shafi'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi'i) school of [Islamic jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_jurisprudence).[^cite_note-193] [Sufism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism), the [mystical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysticism) sect of Islam, is also well established, with many local *jama'a* (*[zawiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaouia)*) or congregations of the various *[tariiqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariqah)* or Sufi orders.[^cite_note-194]

## Languages

Main article: [Somali language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language)



Old Somali stone tablet: After [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language) had lost its ancient writing script,

[^cite_note-WOSL-195]

&#x20;[Somali scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_literature) over the following centuries developed a writing system known as [Wadaad writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadaad_writing) to transcribe the language

The [Somali language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language) (*Af-Soomaali*) is a member of the [Cushitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushitic_languages) branch of the [Afroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afroasiatic_languages) family. Its nearest relatives are the [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_language) and [Saho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saho_language) languages.[^cite_note-196] Somali is the best documented of the Cushitic languages[^cite_note-197] with academic studies dating from the 19th century.[^cite_note-198]

Speech sample in [Standard Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_language)

The exact number of speakers of Somali is unknown. One source estimates that there are 16.3 million speakers of Somali within Somalia and 25.8 million speakers globally.[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200] Recent estimates had approximately 24 million speakers of Somali, spread in [Greater Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Somalia) of which around 17 million resided in Somalia.[^cite_note-eth2-201] The Somali language is spoken by ethnic Somalis in Greater Somalia and the Somali diaspora.[^cite_note-202]

Somali dialects are divided into three main groups: [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Somali), [Benadiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benadiri_Somali), and [Maay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maay_language). Northern Somali (or Northern-Central Somali) forms the basis for Standard Somali. Benadiri (also known as Coastal Somali) is spoken on the [Benadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benadir) coast from [Adale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adale) to south of [Merca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merca), including Mogadishu, as well as in the immediate hinterland. The coastal dialects have additional [phonemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoneme) which do not exist in Standard Somali. Maay is principally spoken by the Digil and Mirifle ([Rahanweyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahanweyn)) clans in the southwestern areas of Somalia.[^cite_note-Dalby-203]

A number of [writing systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing_system) have been used over the years for transcribing the Somali language. Of these, the [Somali Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Latin_alphabet) is the most widely used, and has been the official writing script in Somalia since the government of former [President of Somalia](516101.md) Mohamed Siad Barre formally introduced it in October 1972.[^cite_note-204] The script was developed by the Somali linguist [Shire Jama Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shire_Jama_Ahmed) specifically for the Somali language. It uses all letters of the [Latin alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_alphabet), except *p*, *v*, and *z*. Besides the Latin script, other orthographies that have been used for centuries for writing Somali include the long-established [Arabic script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_alphabet) and [Wadaad writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadaad_writing). Other writing systems developed in the twentieth century including the [Osmanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osmanya_alphabet), [Borama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borama_alphabet) and [Kaddare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaddare_alphabet) scripts, which were invented by [Osman Yusuf Kenadid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osman_Yusuf_Kenadid), [Abdurahman Sheikh Nuur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdurahman_Sheikh_Nuur) and [Hussein Sheikh Ahmed Kaddare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_Sheikh_Ahmed_Kaddare) respectively.[^cite_note-205]

In addition to Somali, [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), which is also an Afro-Asiatic tongue, is an official national language in Somalia and Djibouti. Many Somalis speak it due to millennia-old ties with the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), the far-reaching influence of the Arabic media, and religious education.[^cite_note-Dubnov-206] Somalia and Djibouti are also both members of the [Arab League](52625.md).[^cite_note-2009factbook-46][^cite_note-SCT-207]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Somalia)

Somali culture is a diverse tradition, an amalgamation of traditions developed independently and through interaction with neighboring and far away civilizations, such as other parts of [Northeast Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Africa), the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md), [India](14533.md) and [Southeast Asia](28741.md),[^cite_note-Abdullahi2-208] shaped by centuries of nomadic life, Islamic scholarship, and maritime trade. It encompasses a strong oral heritage, particularly in poetry, which plays a central role in social and political life. Islam is deeply integrated into Somali customs, law, and daily practice. Somali society is traditionally clan-based, with a strong emphasis on hospitality, kinship, and communal responsibility.[^cite_note-euaa2023-209] Artistic expression appears in various forms including music, dance, visual arts, and decorative crafts, often reflecting both Islamic and indigenous influences.[^cite_note-210]

The cultural diffusion of [Somali commercial enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_history_of_Somalia) can be detected in its [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalian_cuisine), which contains Southeast Asian, [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians) and [Arab](2185.md) influences. Due to the Somali people's passionate love for and facility with poetry, Somalia has often been referred to by scholars as a "Nation of Poets" and a "Nation of [Bards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bards)".[^cite_note-211]

### Music

Main articles: [Music of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Somalia) and [List of Somali musicians and musical groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Somali_musicians_and_musical_groups)



Prominent Somali singer [Hibo Nura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibo_Nura), who began her career in 1968, performs in Mogadishu, April 2014

Somali music is a traditional and contemporary art form that plays an important role in cultural expression and social communication. Most Somali songs are [pentatonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentatonic_scale). That is, they only use five [pitches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitch_\(music\)) per [octave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave) in contrast to a [heptatonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heptatonic_scale) (seven note) scale, such as the [major scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_scale). At first listen, Somali music might be mistaken for the sounds of nearby regions such as Ethiopia, [Sudan](27421.md) or [Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia), it is distinguished by its unique melodic structures and performance practices. Somali songs are usually the product of collaboration between [lyricists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyricist) (*midho*), songwriters (*laxan*) and singers (*Codka* or "voice").[^cite_note-212] Traditional music often features vocal performances accompanied by instruments such as the *oud*, *kaban* (a type of lute), and percussion.[^cite_note-Abdullahi-213] In pastoral and nomadic contexts, music functions as a medium for storytelling, oral history, and communal events. Religious music, particularly devotional chants, is also present within the broader musical tradition. In the mid-20th century, Somali music incorporated external influences and expanded through state-supported cultural initiatives and media broadcasts.[^cite_note-214]

### Cinema and theatre

Main article: [Cinema of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Somalia)



Somali film producer and director [Ali Said Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Said_Hassan)

Somali cinema developed out of the country's strong oral storytelling traditions, with the first feature-length films and film festivals appearing in the early 1960s, soon after Somalia gained independence. The establishment of the [Somali Film Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Film_Agency) (SFA) in 1975 marked a key turning point, leading to a period of rapid growth in the national film sector. This era saw the emergence of influential figures such as [Hassan Sheikh Mumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Sheikh_Mumin), a prominent playwright and composer whose play *Shabeel Naagood* (1965) became a cornerstone of Somali literature and theater. The work, later translated into English as *Leopard Among the Women*, explores themes such as gender roles, education, and societal change. Although the issues it describes were later to some degree redressed, the work remains a mainstay of Somali literature.[^cite_note-Aaasc-215] Mumin composed both the play itself and the music used in it.[^cite_note-Haywardlewis-216] The piece is regularly featured in various school curricula, including [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University), which first published the English translation under its [press house](48518.md). In the 1970s and early 1980s, musical dramas known as *riwaayado* dominated the cinematic landscape, forming a bridge between live performance and film. Filmmakers such as [Ali Said Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Said_Hassan) and [Said Salah Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Said_Salah_Ahmed) contributed to the production of epic and historical films, including *The Somali Darwish*, a major work on the Dervish movement. Technological developments and expanding television access facilitated broader distribution of Somali films during this time. In the 1990s and 2000s, a new wave of more entertainment-oriented movies emerged. Referred to as [Somaliwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somaliwood), this upstart, youth-based cinematic movement has energized the Somali film industry and in the process introduced innovative storylines, marketing strategies and production techniques. The young directors Abdisalam Aato of Olol Films and Abdi Malik Isak are at the forefront of this quiet revolution.[^cite_note-Somaliwood-217]

### Art

Main article: [Somali art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_art)



[Iman Mohamed Abdulmajid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iman_\(model\)) (known professionally as Iman) in 1996. She rose to fame in the late 1970s as the first Somali [supermodel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermodel) in Western [high fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_fashion)

[^cite_note-218]

Traditional Somali art includes [pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery), [jewelry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewelry), and [wood carving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_carving), with men historically responsible for carving and women for [textile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile) production. In the medieval period, wealthy urban patrons often commissioned local artisans to decorate interiors with intricate wood and marble carvings, especially within homes, [mihrabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihrab), and [pillars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column) of ancient Somali [mosques](19894.md), which frequently featured elaborate geometric and floral motifs. Among nomadic communities, artistic woodwork was common in everyday objects such as spoons, combs, and bowls, as well as in more complex constructions like the portable [*aqal* house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigwam#Similar_dwellings_elsewhere). In recent decades, traditional carving techniques have been increasingly supplemented by workshop-based production using electrical machinery.[^cite_note-219]

Additionally, [henna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henna) is particularly prominent in festive and ceremonial contexts such as [Eid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_holidays) and weddings. Somali women apply henna to their hands, feet, and arms (often in floral and geometric patterns similar to those seen in the [Arabian peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_peninsula)) and use [kohl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohl_\(cosmetics\)) (*kuul*) around the eyes for decorative purposes. Usage of the [eye cosmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_liner) in the Horn region is believed to date to the ancient [Land of Punt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Punt).[^cite_note-Stiant-220]

### Sports

Main article: [Sports in Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Somalia)



[Mo Farah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo_Farah) (pictured in 2023), a four-time [Olympic](22576.md) and six-time [World champion](218926.md), holding the record for the most global [track distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-distance_running) gold medals

[^cite_note-CanadianRunning_Farah-223]

Sports play an important role in Somali society, with football being the most popular and widely followed sport. The main domestic competitions include the [Somalia League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia_League) and [Somalia Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia_Cup), while the national team, known as the [Ocean Stars](1235063.md), represents the country in international tournaments. The team is multi ethnic.[^cite_note-Nsyem-224] [Basketball](3921.md) is also played in the country. The [FIBA Africa Championship 1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Africa_Championship_1981) was hosted in Mogadishu from 15 to 23 December 1981, during which the [national basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia_national_basketball_team) received the bronze medal.[^cite_note-225] The squad also takes part in the [basketball event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_at_the_Pan_Arab_Games) at the [Pan Arab Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Arab_Games). Other team sports include badminton, baseball, table tennis, and volleyball.[^cite_note-Nsyem-224]

In individual sports, Somali athletes have participated in a range of disciplines, including judo, boxing, athletics, weightlifting, swimming, rowing, fencing, and wrestling.[^cite_note-Nsyem-224] In martial arts, [Faisal Jeylani Aweys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_Jeylani_Aweys) and Mohamed Deq Abdulle achieved notable success by winning silver and fourth place, respectively, at the 2013 Open World Taekwondo Challenge Cup. Mohamed Jama has earned international recognition by securing both world and European titles in [K1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-1) and [Thai Boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muay_Thai).[^cite_note-Gvfmikk-226] Somali athletes have gained international prominence in long-distance running, with figures such as [Mo Farah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo_Farah), [Abdi Bile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdi_Bile) and [Mohammed Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Ahmed_\(runner\)) achieving global success.

### Cuisine

Main article: [Somali cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_cuisine)



*Baasto* (Pasta), popular in the Northwest and Southern regions of Somalia, introduced during the [Italian colonial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Somaliland)

Somali cuisine reflects a diverse mix of different culinary influences. Drawing from [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_cuisine), [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cuisine), and [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_cuisine) flavours, it is shaped by Somalia's history of trade and commerce.[^cite_note-227] The aftermath of [Italian colonial rule in Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Somaliland) left a culinary influence that is seen in the widespread adoption of [pasta](23871.md) (baasto), usually served with a sauce (suugo), and [lasagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasagna).[^cite_note-228][^cite_note-229]

The foundation of Somali dishes encompasses staples like [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) and [pasta](23871.md), paired with a type of [meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat) such as [lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_and_mutton), [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef), or [chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken). Somali culinary traditions include [camel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel) meat and [milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk), which are considered delicacies and important components in the diets of [pastoralists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoralism).[^cite_note-230] Coastal communities also incorporate [seafood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seafood) into their meals. Aromatic [spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice) such as [cumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumin), [cardamom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardamom), and coriander are frequently utilized to add flavor to dishes.[^cite_note-231][^cite_note-232]

Somali cuisine includes an assortment of [stews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stew), traditional [flatbreads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatbread), and [pastries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastry). This includes [Canjeero/Lahooh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahoh), a variation of fermented [pancake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancake)-like [flatbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatbread) that is savored in Somalia and in neighbouring countries like [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injera), [Eritrea](17238590.md), and [Yemen](350939.md).[^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235]

[Somali rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bariis_iskukaris), the usual staple for [dinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinner) or [lunch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunch), is typically [seasoned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasoning) and mixed with various [ingredients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingredient) such as [meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat), [vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable), and, [raisins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raisin). It is not uncommon for this dish to be presented in a visually appealing manner by incorporating multiple colors. The dish may be artificially tinted with shades of yellow or orange using [saffron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffron) and other [spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice) to enhance its aesthetic appeal.[^cite_note-236]



Somali rice (bariis) and fish (kalluun), Liver (beer) with vegetable also (Sabaayad) pancakes

[Tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea) and [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) are also really popular. Somalis were among the early adopters of [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) consumption and the first individuals documented to have enjoyed this drink. Additionally, Somali merchants were some of the first to export [coffee beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_bean).[^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238] The methods for preparing Somali coffee, known locally as '[Qahwo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qahwah)', and tea ('Shah'), involves mixing various [spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice) together to enhance their flavor profile.[^cite_note-239] Bun, which consists of coffee beans fried in [ghee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee) and infused with spices, is traditionally served during religious or social gatherings.[^cite_note-240]

'Xalwo', which is closely associated with '[Halva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halva)', is a smooth jelly-like treat that is made with a mixture of [spices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice), seeds, [nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_\(fruit\)), and [caramelized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caramelization) [sugar](27712.md).[^cite_note-241][^cite_note-242] This [confection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confectionery) is commonly served together with biscuits and Somali tea. After meals, homes are traditionally perfumed using [frankincense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankincense) or [incense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incense) (*unsi*), which is prepared inside an [incense burner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censer) referred to as a *[dabqaad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabqaad)*.[^cite_note-243]

### Literature

Main articles: [Somali literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_literature) and [List_of_Somali_writers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Somali_writers)

Somali literature encompasses a longstanding oral and written tradition, with poetry serving as a central medium for cultural expression, historical transmission, and social discourse. Somali scholars have for centuries contributed to [Islamic literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_literature), producing works in fields such as poetry and [Hadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadith). Some famous poets include [Sayyid Moḥammad Abdallah Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu%E1%B8%A5ammad_ibn_'Abdall%C4%81h_Hassan), [Ali Bu'ul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Bu'ul), [Elmi Boodhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmi_Boodhari), and [Hadrawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrawi). Early Somali writing was primarily in Arabic script and [Wadaad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadaad's_writing) writing, a system used by religious scholars and their associates.[^cite_note-244] Although Arabic was the preferred language for formal scholarship, Somali-language manuscripts were also produced, particularly in the form of religious poetry ([Qasida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qasida)), recitations, and chants. The rest of the historical literature in Somali largely consists of translations from Arabic.[^cite_note-Andrezewski130131-245] Among these texts are the Somali poems by [Sheikh Uways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uways_al-Barawi) and Sheikh Ismaaciil Faarah. The rest of the existing historical literature in Somali principally consists of translations of documents from Arabic.[^cite_note-Andrezewski232-246]

### Law

Main article: [Xeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xeer)



[Mahad Abdalle Awad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahad_Abdalle_Awad), a politician who served as the Second Deputy [Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_\(politics\)) for the [Federal Parliament of Somalia](36817385.md) from 2012 to 2022

Somalis for centuries have practiced a form of [customary law](218798.md), which they call *[xeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xeer)*. Xeer is a [polycentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycentric_law) legal system where there is no monopolistic agent that determines what the law should be or how it should be interpreted. It is assumed to have developed exclusively in the Horn of Africa since approximately the 7th century. Given the dearth of [loan words](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan_word) from foreign languages within the xeer's nomenclature, the customary law appears to have evolved in situ.[^cite_note-misesSHM-248]

Xeer is defined by a few fundamental tenets that are immutable and which closely approximate the principle of *[jus cogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jus_cogens)* in [international law](8195726.md): payment of [blood money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_money_\(term\)) (locally referred to as *[diya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diyya)* or *mag*), assuring good inter-[clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clan) relations by treating women justly, negotiating with "peace emissaries" in good faith, and sparing the lives of socially protected groups (e.g. children, women, the pious, poets and guests), family obligations such as the payment of [dowry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dowry), and sanctions for eloping, rules pertaining to the management of resources such as the use of pasture land, water, and other natural resources, providing financial support to married female relatives and newlyweds, donating livestock and other assets to the poor.[^cite_note-SJS-249] The Xeer legal system also requires a certain amount of [specialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_labour) of different functions within the legal framework. Thus, one can find *odayal* (judges), *xeer boggeyaal* ([jurists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurist)), *guurtiyaal* ([detectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detective)), *garxajiyaal* ([attorneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawyer)), *murkhaatiyal* ([witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witnesses)) and *waranle* ([police officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_officer)) to enforce the law.[^cite_note-HAblele-250]

[Somali architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_architecture) is a rich and diverse tradition of [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_engineering) and [designing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design). It involves multiple different construction types, such as [stone cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonry), [castles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle), [citadels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel), [fortresses](204118.md), [mosques](19894.md), [mausoleums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum), [towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower), [tombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb), [tumuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumulus), [cairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairn), [megaliths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalith), [menhirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menhir), [stelae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stele), [dolmens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolmen), [stone circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_circle), [monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument), [temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temples), [enclosures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclosure_\(archaeology\)), [cisterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistern), [aqueducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueduct_\(water_supply\)), and [lighthouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighthouse). Spanning the ancient, medieval and early modern periods in Greater Somalia, it also includes the fusion of Somali architecture with Western designs in [contemporary](3970948.md) times.[^cite_note-Abdullahi105-251]

In ancient Somalia, [pyramidical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramid) structures known in Somali as *taalo* were a popular burial style. Hundreds of these [dry stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_stone) monuments are found around the country today. Houses were built of [dressed stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonemasonry) similar to the ones in [Ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt).[^cite_note-Man,_God_and_Civilization_pg_216-86] There are also examples of [courtyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtyard) and large stone walls enclosing settlements, such as the [Wargaade Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wargaade_Wall).



[Dervish movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dervish_movement_\(Somali\)) fort in [Eyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyl)

The peaceful introduction of [Islam](6037917.md) in the early medieval era of Somalia's history brought [Islamic architectural influences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_architecture) from [Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia) and [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persia). This had the effect of stimulating a shift in construction from drystone and other related materials to [coral stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral), [sundried bricks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudbrick), and the widespread use of [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) in Somali architecture. Many of the new architectural designs, such as mosques, were built on the ruins of older structures. This practice would continue over and over again throughout the following centuries.[^cite_note-252]

## Ethnic flag

The [Somali flag](510039.md) is an [ethnic flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_flag) conceived to represent ethnic Somalis.[^cite_note-Tifh-253] It was created in 1954 by the Somali scholar [Mohammed Awale Liban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Awale_Liban), after he had been selected by the labour trade union of the [Trust Territory of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_Territory_of_Somalia) to come up with a design.[^cite_note-Crwf-254] Upon [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Somalia\)) in 1960, the flag was adopted as the national flag of the nascent [Somali Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Republic).[^cite_note-Wasf-255] The five-pointed *Star of Unity* in the flag's center represents the Somali ethnic group inhabiting the five territories in [Greater Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Somalia).[^cite_note-Wasf-255][^cite_note-Cwfbsom-256]

## Geographic distribution

Main articles: [Somali diaspora](9662371.md), [List of Somalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Somalis), and [Greater Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Somalia)



[Nadifa Mohamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadifa_Mohamed), a [British-Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Somali) novelist, pictured in 2010

&#x20;The Somali people primarily inhabit the Horn of Africa, in a region often referred to as [Greater Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Somalia). This area encompasses the modern-day nation of Somalia as well as parts of Djibouti, eastern Ethiopia (the Somali Region), and northeastern Kenya.[^cite_note-257][^cite_note-2009factbook-46][^cite_note-258] These territories are historically and culturally linked through shared language, religion, and clan affiliations. The concept of Greater Somalia reflects a longstanding national identity that predates colonial borders. Following the outbreak of the Somali civil war in the early 1990s, significant numbers of Somalis were displaced, leading to the emergence of a widespread global diaspora. Today, substantial Somali communities are found in countries such as the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md),[^cite_note-Sciodb-259] [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), [Yemen](350939.md) United States, the United Kingdom,[^cite_note-2016_UK_estimates-260] Canada,[^cite_note-www12.statcan.ca-261] Sweden,[^cite_note-SCB-262] Norway,[^cite_note-NorwayCensus-263] and the Netherlands among others, where they have established diaspora communities while maintaining strong transnational ties to their homeland.[^cite_note-264] UN migration estimates of the international migrant stock 2015 suggest that 1,998,764 people from Somalia were living abroad.[^cite_note-UN2015-265][^cite_note-266][^cite_note-267]

### Autosomal ancestry

 \

 \

Research shows that Somalis have a mixture of a type of native African ancestry unique and autochthonous to the [Horn of Africa](62142.md), as well as ancestry originating from a non-African back-migration. A medical genomics study concluded that its data reveal a remarkably homogeneous Somali population that shares an ancient origin with Afro-Asiatic-speaking groups in the Horn of Africa and the Middle East.[^cite_note-\:1-286]



Portrait of a Somali man, French Somaliland, 1940s

According to an [autosomal DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autosome) study by Hodgson et al. (2014), the Afro-Asiatic languages were likely spread across Africa and the Near East by an ancestral population(s) carrying a newly identified non-African genetic component, which the researchers dub as the "Ethio-Somali". This component today is most common among Afro-Asiatic-speaking populations in the Horn of Africa. It reaches a frequency peak among ethnic Somalis, representing the majority of their ancestry. The Ethio-Somali component is most closely related to the Maghrebi non-African genetic component, and is believed to have diverged from all other non-African ancestries at least 23,000 years ago. On this basis, the researchers suggest that the original Ethio-Somali carrying population(s) probably arrived in the pre-agricultural period from the Near East, having crossed over into northeastern Africa via the [Sinai Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula). The population then likely split into two branches, with one group heading westward toward the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb) and the other moving south into the Horn.[^cite_note-Hodgson2014-287] Ancient DNA analysis indicates that this foundational ancestry in the Horn region is akin to that of Neolithic farmers of the southern [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant).[^cite_note-Lazaridis2016-288]

Furthermore, according to Hodgson et al. both the African ancestry (Ethiopic) and the non-African ancestry (Ethio-Somali) in Cushitic speaking populations is significantly differentiated from all neighboring African and non-African ancestries today. The overall genetic ancestry of Cushitic and Semitic speaking populations in the Horn of Africa represents ancestries not found outside of HOA populations. The researchers state:

> "The African Ethiopic ancestry is tightly restricted to HOA populations and likely represents an autochthonous HOA population. The non-African ancestry in the HOA, which is primarily attributed to a novel Ethio-Somali inferred ancestry component, is significantly differentiated from all neighboring non-African ancestries in North Africa, the Levant, and Arabia."[^cite_note-289]

Moreover, Hodgson et al. (2014) elaborates further:

> "We find that most of the non-African ancestry in the HOA can be assigned to a distinct non-African origin Ethio-Somali ancestry component, which is found at its highest frequencies in Cushitic and Semitic speaking HOA populations."[^cite_note-290]

Molinaro, Ludovica et al. in 2019 characterized the Non-African ancestry in Ethiopian Somalis as being derived from Anatolia Neolithic groups (similar to Tunisian Jews).[^cite_note-291] Ali, A.A., Aalto, M., Jonasson, J. et al. (2020) using principal component analysis showed that approximately 60% of Somali ancestry is East African and 40% Western Eurasian.[^cite_note-\:1-286]

## Somali studies



Pioneering [Somali Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Studies) scholar, [Osman Yusuf Kenadid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osman_Yusuf_Kenadid)

Main article: [Somali studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_studies)

The scholarly term for research concerning Somalis and Greater Somalia is [Somali studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_studies). It consists of several disciplines such as [anthropology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology), [sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology), [linguistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics), [historiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiography) and [archaeology](18951655.md). The field draws from old [Somali chronicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_literature), records and oral literature, in addition to written accounts and traditions about Somalis from explorers and geographers in the Horn of Africa and the Middle East. Since 1980, prominent *Somalist* scholars from around the world have also gathered annually to hold the International Congress of Somali Studies.
