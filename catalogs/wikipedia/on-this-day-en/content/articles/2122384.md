The **National Assembly** ([Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language): Народно събрание, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Bulgarian): *Narodno sŭbranie*) is the [unicameral](204304.md) parliament and [legislative body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) of [Bulgaria](3415.md).

## History

The Bulgarian National Assembly was established in 1879 with the [Tarnovo Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnovo_Constitution). From 1881 to 1883, under the Regime of Full Powers of [Prince Alexander I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_of_Battenberg), it was replaced by an appointed State Council of Bulgaria. The [unicameral](204304.md) legislature provided by the Tarnovo Constitution, while favored by the [historical liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_\(Bulgaria\)), drew the ire of the [conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(Bulgaria\)) who proposed to replace it with a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) parliament.

During the [communist period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Bulgaria) between 1946 and 1989, the National Assembly was characterized as a [rubber stamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber_stamp_\(politics\)) for the [Bulgarian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Communist_Party) (BKP) or as only being able to affect issues of low sensitivity and salience to the Bulgarian communist regime. The BKP controlled nomination and election processes at every level in its political system, allowing it to stamp out any opposition. As a legacy of the communist-era system of government, where the National Assembly was constitutionally defined as the [supreme organ of state power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature_in_communist_states) and only branch of government, government ministers are formally appointed and dismissed by the Assembly on prime ministerial advice (rather than the President on prime ministerial advice, as in other parliamentary systems) even after the transition to democracy.

## Elections

Three types of candidates participate in the elections: parties, coalitions and independent candidates. Every citizen who has the right to vote can do so for one candidate. The ratio of the number of valid votes for the candidate relative to the total number of valid votes gives the candidate the support of voters. The Central Election Commission is the institution responsible for all elections in the country.

The National Assembly is composed of parliamentary groups and independent members, with parliamentary groups representing a political party, and they, in turn, represent their voters.

Since constitutional changes enacted in 2023, the constitution now states that a dissolved parliament continues to function until the new tenure of the National Assembly is constituted (previously, once a parliament was dissolved the deputies automatically lost their mandates, and the National Assembly could not convene).[^cite_note-1]

## Ordinary National Assembly

By the Constitution (Art. 62 to 91), The National Assembly consists of 240 members elected for a four-year term, elected by [proportional representation](23298.md) in multi-seat [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituencies). Political parties must garner a minimum of 4% of the national vote in order to enter the Assembly. Bulgaria has a multi-party system. In order to be eligible as an MP, one must be a Bulgarian citizen over the age of 21 years who is neither interdicted nor is serving a custodial sentence. Bulgarian citizens with dual nationality must be resident in Bulgaria during the last eighteen months before their election; this residency requirement does not apply to Bulgarian citizens without dual nationality.

The National Assembly is responsible for enactment of laws, approval of the state budget, scheduling of presidential elections, selection and dismissal of the [Prime Minister](335071.md) and other ministers, declaration of war, concluding peace and deployment of troops outside Bulgaria, declares a state of war or other state of emergency on the proposal of the President or the Cabinet, grants amnesty, and ratification of international treaties and agreements. It is headed and presided by the [Chairperson of the National Assembly of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_National_Assembly_of_Bulgaria).

The Assembly administers the publication of the *[State Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Gazette)*, Bulgaria's gazette of record.

### Procedure

The National Assembly is inaugurated by the eldest attending elected member of Parliament. On the first day of sitting, they preside over the election of the Speaker (Chairperson) and Deputy speakers, form the parliamentary groups, and appoint the secretaries of the Assembly.[^cite_note-2]

Once elected, the Speakers retain their party allegiances, which means that they remain as MPs and are allowed to take part in debates and voting.

More than 121 MPs must be present in order for any session to commence, and 50%+1 of those present must vote "for" any point of order or bill to be approved, unless the specific matter of the vote requires another majority.

Parliament sits Wednesday to Friday, and sessions begin at 9 am. Parliamentary committees sit in the afternoons.

### Electing a government

In order to elect a government, the National Assembly must have formed the parliamentary groups and have secured a majority. The President of the Republic calls to consultations for electing a government the parliamentary groups. Per article 99 of the Constitution, the President gives out the first mandate to the candidate for a Prime Minister of the largest parliamentary group in order to elect a government. If after 7 days, the candidate fails to propose a government, the second mandate goes to the PM candidate of the second largest parliamentary group. If the second mandate fails as well, the President gives out the third and last mandate to the candidate of one of the other parliamentary groups. If the mandate is delivered, the President signs it and proposes to the National Assembly to elect that government. In order for the government to be elected, there need to be present more than 121 votes "for".

Ministers may be chosen from among the MPs, or they may be experts outside Parliament. All MPs picked to be Cabinet ministers lose their MP status, and the next candidate from the party list of the constituency takes his place.

### Layout

#### Old Parliament House

In 2020 and 2021, [the old Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Parliament_House,_Sofia) was only used for special occasions, such as the opening and closing of the legislative session or the inauguration of a new president.[^cite_note-partyhouse-3] Following the [April 2021 parliamentary elections](58556414.md), the legislature returned to the old parliament building.

The Chamber is made up of 286 seats, all facing the 5-seat speaker's bench in a 26 x 11 arrangement. In front of the Speaker, also facing the chamber, is the pulpit, in front of which is the stenographers' desk.

Parties sit in parliamentary groups, loosely following the rule that the political left sits to the Speaker's left and the political right to his right. Generally, the largest parties choose the chamber's left, right or centre wings, with smaller blocks accommodating themselves wherever convenient. Individual MPs will sometimes sit entirely outside their block or stand, and, since compulsory electronic registration was implemented, may even vote from any seat in the house.

To the speaker's right, also facing the chamber, is a section with 17 seats reserved for the Cabinet, any of whom may or may not be present at any time during a parliamentary session. Any of them may, however, be called up by Parliament at any time if needed.[^cite_note-4]

#### Largo Parliament House

Located in the former [Bulgarian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Communist_Party) [headquarters building](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9F%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B5%D0%BD_%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%BC) in [the Largo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largo,_Sofia), The Chamber is made up of 270 seats arranged in a [hemicycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemicycle) of twelve rows, all facing the 7-seat speaker's bench. In front of the Speaker, also facing the chamber, is the pulpit containing a stenographer's desk at the center and two sections reserved for the Cabinet, each with 13 seats, at the sides.

The new plenary hall, which is located under a glass roof, is larger than the old one and can be easily remodeled to fit the 400-member [Grand National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Bulgaria).[^cite_note-partyhouse-3] It was used briefly in 2020 and 2021 during the end of [GERB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GERB)'s third government. Following the April 2021 parliamentary elections, the legislature returned to the old parliament building.

However, in 2023, following a decision of the [49th National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_National_Assembly_of_Bulgaria), parliamentary sessions were once again moved to the former Party House building in [the Largo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largo,_Sofia), where the legislature has continued to meet since.

## Grand National Assembly

Main article: [Grand National Assembly of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_National_Assembly_of_Bulgaria)



The building of the first National Assembly of Bulgaria in [Veliko Tarnovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veliko_Tarnovo)

In addition to the ordinary National Assembly, a Grand National Assembly (Велико народно събрание, *Veliko narodno sabranie*) may be convened for matters of special jurisdiction, such as: 1) Adoption of a new Constitution; 2) Amendment of certain articles of the Constitution, e.g. those related with the basic civil rights; 3) Changes in the territory (gain or loss) of the Republic, etc. Before the World War II the Grand National Assembly was also competent in electing the Regency of the Bulgarian Kingdom if the tzar had not come to age. The First and the Third Grand National Assemblies also elected the first two Bulgarian monarchs after the liberation from Ottoman rule – Prince (Knjaz) [Alexander Battenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_of_Battenberg) and Prince (Knjaz) [Ferdinand Saxe Coburg-Gotha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Bulgaria).

As an organ, the Grand National Assembly was introduced with the [Tarnovo Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnovo_Constitution) of 1879, abolished in 1947 and reintroduced with the 1991 constitution. In different constitutional provisions, it was constituted by a different number of representatives. According to the 1991 Constitution, it consists of 400 deputies (as opposed to 240 in the ordinary one). The 1991 Constitution was adopted by the Seventh Grand National Assembly and was composed of 200 members being elected by proportional representation and the other 200 under a [first-past-the-post voting](261709.md) system. The Constitution provides that the elections for Grand National Assembly shall be conducted in the same manner as those for the Ordinary National Assembly.

A [qualified majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualified_majority) of 2/3 during three voting procedures on separate dates is required for a decision to be made. The Grand National Assembly can also serve as an ordinary National Assembly, taking care of regular legislative activities in urgent cases only. After it has concluded its work on the matter for which it was elected, the Grand National Assembly is dissolved *ex lege* and the President of the Republic shall appoint elections for an ordinary National Assembly.

A total of seven Grand National Assemblies have been in operation in Bulgaria, the last one from 10 July 1990 to 12 July 1991 adopting the current constitution.

## Building

Main article: [Bulgaria National Assembly Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_National_Assembly_Building)



View from [Tsar Osvoboditel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar_Osvoboditel)

The National Assembly's main building has been proclaimed a monument of culture for its historic significance. Situated in downtown [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia), it was designed in the [Neo-Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Renaissance) style by [Konstantin Jovanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Jovanovi%C4%87).



Office house of the National Assembly ([former House](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9F%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B5%D0%BD_%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%BC) of the [BCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Communist_Party)) used as National Assembly building from 2020 to 2021 and from 2023&#x20;

Due to insufficient space in the main building at Parliament Square, the National Assembly is now housed by the former headquarters of the Bulgarian Communist Party, located at [the Largo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Largo) - the so-called Party House. Initially, only administrative offices have been relocated, but proposals to convert it into an interior space for the [plenary chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plenary_chamber) have been made since 1996, with the relocation taking place in 2020.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-partyhouse-3] After the April 2021 Bulgarian parliamentary election, the National Assembly moved again to the old Parliament House because [ITN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_Is_Such_A_People), [Democratic Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Bulgaria), [ISMV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Up!_Mafia,_Get_Out!), and [DPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_Rights_and_Freedoms) viewed the Party House building as a symbol of [Bulgaria's communist past](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Bulgaria).[^cite_note-6] In 2023, the 49th National Assembly decided to relocate its plenary sessions back to the former Party House building,[^cite_note-svoboda2023-7] where it continues to convene.
