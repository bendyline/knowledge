**Albay** (IPA: [\[ɐlˈbaɪ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Tagalog)),  officially the **Province of Albay** ([Central Bikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bikol_language): *Probinsya kan Albay*; [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Lalawigan ng Albay*) is a [province](165100.md) in the [Bicol Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Region) of the [Philippines](23440.md), mostly on the southeastern part of the island of [Luzon](69375.md). Its capital (and largest city) is the city of [Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legazpi,_Albay), the regional center of the whole [Bicol Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Region), which is located in the southern foothill of [Mayon Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayon_Volcano).[^cite_note-4]

The province was added to the [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Network of Biosphere Reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Network_of_Biosphere_Reserves) in March 2016.[^cite_note-5]

## History

### Early history

Long before the Spaniards arrived, Albay had a thriving civilization. Formerly called *Ibat*, and then *Libog*, Albay was once ruled by Gat Ibal, an old chief who also founded the old [barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay) of Sawangan, now part of the [City of Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legazpi,_Albay). Historian [William Henry Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Scott_\(historian\)) wrote that in the local epic called *siday* entitled "Bingi of Lawan", an Albay [datu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu) by the name of Dumaraog went to Lawan (present-day [Laoang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laoang), [Northern Samar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Samar)) to ask for the hands of Bingi bringing with him 100 ships. Datu Iberein came from the settlement of Lawan. He was described by Scott as a “Samar datu by the name of Iberein was rowed out to a Spanish vessel anchored in his harbor in 1543 by oarsmen collared in gold; while wearing on his own person earrings and chains.”[^cite_note-6]

### Spanish colonial era



Manalang Ancestral House, an authentic [Bahay na bato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahay_na_bato) in Tabaco City.&#x20;

In July 1569, [Luis Enriquez de Guzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Enriquez_de_Guzman), a member of the expedition led by *Maestro de Campo* Mateo de Saz and Captain [Martin de Goiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_de_Goiti), led a group that crossed from [Burias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burias_\(island\)) and [Ticao islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticao_Island) and landed on a coastal settlement called *Ibalon* in what is now the province of [Sorsogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorsogon). From this point, another expedition was sent to explore the interior and founded the town of [Camalig](2063412.md).



[Cagsawa Ruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagsawa_Ruins), a preliminary excavation of the Cagsawa ruins by the Bulacan State University, show that the Spanish incorporated [Mesoamerican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican) influences in constructing the complex.

[^cite_note-7]

In 1573, [Juan de Salcedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Salcedo) penetrated the [Bicol Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Peninsula) from the north as far south as [Libon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libon,_Albay), establishing the settlement of Santiago de Libon. Jose Maria Peñaranda, a military engineer, was made “*corregidor*” of the province on May 14, 1834. He constructed public buildings and built roads and bridges.

The entire Bicol peninsula was organized as one province with two divisions, [Camarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camarines) in the northwest and [Ibalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibalon) in the southeast. In 1636, the two *partido*s were separated, and Ibalon became a separate province with Sorsogon as the capital. In the 17th century, [Moro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people) slave raiders from the southern Philippines ravaged the northeastern coastal areas of the province of Albay.[^cite_note-8] By the end of the 1700s, Albay had 12,339 native families and 146 [Spanish Filipino families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipinos).[^cite_note-Estadismo1-9]<sup>: 539 </sup>[^cite_note-Estadismo2-10]<sup>: 31, 54, 113 </sup> In the 1800s, the natives numbered 103,955 whereas the mestizos numbered 2,598; this, further broken down to 579 Spanish-Mestizos and 933 Chinese-Mestizos.[^cite_note-Dic-11]<sup>: 285 </sup>

By year 1818, a new census which included the islands of [Masbate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masbate), [Catanduanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catanduanes), and [Ticao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticao_Island), under the province of Albay yielded the presence 35,321 native families and 1,334 Spanish-Filipino families, all under Albay's jurisdiction.[^cite_note-Buzeta-12]<sup>: 603 </sup> The distribution of the Spanish-Filipino families are as follows: 4 ([Manito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manito,_Albay)), 45 (Bacon, now part of [Sorsogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorsogon)), 52 ([Gubat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gubat)), 28 ([Casiguran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casiguran,_Sorsogon)), 18 ([Juban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juban,_Sorsogon)), 149 ([Sorsogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorsogon)), 19 ([Bulusan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulusan)), 16 ([Bulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulan,_Sorsogon)), 55 ([Quipia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jovellar)), 77 ([Bacacay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacacay)), 53 ([Malilipot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malilipot)), 225 ([Tabaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabaco), named after the Native American [Tobacco](30942.md) plant), 241 ([Malinao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malinao,_Albay)), 157 ([Tibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiwi,_Albay)), 18 ([Lagonoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagonoy)), and 114 ([San Jose, now part of Malilipot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malilipot)).[^cite_note-Buzeta-12]<sup>: 603 </sup> Outside of the Albay mainland in the surrounding islands; [Ticao island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticao_island) had 8 Spanish families, [Masbate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masbate) island had 1 Spanish family, and [Catanduanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catanduanes) had 150 Spanish families.[^cite_note-Buzeta-12]<sup>: 603 </sup>

[Mayon Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayon_Volcano), in one of its most violent eruptions, destroyed the five towns surrounding its base on February 1, 1814. This eruption forced the town of Cagsawa to relocate to its present site, [Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legazpi,_Albay).

A [decree](322949.md) was issued by [Governor-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_the_Philippines) [Narciso Claveria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narciso_Claver%C3%ADa_y_Zald%C3%BAa,_1st_Count_of_Manila) in 1846 separating [Masbate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masbate_Island), [Ticao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticao_Island), and [Burias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burias_\(island\)) from Albay to form the *comandancia* of Masbate. Albay was then divided into four districts: [Iraya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraya_\(Albay\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Tabaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabaco), Sorsogon, and [Catanduanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catanduanes).

#### Philippine revolution

Glicerio Delgado, a condemned *_insurecto_* (insurgent), started revolutionary activities in the province. With a headquarters in the mountain of [Guinobatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinobatan,_Albay) town, he joined the [revolutionary government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katipunan) of Albay as a lieutenant in the infantry.

A unit of the [Philippine Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Guard_\(Philippines\)) was then organized by the Spanish military authorities. Mariano Riosa was appointed major of the Tabaco Zone, which comprised all the towns along the seacoast from [Albay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legazpi,_Albay) to [Tiwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiwi,_Albay) while Anacieto Solano was appointed major for the Iraya Zone, which was made up of the towns from [Daraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraga,_Albay) to [Libon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libon,_Albay). Each town was organized into sections of fifty men under the command of a lieutenant.

During the [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution) on September 22, 1898, the provisional revolutionary government of Albay was formed with Anacieto Solano as provisional president. Major General [Vito Belarmino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vito_Belarmino), the appointed military commander, reorganized the [Filipino Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolutionary_Army) in the province.

### American colonial era

Following the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War), the new colonial power the [United States](3434750.md) did not recognize the country's independence, which had been declared in June 1898, and the Americans subsequently acquired direct control of the country as a de facto colony, under the terms of the [Treaty of Paris (1898)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1898\)), over which the Filipinos had no say.[^cite_note-13] During the [Philippine–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine%E2%80%93American_War), Brigadier General [William August Kobbé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_August_Kobb%C3%A9) headed the expedition that landed at the ports of Sorsogon, [Bulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulan,_Sorsogon) and [Donsol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donsol,_Sorsogon). From there, the Americans marched to Legazpi and captured it.

Although a [civil government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_government) was established in Albay on April 26, 1901, Colonel [Harry Hill Bandholtz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Hill_Bandholtz), Commanding Officer of the Constabulary in the Bicol Region, said that General [Simeon Ola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeon_Ola), with a thousand men, continued to defy American authority after the capture of Belarmino in 1901. Ola was later captured with about six hundred of his men.

### Japanese occupation

Following the December 12, 1941, [Japanese invasion of Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Legazpi) during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), the Kimura Detachment of the [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army) occupied Albay. The province was defended only by the [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) unit under the command of Major Francisco Sandico.[^cite_note-14]

During the Japanese Occupation, the military general headquarters of the [Commonwealth Army of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commonwealth_Army) remained active from January 3, 1942, to June 30, 1946, and the 5th Constabulary Regiment of the [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) was established from October 28, 1944, to June 30, 1946, and stationed in Albay. Then came the clearing operations and anti-Japanese insurgency in the [Bicol Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Peninsula), helped by the local Bicolano resistance. Some Bicolano [guerrilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla) groups invaded around the province of Albay during the Japanese Insurgencies between 1942 and 1944 and were supported by local Filipino troops under the Philippine Commonwealth Army and pre-war Philippine Constabulary 5th Infantry Regiments attacking the enemy soldiers of the Japanese Imperial Army. In the aftermath of three years of siege and conflicts, many Bicolano guerrillas were forced to retreat by the Japanese around the province before liberation in 1945 by Allied forces.

### Martial law era

Main article: [Human rights abuses of the Marcos dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship)

[Oas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oas,_Albay), Albay, was one of the localities particularly harmed by the [Dictatorship of Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictatorship_of_Ferdinand_Marcos); in the waning days of the dictatorship, Oas was hit by a series of [political killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_killings_during_the_Marcos_dictatorship) targeting those who dared to speak out against the abuses of the Military and against Ferdinand Marcos.[^cite_note-BantayogProfileClementeRagragio-15]

The most prominent of the victims was Clemente Ragragio, the municipal sanitation inspector of [Ligao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligao) who was later also assigned to Oas. Known for his effectiveness, he had been awarded the 1983 Best Sanitary Inspector for Albay.[^cite_note-AltheaAbergos20161102-16] However, this led the dictatorship's local administrators suspecting him of being a rebel sympathizer, because his close relationship with locals in far-flung barangays allowed him to move around fearlessly, and because he expressed disagreements with the dictatorship's governance. A killer shot him three times in front of his house in the early evening of August 21, 1985, and the government did not investigate his murder. He was later honored by having his name inscribed on the wall of remembrance at the Philippines' [Bantayog ng mga Bayani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantayog_ng_mga_Bayani) (lit. Monument of Heroes), which honors the martyrs and heroes who fought to restore democracy in the wake of the Marcos dictatorship.[^cite_note-BantayogProfileClementeRagragio-15]

## Geography



The [Mayon Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayon_Volcano) dominates the geography of Albay.

Albay has a total land area of 2,575.77 square kilometres (994.51 sq mi),[^cite_note-popcen2015_r3-17] which makes it the 53rd biggest province. The province is bordered by the provinces of [Camarines Sur](255883.md) to the north and [Sorsogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorsogon) to the south. To the northeast lies [Lagonoy Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagonoy_Gulf), which separates the province from [Catanduanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catanduanes). To the southwest of the province is the [Burias Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burias_Pass) with the island of [Burias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burias_Island) of [Masbate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masbate) province located about 14 kilometres (8.7 mi) offshore.

In 2016, an area of 250,000 hectares (620,000 acres) was declared a [UNESCO Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Biosphere_Reserve). The Albay Biosphere Reserve is home to 182 terrestrial plant species, of which 46 are endemic to the Philippines. Its marine waters and coastal area also provide habitat to five of the world's seven [marine turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_turtle) species, as well as mangrove, seagrass and seaweed ecosystems.[^cite_note-albay-18]

### Topography

The province is generally mountainous with scattered fertile plains and valleys. On the eastern part of the province is a line of volcanic mountains starting with the northernmost [Malinao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Malinao) in Tiwi, followed by [Mount Masaraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Masaraga) and the free-standing [Mayon Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayon_Volcano). Separated by the Poliqui Bay is the [Pocdol Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocdol_Mountains) in the town of [Manito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manito,_Albay).

The [stratovolcano](162404.md) of [Mayon](256485.md) standing at around 2,462 metres (8,077 ft), is the highest point of the province. It is the most famous [landform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landform) in Albay and in the whole [Bicol Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Region). This [active volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_volcanoes_in_the_Philippines) falls under the jurisdiction of eight municipalities and cities of Albay: [Camalig](2063412.md), [Daraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraga,_Albay), [Guinobatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinobatan,_Albay), [Legazpi City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legazpi,_Albay), [Ligao City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligao_City), [Malilipot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malilipot,_Albay), [Santo Domingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Domingo,_Albay), and [Tabaco City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabaco_City).

The western coast of the province is mountainous but not as prominent as the eastern range with the highest elevation at around 490 metres (1,610 ft).[^cite_note-19] Among these mountains are Mount Catburawan in Ligao and Mount Pantao in [Oas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oas,_Albay).

The population of Albay in the 2024 census was 1,379,398 people,[^cite_note-29], making it the 20th most populous [province](165100.md) in the country. It had a density of 540 inhabitants per square kilometre or 1,400 inhabitants per square mile. Based on the 2007 census, there were 208,640 households in the province with an average size of 5.22 persons, significantly higher than the national average of 4.99.

### Religion

Prior to colonization, the region had a complex religious system that involved various deities. These deities include: Gugurang, the supreme god who dwells inside of [Mount Mayon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Mayon) where he guards and protects the sacred fire that his brother [Aswang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aswang) was trying to steal. Whenever people disobey his orders, wishes and commit numerous sins, he would cause Mount Mayon to burst lava as a sign of warning for people to mend their crooked ways. Ancient Bikolanos had a rite performed for him called Atang.[^cite_note-ourownvoice.com-30][^cite_note-youtube.com-31] Asuang, the evil god who always tries to steal the sacred fire of Mount Mayon from his brother, Gugurang. Addressed sometimes as Aswang, he dwells mainly inside Mount Malinao. As an evil god, he would cause the people to suffer misfortunes and commit sins.[^cite_note-ourownvoice.com-30][^cite_note-youtube.com-31] Enemy of Gugurang and a friend of Bulan the god of the Moon; Haliya, the masked goddess of the moonlight and the archenemy of Bakunawa and protector of Bulan. Her cult is composed primarily of women. There is also a ritual dance named after her as it is performed to be a countermeasure against Bakunawa.;[^cite_note-32] *Bulan*, the god of the pale moon, he is depicted as a pubescent boy with uncommon comeliness that made savage beast and the vicious mermaids (Magindara) tame. He has deep affection towards Magindang, but plays with him by running away so that Magindang would never catch him. The reason for this is because he is shy to the man that he loves. If Magindang manages to catch Bulan, Haliya always comes to free him from Magindang's grip; *Magindang*, the god of the sea and all its creatures. He has deep affection to the lunar god Bulan and pursues him despite never catching him. Due to this, the Bicolanos reasoned that it is to why the waves rise to reach the Moon when seen from the distant horizon. Whenever he does catch up to Bulan, Haliya comes to rescue Bulan and free him immediately; *Okot*, god of forest and hunting; and *[Bakunawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakunawa)*, a gigantic sea serpent deity who is often considered as the cause of eclipses, the devourer of the Sun and the Moon, and an adversary of Haliya as Bakunawa's main aim is to swallow Bulan, who Haliya swore to protect for all of eternity.[^cite_note-33]

#### Catholicism



[Nuestra Señora de la Porteria Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraga_Church) in [Daraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraga,_Albay), built in 1773

[Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism_in_the_Philippines) is the predominant religion in the province, affiliated by 96.1% of the men and 95.7% of the women of Albay.[^cite_note-34] Each town has its own [fiesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival) for their [patrons](68055.md) and patronesses.

#### Others

Other religious denominations are the [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md) (INC) has 2 percent of the province, other [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) churches such as the [Baptist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist_Church), [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_Church), other [Evangelical Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Christian), [Seventh-day Adventists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh-day_Adventist), [Mormons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon), [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) as well as [Islam](6037917.md).

### Languages

Albay is home to several languages and host to different native languages of Bicol Region. Out of seven Bikol languages (excluding the [Bisakol languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisakol_languages), which are [Bisayan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisayan_languages)), only [Pandan Bikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandan_Bikol_language) of northern [Catanduanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catanduanes) is not used or which the origin is not from Albay. The languages in the province are very diverse which includes the languages of [Albay Bikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albay_Bikol_language) group which comprises the languages of [West Miraya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Miraya_Bikol), East Miraya, [Libon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libon_language) and [Buhinon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buhinon_language). Of the four [Albay Bikol languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albay_Bikol_language), Buhinon is the only one not used in Albay but rather in [Buhi, Camarines Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buhi,_Camarines_Sur).

[Rinconada Bikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinconada_Bikol_language) is a minority language in the province and used by people in barangays of [Libon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libon,_Albay) and [Polangui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polangui) that are near the boundary of [Bato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bato,_Camarines_Sur) and [Iriga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iriga) in [Camarines Sur](255883.md). Another primary language used in the province is [Central Bikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bikol_language) which is the [native tongue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) of the population on the eastern coast of the [Bicol Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Peninsula). Both Albay Bikol languages and Rinconada Bikol are members of [Inland Bikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Bikol_languages) group of languages while [Central Bikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bikol_language) is a language member of [Coastal Bikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Bikol_languages). The Tabaco - Legazpi - Sorsogon dialect of Central Bikol is spoken in [Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legazpi,_Albay), [Tabaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabaco) and neighboring municipalities on the east side of Albay, and some parts of northern [Sorsogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorsogon) (especially in [Sorsogon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorsogon_City)).

The majority of the inhabitants also understand [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) and [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language) as second languages.

[Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) is the main industry in Albay, which produces crops like [coconut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [sugar](27712.md) and [abacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abac%C3%A1). [Handicrafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handicraft) are the major source of rural income and comprises a fairly large share in the small-scale industries of the province. Forestry, cement production and paper-making are other sources of livelihood. The manufacture of abacá products such as [Manila hemp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_hemp), hats, bags, mats, furniture, home decors,[^cite_note-LoqalPH-44] and slippers is one of the main sources of income in the rural areas. Production of abaca fiber experienced a boom from the late 1800s until the American period.[^cite_note-45] Fishing is the main livelihood along both shores of the province. Tourism, especially related to Mayon Volcano, also creates income for Albay. For the year 2013, Albay had a total of 339,445 foreign tourist arrivals.

In the December, 2023 Pili Fiesta, Director Marita A. Carlos of [DOST-PCAARRD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Science_and_Technology_\(Philippines\)) introduced technologies on [pili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarium_ovatum) (C. ovatum tree) production in Albay, [Bicol Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Region). She aimed to promote pili as a flagship industry.[^cite_note-46]

### Roads

Albay has 383.22 kilometers (238.12 mi) of national roads, mostly paved with asphalt, with 5.25 kilometers (3.26 mi) remaining unpaved.[^cite_note-48] [Maharlika Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharlika_Highway) (N1/AH26) serves as the principal road connection between other provinces. Most of the province is served by *secondary national roads*, assigned route numbers in the series, namely **Bicol-630** and **Bicol-640**. Almost all of the cities and municipalities are connected by national roads, except for [Rapu-Rapu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapu-Rapu,_Albay).

In order to spur development in the province, the Toll Regulatory Board declared Toll Road 5 the extension of [South Luzon Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Luzon_Expressway).[^cite_note-49] A 420-kilometer, four lane expressway starting from the terminal point of the now under construction SLEX Toll Road 4 at Barangay Mayao, [Lucena City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucena_City) in [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon) to [Matnog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matnog), Sorsogon, near the Matnog Ferry Terminal. On August 25, 2020, San Miguel Corporation announced that they will invest the project which will reduce travel time from Lucena to Matnog from 9 hours to 5.5 hours.[^cite_note-50]

### Water transport



Legazpi harbour and port

Albay is the region's principal trans-shipment point with its ports: Tabaco International Port, Legazpi National Port, Pio Duran Provincial Port, and the Pantao Regional Port.

### Air transport

Traveling to the province by air is served by the [Bicol International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_International_Airport) in the municipality of [Daraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daraga,_Albay), adjacent municipality of Legazpi City. The airport is the province's gateway from [Manila](184334.md) and [Cebu City](285868.md) in the Visayas. The province was formerly served by the now defunct [Legazpi Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legazpi_Airport).

### Railroads

Albay is served by the mainline of the [Philippine National Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_National_Railways) (PNR), and has commuter service between [Naga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga,_Camarines_Sur) in [Camarines Sur](255883.md). Trains to Manila (Tutuban), the *Mayon Limited*, is suspended from November 2012. Four stations serve Polangui, Ligao, Guinobatan, and Legazpi, respectively.
