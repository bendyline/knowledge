**Eilat** ([/eɪˈlɑːt/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*ay-LAHT*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English) also [/eɪˈlæt/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*ay-LAT*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): אֵילַת [\[eˈlat\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hebrew) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c2/Eilat.ogg/Eilat.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c2/Eilat.ogg/Eilat.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): إِيلَات, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Īlāt*) is [Israel](9282173.md)'s southernmost city, with a population of 56,004,[^cite_note-Israelpopulations-1] a [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Eilat) and popular [resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort) at the northern tip of the [Red Sea](26191.md), on what is known in Israel as the Gulf of Eilat and in [Jordan](7515964.md) as the [Gulf of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Aqaba). The city is considered a tourist destination for domestic and international [tourists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Israel) heading to Israel.

Eilat is located at the southernmost tip of Israel, at the southern end of the [Arava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabah) valley and the [Negev](142273.md) desert, adjacent to the [Egyptian](8087628.md) resort city of [Taba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taba,_Egypt) to the south, the [Jordanian](7515964.md) port city of [Aqaba](328399.md) to the east, and within sight of [Haql](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haql), [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), across the gulf to the southeast.

Eilat hosts numerous hotels, holiday resorts, and beaches. Its renowned [coral reefs](87410.md) make it a popular destination for [diving tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_tourism), with activities such as [snorkeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snorkeling) and [scuba diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuba_diving). The city's shopping centers benefit from its status as a [tax-free zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax-free_shopping). Notable attractions include the [Dolphin Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphin_Reef), where visitors can swim with dolphins; the [Coral Beach Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilat's_Coral_Beach), the northernmost shallow coral reef on Earth; the [Coral World Underwater Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_World_Underwater_Observatory), the Ice Mall, and the city's marina and sea-side promenade, home to many bars, restaurants and shops. The city also offers opportunities for hiking and exploration in nearby natural reserves like [Timna Valley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timna_Valley) and the Eilat Mountains.

Eilat's arid [desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert) and low humidity are moderated by proximity to a warm sea. Temperatures often exceed 40 °C (104 °F) in summer, and 21 °C (70 °F) in winter, while water temperatures range between 20 and 26 °C (68 and 79 °F). Eilat averages 360 sunny days a year.[^cite_note-2]

## Etymology

Further information: [Elath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elath) and [Aqaba § Name](328399.md#Name)

The name *Eilat* was given to the abandoned frontier post of *Umm al-Rashrāsh* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): أم الرشراش) in 1949 by the [Committee for the Designation of Place-Names in the Negev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebraization_of_Palestinian_place_names#1949:_Committee_for_the_Designation_of_Place-Names_in_the_Negev), referring to [Elath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elath), a biblical location thought to be located nearby in modern [Jordan](7515964.md), possibly at [Tell el-Kheleifeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_el-Kheleifeh).[^cite_note-3] Elath was mentioned multiple times in the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible), notably as a [station of the Exodus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stations_of_the_Exodus) where King [Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solomon) built ships. The Jordanian location of Elath was known at the time of the [renaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebraization_of_Palestinian_place_names); one committee member, [Yeshayahu Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshayahu_Press), stated "when the real Eilat finally is in our hands, our settlement will expand and reach over to there."[^cite_note-4]

## Geography



Eilat aerial view

The geology and landscape are varied: igneous and [metamorphic rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphic_rock), sandstone and limestone; mountains up to 892 metres (2,927 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level); broad valleys such as the Arava, and seashore on the Gulf of Aqaba. With an annual average rainfall of 28 millimetres (1.1 in) and summer temperatures of 40 °C (104 °F) and higher, [water resources](18581463.md) and vegetation are limited. "The main elements that influenced the region's history were the copper resources and other minerals, the ancient international roads that crossed the area, and its geopolitical and strategic position. These resulted in a settlement density that defies the environmental conditions."[^cite_note-5]

### Early history

[Archaeological excavations](18951655.md) uncovered impressive prehistoric ([Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Palestine_region#Neolithic)) tombs dating to the [7th millennium BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_millennium_BC) at the western edge of Eilat, while nearby copper workings and mining operations at [Timna Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timna_Valley_Park) are one of the oldest on earth.[^cite_note-6]

An Islamic community of 250–400 residents flourished for one or two generations in the area during the [Umayyad period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_period).[^cite_note-YR-7] It dealt in agriculture, copper mining and [smelting](28716.md), as well as trading with pilgrims by taking advantage of the adjacent *[Darb el-Hajj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darb_el-Hajj?action=edit\&redlink=1)* ('Way of the [Hajj](23487999.md)') route in the 8th century CE.[^cite_note-YR-7] There was some sporadic reuse of the abandoned site by pastoralists during the 9th.[^cite_note-YR-7] It was one of six very similar contemporary villages discovered in close vicinity, two along the northbound leg of the *Darb*, and three to the southwest, along the coastal road, all of them depending on the nearby port of [Ayla](328399.md#History) and the Hajj road.[^cite_note-YR-7] Its remains were found and excavated in 1989, between the industrial zone at the northern edge of Eilat and nearby [Kibbutz](106200.md) [Eilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilot).[^cite_note-YR-7]

### Modern city

Israeli soldiers stabilize a flag pole whilst another soldier climbs it in order to raise an improvised flag; the soldier is seen about halfway up the flag pole. Other soldiers look on.

The raising of the [Ink Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ink_Flag), when Eilat was captured during [Operation Uvda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Uvda)

During the [British Mandate era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), a British police post existed in the area, which was known as *Umm Al-Rashrash*. The area was designated as part of the Jewish state in the [1947 UN Partition Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine). During the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), the abandoned police post, which consisted of five clay huts, was taken without a fight on March 10, 1949, as part of [Operation Uvda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Uvda).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] This marked the end of Israel's war for independence. The [memorial at the historical place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ink_Flag#History) of March 1949 was declared a [National Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Heritage_Sites_of_Israel) in 1994.

The town developed over the following years. [Eilat Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilat_Airport) was built in 1949 and individual ships began arriving in the 1950s, but as there were no dedicated port facilities they unloaded their goods at sea. In the early 1950s, Eilat was a small and remote town, populated largely by port workers, soldiers, and former prisoners. The town's development accelerated in 1955, when it had a population of about 500. The Timna Copper Mines[^cite_note-10] near the [Timna Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timna_Valley) and the [Port of Eilat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Eilat) were opened that year and concerted effort by the Israeli government to populate Eilat began, starting with Jewish immigrant families from [Morocco](19291.md) being resettled there. Eilat began to develop rapidly after the [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis) in 1956, with its tourism industry in particular starting to flourish. The [Israeli Navy](1370788.md)'s Eilat naval base was founded that year.[^cite_note-11] The town's population grew to 5,300 in 1961. [Yoseftal Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoseftal_Medical_Center) and the [Eilat–Ashkelon pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Israel_pipeline) were completed in 1968, and the population increased further, reaching 13,100 in 1972 and 18,900 in 1983.



Eilat in 1963

After the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War) Arab countries maintained a state of hostility with Israel, blocking all land routes; Israel's access to and trade with the rest of the world was by air and sea alone. Further, Egypt denied passage through the [Suez Canal](29323.md) to Israeli-registered ships or to any ship carrying cargo to or from Israeli ports. This made Eilat and its sea port crucial to Israel's communications, commerce and trade with Africa and Asia, and for oil imports. Without recourse to a port on the Red Sea Israel would have been unable to develop its diplomatic, cultural and trade ties beyond the [Mediterranean](19006.md) basin and Europe. This happened in 1956 and again in 1967, when Egypt's closure of the [Straits of Tiran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Tiran) to Israeli shipping effectively blockaded the port of Eilat. In 1956, this led to Israel's participation alongside Britain and France in the war against Egypt sparked by the [Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis), while in 1967, 90% of Israeli oil passed through the Straits of Tiran.[^cite_note-Shlaim224-12] Oil tankers that were due to pass through the straits were delayed.[^cite_note-Shlaim27-13][^cite_note-14] The straits' closure was cited by Israel as an additional *[casus belli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casus_belli)* leading to the outbreak of the [Six-Day War](29328.md). Following [peace treaties](404505.md) signed with [Egypt in 1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian%E2%80%93Israeli_Peace_Treaty) and [Jordan in 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Jordan_peace_treaty), Eilat's borders with its neighbors were finally opened.

#### Israeli–Arab conflict

Eilat is especially defended by its own special forces unit [LOTAR Eilat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LOTAR_Eilat). It is a reservist [special forces unit of the IDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Special_Forces_Units) trained in counter-terrorism and [hostage rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostage_rescue) in the Eilat area, which has taken part in many counter-terrorist missions in the region since its formation in 1974. The Lotar unit is composed solely of reservists, citizens who must be Eilat residents between the ages of 20 and 60, who are on call in case of a terrorist attack on the city. It is one of only three units in the IDF authorized to free hostages on its own command.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] In 2007 the [Eilat bakery bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilat_bakery_bombing) killed three civilian bakers.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-warned-18] This was the first such attack in Eilat proper,[^cite_note-19] although other terror attacks had been carried out in the area.[^cite_note-20]

In 2011, terrorists infiltrated Israel across the [Sinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula) border to execute multiple attacks on Highway 12, including a civilian bus and private car a few miles north of Eilat, in what became known as the [2011 southern Israel cross-border attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_southern_Israel_cross-border_attacks).[^cite_note-Haaretz1.382132-21][^cite_note-22]

In order to prevent terrorist infiltration of Israel from the Sinai, Israel has built the [Egypt–Israel barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt%E2%80%93Israel_barrier), a steel barrier equipped with cameras, radar and motion sensors along the country's southern border.[^cite_note-23] The fence was completed in January 2013.[^cite_note-24]

During the [Gaza war](74998519.md) and ensuing [Red Sea crisis](75207766.md), the port saw an 85% reduction in volumes and by 12 July 2024 the port of Eilat declared bankruptcy resulting in it seeing no economic activity or revenue for eight months.[^cite_note-25]

In March 2026, Eilat was the targeted by missiles and drone attacks during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

#### Future development plans



Eilat's north beach

In July 2012, Israel signed an agreement with China to cooperate in building the [high-speed railway to Eilat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_railway_to_Eilat), a railway line which will serve both passenger and freight trains. It will link Eilat with [Beersheba](312755.md) and [Tel Aviv](31453.md), and will run through the [Arava Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arava_Valley) and [Nahal Zin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahal_Zin?action=edit\&redlink=1) (see [Zin Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zin_Desert#Modern_Desert_of_Zin)).[^cite_note-29]

The former [Eilat Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilat_Airport) was closed on 18 March 2019 after the opening of [Ramon Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Airport). The land occupied by the former airport is to be redeveloped. The new Ramon Airport opened in January 2019, 18 kilometres (11 miles) north of Eilat and replaced both Eilat Airport and the civilian use of [Ovda Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovda_Airport).[^cite_note-30] Hotels and apartment buildings, containing a total of 2,080 hotel rooms and 1,000 apartments will be constructed on the site, as well as 275 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam) of public space and pedestrian paths. The plans also set aside space for the railway line and an underground railway station. The plan's goal is to create an urban continuum between the city center and North Beach, as well as tighten the links between the city's neighborhoods, which were separated by the airport.[^cite_note-31]

In addition, there are plans to move the [Port of Eilat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Eilat) and the [Eilat–Ashkelon pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Israel_pipeline) terminal to the northern part of the city, as well as to turn it into a university town of science and research, and brand it an international sports city. All these projects are part of a plan to increase Eilat's population to 150,000 people and build 35,000 hotel rooms.[^cite_note-32]

In January 2026, Merkot and [Minister of Energy and Infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Energy_and_Infrastructure_\(Israel\)) [Eli Cohen](46195208.md) announced that the water company was investing *[₪](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E2%82%AA)* 800 million [NIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_new_shekel) to expand the capacity of the Sabcha [desalination](156787.md) plant and double the water supply to Eilat.[^cite_note-33]

## Climate

Eilat has a [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BWh*)[^cite_note-34] with very hot, dry summers and warm, similarly dry winters. Winters are usually between 11–23 °C (52–73 °F). Summers are usually between 26–40 °C (79–104 °F). There are relatively small [coral reefs](87410.md) near Eilat; however, 50 years ago they were much larger. They are the only coral reefs in Israel.[^cite_note-35]

The overwhelming majority of Eilat's population are [Jews](25955086.md). Arabs constitute about 4% of the population.[^cite_note-44] Eilat's population includes a large number of foreign workers, estimated at over 10,000 working as caregivers, hotel workers and in the construction trades. Eilat also has a growing [Israeli Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Arab) population, as well as many affluent [Jordanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_people) and [Egyptians](31912046.md) who visit in the summer months.

In 2007, over 200 [Sudanese refugees from Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_refugees_in_Egypt) who arrived in Israel illegally on foot were given work and allowed to stay in Eilat.[^cite_note-boston-45][^cite_note-csm-46][^cite_note-ynetjw-47]

## Education

The educational system of Eilat accommodates more than 9,000 children in eight day-care centers, 67 pre-kindergartens and kindergartens, 10 elementary schools, and 3 six-year high schools. Also, there are some special-education schools and religious schools.[^cite_note-48] [Ben Gurion University of the Negev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Gurion_University_of_the_Negev) maintains a campus in Eilat. The Eilat branch has 1,100 students, about 75 percent from outside the city. The [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem) also maintains a campus in Eilat.[^cite_note-49] In 2010, a new student dormitory was funded and built by the Jewish Federation of Toronto, the Rashi Foundation, Ben-Gurion University of the Negev and the municipality of Eilat.[^cite_note-50] The [SPNI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPNI)'s Eilat Field School on the outskirts of Eilat offers special hiking tours that focus on [desert ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_ecology), the [Red Sea](26191.md), [bird migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_migration) and other aspects of Eilat's flora and fauna.[^cite_note-51] The [Hesder Yeshiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesder) Ayelet Hashachar, is based in Eilat, established in 1997.[^cite_note-52]

## Healthcare

[Yoseftal Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoseftal_Medical_Center), established in 1968, is Israel's southernmost hospital, and the only hospital covering the southern [Negev](142273.md). With 65 beds, the hospital is Israel's smallest. Special services geared to the Red Sea region are a [hyperbaric chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_chamber) to treat victims of diving accidents and [kidney dialysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_dialysis) facilities open to vacationing tourists.[^cite_note-53]

## Economy

In the 1970s tourism became increasingly important to the city's economy as other industries shut down or were drastically reduced. Today tourism is the city's major source of income, although Eilat became a [free trade zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade_zone) in 1985.[^cite_note-61]

### Dive tourism



Coral World Underwater Observatory



Eilat sea

Skin and [scuba diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuba_diving) equipment is for hire on or near all major beaches. Scuba diving equipment rental and [compressed air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_air) are available from diving clubs and schools all year round. Eilat is located in the [Gulf of Aqaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Aqaba), one of the most popular diving destinations in the world. The coral reefs along Eilat's coast remain relatively pristine and the area is recognized as one of the prime diving locations in the world.[^cite_note-MFA-69] About 250,000 dives are performed annually in Eilat's 11 km (6.8 mi) coastline, and diving represents 10% of the tourism income of this area.[^cite_note-70] In addition, given the proximity of many of these reefs to the shore, non-divers can encounter the Red Sea's reefs with relative ease.[^cite_note-MFA-69] Water conditions for SCUBA divers are good all year round, with water temperatures around 21–25 °C (70–77 °F), with little or no currents and clear waters with an average of 20–30 metres (66–98 feet) visibility.

## Archaeology

Despite harsh conditions, the region has supported large populations as far back as 8,000 BCE. Several [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) sites have been discovered in Eilat in its vicinity. One of them is a late Neolithic burial site with [fan scrapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_scraper), dated by archaeologists to 5410–4250 BCE.[^cite_note-71]

Exploration of ancient sites began in 1861, but only 7% of the area has undergone serious [archaeological excavation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_field_survey). Some 1,500 ancient sites are located in a 1,200-square-kilometer (460 sq mi) area. In contrast to the gaps found in settlement periods in the neighbouring Negev Highlands and Sinai, these sites show continuous settlement for the past 10,000 years.

## Voting Patterns

In the [2022 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Israeli_legislative_election), 62 percent of voters in Eilat voted for the parties of the right-wing coalition led by [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md), while 35 percent voted for the parties of the center-left coalition led by [Yair Lapid](2448072.md) and 1 percent voted for the Arab parties.[^cite_note-72]
