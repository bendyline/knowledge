**The Chevron Championship** is a professional women's golf tournament. An event on the [LPGA Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LPGA_Tour), it is one of the tour's five [major championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_major_golf_championships), and has traditionally been the first of the season since its elevation to major status in 1983. Since 2023, it has been played in the [Greater Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Houston) area.

Founded in 1972 by singer and actress [Dinah Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinah_Shore) and [Colgate-Palmolive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colgate-Palmolive) chairman [David Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Foster_\(Royal_Navy_officer\)), it was played at the [Mission Hills Country Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Hills_Country_Club) in [Rancho Mirage, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Mirage,_California), from its inception through 2022. Sometimes referred to as *The Dinah Shore* in deference to its founder, the tournament has had many official sponsored titles, all of which included Shore's name until 2000. It relocated to Houston in 2023 due to a new sponsorship agreement with [Chevron Corporation](284749.md). The tournament was held at the Jack Nicklaus Signature Course in [The Woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Woodlands,_Texas) from 2023 to 2025, and will move to Houston's Memorial Park course beginning 2026.

The championship's time at Mission Hills is associated with several traditions; in 1988, [Amy Alcott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Alcott) established a tradition of the event's champion diving into the pond that surrounds the 18th hole—a tradition that would be carried over to the tournament's future hosts. An annual [women's festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Skirts_Dinah_Shore_Weekend) that originally coincided with the championship has been held since 1991.

## History

Founded in [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_LPGA_Tour) by [Colgate-Palmolive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colgate-Palmolive) chairman David Foster[^cite_note-ofmtfs-1][^cite_note-twtwal-2] and entertainer [Dinah Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinah_Shore),[^cite_note-sivbfrb-3] the championship has been classified as a major since [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_LPGA_Tour).[^cite_note-sivwwjd-4] From its inception, it had been held annually at Shore's favorite course, the [Mission Hills Country Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Hills_Country_Club) in [Rancho Mirage, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Mirage,_California), southeast of [Palm Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Springs,_California).[^cite_note-sivbfrb-3] It is the first major of the year, usually played in late March or early April.

At its debut in 1972 as a 54-hole event, it was the richest event in women's golf;[^cite_note-sivbfrb-3][^cite_note-rnkmntn-5][^cite_note-blawnti-6] its purse was more than double that of the [LPGA Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_LPGA_Championship) or the [U.S. Women's Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_U.S._Women's_Open). The first edition invited all winners of tour events from the previous ten seasons.[^cite_note-sivbfrb-3]

After over twenty years of sponsorship by [Nabisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabisco), and parent company [Kraft Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraft_Foods), Japanese airline [All Nippon Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Nippon_Airways) became the title sponsor of the tournament in late 2014, renaming the tournament the ANA Inspiration (in reference to its slogan "Inspiration of Japan").[^cite_note-ANA-7]

In October 2021, a six-year sponsorship agreement with energy company [Chevron Corporation](284749.md) was announced that would see the tournament renamed The Chevron Championship, with an increased prize fund of \$5 million in 2022 (up from \$3 million in 2021).[^cite_note-\:1-8][^cite_note-\:2-9]

It was also announced that the tournament would move to the Jack Nicklaus Signature Course at The Club at Carlton Woods in [The Woodlands, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Woodlands,_Texas) in 2023, with a new mid-April scheduling. Dinah Shore's daughter Melissa Montgomery established an advisory board to oversee the tournament's transition.[^cite_note-\:1-8][^cite_note-\:2-9] Officials intended the date change to allow for network television coverage on [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf_Channel_on_NBC).[^cite_note-\:1-8][^cite_note-\:2-9] It was also suggested that the tournament was moved in deference to the [Augusta National Women's Amateur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_National_Women's_Amateur), which had begun to conflict with the tournament in 2019.[^cite_note-10] Following the move, the Dinah Shore's traditional weekend at Mission Hills was taken up by the [PGA Tour Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGA_Tour_Champions)' [Galleri Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleri_Classic).[^cite_note-11]

In January 2026, it was announced that the tournament would move to Houston's [Memorial Park Golf Course](3131552.md)—the host of the [PGA Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGA_Tour)'s [Houston Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Open)—for 2026.[^cite_note-12]

### "Poppie's Pond"

From 1988 to 2022, the winner traditionally celebrated her victory by jumping in the pond surrounding the 18th green.[^cite_note-utbett-13] The pond is known as Champions Lake or "Poppie's Pond" as it was dubbed in 2006 honor of [Terry Wilcox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Wilcox), the tournament director from 1994 through 2008; Wilcox is known as "Poppie" to his grandchildren.[^cite_note-poppyspond-14]

[Amy Alcott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Alcott) established the tradition in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Nabisco_Dinah_Shore) to celebrate her second win here,[^cite_note-vicplu-15] and repeated in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Nabisco_Dinah_Shore), including tournament host Dinah Shore.[^cite_note-alctrpr-16] It was not embraced by others until [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Nabisco_Dinah_Shore), when [Donna Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Andrews_\(golfer\)) made the leap, followed by [Nanci Bowen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanci_Bowen) the [next year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Nabisco_Dinah_Shore), and it became an annual tradition.[^cite_note-nbwn-17][^cite_note-nytplung-18] In [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Nabisco_Dinah_Shore), winner [Pat Hurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Hurst) waded in only up to her knees, as she could not swim.[^cite_note-readeg-19][^cite_note-sivhiab-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-ladiesoflake-22] Originally a very natural water hazard, the portion near the bridge was later lined with concrete and has treated water, more like a swimming pool.[^cite_note-23]

The tradition was carried over to The Woodlands; the lake between the 9th and 18th holes was [dredged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dredged), cleaned, and had netting installed to protect against [alligators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alligator), and a dock and ladder was built on the 18th hole.[^cite_note-\:3-24] Prior to the [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Chevron_Championship) tournament, several players expressed uncertainty over whether they would take the jump—now into a natural lake instead of the treated pool used in Mission Hills.[^cite_note-\:3-24] Its first champion, [Lilia Vu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilia_Vu), would take the jump into the lake.[^cite_note-25] With the move to Memorial Park, a man-made pond will be constructed near Memorial Park's 18th green in time for the 2027 tournament, with a temporary concrete-lined pool being used for 2026; organizers hoped the dedicated construction would alleviate the hesitancy over the lake jump used at The Woodlands.[^cite_note-26]

### Associated events

Main article: [Club Skirts Dinah Shore Weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Skirts_Dinah_Shore_Weekend)

A women's festival known as the [Club Skirts Dinah Shore Weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Skirts_Dinah_Shore_Weekend) has been held in the city of [Palm Springs, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Springs,_California), featuring concerts by female musicians, comedy shows, parties, and other events and networking opportunities. The Dinah Shore Weekend was first organized in 1991 by promoter Mariah Hanson; it expanded upon afterparties that had become associated with the Dinah Shore tournament, and, until 2021, was held in the same week.[^cite_note-sivmjpar-27][^cite_note-\:0-28]

A large number of [lesbians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbians) and [bisexual women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisexual) visited the Palm Springs area for the festival and tournament;[^cite_note-sivmjpar-27][^cite_note-\:0-28] the Dinah Shore Weekend was described by [*Los Angeles*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_\(magazine\)) as the "largest annual gathering of queer women and their female allies",[^cite_note-29] while the tournament as a whole had been described as "[spring break](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_break) for lesbians."[^cite_note-spring_break_for_lesbians-30][^cite_note-31] The event continues to be held annually in Palm Springs; ahead of the tournament's relocation, the festival moved to an autumn scheduling beginning in 2021 due to weather conditions and lower hotel costs. The delay was also initially induced by the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]
