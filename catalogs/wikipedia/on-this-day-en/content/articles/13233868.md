**Kristin Kay Mayes** (born September 6, 1971)[^cite_note-1] is an American lawyer, politician, and journalist who has been the 27th [Arizona attorney general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_attorney_general) since 2023. A member of the [Democratic Party](5043544.md), she was previously a professor of practice at [Sandra Day O'Connor College of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Day_O'Connor_College_of_Law) and chair of the [Arizona Corporation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Corporation_Commission).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

Mayes was first elected in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Arizona_Attorney_General_election), defeating [Republican](32070.md) nominee [Abraham Hamadeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Hamadeh) by 280 votes.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] She is the second woman elected to the office (after [Janet Napolitano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Napolitano)), the [third openly lesbian woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first_openly_LGBT_politicians_in_the_United_States) elected [state attorney general](6065438.md) (after [Maura Healey](41875710.md) and [Dana Nessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Nessel)), and the [second openly LGBT person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first_openly_LGBT_politicians_in_the_United_States) elected to statewide office in [Arizona](21883824.md) (after [Kyrsten Sinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrsten_Sinema)).[^cite_note-\:3-7][^cite_note-\:4-8]

## Early life and education

Mayes was born and raised in [Prescott, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescott,_Arizona).[^cite_note-9] After graduating from [Prescott High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescott_High_School_\(Arizona\)) she attended [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University) (ASU) on a [scholarship from the Flinn Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flinn_Scholarship).[^cite_note-Flinn-10] While attending ASU she served as [editor in chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editor_in_chief) of the *[State Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Press)*, the university's newspaper.[^cite_note-ASU-11] She graduated [valedictorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valedictorian) from ASU with a degree in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science).[^cite_note-ASU-11]

## Journalism career

Mayes worked as a general assignment reporter for the *[Phoenix Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Gazette)*, and later as a political reporter for *[The Arizona Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Arizona_Republic)*, covering the [Arizona State Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Legislature).[^cite_note-ASU-11] Mayes then won the [Harry S. Truman Scholarship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman_Scholarship) and attended [graduate school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduate_school) at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in [New York](645042.md), where she earned a [Master of Public Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Public_Administration).[^cite_note-Flinn-10]

Following graduate school, Mayes returned to *The Arizona Republic*, where she covered the [2000 presidential campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_presidential_election) of Senator [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain), former Vice President [Dan Quayle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Quayle), [publisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publisher) [Steve Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Forbes) and Governor [George W. Bush](3414021.md).[^cite_note-ASU-11] From this experience, Mayes co-authored a book entitled *Spin Priests: Campaign Advisors and the 2000 Race for the White House*. After the presidential campaign, Mayes attended [ASU College of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Day_O'Connor_College_of_Law) and graduated [magna cum laude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_cum_laude).[^cite_note-ASU-11]

## Early political career

Mayes was the press secretary for [Janet Napolitano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Napolitano) during the [2002 Arizona gubernatorial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Arizona_gubernatorial_election).[^cite_note-\:1-12]

### Arizona Corporation Commission

Governor Napolitano appointed Mayes, then a Republican, to the [Arizona Corporation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Corporation_Commission) in October 2003.[^cite_note-\:1-12] She devoted much of her effort towards [pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipeline_transport) [safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety), [renewable energy](25784.md) and [natural gas](22131.md) issues.[^cite_note-13]

Mayes was elected to a full term in a [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_elections) special election, defeating [Libertarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_Party_of_Arizona) nominee Rick Fowlkes.[^cite_note-14] In [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Arizona), Mayes considered a candidacy for [Arizona's 1st congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona's_1st_congressional_district).[^cite_note-15] She was term-limited in 2010 and was succeeded in her position by fellow Republican [Brenda Burns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenda_Burns).[^cite_note-ASU-11]

In 2019, Mayes left the Republican Party and joined the Democratic Party, citing the expansion of [Trumpism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpism) within the Republican Party.[^cite_note-\:2-16]

## Arizona Attorney General



Mayes speaking at a 2023 event

### 2022 election

Mayes was the Democratic nominee in the [2022 Arizona Attorney General election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Arizona_Attorney_General_election), running against Republican [Abraham Hamadeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Hamadeh).[^cite_note-\:0-17] The race was one of the closest in Arizona history and required a mandatory recount because the vote difference was significantly less than the 0.5% vote threshold required by state law for recounts.[^cite_note-18] The recount started on December 5, 2022.[^cite_note-19]

In the final vote tally, Mayes led by 510 votes.[^cite_note-20] On December 29, Judge Timothy Thomason announced the results of the recount, confirming Mayes as the winner with a reduced margin of 280 votes.[^cite_note-21]

### Tenure

Mayes took office as Attorney General on January 2, 2023.&#x20;

In January 2024, Mayes announced that the Attorney General's office would file suit against Kroger's [proposed acquisition of Albertsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albertsons#Acquisition_by_Kroger), citing antitrust laws.[^cite_note-22] In June 2024, investigators from Mayes's office raided the [Apache County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_County,_Arizona) Attorney's Office in connection to allegations of County Attorney Michael Whiting's "misuse of public monies" and "threatening and intimidating a political opponent".[^cite_note-23]

On November 1, 2024, [Donald Trump](4848272.md) spoke at a rally in Arizona in which he said of former congresswoman [Liz Cheney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liz_Cheney), "She's a radical [war hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_hawk). Let's put her with a rifle standing there with nine barrels shooting at her. Okay, let’s see how she feels about it. You know when the guns are trained on her face — you know, they’re all war hawks when they’re sitting in Washington in a nice building". That day, Mayes launched an investigation as to whether Trump's statement qualified as a prosecutable [death threat](13262036.md) under Arizona law.[^cite_note-24]

Mayes supported Arizona's [constitutional amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Arizona_Proposition_139) codifying abortion rights.[^cite_note-25]

### Prosecution of 2020 fake electors

Main article: [Arizona prosecution of fake electors](76732452.md)

In July 2023, Mayes's office announced that it was "investigating the [transmission of an alternative slate of electors](71378172.md)" by allies of former President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) to be counted by Congress during the [2021 United States Electoral College vote count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_States_Electoral_College_vote_count).[^cite_note-26] Mayes's office [indicted 18 individuals in April 2024](76732452.md).

### Lawsuits against the Trump administration

As of October 2025, Mayes' office has filed 39 lawsuits against the [Trump administration](78298108.md). Collectively, these lawsuits protected \$1.5 billion in federal funds allocated to Arizona.[^cite_note-27]

## Personal life

Mayes is openly lesbian and has a daughter, Hattie. Mayes is the second woman elected Arizona Attorney General (after Janet Napolitano), the third openly lesbian woman elected attorney general of a state in the United States (after Maura Healey and Dana Nessel), and the second openly LGBT person elected to statewide office in Arizona (after Kyrsten Sinema).[^cite_note-\:3-7][^cite_note-\:4-8]
