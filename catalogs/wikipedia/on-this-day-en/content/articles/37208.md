# Landslide

**Landslides**, also known as **landslips**, **rockslips, rockfalls, mudslides, mudslips** or **rockslides**,[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] are several forms of [mass wasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_wasting) that may include a wide range of ground movements, such as [rockfalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockfall), [mudflows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudflows), shallow or deep-seated [slope failures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slope_failure) and [debris flows](4415356.md).[^cite_note-\:4-6] Landslides occur in a variety of environments, characterized by either steep or gentle slope gradients, from [mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain) ranges to [coastal](5236.md) [cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff) or even underwater,[^cite_note-7] in which case they are called [submarine landslides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_landslides).&#x20;

[Gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity) is the primary driving force for a landslide to occur, but there are other factors affecting [slope stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slope_stability) that produce specific conditions that make a slope prone to failure. In many cases, the landslide is triggered by a specific event (such as heavy [rainfall](19009110.md), an [earthquake](10106.md), a slope cut to build a road, and many others), although this is not always identifiable.&#x20;

Landslides are frequently made worse by human development (such as [urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl)) and resource exploitation (such as [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) and [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation)). [Land degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_degradation) frequently leads to less stabilization of soil by [vegetation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetation).[^cite_note-\:6-8] Additionally, global warming caused by [climate change](5042951.md) and other [human impact on the environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_impact_on_the_environment), can increase the frequency of natural events (such as [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather)) which trigger landslides.[^cite_note-9] [Landslide mitigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslide_mitigation) describes the policy and practices for reducing the risk of human impacts of landslides, reducing the risk of [natural disaster](58921.md).&#x20;

## Causes

Main article: [Causes of landslides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causes_of_landslides)



[The Mameyes Landslide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Puerto_Rico_floods), in the Mameyes [neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhood) of barrio [Portugués Urbano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugu%C3%A9s_Urbano) in [Ponce, Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponce,_Puerto_Rico), was caused by extensive accumulation of rains and, according to some sources, lightning. It buried more than 100 homes.



The landslide at [Surte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surte) in Sweden, 1950. It was a [quick clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quick_clay) slide that killed one person.

Landslides occur when the slope (or a portion of it) undergoes some processes that change its condition from stable to unstable. This is essentially due to a decrease in the [shear strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shear_strength_\(soil\)) of the slope material, an increase in the [shear stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shear_stress) borne by the material, or a combination of the two. A change in the stability of a slope can be caused by a number of factors, acting together or alone. Natural causes of landslides include:&#x20;

- increase in water content (loss of suction) or saturation by rain water infiltration, snow melting, or [glaciers](12463.md) melting;

  [^cite_note-10]

- rising of [groundwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater) and increase of pore water pressure (e.g. due to [aquifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifer) recharge in rainy seasons, or by rain water infiltration);

  [^cite_note-\:2-11]

- increase of hydrostatic pressure in cracks and fractures;

  [^cite_note-\:2-11]

  [^cite_note-\:3-12]

- loss or absence of vertical vegetative structure, [soil nutrients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_nutrients), and [soil structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_structure) (e.g. after a wildfire);

  [^cite_note-13]

- [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) of the top of a slope by rivers or sea [waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave);

  [^cite_note-14]

- physical and chemical [weathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weathering) (e.g. by repeated freezing and thawing, heating and cooling, salt leaking in the groundwater or mineral dissolution);

  [^cite_note-\:0-15]

  [^cite_note-\:1-16]

  [^cite_note-17]

- ground shaking caused by [earthquakes](10106.md), which can destabilize the slope directly (e.g., by inducing [soil liquefaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_liquefaction)) or weaken the material and cause movement that will eventually produce a landslide;

  [^cite_note-\:3-12]

  [^cite_note-18]

  [^cite_note-19]

- [volcanic eruptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano);

  [^cite_note-20]

- changes in pore fluid composition;

  [^cite_note-21]

- changes in temperature (seasonal or induced by climate change).

  [^cite_note-22]

  [^cite_note-23]

Landslides are aggravated by human activities, such as:&#x20;

- [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation), [cultivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tillage) and [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction);

- [vibrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscillation) from [machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinery) or [traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic);

  [^cite_note-24]

- [blasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_material) and [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining);

  [^cite_note-25]

- [earthwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthworks_\(engineering\)) (e.g. by altering the shape of a slope, or imposing new loads);

- in shallow [soils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil), the removal of deep-[rooted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root) [vegetation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetation) that binds [colluvium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colluvium) to [bedrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedrock);

- agricultural or forestry activities ([logging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logging)), and [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization), which change the amount of water infiltrating the soil.

- temporal variation in [land use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_use) and [land cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_cover) (LULC): it includes the human abandonment of farming areas, e.g. due to the economic and social transformations which occurred in Europe after the Second World War. [Land degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_degradation) and extreme rainfall can increase the frequency of [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_erosion) and landslide phenomena.

### Hungr-Leroueil-Picarelli classification

In traditional usage, the term landslide has at one time or another been used to cover almost all forms of [mass movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_movement_\(geology\)) of rocks and [regolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regolith) at the Earth's surface. Under this classification, six types of movement are recognized. Each type can be seen both in rock and in soil. A fall is a movement of isolated blocks or chunks of soil in free-fall. The term topple refers to blocks coming away by rotation from a vertical face. A slide is the movement of a body of material that generally remains intact while moving over one or several inclined surfaces or thin layers of material (also called shear zones) in which large deformations are concentrated. Slides are also sub-classified by the form of the surface(s) or shear zone(s) on which movement happens. The planes may be broadly parallel to the surface ("planar slides") or spoon-shaped ("rotational slides"). Slides can occur catastrophically, but movement on the surface can also be gradual and progressive. Spreads are a form of subsidence, in which a layer of material cracks, opens up, and expands laterally. Flows are the movement of fluidised material, which can be both dry or rich in water (such as in mud flows). Flows can move imperceptibly for years, or accelerate rapidly and cause disasters. Slope deformations are slow, distributed movements that can affect entire mountain slopes or portions of it. Some landslides are complex in the sense that they feature different movement types in different portions of the moving body, or they evolve from one movement type to another over time. For example, a landslide can initiate as a rock fall or topple and then, as the blocks disintegrate upon the impact, transform into a debris slide or flow. An avalanching effect can also be present, in which the moving mass entrains additional material along its path.&#x20;

#### Flows

&#x20;Slope material that becomes [saturated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifer) with water may produce a [debris flow](4415356.md) or [mud flow](2431323.md). However, also dry debris can exhibit flow-like movement.[^cite_note-30] Flowing debris or mud may pick up trees, houses and cars, and block bridges and rivers causing [flooding](50482.md) along its path. This phenomenon is particularly hazardous in [alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate) areas, where narrow gorges and steep valleys are conducive of faster flows. Debris and mud flows may initiate on the slopes or result from the fluidization of landslide material as it gains speed or incorporates further debris and water along its path. River blockages as the flow reaches a main stream can generate temporary dams. As the impoundments fail, a domino effect may be created, with a remarkable growth in the volume of the flowing mass, and in its destructive power.

A rock slide in [Guerrero](195816.md), Mexico

An [earthflow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthflow) is the downslope movement of mostly fine-grained material. Earthflows can move at speeds within a very wide range, from as low as 1 mm/yr[^cite_note-\:0-15][^cite_note-\:1-16] to many km/h. Though these are a lot like [mudflows](2431323.md), overall they are more slow-moving and are covered with solid material carried along by the flow from within. Clay, fine sand and silt, and fine-grained, pyroclastic material are all susceptible to earthflows. These flows are usually controlled by the pore water pressures within the mass, which should be high enough to produce a low shearing resistance. On the slopes, some earthflow may be recognized by their elongated shape, with one or more lobes at their toes. As these lobes spread out, drainage of the mass increases and the margins dry out, lowering the overall velocity of the flow. This process also causes the flow to thicken. Earthflows occur more often during periods of high precipitation, which saturates the ground and builds up water pressures. However, earthflows that keep advancing also during dry seasons are not uncommon. Fissures may develop during the movement of clayey materials, which facilitate the intrusion of water into the moving mass and produce faster responses to precipitation.[^cite_note-b1-31]&#x20;

A rock avalanche, sometimes referred to as [sturzstrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturzstrom), is a large and fast-moving landslide of the flow type. It is rarer than other types of landslides but it is often very destructive. It exhibits typically a long runout, flowing very far over a low-angle, flat, or even slightly uphill terrain. The mechanisms favoring the long runout can be different, but they typically result in the weakening of the sliding mass as the speed increases.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-\:5-34] The causes of this weakening are not completely understood. Especially for the largest landslides, it may involve the very quick heating of the shear zone due to friction, which may even cause the water that is present to vaporize and build up a large pressure, producing a sort of hovercraft effect.[^cite_note-35] In some cases, the very high temperature may even cause some of the minerals to melt.[^cite_note-36] During the movement, the rock in the shear zone may also be finely ground, producing a nanometer-size mineral powder that may act as a lubricant, reducing the resistance to motion and promoting larger speeds and longer runouts.[^cite_note-37] The weakening mechanisms in large rock avalanches are similar to those occurring in seismic faults.[^cite_note-\:5-34]&#x20;

#### Slides

Slides can occur in any rock or soil material and are characterized by the movement of a mass over a planar or curvilinear surface or shear zone.&#x20;

A debris slide is a type of slide characterized by the chaotic movement of material mixed with water and/or ice. It is usually triggered by the saturation of thickly vegetated slopes which results in an incoherent mixture of broken timber, smaller vegetation and other debris.[^cite_note-b1-31] Debris flows and avalanches differ from debris slides because their movement is fluid-like and generally much more rapid. This is usually a result of lower shear resistances and steeper slopes. Typically, debris slides start with the detachment of large rock fragments high on the slopes, which break apart as they descend.&#x20;

Clay and silt slides are usually slow but can experience episodic acceleration in response to heavy rainfall or rapid snowmelt. They are often seen on gentle slopes and move over planar surfaces, such as over the underlying bedrock. Failure surfaces can also form within the clay or silt layer itself, and they usually have concave shapes, resulting in rotational slides.&#x20;

### Shallow and deep-seated landslides



Hotel Panorama at [Lake Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Garda). Part of a hill of [Devonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonian) [shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale) was removed to make the road, forming a dip-slope. The upper block detached along a bedding plane and is sliding down the hill, forming a jumbled pile of rock at the toe of the slide.

&#x20;Slope failure mechanisms often contain large uncertainties and could be significantly affected by heterogeneity of soil properties.[^cite_note-38] A landslide in which the sliding surface is located within the [soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil) mantle or [weathered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weathering) [bedrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedrock) (typically to a depth from few decimeters to some meters) is called a shallow landslide. Debris slides and [debris flows](4415356.md) are usually shallow. Shallow landslides can often happen in areas that have slopes with high permeable soils on top of low permeable soils. The low permeable soil traps the water in the shallower soil generating high water pressures. As the top soil is filled with water, it can become unstable and slide downslope.



Deep-seated landslide on a mountain in Sehara, [Kihō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kih%C5%8D,_Mie), Japan caused by torrential rain of [Tropical Storm Talas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Talas_\(2011\))



Landslide of soil and [regolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regolith) in [Pakistan](23235.md)&#x20;

Deep-seated landslides are those in which the sliding surface is mostly deeply located, for instance well below the maximum rooting depth of trees. They usually involve deep [regolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regolith), weathered rock, and/or [bedrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedrock) and include large slope failures associated with translational, rotational, or complex movements.[^cite_note-39] They tend to form along a plane of weakness such as a [fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)) or [bedding plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedding_plane). They can be visually identified by concave [scarps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_scarp) at the top and steep areas at the toe.[^cite_note-40] Deep-seated landslides also shape [landscapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscapes) over geological timescales and produce sediment that strongly alters the course of [fluvial streams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluvial_landforms_of_streams).[^cite_note-41]&#x20;

## Related phenomena

- An [avalanche](73321.md), similar in mechanism to a landslide, involves a large amount of ice, snow and rock falling quickly down the side of a mountain.

- A [glacier collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacier_collapse) is similar in mechanism to a landslide, but the primary flow material is solid ice that has detached from a glacier. It is distinct from an [avalanche](73321.md) in that glacier collapses occur at much more gradual slope angles, typically 10-20 degrees.

- A [pyroclastic flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroclastic_flow) is caused by a collapsing cloud of hot [ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_ash), gas and rocks from a volcanic explosion that moves rapidly down an erupting [volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano).

- Extreme precipitation and flow can cause [gully formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gully) in flatter environments not susceptible to landslides.

### Resulting tsunamis

See also: [Tsunami § Tsunami generated by landslides](31161.md#Tsunami_generated_by_landslides)

Landslides that occur undersea, or have impact into water e.g. significant rockfall or [volcanic collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_landslide) into the sea,[^cite_note-42] can generate [tsunamis](31161.md). Massive landslides can also generate [megatsunamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megatsunami), which are usually hundreds of meters high. In 1958, one such [tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Lituya_Bay_megatsunami) occurred in [Lituya Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lituya_Bay) in Alaska,[^cite_note-Le_Bas-43][^cite_note-44] and another was triggered in [Tracy Arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy_Arm) in Alaska in 2025.[^cite_note-45]&#x20;

## Landslide prediction mapping

See also: [Slope stability analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slope_stability_analysis)

Landslide hazard analysis and mapping can provide useful information for catastrophic loss reduction, and assist in the development of guidelines for sustainable [land-use planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-use_planning). The analysis is used to identify the factors that are related to landslides, estimate the relative contribution of factors causing slope failures, establish a relation between the factors and landslides, and to predict the landslide hazard in the future based on such a relationship.[^cite_note-46] The factors that have been used for landslide hazard analysis can usually be grouped into [geomorphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomorphology), [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology), land use/land cover, and [hydrogeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogeology). Since many factors are considered for landslide hazard mapping, [geographic information system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_information_system) (GIS) is an appropriate tool because it has functions of collection, storage, manipulation, display, and analysis of large amounts of spatially referenced data which can be handled fast and effectively.[^cite_note-47] Cardenas reported evidence on the exhaustive use of GIS in conjunction of uncertainty modelling tools for landslide mapping.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] [Remote sensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_sensing) techniques are also highly employed for landslide hazard assessment and analysis. Before and after aerial photographs and satellite imagery are used to gather landslide characteristics, like distribution and classification, and factors like slope, [lithology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithology), and land use/land cover to be used to help predict future events.[^cite_note-50] Before and after imagery also helps to reveal how the landscape changed after an event, what may have triggered the landslide, and shows the process of regeneration and recovery.[^cite_note-51]&#x20;

Using satellite imagery in combination with GIS and on-the-ground studies, it is possible to generate maps of likely occurrences of future landslides.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] Such maps should show the locations of previous events as well as clearly indicate the probable locations of future events. In general, to predict landslides, one must assume that their occurrence is determined by certain geologic factors, and that future landslides will occur under the same conditions as past events.[^cite_note-54] Therefore, it is necessary to establish a relationship between the geomorphologic conditions in which the past events took place and the expected future conditions.[^cite_note-55]&#x20;

[Natural disasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_disasters) are a dramatic example of people living in conflict with the environment. Early predictions and warnings are essential for the reduction of property damage and loss of life. Because landslides occur frequently and can represent some of the most destructive forces on earth, it is imperative to have a good understanding as to what causes them and how people can either help prevent them from occurring or simply avoid them when they do occur. [Sustainable land management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_land_management) and development is also an essential key to reducing the negative impacts felt by landslides.&#x20;



A Wireline [extensometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extensometer) monitoring slope displacement and transmitting data remotely via radio or Wi-Fi. In situ or strategically deployed extensometers may be used to provide early warning of a potential landslide.

[^cite_note-56]

GIS offers a superior method for landslide analysis because it allows one to capture, store, manipulate, analyze, and display large amounts of data quickly and effectively. Because so many variables are involved, it is important to be able to overlay the many layers of data to develop a full and accurate portrayal of what is taking place on the Earth's surface. Researchers need to know which variables are the most important factors that trigger landslides in any given location. Using GIS, extremely detailed maps can be generated to show past events and likely future events which have the potential to save lives, property, and money.&#x20;

Since the '90s, GIS have been also successfully used in conjunction to [decision support systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_support_system), to show on a map real-time risk evaluations based on monitoring data gathered in the area of the [Val Pola disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Pola_landslide) (Italy).[^cite_note-57]&#x20;

## Extraterrestrial landslides

Evidence of past landslides has been detected on many bodies in the [Solar System](26903.md), but since most observations are made by probes that only observe for a limited time and most bodies in the Solar System appear to be geologically inactive not many landslides are known to have happened in recent times. Both Venus and Mars have been subject to long-term mapping by orbiting satellites, and examples of landslides have been observed on both planets.&#x20;

## Landslide mitigation

This section is an excerpt from [Landslide mitigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslide_mitigation).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Landslide_mitigation\&action=edit)]



&#x20;Two [retaining walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retaining_wall) and other [soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil) [slope stabilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slope_stabilization) methods in [Chieti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chieti), [Italy](14532.md).

[Landslide mitigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslide_mitigation) refers to human construction activities on [slopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_\(slope\)) undertaken with the goal of lessening the effect of landslides. Landslides can be triggered by many, sometimes [concomitant](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/concomitant) causes. In addition to shallow [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) or reduction of [shear strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shear_strength) caused by [seasonal rainfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seasonal_rainfall), landslides may be triggered by [anthropic](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/anthropic) activities, such as adding excessive weight above the slope or digging at either the mid-slope or foot of the slope.&#x20;

Often, individual phenomena join to generate instability over time, which often does not allow a reconstruction of the evolution of a particular landslide. Therefore, landslide hazard mitigation measures are not generally classified according to the phenomenon that might cause a landslide.[^cite_note-Landslide_mitigation_Landslides_Monitoring-68] Instead, they are classified by the sort of [slope stabilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slope_stabilization) method used:&#x20;

- Geometric methods, in which the geometry of the hillside is changed (in general the slope);

- [Hydrogeological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogeological) methods, in which an attempt is made to lower the groundwater level or to reduce the water content of the material

- Chemical and mechanical methods, in which attempts are made to increase the shear strength of the unstable mass or to introduce active external forces (e.g. [anchors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_anchor), rock or ground [nailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nailing)) or passive (e.g. structural wells, piles or reinforced ground) to counteract the destabilizing forces.

Each of these methods varies somewhat with the type of material that makes up the slope.&#x20;

## Landslide monitoring

The monitoring of landslides is essential for estimating the dangerous situations, making it possible to issue alerts on time, to avoid loses of lives and property, and to have proper planning and reducing measures in place. Currently, there exist different type of techniques aimed to monitor landslides:&#x20;

### [Remote sensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_sensing) techniques

- [InSAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interferometric_synthetic-aperture_radar) (Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar): This remote sensing technique measures ground displacement over time with high precision. It is ideal for large-scale monitoring.

  [^cite_note-69]

  [^cite_note-\:7-70]

- [LiDAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidar) (Light Detection and Ranging): Provides detailed 3D models of terrain to detect changes over time by comparison of different point clouds acquired over time.

  [^cite_note-71]

  [^cite_note-72]

- Optical satellite imagery: Useful for identifying surface changes, geomorphological features (e.g. cracks and scarps) and mapping landslide-prone areas.

  [^cite_note-\:7-70]

- [UAVs](58900.md) (Unmanned Aerial Vehicles): This technique captures high-resolution images and topographic data in inaccessible areas.

  [^cite_note-73]

- Thermal imaging: Thermal images enable to detects temperature variations that may indicate water movement or stress in the slope.

  [^cite_note-74]

### Ground-based techniques

- [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) (Global Positioning System): Tracks ground movements at specific points over time using a constellation of satellites orbiting around the Earth.

- [Topographic surveys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topographic_survey): Measures displacements of marked targets on a slope.

- [Ground-based radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground-Based_Radar_Prototype) (GB-SAR): Continuously monitors surface deformation using a SAR sensor and detects movement in real-time. It follows the same principle than InSAR.

  [^cite_note-75]

### Geotechnical instrumentation

- [Piezometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piezometers): Monitors groundwater levels and pore water pressure, which are critical triggers for landslides.

- [Load cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Load_cell): Measures stress changes in retaining structures or anchors.

- [Tiltmeters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiltmeter): Detects small angular changes in the slope surface or retaining walls.

- [Extensometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extensometer): Measures displacement along cracks or tension zones.

- [Inclinometers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclinometer): Detects subsurface movements by monitoring changes in the inclination of a borehole.

  [^cite_note-76]

### Seismic techniques

•Geophones and accelerometers: Detect seismic vibrations or movements that might indicate slope instability.&#x20;

## Climate-change impact on landslides

Climate-change impact on temperature, both average rainfall and rainfall extremes, and evapotranspiration may affect landslide distribution, frequency and intensity.[^cite_note-77] However, this impact shows strong variability in different areas.[^cite_note-\:8-78] Therefore, the effects of climate change on landslides need to be studied on a regional scale. Climate change can have both positive and negative impacts on landslides. Temperature rise may increase evapotranspiration, leading to a reduction in soil moisture and stimulate vegetation growth, also due to a CO<sub>2</sub> increase in the atmosphere. Both effects may reduce landslides in some conditions. On the other side, temperature rise causes an increase of landslides due to&#x20;

- the acceleration of snowmelt and an increase of rain on snow during spring, leading to strong infiltration events.

  [^cite_note-79]

- Permafrost degradation that reduces the cohesion of soils and rock masses due to the loss of interstitial ice.

  [^cite_note-80]

  &#x20;This mainly occurs at high elevation.

- Glacier retreat that has the dual effect of relieving mountain slopes and increasing their steepness.

Since the average precipitation is expected to decrease or increase regionally,[^cite_note-\:8-78] rainfall induced landslides may change accordingly, due to changes in infiltration, groundwater levels and river bank erosion. Weather extremes are expected to increase due to climate change including heavy precipitation.[^cite_note-\:8-78] This yields negative effects on landslides due to focused infiltration in soil and rock[^cite_note-81] and an increase of runoff events, which may trigger debris flows.&#x20;
