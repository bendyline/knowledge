**Ladakh** (English: [/ləˈdɑːk/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), Hindustani [\[ləd̪ːaːx\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu))[^cite_note-OED-Ladakhi-12] is a region administered by [India](14533.md) as a [union territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory)[^cite_note-ladakh-britannica-current-1] and constitutes an eastern portion of the larger [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir) region that has been the subject of a [dispute](3030955.md) between India and [Pakistan](23235.md) since 1947 and India and [China](5405.md) since 1959.[^cite_note-tertiary-kashmir-2] Ladakh is bordered by the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) of China[^cite_note-13] to the east, the Indian state of [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md) to the south, both the Indian-administered union territory of [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) and the Pakistani-administered [Gilgit-Baltistan](18807336.md) to the west, and the southwest corner of [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) across the [Karakoram Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram_Pass) in the far north. It extends from the [Siachen Glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siachen_Glacier) in the [Karakoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram) range to the north to the main Great [Himalayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya) to the south.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The eastern end, consisting of the uninhabited [Aksai Chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksai_Chin) plains, is claimed by the [Indian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Government) as part of Ladakh, but has been under Chinese control since 1962.[^cite_note-Columbia_Gazetteer-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-Brookings_2022_f218-18][^cite_note-19]

In the past, Ladakh gained importance from its strategic location at the crossroads of important trade routes,[^cite_note-TransHimalayan-20] but as Chinese authorities closed the borders between Tibet Autonomous Region and Ladakh in the 1960s, international trade dwindled. Since 1974, the Government of India has encouraged [tourism in Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Ladakh). Ladakh was established as a union territory of India on 31 October 2019, following the passage of the [Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_Reorganisation_Act,_2019). Prior to that, it was part of the [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(state\)) state.

The largest city in Ladakh is [Leh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leh), followed by [Kargil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil);[^cite_note-21] as of 2026, the territory consists of seven [notified districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Ladakh). Main [river valleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_valleys) include the [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River), [Shyok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shyok_River), [Nubra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubra_River), [Suru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suru_River_\(Indus\)), [Dras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dras_River) and [Zanskar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanskar_River). The main populated regions are these river valleys, but the mountain slopes also support pastoral [Changpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changpa) nomads.[^cite_note-Crossroads-22] The main religious groups in the region are [Muslims](6037917.md) (46%, mainly [Shia](26961.md)), [Buddhists](3267529.md) (40%, mainly [Tibetan Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism)), and [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) (12%) with the remaining 2% made up of other religions.[^cite_note-census2011-C01-23] Ladakh is the [largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_area) and the [second least populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_population) union territory administered by India.[^cite_note-24] Its culture and history are closely related to those of [Tibet](31516.md).[^cite_note-culturetib-25] As Ladakh is strategically important, the [Indian military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_military) maintains a strong presence in the region.

## Name and etymology

The classical name in [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_script): ལ་དྭགས, [Wylie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wylie_transliteration): La dwags, [THL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/THL_Simplified_Phonetic_Transcription): la dak means the "land of high passes". *Ladak* is its pronunciation in several [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetic_languages) dialects. The English spelling *Ladakh* is derived from [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): *ladāx*.[^cite_note-Francke1992-26][^cite_note-27]

Originally, Ladakh was an umbrella term for the kingdoms of [Maryul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Maryul) and Baltiyul. Today, it commonly refers only to Maryul, which is administered by India, while the term [Baltistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltistan) is now used for Baltiyul, which is administered by Pakistan.

Medieval Islamic scholars called Ladakh the "Great Tibet" (derived from Turko-Arabic *Ti-bat*, meaning "highland"); Baltistan and other trans-Himalayan states in Kashmir's vicinity were referred to as "Little Tibets".[^cite_note-luciano-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-31]

The 7th-century Buddhist traveller [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang) describes the region in his accounts as *Mo-lo-so*[^cite_note-32], which has been reconstructed by academics as *\*Malasa*, *\*Marāsa*, or *\*Mrāsa*, which is believed to have been the original name of the region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPetech,_The_Kingdom_of_Ladakh19777–8-33][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHoward_&_Howard,_Historic_Ruins_in_the_Gya_Valley201486-34]

It has also been called Lal Bhumi (*Red Earth*). Names in the local language include Kanchapa (*Land of snow*) and Ripul (*Country of mountains*).[^cite_note-Mann_2002_p._15-35]

## History

Main article: [History of Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ladakh)

### Ancient history



South Asia in c. 565 CE

Rock carvings found in many parts of Ladakh indicate that the area has been inhabited from [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) times.[^cite_note-LoramCharlie-36] Ladakh's earliest inhabitants were nomads known as Kampa.[^cite_note-Zutshi_126-37] Later settlements were established by Mons from [Kullu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kullu) and [Brokpas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokpa) who originated from [Gilgit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgit).[^cite_note-Zutshi_126-37] Around the 1st century, Ladakh was a part of the [Kushan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire). [Buddhism](3267529.md) spread into western Ladakh from Kashmir in the 2nd century.

For much of the first millennium, western Tibet comprised [Zhangzhung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhung) kingdom(s), which practised the [Bon religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_religion). Sandwiched between Kashmir and Zhangzhung, Ladakh is believed to have been alternatively under the control of one or other of these powers. Academics find strong influences of Zhangzhung language and culture in "upper Ladakh" (from the middle section of the Indus valley to the southeast).[^cite_note-38] The penultimate king of Zhangzhung is said to have been from Ladakh.[^cite_note-39]

From around 660 CE, the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) and the [Tibetan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Empire) started contesting the "four garrisons" of the [Tarim Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarim_Basin) (present day [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang)), a struggle that lasted three centuries. Zhangzhung fell victim to Tibet's ambitions in c. 634 and disappeared. [Karkota Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karkota_Empire) and the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) too joined the contest for Xinjiang soon afterwards. Baltistan and Ladakh were at the centre of these struggles.[^cite_note-40] Academics infer from the slant of *[Ladakh Chronicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh_Chronicles)* that Ladakh may have owed its primary allegiance to Tibet during this time, but that it was more political than cultural. Ladakh remained Buddhist and its culture was not yet Tibetan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFisher,_Rose_&_Huttenback,_Himalayan_Battleground196315–16-41]

In the 9th century, Tibet's ruler [Langdarma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langdarma) was assassinated and Tibet [fragmented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era_of_Fragmentation). [Kyide Nyimagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyide_Nyimagon), Langdarma's great-grandson, fled to West Tibet c. 900 CE, and founded a new West Tibetan kingdom at the heart of the old Zhangzhung, now called [Ngari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngari_Prefecture) in the Tibetan language.



Royal drinking scene at [Alchi Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchi_Monastery), Ladakh, c. 1200 CE. The king wears a decorated [Qabā'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaba), of [Turco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages)-[Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persianate) style. It is similar to another royal scene at nearby [Mangyu Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangyu_Monastery)

[^cite_note-42]

Nyimagon's eldest son, [Lhachen Palgyigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhachen_Palgyigon), is believed to have conquered the regions to the north, including Ladakh and [Rutog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutog_County). After the death of Nyimagon, his kingdom was divided among his three sons, Palgyigon receiving Ladakh, Rutog, [Thok Jalung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thok_Jalung) and an area referred to as [Demchok Karpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demchok_Karpo) (a holy mountain near the present-day [Demchok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demchok,_Ladakh) village). The second son received [Guge–Purang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guge-Purang_Kingdom) (called "Ngari Korsum") and the third son received [Zanskar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanskar) and [Spiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiti) (to the southwest of Ladakh). This three-way division of Nyimagon's empire was recognised as historic and remembered in the chronicles of all the three regions as a founding narrative:

> He gave to each of his sons a separate kingdom, viz., to the eldest *Dpal-gyi-gon*, *Maryul* of *Mngah-ris*, the inhabitants using black bows; *ru-thogs* \[Rutog] of the east and the Gold-mine of *Hgog* \[possibly Thok Jalung]; nearer this way *Lde-mchog-dkar-po* \[Demchok Karpo]; ...

The first West Tibetan dynasty of Maryul founded by Palgyigon lasted five centuries, being weakened towards its end by the conquests of the Mongol/Mughal noble [Mirza Haidar Dughlat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirza_Haidar_Dughlat). Throughout this period the region was called "Maryul", possibly from the original proper name *\*Mrasa* (Xuangzhang's, *Mo-lo-so*), but in the Tibetan language it was interpreted to mean "lowland" (the lowland of Ngari). Maryul remained staunchly Buddhist during this period, having participated in the second diffusion of Buddhism from India to Tibet via Kashmir and Zanskar.

### Medieval history

Main article: [Namgyal dynasty of Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namgyal_dynasty_of_Ladakh)



Ladakh at its largest extent c. 1560–1600 CE

[King Bhagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhachen_Bhagan) united and strengthened Ladakh in 1460 by overthrowing the king of Leh, and founded the [Namgyal dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namgyal_dynasty_of_Ladakh) (*Namgyal* means "victorious" in several Tibetan languages).[^cite_note-luciano-28]<sup>: 25, 171 </sup> The Namgyals repelled most Central Asian raiders and temporarily extended the kingdom as far as [Nepal](171166.md).[^cite_note-LoramCharlie-36]

Between the 1380s and early 1510s, many [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) missionaries propagated [Islam](6037917.md) and proselytised the Ladakhi people. [Mir Sayyid Ali Hamadani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_Sayyid_Ali_Hamadani) was the first one to make Muslim converts in Ladakh and is often described as the founder of Islam in Ladakh. His principal disciple, [Mir Muhammad Nurbakhsh Qahistani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Nurbakhsh_Qahistani) followed in his footsetps and also propagated Islam to Ladakhis and the [Balti people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balti_people), who rapidly converted to Islam. [Noorbakshia Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noorbakshia_Islam) is named after him and his followers are only found in Baltistan and Ladakh. In 1505, [Mir Shams-ud-Din Araqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_Shams-ud-Din_Araqi), a prominent Shia scholar, visited Kashmir and Baltistan. He helped in the spread of [Shia Islam](26961.md) in Kashmir and converted the majority of Muslims in Baltistan to his school of thought. Several mosques were built in Ladakh during this period, including in Mulbhe, [Padum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padum) and [Shey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shey), then the capital of Ladakh. The Kashmiri Sufi saint, [Zain ud-Din Wali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zain_ud-Din_Wali), also proselytised in Ladakh upon his expulsion by [Zayn al-Abidin the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zayn_al-Abidin_the_Great) from Kashmir.[^cite_note-Howard-43][^cite_note-Sheikh-44]

It is unclear what happened to Islam after this period and it seems to have received a setback. [Mirza Muhammad Haidar Dughlat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirza_Muhammad_Haidar_Dughlat), who invaded and briefly conquered Ladakh in 1532, 1545, and 1548, did not record any presence of Islam in [Leh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leh) during his invasion. However, Shia Islam and Noorbakshia Islam continued to flourish in other regions of Ladakh.[^cite_note-Howard-43][^cite_note-Sheikh-44]



[Jama Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregational_mosque) of Leh next to the [Leh Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leh_Palace)

During the Balti invasion in late 16th century, led by [Raja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja) of [Skardu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skardu), [Ali Sher Khan Anchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Sher_Khan_Anchan), many Buddhist temples and artefacts were damaged and destroyed. Anchan took the king [Jamyang Namgyal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamyang_Namgyal) and his soldiers as captives to Skardu. Namgyal was later restored to the throne by Anchan and given the hand of a Balti princess in marriage, named [Gyal Khatun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyal_Khatun) or Argyal Khatun. Historical accounts differ upon who her father was. Some identify Anchan's ally and Raja of [Khaplu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaplu), Yabgo Shey Gilazi as her father,[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-\:0-47] while others identify Anchan himself as the father.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] In return, the daughter of Namgyal, [Mandok Gyalmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandok_Gyalmo) was married to Anchan.[^cite_note-\:0-47] Islam began to take root in the Leh area in the beginning of the 17th century after the Balti invasion and the marriage of Gyal to Jamyang. A large group of Muslim servants and musicians were sent along with Gyal to Ladakh and private mosques were built where they could pray. The Muslim musicians later settled in Leh. Several hundred Baltis migrated to the kingdom and according to oral tradition, many Muslim traders were granted land to settle, while many others were invited over the following years for various purposes.[^cite_note-50]

In the early 17th century, efforts were made to restore the destroyed artefacts and [gompas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gompa) by [Sengge Namgyal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sengge_Namgyal), Jamyang's son and successor. He also ordered the building of [Hemis Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemis_Monastery), Henle Gompa as well as [Leh Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leh_Palace).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] During his reign, he expanded the kingdom into Zanskar and [Spiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiti).

In the late 17th century, Ladakh sided with [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan) in its dispute with Tibet which, among other reasons, resulted in its invasion by the [Tibetan Central Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganden_Phodrang). This event is known as the [Tibet–Ladakh–Mughal war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet%E2%80%93Ladakh%E2%80%93Mughal_war) of 1679–1684.[^cite_note-53] The war resulted in the [Treaty of Tingmosgang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tingmosgang), whereby Tibet recognised the independence of Ladakh and Ladakh was reduced to its present day borders.[^cite_note-luciano-28]<sup>: 171, 172 </sup>[^cite_note-54] Kashmiri historians assert that the king converted to Islam in return for the assistance by the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) after this, however, *[Ladakh chronicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh_chronicles)* do not make any such mention. The king agreed to pay tribute to the Mughals in return for defending the kingdom.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] The Mughals, however, withdrew after being paid off by the [5th Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Dalai_Lama).[^cite_note-57]

### Princely state of Jammu and Kashmir

Main article: [Ladakh Wazarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh_Wazarat)



The disputed territory of the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir: divided between [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan-administered_Kashmir) (green), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India-administered_Kashmir) (blue) and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_administered_Kashmir) (yellow)

In 1834, the [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) [Zorawar Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Zorawar_Singh), a general of [Raja Gulab Singh of Jammu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulab_Singh), invaded and annexed Ladakh to Jammu under the suzerainty of the [Sikh Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire). After the defeat of the Sikhs in the [First Anglo-Sikh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Sikh_War), the state of [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)) was established as a separate [princely state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) under British [suzerainty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire). The Namgyal family was given the *[jagir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagir)* of [Stok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stok), which it nominally retains to this day. European influence began in Ladakh in the 1850s and increased. Geologists, sportsmen, and tourists began exploring Ladakh. In 1885, Leh became the headquarters of a mission of the [Moravian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_Church).

Ladakh was administered as a *wazarat* under [Dogra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogra_dynasty) rule, with a governor termed *wazir-e-wazarat*. It had three tehsils, based at Leh, [Skardu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skardu) and [Kargil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil). The headquarters of the *wazarat* was at Leh for six months of the year and at [Skardu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skardu) for six months. When the legislative assembly, called *Praja Sabha*, was established in 1934, Ladakh was given two nominated seats in the assembly.



[Hemis Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemis_Monastery) in the 1870s

Ladakh was claimed as part of Tibet by [Phuntsok Wangyal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phuntsok_Wangyal), a Tibetan [Communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist) leader.[^cite_note-TuttleSchaeffer2013-58]

### Indian state of Jammu and Kashmir

Main article: [Jammu and Kashmir (state)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(state\))

At the time of the [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) in 1947, the Dogra ruler [Maharaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja) [Hari Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Singh) chose to remain independent of India or Pakistan. Following the [Poonch Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Poonch_rebellion) in the western districts of the princely state, the maharaja sought defence assistance from India, but was told by Nehru to sign the [Instrument of Accession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_of_Accession_\(Jammu_and_Kashmir\)) to India, and military operations were initiated to counter the invasion. Hearing the news of accession, the [Gilgit Scouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgit_Scouts) stationed at Gilgit rebelled and reached into Ladakh by November. The wartime conversion of the pony trail from [Sonamarg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonamarg) to [Zoji La](30863535.md) by army engineers permitted tanks to move up and successfully capture the pass. The advance continued. [Dras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dras), Kargil and Leh were taken and Ladakh cleared of the Pakistanis.[^cite_note-Madras_Sappers-59]

In 1949, China closed the border between [Nubra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubra_Valley) and [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang), blocking old trade routes. In 1955 China began to build roads connecting Xinjiang and Tibet through the [Aksai Chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksai_Chin) area. The Indian effort to gain control of Aksai Chin led to the [Sino-Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Indian_War) of 1962, which India lost. China also built the [Karakoram highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram_highway) jointly with Pakistan. India built the [Srinagar-Leh Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srinagar-Leh_Highway) during this period, cutting the journey time between Srinagar and Leh from 16 days to two. The route, however, remains closed during the winter months due to heavy snowfall.[^cite_note-LoramCharlie-36] To make the route functional throughout the year, a 6.5 km (4.0 mi) tunnel across Zoji La pass, the [Zoji-la Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoji-la_Tunnel), is under construction.[^cite_note-60]



National Highway 1 which runs between Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh

The [Kargil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_War) of 1999, codenamed "Operation Vijay" by the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army), saw infiltration by Pakistani troops into parts of Western Ladakh, namely Kargil, Dras, [Mushkoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushkoh_Valley), Batalik and Chorbatla, overlooking key locations on the [Srinagar-Leh highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_1_\(India\)). Extensive operations were launched in high altitudes by the Indian Army with considerable artillery and air force support. Pakistani troops were evicted from the Indian side of the [Line of Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_Control) which the Indian government ordered was to be respected and which was not crossed by Indian troops. The Indian government was criticised by the Indian public because India respected geographical co-ordinates more than India's opponents: Pakistan and China.[^cite_note-Bammi-61]

The Ladakh region was divided into the Kargil and Leh districts in 1979. In 1989, there were violent riots between Buddhists and Muslims. Following demands for autonomy from the [Kashmiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiris)-dominated state government, the [Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh_Autonomous_Hill_Development_Council,_Kargil) was created in the 1990s. [Leh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leh_district) and [Kargil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_district) districts now each have their own locally elected Hill Councils with some control over local policy and development funds. In 1991, a [Peace Pagoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Pagoda) was erected in Leh by [Nipponzan Myohoji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipponzan_Myohoji).

There was a heavy presence of Indian Army and [Indo-Tibetan Border Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Tibetan_Border_Police) forces in Ladakh. These forces and [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) forces from China have, since the 1962 Sino-Indian War, had frequent stand-offs along the Ladakh portion of the [Line of Actual Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_Actual_Control). Out of the 857-kilometre-long (533 mi) border in Ladakh, only 368 km (229 mi) is the International Border, and the remaining 489 km (304 mi) is the Line of Actual Control.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] The stand-off involving the most troops was in September 2014 in the disputed [Chumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chumar) region when 800 to 1,000 Indian troops and 1,500 Chinese troops came into close proximity to each other.[^cite_note-64]

#### Ladakh Division

On 8 February 2019, Ladakh became a separate [Revenue and Administrative Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisions_of_India) within Jammu and Kashmir, having previously been part of the [Kashmir Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_Division). As a division, Ladakh was granted its own [Divisional Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisional_Commissioner_\(India\)) and [Inspector General of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_General_of_Police).[^cite_note-65]

Leh was initially chosen to be the headquarters of the new division however, following protests, it was announced that Leh and Kargil will jointly serve as the divisional headquarters, each hosting an Additional Divisional Commissioner to assist the Divisional Commissioner and Inspector General of Police who will spend half their time in each town.[^cite_note-kln-66]

### Union territory of Ladakh

See also: [Ladakh Union Territory Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh_Union_Territory_Front)



Ladakh (L) shown in the wider Kashmir region

The people of Ladakh had been demanding Ladakh to be constituted as a separate territory since 1930s, because of perceived unfair treatment by Kashmir and Ladakh's cultural differences with predominantly Muslim [Kashmir valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_valley), while some people in Kargil opposed union territory status for Ladakh.[^cite_note-LoramCharlie-36][^cite_note-Statesman-67] The first organised agitation was launched against Kashmir's "dominance" in the year 1964. In late 1980s, a much larger mass agitation was launched to press their demand for [union territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory) status.[^cite_note-68]

In August 2019, a [reorganisation act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_Reorganisation_Bill,_2019) was passed by the [Parliament of India](410238.md) which contained provisions to reconstitute Ladakh as a union territory, separate from the rest of Jammu and Kashmir on 31 October 2019.[^cite_note-egazette.nic.in-3][^cite_note-auto-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Under the terms of the act, the union territory is administered by a [Lieutenant Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_Ladakh) acting on behalf of the union Government of India and does not have an elected legislative assembly or chief minister. Each district within the union territory continues to elect an [autonomous district council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_district_council) as done previously.[^cite_note-dailyexcelsior.com-72]

The demand for Ladakh as separate union territory was first raised by the parliamentarian [Kushok Bakula Rinpoche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushok_Bakula_Rinpoche) around 1955, which was later carried forward by another parliamentarian [Thupstan Chhewang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thupstan_Chhewang).[^cite_note-happy1-73] The former Jammu and Kashmir state use to obtain large allocation of annual funds from the union government based on the fact that the large geographical area of the Ladakh (comprising 65% of total area), but Ladakh was allocated only 2% of the state budget based on its relative population.[^cite_note-happy1-73] Within the first year of the formation of Ladakh as separate union territory, its annual budget allocation has increased 4 times from ₹57 [crore](121420.md) to ₹232 crore.[^cite_note-happy1-73]

On 24 September 2025, the [protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh_protests) march led by [Sonam Wangchuk](52221506.md) demanded full statehood of Ladakh.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Ladakh)



Map of the central Ladakh region

Ladakh is the highest plateau in India with most of it being over 3,000 m (9,800 ft).[^cite_note-Crossroads-22] It extends from the [Himalayan](37966.md) to the [Kunlun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunlun_Mountains)[^cite_note-76] Ranges and includes the upper [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River) valley.



The confluence of the Indus (flowing left-to-right) and [Zanskar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanskar_River) (coming in from top) rivers.



View of Leh Town along with [Stok Kangri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stok_Kangri)

Historically, the region included the [Baltistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltistan) ([Baltiyul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltiyul)) valleys (now mostly in Pakistani-administered [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir)), the entire upper [Indus Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley), the remote [Zanskar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanskar), [Lahaul and Spiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahaul_and_Spiti_district) districts to the south, much of [Ngari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngari_Prefecture) (including the [Rudok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudok) region and [Guge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guge) in the east), [Aksai Chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksai_Chin) in the northeast, and the [Nubra Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubra_Valley) to the north, over [Khardong La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khardung_La) in the Ladakh Range. Contemporary Ladakh borders [Tibet](31516.md) to the east, the Lahaul and Spiti regions to the south, the [Vale of Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_Valley), [Jammu](43066157.md) and Baltiyul regions to the west, and the southwest corner of [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang), China across the [Karakoram Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakoram_Pass) in the far north. The historically vague divide between Ladakh and the [Tibetan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Plateau) commences to the north in an intricate maze of ridges to the east of [Rudok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudok), including Aling Kangri and Mavang Kangri, continuing southeastward toward northwestern [Nepal](171166.md). Before partition, Baltistan, now part of Pakistan, had been a district of Ladakh; [Skardu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skardu) was the winter capital of Ladakh, with Leh being the summer capital.

The mountain ranges in this region were formed over 45 million years by the folding of the [Indian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Plate) into the more stationary [Eurasian Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Plate). The drift continues, causing frequent earthquakes in the Himalayan region.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] The peaks in the Ladakh Range are at a medium altitude close to the [Zoji-la](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoji-la) (5,000–5,500 m or 16,400–18,000 ft) and increase toward southeast, culminating in the twin summits of [Nun-Kun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nun-Kun) (7,000 m or 23,000 ft).



The Ladakh region has high altitude

The [Suru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suru_Valley) and Zanskar Valleys form a great trough, enclosed by the Himalayas and the [Zanskar Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanskar). [Rangdum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangdum) is the highest inhabited region in the Suru valley, after which the valley rises to 4,400 m (14,400 ft) at [Pensi-la](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensi-la), the gateway to Zanskar. [Kargil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil), the only town in the Suru Valley, is the second-most important town in Ladakh. It was an important staging post on the routes of trade [caravans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_\(travellers\)) prior to 1947, being more-or-less equidistant (at about 230 kilometres) from [Srinagar](294148.md), Leh, Skardu and [Padum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padum). The Zanskar Valley lies in the troughs of the rivers [Stod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stod_River) and [Lungnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lungnak_River?action=edit\&redlink=1). The region experiences heavy snowfall; the Pensi-la is open only between June and mid-October. [Dras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dras) and the [Mushkoh Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushkoh_Valley) form the western extremity of Ladakh.

The Indus River is the backbone of Ladakh. Most major historical and current towns – [Shey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shey), Leh, [Basgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basgo) and [Tingmosgang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tingmosgang) (but not Kargil), are close to the Indus River. After the Indo-Pakistani War of 1947, the stretch of the Indus flowing through Ladakh became the only part of this river, which is greatly venerated in the Hindu religion and culture, that still flows through India.

The [Siachen Glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siachen_Glacier) is in the eastern Karakoram Range in the Himalaya Mountains along the disputed India-Pakistan border. The Karakoram Range forms a great watershed that separates China from the Indian subcontinent and is sometimes called the "Third Pole". The glacier lies between the Saltoro Ridge immediately to the west and the main Karakoram Range to the east. At 76 km (47 mi) long, it is the longest glacier in the Karakoram and second-longest in the world's non-polar areas. It falls from an altitude of 5,753 m (18,875 ft) above sea level at its source at [Indira Col](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Col) on the China border down to 3,620 m (11,880 ft) at its snout. [Saser Kangri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saser_Kangri) is the highest peak in the Saser Muztagh, the easternmost subrange of the Karakoram Range in India, Saser Kangri I having an altitude of 7,672 m (25,171 ft).

The [Ladakh Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh_Range) has no major peaks; its average height is a little less than 6,000 m (20,000 ft), and few of its passes are less than 5,000 m (16,000 ft). The [Pangong range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangong_range) runs parallel to the Ladakh Range for about 100 km (62 mi) northwest from [Chushul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chushul) along the southern shore of the [Pangong Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangong_Tso). Its highest point is about 6,700 m (22,000 ft) and the northern slopes are heavily glaciated. The region comprising the valley of the Shyok and Nubra rivers is known as Nubra. The Karakoram Range in Ladakh is not as mighty as in Baltistan. The massifs to the north and east of the Nubra–Siachen line include the [Apsarasas Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsarasas_Kangri) (highest point at 7,245 m or 23,770 ft) the [Rimo Muztagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimo_Muztagh) (highest point at 7,385 m or 24,229 ft) and the [Teram Kangri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teram_Kangri) Group (highest point at 7,464 m or 24,488 ft) together with [Mamostong Kangri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamostong_Kangri) (7,526 m or 24,692 ft) and [Singhi Kangri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singhi_Kangri) (7,202 m or 23,629 ft). North of the Karakoram lies the Kunlun. Thus, between Leh and eastern Central Asia there is a triple barrier – the Ladakh Range, Karakoram Range, and Kunlun. Nevertheless, a major trade route was established between Leh and [Yarkand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarkent_County).



Monthly average temperature in Leh

Ladakh is a high-altitude desert; the Himalayas create a [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) which generally denies passage of any monsoon clouds. The main source of water is the winter snowfall on the mountains. Recent flooding in the region (e.g., the [2010 floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Leh_floods)) has been attributed to abnormal rain patterns and retreating glaciers, both of which have been found to be linked to global climate change.[^cite_note-79] The Leh Nutrition Project, headed by [Chewang Norphel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chewang_Norphel)—also known as the "Glacier Man"—creates artificial glaciers as one solution for retreating glaciers.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

The regions on the north flank of the Himalayas – Dras, the Suru valley and Zangskar – experience heavy snowfall and remain cut-off from the rest of the region for several months during the year, just as the entire region remains isolated by road from the rest of the country. Summers are short, though they are long enough to grow crops; summer weather is dry and pleasant. Peak temperatures range from 3 to 35 [°C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius) (37 to 95 °F) in the summer and minimums range from −20 to −35 °C (−4 to −31 °F) in winter.[^cite_note-82]

The Zanskar river, along with its tributaries is the primary waterway of the region. When it freezes solid during the winter, the [Chadar trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chadar_trek) is traditionally the only means of travel to and from the area.

### Flora and fauna

Main article: [Wildlife of Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Ladakh)



The [black-necked crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-necked_crane) is the state bird of Ladakh. Photograph taken in [Hanle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanle_\(village\))

Scant precipitation makes Ladakh a high-altitude desert with extremely scarce vegetation over most of its area. Natural vegetation mainly occurs along water courses and on high altitude areas that receive more snow and cooler summer temperatures. Human settlements, however, are richly vegetated due to irrigation.[^cite_note-83] Natural vegetation commonly seen along watercourses includes [seabuckthorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabuckthorn) (*Hippophae* spp.), wild roses of pink or yellow varieties, tamarisk (*Myricaria* spp.), [caraway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraway), [stinging nettles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urtica_dioica), mint, *[Physochlaina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physochlaina) praealta*, and various grasses.[^cite_note-84] About 1250 plant species, including crops, were reported from Ladakh.[^cite_note-85] The plant *Ladakiella klimesii*, growing up to 6,150 metres (20,180 ft) above sea level, was first described here and named after this region.[^cite_note-86] The first European to study the wildlife of this region was [William Moorcroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Moorcroft_\(explorer\)) in 1820, followed by [Ferdinand Stoliczka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Stoliczka), an [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_people)-[Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_people) [palaeontologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeontologist), who carried out a massive expedition there in the 1870s. There are many lakes in Ladakh such as [Kyago Tso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyago_Tso).

The [bharal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharal) (or blue sheep) is the most abundant mountain [ungulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ungulate) in the Ladakh region, although it is not found in some parts of Zangskar and Sham areas. The bharal is one of the preferred choices of prey of the rare [snow leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_leopard).[^cite_note-87] The [Asiatic ibex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiatic_ibex) is a mountain goat that is distributed in the western part of Ladakh. It is the second-most abundant mountain ungulate in the region, with a population of about 6,000 individuals. It is adapted to rugged areas where it easily climbs near-vertical rock faces when threatened.[^cite_note-88] The Ladakhi [urial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urial) is another unique mountain sheep that inhabits the mountains of Ladakh. The population is declining, however, and there are not more than 3,000 individuals left in Ladakh.[^cite_note-89] The urial is endemic to Ladakh, where it is distributed only along two major river valleys, namely the Indus and Shyok. The animal is often persecuted by farmers, whose crops are allegedly damaged by flocks of urial. Its population declined dramatically in the late twentieth century, due to indiscriminate shooting by hunters along the Leh-Srinagar Highway.



A male and female Ibex in [Phyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyang)



[Yaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yak) on the way to [Pangong Tso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangong_Tso)

The [Tibetan argali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argali) (or *nyan*) is the largest wild sheep species in the world, standing 1.1 to 1.2 metres (3.5 to 4 ft) at the shoulder, possessing very large, curled horns measuring 900–1,000 mm (35–39 in). It is distributed on the Tibetan Plateau and its marginal ranges, encompassing a total home range of 2.5 million km<sup>2</sup> (0.97 million sq mi); however, there is only a small population, of about 400 animals, in Ladakh. Unlike other mountain sheep and goat species, the argali prefers open, grassy fields and rolling hills as it prefers to run, rather than climb into steep terrain, to flee from danger.[^cite_note-90] The endangered [Tibetan antelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_antelope), or *chiru* in Indian English (or Ladakhi *tsos*), has traditionally been hunted for its wool (*[shahtoosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahtoosh)*), a natural fibre of some of the finest quality. The wool of the Tibetan antelope is prized for its lightweight feel and as a [status symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_symbol). The wool must be pulled out by hand, a process done after the animal is killed. The fibre is smuggled into Kashmir and woven into exquisite shawls by Kashmiri workers. Ladakh is also home to the [Tibetan gazelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_gazelle), which inhabits the vast rangelands in eastern Ladakh bordering Tibet.[^cite_note-91]

The [kiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiang), or Tibetan wild ass, is common in the grasslands of Changthang, numbering about 2,500 individuals. These animals are in conflict with the nomadic people of Changthang who hold the Kiang responsible for pasture degradation.[^cite_note-92] There are about 200 [snow leopards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_leopard) in Ladakh of an estimated 7,000 worldwide. The [Hemis High Altitude National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemis_High_Altitude_National_Park) in central Ladakh is an especially good habitat for this predator as it has abundant prey populations. The [Eurasian lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_lynx) is another rare cat that preys on smaller herbivores in Ladakh. It is mostly found in Nubra, Changthang and Zangskar.[^cite_note-93] The [Pallas's cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallas's_cat), which looks somewhat like a house cat, is very rare in Ladakh and not much is known about the species. The [Tibetan wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_wolf), which sometimes preys on the livestock of the Ladakhis, is the most persecuted among the predators.[^cite_note-94] There are also a few [brown bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_bear) in the [Suru Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suru_Valley) and the area around Dras. The [Tibetan sand fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulpes_ferrilata) has been discovered in this region.[^cite_note-95] Among smaller animals, [marmots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmot), [hares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hare), and several types of [pika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pika) and [vole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vole) are common.[^cite_note-96]

## Administration

Main articles: [Administration of Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administration_of_Ladakh) and [Politics of Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Ladakh)



[Banner of the Administration of Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblem_of_Ladakh)

[^cite_note-97]

Under the terms of the [Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_Reorganisation_Act,_2019), Ladakh is administered as a union territory without a [legislative assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Legislative_Assembly_\(India\)) or elected government. The head of government is a [Lieutenant Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lieutenant_governors_of_Ladakh) appointed by the [President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) who is assisted by civil servants of the [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service).[^cite_note-98]

#### Autonomous District Councils

Ladakh is administered by 2 [autonomous district councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_district_council), they are:

- [Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Council, Kargil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh_Autonomous_Hill_Development_Council,_Kargil)

- [Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Council, Leh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh_Autonomous_Hill_Development_Council,_Leh)

The two autonomous district councils work with village [panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayati_raj) to take decisions on economic development, healthcare, education, land use, taxation, and local governance which are further reviewed at the block headquarters in the presence of the chief executive councillor and executive councillors.[^cite_note-101] The government of Jammu and Kashmir looks after law and order, the judicial system, communications and the higher education in the region. The two autonomous district councils continue to exist following the formation of the union territory of Ladakh on 31 October 2019.[^cite_note-dailyexcelsior.com-72]

The [union territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory) of Ladakh in India consists of seven [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_India). In August 2024, the [Government of India](553883.md) announced the creation of five new districts—[Zanskar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanskar_district), [Drass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drass_district), [Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sham_district), [Nubra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubra_district), and [Changthang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changthang_district)—with the stated objective of improving administrative efficiency and local representation.[^cite_note-102] The five districts were officially notified in April 2026, increasing the total number of districts in Ladakh from two to seven.[^cite_note-newd1-99]

According to the revised administrative boundaries, the Muslim-majority districts of [Kargil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_district) and [Drass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drass_district) contain 80 and 19 revenue villages respectively, for a combined total of 99 villages. The Buddhist-majority districts of [Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sham_district) (27 villages), [Changthang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changthang_district) (24), [Nubra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubra_district) (30), [Leh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leh_district) (44), and [Zanskar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanskar_district) (26) contain a combined total of 151 revenue villages.[^cite_note-rev1-103]

Following the reorganisation, five of Ladakh's seven districts are Buddhist-majority, while two are Muslim-majority. According to demographic estimates cited in media reports, the five Buddhist-majority districts account for approximately 39.65% of Ladakh's population, while the two Muslim-majority districts account for approximately 46.40%.[^cite_note-104]

Ladakh is under the jurisdiction of the [High Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Jammu_&_Kashmir_and_Ladakh).[^cite_note-105] The union territory of Ladakh has its own [police force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh_Police) headed by a [director general of police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_general_of_police).[^cite_note-106]

Ladakh sends one member (MP) to the lower house of the [Indian parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_parliament), the [Lok Sabha](275456.md). ## Economy



Preparing apricots, Alchi Monastery

The land is irrigated by a system of channels which funnel water from the ice and snow of the mountains. The principal crops are [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley) and wheat. Rice was previously a luxury in the Ladakhi diet, but, subsidised by the government, has now become a cheap staple.[^cite_note-Crossroads-22]

Naked barley (Ladakhi: *nas*, Urdu: *grim*) was traditionally a staple crop all over Ladakh. Growing times vary considerably with altitude. The extreme limit of cultivation is at [Korzok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korzok), on the [Tso-moriri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tso-moriri) lake, at 4,600 m (15,100 ft), which has what are widely considered to be the highest fields in the world.[^cite_note-Crossroads-22]

A minority of Ladakhi people were also employed as merchants and caravan traders, facilitating trade in textiles, carpets, [dyestuffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dye) and [narcotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcotics) between Punjab and [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang). However, since the Chinese Government closed the borders between Tibet Autonomous Region and Ladakh, this international trade has completely dried up.[^cite_note-LoramCharlie-36][^cite_note-LP-109]



Street market in Leh

Indus river flowing in the Ladakh region is endowed with vast hydropower potential. Solar and wind power potentials are also substantial. Though the region is a remote hilly area without all-weather roads, the area is also rich in limestone deposits to manufacture cement from the locally available cheap electricity for various construction needs.[^cite_note-110]

Since 1974, the Indian Government has encouraged a shift in trekking and other tourist activities from the troubled Kashmir region to the relatively unaffected areas of Ladakh. Although tourism employs only 4% of Ladakh's working population, it accounted for 50% of the region's [GNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_National_Product) in the year 2000.[^cite_note-LoramCharlie-36]

Since then, tourism has increased, exceeding 500,000 people in 2022 and 2023. The sharpest growth began after 2010 when the Bollywood film 3 Idiots —filmed in part on the [Pangong Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangong_Tso) in Ladakh — became a big hit in India. This is a contrast to the population of Leh, 31,000. This increase adds to the economy but it is having negative effects on the land due to the increase in waste and increasing water scarcity.[^cite_note-111]

This era is recorded in Arthur Neves *The Tourist's Guide to Kashmir, Ladakh, and Skardo*, first published in 1911, described in the Lonely Planet travel guide in 2002.[^cite_note-LP-109]

## Transportation



A vehicle on the Himalaya Highway 3

There are about 1,800 km (1,100 mi) of roads in Ladakh of which 800 km (500 mi) are surfaced.[^cite_note-PlanningCommision2-112] The majority of roads in Ladakh are looked after by the [Border Roads Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Roads_Organisation). There are two main roads that connect Ladakh with the rest of the country, [NH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_1_\(India\)) connecting Srinagar to Kargil and Leh, and [NH3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_3_\(India\)) connecting [Manali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manali,_Himachal_Pradesh) to Leh. A third road to Ladakh is the [Nimmu–Padam–Darcha road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimmu%E2%80%93Padam%E2%80%93Darcha_road), which is under construction.[^cite_note-113]



[Kushok Bakula Rimpochee Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushok_Bakula_Rimpochee_Airport)

There is an airport in Leh, [Kushok Bakula Rimpochee Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushok_Bakula_Rimpochee_Airport), from which there are daily flights to [Delhi](37756.md) and weekly flights to Srinagar and Jammu. There are two airstrips at [Daulat Beg Oldie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daulat_Beg_Oldie) and [Fukche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukche) for military transport.[^cite_note-114] The airport at Kargil, [Kargil Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_Airport), was intended for civilian flights but is currently used by the Indian Army. The airport is a political issue for the locals who argue that the airport should serve its original purpose, i.e., should open up for civilian flights. Since past few years the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) has been operating AN-32 air courier service to transport the locals during the winter seasons to [Jammu](43066157.md), [Srinagar](294148.md) and [Chandigarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandigarh).[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] A private aeroplane company [Air Mantra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Mantra) landed a 17-seater aircraft at the airport, in the presence of dignitaries like the Chief Minister [Omar Abdullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Abdullah), marking the first ever landing by a civilian airline company at Kargil Airport.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

### Ethnicity

The population of Ladakh comprises diverse linguistic, ethnic, and religious groups.[Tibetans](249458.md), [Mons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mon_people), and [Dards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dards) are the three major ethnolibguistic groups in Ladakh.[^cite_note-124] Other main ethnolinguistic groups include [Ladakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakhis), [Boto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boto_people), [Changpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changpa), [Brokpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokpa), [Argon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argon_people), [Balti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balti_people), [Garra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garra_people), [Beda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beda_people), [Purik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purigpa), and [Shina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shina_people).[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126]

Ladakh is also home to minority groups such as [Gujjars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurjars_in_Kashmir) and [Bakarwals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakarwal_people), who mainly live in the [Zanskar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanskar_district), Leh, [Nubra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubra_district), and [Kargil districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_district).[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130]

As per 2011 census, [Scheduled Tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Tribes) constituted 79.5% of Ladakh's population,[^cite_note-131] [Scheduled Castes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_and_Scheduled_Tribes) constituted 0.37% and the remaining 20.13% belonged to [Forward Castes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_caste). Eight tribes, such as the Bot, Mon, Beda, Purigpa, Brokpa, Shina, Balti, and Changpa, were given [Scheduled Tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_and_Scheduled_Tribes) status by the [Government of India](553883.md) in 1989.[^cite_note-132]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Ladakh)



A traditional Ladakhi *perak* headdress worn by women, made of turquoise, coral, silver and wool

Ladakhi culture is similar to [Tibetan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_culture).[^cite_note-culturetib-25][^cite_note-142] Weaving is an important part of traditional life in eastern Ladakh. Both women and men weave, on different looms.[^cite_note-143]

### Cuisine



[Thukpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thukpa)

Ladakhi food has much in common with [Tibetan food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_food), the most prominent foods being *[thukpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thukpa)* (noodle soup) and *[tsampa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsampa)*, known in Ladakhi as *ngampe* (roasted barley flour). Edible without cooking, *tsampa* makes useful trekking food. Strictly Ladakhi dishes include *skyu* and *chutagi*, both heavy and rich soup pasta dishes, *skyu* being made with root vegetables and meat, and *chutagi* with leafy greens and vegetables.[^cite_note-144] As Ladakh moves toward a cash-based economy, foods from the plains of India are becoming more common.[^cite_note-145]

Much like in other parts of Central Asia, tea in Ladakh is traditionally made with strong green tea, butter, and salt. It is mixed in a large churn and known as *[gurgur cha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter_tea)*, after the sound it makes when mixed. Sweet tea (*cha ngarmo*) is common now, made in Indian style with milk and sugar. Most of the surplus barley that is produced is fermented into *chang*, an alcoholic beverage drunk especially on festive occasions.[^cite_note-WomensAlliance-146]

### Music and dance

Main article: [Music of Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Ladakh)



Dancer in masked dance festival

The music of Ladakhi Buddhist monastic festivals, like [Tibetan music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Tibet), often involves religious [chanting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanting) in [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Tibetan) as an integral part of the religion. These chants are complex, often recitations of sacred texts or in celebration of various festivals. *Yang* chanting, performed without metrical timing, is accompanied by resonant drums and low, sustained syllables. Religious mask dances are an important part of Ladakh's cultural life. [Hemis monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemis_monastery), a leading centre of the [Drukpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drukpa_Lineage) tradition of Buddhism, holds an annual masked dance festival, as do all major Ladakhi monasteries.&#x20;

Ladakh has a variety of dances such as the *khatok chenmo*, which is headed by a respectable family member, and *shondol*.[^cite_note-147] Some other dance forms include *kompa tsum-tsak*, *jabro*, *[cham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cham_dance)*, *chabs-skyan tses*, *raldi tses*, and *alley yaato*.[^cite_note-148]. The dances typically narrate a story of the fight between good and evil, ending with the eventual victory of the former.[^cite_note-149]

### Sport

The most popular sport in Ladakh is [ice hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_in_India), which is played only on natural ice generally mid-December through mid-February.[^cite_note-150] [Cricket](25675557.md) is also very popular.

[Archery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archery) is a traditional sport in Ladakh, and many villages hold archery festivals, which are as much about traditional dancing, drinking and gambling, as they are about the sport. The sport is conducted with strict etiquette, to the accompaniment of the music of *surna* and *daman* ([shehnai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shehnai) and drum). [Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo), the other traditional sport of Ladakh, is indigenous to [Baltistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltistan) and [Gilgit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilgit), and was probably introduced into Ladakh in the mid-17th century by King [Singge Namgyal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sengge_Namgyal), whose mother was a Balti princess.[^cite_note-151] Polo, popular among the Baltis, is an annual affair in the Dras region of Kargil district.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]



&#x20;First ever [Khelo India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khelo_India_Youth_Games) Winter Games were held in Ladakh in 2020

The [Ladakh Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladakh_Marathon) is a high-altitude [marathon](49132.md) held in Leh every year since 2012. Held at a height of 11,500 to 17,618 feet (3,505 to 5,370 m), it is one of the world's highest marathons.[^cite_note-156]

### Social status of women

A feature of Ladakhi society that distinguishes it from the rest of India is the high status and relative emancipation enjoyed by women compared to other rural parts of India. Fraternal [polyandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyandry) and inheritance by [primogeniture](136535.md) were common in Ladakh until the early 1940s, when these were made illegal by the government of Jammu and Kashmir. However, the practice remained in existence into the 1990s especially among the elderly and the more isolated rural populations.[^cite_note-Gielen,_U._P._1993-157] Another custom is known as *khang-bu*, or 'little house', in which the elders of a family, as soon as the eldest son has sufficiently matured, retire from participation in affairs, yielding the headship of the family to him and taking only enough of the property for their own sustenance.[^cite_note-Crossroads-22]

### Traditional medicine

Main article: [Traditional Tibetan medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Tibetan_medicine)

Traditional Tibetan medicine, also known as Sowa Rigpa has been the traditional health system of Ladakh for over a thousand years. This school of traditional healing contains elements of [Ayurveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayurveda) and [Chinese medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_medicine), combined with the philosophy and [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology) of Tibetan Buddhism. For centuries, the only medical system accessible to the people has been the *amchi*, traditional doctors following the Traditional Tibetan medicine. Traditional medicine remains a component of public health, especially in remote areas.[^cite_note-Plantlife-158]

Programmes by the government, local and international organisations are working to develop and rejuvenate this traditional system of healing.[^cite_note-Plantlife-158][^cite_note-159] Efforts are underway to preserve the intellectual property rights of *amchi* medicine for the people of Ladakh. The government has also been trying to promote the [sea buckthorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippophae) in the form of juice and jam, as some claim it possesses medicinal properties.

The [National Research Institute for Sowa-Rigpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Research_Institute_for_Sowa-Rigpa?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Leh is an institute for research into traditional medicine and a hospital providing traditional treatments.[^cite_note-160]

## Education

According to the 2001 census, the overall literacy rate in Leh District is 62% (72% for males and 50% for females), and in Kargil District 58% (74% for males and 41% for females).[^cite_note-161] Traditionally there was little or nothing by way of formal education except in the monasteries. Usually, one son from every family was obliged to master the [Tibetan script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_script) in order to read the holy books.[^cite_note-Crossroads-22]

The [Moravian Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_Church) opened a school in Leh in October 1889, and the *Wazir-i Wazarat* (*ex officio* Joint Commissioner with a British officer) of Baltistan and Ladakh ordered that every family with more than one child should send one of them to school. This order met with great resistance from the local people who feared that the children would be forced to convert to Christianity. The school taught Tibetan, Urdu, English, Geography, Sciences, Nature study, Arithmetic, Geometry and Bible study.[^cite_note-Ray-162] It is still in existence today. The first local school to provide western education was opened by a local Society called "Lamdon Social Welfare Society" in 1973. Later, with support from [Dalai Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalai_Lama) and some international organisations, the school, now known as Lamdon Model Senior Secondary School, has grown to accommodate approximately two thousand pupils in several branches. It prides itself on preserving Ladakhi tradition and culture.[^cite_note-Lamdon-163]

Schools are well distributed throughout Ladakh but 75% of them provide only primary education. 65% of children attend school, but absenteeism of both students and teachers remains high. In both districts the failure rate at school-leaving level ([class X](293121.md)) had for many years been around 85%–95%, while of those managing to scrape through, barely half succeeded in qualifying for college entrance (class XII). Before 1993, students were taught in Urdu until they were 14, after which the medium of instruction shifted to English. In 1994 the [Students' Educational and Cultural Movement of Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Students'_Educational_and_Cultural_Movement_of_Ladakh) (SECMOL) launched Operation New Hope (ONH), a campaign to provide "culturally appropriate and locally relevant education" and make government schools more functional and effective.[^cite_note-164] [Central Institute of Buddhist Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Institute_of_Buddhist_Studies) a [Deemed University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deemed_University) in Leh is the oldest institution in this region which mainly focuses on Buddhist philosophy but also offers degrees in different fields.[^cite_note-165]



[Indian Astronomical Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Astronomical_Observatory) near Leh

The [University of Ladakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ladakh) with its two campuses (One each in Kargil & Leh) and its constituent colleges enables students to pursue higher education without having to leave Ladakh.[^cite_note-166] A central University has also been approved to be set up in Ladakh by the Union Cabinet.[^cite_note-167] The [Indian Astronomical Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Astronomical_Observatory) is located in [Hanle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanle_\(village\)) and is operated by the [Indian Institute of Astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Astrophysics).[^cite_note-168]

In December 2019, the union minister of state for home affairs Mr [G Kishan Reddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._Kishan_Reddy), in a written response has stated in Parliament that the Government of India has approved to establish a Medical College and National Research Institute for Sowa-Rigpa in the district of Leh.[^cite_note-169]

In August 2021, the [Parliament of India](410238.md) amended the [Central Universities Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_university_\(India\)) to establish a central university in Ladakh named "[Sindhu Central University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhu_Central_University)".[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] As of January 2022, there were 904 Government run schools in Ladakh and 113 publicly run private schools in Ladakh.[^cite_note-172]
