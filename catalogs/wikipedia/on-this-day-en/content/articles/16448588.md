A total [lunar eclipse](18298.md) occurred at the Moon's [descending node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_node) of orbit on Tuesday, March 3, 2026,[^cite_note-1] with an umbral [magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitude_of_eclipse) of 1.1507. A lunar eclipse occurs when the [Moon](19331.md) moves into the [Earth's shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_shadow), causing the Moon to be darkened. A total lunar eclipse occurs when the Moon's near side entirely passes into the Earth's umbral shadow. Unlike a [solar eclipse](5024887.md), which can only be viewed from a relatively small area of the world, a lunar eclipse may be viewed from anywhere on the [night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night) side of Earth. A total lunar eclipse can last up to nearly two hours, while a total solar eclipse lasts only a few minutes at any given place, because the Moon's [shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbra,_penumbra_and_antumbra) is smaller. The Moon's apparent diameter was near the average, as it occurred 6.7 days after [perigee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsis) (on February 24, 2026, at 18\:15 UTC) and 6.9 days before [apogee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsis) (on March 10, 2026, at 09\:45 UTC).[^cite_note-2]

This lunar eclipse was the third of an [almost tetrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrad_\(astronomy\)), with the others being on [March 14, 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2025_lunar_eclipse) (total); [September 8, 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2025_lunar_eclipse) (total); and [August 28, 2026](22043242.md) (partial).

During the eclipse, the [Moon](19331.md) occulted [NGC 3423](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NGC_3423) over [North America](21139.md). [Deep-sky objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep-sky_object) are rarely occulted during a total eclipse from any given spot on Earth.[^cite_note-Mus2002-3]<sup>: 161 </sup>

This eclipse fell on the [Lantern Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lantern_Festival), the first since [February 11, 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2017_lunar_eclipse).

## Visibility

The eclipse was completely visible over [northeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Asia), northwestern [North America](21139.md), and the central [Pacific Ocean](23070.md), seen rising over much of [Asia](689.md) and [Australia](4689264.md) and setting over North and [South America](26769.md).[^cite_note-4]

## Eclipse season

See also: [Eclipse cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_cycle)

This eclipse is part of an [eclipse season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_season), a period, roughly every six months, when eclipses occur. Only two (or occasionally three) eclipse seasons occur each year, and each season lasts about 35 days and repeats just short of six months (173 days) later; thus two full eclipse seasons always occur each year. Either two or three eclipses happen each eclipse season. In the sequence below, each eclipse is separated by a [fortnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnight).

### Saros 133

The series started with a penumbral lunar eclipse on May 13, 1557. It contains partial eclipses from August 7, 1683 through December 17, 1899; total eclipses from [December 28, 1917](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_1917_lunar_eclipse?action=edit\&redlink=1) through August 3, 2278; and a second set of partial eclipses from August 14, 2296 through March 11, 2639. The series ends at member 71 as a penumbral eclipse on June 29, 2819.

The longest duration of totality will be produced by member 35 at 101 minutes, 41 seconds on [May 30, 2170](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2170_lunar_eclipse). All eclipses in this series occur at the Moon’s [descending node](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_node) of orbit.[^cite_note-7]

### Half-Saros cycle

A lunar eclipse will be preceded and followed by solar eclipses by 9 years and 5.5 days (a [half saros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saros_\(astronomy\)#Relationship_between_lunar_and_solar_saros_.28sar.29)).[^cite_note-9] This lunar eclipse is related to two annular solar eclipses of [Solar Saros 140](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_Saros_140).
