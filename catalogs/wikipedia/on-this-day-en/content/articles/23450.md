The **Pitcairn Islands** ([/ˈpɪtkɛərn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*PIT-kairn*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-8] [Pitkern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitkern): *_Pitkern Ailen_*), officially **Pitcairn, Henderson, Ducie and Oeno Islands**,[^cite_note-BNA1981-9][^cite_note-Order2010-10][^cite_note-PitcainLaws-11][^cite_note-TheOverTerr-12] are a group of four volcanic islands in the southern [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) that form the sole [British Overseas Territory](207114.md) in the Pacific Ocean. The four islands—[Pitcairn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitcairn_Island), [Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson_Island_\(Pitcairn_Islands\)), [Ducie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ducie_Island) and [Oeno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oeno_Island)—are scattered across several hundred kilometres of ocean and have a combined land area of about 47 square kilometres (18 square miles). Henderson Island accounts for 86% of the land area, but only Pitcairn Island is inhabited. The inhabited islands nearest to the Pitcairn Islands are [Mangareva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangareva) (of [French Polynesia](10737.md)), 688 km (428 miles) to the west,[^cite_note-13] as well as [Easter Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Island), 1,929 km (1199 miles) to the east.

The [Pitcairn Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitcairn_Islanders) are descended primarily from nine British [HMS *Bounty* mutineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutiny_on_the_Bounty) and twelve [Tahitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahitians) women. In 2023, the territory had a permanent population of 35, making it the smallest territory in the world by number of permanent residents.[^cite_note-immigration-5] Owing to the island's extreme isolation and small population, incidents of widespread [sexual abuse](7397019.md) went undetected until 1999, culminating in a high-profile [sexual assault trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Pitcairn_Islands_sexual_assault_trial) in 2004.

## History

Main article: [History of the Pitcairn Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Pitcairn_Islands)

### Polynesian settlement

Various forms of evidence show the earliest settlers of the Pitcairn Islands were [Polynesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesians) who occupied Pitcairn and Henderson for several centuries until the islands were abandoned: Henderson most likely before the 16th century and Pitcairn in the 17th or early 18th century. The islands were uninhabited when they were rediscovered by Europeans.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-Molle-Aymericic-15]

### Early European sightings



West side of Pitcairn Island



Pitcairn landing

Portuguese sailor [Pedro Fernandes de Queirós](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Fernandes_de_Queir%C3%B3s) came upon Ducie and Henderson Islands while sailing for the Spanish Crown, arriving on 26 January 1606. He named them La Encarnación ("[The Incarnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incarnation_\(Christianity\))") and San Juan Bautista ("Saint [John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Baptist)"), respectively. However, some sources express doubt about exactly which of the islands were visited and named by Queirós, suggesting that La Encarnación may actually have been Henderson Island, and San Juan Bautista may have been Pitcairn Island.[^cite_note-histgovtlaws-16]

Pitcairn Island was sighted on 3 July 1767 by the crew of the British [HMS *Swallow*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Swallow_\(1745\)), commanded by Captain [Philip Carteret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Carteret). The island was named after midshipman [Robert Pitcairn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pitcairn_\(Royal_Navy_officer\)), a 15-year-old crew member who was the first to sight the island. Robert Pitcairn was a son of British Marine Major [John Pitcairn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Pitcairn), who was later killed at the 1775 [Battle of Bunker Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bunker_Hill) in the [American War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution).[^cite_note-DNB-17]

Carteret, who sailed without the newly invented [marine chronometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_chronometer), charted the island at [25°02′S 133°21′W  /  25.033°S 133.350°W  / -25.033; -133.350](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Pitcairn_Islands\&params=25_02_S_133_21_W_), and although the [latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude) was reasonably accurate, his recorded longitude was incorrect by about 3°, putting his coordinates 330 km (210 mi) to the west of the actual island. This made Pitcairn difficult to find, as highlighted by the failure of Captain [James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cook) to locate the island in July 1773.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

### *Bounty* mutiny and aftermath

Further information: [HMS Bounty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Bounty) and [Mutiny on the Bounty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutiny_on_the_Bounty)



The mutineers turning Bligh and some of the officers and crew adrift from [HMS *Bounty*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Bounty) on 29 April 1789.



Adamstown, the only settlement on the Islands

In 1790, nine of the mutineers from the British naval vessel *HMS Bounty*, along with the native Tahitian men and women who were with them (six men, 11 women, and a baby girl), settled on Pitcairn Island and set fire to the *Bounty*. Later inhabitants of the island remained aware of the *Bounty*'s location, which is still visible underwater in [Bounty Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounty_Bay), but the wreckage gained significant attention in 1957 when documented by *[National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_Society)* explorer [Luis Marden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Marden). Although the settlers survived by farming and fishing, the initial period of settlement was marked by serious tensions among them. [Alcoholism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholism), [murder](20171.md), disease and other ills took the lives of most mutineers and Tahitian men. [John Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams_\(mutineer\)) and [Ned Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ned_Young) turned to the [scriptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_text), using the ship's [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounty_Bible) as their guide for a new and peaceful society. Young eventually died of an [asthmatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthma) infection.

Ducie Island was rediscovered in 1791 by Royal Navy captain [Edward Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Edwards_\(Royal_Navy_officer\)) aboard [HMS *Pandora*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Pandora_\(1779\)), while searching for the *Bounty* mutineers. He named it after [Francis Reynolds-Moreton, 3rd Baron Ducie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Reynolds-Moreton,_3rd_Baron_Ducie), also a captain in the Royal Navy.

The Pitcairn islanders reported it was not until 27 December 1795 that the first ship since the *Bounty* was seen from the island, but it did not approach the land and they could not make out the nationality. A second ship appeared in 1801, but made no attempt to communicate with them. A third came sufficiently near to see their house, but did not try to send a boat on shore. Finally, the American [sealing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_hunting) ship *Topaz*, under [Mayhew Folger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayhew_Folger), became the first to visit the island, when the crew spent ten hours on Pitcairn in February 1808.[^cite_note-20] Whalers subsequently became regular visitors to the island. The last recorded whaler to visit was the *[James Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Arnold_\(ship\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)* in 1888.[^cite_note-21]



View of Pitcairn's Island, South Seas, 1814, J. Shillibeer

A report of Folger's discovery was forwarded to the [Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty), mentioning the mutineers and giving a more precise location of the island: [25°02′S 130°00′W  /  25.033°S 130.000°W  / -25.033; -130.000](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Pitcairn_Islands\&params=25_02_S_130_00_W_).[^cite_note-22] However, this was not known to Sir [Thomas Staines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Staines), who commanded a Royal Navy [flotilla](981096.md) of two ships, HMS *Briton* and HMS *Tagus*, which found the island at [25°04′S 130°25′W  /  25.067°S 130.417°W  / -25.067; -130.417](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Pitcairn_Islands\&params=25_04_S_130_25_W_) (by meridian observation) on 17 September 1814. Staines sent a party ashore and wrote a detailed report for the Admiralty.[^cite_note-hist-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] By that time, only one mutineer, John Adams, remained alive. He was granted amnesty for his part in the mutiny.[^cite_note-hist-23]

Henderson Island was rediscovered on 17 January 1819 by British Captain James Henderson of the British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) ship *Hercules*.[^cite_note-27] Captain Henry King, sailing on *Elizabeth*, landed on 2 March to find the king's [colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_colours,_standards_and_guidons) already flying. His crew scratched the name of their ship into a tree. Oeno Island was discovered on 26 January 1824 by American captain [George Worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Worth_\(sea_captain\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) aboard the whaler [*Oeno*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oeno_\(ship\)).

In 1832, having tried and failed to petition the British government and the [London Missionary Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Missionary_Society), [Joshua Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Hill_\(Pitcairn_Island_leader\)), an American adventurer, arrived.[^cite_note-28] He reported that by March 1833, he had founded a [temperance society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperance_society) to combat drunkenness, a "[Maundy Thursday Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maundy_Thursday)", a monthly prayer meeting, a juvenile society, a Peace Society and a school.[^cite_note-29]

### British colony

Traditionally, Pitcairn Islanders consider that their islands officially became a British colony on 30 November 1838, at the same time becoming [one of the first territories to extend voting rights to women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_women's_suffrage). By the mid-1850s, the Pitcairn community was outgrowing the island; its leaders appealed to the British government for assistance, and were offered [Norfolk Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Island). On 3 May 1856, the entire population of 193 people set sail for Norfolk on board the *Morayshire*, arriving on 8 June after a difficult five-week trip. However, just 18 months later, 17 of the Pitcairn Islanders returned to their home island, and another 27 followed five years later.[^cite_note-hist-23]

[HMS *Thetis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Thetis_\(1871\)) visited Pitcairn Island on 18 April 1881 and "found the people very happy and contented, and in perfect health". At that time the population was 96, an increase of six since the visit of [Admiral de Horsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algernon_Frederick_Rous_de_Horsey) in September 1878. Stores had recently been delivered from friends in England, including two whale-boats and [Portland cement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_cement), which was used to make the reservoir watertight. HMS *Thetis* gave the islanders 200 lb (91 kg) of [ship's biscuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardtack), 100 lb (45 kg) of candles, and 100 lb of soap and clothing to the value of £31, donated by the [ship's company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship's_company). An American trading ship called *Venus* had in 1882 bestowed a supply of [cotton seed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), to provide the islanders with a crop for future trade.[^cite_note-30]



Pitcairn islanders, 1916

In 1886, the [Seventh-day Adventist](28632.md) layman [John Tay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tay) visited Pitcairn and persuaded most of the islanders to accept his faith. He returned in 1890 on the missionary schooner [*Pitcairn*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitcairn_\(schooner\)) with an ordained minister to perform [baptisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism). Since then, the majority of Pitcairn Islanders have been Adventists.[^cite_note-31]

In 1898, for the purposes of a murder trial, the Pitcairn Islands were placed by the [Colonial Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Colonies) under the jurisdiction of the [High Commissioner for the Western Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Commissioner_for_the_Western_Pacific), based in Fiji.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEshleman201122-32] The islands of Henderson, Oeno and Ducie were annexed by Britain in 1902: Henderson on 1 July, Oeno on 10 July, and Ducie on 19 December.[^cite_note-histgovtlaws-16] In 1938, the three islands, along with Pitcairn, were incorporated into a single administrative unit called the "Pitcairn Group of Islands". The population peaked at 233 in 1937.[^cite_note-33] It has since decreased owing to emigration, primarily to Australia and New Zealand.[^cite_note-CIA-34]

### Modern era

In 1952, Pitcairn was removed from the jurisdiction of the Western Pacific High Commission – which had relocated to the [British Solomon Islands Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Solomon_Islands_Protectorate) – and placed under the direct jurisdiction of the [Governor of Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Fiji). It was administered by the Pitcairn Office in [Suva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suva),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEshleman201137–38-35] although due to its isolation visits from British officials continued to occur only once or twice per decade.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEshleman201140–41-36] Following Fiji's independence in 1970, the *Pitcairn Order 1970* created a new office of [Governor of Pitcairn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Pitcairn).[^cite_note-37] The governorship was vested in the [British high commissioner to New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_British_High_Commissioners_to_New_Zealand), with the administration based in the British consulate in [Auckland](18660332.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEshleman201147–49-38]

In the post-war period, the advent of jet airliners and [containerisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Containerisation) contributed to a decline in ship traffic, which severely affected the island's economy. There was a mass exodus of residents in the early 1960s, with the population dropping from 140 in 1960 to 90 in 1962.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEshleman201143–44-39] Advances in communication were slow to reach the island – until the 1980s, [radiograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiogram_\(message\)) and [Ham radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ham_radio) were the primary methods of off-island communication, with [Morse code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_code) used for official communications until 1982. Pitcairn was connected to the international phone system in 1985 and received internet access in 2002.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEshleman201145–46-40]

Three cases of imprisonment for raping underage girls were reported in the 1950s.[^cite_note-41] In 1999, Gail Cox, a police officer from [Kent](16766.md), United Kingdom, served on a temporary assignment on Pitcairn, and uncovered allegations of sexual abuse. When a 15-year-old girl decided to press [rape](7980471.md) charges in 1999, criminal proceedings (code-named "Operation Unique") were set in motion. The charges included 21 counts of [rape](7980471.md), 41 of [indecent assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indecent_assault), and two of [gross indecency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indecency_with_Children_Act_1960) with a child under 14. Over the following two years, police officers in Australia, New Zealand and the United Kingdom interviewed every woman who had lived on Pitcairn in the past 20 years, as well as all of the accused men. These interviews revealed stories of girls as young as three being sexually assaulted and as young as 10 being gang-raped.[^cite_note-auto-42] The file was held by Pitcairn's first Public Prosecutor Simon Moore, an Auckland Crown Solicitor appointed to the position by the British government for the purposes of the investigation.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-auto-42]

Australian [Seventh-day Adventist](28632.md) pastor Neville Tosen, who spent two years on Pitcairn around the turn of the millennium, said that on his arrival, he had been taken aback by the conduct of the children, but he had not immediately realised what was happening. "I noticed worrying signs such as inexplicable mood swings," he said. "It took me three months to realise they were being abused." Tosen tried to bring the matter before the Island Council (the legislative body which doubles as the island's court), but was rebuffed. One councillor told him, "Look, the [age of consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_consent) has always been 12, and it doesn't hurt them."[^cite_note-Paradise_under_a_cloud-44] A study of island records confirmed anecdotal evidence that most girls bore their first child between the ages of 12 and 15. "I think the girls were conditioned to accept that it was a man's world and once they turned 12, they were eligible," Tosen said. Mothers and grandmothers were resigned to the situation, telling him that their own childhood experience had been the same; they regarded it as just a part of life on Pitcairn. One grandmother wondered what all the fuss was about.

Tosen was convinced, however, that the early sexual experience was very damaging to the girls, outright stating, "They can't settle or form solid relationships. They did suffer, no doubt about it."[^cite_note-Paradise_under_a_cloud-44][^cite_note-45] In 2016, [Mike Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Warren_\(mayor\)), Pitcairn mayor from 2008 to 2013, was convicted and sentenced to 20 months imprisonment for possession of child pornography.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

#### Sexual assault trials of 2004

Main article: [2004 Pitcairn Islands sexual assault trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Pitcairn_Islands_sexual_assault_trial)

In 2004, charges were laid against seven men living on Pitcairn and six residing abroad. This represented nearly a third of the male population and half of the island's adult males. After extensive trials, most were convicted, some on multiple counts of sexual assaults on children.[^cite_note-Tweedie_2004-48] On 25 October 2004, six men were found guilty, including [Steve Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Christian), the island's mayor at the time.[^cite_note-Fickling_2004-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

In the same year, the islanders surrendered about 20 firearms ahead of the [sexual assault trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitcairn_sexual_assault_trial_of_2004).[^cite_note-52] Following the loss of their final appeal, the British government constructed a prison at Bob's Valley to house the convicted men.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] The men began serving their sentences in late 2006, and by 2010 all had either completed their terms or been granted home detention status.[^cite_note-55]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of the Pitcairn Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_the_Pitcairn_Islands)



Map of the Pitcairn Islands

The Pitcairn Islands form the southeasternmost extension of the geological [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) of the [Tuamotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuamotus) of [French Polynesia](10737.md), and consist of four islands: [Pitcairn Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitcairn_Island), [Oeno Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oeno_Island) (atoll with five islets, one of which is Sandy Island), [Henderson Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson_Island_\(Pitcairn_Islands\)) and [Ducie Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ducie_Island) (atoll with four islets).

The Pitcairn Islands were formed by a centre of upwelling [magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma) called the [Pitcairn hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitcairn_hotspot). [Pitcairn Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitcairn_Island) is a volcanic remnant primarily formed of [tuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff), where the north side of the cone has been eroded.[^cite_note-PitcairnFurey-56] Pitcairn is the only permanently inhabited island. Adamstown, the main settlement on the island, lies within the volcanic basin.[^cite_note-PitcairnFurey-56] Pitcairn is accessible only by boat through Bounty Bay, due to the island's steep cliffs.[^cite_note-PitcairnFurey-56] Henderson Island, covering about 86% of the territory's total land area and supporting a rich variety of animals in its nearly inaccessible interior, is also capable of supporting a small human population despite its scarce fresh water, but access is difficult, owing to its outer shores being steep limestone cliffs covered by sharp coral. In 1988, this island was designated as a UNESCO World Heritage site.[^cite_note-Britannica-57] The other islands are at a distance of more than 100 km (62 mi) and are not habitable.

Pitcairn Island has no permanent water source; however, the island has three seasonal semi-permanent springs.[^cite_note-PitcairnFurey-56]

### Pitcairn Islands Dark Sky Sanctuary

In March 2019 the [International Dark-Sky Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Dark-Sky_Association) approved the Pitcairn Islands as a Dark Sky Sanctuary. The sanctuary encompasses all 4 islands in the Pitcairn Islands Group for a total land area of 43.25 km<sup>2</sup> (163⁄4 sq. mi.).[^cite_note-60]

## Ecology

### Flora

About 16 plant species are thought to be [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) to Pitcairn. These include tapau, formerly an important timber resource, and the [giant nehe fern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiopteris). Some, such as red berry (*[Coprosma benefica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coprosma_benefica?action=edit\&redlink=1)*), are perilously close to extinction.[^cite_note-61] The plant species *[Glochidion pitcairnense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glochidion_pitcairnense)* is endemic to Pitcairn and Henderson Islands.[^cite_note-62] Pitcairn is part of the [Tuamotu tropical moist forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuamotu_tropical_moist_forests) terrestrial ecoregion.[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-63]

### Fauna

See also: [List of birds of the Pitcairn Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_birds_of_the_Pitcairn_Islands) and [List of mammals of Pitcairn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mammals_of_Pitcairn)

Between 1937 and 1951, [Irving Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving_Johnson), skipper of the 29-metre (96 ft) brigantine *Yankee Five*, introduced five [Galápagos giant tortoises](7934681.md) to Pitcairn. Turpen, also known as [Mr Turpen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr_Turpen), or Mr. T, is the sole survivor. Turpen usually lives at Tedside by Western Harbour. A protection order makes it an offence should anyone kill, injure, capture, maim, or cause harm or distress to the tortoise.[^cite_note-64]

The birds of Pitcairn fall into several groups. These include seabirds, wading birds and a small number of resident land-bird species. Of 20 breeding species, Henderson Island has 16, including the unique flightless [Henderson crake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson_crake); Oeno hosts 12; Ducie 13 and Pitcairn six species. Birds breeding on Pitcairn include the [fairy tern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairy_tern), [common noddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_noddy) and [red-tailed tropicbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-tailed_tropicbird). The [Pitcairn reed warbler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitcairn_reed_warbler), known by Pitcairners as a "sparrow", is endemic to Pitcairn Island; formerly common, it was added to the [endangered species](20110668.md) list in 2008.[^cite_note-65]

A small population of [humpback whales](231728.md) migrate to the islands annually, to [over-winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overwintering) and breed.[^cite_note-66]

#### Important bird areas

The four islands in the Pitcairn group have been identified by [BirdLife International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BirdLife_International) as separate [Important Bird Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Important_Bird_Area) (IBAs). Pitcairn Island is recognised because it is the only nesting site of the [Pitcairn reed warbler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitcairn_reed_warbler). Henderson Island is important for its endemic land-birds as well as its breeding [seabirds](147019.md). Oeno's ornithological significance derives principally from its [Murphy's petrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murphy's_petrel) colony. Ducie is important for its colonies of [Murphy's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murphy's_petrel), [herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herald_petrel) and [Kermadec petrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kermadec_petrel), and [Christmas shearwaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_shearwater).[^cite_note-67]

### Pitcairn Islands Marine Reserve

In March 2015 the British government established [one of the largest marine protected areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_protected_areas) in the world around the Pitcairn Islands. The reserve covers the islands' entire [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone)—834,334 square kilometres (322,138 sq mi). The intention is to protect some of the world's most pristine ocean habitat from [illegal fishing](6346579.md) activities. A satellite "watchroom" dubbed Project Eyes on the Seas has been established by the [Satellite Applications Catapult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Space_Agency) and [the Pew Charitable Trusts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pew_Charitable_Trusts) at the [Harwell Science and Innovation Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harwell_Science_and_Innovation_Campus) in [Harwell, Oxfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harwell,_Oxfordshire) to monitor vessel activity and to gather the information needed to prosecute unauthorised trawling.[^cite_note-Gauke_2015-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

## Politics

Main article: [Politics of the Pitcairn Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_the_Pitcairn_Islands)

The Pitcairn Islands are a [British overseas territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_overseas_territory) with a degree of local government. The [King of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_the_United_Kingdom) is represented by a Governor, who also holds office as [British High Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_High_Commissioners_of_the_United_Kingdom_to_New_Zealand) to New Zealand and is based in [Wellington](33804.md).[^cite_note-72]

The 2010 constitution gives authority for the islands to operate as a [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy), with the United Kingdom retaining responsibility for matters such as defence and foreign affairs. The Governor and the Island Council may enact laws for the "[peace, order and good government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace,_order_and_good_government)" of Pitcairn. The [Island Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Council_\(Pitcairn\)) customarily appoints a [Mayor of Pitcairn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_the_Pitcairn_Islands) as a day-to-day head of the local administration.

Since 2015, same-sex marriage has been legal on Pitcairn Island, although there are no people on the island known to be in such a relationship.[^cite_note-73]

The Pitcairn Islands have the smallest population of any democracy in the world.

The [United Nations Committee on Decolonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Committee_on_Decolonization) includes the Pitcairn Islands on the [United Nations list of non-self-governing territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_list_of_non-self-governing_territories).[^cite_note-74]

## Military

The Pitcairn Islands are a [British Overseas Territory](207114.md); defence is the responsibility of the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Ministry_of_Defence) and the [British Armed Forces](31730.md).[^cite_note-CIA-34] The [Royal Navy](26061.md) maintains two [offshore patrol vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_patrol_vessel) in the Indo-Pacific region, [HMS *Tamar*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Tamar_\(P233\)) and [HMS *Spey*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Spey_\(P234\)). Either may be periodically employed for sovereignty protection and other duties around Pitcairn and her associated islands.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

### Agriculture

The fertile soil of the Pitcairn valleys, such as Isaac's Valley on the gentle slopes southeast of [Adamstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamstown,_Pitcairn_Islands), produces a wide variety of fruits, including bananas (Pitkern: *plun*), [papaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papaya) (*paw paws*), [pineapples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple), [mangoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango), [watermelons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watermelon), [cantaloupes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantaloupe), [passionfruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passionfruit), [breadfruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadfruit), [coconuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut), [avocadoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avocado), and [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus) (including [mandarin oranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_orange), [grapefruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grapefruit), lemons and [limes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lime_\(fruit\))). Vegetables include [sweet potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potato) (kumura), carrots, [sweet corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_corn), tomatoes, [taro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taro), [yams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yam_\(vegetable\)), [peas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peas), and [beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bean). [Arrowroot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrowroot) (*Maranta arundinacea*) and [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane) are grown and harvested to produce arrowroot flour and [molasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molasses), respectively. Pitcairn Island is remarkably productive and its benign climate supports a wide range of tropical and temperate crops.[^cite_note-79] All land allocation for any use including agriculture is under the discretion of the government. If the government deems agricultural production excessive, then it may tax the land. If the agricultural land has been deemed not up to the standards of the government, it may confiscate and transfer the land without compensation.[^cite_note-80]

Fish are plentiful in the seas around Pitcairn. [Spiny lobster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiny_lobster) and a large variety of fish are caught for meals and for trading aboard passing ships. Almost every day, someone will go fishing, whether it is from the rocks, from a longboat, or diving with a spear gun. There are numerous types of fish around the island. Fish such as nanwee, white fish, moi, and opapa are caught in shallow water, while snapper, big eye, and cod are caught in deep water, and yellow tail and wahoo are caught by trawling.

### Minerals

Manganese, iron, copper, gold, silver and zinc have been discovered within the [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone), which extends 370 km (230 mi) offshore and comprises 880,000 km<sup>2</sup> (340,000 sq mi).[^cite_note-81]

### Honey production

In 1998, the UK's overseas aid agency, the [Department for International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_International_Development), funded an [apiculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apiculture) programme for Pitcairn which included training for Pitcairn's [beekeepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beekeeper) and a detailed analysis of Pitcairn's bees and honey with particular regard to the presence or absence of disease. Pitcairn has one of the best examples of disease-free bee populations anywhere in the world and the honey produced was and remains exceptionally high in quality. Pitcairn bees are also a placid variety and, within a short time, beekeepers are able to work with them wearing minimal protection.[^cite_note-82] As a result, Pitcairn exports honey to New Zealand and to the United Kingdom. In London, [Fortnum & Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnum_&_Mason) sells it and it is reportedly a favourite of [King Charles](125248.md) and formerly [Queen Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II).[^cite_note-83] The Pitcairn Islanders, under the "Bounty Products" and "Delectable Bounty" brands, also export dried fruit including bananas, papayas, pineapples, and mangoes to New Zealand.[^cite_note-84] Honey production and all honey-related products are a protected monopoly.[^cite_note-85] All funds and management are under the supervision and discretion of the government.[^cite_note-pitcairn.pn-86][^cite_note-government.pn-87]

### Cuisine

Cuisine is not very developed because of Pitcairn's small population. The most traditional meal is pota, mash from [palm leaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_leaves) and [coconut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut).[^cite_note-88] Domestic [tropical plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_plants) are abundantly used. These include [basil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil), [breadfruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadfruit), [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane), [coconut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut), [bananas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bananas) and [beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beans). [Meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat) courses consist mainly of [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish) and [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef). Given that most of the population's ancestry is from the UK, the cuisine is influenced by [British cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_cuisine); for example, the [meat pie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat_pie).[^cite_note-89]

The cuisine of [Norfolk Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Island) is very similar to that of the Pitcairn Islands, as Norfolk Islanders trace their origins to Pitcairn. The local cuisine is a blend of [British cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_cuisine) and [Tahitian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahitian_cuisine?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

Recipes from Norfolk Island of Pitcairn origin include mudda (green banana dumplings) and kumara pilhi.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] The island's cuisine also includes foods not found on Pitcairn, such as chopped salads and fruit pies.[^cite_note-94]

### Tourism

Tourism plays a major role on Pitcairn. Tourism is the focus for building the economy. It focuses on small groups coming by charter vessel and staying at "home stays". About ten times a year, passengers from expedition-type cruise ships come ashore for a day, weather permitting.[^cite_note-FCO-95][^cite_note-96] As of 2019, the government has been operating the MV *Silver Supporter* as the island's only dedicated passenger/cargo vessel, providing adventure tourism holidays to Pitcairn every week. Tourists stay with local families and experience the island's culture while contributing to the local economy. Providing accommodation is a growing source of revenue, and some families have invested in private self-contained units adjacent to their homes for tourists to rent.

Entry requirements for short stays, up to 14 days, which do not require a visa, and for longer stays, that do require prior clearance, are explained in official documents.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] All persons under 16 years of age require prior clearance before landing, irrespective of the length of stay.[^cite_note-99]

### Crafts and external sales

See also: [Postage stamps and postal history of the Pitcairn Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postage_stamps_and_postal_history_of_the_Pitcairn_Islands)



[Stamp of the Pitcairn Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postage_stamps_and_postal_history_of_the_Pitcairn_Islands), 1940, displaying portraits of King [George VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_VI) and Fletcher Christian

The government holds a monopoly over "any article of whatsoever nature made, manufactured, prepared for sale or produced by any of the inhabitants of Pitcairn Island".[^cite_note-pitcairn.pn-86] The flow of funds from these revenue sources are from customer to the government to the Pitcairners.[^cite_note-government.pn-87] The Pitcairners are involved in creating crafts and curios (made out of wood from [Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson_Island_\(Pitcairn_Islands\))). Typical woodcarvings include sharks, fish, whales, dolphins, turtles, vases, birds, walking sticks, book boxes, and models of the *Bounty*. Miro (*[Thespesia populnea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thespesia_populnea)*), a dark and durable wood, is preferred for carving. Islanders also produce [tapa cloth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapa_cloth) and painted Hattie leaves.[^cite_note-100]

The major sources of revenue have been the sale of coins and postage stamps to collectors, [.pn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.pn) domain names, and the sale of handicrafts to passing ships, most of which are on the United Kingdom to New Zealand route via the [Panama Canal](24849.md).[^cite_note-101] The Pitcairn Islands issued their first stamp in 1940. These became very popular with [stamp collectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamp_collecting), and their sale became the dominant source of revenue for the community. Profits went into a general fund which enabled the island to be mostly self-sufficient. This fund was used to meet the regular needs of the community, and pay wages. Funds in excess of regular expenses were used to build a school and hire a teacher from New Zealand, the first professional teacher hired on the island. The fund was also used to subsidise imports and travel to New Zealand. At later points, the sale of coins and .pn domain names also contributed to the fund. Towards the end of the 20th century, as writing letters became less common and stamp collecting became less popular, revenue for the fund declined.[^cite_note-102] In 2004, the island went bankrupt, with the British government subsequently providing 90% of its annual budget.[^cite_note-103]

### Electricity

Diesel generators provide the island with electricity. A wind power plant was planned to be installed to help reduce the high cost of power generation associated with the import of diesel, but was cancelled in 2013 after a project overrun of three years and a cost of £250,000.[^cite_note-104]

All homes have solar systems generating over 95% of the electricity required for home use.

The only qualified high-voltage electrician on Pitcairn, who manages the electricity grid, reached the age of 67 in 2020.[^cite_note-government.pn-87]

## Demographics

Main article: [Pitcairn Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitcairn_Islanders)

The islands have suffered a substantial population decline since 1940, and the island's community recognise that for the long-term sustainability repopulation is the number one strategic development objective (see [§ Population decline](#Population_decline), below). The government is committed to attracting migrants.[^cite_note-star-105]

Only two children were born on Pitcairn in the 21 years prior to 2012. However, in this period, other children were born to Pitcairn mothers who travelled to New Zealand to receive increased health care safeguards during pregnancy and childbirth.[^cite_note-106] In 2005, Shirley and Simon Young became the first married outsider couple in history to obtain citizenship on Pitcairn.[^cite_note-107]

### Language

Over 60% of Pitcairn Islanders are [descendants of the *Bounty* mutineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descendants_of_the_Bounty_mutineers) and Tahitians (or other Polynesians). [Pitkern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitkern) is a [creole language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_language) derived from 18th-century English, with elements of the [Tahitian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahitian_language).[^cite_note-CIA-34][^cite_note-Britannica-57] It is spoken as a first language by the population and is taught alongside English at the island's only school. It is closely related to the creole language [Norfuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfuk_language), spoken on [Norfolk Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Island), because Norfolk was repopulated in the mid-19th century by Pitcairners.

### Religion

The only church building on the island is [Seventh-day Adventist](28632.md).[^cite_note-CIA-34] The Seventh-day Adventist Church is not a [state religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion), as no laws concerning its establishment were passed by the [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government). A successful Seventh-day Adventist mission in the 1890s was important in shaping Pitcairn society. In recent years, the church population has declined, and as of 2000, eight of the then forty islanders attended services regularly,[^cite_note-ANN-108] but most attend church on special occasions. From Friday at sunset until Saturday at sunset, Pitcairners observe a day of rest in observance of the [Sabbath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabbath_and_Seventh-day_Adventism), or as a mark of respect for observant Adventists.

Church of Adamstown

The church was built in 1954. The Sabbath School meets at 10 am on Saturday mornings, and is followed by Divine Service an hour later. On Tuesday evenings, there is another service in the form of a prayer meeting.

### Education

Education is free and compulsory between the ages of five and 15.[^cite_note-PUCEd-109] Children up to the age of 12 are taught at [Pulau School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulau_School), while children of 13 and over attend secondary school in New Zealand, or are educated via correspondence school.[^cite_note-110]

The island's children have produced a book in [Pitkern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitkern_language) and English called *Mi Bas Side orn Pitcairn* or *My Favourite Place on Pitcairn*.

The school on Pitcairn, Pulau School, provides pre-school and primary education based on the New Zealand syllabus. The teacher is appointed by the governor from qualified applicants who are registered in New Zealand as teachers. The government officially took responsibility for education in 1958; the Seventh-day Adventist Church had done so from the 1890s until 1958. There were ten students in 1999; enrolment was previously 20 in the early 1950s, 28 in 1959, and 36 in 1962. The Pulau School has a residence for teachers built in 2004; there was a previous such facility built in 1950.[^cite_note-PUCEd-109]

#### Population decline

As of April 2021, the total resident population of the Pitcairn Islands was 47.[^cite_note-bbc.com-118] It is rare for all the residents to be on-island at the same time; it is common for several residents to be off-island for varying lengths of time visiting family, for medical reasons, or to attend international conferences. A [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora) survey completed by Solomon Leonard Ltd in 2014 for the [Pitcairn Island Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Council_\(Pitcairn\)) and the [United Kingdom Government](25318118.md) projected that by 2045, if nothing were done, only three people of working age would be left on the island, with the rest being very old. In addition, the survey revealed that residents who had left the island over the past decades showed little interest in returning. Of the hundreds of emigrants contacted, only 33 were willing to participate in the survey and just three expressed a desire to return.[^cite_note-Solomon_2014-121]

As of 2014, the labour force consisted of 31 able-bodied persons: 17 males and 14 females between the ages of 18 and 64. Of the 31, just seven are younger than 40, but 18 are over the age of 50.[^cite_note-government.pn-87] Most of the men undertake the more strenuous physical tasks on the island such as crewing the longboats, cargo handling, and the operation and maintenance of physical assets. Longboat crew retirement age is 58. There were then 12 men aged between 18 and 58 residing on Pitcairn. Each [longboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longboat) requires a minimum crew of three; of the four longboat [coxswains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxswain), two were in their late 50s.[^cite_note-government.pn-87]

The Pitcairn government's attempts to attract migrants have met with some success. Since 2015 settlement applications were approved for 8 persons, 3 of whom are living on Pitcairn.[^cite_note-government.pn-87][^cite_note-star-105][^cite_note-122] As of 2016, migrants are expected to have at least [NZ\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NZ\$)30,000 per person in savings and are expected to build their own house at average cost of NZ\$140,000.[^cite_note-billhaigh-123][^cite_note-Menzies-124] It is also possible to bring off-island builders at an additional cost of between NZ\$23,000 and NZ\$28,000.[^cite_note-Menzies-124] The average annual cost of living on the island is NZ\$9,464.[^cite_note-billhaigh-123] There is, however, no assurance of the migrant's right to remain on Pitcairn; after their first two years, the council must review and reapprove the migrant's status.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126][^cite_note-Gay_2013-127]\[^cite_note-[www.gov.uk-128](http://www.gov.uk-128/)]

Freight from [Tauranga](31495916.md) to Pitcairn on the MV *Claymore II* (Pitcairn Island's dedicated passenger and cargo ship chartered by the Pitcairn government) is charged at NZ\$350/m<sup>3</sup> for Pitcairners and NZ\$1,000/m<sup>3</sup> for all other freight.[^cite_note-129] Additionally, Pitcairners are charged NZ\$500 for a one-way trip; others are charged NZ\$5,000.[^cite_note-government.pn-87]

In 2014, the government's Pitcairn Islands Economic Report stated that "\[no one] will migrate to Pitcairn Islands for economic reasons as there are limited government jobs, a lack of private sector employment, as well as considerable competition for the tourism dollar." The Pitcairners take turns to accommodate those few tourists who occasionally visit the island.[^cite_note-government.pn-87]

As the island remains a [British Overseas Territory](207114.md), the British government may at some stage be required to make a decision about the island's future.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

## Culture

The once-strict moral codes, which prohibited dancing, [public displays of affection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_displays_of_affection), smoking, and consumption of alcohol, have been relaxed. Islanders and visitors no longer require a six-month licence to purchase, import, and consume alcohol.[^cite_note-132] There is now one licensed café and bar on the island, and the government store sells alcohol and cigarettes.

Fishing and swimming are two popular recreational activities. A birthday celebration or the arrival of a ship or yacht will involve the entire Pitcairn community in a public dinner in the Square, Adamstown. Tables are covered in a variety of foods, including fish, meat, chicken, pilhi, baked rice, boiled *plun* (banana), breadfruit, vegetable dishes, an assortment of pies, bread, breadsticks, an array of desserts, pineapple, and watermelon.

Paid employees maintain the island's numerous roads and paths. As of 2011, the island had a [labour force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_force) of over 35 men and women.[^cite_note-CIA-34]

[Bounty Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounty_Day) is an annual [public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holiday) celebrated on Pitcairn on 23 January[^cite_note-133] to commemorate the day in 1790 when the mutineers arrived on the island aboard [HMS *Bounty*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Bounty).

### Sport

There is a [tennis court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_court) on the island.[^cite_note-Visit_Pitcairn-134] The Pitcairn Islands are the only member of the [Pacific Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Community) that does not take part in the [Pacific Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Games).[^cite_note-NRL_2024-135] In 2019, the territory approached the [Pacific Games Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Games_Council) about the possibility of membership.[^cite_note-Michael_2019-136]

Australian [National Rugby League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rugby_League) player [Dylan Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan_Walker)'s mother is from Pitcairn.[^cite_note-NRL_2024-135]

## Media and communications

### Post

The [UK Postcode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_codes_in_the_Pitcairn_Islands) for directing mail to Pitcairn Island is PCRN 1ZZ.[^cite_note-137]

### Newspapers

The *Pitcairn Miscellany* is a monthly newspaper available in print and online editions.[^cite_note-138] *Dem Tull* was an online monthly newsletter published between 2007 and 2016.[^cite_note-139]

### Telecommunications

Further information: [Telephone numbers in the Pitcairn Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_numbers_in_the_Pitcairn_Islands)

Pitcairn uses New Zealand's [international calling code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_country_calling_codes), [+64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/+64). It is still on the [manual telephone system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_exchange#Manual_service_exchanges).

### Radio

There is no broadcast station. Marine band [walkie-talkie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkie-talkie) radios are used to maintain contact among people in different areas of the island. Foreign stations can be picked up on [shortwave radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortwave_radio).

### Amateur radio

Callsign website [QRZ.COM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QRZ.COM) lists six [amateur radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_radio) operators on the island, using the [ITU prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITU_prefix) (assigned through the UK) of VP6, two of whom have a second VR6 callsign. However, two of these six are listed by QRZ.COM as deceased, while others are no longer active. Pitcairn Island has one callsign allocated to its Club Station, VP6PAC.

QRZ.COM lists 29 VP6 callsigns being allocated in total, 20 of them to off-islanders. Of these, five were allocated to temporary residents and ten to individuals visiting. The rest were assigned to the [DX-peditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DX-pedition) to Pitcairn, one of which took place in 2012.[^cite_note-140] In 2008 and 2018, major DX-peditions visited [Ducie Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ducie_Island).[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

### Television

Pitcairn can receive a number of television channels but only has capacity to broadcast two channels to houses at any one time. The channels are currently switched on a regular basis.[^cite_note-143] The transmitter was installed in 2006.[^cite_note-144]

### Internet

There is one government-sponsored [satellite Internet](769088.md) connection, with networking provided to the inhabitants of the island. Pitcairn's [country code top-level domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_code_top-level_domain) is [.pn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.pn). Residents pay NZ\$120 (about £60) for unlimited data per month.[^cite_note-145] In 2012, a single 1 Mbit/s link installed provided the islanders with an Internet connection, the 1 Mbit/s was shared across all families on the island. By December 2017, the British Government implemented a 4G LTE mobile network in Adamstown with shared speeds of 5 Mbit/s across all islanders.[^cite_note-146] [Starlink](45111627.md) became available in February 2024.[^cite_note-147]

## Transport

All settlers of the Pitcairn Islands arrived by boat or ship. Pitcairn Island [does not have an airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_without_an_airport), airstrip or seaport; the islanders rely on [longboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longboat) to ferry people and goods between visiting ships and shore through Bounty Bay.[^cite_note-FCO-95] Access to the rest of the shoreline is restricted by jagged rocks. The island has one shallow harbour with a launch ramp accessible only by small longboats.[^cite_note-148] In 2014, a medical emergency requiring transport to a hospital in [Papeete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papeete) involved a 335 [nautical mile](21289.md) (540 km) trip in an [open boat](3996.md) to the island of [Mangareva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangareva), then an [air ambulance](32993239.md) flight 975 [nautical miles](21289.md) (1570 km) to [Papeete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papeete). It was organized by medical authorities in the United Kingdom and New Zealand, and French authorities in [Mangareva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangareva) and [Papeete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papeete). The [British High Commissioner to New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_High_Commissioners_of_the_United_Kingdom_to_New_Zealand) said "It can be a hazardous sea voyage from Pitcairn to Mangareva. This is especially so for open long boats. However, I'm pleased to say that all went well and both boats arrived safely in Mangareva mid-morning today, New Zealand time."[^cite_note-149]

A dedicated passenger and cargo supply ship chartered by the Pitcairn Island government, the [MV *Claymore II*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Claymore_II), was until 2018 the principal transport from [Mangareva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangareva) in the [Gambier Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambier_Islands) of [French Polynesia](10737.md). The supply ship was replaced in 2019 by [MV *Silver Supporter*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Silver_Supporter?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-150]

[Totegegie Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totegegie_Airport) in Mangareva can be reached by air from the French Polynesian capital [Papeete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papeete).[^cite_note-151]

There is one 6.4-kilometre (4 mi) paved road leading up from Bounty Bay through Adamstown.

The main modes of transport on Pitcairn Islands are by four-wheel drive [quad bikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-terrain_vehicle) and on foot.[^cite_note-FCO-95] Much of the road and track network and some of the footpaths of Pitcairn Island are viewable on [Google](1092923.md)'s [Street View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Street_View).[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153]
