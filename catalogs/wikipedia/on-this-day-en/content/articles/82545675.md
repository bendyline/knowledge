At around 12\:53 p.m. on 28 February 2026, following the coordinated [2026 United States–Israeli strikes on Iran](82533582.md), the [Islamic Republic of Iran](14653.md) launched a multiday series of missile and drone airstrikes on the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md). As of 9 April 2026, the UAE has intercepted and destroyed 537 [ballistic missiles](38025.md), 2,256 [drone attacks](38212231.md) and 26 [cruise missiles](6590.md) fired from Iran using their [THAAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_High_Altitude_Area_Defense) and [Patriot](52024.md) missile defence systems acquired from the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-5] The attacks have killed 15 people, including two military personnel, one civilian contractor, and 12 other civilians, and injured 246 others, ranging from minor to severe cases.[^cite_note-United_Arab_Emirates-4][^cite_note-MOD_UAE_on_X-6] According to the [*Wall Street Journal*](173070.md), the UAE responded with strikes on Iranian infrastructure using warplanes and drones in coordination with the U.S. and [Israel](9282173.md), which included the [Lavan Island attack](83180494.md).[^cite_note-Reuters-WSJMay11-7][^cite_note-8]

## Background

Main articles: [2026 Iran–United States crisis](82533582.md), [Iran–Israel proxy conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Israel_proxy_conflict), and [2026 United States military buildup in the Middle East](82251337.md)

Following the high tensions and military conflict that began during late 2023, the ongoing tensions between Iran and the US, the [reinstatement of economic sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_2231) and Iran and the [2025–2026 Iranian protests](81976180.md) and [massacres](82036668.md), the U.S. initiated a [military buildup in the Middle East](82251337.md), with the aim for [regime change in Iran](82756412.md).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] On 28 February, joint U.S.–Israeli strikes were launched against Iran, targeting its leaders and main military infrastructures and air defense systems. In retaliation, Iran launched ballistic missiles and drones against U.S. bases and other targets in several [Arab countries across West Asia](82751620.md) that included the UAE, [Bahrain](82555742.md),[^cite_note-13] [Oman](82559572.md),[^cite_note-14] [Qatar](82557695.md),[^cite_note-15] [Kuwait](82554211.md),[^cite_note-Kuwait-16] [Saudi Arabia](82554167.md),[^cite_note-17] and [Israel](82636370.md).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

As of 1 April 2026, Iran had fired a total of 438 ballistic missiles, 2,012 drones, and 19 cruise missiles at targets in the UAE, according to the UAE Ministry of Defence.[^cite_note-Emirates_News_Agency-20260401-24] Most of the missiles and drones were intercepted by UAE air defences.[^cite_note-MOD-25][^cite_note-Day2-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-BBC-News-2026-29] Even though most of them were destroyed, interception debris and falling projectiles fell on populated areas in [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md) and [Dubai](211583.md) causing damage to civilian infrastructure and starting fires.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-Kuwait-16][^cite_note-Iranian-Shahed-2026-31][^cite_note-BBC-News-2026-29]

Iran launched the first wave of ballistic missiles and drones toward US bases in Abu Dhabi, including [Al Dhafra Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Dhafra_Air_Base). UAE defences intercepted most, but debris fell in a residential area near [Zayed International Airport](487794.md), killing one civilian (a Pakistani national) and injuring seven others from shrapnel and blasts. Loud explosions shook the Corniche, Al Dhafra, and Bateen districts, with smoke rising over the city. This strike targeted US forces at Al Dhafra, causing minor structural damage to base facilities from secondary explosions.[^cite_note-BBC-News-2026-29][^cite_note-32]

### 1 March



An emergency alert sent to residents of the United Arab Emirates shortly before Dubai International Airport was struck at around 12\:30 a.m. local time on 1 March

[^cite_note-Tangalakis-Lippert-33]

During the morning of 1 March, [Dubai International Airport](154742.md) was struck by a suspected air strike from an Iranian plane, leading to five staff being injured[^cite_note-Tangalakis-Lippert-33] and an evacuation.[^cite_note-The-Times-of-India-2026-34] The airport sustained "minor damage", and emergency teams were deployed immediately during evacuation.[^cite_note-35] The incident was contained as contingency plans were already put in place.[^cite_note-The-Times-of-India-2026-34] Videos were later posted on social media at around 1\:30 AM local time, indicating that the airport was struck in [Dubai International Terminal 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_International_Terminal_3) resulting in minor injuries to multiple people and causing damage inside the airport.[^cite_note-36] A fire also broke out at the [Jebel Ali Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebel_Ali_Port), which the Dubai government attributed to debris from the "aerial interception" of an Iranian attack.[^cite_note-37] French naval air base [Camp de la Paix](24598368.md) near [Zayed Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zayed_Port) in Abu Dhabi was also struck by drones.[^cite_note-38]

[AWS](1691376.md) reported that, at 4\:30 AM [Pacific Standard Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Standard_Time) (4\:30 PM [Gulf Standard Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Standard_Time)), one of its data centres in [mec1](69328.md)-[az](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Availability_zone)2 was on fire after being struck by 'objects' and the power was then shut down. Later that day, AWS reported 'localised power issues' in az3.[^cite_note-39]

An EAS warning sent out to residents in the UAE confirming safety.

&#x20;On 2 March, UAE Defence Ministry reported the extent of its operations facing Iran. The Ministry updated its interception figure to 174 ballistic missiles tracked (161 intercepted and 13 falling to the sea) and 689 detected drones (645 intercepted) plus eight cruise missiles which struck the country.[^cite_note-40] Educational institutions shifted to [remote learning](8997.md).[^cite_note-SalimKJ-41] France deployed [Rafale](33731380.md) jets to protect its bases following the attacks at Camp de la Paix and Al Dhafra.[^cite_note-42] An Iranian drone strike on the United States consulate in Dubai caused a fire to break out. The government's media office claimed to have contained it with no injuries.[^cite_note-43] At approximately near midnight of 3 March, explosions were heard in a US consulate. Authorities said that a limited fire broke out in the consulate following a drone targeting operation, and was quickly contained with no injuries.[^cite_note-44] Ship refuelling slowed at [Fujairah](58033142.md) after debris from a drone interception fell in the area of the [Fujairah Free Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujairah_Free_Zone).[^cite_note-45] Debris from drone interceptions rained down on ships off the coast of Fujairah, causing damage.[^cite_note-46] Two oil storage tanks that were severely damage in Fujairah were burning and releasing dark plumes of smoke into the sky, while another had burned.[^cite_note-47]

No injuries were reported, but the tanker was damaged.[^cite_note-103] According to the Ministry of Defense, it engaged eight ballistic missiles, four cruise missiles, and 36 drones launched from Iran on 31 March,[^cite_note-Emirates_News_Agency-20260331-104] that injured four people in a residential community in southern Dubai and caused property damage.[^cite_note-105] According to the [Umm Al Quwain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umm_Al_Quwain) Government Media Office, one person of Indian nationality was severely injured from an intercepted drone, that fell in an industrial building in the Umm Al Thaoub area. According to the report, since the start of the conflict, UAE air defences have engaged a total of 475 ballistic missiles, 23 cruise missiles, and 2,085 UAVs.[^cite_note-110]

### Post-ceasefire

On 8 April, despite the US-Iran [ceasefire agreement](82888027.md), UAE reported that its air defences are intercepting Iranian missiles and drones. According to the report Iran launched 17 ballistic missile and 35 UAVs.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] At the [Habshan](8495134.md) gas complex, two Emiratis and one Indian were injured by debris from an interception. Drones and missiles also attacked an oil terminal in Fujairah causing damage and fires, with no injuries reported.[^cite_note-125]

Iran accused the UAE of launching attacks on a refinery of the [National Iranian Oil Refining and Distribution Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Iranian_Oil_Refining_and_Distribution_Company) on [Lavan Island](1954991.md) just after the ceasefire on 8 April. Some military analysts and Iranian state media identified [United Arab Emirates Air Force](10815770.md) [Mirage 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_2000) fighter jets as having launched the attack, and while the UAE confirmed that Mirage jets were scrambled to intercept Iranian drones, it did not confirm or deny reports that they attacked Lavan Island.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127] In May 2026, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* and other publications reported that the UAE had launched strikes on Iran, including the one on the [oil refinery](195137.md) on Iran's Lavan Island, that had taken place in "early April around the time President Trump was announcing a ceasefire".[^cite_note-WSJ-UAEattacks-128][^cite_note-Reuters-WSJMay11-7][^cite_note-129] Chinese [Wing Loong II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_Loong_II) drones and French [Dassault Mirage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage) jets, both used by the UAE, were spotted over Iran by [OSINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OSINT) researchers.[^cite_note-WSJ-UAEattacks-128]

On 4 May, Iran launched a small scale attack over the UAE. Three Indians were reported to have been injured in Fujairah.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

## Damage

The [Al Minhad Air Base](29156193.md) was also attacked with drones and missiles launched from Iran. The air base is operated by the [United Arab Emirates Air Force](10815770.md) and the [Royal Air Force](25679.md). It is also home to Camp Baird, the [Australian Defence Force](67538.md) Headquarters Middle East (HQME).[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]

Reports indicated that a [Shahed](75153890.md)-type drone struck the [Fairmont The Palm Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairmont_The_Palm) on [Palm Jumeirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Jumeirah), causing a large explosion and fire. Four individuals were critically injured, primarily from debris and blast effects. The attack disrupted the luxurious district, with windows shattering in nearby buildings, a drone attack in the city walk area of Dubai caused a fire and damage which was later contained.[^cite_note-134] The UAE said that it had intercepted a "new wave" of Iranian missiles and that "debris from the interceptions" had fallen in Abu Dhabi and Dubai, causing damage and fire to the lower floor of the [Burj Al Arab hotel](538922.md).[^cite_note-135]

Fallen debris caused damage to structures in Dubai, including areas around [Palm Jumeirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Jumeirah) and other areas of Dubai.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-Iranian-Shahed-2026-31] In the residential and commercial areas of Abu Dhabi and Dubai, debris had caused damage to properties killing several civilians and injuring multiple others.[^cite_note-Day2-26]

The [23 Marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23_Marina) tower was also damaged on multiple occasions.[^cite_note-The_Wall_Street_Journal-20260407-49][^cite_note-9news-20260308-50] On 1 March, Dubai International Airport was hit by debris from a fallen missile that caused multiple injuries,[^cite_note-Tangalakis-Lippert-33] and a large fire that broke out on 16 March was caused by a drone attack that had resulted in an oil tanker explosion.[^cite_note-Ehab1603-73][^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-20260316-74]

## Effects

Some expats have attempted to flee the country out of safety concerns while companies have also attempted to evacuate employees.[^cite_note-146] Costs to flee the country by private jet were as high as \$250,000 on 3 March 2026.[^cite_note-147]

Many loud noises have been heard by residents and citizens in the cities of Dubai and Abu Dhabi. The government and many news outlets ensured the people that they were caused by successful interceptions.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149]

Several reports were issued on the many pets being left abandoned in the streets of Dubai by fleeing [expats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate). Local veterinarians and pet hospitals described being overwhelmed by the abandoned pets.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-The_Standard-20260309-151] Some pets were left tied to poles or stranded in the desert.[^cite_note-152] Others were reported being [euthanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_euthanasia).[^cite_note-The_Standard-20260309-151]

There are some reports that the online posts of social media [influencers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Influencers) in Dubai are being monitored.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155] During the 2026 Iranian missile and drone attacks on the United Arab Emirates, authorities imposed strict restrictions on filming and sharing footage of the incidents. According to international media reports, several individuals, including foreign nationals, were arrested or charged for recording or disseminating videos of the strikes. Authorities also accused some tourists and journalists of "spreading panic" for sharing footage of the damage. Reports indicated that penalties for violations included fines, detention, or deportation.[^cite_note-156] Twenty-one people were reportedly arrested in Dubai for violating the UAE cybercrime law after they filmed, shared, or posted content related to the attacks.[^cite_note-157] Forty-five others were arrested in Abu Dhabi.[^cite_note-158]

As bookings were cancelled and vacancies increased, hotels in Dubai slashed prices and offered discounts.[^cite_note-159]

By early March, [oil production](8082027.md) in the UAE had dropped by between 500,000 and 800,000 barrels per day.[^cite_note-160] The [International Energy Agency](285292.md)'s April oil market report stated that the UAE had pumped 2.4 million barrels/day in March, a decline of one-third from the 3.6 millions barrels/day in February, before the war started.[^cite_note-161]

## Responses

### United Arab Emirates

Officials from the United Arab Emirates condemned the attacks by Iran, calling them a "flagrant violation of national sovereignty and international law", while stating the country has a right to protect itself and its civilians. Safety measures were implemented including airspace closures and public safety advisories, and urged residents to rely on official sources for information.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163] Following the ongoing attacks by Iran, the UAE closed its embassy in Tehran and withdrew its ambassador and all diplomatic staff.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] The UAE reasserted its right to self-defense, rejecting accusations by Iranian Foreign Minister [Abbas Araghchi](21951274.md).[^cite_note-166] A UAE official also stated that they would be open to mediation only if Iran stops the attacks.[^cite_note-167]

In addition, the UAE revoked the operation licenses of five Iranian schools located in the UAE.[^cite_note-168]

UAE President [Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan](2121945.md), together with Saudi Crown Prince [Mohammed bin Salman](38689358.md), warned that continued Iranian attacks on other Gulf states may lead to a regional escalation.[^cite_note-169]

Sultan Al Jaber, Chief Executive of [ADNOC](7992255.md) said that energy infrastructure has been targeted throughout the region, saying that it constitutes "global economic warfare."[^cite_note-170] On 19 March the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs called the Iranian attacks as "Terrorist Attacks".[^cite_note-171] UAE foreign minister Sheikh Abdullah bin Zayed Al Nahyan said that the UAE would not be "blackmailed by terrorists."[^cite_note-172] UAE presidential adviser [Anwar Gargash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_Gargash) said that the war needs to end with a long-term solution for security in the Persian Gulf, and discouraged a ceasefire that will not accomplish that.[^cite_note-173] Analysts argued that the strikes marked a permanent rupture in the UAE's strategic assumptions, with Gulf states facing a more dangerous and complex security environment after the war's conclusion than before it began, regardless of the ceasefire outcome.[^cite_note-174] On 26 March 2026, the UAE along with other five Gulf countries (Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, and Jordan) issued a joint condemnation about Iran and its affiliated armed groups in Iraq, attacks against countries in the region and their facilities and infrastructure.[^cite_note-175] [Yousef Al Otaiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousef_Al_Otaiba), UAE ambassador to the US wrote in a March 2026 WSJ article, that the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), is proof that the [Iranian Revolution](347268.md) that took place 50 years ago is still a threat to global security and economy stability. To resolve this immense military threat Iran imposes, a conclusive solution must be implemented.[^cite_note-176]

UAE Foreign Minister Sheikh Abdullah bin Zayed held a phone call with officials from Afghanistan, Angola, Iraq, and Kenya to discuss the response to the Iranian attacks.[^cite_note-177]

On 28 March, the UAE announced that it was beginning to revoke residency permits for Iranian nationals.[^cite_note-178] On 31 March, the UAE arrested dozens of IRGC-linked money changers.[^cite_note-179]

[Anwar Gargash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_Gargash) said that any deal with Iran would need to include guarantees that Iran would not attack again and reparations for the damage that they caused.[^cite_note-180]

On 19 April, the UAE held initial talks with the US about the possibility of receiving financial support from the US if the Iran war continues to strain the UAE's economy.[^cite_note-181]

### Iran

According to reports citing senior Iranian security sources in March 2026, Tehran alleges the United Arab Emirates played an active role in the US-Israeli war against Iran from its inception. Iran concluded that UAE actions went beyond hosting US military facilities, which had already been targeted by Iranian forces.[^cite_note-182]

In a leaked message, former Iranian foreign minister [Mohammad Javad Zarif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Javad_Zarif) supported the strikes on the UAE, saying that the United Arab Emirates and Israel are 'one and the same'.[^cite_note-183] Tehran accused the UAE of being the launch point of the [US attack on Kharg Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Kharg_Island_raid) on 13 March, urging an evacuation of three ports in the UAE in preparation for its response.[^cite_note-184]

[Ali Larijani](531848.md) rebuked Islamic countries - particularly the UAE - for "abandoning Iran" and siding with the US and Israel.[^cite_note-185]

The Iranian ambassador to the United Nations wrote letters to the Security Council president and the UN secretary-general demanding reparations from Qatar for allowing its territory to be used for attacks on Iran.[^cite_note-186]

In response to UAE revoking residency permits for Iranian nationals, Iran revoked residency permits for 1,200 UAE nationals, and ordered them to leave Iran within a week.[^cite_note-187]

### United States

In March 2026, Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) confirmed the US's commitment to the UAE's security.[^cite_note-188]

### United Kingdom

UK human rights ambassador Eleanor Sanders condemned Iranian missile and drone attacks on its neighboring countries.[^cite_note-189]

### European Union

On 14 April, [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) president [Antonio Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_Costa) stated that the EU was trying to work with the UAE to restore stability to the region and open the Strait of Hormuz.[^cite_note-190]

### G7

The foreign ministers of the G7 condemned Iran’s "unjustifiable" and "reckless" attacks on sites in the region.[^cite_note-191]

### BRICS

The 2026 Iran war exposed significant rifts within the expanded [BRICS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRICS) alliance, paralysing its ability to form a cohesive response or issue a joint statement. The direct involvement of members Iran and the UAE on opposing sides prevented consensus, exposing the limits of the bloc's political unity.[^cite_note-192]
