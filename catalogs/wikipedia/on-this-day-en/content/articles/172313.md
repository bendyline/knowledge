**Gerhard** "**Gerd**" **Müller** (German: [\[ɡɛʁt ˈmʏlɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German); 3 November 1945 – 15 August 2021)[^cite_note-guardianobituary-2] was a German professional [footballer](10568.md). A prolific [striker](428681.md#Striker), especially in and around the [six-yard box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-yard_box), he is widely regarded as one of the greatest goalscorers and players in the history of the sport.[^cite_note-FIFA_Muller-3] With success at both the club level and the international level, he is one of eleven players to have won the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) and the [Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballon_d'Or).

At international level with [West Germany](250204.md), he scored [68 goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_goals_scored_by_Gerd_M%C3%BCller) in 62 appearances, and at club level, in 15 years with [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich), in which he scored 365 goals in 427 [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) matches, he became—and still is—[record holder of that league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Bundesliga_top_scorers). In 74 European club games he scored 65 goals. Averaging over a goal a game with West Germany, Müller is currently joint-29th on the list of [all time international goalscorers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_men's_footballers_with_50_or_more_international_goals), despite having played fewer matches than every other player in the top 37. Among the top scorers, he has the third-highest goal-to-game ratio. He also had the highest ratio of 0.97 goals per game in the [European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Cup), scoring 34 goals in 35 matches.[^cite_note-4]

Nicknamed "*Bomber der Nation*" ("the nation's Bomber") or simply "***Der Bomber***",[^cite_note-FIFA_Muller-3] Müller was named [European Footballer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Footballer_of_the_Year) in 1970. After a successful season at Bayern Munich, he scored ten goals at the [1970 FIFA World Cup](157227.md) for West Germany where he received the [Golden Boot](3482503.md#Golden_Boot) as top goalscorer, before winning the [1970 Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Ballon_d'Or). In 1972, he won the [UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1972) and was the top goalscorer, scoring two goals in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1972_Final). Two years later, he scored 4 goals in the [1974 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup), including the winning goal in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup_Final).

Müller held the [all-time goal-scoring record in the World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_top_goalscorers) with 14 goals for 32 years.[^cite_note-Ronaldo-5] In 1999, Müller was ranked ninth in the European Player of the Century election held by the [International Federation of Football History & Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_Football_History_&_Statistics) (IFFHS), and he was voted 13th in the IFFHS' [World Player of the Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Player_of_the_Century#Vote_specialists_\(IFFHS\)) election.[^cite_note-IFFHS_Century_Elections-6] In 2004, [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9) named Müller in the [FIFA 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_100) list of the world's greatest living players.[^cite_note-FIFA_Muller-3]

## Early life

Gerhard Müller was born on 3 November 1945 in [Nördlingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%B6rdlingen), Bavaria, Germany, the son of Johann Heinrich Müller and Christina Carolina Jung.[^cite_note-7]

## Club career

### TSV 1861 Nördlingen

Müller began his football career at his hometown club [TSV 1861 Nördlingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TSV_1861_N%C3%B6rdlingen) youth ranks in 1958. In 1963 he was promoted to the first team and he scored 51 league goals in 31 appearances in his first season in the [Bayernliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayernliga), Bavaria's regional league.

### Bayern Munich

In 1963, aged 18, Müller joined [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich), where he teamed up with future stars [Franz Beckenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Beckenbauer) and [Sepp Maier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepp_Maier). The club, which would go on to become the most successful German club in history, was then still in the [Regionalliga Süd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regionalliga_S%C3%BCd_\(1963%E2%80%931974\)) *(Regional League South)*, which was one level below the [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) at the time.



Müller autographing a football in 1967. To his left are [Franz Beckenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Beckenbauer) and [Werner Olk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Olk)

After one season, Bayern Munich advanced to the Bundesliga and started a long string of successes. With his club, Müller amassed titles during the 1960s and 1970s: he won the German Championship four times, the [DFB-Pokal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DFB-Pokal) four times, the [European Cup](44220.md) three consecutive years (the first West German team to win it; Müller scored in the [1974 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_European_Cup_final) replay and the [1975 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_European_Cup_final)), the [Intercontinental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European/South_American_Cup) once, and the [European Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) once.[^cite_note-\:1-8]

An opportunistic goal-scorer, Müller also became German top scorer seven times and European top scorer twice. Müller scored 365 goals in 427 Bundesliga matches for Bayern Munich, 53 goals over the second-most successful Bundesliga scorer, [Robert Lewandowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lewandowski), since 2022.[^cite_note-\:1-8]

#### Club goal records

At his peak, Müller held the single-season Bundesliga record with 40 goals in [season 1971–72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_Bundesliga), a record that would be held until Lewandowski scored 41 goals during the [2020–21 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Bundesliga), also for Bayern Munich.[^cite_note-9] Then, Müller also held the record for most club goals scored in all European club competitions in one season for 40 years, [scoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoring_in_association_football) 67 goals during the [1972–73 FC Bayern Munich season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972%E2%80%9373_FC_Bayern_Munich_season), and 85 (72 for Munich, 13 West Germany) goals in total during 1972. However, that record was surpassed by [Lionel Messi](2150841.md) of [FC Barcelona](68187.md) in Spain who scored 73 in the [2011/12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_FC_Barcelona_season) season, and 91 goals in the calendar year of 2012.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

### Fort Lauderdale Strikers

After his career in the Bundesliga, Müller went to the [United States](3434750.md), where he joined the [Fort Lauderdale Strikers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lauderdale_Strikers_\(1977%E2%80%931983\)) (based in the [Miami area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_metropolitan_area)) of the [North American Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Soccer_League_\(1968%E2%80%931984\)) (NASL) in 1979.[^cite_note-\:2-13] He played three seasons with this team, scoring 38 goals,[^cite_note-\:2-13] and reached, but lost, the league final in 1980.[^cite_note-Zeitung_2017-14]

## International career



Müller (right) celebrating after winning the [1974 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup). On the left is [Wolfgang Overath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Overath).

Müller scored [68 goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_goals_scored_by_Gerd_M%C3%BCller) in 62 games for [West Germany](250204.md).[^cite_note-\:1-8] He was Germany's all-time leading scorer for almost 40 years until surpassed by [Miroslav Klose](201840.md) in 2014, though Klose required over double the number of caps to do so, scoring his 69th goal in his 132nd appearance.[^cite_note-15] Müller is one of six players to reach 50 international goals in under 50 appearances, doing so in 41 games.[^cite_note-\:8-16] Müller's international career started in 1966 and ended on 7 July 1974 with victory at the [1974 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup) at his [home stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Stadium_\(Munich\)) in Munich.[^cite_note-17] He scored the winning goal for the 2–1 victory over [Johan Cruyff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Cruyff)'s [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) and later having a goal wrongfully disallowed for offside. His four goals in that tournament and his ten goals at the [1970 FIFA World Cup](157227.md) combined made him the all-time highest [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) goalscorer at the time with 14 goals. His record stood until the [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) tournament, coincidentally held in Germany, when it was broken by Brazilian striker [Ronaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldo_\(Brazilian_footballer\)), who also required more matches than Müller to achieve his tally.[^cite_note-Ronaldo-5] Müller also participated in the [1972 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1972), becoming top scorer with four goals (including two in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1972_Final)) and winning the Championship with the West German team.[^cite_note-\:1-8]

Müller quit playing for West Germany after the 1974 FIFA World Cup triumph. While there was an argument with the [German Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Football_Association) (DFB) at the post-tournament celebration, when officials' wives were allowed to attend but players' wives were not, Müller said that he had made the decision already before the end of the tournament.[^cite_note-18]

## Life after football



Müller in 2006

After Müller ended his career in 1981, he fell into a slump and suffered from [alcoholism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholism). However, his former companions at Bayern Munich convinced him to go through [alcohol rehabilitation](427922.md). When he emerged, they gave him a job as a coach at [Bayern Munich II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich_II).

He held the position from 1992 until he retired in 2014 due to health problems. There is also a collection of apparel released by sporting giants [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) under the Gerd Müller name. It is part of the Adidas originals series. In July 2008, the Rieser Sportpark, in Nördlingen, where Müller had begun his career, was renamed the Gerd-Müller-Stadion in his honour.[^cite_note-Milde_2019-19][^cite_note-Abendzeitung_2008-20]

On 6 October 2015, it was announced that Müller was suffering from [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease).[^cite_note-21] He died on 15 August 2021 in a nursing home in [Wolfratshausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfratshausen), aged 75.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

## Player profile

### Style of play



Müller, [Ruud Krol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruud_Krol) and [Johan Cruyff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Cruyff) in 1978

In his book, *[Brilliant Orange: The Neurotic Genius of Dutch Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brilliant_Orange:_The_Neurotic_Genius_of_Dutch_Football)*, author [David Winner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Winner_\(author\)) writes, "Müller was short, squat, awkward-looking and not notably fast; he never fitted the conventional idea of a great footballer, but he had lethal acceleration over short distances, a remarkable aerial game, and uncanny goalscoring instincts. His short legs gave him a low center of gravity, so he could turn quickly and with perfect balance in spaces and at speeds that would cause other players to fall over. He also had a knack of scoring in unlikely situations."[^cite_note-Schaaf_2012-24]

Müller used extreme acceleration, agility, and deceptive changes of pace to get to loose balls first, and bypass defenders.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-Der_Bomber_turns_75-26] His teammate [Franz Beckenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Beckenbauer) has emphasized Müller's unusual speed: "His pace was incredible. In training I have played against him and I never had a chance."[^cite_note-27]

A prolific goalscorer, Müller was also known for his movement, coordination, intelligence, and clinical finishing inside the penalty area.[^cite_note-Der_Bomber_turns_75-26][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-30_greatest_strikers-29][^cite_note-30] He had the ability to score goals from awkward positions with almost any part of his body, not only with his head or foot.[^cite_note-Der_Bomber_turns_75-26][^cite_note-31]

### Legacy

Müller is widely considered to be one of the greatest strikers and goalscorers in the history of football. He is seen as the greatest "[goal poacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football_positions#Goal_poacher)" in history,[^cite_note-30_greatest_strikers-29][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] with [Gary Lineker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Lineker) calling him "the ultimate goal poacher".[^cite_note-34] Müller is one of only two players, alongside [Lionel Messi](2150841.md), to have won the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md), the [Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballon_d'Or) and the [European Golden Shoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Golden_Shoe).[^cite_note-35] After his death in 2021, [FC Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) president [Herbert Hainer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hainer) declared that Müller was "the greatest striker there's ever been", while [Franz Beckenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Beckenbauer) stated that Müller was "the most important player in the history of Bayern Munich".[^cite_note-36]

This score was bettered in 2006 by Brazil's Ronaldo, and eight years later by German Miroslav Klose who achieved a total of 16 World Cup goals, and who also broke Müller's record for goals for Germany with 71 goals in 137 matches.[^cite_note-rsssf-internationalgoals-53][^cite_note-goal-record-54] However, Müller managed to score eight hat-tricks in his international career.[^cite_note-55]
