The **Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force** ([Japanese](15606.md): 海上自衛隊, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *Kaijō Jieitai*), abbreviated **JMSDF (海自, *Kaiji*)**,[^cite_note-jmsdf-tatsu_s33.04.01-4] also simply known as the **Japanese Navy**,[^cite_note-5] is the [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) branch of the [Japan Self-Defense Forces](2785204.md), tasked with the naval defense of Japan. The JMSDF was formed on 1 July 1954, 9 years after the dissolution of the [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy) (IJN) in 1945, following their defeat in [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-6] The JMSDF has a fleet of 160 [^cite_note-7] ships, 346 aircraft and 50,800 personnel.

## History

### Origin

Main article: [Safety Security Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_Security_Force)

Following [Japan's defeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan) in [World War II](32927.md), the [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy) was dissolved by the [Potsdam Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Declaration) acceptance. Ships were disarmed, and some of them, such as the battleship [*Nagato*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_battleship_Nagato), were taken by the Allied Powers as reparations. The remaining ships were used for repatriation of the Japanese soldiers from abroad and also for minesweeping in the area around Japan, initially under the control of the *Second Bureau of the Demobilization Ministry*.[^cite_note-8] The [minesweeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minesweeping) fleet was eventually transferred to the newly formed [Maritime Safety Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Coast_Guard), which helped maintain the resources and expertise of the navy.

Japan's [1947 Constitution](62699.md) was drawn up after the conclusion of the war, which contained [Article 9](62710.md), which specified that "The Japanese people forever renounce war as a sovereign right of the nation and the threat or use of force as a means of settling international disputes." The prevalent view in Japan is that this article allows for military forces to be kept for the purposes of self-defense. Due to [Cold War](325329.md) pressures, the [United States](3434750.md) was also happy for Japan to provide part of its own defense, rather than have it fully rely on [American forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Forces_Japan).[^cite_note-9]

In 1952, the [Safety Security Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_Security_Force) was formed within the [Maritime Safety Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Coast_Guard), incorporating the minesweeping fleet and other military vessels, mainly destroyers, given by the United States. In 1954, the SSF was separated, and the JMSDF was formally created as the naval branch of the [Japan Self-Defense Forces](2785204.md) (JSDF), following the passage of the 1954 Self-Defense Forces Law.

The first ships in the JMSDF were former [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) destroyers, transferred to Japanese control in 1954. In 1956, the JMSDF received its first domestically produced destroyer since World War II, *[Harukaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JDS_Harukaze)*. Due to the Cold War threat posed by the [Soviet Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Navy)'s sizable and powerful submarine fleet, the JMSDF was primarily tasked with an anti-submarine role.

### Post-Cold War

Following the end of the [Cold War](325329.md), the role of the JMSDF has vastly changed. In 1991, after international pressure, the JMSDF dispatched four minesweepers, a fleet oiler ([JDS *Tokiwa*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Towada-class_replenishment_ship)) and a minesweeping tender (JDS *Hayase*) to the Persian Gulf in the aftermath of the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), under the name of Operation Gulf Dawn, to clear mines sown by [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein)'s defending forces.[^cite_note-kata-10] Starting with a mission to Cambodia in 1993 when JSDF personnel were supported by [JDS *Towada*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Towada-class_replenishment_ship),[^cite_note-kata-10] it has been active in a number of [UN-led peacekeeping operations](15652027.md) throughout Asia.[^cite_note-11]

In 1993, the JMSDF commissioned its first [Aegis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegis_combat_system)-equipped destroyer, [*Kongō*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Kong%C5%8D). It has also been active in joint naval exercises with other countries, such as the United States. The JMSDF has dispatched a number of its destroyers on a rotating schedule to the Indian Ocean in an escort role for allied vessels as part of the UN-led [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom).

### 21st century

The [*Sōryū*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%8Dry%C5%AB-class_submarine) attack submarine *[Kenryū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Kenry%C5%AB_\(SS-504\))*



[JS *Izumo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Izumo), a [multi-role aircraft-carrying cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izumo-class_destroyer) being refitted to carry [F-35B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35B) stealth fighters

The JMSDF, along with the [Japan Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Coast_Guard), has also been active in preventing [North Korean](21255.md) infiltrators from reaching Japan and on 22 December 2001, [engaged and sank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_of_22_December_2001) a North Korean [spy ship](2521578.md) in the [Battle of Amami-Ōshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Amami-%C5%8Cshima).[^cite_note-12]

In 2002, the JMSDF deployed ships to the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) in support of [Operation Anaconda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Anaconda) during the [War in Afghanistan](19666611.md).[^cite_note-13]

In August 2003, a new "helicopter carrier" class was ordered, the [*Hyūga*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hy%C5%ABga-class_helicopter_destroyer) [helicopter destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_destroyer). Due to the size and features of the ship, including a full-length [flight deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_deck), it was classified as a [helicopter carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_carrier) by [Lloyd's Register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd's_Register) — similar to the [United Kingdom](31717.md)'s [HMS *Ocean*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Ocean_\(L12\)). The Self-Defense Forces are not allowed to possess [ICBMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile), [strategic bombers](214209.md), or "attack aircraft carriers".[^cite_note-14] However, according to the Japanese government's definition, "attack aircraft carriers" refer specifically to carriers designed solely for nuclear bombers to conduct nuclear strikes.[^cite_note-15] Aircraft carriers equipped with helicopters, conventional fighter jets or attack aircraft—whether they are small light carriers or large nuclear-powered carriers—are not considered illegal.[^cite_note-16]

With an increase in tensions with [North Korea](21255.md) following the 1993 test of the [Nodong-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nodong-1) missile and the 1998 test of the Taepodong-1 missile over northern Japan, the JMSDF has increased its efforts in [air defense](146640.md). A ship-based [anti-ballistic missile](1791.md) system was successfully test-fired on 18 December 2007 and has been installed on Japan's Aegis-equipped destroyers.

In November 2009, the JMSDF announced plans for a larger "helicopter carrier", the [*Izumo*-class helicopter destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izumo-class_helicopter_destroyer). In 2025, these vessels were re-classified as "multi-role aircraft-carrying cruisers" as part of their conversion to operate [F-35B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35B) V/STOL fighters.[^cite_note-17] This made them *de facto* [aircraft carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_carriers). To avoid controversy, the ruling parties initially classified the vessels "multi-purpose operation destroyers" and then, in 2025, as "multi-role aircraft-carrying cruisers". The first of these ships was laid down in 2012[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] and was launched on 6 August 2013.[^cite_note-21] Two vessels of the class were in service as of 2025.

The [submarine](28825.md) fleet of the JMSDF consists of some of the most technologically advanced diesel-electric submarines in the world. This is due to careful defense planning in which the submarines are routinely retired from service ahead of schedule and replaced by more advanced models.[^cite_note-22] In 2010 it was announced that the Japanese submarine fleet would be increased in size for the first time in 36 years.[^cite_note-23]

After a meeting between the [Japanese Foreign Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Foreign_Affairs_\(Japan\)) [Fumio Kishida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumio_Kishida) ([Second Abe Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Abe_Cabinet)) and U.S. Ambassador to Japan [Caroline Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Kennedy) on 4 March 2014, the [Japanese Defense Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Japan\)) and [U.S. Department of Defense](7279897.md) announced they would hold studies for the joint development of the littoral vessel under the bilateral Mutual Defense Assistance Agreement. The vessel is planned to be a [high-speed trimaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Multi_Purpose_Trimaran_concept) designed for operations in shallow coastal waters capable of carrying helicopters, possibly a lighter variant of the American 3,000-tonne (3,000-long-ton) [littoral combat ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_combat_ship).[^cite_note-thediplomat.com-24]

The study was conducted in response to the growth of the Chinese [People's Liberation Army Navy](24918.md) and budgetary issues with the U.S. military that may affect their ability to operate in the Pacific. The J-LCS would be used to intervene during Chinese ship incursions near the [Senkaku Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senkaku_Islands) and other contested areas in the [East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Sea), and possibly counter similar Chinese vessels like the [Type 056 corvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_056_corvette) and [Type 022 missile boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_22_missile_boat). A 1,000-tonne (980-long-ton) J-LCS with an enlarged hull could operate the [SH-60K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SH-60_Seahawk) anti-submarine helicopter or the MCH-101 airborne mine countermeasures (AMCM) helicopter.[^cite_note-thediplomat.com-24]

On May 1, 2017, [JS *Izumo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Izumo) was dispatched to protect a [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) supply vessel in the Pacific. This was the first time the JMSDF was used to defend allied vessels since the 2016 amendment to the Japanese Constitution.[^cite_note-25]

Japan christened the 84 m (275 ft 7 in) long, 2,950 t (2,900-long-ton) [JS *Ōryū*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_%C5%8Cry%C5%AB_\(SS-511\)) submarine on October 4, 2018. It is Japan's first submarine powered by [lithium-ion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion) batteries and was developed by [Mitsubishi Heavy Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Heavy_Industries). It was commissioned in March 2020.[^cite_note-26]

Japan and the United States conducted the biggest military exercise around Japan in the biennial Keen Sword from 29 October to 2 November 2018. It included a total of 57,000 sailors, marines and airmen. 47,000 service members were from the JSDF and 10,000 from the [U.S. Armed Forces](32212.md). A naval supply ship and a frigate of the [Royal Canadian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Navy) also participated. There were simulations of air combat, ballistic missile defense and amphibious landings.[^cite_note-27]

On 23 May 2019, retired MSDF vice-admiral Toshiyuki Ito stated that Japan requires at least four *Izumo*-class destroyers to be viable for real naval combat operations. He said "If you only have two vessels, you can only use them for training personnel for taking off and landing operations, so this plan doesn't make sense for MSDF officers, frankly speaking." As [aircraft carriers](2219.md), the *Izumo*-class destroyers are relatively small, only able to carry approximately 10 F-35Bs, which Ito argued were too few to provide effective air defense.[^cite_note-28]

In 2019, the [National Diet](163847.md) of Japan approved the order of 42 STOVL Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II aircraft in addition to 135 F-35A model conventional takeoff and landing fighters for the [Japan Air Self-Defense Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Air_Self-Defense_Force) to operate from their land bases; the F-35B is same model aircraft that the US Marines operate from US Navy aircraft carriers and amphibious assault ships, the US Marines also plan to fly from the Japanese *Izumo* class after the STOVL modifications and refit.[^cite_note-29]

On October 14, 2020, the 3,000-ton submarine [*Taigei*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Taigei) was unveiled. This is the first vessel of the *Taigei* class and the 22nd submarine vessel of the JMSDF. The *Taigei* entered service in 2022, and the second ship of the class, [*Hakugei*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Hakugei_\(SS-514\)), was commissioned in 2023.[^cite_note-30]



[Aegis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegis_Combat_System) [guided-missile destroyer](232005.md) destroyer [JS *Maya*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Maya)

On 30 June 2022, the [Japan Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Japan\)) announced the construction of [12 offshore patrol vessels (OPVs)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next-Generation_Offshore_Patrol_Vessel_\(OPV\)_program) by [Japan Marine United Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Marine_United_Corporation) (JMU) for the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force (JMSDF) at a cost of ¥ 9 billion (US\$66 million) per ship. The purpose of this OPV program is to provide enhanced maritime security, particularly around the southwestern [Ryukyu Islands](8458939.md), including the [disputed Senkaku/Diaoyu Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senkaku_Islands_dispute) in the [East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Sea), by boosting JMSDF patrol activities in the region. These vessels are highly automated and configurable to meet a wide range of missions involving "enhanced steady-state [intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence,_surveillance,_and_reconnaissance) (ISR) in the waters around Japan". Under the contract, JMU is charged with delivering the 12 vessels to the JMSDF from fiscal year 2023, which starts on April 1, 2023.[^cite_note-Takahashi-31][^cite_note-Ng-32]

On August 31, 2022, the [Japan Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Japan\)) announced that JMSDF will operate two "[Aegis system equipped ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegis_system_equipped_vessels_\(ASV\))" (イージス・システム搭載艦 in Japanese) to replace the earlier plan of Aegis Ashore installations, commissioning one by the end of fiscal year 2027, and the other by the end of FY2028. The budget for design and other related expenses are to be submitted in the form of "item requests", without specific amounts, and the initial procurement of the lead items are expected to clear legislation by FY2023. Construction is to begin in the following year of FY2024. At 20,000 tons each, both vessels will be the largest [surface combatant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_combatant) warships operated by the JMSDF, and according to *[Popular Mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Mechanics)*, they will "arguably \[be] the largest deployable surface warships in the world".[^cite_note-Lia-33][^cite_note-Mahadzir-34][^cite_note-Yoshihiro-35][^cite_note-Mizokami-36]

On 16 November 2022, the guided-missile destroyer [*Maya*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Maya) fired an SM-3 Block IIA missile, successfully intercepting the target outside the atmosphere in the first launch of the missile from a Japanese warship. On 18 November 2022, the [*Haguro*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Haguro) likewise fired an SM-3 Block IB missile with a successful hit outside the atmosphere. Both test firings were conducted at the [Pacific Missile Range Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Missile_Range_Facility) on [Kauaʻi Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaua%CA%BBi_Island), Hawaii, in cooperation with the U.S. Navy and [U.S. Missile Defense Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Missile_Defense_Agency). This was the first time the two ships conducted SM-3 firings in the same time period, and the tests validated the ballistic missile defense capabilities of Japan's newest [*Maya*-class destroyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya-class_destroyer).[^cite_note-news.usni.org-37]

On 16 December 2022, [Second Kishida Reshuffled Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Kishida_Cabinet_\(Reshuffled\)) approved a trio of defense-related policy documents, including its new National Security Strategy (NSS or 国家安全保障戦略), the strategic guideline document for the Japanese government's policies regarding diplomacy, defense, and economic security for the next decade. Based on the NSS, the National Defense Strategy (国家防衛戦略) outlined Japan's defense policy goals and the means to achieve them while the Defense Buildup Program (防衛力整備計画) outlined the scale of the introduction of specific defense equipment within the budgetary objectives. According to the Defense Buildup Program, the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force (JMSDF) will increase the number of Aegis-equipped guided-missile destroyers (DDG) from the current eight to ten, as well as two Aegis system-equipped vessels (ASEV) to be deployed in ballistic missile defense (BMD) operations. By the end of the decade, the JMSDF will operate 12 ships equipped with [Aegis Weapon System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegis_Combat_System) (AWS) and likewise plans to replace its fleet of older, less capable destroyers and [destroyer escorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abukuma-class_destroyer_escort) with [*Mogami*-class frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogami-class_frigate).[^cite_note-Yoshihiro2-38]

## Capabilities

The JMSDF has an official strength of 50,000 personnel, but presently numbers around 50,800 active personnel.

As a result of continuing effective defense investment due to [Japan's economic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_economic_miracle) and an end to the [Cold War](325329.md), the JMSDF became the world's fourth largest navy by total tonnage by 2000.[^cite_note-\:0-39] Japan has the eighth largest [Exclusive Economic Zone](58123407.md) (EEZ) in the world,[^cite_note-40] and the JMSDF is responsible for protecting this large area. As an island nation, dependent on maritime trade for the majority of its resources, including food and raw materials, maritime operations are a very important aspect of Japanese defense policy.

The JMSDF is known in particular for its [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) and [minesweeping](22102.md#Mine_sweeping) capabilities. Defense planners believe the most effective approach to combating hostile [submarines](28825.md) entails mobilizing all available weapons, including surface combatants, submarines, [patrol planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrol_plane), and [helicopters](8286923.md). They are also known to operate at least fourteen listening stations all over the country that have ELINT and marine surveillance radar warning systems.[^cite_note-41]

Historically, the [Japan Air Self-Defense Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Air_Self-Defense_Force) (JASDF) has been relied on to provide air cover at sea, a role that is subordinate to the JASDF's primary mission of air defense of the home islands. Extended patrols over sea lanes are beyond the JASDF's current capabilities.

The Japanese fleet's capacity to provide ship-based [antiaircraft warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiaircraft_warfare) protection is limited by the absence of full-sized [aircraft carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_carriers), though its [destroyers](8779.md) and [frigates](11464.md) equipped with the [Aegis combat system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegis_combat_system) provide a formidable capability in antiaircraft and [antimissile warfare](1791.md). Additionally, the deployment of F-35B V/STOL fighters on the [Izumo-class aircraft-carrying cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izumo-class_destroyer) are force multipliers for the Japanese fleet, allowing force projection of Japan's sizable surface combatant force far from home waters. Acquiring such capabilities has been contentious in the context of Japan's "passive" defense policy after World War II.

Long-range strike capability will be introduced as soon as Tomahawk cruise missiles are deployed on JMSDF destroyers.[^cite_note-42]

#### Mission in the Indian Ocean

Destroyers and combat support ships of Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force were dispatched to the Indian Ocean from 2001 to 2008 to participate in OEF-MIO (Operation Enduring Freedom-Maritime Interdiction Operation).[^cite_note-43] Their mission is to prevent the marine transportation of illegal weapons and ammunition, and the drugs which fund terrorist activity. Since 2004, the JMSDF has provided ships of foreign forces with fuel for their ships and ship-based helicopters, as well as freshwater.

This was the third time Japanese military vessels had been dispatched overseas since World War II, following the deployments of mine-sweeping units during the Korean War and the Persian [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War). The law enabling the mission expired on 2 November 2007, and the operation was temporarily canceled due to a veto of a new bill authorizing the mission by the opposition-controlled upper chamber of the [Japanese Diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Councillors_\(Japan\)).

In January 2010, the defense minister ordered the JMSDF to return from the Indian Ocean, fulfilling a government pledge to end the eight-year refueling mission. Prime Minister [Yukio Hatoyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukio_Hatoyama) refused to renew the law authorizing the mission, ignoring requests from the American government for continuation. Both the Western alliance country typified by the [Royal Australian Navy](26327.md) and the [Royal Danish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Navy), doing friendship activities in the Indian Ocean.[^cite_note-44]

#### Military exercises and exchanges



[JS *Hyūga*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Hy%C5%ABga), [USS *George Washington*](198834.md) and [JS *Kunisaki*](31244097.md) in formation with other JMSDF ships at the culmination of Exercise Keen Sword 2013, [East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Sea)

The JMSDF and the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) frequently carry out joint exercises and "U.S. Navy officials have claimed that they have a closer daily relationship with the JMSDF than any other navy in the world".[^cite_note-46] The JMSDF participates in [RIMPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIMPAC), the annual multi-national military exercise near Hawaii that has been hosted by the U.S. Navy since 1980. ### JMSDF Aegis Afloat

Main article: [JMSDF Aegis Afloat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JMSDF_Aegis_Afloat)

#### Operational concept



Examples of operations (JSDF image)

On 16 December 2022, the [Japanese Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Cabinet) approved a trio of defense-related policy documents, including its new National Security Strategy (NSS or 国家安全保障戦略), the strategic guideline document for the Japanese government's policies regarding diplomacy, defense, and economic security for the next decade. Based on the NSS, the National Defense Strategy (NDS or 国家防衛戦略) document outlined Japan's defense policy goals and the means to achieve them while the Defense Buildup Program (DBP or 防衛力整備計画) document outlined the scale of the introduction of specific defense equipment within the budgetary objectives. According to the Defense Buildup Program document, the JMSDF will increase the number of Aegis-equipped guided-missile destroyers (DDG) from the current 8 to 10 warships, as well as the introduction of two Aegis system-equipped vessels (ASEV) to be deployed in ballistic missile defense (BMD) operations. By the end of the decade, the JMSDF will operate 12 ships equipped with [Aegis Weapon System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegis_Combat_System) (AWS) and likewise plans to replace its fleet of older, less capable destroyers and [destroyer escorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abukuma-class_destroyer_escort) with [*Mogami*-class frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogami-class_frigate).[^cite_note-Yoshihiro2-38][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

On 23 December 2022, the Japanese Ministry of Defense's 2023 budget and program guidance documented provided examples of operations (運用の一例) for the Aegis-equipped naval forces of the Japanese Maritime Self Defense Force (MSDF). The two ASEV warship would be exclusively tasked for dedicated ballistic missile defense (BDM) missions (BMD等) and operate off the Korean peninsula in the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan), allowing the other Aegis guided-missile destroyers to meet other contingencies (侵攻阻止) while operating independently to maintain the [maritime domain awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_domain_awareness) (MDA) and keep the [sea lines of communication](1365004.md) (SLOC) open in the [East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Sea) southwest of the Japanese home islands (*pictured*).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

#### BMD exercises



BMD maneuvers, October 2022)

On 6 October 2022, five warships from the United States, Japan, and South Korea held a multilateral ballistic missile defense exercise in the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan) (*pictured*) as part of the military response to [ongoing North Korean intermediate-range ballistic missile tests over the Japanese home islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%932022_North_Korean_missile_tests).[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] On 16 November 2022, the guided-missile destroyer [*Maya*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Maya) fired an SM-3 Block IIA missile, successfully intercepting the target outside the atmosphere in the first launch of the missile from a Japanese warship. Two days later, the [*Haguro*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Haguro) fired an SM-3 Block IB missile with a successful hit outside the atmosphere. Both test firings were conducted at the U.S. [Pacific Missile Range Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Missile_Range_Facility) on [Kauaʻi Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaua%CA%BBi_Island), Hawaii, in cooperation with the U.S. Navy and [U.S. Missile Defense Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Missile_Defense_Agency). This was the first time the two ships conducted SM-3 firings in the same time period, and the tests validated the ballistic missile defense capabilities of Japan's newest [*Maya*-class destroyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya-class_destroyer).[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

On 22 February 2023, five warships from the United States, Japan, and South Korea held a multilateral ballistic missile defense exercise in the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan) in response to the launch of a North Korean [Hwasong-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwasong-15) ballistic missile on 18 February 2023, landing in [Japan's exclusive economic zone (EEZ)](58123407.md) in the Sea of Japan, in an area 125 miles west of the island of [Ōshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshima_\(Hokkaido\)), which lies 30 miles (48 km) west of the main island of [Hokkaido](58092.md). Two additional IBCBMs were subsequently launched on 20 February 2023, with both landing in the Sea of Japan off the east coast of the [Korean Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Peninsula).[^cite_note-57] On 19 December 2023, United States, Japan, and South Korea announced the activation of a real-time North Korea missile warning system as well as jointly established a multi-year trilateral exercise plan in response to North Korea's continued ballistic missile launches.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

### Ships and submarines

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of active Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_Japan_Maritime_Self-Defense_Force_ships).

The [ship prefix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_prefix) **JDS** (Japanese Defense Ship) was used until 2008, at which time JMSDF ships started using the prefix **JS** (Japanese Ship) to reflect the upgrade of the Japanese Defense Agency to the [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Japan\)). The JMSDF operates two multi-purpose operation destroyers (de facto [aircraft carriers](2219.md)), two [helicopter carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_carriers) (*called* *[helicopter destroyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_destroyer)*), 36 [destroyers](8779.md), six [frigates](11464.md), six [destroyer escorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destroyer_escort), 23 [attack submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_submarine), 19 [mine countermeasure vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mine_countermeasure_vessel), six [patrol vessels](536773.md), three [landing ship tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_Ship,_Tank), seven training vessels, and a fleet of various [auxiliary ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_ship). The fleet has a total displacement of approximately 624,000 tonnes, excluding auxiliary vessels.

### Aircraft

Main article: [Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Maritime_Self-Defense_Force_aviation)

The Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force aviation maintains a large naval air force, including 201 fixed-wing aircraft and 145 helicopters. Most of these aircraft are used in anti-submarine warfare operations.

## Organization, formations and structure



JMSDF destroyers and training ships docked at [Maizuru Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JMSDF_Maizuru_Naval_Base)



[MV-22Bs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Boeing_V-22_Osprey) landing on [JS *Shimokita*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Shimokita)

The JMSDF is commanded by the Chief of the Maritime Staff. Its structure consists of the Maritime Staff Office, the Self Defense Fleet, five regional district commands, the air-training squadron and various support units, such as hospitals and schools. The Maritime Staff Office, located in Tokyo, serves the Chief of Staff in commanding and supervising the force.

The Self-Defense Fleet, headquartered at [Yokosuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokosuka), consists of the JMSDF's military shipping. It is composed of Fleet Surface Force, based in [Yokosuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokosuka,_Kanagawa), [Sasebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasebo,_Nagasaki), [Maizuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maizuru,_Kyoto) and [Kure](329326.md), the [Fleet Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Air_Force) headquartered at [Atsugi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atsugi,_Kanagawa), the [Fleet Submarine Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Submarine_Force) based at Yokosuka and Kure, the [Mine Warfare Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mine_Warfare_Force_\(Japan\)) based at Yokosuka and the Fleet Training Command at Yokosuka.[^cite_note-loc-60]

In March 2018, [Ryoko Azuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryoko_Azuma) became the first female squadron commander in the JMSDF. Her unit includes the flagship *[Izumo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Izumo)*, the largest warship in the JMSDF. She commands four warships making up a division with a total of 1,000 crew members.[^cite_note-61]

Each Escort Flotilla is formed as a fleet of eight destroyers and eight on-board helicopters. Each force is composed of one helicopter destroyer (DDH)/aircraft carrier (CVM) acting as a command ship, two guided-missile destroyers (DDG) and five standard or ASW destroyers (DD). The JMSDF is planning to reorganize the respective Escort Flotillas into a DDH/CVM group and DDG group, enabling faster overseas deployments.

JMSDF District Forces

### District Forces

Five district units act in concert with the fleet to guard the waters of their jurisdictions and provide shore-based support. Each district is home to a major JMSDF base and its supporting personnel and staff. Each district is home to one or two regional escort squadrons, composed of two to three destroyers or destroyer escorts (DE). The destroyers tend to be of older classes, mainly former escort force ships. The destroyer escorts tend to be purpose built vessels. Each district has a number of minesweeping ships.

### Fleet Air Force

Main article: [Fleet Air Force (JMSDF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Air_Force_\(JMSDF\))

The Fleet Air Force is tasked with patrol, ASW and rescue tasks. It is composed primarily of seven aviation groups. Prominent bases are maintained at [Kanoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanoya_Air_Field), [Hachinohe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JMSDF_Hachinohe_Air_Base), [Atsugi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Air_Facility_Atsugi), [Naha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naha_Air_Base), [Tateyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tateyama_Air_Field), [Omura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cmura,_Nagasaki) and [Iwakuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Iwakuni). The Fleet Air Force is built up mainly with patrol aircraft such as the [Lockheed P-3 Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_P-3_Orion), rescue aircraft such as the [Shin Meiwa US-1A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin_Meiwa_US-1A) and helicopters such as the [Mitsubishi SH-60J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_SH-60J). In the JMSDF, helicopters deployed to each escort force are actually members of Fleet Air Force squadrons based on land.

### Special Forces and Security Units

Special forces and tactical boarding units consist of the following:

[Special Boarding Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Boarding_Unit) (SBU): The JMSDF's designated special forces unit, trained for high-risk, non-compliant maritime interdictions, hostage rescue, and counter-terrorism.&#x20;

Maritime Interception Teams (MIT) (Tachiri Kensatai): Standard, shipboard VBSS (Visit, Board, Search, and Seizure) teams organically assigned to individual destroyers and frigates. Composed of regular ship crew members with specialized security training, they are used for routine, compliant boarding operations and maritime law enforcement.

## Culture and traditions

Main article: [Gosei (meditation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosei_\(meditation\))

### Flag and insignia

The [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy) first adopted the [off-set](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/offset) naval ensign *_Jūrokujō-Kyokujitsu-ki_* (十六条旭日旗) on May 15, 1870, and it was used until the end of World War II in 1945. On June 30, 1954, when the [JSDF](2785204.md) was established, the JSDF and [JGSDF](2978818.md) adopted a different [rising sun flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rising_sun_flag) with 8-rays and an 8\:9 ratio.[^cite_note-FS-63][^cite_note-law\.e-gov-64] The old off-set navy flag with the sun and 16 rays was re-adopted as the ensign of the Maritime Self-Defense Force, but it was modified with a brighter red color. The original flag is darker red ([RGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RGB_color_model) #b12d3d) while the post-WW2 version is brighter red (RGB #bd0029).[^cite_note-FS-63]

The ["Z" maritime signal flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_flag#In_Japan) was used by Admiral Tōgō as a victory flag at the Battle of Tsushima, and has been used on occasion by the JMSDF as an unofficial ensign to avoid controversy with the Rising Sun flag.[^cite_note-65]

### Music and traditions

The JMSDF has maintained some historic links with the [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy). Today's JMSDF continues to use the same martial songs, naval flags, signs, and technical terms as the IJN. The JMSDF still uses the Warship March, the old service march of the IJN, as its official service march. It also maintains the IJN [bugle calls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugle_call) tradition, as every ship and shore establishment command maintain a platoon or squad of [bugle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugle) players.

The [JMSDF band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukari_Miyake) has also added popular anime soundtracks to their repertoire, with the soundtrack from *[Space Battleship Yamato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Battleship_Yamato)* becoming a signature song for the band.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

### Collaboration

The JMSDF started an official collaboration with the warship game franchise [Kantai Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantai_Collection), notably using girls from the franchise as mascots for their ship using the same name. For example, [JS Kaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Kaga) has the ship girl wearing the JMSDF women uniform as her official mascot, and the game introduced the "Kaga Kai Ni Go" ship remodel, notably featuring amament from the early JMSDF, and a flight deck copied from JS Kaga.[^cite_note-68] The girl Shigure is even the official mascot for the [Sasebo Naval District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasebo_Naval_District).[^cite_note-69]

### Food

[Curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_curry) was introduced to Japan by the British [Royal Navy](26061.md).[^cite_note-mod-curry-70] It soon gained popularity among the civilian population and it's not clear whether it became popular as returning Japanese military veterans introduced it, or that the Japanese military adopted it as a staple due to its popularity, however the [Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_curry#Navy_curry) discovered its high thiamine content help combat [beriberi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beriberi), then a constant threat due to the difficulty in providing fresh vegetables on long sea voyages, and dictated it should be served weekly. It quickly became known as a quintessential Navy dish, as Navy cooks adapted the dish to the Japanese palate and further cemented its place in Japanese cuisine.[^cite_note-jmod-71]

Up until the decommission of the JS ***Hachijō* (MSO-303)**, it has continued the tradition of serving curry every Friday, with each galley unofficially competing with other ships to develop the best curry in the fleet, and each ship's recipe a highly guarded secret.[^cite_note-OnoSalat2013-72][^cite_note-times-curry-73]

## Recruitment and training



Members of the crew of [JS *Kongō*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JS_Kong%C5%8D)



The Officer Candidate School

JMSDF recruits receive three months of basic training followed by courses in patrol, gunnery, minesweeping, convoy operations, and maritime transportation. Flight students, all upper-secondary school graduates, enter a two-year course. Officer candidate schools offer six-month courses to qualified enlisted personnel and those who have completed flight school.

Graduates of four-year universities, the four-year [National Defense Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Academy_of_Japan), and particularly outstanding enlisted personnel undergo a one-year officer course at the [Officer Candidate School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Academy_Edashima) at [Etajima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etajima,_Hiroshima), site of the former Imperial Naval Academy. The JMSDF operates a staff college in Tokyo for senior officers.

The large volume of coastal commercial fishing and maritime traffic around Japan limits in-service sea training, especially in the relatively shallow waters required for mine laying, minesweeping, and submarine rescue practice. Training days are scheduled around slack fishing seasons in winter and summer—providing about ten days during the year.

The JMSDF maintains two oceangoing training ships and conducted annual long-distance on-the-job training for graduates of the one-year officer candidate school.[^cite_note-loc-60]
