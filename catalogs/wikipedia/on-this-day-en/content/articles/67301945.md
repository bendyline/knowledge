# 2026 Kosovan presidential election

The [Assembly](1068656.md) of [Kosovo](17391.md) is holding an [indirect election](652583.md) for [president](1260820.md) in 2026, with a second or third round of voting if necessary.

Under Article 86 of the [Constitution of Kosovo](9516144.md), the [Assembly](1068656.md) must elect a president no later than 30 days before the end of the incumbent’s term.[^cite_note-2]

An initial attempt on 5 March 2026 failed due to lack of quorum, opening a 34-day constitutional window.[^cite_note-3] President [Vjosa Osmani](61979540.md) dissolved the Assembly the next day and moved toward snap elections; [Vetëvendosje](3225595.md) challenged the decree.[^cite_note-4] On 9 March the [Constitutional Court](26764195.md) suspended it.[^cite_note-5] On 25 March the Court ruled the dissolution had no legal effect and gave the Assembly until 28 April to elect a president.[^cite_note-6]

Osmani’s term expired on 4 April. Assembly Speaker [Albulena Haxhiu](43297113.md) ([LVV](3225595.md)) became acting president for up to six months.[^cite_note-7] A second attempt around 27–28 April was boycotted by the main opposition parties ([PDK](1260606.md), [LDK](1256321.md), [AAK](1260747.md)), who called it a “propaganda show.” The Assembly again failed to elect a president by the Court’s deadline and was dissolved.[^cite_note-8] Haxhiu called snap parliamentary elections for 7 June 2026.[^cite_note-9]

[Vetëvendosje](3225595.md) and it's allies once again won a majority in the [June election](82589105.md) but still lacked the two-thirds support needed for a president.[^cite_note-10] On 31 August, [LVV](3225595.md) and [LDK](1256321.md) agreed to nominate [Bekim Sejdiu](84106691.md) (former [Constitutional Court](26764195.md) judge, diplomat, and law professor) as a consensus candidate.[^cite_note-11] Internal [LDK](1256321.md) opposition (six MPs) immediately blocked the required 80-vote quorum in the first two rounds (or 61 in a third).[^cite_note-12] [LDK](1256321.md) leader [Lumir Abdixhiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumir_Abdixhiku) later indicated the rejection pointed toward new snap elections.[^cite_note-13]

If no President is elected by then, on 4 October (which is the deadline for the acting president’s term), the Acting President role will once again transfer to the Speaker of the Assembly, sparking speculation in the media about whether newly elected on 9 September Speaker and President Albulena Haxhiu will be able to constitutionally remain in her Acting President role beyond 4 October.[^cite_note-14]

Disputes over deputy speakers continued to delay full inauguration until 13 September when, despite the non participation of the second biggest party, [PDK](1260606.md), the Assembly was constituted as all other deputy speakers were elected by [LVV](3225595.md) and it's allies' parliamentary majority.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The same day the [Fourth Cabinet of Albin Kurti](84217976.md) was sworn in obtaining the support of a majority of 62 MPs, triggering at the same time a 60-day window to elect the president before snap elections would have to be held.[^cite_note-17]

On 23 September the [Constitutional Court](26764195.md) shortened the time window, ruling that lawmakers must elect a new president by October 6, failing to do so would lead to new snap elections, the fourth in two years.[^cite_note-18]

## Background

Incumbent president Vjosa Osmani took office on 4 April 2021, and her five-year term ended on 4 April 2026.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Under the Constitution, she was eligible to seek one further term.[^cite_note-21]

Instead of nominating her, on 4 March 2026, Vetëvendosje nominated Glauk Konjufca as its presidential candidate ahead of the Assembly session.[^cite_note-22] No other party submitted a candidate. Speaker of the Assembly Albulena Haxhiu said that a session to elect the president could not be called without at least two candidates.[^cite_note-23] Opposition parties, including the LDK, PDK and AAK, rejected Vetëvendosje's approach and called instead for a consensual candidate, contributing to the deadlock that led to the failed 5 March session.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

### Proposed constitutional amendments

On 5 March 2026, amid the deadlock over the presidential election, President Vjosa Osmani submitted constitutional amendments that would have changed the method of electing the president from an Assembly vote to direct popular election.[^cite_note-26] The proposal was based on earlier draft amendments prepared in 2011.[^cite_note-27] Vetëvendosje supported the initiative, and the Assembly's Committee on Legislation approved sending it forward, but opposition parties criticised the move and argued that such changes could not be adopted through a rushed procedure.[^cite_note-28] Because constitutional amendments in Kosovo require a two-thirds majority of all deputies, including two-thirds of deputies holding reserved or guaranteed seats for non-majority communities, the proposal faced long odds.[^cite_note-29] Later that day, the Assembly failed to vote on the amendments because there was no quorum.[^cite_note-30]

### Constitutional Court ruling and April 28 deadline

After the Constitutional Court's ruling of 25 March 2026, which invalidated the dissolution of the Assembly, lawmakers were given until 28 April 2026 to complete the presidential election.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] Although the Assembly retained that deadline, President Vjosa Osmani's term ended on 4 April 2026.[^cite_note-33] Under Kosovo's constitutional arrangements, Speaker of the Assembly Albulena Haxhiu then assumed the office of acting president until a new president is elected.[^cite_note-34] The Court's ruling also restored the Assembly's role in continuing the interrupted election process.[^cite_note-35]

### Albulena Haxhiu as Acting President



[Albulena Haxhiu](43297113.md) assumes the duties of Acting [President of Kosovo](1260820.md) on 4 April 2026, after the expiration of [Vjosa Osmani](61979540.md)'s presidential term.

On 4 April 2026, Vjosa Osmani's five-year term as president ended, and Speaker of the Assembly Albulena Haxhiu became acting president in accordance with Kosovo's constitutional procedure.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] She will remain in office until the Assembly elects a new president, for up to six months if necessary.[^cite_note-38] The Constitutional Court had previously set 28 April 2026 as the deadline for the Assembly to complete the presidential election.[^cite_note-39] Haxhiu said she would exercise the office in accordance with the Constitution and in the public interest.[^cite_note-40]

On 3 September 2026, as Haxhiu's constitutional six-month mandate neared its expiration on 4 October, the Presidency of Kosovo clarified the institutional succession path.[^cite_note-ZeriSept3-41] The office stated that the country cannot remain without an acting head of state and announced that once the 11th legislature of the Assembly is fully constituted, the role of acting president will automatically transfer to the newly elected Speaker of the Assembly until a full-term president is officially chosen.[^cite_note-ZeriSept3-41] Furthermore, the Presidency noted that it remained open to requesting a legal clarification from the Constitutional Court if the Assembly fails to elect a new president by the 4 October deadline.[^cite_note-ZeriSept3-41]

## Electoral system

Initially, a candidate is required to receive at least 80 votes, equivalent to two-thirds of the 120 members of the [Assembly](1068656.md), in order to be elected.[^cite_note-constitution-42] However, if no candidate succeeds during the first two rounds, a third round is held between the top two candidates of the second round, and the requirement is reduced to a simple majority of 61 votes.[^cite_note-constitution-42][^cite_note-43] If the third round also does not produce a successful candidate, the Assembly is dissolved, with new elections to take place within 45 days.[^cite_note-constitution-42]

Article 86 states that the election of the President shall take place no later than 30 days before the end of the current president's term of office.[^cite_note-constitution-42] Additionally, Article 82 also states that the Assembly will dissolve if they do not elect a President within 60 days from the date of the beginning of the president's election procedure.[^cite_note-Constitution2-44] With the inaugural voting session having convened on 5 March, this 60-day countdown has been officially activated.[^cite_note-Lapsi60days-45]

Following the unsuccessful parliamentary session on 5 March due to a lack of a quorum, [Lëvizja Vetëvendosje](3225595.md) formally petitioned the [Constitutional Court of Kosovo](26764195.md) to impose a temporary measure suspending these strict constitutional deadlines until the court issues a final judgment.[^cite_note-46] The party argued that the intentional obstruction of the quorum by members of parliament who refused to participate constitutes a constitutional violation. In their request, they asked the court to mandate that all deputies have a binding obligation to participate in the presidential vote—whether voting in favor, against, or abstaining—to prevent the country from being forced into early legislative elections due to political blockades.[^cite_note-47] On 30 March, the Constitutional Court rejected Vetëvendosje's request and declined to impose an interim measure, thereby determining that deputies are not obliged to remain in the parliament for the presidential vote.[^cite_note-48]

Despite securing 74 votes for Bekim Sejdiu, the coalition fell short of the 80-deputy quorum required by Article 86, allowing just six dissenting LDK MPs to block the election.[^cite_note-49]

## Results

**First attempt (Legislature X)**

The first attempt to elect the president took place on 5 March 2026. Lawmakers from the [Democratic Party of Kosovo](1260606.md) (PDK) walked out of the parliamentary session, preventing the quorum required for the election.[^cite_note-QuorumFails2-87] The [Democratic League of Kosovo](1256321.md) (LDK) also refused to participate, with leader [Lumir Abdixhiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumir_Abdixhiku) stating that the country was heading toward a new election cycle.[^cite_note-88] Only 66 deputies remained in the chamber, failing to meet the minimum of 80 required for a quorum, leading to the suspension of the session.[^cite_note-SuspendedSession-89]

**Second attempt (Legislature X)**

A second attempt to elect the president took place on 27 April 2026. Ahead of the session, the LDK, PDK, and AAK maintained a united opposition boycott, effectively preventing a quorum. The LDK reiterated its stance that the vote was a "propaganda show," while the PDK and AAK criticized the lack of political consensus and "seriousness" surrounding the session.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] Despite the lack of quorum, a voting round took place which resulted in 63 ballots being cast for [Feride Rushiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feride_Rushiti), one invalid vote, and zero votes for Hatixhe Hoxha.[^cite_note-93]

## Aftermath

After the Assembly failed to elect a president on 5 March 2026 because only 66 deputies were present, President Vjosa Osmani issued a decree on 6 March dissolving the Assembly and opening the way for snap parliamentary elections.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] Prime Minister Albin Kurti and Vetëvendosje challenged the decree before the Constitutional Court, arguing that the Assembly still had time to complete the presidential election process under the Constitution.[^cite_note-98] On 9 March, the Court suspended the decree pending review.[^cite_note-99] On 25 March, it ruled that the decree had no legal effect and gave the Assembly 34 days, until 28 April 2026, to elect a president.[^cite_note-100]

### International reactions

The failure of the Assembly to elect a new president and the subsequent announcement of snap elections drew calls for constitutional adherence from the international community.

-  [Germany](11867.md): The German Embassy in Pristina stated that it was following the ongoing developments closely. Embassy official Catalina Heine emphasized that Germany "expects all actors to act in accordance with the Constitution" during the election process.

  [^cite_note-Telegrafi2026Apr29-101]

  [^cite_note-Balkanweb2026Apr28-102]

-  [European Union](9317.md): The EU Office in Pristina reacted to the political developments by emphasizing that the "normal functioning of institutions" is a prerequisite for Kosovo to benefit from the Western Balkans Growth Plan. While not naming the presidential process directly, spokesperson Nikola Gaon urged all political forces to implement the Constitution to ensure institutional stability.

  [^cite_note-Balkanweb2026Apr28-102]

-  [United Kingdom](31717.md): The British Embassy issued a clarification denying claims made by candidate [Feride Rushiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feride_Rushiti) that she had received its support. The embassy stated: "The United Kingdom has no role in proposing or supporting any specific candidate in Kosovo and has not done so." Following the deadlock, the embassy noted that "Kosovo will hold new national elections" and urged all political parties to act "in accordance with the law and the Constitution" and to conduct the upcoming campaign "constructively and with respect."

  [^cite_note-RTV212026Apr29-103]

-  [United States](3434750.md): The [U.S. Embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_the_United_States,_Pristina) in Pristina dismissed claims that it had requested or proposed the candidacy of [Feride Rushiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feride_Rushiti). An embassy spokesperson clarified that the United States does not "propose or support specific candidates for president" and emphasized that the selection process is a matter for Kosovo's leaders and its people. The embassy further called on political actors to work together constructively to resolve the political impasse.

  [^cite_note-Veriu2026Apr29-104]
