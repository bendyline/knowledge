**Staffordshire** ([/ˈstæfərdʃɪər, -ʃər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-5] postal abbreviation *Staffs*.) is a [ceremonial county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England) in the [West Midlands](351076.md) of England. It borders [Cheshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire) to the north-west, [Derbyshire](71070.md) and [Leicestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicestershire) to the east, [Warwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwickshire) to the south-east, the [West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_\(county\)) county and [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire) to the south, and [Shropshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire) to the west. The largest settlement is the city of [Stoke-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke-on-Trent).

The county has an area of 1,713 square kilometres (661 sq mi) and had an estimated population of 1,177,578 in 2024. Stoke-on-Trent is in the north of the county, and the town of [Newcastle-under-Lyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle-under-Lyme) immediately to the west. [Stafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford) is in the centre, [Burton upon Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_upon_Trent) in the east, and [Tamworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamworth,_Staffordshire) and the city of [Lichfield](159948.md) in the south-east. For local government purposes Staffordshire comprises a [non-metropolitan county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_county), with eight districts, plus the [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England) area of Stoke-on-Trent. The county [historically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_counties_of_England) included the north-west of the West Midlands county, including [Walsall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walsall), [West Bromwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bromwich), and [Wolverhampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton).

Staffordshire is hilly to the north and south. The southern end of the [Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennines) is in the north, containing part of the [Peak District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_District) [National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_parks_of_the_United_Kingdom). The [Cannock Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannock_Chase) [AONB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AONB) and parts of the [National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Forest,_England) and [Kinver Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinver_Edge) are in the south. The [River Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Trent) and its tributaries drain most of the county. From its source, near [Biddulph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biddulph), the river flows through Staffordshire in a southwesterly direction, meeting the [Sow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Sow) just east of Stafford; it then meets the [River Tame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Tame,_West_Midlands) and turns north-east, exiting into [Derbyshire](71070.md) immediately downstream of Burton upon Trent.

Staffordshire contains a number of [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) [tumuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumulus) and [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain) camps, and was settled by the [Angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angles_\(tribe\)) in the sixth century; the oldest [Stafford knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford_knot), the county's symbol, can be seen on an Anglian cross in the churchyard of [Stoke Minster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke_Minster). The county was formed in the early tenth century, when Stafford became the capital of [Mercia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercia). The county was relatively settled in the following centuries, and rapidly industrialised during the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution), when the [North Staffordshire coalfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Staffordshire_Coalfield) was exploited and fuelled the iron and automobilie industries in the south of the county. [Pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_Potteries) is the county's most famous export; a limited amount is still produced in Stoke-on-Trent.

Historically, Staffordshire was divided into five [hundreds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_\(division\)): [Cuttlestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuttlestone), [Offlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offlow), [Pirehill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirehill_Hundred), [Seisdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seisdon), and [Totmonslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totmonslow).



[Lichfield Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichfield_Cathedral)

The historic boundaries of Staffordshire cover much of what is now the [metropolitan county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_county) of [West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_\(county\)). An [administrative county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_counties_of_England) of Staffordshire was set up in 1889 under the [Local Government Act 1888](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1888) covering the county, except for the [county boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_borough) of [Wolverhampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton), [Walsall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walsall), and [West Bromwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bromwich) in the south (the area known as the [Black Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Country)), and [Hanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanley,_Staffordshire) in the north. The Act also saw the towns of [Tamworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamworth,_Staffordshire) (partly in Warwickshire) and [Burton upon Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_upon_Trent) (partly in Derbyshire) united entirely in Staffordshire. In 1553, [Queen Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_I_of_England) made [Lichfield](159948.md) a [county corporate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_corporate), meaning it was administered separately from the rest of Staffordshire, remaining so until 1888.

[Handsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handsworth,_West_Midlands) and [Perry Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry_Barr) became part of the county borough of [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham), and thus [Warwickshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwickshire), in 1911 and 1928 respectively. Burton, in the east of the county, became a county borough in 1901, and was followed by [Smethwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smethwick), another town in the Black Country in 1907. In 1910 the six towns of the Staffordshire Potteries, including Hanley, became the single county borough of [Stoke-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke-on-Trent).



The [Staffordshire Hoard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_Hoard), discovered in a field near [Lichfield](159948.md) in July 2009, is perhaps the most important collection of [Anglo-Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon) objects found in England.

A significant boundary change occurred in 1926 when the east of [Sedgley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedgley) was transferred to [Worcestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcestershire) to allow the construction of the new [Priory Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priory_Estate) on land purchased by [Dudley County Borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_County_Borough) council.[^cite_note-6]

A major reorganisation in the Black Country in 1966, under the recommendation of the [Local Government Commission for England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Commission_for_England_\(1958%E2%80%931967\)), led to the creation of an area of contiguous county boroughs. The [County Borough of Warley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Borough_of_Warley) was formed by the merger of the county borough of Smethwick and municipal borough of [Rowley Regis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowley_Regis) with the Worcestershire borough of [Oldbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldbury,_West_Midlands): the resulting county borough was associated with Worcestershire. Meanwhile, the county borough of [Dudley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley), historically a [detached part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave) of Worcestershire, expanded and became associated with Staffordshire instead. This reorganisation led to the administrative county of Staffordshire having a thin protrusion passing between the county boroughs (to the east) and Shropshire, to the west, to form a short border with Worcestershire.

Under the [Local Government Act 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972), on 1 April 1974, the county boroughs of the Black Country and the [Aldridge-Brownhills Urban District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldridge-Brownhills_Urban_District) of Staffordshire became, along with Birmingham, Solihull, and Coventry and other districts, a new metropolitan county of West Midlands. County boroughs were abolished, with Stoke becoming a non-metropolitan district in Staffordshire, and Burton forming an [unparished area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unparished_area) in the district of [East Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Staffordshire). On 1 April 1997, under a recommendation of the [Banham Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Commission_for_England_\(1992\)), Stoke-on-Trent became a unitary authority independent of Staffordshire once more.

In July 2009, the largest hoard of Anglo-Saxon gold ever found in Britain was discovered in a field near Lichfield. The artefacts, known as [The Staffordshire Hoard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Staffordshire_Hoard), have tentatively been dated to the 7th or 8th centuries, placing the origin of the items in the time of the [Kingdom of Mercia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mercia).

## Education

Main article: [List of schools in Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Staffordshire)

Staffordshire has a completely comprehensive system with eight independent schools. Most secondary schools are from 11 to 16 or 18, but two in Staffordshire Moorlands and South Staffordshire are from 13 to 18. Resources are shared where appropriate.

There are two universities in the county, [Keele University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keele_University), west of [Newcastle-under-Lyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle-under-Lyme), and the [University of Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_University), which has campuses in Stoke-on-Trent, Stafford, London, [Lichfield](159948.md) and [Shrewsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrewsbury).[^cite_note-7]

## Sport

The modern county of Staffordshire currently has three professional football clubs – [Stoke City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke_City_F.C.) and [Port Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Vale_F.C.), both from Stoke-on-Trent, and [Burton Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_Albion_F.C.), who play in [Burton upon Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_upon_Trent).

Stoke City, one of the oldest professional football clubs in existence, was founded in 1863 and played at the [Victoria Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Ground) for 119 years from 1878 until its relocation to the Britannia Stadium (now named the [Bet365 Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bet365_Stadium)) in 1997. They were among the 12 founder members of the [Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League) in 1888.[^cite_note-8] By the late 1930s, they were established [First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division) members and boasted arguably the finest footballer in England at the time in right-winger [Stanley Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Matthews), who had two spells with the club between 1930 and his retirement in 1965 at the age of 50.[^cite_note-9] In 1972, the club finally won a major trophy when they lifted the [Football League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Cup),[^cite_note-10] but after relegation from the First Division in 1985 they would not experience top flight football for 23 years.[^cite_note-11] After spending some two decades bouncing between the second and third tiers of the English league, they finally reclaimed their top flight status in 2008 by securing promotion to the [Premier League](11250.md).[^cite_note-12] Stoke City reached their first [FA Cup](11237.md) final in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_FA_Cup_Final), but lost 1–0 to [Manchester City](165813.md).[^cite_note-13]

Port Vale, who like Stoke City play in Stoke-on-Trent, was formed in 1876 and became members of the Football League in 1892. After more than 70 years at various stadiums around the city, the club moved to its present home, [Vale Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_Park), in 1950. In early 1936, they had eliminated First Division champions [Sunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunderland_A.F.C.) from the FA Cup. Another FA Cup success came in February 1988 when they eliminated seven-time winners [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) from the [competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987-88_FA_Cup). Promotion to the [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division) for the first time since the 1960s was secured in 1989, and Vale would spend nine of the next 11 years at this level. However, the club has been less successful since the turn of the 21st century, and suffered relegation to [League Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Two) – the fourth tier of the English league – in 2008. The club has seen an upturn in its fortunes as the club was promoted to League One in the 2012–13 season. In the [2016–17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_EFL_League_One) season Port Vale were relegated back to League Two.[^cite_note-14]

[West Bromwich Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bromwich_Albion_F.C.), [Wolverhampton Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton_Wanderers_F.C.) and [Walsall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walsall_F.C.) are also notable clubs based in the historic county boundaries.

The county's other professional football team is [Burton Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_Albion_F.C.), based in [Burton upon Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_upon_Trent), who currently play in [League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_League_One).

The county has a number of non-league football clubs, including [Tamworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamworth_F.C.),[^cite_note-15] [Stafford Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford_Rangers_F.C.),[^cite_note-16] [Hednesford Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hednesford_Town_F.C.),[^cite_note-17] [Chasetown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chasetown_F.C.) and [Leek Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leek_Town_F.C.).[^cite_note-18]

In [cricket](25675557.md), Staffordshire is one of the nineteen [Minor counties of English and Welsh cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_counties_of_English_and_Welsh_cricket). It is represented in Minor counties cricket by [Staffordshire County Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_County_Cricket_Club) who have played in the [Minor Counties Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Counties_Championship) since 1895, a competition which it has won outright eleven times, making it the most successful Minor counties team. Famous international cricketers produced by the county include [Sydney Barnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Barnes), [Bob Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Taylor_\(cricketer\)) and [Dominic Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Cork), all of whom went on to represent [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_cricket_team).

## Geography

See also: [Geology of Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Staffordshire)

See also: [List of reservoirs in Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reservoirs_in_Staffordshire)



[Mow Cop Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mow_Cop_Castle) on the [Cheshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire) border

In the north and in the south, the county is hilly, with the southern foothills and uplands of the [Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennines) in the north, with parts of it in the [Peak District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_District) National Park.[^cite_note-19] Most of the northern upland terrain is in the [Staffordshire Moorlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_Moorlands) district. [Cannock Chase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannock_Chase), an area of natural beauty, and parts of the [National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Forest,_England) and [Kinver Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinver_Edge) are in the south. In the middle regions, the landscape is low and undulating. In parts of the county are once significant [coalfields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalfield), including the [South Staffordshire coalfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Staffordshire_coalfield). In the southern part, there are also rich iron ore deposits. The soil is chiefly clay and agriculture was not highly developed until the mechanisation of farms.

Staffordshire is home to the highest village in Britain, [Flash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash,_Staffordshire). The village, in the Staffordshire Moorlands, stands at 1,519 ft (463 m) above sea level. This record was confirmed in 2007 by the [Ordnance Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordnance_Survey) after [Wanlockhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanlockhead) in Scotland also claimed the record. The [BBC](19344654.md)'s *[The One Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_One_Show)* investigated the case in a bid to settle the argument and Flash was confirmed as the higher of the two. The highest point in Staffordshire is [Cheeks Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheeks_Hill).[^cite_note-20] The largest river is the [River Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Trent), which drains most of the county along with its tributaries. From its source, near [Biddulph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biddulph), the Trent flows through Staffordshire in a southwesterly direction and meets the [River Sow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Sow) just east of Stafford. It then meets the [River Tame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Tame,_West_Midlands) and turns north-east, exiting into [Derbyshire](71070.md) immediately downstream of Burton upon Trent.

### Green belt

Further information: [West Midlands Green Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_Green_Belt), [Stoke-on-Trent Green Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke-on-Trent_Green_Belt), and [Burton upon Trent and Swadlincote Green Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_upon_Trent_and_Swadlincote_Green_Belt)

Staffordshire contains sectors of three [green belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_belt_\(United_Kingdom\)) areas, two of which surround the large conurbations of [Stoke-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Potteries_Urban_Area) and the [West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_conurbation), and were first drawn up from the 1950s. All the county's districts contain some portion of belt.

## Demographics

According to the [2001 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_United_Kingdom_census) the population of the non-metropolitan Staffordshire is 806,744 and the population of Stoke-on-Trent was 240,636 making a total population of 1,047,380. In non-metropolitan Staffordshire, White British is the largest ethnicity, making up 96% of the population. This is followed by Irish, making up 0.6%. Non-White citizens make up 2% of the population. The largest non-White ethnic group are [British Pakistanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Pakistanis).[^cite_note-21] 94% of the population was born in England, and those born in [Scotland](26994.md) and [Wales](69894.md) together make up 1% of the total population.[^cite_note-22]

### County council

[Staffordshire County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_County_Council) is the top-tier [local council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_England) for the non-metropolitan county. For [Eurostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostat) purposes, it is a [NUTS 3 region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NUTS_of_the_United_Kingdom) (code UKG22).

Staffordshire operates a [cabinet-style council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet-style_council). There are 62 councillors for Staffordshire. The Full Council elects a cabinet of 10 councillors, including the council leader, from the majority party. Each cabinet member has their own portfolio about which they make the "day to day" decisions.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

### Future

Main article: [Upcoming structural changes to local government in England § Staffordshire and Stoke-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upcoming_structural_changes_to_local_government_in_England#Staffordshire_and_Stoke-on-Trent)

In July 2026, the [UK Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Government) announced that it intends to reorganise Staffordshire into two [unitary authority areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority_areas) by April 2028. The county will be divided north-south, with the northern area combining the current districts of Newcastle-under-Lyme, Stoke-on-Trent, and Staffordshire Moorlands, to form a new [North Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Staffordshire_\(district\)) unitary authority, and the southern area combining East Staffordshire, Lichfield, South Staffordshire, Stafford, Stone, and Tamworth to form a new [Southern and Mid Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_and_Mid_Staffordshire) unitary authority.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] Elections to the new unitary councils will take place in May 2027.[^cite_note-33]

### Church of England

The only cathedral in the county is Lichfield Cathedral in the city of Lichfield. The [Diocese of Lichfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Lichfield) covers the whole county with the exception of [Stapenhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stapenhill) and [Amington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amington), the north of the nearby county of [Shropshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire) and the [Black Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Country) area of the [West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_\(county\)). The county is covered by the archdeaconries of [Stoke-upon-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke-upon-Trent) and [Lichfield](159948.md). The current [Bishop of Lichfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Lichfield) is [Michael Ipgrave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Ipgrave) and the current [Bishop of Stafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Stafford) [Geoff Annas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Annas). There are 298 [Church of England](5955.md) churches in the county.

### Roman Catholic Church

Staffordshire is part of the [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Birmingham). The current archbishop is [Bernard Longley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Longley).

### Methodism

[Primitive Methodism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primitive_Methodism) was founded in Staffordshire by [Hugh Bourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Bourne), a native of Stoke-on-Trent, at a public gathering in the village of [Mow Cop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mow_Cop). He originally followed the [Wesleyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wesley) form of [Methodism](20119.md) but in 1801 he reformed the Methodist service by conducting it outside. By 1811 with his brother he founded the first chapel in the [Tunstall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunstall,_Staffordshire) area of Stoke-on-Trent.[^cite_note-36] Since 1932 the Primitive and Wesleyan movements have been a single organisation, the [Methodist Church of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_Church_of_Great_Britain).[^cite_note-37] The Methodist Churches of Staffordshire fall within three Districts: Birmingham District, Wolverhampton and Shrewsbury District and Chester & Stoke-on-Trent District.[^cite_note-38]

### Judaism

The most popular synagogue in the county is on London Road in [Newcastle-Under-Lyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle-Under-Lyme), which opened in 2006 and replaced the former Birch Terrace synagogue in Hanley.[^cite_note-39] According to the 2001 census there were 407 Jews in the non-metropolitan county of Staffordshire,[^cite_note-40] and 83 in Stoke-on-Trent.[^cite_note-41]

### Islam

There are 15 [mosques](19894.md) in Stoke-on-Trent, 5 in [Burton-upon-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton-upon-Trent) and 1 in both Stafford and Lichfield.[^cite_note-42] As of 2019 a new [mosque](https://citycentralmosque.org/) has finished construction in the Hanley area of Stoke-on-Trent and is the first purpose-built mosque in the area. At the 2001 census there were 7,658 Muslims in Stoke-on-Trent and 6,081 in the rest of Staffordshire, with a total of 13,739 making up 1.3% of the population. 62.9% (3823) of the Muslims in the rest of Staffordshire are from the town of Burton-upon-Trent.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

## Transport

### Canals

Staffordshire has an extensive network of canals including the [Birmingham and Fazeley Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_and_Fazeley_Canal), [Caldon Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldon_Canal), [Coventry Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coventry_Canal), [Shropshire Union Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shropshire_Union_Canal), [Staffordshire and Worcestershire Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_and_Worcestershire_Canal) and [Trent and Mersey Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent_and_Mersey_Canal).

### Railways

Further information: [Rail transport in Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Staffordshire)



[Stone railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_railway_station) in [Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone,_Staffordshire)

Staffordshire has several railways that pass through and serve settlements within the county. The most important of these is the [West Coast Main Line](182538.md), which facilitates through services between London and Scotland, Manchester and Liverpool.&#x20;

Located on the West Coast Main Line, [Stafford railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford_railway_station) is at a junction with the line to [Birmingham New Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_New_Street_railway_station), a major hub, and is predominantly served by [London Northwestern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Midlands_Trains). Rugeley Trent Valley, Lichfield Trent Valley and Penkridge are also on mainline railway routes.

[Stoke-on-Trent railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke-on-Trent_railway_station) is the busiest station in Staffordshire[^cite_note-51] and is served by long-distance CrossCountry and Avanti West Coast trains to Manchester, Birmingham, London, Oxford, Reading, Bristol, Southampton and Bournemouth. This station is also the terminus of the North Staffordshire line to Derby and Nottingham via Uttoxeter, which narrowly avoided closure in the 1960s. It also offers local commuter services to Manchester with Northern Railways.

Stone railway station reopened in 2008.

### Roads

The county has relatively good links to the national roads network. Several major roads intersect the county, making it a popular location for commuters working in Birmingham. The [M42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M42_motorway) junction 10 is in Tamworth and the motorway heads southwest towards Birmingham. The [M6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M6_motorway) runs north–south through the county, which contains junctions 10A–16. The [M6 Toll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M6_Toll), the UK's first [toll motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_road), runs through the county with junctions in Weeford near Lichfield, Cannock and joins the M6 south of Stafford.

The [A5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A5_road_\(Great_Britain\)) and [A34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A34_road_\(Great_Britain\)) run through the county. The former has been significantly widened to a dual carriageway at several sections, although much of it remains single carriageway.

### Air

There are currently no airports with scheduled flights in the county, with the nearest ones being [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Airport), [East Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands_Airport) and [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Airport). Depending on the location, there is, however, [Wolverhampton Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton_Airport) in [Bobbington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobbington) and [Tatenhill Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatenhill_Airfield) near Burton-upon-Trent, both of which are small airports catering for general aviation.

### Bus

Services within the county are chiefly provided by [Arriva Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arriva_Midlands), [D\&G Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D\&G_Bus) and [First Potteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Potteries). National Express coaches serve towns and cities on a daily basis.

## Media

### Newspapers

Daily Newspapers in Staffordshire are *[The Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sentinel_\(Staffordshire\))*, covering Stoke-on-Trent, Newcastle-under-Lyme and the [Staffordshire Moorlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_Moorlands), *[Burton Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_Mail)* which covers the town of Burton-upon-Trent and the *[Express & Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Express_&_Star)* which has several editions covering Tamworth, Lichfield, Cannock Chase and [Stafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford).

### Radio

The local BBC radio stations covering Staffordshire are [BBC Radio Stoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Stoke) covering Mid and North Staffordshire, [BBC Radio WM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_WM) covering the south of the county and [BBC Radio Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Derby) covering [East Staffordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Staffordshire). The local commercial radio stations are [Hits Radio Staffordshire & Cheshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hits_Radio_Staffordshire_&_Cheshire) and [Greatest Hits Radio Staffordshire & Cheshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_Hits_Radio_Staffordshire_&_Cheshire) which cover North and Mid Staffordshire, and [Capital Mid-Counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Mid-Counties), which covers [Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton-upon-Trent), [Lichfield](159948.md) and [Tamworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamworth,_Staffordshire). Further stations which cover parts of Staffordshire include [Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_West_Midlands), [Smooth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_West_Midlands), and [Greatest Hits Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_Hits_Radio_West_Midlands) which cover the southern parts of the county. [Hits Radio Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hits_Radio_Birmingham) covers Lichfield and Tamworth, and [Hits Radio Black Country & Shropshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hits_Radio_Black_Country_&_Shropshire) covers the [Cannock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannock) area.

[United Christian Broadcasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Christian_Broadcasters), which has facilities in [Burslem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burslem) and Hanchurch, has been involved in radio broadcasting since 1987. Today it is broadcast nationally in the UK through DAB digital radio.

Community radio

Staffordshire is served by a number of [community radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_radio) stations. In North Staffordshire, there are four community radio stations – [Moorlands Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorlands_Radio) in [Leek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leek,_Staffordshire), [6 Towns Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6_Towns_Radio), based in [Burslem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burslem), The Hitmix, based in [Newcastle-under-Lyme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle-under-Lyme) and [Cross Rhythms City Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Rhythms_City_Radio) based in [Hanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanley,_Staffordshire)

In [Stafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford) there is [Vibe 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibe_1), which broadcasts to the town on 107.3 FM.

In the [Cannock Chase District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannock_Chase_District), there is [Cannock Chase Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannock_Chase_Radio), which broadcasts on 89.6, 89.8 and 94.0 FM, and in [Tamworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamworth,_Staffordshire), there is [Radio Tamworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Tamworth), which broadcasts on 106.8 FM.

### Television

Staffordshire is served by the [ITV Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Central) and [BBC West Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_West_Midlands) television regions, both of which have their studios in [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham). The far north of the county, around [Biddulph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biddulph), is served by [ITV Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Granada) and [BBC North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_North_West) from [MediaCityUK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MediaCityUK) in [Salford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Salford).
