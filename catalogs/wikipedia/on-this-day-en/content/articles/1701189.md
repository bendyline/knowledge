**SpiceJet** is an Indian [low-cost airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-cost_airline) headquartered in [Gurgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurgaon), Haryana. As of May 2025, it is the fourth largest airline in India by number of domestic passengers carried, with a market share of 4%.[^cite_note-\:0-5]

Established as [air taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_taxi) provider [ModiLuft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ModiLuft) in 1994, the company was acquired by Indian entrepreneur [Ajay Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajay_Singh_\(entrepreneur\)) in 2004 and renamed SpiceJet. The airline operated its first flight in May 2005. Indian media baron [Kalanidhi Maran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalanidhi_Maran) acquired a controlling stake in SpiceJet in June 2010 through [Sun Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Group) which was sold back to Ajay Singh in January 2015. The airline operates a fleet of [Boeing 737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) and [Bombardier Dash 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Dash_8) aircraft.

## History

### 1984–1996: ModiLuft era

Main article: [ModiLuft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ModiLuft)

The origins of SpiceJet can be tracked back to March 1984 when the company was established by Indian industrialist Satish Kumar Modi to provide private [air taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_taxi) services.[^cite_note-History-6] On 17 February 1993, the company was named as MG Express and entered into technical partnership with the German flag carrier [Lufthansa](97733.md). The airline provided passenger and cargo services under the name of *ModiLuft* before ceasing operations in 1996.[^cite_note-History-6]

### 2005–2013: Inception and expansion

In 2004, the company was acquired by [Ajay Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajay_Singh_\(entrepreneur\)) and the airline planned to restart operations as SpiceJet following the [low-cost](428422.md) model.[^cite_note-History-6] SpiceJet leased two [Boeing 737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-800) aircraft in 2005 and planned to order ten new aircraft for expansion.[^cite_note-lease-7] SpiceJet opened bookings on 18 May 2005 and the first flight was operated between [Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi_International_Airport) and [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhatrapati_Shivaji_International_Airport) on 24 May 2005.[^cite_note-8] By July 2008, it was India's third-largest low-cost carrier in terms of market share after [Air Deccan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Deccan) and [IndiGo](2085799.md).[^cite_note-9] Indian media baron [Kalanithi Maran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalanithi_Maran) acquired 37.7% stake in SpiceJet in June 2010 through [Sun Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Group).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-hindu-11] The airline ordered 30 Boeing 737-8 aircraft worth US\$2.7 billion July 2010 and a further 15 [Bombardier Q4 Dash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Dash_8) short-haul aircraft worth US\$446 million in December 2010.[^cite_note-12]

In 2012, SpiceJet suffered a loss of over [₹](490783.md)39 [crore](121420.md) (US\$4.0 million) owing to increase in global crude prices.[^cite_note-13] On 9 January 2012, the [Directorate General of Civil Aviation](23160430.md), reported that several airlines in India, including SpiceJet, have not maintained crucial data for the [flight operations quality assurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_operations_quality_assurance).[^cite_note-14] The [Bombay Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Stock_Exchange) announced that ever since June 2011, SpiceJet had been suffering losses.[^cite_note-15] In 2012, Kalanidhi Maran increased his stake in the airline by investing ₹100 crore (US\$10 million) in the airline.[^cite_note-16] The airline returned to profits at the end of the same year.[^cite_note-17] SpiceJet entered into an inter airline pact with [Tigerair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigerair) on 16 December 2013 which was scrapped in January 2015.[^cite_note-18]

### 2014–present: Downturn and recovery

In January 2015, the [Sun Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Group) sold its entire shareholding and transferred control to Ajay Singh.[^cite_note-19] In September 2017, the airline placed an order for 25 [Q400 turboprop aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Dash_8#Series_400).[^cite_note-20] In 2019, SpiceJet took over 30 aircraft that were grounded by [Jet Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Airways).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

The airline posted net losses of US\$34.6 million during the fiscal quarter ended 31 March 2021, as revenue fell by 28% annually to \$294.8 million. The airline planned to raise funds to the tune of \$337.2 million to ensure its long term sustainability.[^cite_note-23] In May 2022, [Directorate General of Civil Aviation](23160430.md) started a safety investigation into the company's aircraft following a series of incidents involving malfunctioning plane equipment.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] In July 2022, DGCA imposed restrictions on the number of flights to be capped at 50% of the existent flights with strict monitoring.[^cite_note-26] As of June 2024, it is the sixth largest airline in India by number of domestic passengers carried, with a market share of 4%.[^cite_note-\:0-5]

On 12 September 2024, SpiceJet announced that its [shareholders](146081.md) approved a fund raising initiative of [₹](490783.md)3,000 [crore](121420.md) (equivalent to ₹32 billion or US\$330 million in 2026) through Qualified Institutional Placement (QIP) shares in order to pay pending dues amid financial challenges, legal battles and grounding of aircraft.[^cite_note-\:2-27] The airline's downturn and requirement of funds was due to two consecutive events, [Boeing 737 MAX groundings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_MAX_groundings) and the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). However, the funds have been realised and the airline plans to return to the market with full capacity and fleet expansion. The airline will clear a debt of [₹](490783.md)750 [crore](121420.md) (equivalent to ₹799 crore or US\$83 million in 2026) to the lessors with the newly acquired funds.[^cite_note-\:4-28] On 5 October, it was reported that the airline cleared the four-month due salaries and [GST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goods_and_Services_Tax_\(India\)) and deposited 10-months dues of Provident Fund (PF).[^cite_note-29]

As of October 2024, SpiceJet is also scheduled to receive [₹](490783.md)736 [crore](121420.md) (equivalent to ₹784 crore or US\$81 million in 2026) from a previous funding round.[^cite_note-\:5-30]

### Ownership and structure

The company is publicly traded under [NSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Stock_Exchange_of_India): SPICEJET, with a market capitalization of about [₹](490783.md)4,255 crore (US\$440 million) as of January 2024.[^cite_note-44] On 30 March 2020, [HDFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDFC_Bank) bought 3.4 crore shares of SpiceJet from the open market constituting 5.45% stake.[^cite_note-45]

### Headquarters

SpiceJet is headquartered in Gurgaon, India.[^cite_note-SpiceJetHQ-2] Ajay Singh serves as the managing director of the airline since January 2015.[^cite_note-46] The airline's logo consists of 15 dots arranged in three rows of five each in the order of their reducing sizes on a red background. In June 2015, the airline unveiled its current logo with a new tagline *Red. Hot. Spicy.*[^cite_note-47] SpiceJet names all its aircraft with the name of an [Indian spice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spices).[^cite_note-48]

### Subsidiaries

Launched in September 2018, **SpiceXpress** is the air cargo division of the airline.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] The airline took delivery of its first [737-800 Boeing Converted Freighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_Next_Generation#737-800BCF) (BCF) in September 2019, becoming the first South Asian carrier to induct the converted freighter into its fleet.[^cite_note-51]

**Spice Shuttle** was a fully owned subsidiary of SpiceJet which operated sea planes and other shuttle airplane services.[^cite_note-52] The airline initiated its service on 31 October 2020 with a fleet consisting of a 15-seater [DHC-6-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DHC-6-300). The flight operated between [Sabarmati Riverfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabarmati_Riverfront), [Ahmedabad](206337.md) and [Statue of Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Unity), [Kevadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevadia). This route was inaugurated under the [UDAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UDAN) scheme. SpiceJet had conducted seaplane trials in India in phase I ([Nagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagpur), [Guwahati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guwahati)) and phase II ([Girgaon Chowpatty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girgaon_Chowpatty), [Mumbai](19189.md)) since 2017.[^cite_note-\:3-53] However, it terminated services in April 2021.[^cite_note-54]

## Destinations

Main article: [List of SpiceJet destinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_SpiceJet_destinations)

SpiceJet operates hubs at [Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi_International_Airport) and [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi_International_Airport), which is the primary base for its fleet of Bombardier Q400 aircraft.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] After completing five years of flying, SpiceJet was allowed to commence international flights by [Directorate General of Civil Aviation](23160430.md) on 7 September 2010. SpiceJet launched flights from Delhi to [Kathmandu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_International_Airport) and [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai_International_Airport) to [Colombo](2870845.md) and the first international flight took off on 7 October 2010 from Delhi.[^cite_note-57]

### Fleet development

SpiceJet placed its first firm order for 20 Next-Generation Boeing 737-800s in March 2005, with deliveries scheduled up to 2010.[^cite_note-lease-7] In November 2010, the airline ordered 30 Boeing 737-800s.[^cite_note-65] On 9 December 2010, [Bombardier Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Aerospace) announced that SpiceJet had placed a firm order for fifteen Q400 NextGen turboprop airliners with [options](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_\(aircraft_purchasing\)) for another fifteen. SpiceJet used its fleet of Q400s for short-haul operations.[^cite_note-bombaspice-66]

In March 2014, the airline signed a US\$4.4 billion deal with Boeing for the procurement of 42 [737 MAX 8](32924090.md) aircraft.[^cite_note-67] In 2015, SpiceJet was in talks with both Boeing and Airbus for a possible order of more than 100 single aisle aircraft, either [Airbus A320neo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A320neo_family) or the Boeing 737 MAX with the same being confirmed by managing director, Ajay Singh, in a conference in Dubai.[^cite_note-68]

In January 2017, the airline placed a firm order for 100 737 MAX 8 aircraft, and revealed itself as the airline behind the 13 MAX 8 aircraft previously attributed to an unidentified customer, taking its total order to 155 MAX 8 aircraft with purchase rights for 50 additional MAX 8 and wide-body aircraft.[^cite_note-69] The carrier planned to grow its operational fleet to 200 airplanes and expand regionally with the new 737 MAX family of airplanes.[^cite_note-70] In June 2017, the airline signed a letter of intent with [Bombardier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Inc.) to purchase up to 50 Q400 aircraft for catering to growth in passenger traffic arising from its participation in the [Indian government](553883.md)'s [UDAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UDAN) regional connectivity scheme.[^cite_note-71]

In 2019, SpiceJet announced plans to induct 16 [ Boeing 737-800 NG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) to fulfil local and international demands due to the grounding of the [Boeing 737 MAX](32924090.md) fleet.[^cite_note-72] In May 2020, when [Jet Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Airways) ceased operations, the airline placed orders for six additional Boeing 737-800 NG and five Bombardier Q400 to cover the shortage.[^cite_note-73] In June 2023, SpiceJet announced plans to induct 10 Boeing 737 aircraft of which five would be 737 MAX aircraft to cater to the surge in passenger demand.[^cite_note-B737-74]

In 2019, SpiceJet had a fleet size of 74 aircraft and had progressively inducted more aircraft. In 2020, the airline had a fleet of 74 Boeing 737, 26 Bombardier Q-400s, 11 B737 MAX & Bombardier Q-400 freighters.[^cite_note-\:3-53]

As of June 2024, of the 64 airlines of the fleet, 36 were grounded and of the 28 airworthy aircraft, fewer was being deployed for operations.[^cite_note-\:5-30]

As of September 2024, due to financial constraints and legal reasons, the airline operates 20 aircraft while the others remain grounded.[^cite_note-\:2-27] The airline revealed that it has around 30 aircraft grounded which is to return to the air soon and it also plans to have a 100-aircraft fleet by 2026. The airline revealed that it has enough slots, international traffic rights, pilots and crew, as well as the maintenance infrastructure for such an expansion. The airline could not receive new aircraft post-COVID like other [Indian airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_airlines). By March 2025, the airline will have a fleet of 40 and then add another 40 in the next year. It also plans for short term wet leases for extended operations during high-demand seasons. In fact, SpiceJet will induct 8 aircraft on lease in 2024-end.[^cite_note-\:4-28]

As per reports on 8 October 2024, SpiceJet will induct seven aircraft on lease by 15 November. The deal for the lease has been signed while the first two aircraft have arrived in India and shall be inducted soon. The airline also said that three grounded aircraft will restart operations in November end and a total of seven will be ungrounded by year-end. The entire fleet is expected to be ungrounded within 18–24 months and [₹](490783.md)400 [crore](121420.md) (equivalent to ₹426 crore or US\$44 million in 2026) (from the qualified institutional placement (QIP) secured recently) is to be invested for spares, maintenance and engineering of the grounded fleet. The airline expects to have a 100-aircraft fleet by 2026.[^cite_note-\:5-30][^cite_note-75]

As of 31 December 2024, the airline has 34 Boeing 737 jets (including 13 B737 MAX), 24 Q400 and 6 wet-leased aircraft of which 28 are operational. In 2025, the carrier plans to unground 30 aircraft and negotiate to restart deliveries of the 200 B737 MAX aircraft ordered in 2017. Spicejet also signed an agreement with US-based MRO company, StandardAero Inc. to support the ungrounding of the 737 MAX fleet, 3 of which are to be operationalised by April 2025.[^cite_note-76]

As of 10 January 2025, the airline had a 62-aircraft fleet of which, 20 Boeing 737 (including leased aircraft), 2 Airbus A320 (on lease) and 6 DHC-8 Dash 8 were operational.[^cite_note-\:6-77]

By April 2025, all ten of the aircraft planned for induction since October 2024 was completed and this included seven aircraft on lease. The carrier planned to double its fleet by the next year.[^cite_note-\:6-77] In September 2025, the airline signed an agreement to lease eight additional aircraft besides the two earlier agreements to lease ten aircraft. The ten to-be-leased aircraft are expected to be inducted from October onwards.[^cite_note-78] On 10 and 11 October, the airline inducted an [Airbus A340](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A340) and two Boeing 737 aircraft of the total 20 aircraft that will be acquired on damp lease for until November. By mid-December, four additional grounded jets will also be revived.[^cite_note-79]

### Services

SpiceJet has moved away from the typical low-cost carrier service model of [economy class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_class)-only seating.[^cite_note-FAQ-80] The airline offers premium services under the name *SpiceMax*, whereby passengers can obtain additional benefits including pre-assigned seats with extra legroom; meals on board; priority check-in and boarding; and priority baggage handling; at a higher fare.[^cite_note-81] Otherwise SpiceJet does not provide complimentary meals in any of its flights. It does sell full in-flight meals on some flights.[^cite_note-FAQ-80] SpiceJet operates its [frequent-flyer programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequent-flyer_programme) but does not provide any [in-flight entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-flight_entertainment) options.[^cite_note-FAQ-80] SpiceJet has partnered with Tripfactory and [EaseMyTrip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EaseMyTrip) for selling holiday packages on its platform.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]
