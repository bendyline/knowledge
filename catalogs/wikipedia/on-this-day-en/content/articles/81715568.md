[Carlos Alcaraz](63121147.md) defeated [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) in the final, 2–6, 6–2, 6–3, 7–5 to win the men's singles tennis title at the [2026 Australian Open](81711108.md).[^cite_note-1] It was his first [Australian Open](97851.md) title and seventh [major](197638.md#Tournaments) title overall, becoming the ninth player and youngest man to complete the [career Grand Slam](2092499.md#Career_Grand_Slam) in singles (at 22 years, 272 days old). He also became the youngest man in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_tennis#Open_Era) to reach the singles final of all four majors at 22 years, 270 days  old, and became the youngest man to win a seventh major title.[^cite_note-2] The final was a rematch of the [previous year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles)'s quarterfinal match, won by Djokovic. With the win, Alcaraz ended Djokovic's unbeaten 10–0 record in Australian Open finals, making Djokovic the fourth man in the Open Era to finish runner-up at all four majors (after [Ivan Lendl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Lendl), [Roger Federer](262376.md), and [Andy Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Murray)).[^cite_note-3]

[Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) was the two-time defending champion,[^cite_note-4] but lost in the semifinals to Djokovic in a rematch of their semifinal match [two years prior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles). Sinner's loss ended his streak of five consecutive major finals, dating back to the [2024 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-5]

At 38 years and 255 days old, Djokovic became the oldest man in history to reach the Australian Open final, and the oldest major finalist overall since [Ken Rosewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Rosewall) at the [1974 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-6] By making his 81st career major appearance, he equaled Federer and [Feliciano López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feliciano_L%C3%B3pez)'s joint [all-time record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Grand_Slam_and_related_tennis_records#Participation) for the most total major main draw appearances, and equaled Federer's record 21 Australian Open main draw appearances. With his quarterfinal win, he recorded his 103rd Australian Open match win, breaking Federer's record for most match wins at the Australian Open. Djokovic was also the first man to record over 100 main-draw match wins at three different majors, having also done so at the [French Open](147724.md) and [Wimbledon Championships](91195.md).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

At 5 hours and 27 minutes long, the semifinal between Alcaraz and [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md) was the longest Australian Open semifinal (surpassing the [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) semifinal between [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) and [Fernando Verdasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Verdasco)), and third-longest overall match in tournament history.[^cite_note-10] [Valentin Vacherot](68016649.md) became the first Monégasque singles player to reach the third round of a major in the Open Era.[^cite_note-11] [Learner Tien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learner_Tien) was the youngest player to reach the Australian Open quarterfinals since [Nick Kyrgios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Kyrgios) in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-12]

For the first time in the Open Era, and first time since the 32-seed system was introduced at the [2001 Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), all the players who reached the fourth round at a major were seeded.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] For the first time in the Open Era, the top six seeds reached the quarterfinals in both the men's and [women's](81715708.md) singles competitions.[^cite_note-15] This was also the first time since the [2019 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) that the top four men's seeds all reached the semifinals of a major, and the first such occurrence at the Australian Open since [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-16]

The tournament marked the final Australian Open appearance of [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) champion, three-time major champion, and former world No. 3 [Stan Wawrinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Wawrinka); he lost in the third round to [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz).[^cite_note-17] At 40 years and 296 days old, Wawrinka was the oldest player to reach the third round of a major since Rosewall at the [1978 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles). His second-round win marked his 58th five-set match, tied with Lendl for the most by any player in the Open Era.[^cite_note-18]
