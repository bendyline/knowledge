**Ursula Gertrud von der Leyen**[^cite_note-1] ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) **Albrecht**; born 8 October 1958) is a German politician and physician who has served as [President of the European Commission](24468.md) since 2019. She served in the [German federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Germany) between 2005 and 2019, holding positions in [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel)'s cabinet, most recently as [Federal Minister for Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Minister_for_Defence_\(Germany\)). She is a member of the centre-right [Christian Democratic Union](60584.md) (CDU) and its affiliated [European political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_political_party), the [European People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_People's_Party) (EPP). On 7 March 2024, the EPP elected her as its [Spitzenkandidat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spitzenkandidat) to lead the campaign for the 2024 [European Parliament](9581.md) elections. She was re-elected to head the Commission in July 2024.[^cite_note-EU_reelect-2]

Von der Leyen was born and raised in [Brussels](3708.md), Belgium, to German parents. Her father, [Ernst Albrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Albrecht_\(politician,_born_1930\)), was one of the first [European civil servants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_civil_servant). She was brought up bilingually in German and French, and moved to Germany in 1971 when her father became involved in German politics. She graduated from the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) in 1978, and in 1987, she acquired her medical licence from Hanover Medical School. After marrying fellow physician [Heiko von der Leyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiko_von_der_Leyen), she lived for four years in the United States with her family in the 1990s. After returning to Germany she became involved in local politics in the [Hanover region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover_\(region\)) in the late 1990s, and she served as a cabinet minister in the state government of [Lower Saxony](18435.md) from 2003 to 2005.

In 2005, von der Leyen joined the federal cabinet, first as [Minister for Family Affairs and Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_of_Family_Affairs,_Senior_Citizens,_Women_and_Youth) from 2005 to 2009, then taking on the role of [Minister for Labour and Social Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_of_Labour_and_Social_Affairs_\(Germany\)) from 2009 to 2013, and finally serving as Minister for Defence from 2013 to 2019, the first woman to do so.[^cite_note-businessweek.com-3] When she left office, she was the only minister to have served continuously in Merkel's cabinet since Merkel became chancellor. She served as a deputy leader of the CDU from 2010 to 2019, and was regarded as a leading contender to succeed Merkel as the [chancellor of Germany](20890626.md) and as the favourite to become the [secretary general of NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_general_of_NATO) after [Jens Stoltenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Stoltenberg). British defence secretary [Michael Fallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fallon) described her in 2019 as "a star presence" in the NATO community and "the doyenne of NATO ministers for over five years".[^cite_note-telegraph2019-4] In 2023, she was again regarded as the favourite to take the role.[^cite_note-5]

On 2 July 2019, von der Leyen was proposed by the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) as the candidate for president of the [European Commission](9974.md).[^cite_note-BBC-2019-6][^cite_note-POLITICO-2019-7] She was then elected by the European Parliament on 16 July;[^cite_note-BBCvote-8][^cite_note-10] she took office on 1 December, becoming the first woman to hold the office. Her tenure has been marked by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Europe), the [European Green Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Deal), the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md), as well as [American threats to annex Greenland](82044223.md). In November 2022 she announced that her commission would work to establish an [International Criminal Tribunal for the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Tribunal_for_the_Russian_Federation).[^cite_note-11] The EU–US [Agreement on Reciprocal, Fair, and Balanced Trade](80925459.md), announced in August 2025, exposed her to criticism from France and Germany.

On 18 July 2024, von der Leyen was re-elected as Commission President by the European Parliament with an absolute majority of 401 [members of the European Parliament](258909.md) out of 720; strengthened by around 30 votes compared to her election in 2019.[^cite_note-12] She was named the [most powerful woman in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_list_of_the_World's_100_Most_Powerful_Women) by *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* in 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025.[^cite_note-Forbes2022-13][^cite_note-Forbes2023-14][^cite_note-\:0-15]

## Family and early life

Main article: [Albrecht family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_family)



Von der Leyen [family arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_der_Leyen_\(family_from_Krefeld\))

Von der Leyen was born in 1958 in [Ixelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ixelles), Brussels, Belgium, where she lived until she was 13 years old. In 1971, she relocated to [Sehnde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sehnde) in the [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover_\(district\)) region after her father had become CEO of the food company [Bahlsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahlsen) and involved in state politics in Lower Saxony.[^cite_note-16]

In 1986, she married physician [Heiko von der Leyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiko_von_der_Leyen), a member of the [von der Leyen family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_der_Leyen_\(family_from_Krefeld\)), which belonged to the [German nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nobility) and made its fortune as silk merchants. They have seven children, born between 1987 and 1999.[^cite_note-17] Von der Leyen is [Lutheran](23371382.md).[^cite_note-irishtimes-18][^cite_note-K+L-19]

She lives with her family on a farm in [Burgdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgdorf,_Hanover) near Hanover where they keep horses.[^cite_note-20] She is a keen [equestrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrianism) and has been involved in competitive horseriding.[^cite_note-21]

## Education and professional career



*Portrait of Mrs. [Robert Gilmor Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gilmor_Jr.)* — Ursula von der Leyen's great-grandmother of the influential South Carolinian [planter class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planter_class) [family Ladson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladson_family) — painting by [Thomas Sully](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Sully), c. 1823. Von der Leyen lived in London in the late 1970s under the alias Rose Ladson

Von der Leyen moved to the [Hanover Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover_Region) in 1971 when her father entered politics to become [minister-president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_President_of_Lower_Saxony) of the state of [Lower Saxony](18435.md) in 1976. In 1977, she started studying economics at the [University of Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_G%C3%B6ttingen). At the height of the fear of [communist terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_terrorism) in West Germany, she fled to London in 1978 after her family was told that the [Red Army Faction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army_Faction) (RAF) was planning to [kidnap](169376.md) her due to her being the daughter of a prominent politician. She spent more than a year in hiding in London, where she lived with protection from [Scotland Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_Yard) under the name Rose Ladson to avoid detection and enrolled at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-Mendick-2019-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] A German diminutive of Rose, Röschen, had been her nickname since childhood,[^cite_note-26] while [Ladson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladson_family) was the name of her American great-grandmother's family, originally from [Northamptonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northamptonshire), who immigrated to [Charleston, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston,_South_Carolina) in 1679, became wealthy landowners and slave traders of the [Planter class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planter_class), related to families of governors of the [Province of Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Carolina).[^cite_note-27] She said that she "lived more than she studied",[^cite_note-28] and that London was "the epitome of modernity: freedom, the joy of life, trying everything" which "gave me an inner freedom that I have kept till today".[^cite_note-Mendick-2019-23] She returned to Germany in 1979 but lived with a security detail at her side for several years.[^cite_note-29]

In 1980, after three years of studying economics, she switched to medicine with a specialisation in gynaecology, at the [Hannover Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannover_Medical_School), where she obtained her medical licence as a physician.[^cite_note-KASBio-30][^cite_note-LSE2019-31]

From 1988 to 1992, she worked as an assistant physician at the Women's Clinic of the Hannover Medical School. Upon completing her doctoral studies, she defended the thesis[^cite_note-32] and graduated as a [Doctor of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine) in 1991. Following the birth of twins, she was a housewife in [Stanford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford,_California), California, from 1992 to 1996, while her husband was a faculty member of [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), returning to Germany in 1996.[^cite_note-33]

From 1998 to 2002, she taught at the Department of Epidemiology, Social Medicine and Health System Research at the [Hanover Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover_Medical_School).[^cite_note-34] In 2001, she earned a [Master of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Public_Health) degree at the institution.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-cv_von_der_leyen-37]

### Plagiarism accusations

In 2015, researchers collaborating at the [VroniPlag Wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VroniPlag_Wiki) reviewed von der Leyen's 1991 [doctoral dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis) and alleged that 43.5% of the thesis pages contained [plagiarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plagiarism) and in 23 cases citations were used that did not verify claims for which they were given.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] Multiple notable German academics such as [Gerhard Dannemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Dannemann?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Volker Rieble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volker_Rieble?action=edit\&redlink=1) publicly accused von der Leyen of intended plagiarism.[^cite_note-40] The Hannover Medical School conducted an investigation and concluded in March 2016 that while the thesis contains plagiarism, no intention to deceive could be proven.[^cite_note-Spiegel_Online-2016-41][^cite_note-Greiner-2016-42]

The university decided not to revoke von der Leyen's medical degree.[^cite_note-Spiegel_Online-2016-41] Critics questioned the independence of the commission that reviewed the thesis as von der Leyen personally knew its director from joint work for an alumni association.[^cite_note-Greiner-2016-42] Various media outlets also criticised the decision for being non-transparent, not according to established rules, and failing to secure high academic standards.[^cite_note-Greiner-2016-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

## Early political career

Ursula von der Leyen joined the [CDU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Union_\(Germany\)) in 1990, and became active in local politics in Lower Saxony in 1996, shortly after she had returned to Germany after living in California. She was a member of the committee on social policy of CDU in Lower Saxony from 1996, and also became active in the association of medical doctors in the CDU party.[^cite_note-45]

### In the Lower Saxony Landtag, 2003–2005

Ursula von der Leyen was elected to the [Parliament of Lower Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag_of_Lower_Saxony) in the [2003 state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Lower_Saxony_state_election) for Lehrte, the same constituency then-Chancellor Gerhard Schröder represented from 1986 to 1998. From 2003 to 2005 she was a minister in the state government of Lower Saxony, serving in the cabinet of [Christian Wulff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Wulff), with responsibility for social affairs, women, family, and health.[^cite_note-46]

In 2003, von der Leyen was part of a group assigned by the then-opposition leader and CDU chairwoman [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel) to draft alternative proposals for social welfare reform in response to Chancellor Gerhard Schröder's "[Agenda 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agenda_2010)". The so-called Herzog Commission, named after its chairman, the former German president [Roman Herzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Herzog), recommended a comprehensive package of reform proposals including, among other things, decoupling health and nursing care premiums from people's earnings and levying a monthly lump sum across the board instead.[^cite_note-47]



2005 campaign poster featuring von der Leyen

Ahead of the [2005 federal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_German_federal_election), Angela Merkel chose Ursula von der Leyen to cover the family and social security portfolio in her [shadow cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_cabinet).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] In the negotiations to form a government following the election, von der Leyen led the [CDU/CSU](10796483.md) delegation in the working group on families; her co-chair from the [SPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPD) was [Renate Schmidt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Schmidt).[^cite_note-50]

### In the Bundestag, 2005–2019

#### Minister of Family Affairs and Youth, 2005–2009

In 2005, Ursula von der Leyen was appointed [Federal Minister of Family Affairs and Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_for_Family_Affairs,_Senior_Citizens,_Women_and_Youth) in the cabinet of [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel). On the 60th anniversary of the founding of Israel, von der Leyen participated in the first joint cabinet meeting of the governments of Germany and Israel in Jerusalem in March 2008.[^cite_note-51]

#### Minister of Labour and Social Affairs, 2009–2013

At the [federal election of 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_German_federal_election), von der Leyen was elected to the [Bundestag](3768.md), Germany's Parliament, representing the 42nd electoral district of [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover), alongside [Edelgard Bulmahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edelgard_Bulmahn) of the Social Democrats. In the negotiations to form a [coalition government](6036.md) following the elections, she led the CDU/CSU delegation in the working group on health policy; her co-chair from the [FDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party_\(Germany\)) was [Philipp Rösler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_R%C3%B6sler). She was reappointed as family minister,[^cite_note-52] but on 30 November 2009 succeeded [Franz Josef Jung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Josef_Jung) as Federal Minister of Labour and Social Affairs.[^cite_note-53]



Ursula von der Leyen at a horse show in [Hagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagen,_Osnabr%C3%BCck) in [Osnabrück](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osnabr%C3%BCck), Germany, in 2013

During her time in office, von der Leyen cultivated the image of being the social conscience of the CDU[^cite_note-A_guide_to_future_chancellors-54] and helped Merkel to move the CDU into the political centre-ground.[^cite_note-ft.com-55] In speaking out for increasing the number of childcare nurseries, for the introduction of a women's quota for listed companies' main boards, for [gay marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_marriage) and a nationwide [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage), von der Leyen made enemies among the more traditionalist party members and won admirers on the left.[^cite_note-theguardian.com-56]

Von der Leyen also lobbied for lowering the barriers to immigration for some foreign workers, to fight shortages of skilled workers in Germany.[^cite_note-57] In 2013, she concluded an agreement with the [Government of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Philippines) that was aimed at helping Filipino health care professionals to gain employment in Germany. A vital provision of the agreement is that the Filipino workers are to be employed on the same terms and conditions as their German counterparts.[^cite_note-58]

Von der Leyen was initially considered the front-runner to be nominated by the ruling [CDU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Union_\(Germany\))/[CSU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Social_Union_of_Bavaria) parties for election as [President of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Germany) in the [2010 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_German_presidential_election),[^cite_note-nachrp-59] but [Christian Wulff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Wulff) was eventually chosen as the parties' candidate. The news media later reported that Wulff's nomination came as a blow to Merkel, whose choice of von der Leyen had been blocked by the two parties' more conservative state premiers.[^cite_note-60]

In November 2010, von der Leyen was elected (with 85% of the votes) as one of four deputies of CDU chairwoman Merkel, serving alongside [Volker Bouffier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volker_Bouffier), [Norbert Röttgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norbert_R%C3%B6ttgen) and [Annette Schavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annette_Schavan). Later that month, she told the *[Bild am Sonntag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bild_am_Sonntag)* newspaper that the CDU should consider establishing a formal voting process for choosing future candidates for Chancellor.[^cite_note-61] In 2012, she was re-elected (with 69% of the votes) as one of Merkel's deputies as CDU chairwoman, this time serving alongside Bouffier, [Julia Klöckner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Kl%C3%B6ckner), [Armin Laschet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armin_Laschet) and [Thomas Strobl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Strobl).[^cite_note-62]

In the negotiations to form a government following the [2013 federal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_German_federal_election), von der Leyen led the CDU/CSU delegation in the labour policy working group, with [Andrea Nahles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Nahles) of the SPD as her co-chair.[^cite_note-63]

#### Minister of Defence, 2013–2019

In December 2013, Ursula von der Leyen was appointed by Merkel as Germany's first female [defence minister](9473138.md).[^cite_note-theguardian.com-56] By placing a significant party figure such as von der Leyen at the head of the [Defence Ministry](2563857.md), Merkel was widely seen as reinvigorating the scandal-ridden ministry's morale and prestige.[^cite_note-64] Until her 2019 appointment as the president of the European Commission, she was the only minister to remain with Merkel since she became chancellor in 2005.[^cite_note-businessweek.com-3] In December 2014, von der Leyen had her fingerprint cloned by a German hacker who was able to use the commercially available VeriFinger product from [Neurotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotechnology_\(company\)) UAB to replicate her fingerprint using photographs taken with a "standard photo camera".[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] In August 2016, von der Leyen joined the [World Economic Forum](172185.md) board of trustees.[^cite_note-wefbot16-67] In September 2016, von der Leyen chaired the [EPP Defence Ministers Meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_People's_Party), which gathers EPP defence ministers ahead of meetings of the [Council of the European Union](5866.md).[^cite_note-68] Former British [Secretary of State for Defence](302332.md) [Michael Fallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fallon) noted in 2019 that she had been "a star presence" in the [NATO](21133.md) community and "the doyenne of NATO ministers for over five years".[^cite_note-telegraph2019-4] She has faced domestic criticism for her leadership style, reliance on outside consultants, and continued gaps in military readiness.[^cite_note-Andrea_Shalal-69]

#### International crises



[Chuck Hagel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Hagel) and Ursula von der Leyen at the September 2014 NATO summit in [Newport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Wales), Wales

Within her first year in office, von der Leyen visited the [Bundeswehr](288188.md) troops stationed in [Afghanistan](737.md) three times and oversaw the gradual withdrawal of German soldiers from the country as [NATO](21133.md) was winding down its 13-year [combat mission](19666611.md) [ISAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force).[^cite_note-70] In September 2015, she signalled that she was open to delaying the withdrawal of 850 German soldiers from Afghanistan beyond 2016 after the [Taliban](30635.md)'s surprise seizure of the northern city of [Kunduz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunduz). German forces had been based in Kunduz as part of NATO-led [ISAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISAF) and remained stationed in the north of the country.[^cite_note-71] She later opposed the [troop withdrawal from Afghanistan](19666611.md).[^cite_note-72]



Von der Leyen and General Bekir Ercan Van (far left), the commander of [Incirlik Air Base](1597543.md), who was accused of complicity in the [2016 Turkish coup d'état attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Turkish_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt)

In the summer of 2014, she was instrumental in Germany's decision to resupply the Kurdish [Peshmerga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshmerga) fighters with lethal assistance.[^cite_note-73] Following criticism from German officials of Turkish president [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan)'s escalation of the [Kurdish–Turkish conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish%E2%80%93Turkish_conflict_\(2015%E2%80%93present\)) in August 2015, von der Leyen decided to let Germany's three-year [Patriot missile batteries](52024.md) mission to southern Turkey lapse in January 2016 instead of seeking parliamentary approval to extend it. That same month, she participated in the first joint cabinet meeting of the governments of Germany and [Turkey](11125639.md) in Berlin.[^cite_note-Erste_Deutsch-74] By April 2016, under von der Leyen's leadership, the [German Federal Armed Forces](288188.md) announced they would commit 65 million Euro to establish a permanent presence at [Incirlik Air Base](1597543.md), as part of Germany's commitment to the [military intervention against ISIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_intervention_against_ISIL_order_of_battle).[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

At the [Munich Security Conference](1464620.md) in February 2015, von der Leyen publicly defended the German refusal to supply Ukraine with weapons. Stressing that it was necessary to remain united in Europe over Ukraine, she argued that negotiations with Russia, unlike with the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) jihadists, were possible. [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel) said that she saw Ukraine and Russia as a chance to prove that in the 21st century, developed nations should solve disputes at the negotiating table, not with weapons. She also noted that Russia has an almost infinite supply of weapons it could send to Ukraine. She questioned whether any effort by the West could match that or, more importantly, achieve the outcome sought by Ukraine and its supporters.[^cite_note-hnyt-78] On the contrary, von der Leyen said that giving the Ukrainians arms to help them defend themselves could have unintended and fateful consequences. "Weapons deliveries would be a fire accelerant", von der Leyen told the *[Süddeutsche Zeitung](30873120.md)* daily. She agreed with [NATO](21133.md) [SACEUR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SACEUR) General [Philip Breedlove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Breedlove) that "it could give the Kremlin the excuse to openly intervene in this conflict".[^cite_note-natnyt-79]

After [Hungary](13275.md) used a water cannon and tear gas to drive asylum seekers back from the [Hungarian-Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_border_barrier) border in September 2015, during the [European migrant crisis](46415102.md), von der Leyen publicly criticised the government of Prime Minister [Viktor Orbán](750582.md) and called the measures "not acceptable and \[...] against the European rules that we have".[^cite_note-80]

Under von der Leyen's leadership, the German parliament approved government plans in early 2016 to send up to 650 soldiers to [Mali](19127.md), boosting its presence in the UK [peacekeeping mission](15652027.md) [MINUSMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Multidimensional_Integrated_Stabilization_Mission_in_Mali) in the West African country.[^cite_note-81]

#### Armed forces reform



Von der Leyen during the [MSC](1464620.md) 2017

In June 2014, von der Leyen introduced a €100 million plan to make the [Bundeswehr](288188.md) more attractive to recruits, including by offering [crèches](389615.md) for soldiers' children, limiting postings to match school term dates, and considerable rises in hardship allowances for difficult postings.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

In August 2014 in a debate over funding priorities, von der Leyen categorised as "vital to national interests" only sensor and cryptotechnology and left all other funding items as secondary. Economy Minister [Sigmar Gabriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmar_Gabriel) was unhappy with her and said that "this will have significant consequences for national defence procurement and European cooperation" as the key focus of the debate would determine where funding will be allocated. She admitted that "Germany would at present be unable to meet NATO requirements". For example, at this time the majority of the [German Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Air_Force) was grounded, with 42 of its 109 [Eurofighter Typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurofighter_Typhoons) and 38 of 89 [Tornado fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_fighters) ready for deployment. An external report had been commissioned and, with cost overruns rising into the billions of euros, all nine of the Bundeswehr's major projects had been delayed by between 30 and 360 months. This occurred one year into her tenure at Defense.[^cite_note-dwva-84]

In 2015, as a result of severe NATO–Russian tensions in Europe, Germany announced an increase in defence spending. In May 2015, the German government approved an increase in defence spending, at the time 1.3% of GDP, by 6.2% over the following five years, allowing the Ministry of Defense to modernise the army fully.[^cite_note-usde-85] Plans were also announced to expand the tank fleet to a potential number of 328, order 131 more [Boxer armoured personnel carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_armoured_personnel_carriers), increase the submarine fleet, and to develop a new fighter jet to replace the [Tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panavia_Tornado).[^cite_note-Defense_News-2015-86][^cite_note-Channel_NewsAsia-87][^cite_note-88] Germany considered increasing the size of the army,[^cite_note-89] and in May 2016 von der Leyen announced it would spend €130 billion on new equipment by 2030 and add nearly 7,000 soldiers by 2023 in the first German military expansion since the end of the Cold War.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] In February 2017, she announced that the number of Bundeswehr professional soldiers would increase from 178,000 to 198,000 by 2024.[^cite_note-gebdw-92]

In April 2017, after Bundeswehr officials failed to properly investigate persistent reports of brutal hazing rituals, sexual humiliation, and bullying in military training, von der Leyen fired the army's training commander, Major General [Walter Spindler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Spindler?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-93]

#### European Army efforts

As a consequence of improved Dutch–German cooperation, since 2014 two of the three [Royal Netherlands Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Army) Brigades are under German Command. In 2014, the [11th Airmobile Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Airmobile_Brigade_\(Netherlands\)) was integrated into the German Division of fast forces (DSK). The German [414 Tank Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/414_Tank_Battalion) was integrated into the Dutch [43rd Mechanized Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/43rd_Mechanized_Brigade_\(Netherlands\)). In turn, the Dutch 43rd Mechanized Brigade will be assigned to the [1st *Panzer* Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Panzer_Division_\(Bundeswehr\)) of the German army, with the integration starting at the beginning of 2016, and the unit becoming operational at the end of 2019.[^cite_note-janes16-94] In February 2016 it was announced that the [*Seebatallion*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seebataillon#German_Navy) of the German Navy would start to operate under [Royal Dutch Navy](198478.md) command.[^cite_note-duint-95] The Dutch-German military cooperation was seen in 2016 by von der Leyen and Dutch Minister of Defence [Jeanine Hennis-Plasschaert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeanine_Hennis-Plasschaert) as an example for setting up a European defence union.[^cite_note-duint-95]

A further proposal by von der Leyen, to allow non-German [EU nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_of_the_European_Union) to join the [Bundeswehr](288188.md), was met in July 2016 by strong opposition, even from her own party.[^cite_note-96]

According to a policy dictated by von der Leyen in February 2017, the Bundeswehr is to play a greater role as an "anchor army" for smaller [NATO](21133.md) states, by improving coordination between its divisions and smaller members' [Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade).[^cite_note-anchorarmy-97]

It was announced in February 2017 that the [Czech Republic](5321.md)'s [4th Rapid Deployment Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Rapid_Deployment_Brigade_\(Czech_Republic\)) and [Romania](25445.md)'s [81st Mechanized Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/81st_Mechanized_Brigade_\(Romania\)) would be integrated into Germany's [10th Armoured Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Armoured_Division_\(Bundeswehr\)) and Rapid Response Forces Division.[^cite_note-Germany-98]

#### Military procurement



Ursula von der Leyen with US secretary of defence [Ashton Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashton_Carter) (2015 in Berlin)

In October 2014, von der Leyen pledged to get a grip on Germany's military equipment budget after publishing a [KPMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPMG) report on repeated failures in controlling suppliers, costs and delivery deadlines, e.g., with the [Airbus A400M Atlas](371265.md) transport plane, [Eurofighter Typhoon](167667.md) jet and the [Boxer armoured fighting vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_\(armoured_fighting_vehicle\)).[^cite_note-99]

In January 2015, von der Leyen publicly criticised Airbus over delays in the delivery of A400M military transport planes, complaining that the company had a serious problem with product quality.[^cite_note-100] Under her leadership, the ministry agreed to accept 13 million euros in compensation for delays in deliveries of both the second and third A400M aircraft. In 2016, she asked for an additional 12.7 million euros in damages for delays in the delivery of a fourth plane.[^cite_note-101] Also in 2015, von der Leyen chose [MBDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBDA), jointly owned by [Airbus](26220236.md), Britain's [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems), and Italy's [Leonardo S.p.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_S.p.A.), to build the [Medium Extended Air Defense System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_Extended_Air_Defense_System), but set strict milestones for it to retain the contract.[^cite_note-102]

#### Arms exports



German defence minister Ursula von der Leyen after being received by [Vice Admiral AR Karve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhay_Raghunath_Karve), Chief of Staff, [Western Naval Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Naval_Command) during her visit to India

During her May 2015 visit to India, von der Leyen expressed support for a project initiated by the Indian government to build six small German [TKMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ThyssenKrupp_Marine_Systems) diesel-electric submarines for a total cost of \$11 billion.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]

In 2019, she also promoted the German government's decisions on arms exports to [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) and [Turkey](11125639.md).[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

#### "Consultants affair"

Since 2018 an investigative committee organised by Germany's Federal Audit Office has been looking into how contracts worth tens of millions of euros were awarded to external consultancy firms.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-Andrea_Shalal-69] The auditing office has found several irregularities in how the contracts were awarded. During the investigation, two of von der Leyen's phones were confiscated, but data from both phones had been deleted before being returned to the defence ministry.[^cite_note-109] In turn, opposition lawmaker [Tobias Lindner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobias_Lindner) has filed a criminal complaint against von der Leyen, suspecting deliberate destruction of evidence relevant for the case.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111]

#### CDU party career

Von der Leyen was elected as a member of the CDU executive board in December 2014 and received 70.5% of the votes. Compared to her re-elections in 2016 (72.4%) and 2018 (57.47%), it was the weakest of all the results.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-Stefan_Wagstyl2-115]

As a cabinet member, von der Leyen was, for several years, regarded as one of the leading contenders to succeed [Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkel) as [Chancellor](20890626.md).[^cite_note-A_guide_to_future_chancellors-54][^cite_note-ft.com-55][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-Merkel_takes_oath_of_office-117][^cite_note-nytimes.com-118][^cite_note-Stefan_Wagstyl_2014-119] In 2010 she was Merkel's preferred candidate for [President of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Germany), but her nomination was blocked by the conservative wing of the CDU/CSU.[^cite_note-120] From 2018 until her nomination as European Commission president, she was described as the favourite to succeed [Jens Stoltenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Stoltenberg) as [Secretary General of NATO](22194433.md).[^cite_note-fp18-121][^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123] *[Die Welt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Welt)* reported that von der Leyen "is highly respected in the alliance" and that "all the \[NATO] defence ministers listen when she speaks".[^cite_note-weltnato-124]

### 2019

On 2 July 2019, von der Leyen was proposed by the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) as their candidate for the office of [President of the European Commission](24468.md).[^cite_note-BBC-2019-6][^cite_note-POLITICO-2019-7] On 16 July, her nomination was approved by the European Parliament with 383 to 327 votes.[^cite_note-BBCvote-8] Germany abstained from the vote to nominate her. An article in *The Guardian* said that the reason for Germany's refusal to support her nomination in the European Council was that von der Leyen was considered divisive in her home country.[^cite_note-125] She is the first woman to hold the office[^cite_note-126] and the first German since the commission's first president, [Walter Hallstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Hallstein).[^cite_note-127]

When she lived in Brussels, her little sister Benita-Eva died of cancer at the age of eleven and she remembered "the enormous helplessness of my parents" in view of the cancer. This inspired her to make cancer a focus of [her commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_der_Leyen_Commission_I).[^cite_note-128]

At the press conference announcing her nomination, European Council President Donald Tusk noted von der Leyen's intention to retain Commission First-Vice-President [Frans Timmermans](9524458.md) during her administration. Timmermans has previously been one of the "lead candidates" (German: *[Spitzenkandidat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spitzenkandidat)*) for the commission's presidency. As a candidate, she published a document entitled "My agenda for Europe",[^cite_note-ppuvdl-129] and was fêted for her commitment to "gender equality and gender mainstreaming" by at least one observer who sought to advance the "professional development of women in the field of international peace and security".[^cite_note-wiis-130]

Following her nomination as a candidate for Commission president, the Commission provided her with a salary, office, and staff in Brussels to facilitate negotiations between the EU institutions as to her election. These arrangements were extended, to enable a smooth transition, during her period as president-elect, until the new College of Commissioners was confirmed by the European Parliament and took office in November.[^cite_note-Kostaki-2019-131] In light of her new role, von der Leyen resigned her seat in the German Bundestag on 31 July 2019.[^cite_note-Deutscher_Bundestag-132]

Von der Leyen supported the proposed [European Union–Mercosur free trade agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union%E2%80%93Mercosur_free_trade_agreement),[^cite_note-merco-133] which would form one of the world's largest free trade areas.[^cite_note-134] The fear is that the deal could lead to more [deforestation of the Amazon rainforest](19732090.md) as it expands market access to Brazilian beef.[^cite_note-The_Guardian-2019-135]



Von der Leyen with her proposed College of Commissioners on 19 November 2019

Von der Leyen unveiled the new proposed EU Commission's structure (whom she deemed to be a "geopolitical" one)[^cite_note-euractiv-136] on 10 September 2019, renaming a number of posts of the College of Commissioners to make them sound less formal and more goal-oriented, including the controversial portfolio for "Protecting our European Way of Life",[^cite_note-Schumacher-2019-137][^cite_note-fascist-138] a vice-presidency responsible for the coordination of migration, security, employment and education policies.[^cite_note-euobs-139][^cite_note-140] The later portfolio's name drew heavy criticism, as it was considered to carry a xenophobic message linking the protection of the "European Way of Life" to migration policies.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-Schumacher-2019-137] The proposed structure for the college also saw the "unexpected" promotion of EPP's [Valdis Dombrovskis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdis_Dombrovskis) to a role of executive vice-president, up to a number of three executive vice-presidencies, equalling the roles entrusted to Timmermans and [Margrethe Vestager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margrethe_Vestager).[^cite_note-euractiv-136]

As part of her efforts to be elected President of the EU Commission, von der Leyen made remarks in favour of EU parliamentarians being given the right to initiate legislation,[^cite_note-144] but reversed course shortly after assuming office.[^cite_note-145]

#### Controversy and investigation over award of contracts by German defence ministry

At the time of von der Leyen's nomination as president of the Commission, an investigative committee of the [German parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_parliament) was investigating how, during her time as minister of defence of Germany, lucrative contracts from her ministry were awarded to outside consultants without proper oversight, and whether a network of informal personal connections facilitated those deals.[^cite_note-146]

In December 2019, German parliamentarians accused the [German Defence Ministry](2563857.md) of torpedoing the investigation into alleged wrongdoing in its consultant contracts by deleting data from the official phone of von der Leyen from the time she was Minister of Defence after it was declared evidence in the investigation, and by arguing that the deletion of the phone data was "for security reasons".[^cite_note-147]

### 2020



Von der Leyen delivering her first [State of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Union_\(European_Union\)) address on 16 September 2020

In March 2020, von der Leyen's Commission turned down the idea of suspending the [Schengen Agreement](179592.md) to introduce border controls around Italy, at that time the centre of the [COVID-19 pandemic in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Europe).[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] The decision drew criticism from some European politicians.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152] After some EU member states announced closure of their national borders to foreign nationals due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), she said: "Certain controls may be justified, but general travel bans are not seen as being the most effective by the World Health Organization. Moreover, they have a strong social and economic impact, they disrupt people's lives and business across the borders."[^cite_note-153] She condemned the US decision to restrict travel from the coronavirus-affected Europe to the United States.[^cite_note-154]

Von der Leyen supported the EU's imposition of [sanctions against Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_against_Belarus) after the security services violently cracked down [on street protests in Minsk and elsewhere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Belarusian_protests) against the 26-year authoritarian rule under President [Alexander Lukashenko](156589.md). The protests took place after a [disputed presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Belarusian_presidential_election), which was contested by the opposition and designated by the EU as not [free and fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_fair_election).[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-Amaro-156] Sanctions were imposed after the Belarusian government [diverted a civilian aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryanair_Flight_4978) to seize an opposition figure, [Roman Protasevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Protasevich).[^cite_note-Amaro-156]

Greek prime minister [Kyriakos Mitsotakis](2780816.md) called for EU sanctions against Turkey (citing Belarus as precedent) over [Turkey's incursions into Greek maritime zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_dispute) in the [eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean), including [illegal drilling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus%E2%80%93Turkey_maritime_zones_dispute) and the passage of the *[Oruç Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RV_MTA_Oru%C3%A7_Reis)*, accompanied by a [Turkish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Navy) ship, in Greek waters. Sanctions would require a unanimous decision of the [EU Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Council_of_Ministers). While France and Austria fully backed Greece's position, Germany (which at the time held the [rotating EU presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_the_Council_of_the_European_Union)) took a more ambiguous stance. Von der Leyen said that Turkey and Belarus are "two different situations".[^cite_note-mermac-157]

### 2021



Finnish prime minister [Sanna Marin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanna_Marin) and von der Leyen meeting in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) on 4 October 2021



Von der Leyen with US president [Joe Biden](145422.md), 2021 G20 summit in [Rome](25458.md), 31 October 2021

[French-US](686250.md) and [French-Australia relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France%E2%80%93Australia_relations) suffered a period of tension in September 2021 due to fallout from the [AUKUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AUKUS) defence pact between the US, the United Kingdom, and Australia. The security pact is directed at countering [Chinese power](5405.md) in the [Indo-Pacific](330456.md) region.[^cite_note-158] As part of the agreement, the US agreed to provide [nuclear-powered submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear-powered_submarine) to Australia. After entering into AUKUS, the Australian government cancelled an agreement that it had made with France for the [provision of French conventionally powered submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack-class_submarine). Von der Leyen called the way France was treated "unacceptable" and demanded an explanation.[^cite_note-159] The EU also demanded an apology from Australia.[^cite_note-160]



Von der Leyen speaking at the [2021 United Nations Climate Change Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference) in Glasgow

Due to a combination of unfavourable conditions, which involved soaring demand of natural gas, its diminished supply from Russia and Norway to the European markets, and less power generation by [renewable energy sources](25784.md) such as wind, water and solar energy, Europe faced steep [increases in energy prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_global_energy_crisis) in 2021.[^cite_note-161] Some critics blamed a record-breaking surge in energy prices on the European Commission's [Green Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Deal), which aims to make the EU [climate neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_neutrality) by 2050.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164] She said: "Europe today is too reliant on gas and too dependent on gas imports. The answer has to do with diversifying our suppliers ... and, crucially, with speeding up the transition to clean energy."[^cite_note-165]

During the [2021 Israel–Palestine crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Israel%E2%80%93Palestine_crisis), von der Leyen condemned "arbitrary attacks by [Hamas](13913.md) against Israel" adding that "civilians on both sides must be protected".[^cite_note-166]

In December 2021 von der Leyen expressed concern that one-third of the European population were not vaccinated: "EU nations should open a debate around making COVID-19 vaccinations mandatory because too many people still refuse to get shots voluntarily."[^cite_note-167]

#### COVID-19 vaccine deal

Main article: [Pfizergate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfizergate)

During the COVID-19 pandemic when European countries were scrambling for vaccines, [Pfizer](62304.md) was able to close a deal worth €35 billion to provide 900 million doses of the [Pfizer–BioNTech COVID-19 vaccine](65797150.md) with an additional 900 million doses available for purchase.[^cite_note-168] The deal was initially well-received, although a later low-range estimate according to *[Politico](9172097.md)* suggested that over 100 million doses worth approximately €4 billion were discarded, raising concerns about [vaccine equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccine_equity).[^cite_note-politico.eu2024-169][^cite_note-170]

In April 2021, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that von der Leyen had exchanged electronic correspondence with Pfizer CEO [Albert Bourla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Bourla) negotiating terms of sale of the COVID-19 vaccine to the European Union.[^cite_note-171] [Emily O'Reilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_O'Reilly), the [European Ombudsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Ombudsman), accused von der Leyen of "maladministration" for failure to disclose that correspondence upon a FOI request, and for claiming that the messages had disappeared, and for further claiming that the vaccine line item of the EU's budget was confidential.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173]



Von der Leyen and [Frans Timmermans](9524458.md) in 2021

Von der Leyen was first reported to have taken a personal role in negotiating the vaccine deal. Health Commissioner [Stella Kyriakides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stella_Kyriakides) later informed the [European Parliament](9581.md) that von der Leyen played no (formal) role and "was not involved in the negotiations on the Covid vaccine contract". The last COVID-19 vaccines will according to the contract with Pfizer be delivered in 2027.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175]

The incident has been reported as "Pfizergate".[^cite_note-176] In 2024 the [European Public Prosecutor's Office](1707190.md) (EPPO) took over the investigation from Belgian authorities. The original complaint, from a Belgian lobbyist, cited "interference in public functions, destruction of SMS, corruption and conflict of interest".[^cite_note-politico.eu2024-169]

In early May 2024, a few days before the hearing in Liège was supposed to take place, Baldan's lawyer, Diane Protat, visited the EPPO's offices in Brussels and Luxembourg to request a copy of its case file, but she was told that there was no such file and security was called on her. A few days later, it was reported that Hungary and Poland had joined the lawsuit.[^cite_note-FrS-177] By the end of the month, the plaintiff asked "the [European People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_People's_Party) to withdraw the candidacy of Ms von der Leyen for the post of President of the European Commission" as well as "prohibiting anyone from presenting the candidature of Mrs von der Leyen to the post of President of the European Commission or any other post within the European institutions as long as she is the subject of criminal proceedings".[^cite_note-EUN-178]

#### Second candidacy

See also: [von der Leyen Commission II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_der_Leyen_Commission_II)

In March 2024 von der Leyen was once again confirmed as the [EPP's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_People's_Party) candidate for the Presidency of the European Commission. Opinion polls deemed her re-election as president very likely.[^cite_note-212]

In April 2024, she praised the [EU Asylum and Migration Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Pact_on_Migration_and_Asylum) as a "huge achievement for Europe".[^cite_note-213] Countries where migrants first arrive will newly be able to relocate a total of up to 30,000 migrants per year to other EU member states. The Pact will institute a "mandatory solidarity mechanism".[^cite_note-214] The Pact has been criticised by some right-wing politicians for not going far enough to prevent illegal immigration, such as missing provisions relating to migrant returns.[^cite_note-215]

On 27 June 2024, the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) nominated von der Leyen for a second term as [President of the European Commission](24468.md). On 18 July 2024, she was re-elected for a further five-year term after a secret ballot among MEPs at the [European Parliament](9581.md), winning 401 votes in favour compared to 284 against with 22 blank or invalid ballot papers.[^cite_note-EU_reelect-2][^cite_note-216]

In the fall of 2024, von der Leyen became an advocate for [nuclear power](22153.md), along with her party, CDU.[^cite_note-217]

Promoting the [European Green Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Deal) and the [green transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_transition) remained a priority for the second von der Leyen Commission.[^cite_note-218]

## Political views

### Childcare and parental leave

Ursula von der Leyen assumed her office as [Federal Minister for Family Affairs, Senior Citizens, Women and Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_of_Family_Affairs,_Senior_Citizens,_Women_and_Youth) in 2005. Amidst protest (particularly from the conservative wing of the [CDU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Union_\(Germany\))), she introduced the [Child Advancement Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_Advancement_Act?action=edit\&redlink=1) (*Kinderförderungsgesetz*), which reserved 4.3 billion euros for the creation of childcare structures throughout Germany.[^cite_note-251]

Von der Leyen also introduced the German *Elternzeit*, a paid [parental leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parental_leave) scheme. Following Scandinavian models, the scheme reserves two additional months for fathers who go on parental leave as well (*Vätermonate* in German). This part of the law, in particular, attracted protest from some German conservatives. [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) Bishop [Walter Mixa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Mixa) accused von der Leyen of turning women into "birthing machines". Meanwhile, [Bavarian](3764.md) colleagues from von der Leyen's sister party, the [CSU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Social_Union_of_Bavaria), complained that men did not need a "diaper-changing internship".[^cite_note-252] Von der Leyen successfully influenced public opinion of her reforms with a 3-million-euro PR campaign, which was criticised for using public funds for political advocacy and for employing [embedded marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embedded_marketing) techniques.[^cite_note-253]

### Blocking internet child pornography



Demonstration on 17 April 2009 against internet censorship

Ursula von der Leyen advocated the initiation of a mandatory blockage of [child pornography](25211885.md) on the Internet through service providers via a block list maintained by the [Federal Criminal Police Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Criminal_Police_Office_\(Germany\)) of Germany (BKA), thus creating the necessary infrastructure for extensive [censorship of websites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_censorship_in_Germany) deemed illegal by the BKA.[^cite_note-254]

These actions brought her the nickname "Zensursula", a [portmanteau word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmanteau) blending the German word for censorship ("Zensur") and her given name ("Ursula").[^cite_note-255] The combination of a sensitive topic like child pornography and internet censorship is said to have caused a rising interest in the [Pirate Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirate_Party_Germany).[^cite_note-256]

In July 2009, she referred to the problems of struggling against paedophile pornography on the internet as the responsible persons often use servers located in Africa or India, where, she said, "child pornography is legal".[^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258] This claim was based on a 2006 study by the [International Centre for Missing & Exploited Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_Missing_&_Exploited_Children).[^cite_note-259] As child pornography is illegal in India, and the country has [stricter rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pornography_in_India) about all pornography than Germany, she later expressed regret for citing an inaccurate study.[^cite_note-260]

### Women board quota

In 2013, von der Leyen unsuccessfully campaigned for a statutory quota for female participation in the supervisory boards of companies in Germany, requiring company boards to be at least 20% female by 2018, rising to 40% by 2023.[^cite_note-261]

### German foreign policy



Von der Leyen with US president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) in January 2020

Von der Leyen is a proponent of a more assertive German foreign policy.[^cite_note-smale1-262][^cite_note-263] One striking example was the decision in September 2014 to send arms to Kurdish and Iraqi security forces. This decision broke a longstanding taboo on Germany's dispatching of weapons to a conflict zone.[^cite_note-nytimes.com-118]



Von der Leyen and Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) at the International Conference on [Libya](17633.md), 19 January 2020

On the deteriorating relationship between Europe and Russia during the [annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), she argued that "the reliance on a functioning business relationship with Europe is much, much bigger in Russia" and that sanctions should prod the oligarchs and Russian business.[^cite_note-264] She also called for more significant [NATO](21133.md) backing of the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states) during Russia's invasion of Crimea in 2014.[^cite_note-265]

Von der Leyen has supported close [security cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_Angela_Merkel_government) with [Saudi Arabia](349303.md).[^cite_note-266] German opposition parties criticised Germany's defence plan with Saudi Arabia, which has been [waging war in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen) and was condemned for massive [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Saudi_Arabia) violations.[^cite_note-267][^cite_note-268] In 2016, von der Leyen caused controversy after she refused to wear a [hijab](68301.md) while visiting Saudi Arabia. She said: "It annoys me when women are to be pushed into wearing the [abaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abaya)."[^cite_note-269]

In 2017 von der Leyen noted that "healthy democratic resistance of the younger generation" in [Poland](22936.md) must be supported.[^cite_note-270] In some Polish media, it was understood that she instigated opposition aimed to overthrow the allegedly anti-democratic and authoritarian [PiS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_and_Justice_\(Poland\)) government; the statement was branded as scandalous.[^cite_note-271] The [Polish Foreign Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witold_Waszczykowski) made sarcastic comments about "Prussian tone of the [Ode to Joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ode_to_Joy)".[^cite_note-272] The Polish [Minister of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Macierewicz) summoned the Germany military attache and demanded explanations.[^cite_note-273] The German embassy in Warsaw and spokesman for the German defence ministry in Berlin issued conciliatory statements.[^cite_note-274] The German media mostly ignored the incident; some acknowledged a "minor slip of the tongue"[^cite_note-275] on the part of von der Leyen, yet also noted that [German-Polish relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany%E2%80%93Poland_relations) were "severely damaged".[^cite_note-276]

Von der Leyen responded to [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s criticism of the Russian-backed [Nord Stream 2](50335419.md)—a pipeline for delivering [natural gas from Russia](24648652.md) to Germany—in an interview with the BBC: "We have an independent energy supply, we are an independent country, we are just diversifying."[^cite_note-277]

### European integration



[Manfred Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred_Weber), [Annegret Kramp-Karrenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annegret_Kramp-Karrenbauer) and von der Leyen in April 2019

In a 2011 interview with *[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel)*, von der Leyen expressed her preference for "a [united states of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Federation)—run along the lines of the federal states of Switzerland, Germany or the USA" which would capitalise on Europe's size by agreeing on core issues relating to finance, tax and economic politics.[^cite_note-278]

With 2014 marking the centenary of the start of [World War I](4764461.md), von der Leyen—in her capacity as defence minister—inaugurated a memorial for the [Armistice Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_Day) in [Ablain-Saint-Nazaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ablain-Saint-Nazaire) alongside French president [François Hollande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Hollande) and [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md) State Premier [Hannelore Kraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannelore_Kraft), as well as British and Belgian officials.[^cite_note-279]

In 2015, von der Leyen argued that a form of [EU army](1074631.md) should be a long-term goal. She also said that she was convinced about the goal of a combined military force, just as she was convinced that "perhaps not my children, but then my grandchildren will experience a [United States of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Federation)".[^cite_note-280] In March 2015, she and her counterparts from France and [Poland](22936.md), [Jean-Yves Le Drian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Yves_Le_Drian) and [Tomasz Siemoniak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomasz_Siemoniak), revived a meeting format intended to promote cooperation between the three countries in crisis zones by holding their first meeting between the [Weimar Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Triangle) defence ministers since 2007.[^cite_note-281]



Von der Leyen with Italian prime minister [Giorgia Meloni](29228976.md) and Dutch prime minister [Mark Rutte](4321056.md), 3 November 2022

Following the [2016 European Union membership referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum) in the United Kingdom, she argued that the UK had "paralysed" European efforts to integrate security policy and "consistently blocked everything with the label 'Europe' on it".[^cite_note-282] She has described [Brexit](41688778.md) as "a burst bubble of hollow promises".[^cite_note-283] In an interview with *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* days after her election to succeed [Jean-Claude Juncker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Juncker) as [President of the European Commission](24468.md), she stated that the withdrawal deal agreed between [Theresa May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresa_May) and chief Brexit negotiator [Michel Barnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Barnier) would remain the basis of any future talks. She also stated that the EU should extend the Brexit deadline beyond 31 October 2019.[^cite_note-284] In November 2019, at Paris Peace Forum, von der Leyen said that there is need for stable and responsible leadership in Europe and that the bloc must increase foreign policy budget spending by one-third.[^cite_note-285]

### Human rights in China

See also: [Persecution of Uyghurs in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Uyghurs_in_China) and [China–European Union relations](2016720.md)



Ursula von der Leyen, French president [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) and Chinese leader [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) in Beijing, China, 6 April 2023

Von der Leyen and French president [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) raised the issue of [human rights in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_China) during [their visit to China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_France%E2%80%93China_Summit) in April 2023, amid growing international criticism of China's repression of ethnic minorities, political dissidents, and civil society activists under the [general secretaryship of Xi Jinping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_secretaryship_of_Xi_Jinping).[^cite_note-286] They expressed their concerns over the situation in [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang), where the Chinese government has detained an estimated one million [Uyghurs](31783.md) and other Turkic Muslims in [internment camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang_internment_camps), subjected them to [forced labour](541048.md), [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance), and abuse.[^cite_note-287] They also urged China to respect the autonomy and freedoms of Hong Kong.[^cite_note-288]

### Same-sex marriage

When the [Federal Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Constitutional_Court_of_Germany) ruled in favour of tax equality for same-sex couples in 2013, von der Leyen came forward in support of [equal adoption rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_adoption), arguing, "I know of no study that says that children growing up in same-sex partnerships fare any differently than children who grow up in heterosexual marriages or partnerships."[^cite_note-289] In June 2017, von der Leyen voted against her parliamentary group's majority and in favour of Germany's introduction of [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_of_same-sex_unions_in_Germany).[^cite_note-290]

### Israel–Palestine



Von der Leyen with Israeli president [Isaac Herzog](4645588.md) in Brussels, January 2023

On the 75th anniversary of [Israel's independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel's_independence), von der Leyen referred to Israel as a "vibrant democracy" in the Middle East that made "the desert bloom". These remarks were criticised as racist by the foreign ministry of the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md).[^cite_note-aljaz-20230427-291][^cite_note-bbc-20230427-292]

In 2022, von der Leyen received an honorary doctorate degree from [Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben-Gurion_University_of_the_Negev).[^cite_note-auto-293] During her acceptance speech, she addressed the rise of antisemitism and the challenges it poses to democracy, fighting antisemitism while fostering Jewish life, and the bond that Europe shares with Israel.[^cite_note-294] In her speech at [Ben-Gurion University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben-Gurion_University_of_the_Negev) Ursula von der Leyen stated that "Europe is the values of the [Talmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talmud)," highlighting shared ethical foundations like personal responsibility, justice, and solidarity.[^cite_note-295]

During the 2023 [Gaza war](74998519.md), she was criticised by EU lawmakers and diplomats for supporting Israel and not calling for a ceasefire.[^cite_note-296] On 13 October 2023, she visited Israel to express solidarity with the country.[^cite_note-297] EU foreign policy chief [Josep Borrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josep_Borrell) criticised her for the pro-Israeli stance she took on the trip which, he said, "has had a high geopolitical cost for Europe".[^cite_note-El_Pais_2024-298][^cite_note-299]

In October 2023, 841 EU staff signed a letter to von der Leyen criticising her stance on the conflict. It stated the commission was giving "a free hand to the acceleration and the legitimacy of a war crime in the Gaza Strip" and warned that the EU was "losing all credibility and the position as a fair, equitable and humanist broker".[^cite_note-it-20231020-300] In May 2025, [Francesca Albanese](72566699.md), UN special rapporteur for the Palestinian territories, suggested von der Leyen be charged for complicity in Israeli war crimes.[^cite_note-301]

### Environment

Von der Leyen promoted the [European Green Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Deal).[^cite_note-302] and the [Open Coalition on Compliance Carbon Markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Coalition_on_Compliance_Carbon_Markets).[^cite_note-303] She complained that, "Global markets are now flooded with cheaper Chinese electric cars, and their price is kept artificially low by huge state subsidies."[^cite_note-304]

### New world order

In March 2026, von der Leyen declared the traditional 'rules-based' international order finished, advocating for a more interest-driven and [realistic EU foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realpolitik) in response to rising global instability.[^cite_note-305] Addressing the [US-Israeli strikes on Iran](82533582.md), she argued 'no tears should be shed' for the [Iranian regime](50913538.md), despite noting security risks to Europe.[^cite_note-306] This rhetoric, particularly her dismissal of the rules-based framework, sparked significant criticism from several EU capitals and threats of a no-confidence motion from [S\&D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Alliance_of_Socialists_and_Democrats) lawmakers.[^cite_note-307]
