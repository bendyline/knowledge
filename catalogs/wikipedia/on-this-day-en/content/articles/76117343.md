**SPACE ONE CO., LTD.** ([Japanese](15606.md): スペースワン株式会社, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *Supēsuwan Kabushikigaisha*; [Corporate Number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_Number): 8010401132607[^cite_note-1]), or **Space One** (*スペースワン*), is a Japanese [aerospace manufacturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_manufacturer), orbital [launch service provider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_launch_service_providers#Corporate), and [spaceport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceport) operator. It is a [private spaceflight](752732.md) company developing and operating the [KAIROS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAIROS_\(rocket\)) orbital [launch vehicle](1306157.md) for [small satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_satellite) launches; KAIROS flew its unsuccessful maiden flight in March 2024.[^cite_note-2] The company was founded by investments from [Canon Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_Electronics?action=edit\&redlink=1) (a listed subsidiary of [Canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_Inc.)), [IHI Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHI_Aerospace) (a subsidiary of [IHI Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHI_Corporation)), [Shimizu Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimizu_Corporation) and the [Development Bank of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_Bank_of_Japan) in July 2018.[^cite_note-3]

## Rockets

Main article: [KAIROS (rocket)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAIROS_\(rocket\))

The namesake of the [KAIROS](76117481.md) rocket, or [Kii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kii_Peninsula)-based Advanced & Instant Rocket System,[^cite_note-4] is the Greek word *[Kairos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kairos)*, which means 'time' and is also an alternate spelling of the name of [Caerus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caerus), the Greek deity of luck and opportunity.[^cite_note-\:0-5]

## Launch site

Shimizu Corporation constructed Space One's own launch site for KAIROS, named [Spaceport Kii](76449414.md), as Japan's first-ever [private sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_sector) spaceport, in [Kushimoto, Wakayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushimoto,_Wakayama), Japan between 2019 and 2021. The launch site provides access to various orbit inclinations.[^cite_note-\:1-6]

This spaceport is accessible from [Tokyo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haneda_Airport) (Haneda Airport) via [Nanki–Shirahama Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanki%E2%80%93Shirahama_Airport) in approximately 2 hours and 20 minutes, and from [Kansai International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_International_Airport) in approximately 2 hours and 30 minutes.[^cite_note-\:1-6]

## Accidents and incidents

### Inaugural flight

On March 13, 2024, the Kairos rocket exploded moments into its maiden voyage, aiming to become the first Japanese private company to deploy a satellite into orbit. The 59-foot [solid-fueled rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-propellant_rocket) disintegrated shortly after liftoff from the Kii peninsula in western Japan, leaving behind debris and a cloud of smoke; there were no immediate reports of injuries.[^cite_note-7]

### Second flight

The second flight took place on 18 December 2024, which was canceled because of a failed rocket shortly after launch.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] The mission was planned to carry five satellites, including four [CubeSats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CubeSat) and one [microsatellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_satellite).[^cite_note-10] These satellites include payloads developed by [Taiwan Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Space_Agency), [Space Cubics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Cubics?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Terra Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_Space?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Lagrapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrapo), as well as an additional satellite owned by an unnamed customer.[^cite_note-11] The rocket began tumbling during 1st stage burn. The flight was terminated some time after.[^cite_note-\:4-12]

### Third flight

The third flight of KAIROS took place on March 4, 2026, lifting off from Spaceport Kii at 9\:10 pm but was also unsuccessful with flight termination measures being implemented at around 70 seconds after liftoff.[^cite_note-13] With this third failure the commercial viability of Space One was thrown into doubt as Japan seeks to increase its domestic launch industry.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]
