&#x20;As of 27 September 2026, at least 77,271 people (75,225+ Palestinians[^cite_note-12] and 2,046+ Israelis)[^cite_note-defense-ministry-data-13][^cite_note-israeli-civilian-casualties-14][^cite_note-wp-hostagecount-15][^cite_note-ap-74000-16] have been reported killed in the [Gaza war](74998519.md) according to the [Gaza Health Ministry](52186562.md) (GHM) and Israeli Ministry of Foreign Affairs, including [270 journalists and media workers](75146557.md),[^cite_note-17] 120 academics,[^cite_note-18] and over 560 humanitarian aid workers,[^cite_note-19] a number that includes 393 employees of [UNRWA](32270.md).[^cite_note-20] A study by [OHCHR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OHCHR) found that 70% of the Palestinians killed in residential buildings or similar housing were [women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_the_Gaza_war) and [children](75987631.md).[^cite_note-axios20241108-21][^cite_note-ohchr-report-20241108-22]

In January 2025, an analysis of deaths between October 2023 and 30 June 2024 estimated 64,260 deaths from traumatic injury during this period, and likely exceeding 70,000 by October 2024, with 59.1% of them being women, children and the elderly. It concluded that the GHM undercounted trauma-related deaths by 41% in its report, and also noted that its findings "do not account for non-trauma-related deaths."[^cite_note-Lancet2025-23] A comparable estimate up to May 2025 would be 93,000 (77,000 to 109,000), representing 4–5% of Gaza's pre-war population.[^cite_note-economist20250508-24] Another casualty analysis, published in February 2026, estimated 75,200 violent deaths and 8,540 excess non-violent deaths between 7 October 2023 and 5 January 2025. The estimate of violent deaths is 34.7% higher than the GHM's casualty count at the time. Of the violent deaths, the researchers estimate that 56.2% were women, children, and elderly individuals.[^cite_note-25]

A May 2025 investigation found that a classified IDF database listed 8,900 Palestinian fighters as dead or likely dead, or 17% of the 53,000 death toll at the time, which if correct would indicate that 83% of the dead were civilians—among conflicts since 1989, only the [Srebrenica massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica_massacre) (though not the [Bosnian war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_war) overall), the [siege of Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Mariupol), and the [Rwandan genocide](676659.md) have seen a higher rate of civilian casualties.[^cite_note-theguardian20250821-26] In response, the IDF said that the numbers in the article are incorrect.[^cite_note-27]

A survey by the [Palestinian Center for Policy and Survey Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Center_for_Policy_and_Survey_Research) (PCPSR) reported showed over 60% of Gazans have lost family members since the war began.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] Thousands of more dead bodies are thought to be under the rubble of destroyed buildings.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] The number of injured is greater than 100,000;[^cite_note-32] United Nations agencies have reported that Gaza is now home to the highest number of child amputees per capita in the world.[^cite_note-33]

The majority of casualties have been in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md). The GHM total casualty count does not distinguish Palestinian deaths by Israeli attacks from those by other means, like friendly fire, and describes all casualties as victims of "Israeli aggression".[^cite_note-34] On 3 March 2026, the GHM published the names, gender and birth date of 72,004 individual Palestinians whose identities were confirmed and continues to attempt to identify all casualties.[^cite_note-haaretz20260303-35]

The [October 7 attacks](74998317.md) on Israel killed 1,195 people, including 815 civilians.[^cite_note-hrw20240717-1][^cite_note-36] Casualties have also occurred in other parts of Israel, as well as in southern [Lebanon](17771.md),[^cite_note-37] [Syria](7515849.md),[^cite_note-38] [Yemen](350939.md),[^cite_note-39] and [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-40] As reported by the Defense Ministry, the total number of Israeli fatalities as of October 2025 is 1,152. This figure encompasses soldiers, police personnel, Shin Bet operatives, and civilian security officials who died on duty, as well as those who succumbed to accidents, illness, or suicide. Notably, around 42% of the deceased were individuals under the age of 21.[^cite_note-41] According to the Israeli Ministry of Defense's Rehabilitation Division, about 1,000 soldiers are wounded every month.[^cite_note-42] In August 2024, the ministry predicted that it would have to account for 100,000 disabled IDF veterans by 2030 due to the war.[^cite_note-43] An August 2025 report indicated that the rise in suicides among IDF soldiers was due to psychological trauma resulting from the prolonged conflict.[^cite_note-44]

The Gaza Strip suffered significant civilian casualties from [Israeli bombardments](76139149.md) in the beginning of the war.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

On 3 November 2023, at least 10 cemetery workers were killed by an Israeli airstrike while working at a graveyard in [Beit Lahia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Lahia).[^cite_note-50] On 4 November, an unnamed Israeli official said that around 20,000 people had been killed in Gaza, "most of them terrorists."[^cite_note-51] On 14 November, two volleyball players, Hassan Zuaiter and [Ibrahim Qusaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Qusaya), were killed in an [Israeli airstrike on Jabalia refugee camp](75014301.md).[^cite_note-52] As of 1 December 102 [UNRWA](32270.md) employees in Gaza had been killed in Israeli airstrikes.[^cite_note-UNRWAGazadeaths2-53] On 29 December, [UNRWA](32270.md) reported 308 people had been killed in UNRWA shelters.[^cite_note-54] [Euro-Mediterranean Human Rights Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro-Mediterranean_Human_Rights_Monitor) reported that the IDF was taking and holding Palestinian bodies from Gaza, prompting calls for an international investigation on [organ theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_theft) suspicions.[^cite_note-55] The organization further stated that Israel had systematically killed hundreds of tech specialists, including "[programmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programmer), information technology experts, and computer engineering analysts".[^cite_note-56] In March 2024, [Al Jazeera English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English)'s news blog reported that Israeli forces conducted a pattern of killing entire families by targeting the homes they were sheltering in.[^cite_note-57] An entire family, including both parents and four sons, were shot dead by the IDF in December 2023.[^cite_note-58]

According to the Gaza Health Ministry, at least 50 people were killed by Israeli strikes on Jabalia on 31 October and 1 November.[^cite_note-59] Significant civilian casualties were reported following the [Al-Ahli Arab Hospital explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ahli_Arab_Hospital_explosion), the [Jabalia camp airstrike on 31 October](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31_October_2023_Jabalia_refugee_camp_airstrike), and the [Fakhoora school airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Fakhoora_school_airstrikes) on 4 November. Other mass casualty events included the [Church of Saint Porphyrius airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Porphyrius_airstrike) and the [al-Shati refugee camp airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shati_refugee_camp_airstrike), as well as numerous attacks on [refugee camps](75393092.md), [schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attacks_on_schools_during_the_Gaza_war), and [healthcare facilities](75314000.md). The United Nations stated they had recorded seven mass casualty incidents just between 24 and 29 October 2024 in the Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-60]

By late-June 2024, a Palestinian NGO reported that as many as 10,000 Palestinians had been disabled by injuries related to the war.[^cite_note-61] According to public health experts, such as chair of global public health at the [University of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Edinburgh) [Devi Sridhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Sridhar), the death tolls in the Gaza Strip are likely an undercount.[^cite_note-theguardian20240905-62]

In September 2025, *The Guardian* citing data from independent conflict tracker [ACLED](43040226.md), that is backed by western governments and the UN, reported a vast majority of Palestinians killed were civilians since Israel's renewed offensive earlier in March. Over a six-month period, ACLED had tracked reports on losses inflicted on Hamas and allied armed groups from "reliable local and international media", Israeli military statements and statements from Hamas and found that Israel's claim of combatants killed had exceeded what could be independently verified. ACLED report wrote, "Since 18 March, Israel claims it killed more than 2,100 operatives, though Acled data indicates that the number is closer to 1,100, and includes Hamas’ political figures, as well as fighters from other groups".[^cite_note-63] Earlier in August 2025, *The Guardian* cited a classified Israeli military internal report, noting that, if the data was accurate, approximately 83% of Palestinians killed would have been civilians. This ratio of civilian to combatants among dead, is considered unusually high for modern warfare even when compared with conflicts like Sudanese and Syrian Civil War, renown for their indiscriminate killings. The Israeli military had rejected the analysis, arguing that militant deaths may have been undercounted, but did not release alternative casualty figures or a detailed methodology to support its position.[^cite_note-Revealed-64][^cite_note-\:0-65]

Investigative reports by *[+972 Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/+972_Magazine)* and *[Local Call](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Call)*, as well as reporting by *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)*, indicates that the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF) have, at times, misreported or classified a high number of civilian deaths as "militants". For example, *+972* and *Local Call* reported that an Israeli battalion stationed in [Rafah](584373.md) recorded around 100 Palestinians as militants; however an officer in that battalion later stated that only two of the 100 Palestinians were armed. In a separate case, *Haaretz* reported that an IDF spokesman stated that all 200 Palestinians who had been killed by the [252nd Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/252nd_Division_\(Israel\)) in the [Netzarim Corridor](76319177.md) were "terrorists", but only 10 of the 200 individuals were subsequently verified as Hamas operatives.[^cite_note-\:0-65] In April 2024, members of [Israeli Knesset's Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_and_Defense_Committee) had expressed doubt on the figures given to them by the army and later found the army had inflated the militant casualties "in order to create a 2\:1 ratio" of civilian to militant deaths. An Israeli intelligence source told *The Guardian, +972,* and *Local Call* that they had used figures based primarily on officer testimonies, which likely overcounted Hamas deaths. In contrast, the figures in Israel's internal intelligence database are the only numbers that the army could rely on with "a high degree of certainty" as it uses person-by-person analysis; however, this method may also undercount the number of Hamas deaths.[^cite_note-\:0-65][^cite_note-66]

#### Demographics

In November 2025, a study by the [Max Planck Institute for Demographic Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck_Institute_for_Demographic_Research) estimated that the total number of violent deaths in Gaza was between 100,000 and 126,000, of which 27% were children under 15 years old, and 24% women (together making up 51% of the total).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

In early September 2025, total casualties exceeded 64,231, with children accounting for at least 30%, and women about 16% (together, 46%).[^cite_note-hums42-70] There were 2,596 children who had lost both parents. Furthermore, 53,724 children had lost one parent; 47,804 their father and 5,920 their mother.[^cite_note-nofamn-71] These September numbers were all cited from the [Gaza Health Ministry](52186562.md).[^cite_note-hums42-70][^cite_note-nofamn-71]

In April 2024, about half a year since the war started, it was initially reported that children comprised 44% and women 26% (making up 70% together).[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] In May 2024, the United Nations adjusted its estimate of the proportion of women and children among those killed in Gaza to 52% – reporting that 32% were children, 20% women – 40% men, and 8% elderly.[^cite_note-unrevises-74][^cite_note-75]

Two and a half months into the war, [OCHA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OCHA) estimated a total of 20,000 casualties. More than 40% were children, over 31% were women (71% together).[^cite_note-76] In March 2024, five months into the war, the casualties consisted of an estimated 43% children and 29% women (72% together).[^cite_note-77] Another source later that same month reported that children made up 31.2% of the fatalities, and among adults, including the elderly, 25.3% were women and 43.5% were men.[^cite_note-78]

### Children

See also: [Effect of the Gaza war on children in the Gaza Strip § Casualties](75987631.md#Casualties)



Wounded children wait for treatment at the overcrowded emergency ward of [Al-Shifa hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shifa_hospital) in Gaza City

By 25 October 2023, when over 1000 children had been killed, Qatar's Foreign Minister [Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani](49695374.md) criticized the international community for "acting as if the lives of Palestinian children do not count".[^cite_note-79] In a statement, [UNICEF](19698110.md) regional director Adele Khodr stated Gaza's child death toll was a "growing stain on our collective conscience".[^cite_note-80] On 28 October 2023, the number of families who had been killed entirely had risen to 825.[^cite_note-81] On 30 October 2023, [Save the Children](436885.md) reported more children had died in three weeks in Gaza than in the entire sum of conflicts around the world in the past four years.[^cite_note-82] UNRWA Commissioner-General [Philippe Lazzarini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Lazzarini) briefed the UN Security Council, sharing Save the Children's analysis.[^cite_note-83] By the end of October 2023 over 3000 children had been killed.[^cite_note-84] The death of [Hind Rajab](76056228.md) drew significant media coverage following the release of her emergency call and her subsequent disappearance for 12 days.[^cite_note-85]

On 29 February 2024, Gaza's Ministry of Health reported that 44% (i.e. over 13,000) of the fatalities were children.[^cite_note-bbccasualties-86] This disproportionate amount is mainly a result of the strip's very young population, of which 40% are below 14 years of age.[^cite_note-87]

On 8 May 2024, the UN officially revised previous numbers regarding the breakdown of casualties following more formal investigations, the total number killed remaining the same. Only considering identifiable casualties, the proportion of children killed in Gaza was reported as 31.6% or 7797 identified children casualties out of 24,686 identified bodies.[^cite_note-unrevises-74][^cite_note-NPR-revised-88] A joint report by [Oxfam](19698439.md) and [Action on Armed Violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_on_Armed_Violence) in October 2024 found the Israeli military had killed more women and children in Gaza than in any other conflict around the world in the past two decades.[^cite_note-89]

The Gaza Ministry of Health casualty numbers do not provide the proportion of casualties who are civilian; as a result, varying estimates have been given by analysts. A study by the [London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Hygiene_and_Tropical_Medicine) in *[The Lancet](463899.md)* covering the period 7–26 October estimated 68.1% of casualties were children, women or elders and therefore likely non-combatants,[^cite_note-102] while an analysis published in December in *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)* by Israeli sociologist Yagil Levy estimated at least 61% of the casualties were in this category.[^cite_note-Levy_2023_h080-103][^cite_note-Borger_2023_r615-104] Both studies were based on figures from Gaza's Ministry of Health. Considering only women, children and elderly as civilians (i.e. classifying all adult men as combatants) gives a conservative figure for civilians, although the true proportion of civilians is likely higher.[^cite_note-ga-105] In early December, [Euro-Mediterranean Human Rights Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro-Mediterranean_Human_Rights_Monitor) estimated that 90% of the casualties were civilians.[^cite_note-Monitor_2023_z733-100][^cite_note-106] In December, Israel's military said it estimated two out of three (66%) of those killed to be civilians.[^cite_note-McCluskey_Greene_2023_f197-107][^cite_note-Bachner_Fabian_2023_o675-108] On 30 May, professor Adam Gaffney of [Harvard Medical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Medical_School) estimated civilians constituted 80% of total killed.[^cite_note-thenation20240530-9] On 2 August, professor [Michael Spagat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Spagat) also estimated that roughly 80% of GHM recorded deaths constituted civilians.[^cite_note-aoav20240802-8]

Even the conservative figure of 61% is higher than the average civilian death rate in all world conflicts "from the Second World War to the 1990s", according to Yigal Levy.[^cite_note-Borger_2023_r615-104] The number of casualties is higher than in any conflict in Gaza's recent history, with [Neta Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neta_Crawford) of the [Costs of War Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costs_of_War_Project) at [Brown University](4157.md) stating, "This is, in the 21st century, a significant and out-of-the-norm level of destruction".[^cite_note-109] On 31 December, *[Al Jazeera English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English)* stated 2023 was the deadliest year for Palestinians since the 1948 [Nakba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakba).[^cite_note-110]

In late April 2024 Khalil al-Hayya, a top Hamas official, said that no more than 20% of Hamas's fighters had been killed.[^cite_note-111]

US intelligence estimated in June 2024 that between 11,000 and 13,000 militants have been killed.[^cite_note-112]

#### Israeli military reports

In early December 2023, an Israeli official reported Israel had killed 5,000 militants,[^cite_note-Bachner_Fabian_2023_o675-108][^cite_note-McCluskey_Greene_2023_f197-107] but provided no evidence to support the report.[^cite_note-aljazeera20231222-95] On 29 December, the IDF said it had killed 8,000 Hamas fighters.[^cite_note-Sky_News_20231229-113][^cite_note-bbccasualties-86] On 30 December 2023, [Euro-Mediterranean Human Rights Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro-Mediterranean_Human_Rights_Monitor) estimated 2,353 militant deaths (based 30,034 total and 27,681 civilian deaths).[^cite_note-114] On 19 February 2024, one Hamas official told Reuters 6,000 of its militants had been killed,[^cite_note-115] but a second Hamas official denied this figure in an interview with BBC.[^cite_note-bbccasualties-86] Hamas also put out an official statement denying the 6,000 figure. The same day, the IDF said it had killed 12,000 militants up to that point.[^cite_note-Fabian_2024_h142-116] The IDF did not confirm that number to the BBC, but in two separate responses, said the figure is "approximately 10,000" and "more than 10,000", with the Israeli embassy in London giving a similar figure.[^cite_note-bbccasualties-86] On 7 April, the IDF said that more than 13,000 operative of Hamas and its allies had been killed by Israel in Gaza in the fighting (although an IDF press release gave 12,000+), in addition to the 1,000 killed on 7 October. The IDF said these deaths included five Hamas brigade commanders or equivalent, 20 battalion commanders, and over 100 company commanders or equivalent.[^cite_note-Fabian_2024_z726-117][^cite_note-Eglash_2024_j647-118][^cite_note-IDF_2024_k713-119] On 12 May Netanyahu said about 14,000 militants and 16,000 civilians had been killed in Gaza – that about 47% of the deaths were of militants.[^cite_note-Netanyahu_16000-92] On 15 August 2024 the IDF said more than 17,000 militants had been killed.[^cite_note-IDF_17000-120]

The AP, CNN, Washington Post, NBC and others have all said that the IDF has provided no evidence for its reports of how many militants it has killed, or been unable to verify what the IDF has said.[^cite_note-121]

Sources have doubted the Israeli figures by questioning the IDF's ability to distinguish civilians from combatants[^cite_note-lorientlejour20240306-96][^cite_note-bbc20240229-94] (for example, [the IDF mistakenly identified three Israeli civilians as Palestinian militants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Alon_Shamriz,_Yotam_Haim,_and_Samer_Talalka)).[^cite_note-122] Many observers believe Israel simply treats all adult male casualties as militants.[^cite_note-thenationalnews20240326-93][^cite_note-ga-105][^cite_note-lemonde20231218-97] Other observers argue that Israel could be arriving at inflated figures of militant deaths by including all civil servants as militants.[^cite_note-lorientlejour20240306-96]

BBC Verify repeatedly asked IDF for its methodology on counting militant deaths, but the IDF never responded.[^cite_note-bbccasualties-86] BBC Verify attempted to count militant deaths by compiling all announcements of militant deaths on the IDF's official telegram channel; it found the IDF had made 160 such announcements, summing up to 714 total militant deaths in the Gaza Strip (as of 29 February), however 247 references used terms such as “several”, “dozens”, or “hundreds” which made a meaningful tally impossible.[^cite_note-lorientlejour20240306-96][^cite_note-bbccasualties-86] BBC Verify also viewed all 280 videos posted on the IDF's [YouTube](3524766.md) channel of Gaza operations, and found that only one of those videos actually showed dead bodies of Palestinian militants.[^cite_note-lorientlejour20240306-96]

An analysis by Zoran Kusovac in Al-Jazeera in December 2023, said that IDF's own numbers imply 62 Hamas fighters killed for every Israeli soldier killed in Gaza operations. Kusovac argued that if only half the militants were killed in combat, a constant [loss exchange ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loss_exchange_ratio) of 31\:1 over many months would be so [demoralizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morale) that Hamas fighters would rout, yet as Hamas continues to fight, its losses must be smaller than reported.[^cite_note-aljazeera20231209-98]

In March 2024, *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)* interviewed several standing army commanders and reserve commanders who cast doubt on Israel's official figures of how many "terrorists" it had killed.[^cite_note-haaretz20240331-123] Many of those included in the combatant killed count were simply "Palestinians who never held a gun in their lives".[^cite_note-haaretz20240331-123]

In January 2025, [The Jerusalem Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jerusalem_Post) said the "numbers don't add up" regarding initial Israeli estimates of Hamas' forces, unless Hamas has fully replaced its initial fighting force.[^cite_note-jpost-124] In October 2023, the IDF had estimated Hamas 25,000 fighters; in 2024 the IDF said it had killed 17–20,000 fighters, wounding another 14–16,000 and capturing 6,000. Yet in January, it said there were still 12–23,000 fighters left. To reconcile the discrepancy, the IDF revised its October 2023 estimate to 40,000 and said Hamas must have recruited thousands more.[^cite_note-jpost-124]

### Impacts



Palestinian man with body bags in [Jabalia refugee camp](18169472.md)

On 13 October, the Palestinian Ministry of Health noted 20 [surnames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surname) had been removed from Gaza's [civil registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_registry), meaning every single person in that entire family had been killed.[^cite_note-125] *[The New York Times](30680.md)* stated, "Family trees have been dismembered, and whole branches obliterated."[^cite_note-126] An [Associated Press](18935732.md) investigation found 60 Palestinian families where at least 25 people were killed between October and December 2023, sometimes across four generations.[^cite_note-127]

On 16 October 2023, UNRWA stated there were so many deaths in Gaza that there were no longer enough [body bags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_bags).[^cite_note-128] Because the morgues were so overcrowded, bodies were kept in [ice cream trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_cream_trucks).[^cite_note-129] On 11 November 2023, Monir al-Bashr, the director of the Health Ministry of Gaza, stated graves were being dug by hand.[^cite_note-130] On 12 November 2023, [Mai al-Kaila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mai_al-Kaila) noted staff at Al-Shifa were unable to bury 100 decomposing bodies.[^cite_note-131] On 14 November 2023, the [Palestinian Red Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Red_Crescent) noted it was unable to rescue the wounded and injured beneath the rubble, noting, "Those injured are left there in agony to suffer and die with no response to their calls of help."[^cite_note-132] By August 2024, the dead were being buried in the streets and house yards, as well as in [mass graves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip_mass_graves).[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-newarab.com-134]

At the end of January 2024 *BBC* reported that based on a recent report from the [Euro-Mediterranean Human Rights Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro-Mediterranean_Human_Rights_Monitor) more than 24,000 children have lost one or both parents due to the war. The United Nations agency [UNICEF](19698110.md) have estimated that there are about 19,000 orphaned or unaccompanied children in Gaza, with some being dug out of rubble or found throughout the strip.[^cite_note-135]

The [Gaza war](74998519.md) has led to imminent [famine](51329.md) conditions in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), resulting from [Israeli airstrikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Gaza) and the ongoing blockade of the Gaza Strip by Israel, which includes restrictions on [humanitarian aid](76066918.md).[^cite_note-We_are_about_to_witness-139][^cite_note-140][^cite_note-un20240318-141] By February 2024, 2.2 million people in Gaza were experiencing food insecurity at emergency level.[^cite_note-142]

Airstrikes have destroyed food infrastructure, such as bakeries, mills, and food stores, and there is a widespread scarcity of essential supplies due to the blockade of [aid](434679.md).[^cite_note-144] By March 2024, this had caused starvation for more than half a million Gazans and is part of a broader [humanitarian crisis](75277791.md) in the Strip. It is the "highest number of people facing catastrophic hunger" ever recorded on the [IPC scale](16431152.md),[^cite_note-un20240318-141] and is widely expected to be the most intense man-made famine since the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War).[^cite_note-We_are_about_to_witness-139][^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

In March 2024, the [Integrated Food Security Phase Classification](16431152.md) (IPC) aid partnership said that the residents of Gaza are perishing due to starvation. The alarming pace at which this crisis of hunger and malnutrition, caused by humans, is spreading in Gaza is deeply concerning. Half of Gaza's entire population is currently facing catastrophic conditions and is on the brink of famine, a situation that has never been seen before. Approximately 1.1 million individuals in Gaza – half its population – were grappling with severe hunger.[^cite_note-s619-147] By October 2024, the IPC and World Food Programme reported that more than 1.8 million Palestinians in Gaza were experiencing "extremely critical" levels of hunger, resulting from 70% of crop fields destroyed and livelihoods decimated during Israel's offensive.[^cite_note-w735-148]



Four-year-old Palestinian girl who died from [malnutrition](258979.md) and lack of treatment, August 2024

A letter sent to President [Joe Biden](145422.md), Vice President [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris), and others on 2 October 2024 by 99 American healthcare workers who have served in the Gaza Strip since 7 October 2023, using the [Integrated Food Security Phase Classification](16431152.md) standards and the available data on the severity of food shortage in different parts of Gaza, calculated that there had been at the very least 62,413 deaths in Gaza from starvation (most of them young children) and at least 5,000 deaths from lack of access to care for chronic diseases.[^cite_note-MotherJonesTotalDeaths-149][^cite_note-GazaHealthcareLettersEstimatedDeaths-150] This estimate was included in the estimate for indirect deaths in the war in a study from the [Watson Institute for International and Public Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watson_Institute_for_International_and_Public_Affairs) at [Brown University](4157.md).[^cite_note-WatsonInstituteEstimatedDeaths-151] According to [UNRWA](32270.md), in the four months to mid December 2024, some 19,000 children were treated for acute malnutrition.[^cite_note-152]

As of August 2025, [Integrated Food Security Phase Classification](16431152.md) (IPC) projections show 100% of the population are experiencing "high levels of acute food insecurity", and 32% are projected to face Phase 5 catastrophic levels by 30 September 2025.[^cite_note-IPC_22_August_2025-153] On 22 August 2025, the IPC said that famine is taking place in one of the five [governorates](5811262.md) in the Gaza Strip: specifically, the [Gaza Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Governorate) which includes [Gaza City](55387.md). The IPC added that, within the next month, famine was likely to also occur in both the [Deir al-Balah Governorate](14704596.md) and [Khan Yunis Governorate](13900254.md). The IPC had insufficient data on the [North Gaza Governorate](8689663.md) for a classification but concluded that conditions were likely similar or worse than in the Gaza Governorate.[^cite_note-IPC_22_August_2025-153][^cite_note-reuters20250822-154] Within the next 6 weeks as of 16 August, the number of people in IPC Phase 5 is expected to rise from 500,000 to over 640,000.[^cite_note-155]

### Death toll



[UN OCHA](1602284.md) casualties summary, as of 19 June 2024

On 25 October 2023, US President [Joe Biden](145422.md) stated he had "no confidence" in the death totals reported by the Gaza Health Ministry.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-Gaza_death_toll-157][^cite_note-158] In response, [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) stated that after three decades working in Gaza and conducting its own investigation, it considers Gaza Health Ministry's totals to be reliable.[^cite_note-Gaza_death_toll-157] [Matthew Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Miller_\(spokesperson\)) made a similar claim to Biden's, despite the fact that the [US Department of State](31975.md) cites the Gaza Health Ministry's death tolls in its own internal reports.[^cite_note-159] On 26 October, the Gaza Health Ministry responded by releasing a 212-page document of 6,747 individual names and [ID numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identifier), as well as 281 unidentified fatalities.[^cite_note-160] The US State Department Assistant Secretary of State for Near Eastern Affairs told a Congressional hearing on 9 November that the death toll was "very high, frankly, and it could be that they're even higher than are being cited."[^cite_note-161] The [Biden administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biden_administration) ultimately changed to faith in the health ministry statistics.[^cite_note-GHM_deniers-162] The GHM's casualty count was also initially disputed by the [Trump administration](78298108.md) but was later cited by [President Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Trump).[^cite_note-163]

Every death registered in Gaza is the result of a verified change in the [population registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_registration) approved by the [Government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinator_of_Government_Activities_in_the_Territories).[^cite_note-164] The Israeli government notes that its "Population Registry Office works to update population registry files located on the Israeli side to match the files that are held" in the West Bank and Gaza.[^cite_note-165] On 26 October, the [United Nations humanitarian office](1602284.md) added they use the Gaza Ministry of Health's death totals because they are "clearly sourced"[^cite_note-166] and their estimates have been described as trustworthy by the [World Health Organization](33583.md)'s (WHO) regional emergency director Richard Brennan.[^cite_note-bbccasualties-86] On 6 December, a peer-reviewed article by [Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_Bloomberg_School_of_Public_Health) scholars in *[The Lancet](463899.md)* concluded the Gaza Ministry of Health's death tolls were accurate.[^cite_note-167] In May 2024, *The Economist* argued that the Ministry of Health's death tolls were "legitimate" and marked the lower bound of lives lost.[^cite_note-168]

Around mid-November, the Gaza Health Ministry had begun to lose count of deaths stating that it struggled to update casualty tolls as a result of blackouts, high death tolls, and the [destruction of the healthcare system](75314000.md).[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170] On 6 January 2024, the Gaza Health Ministry requested that civilians register their dead online, as the [healthcare system collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Gaza_Strip_healthcare_collapse) had resulted in the ministry being unable to maintain a regularly updated death toll.[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172] In February 2024, a joint study by the [London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Hygiene_and_Tropical_Medicine) and the Johns Hopkins Center for Humanitarian Health at [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) found the war continuing at status quo would result in between 58,260 and 74,290 excess deaths by 6 August.[^cite_note-173]



Photos of Palestinian healthcare workers killed in the Gaza war, 25 November 2023

As of 29 February, the Gaza Health Ministry stated that its daily tallies now rely upon "a combination of accurate death counts from hospitals that are still partially operating, and on estimates from media reports to assess deaths in the north of Gaza", but did not "cite or say which sources those are."[^cite_note-174] On 31 March, it stated that 15,070 fatalities (45.8% of the then total) had been compiled via "reliable media sources" instead of direct reporting.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176] The Ministry further clarified in reports made on 1 and 4 April that it had "incomplete data" for 12,263 (later reduced 11,371) of its 33,091 reported fatalities.[^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178]

On 25 June, Euro-Mediterranean Human Rights Monitor estimated 51,000 extra natural (non-casualty) deaths have resulted from the blockade and from the collapse of the health system.[^cite_note-179]

On 16 September 2024, the Gaza health ministry released another 649-page document, listing the names of 34,344 Palestinians who had been killed and identified by 31 August. The first 187 pages of the document entirely contained children who were under 16 years old.[^cite_note-180]

On 23 March 2025, after two months of collecting bodies during the ceasefire, the Gaza health ministry published a third document 1,516 pages long, listing the names of 50,021 Palestinians who had been killed. The first 350 pages of the document entirely contained children who were under 16 years old.[^cite_note-181]

In January 2026, Israeli sources reported that, for the first time, an Israeli military official accepted figures released by Gaza's health ministry, showing that more than 71,000 Palestinians were killed since October 2023 by direct Israeli fire, not by other causes such starvation and those buried under the rubble. The official stated that the IDF is currently analysing the data to calculate the number of people who were killed are combatants and civilians.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-ToI_70K-183]

### Journalists in Gaza

Main article: [Killing of journalists in the Gaza war](75146557.md)

See also: [List of journalists killed in the Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_journalists_killed_in_the_Gaza_war)



Al Jazeera's correspondent [Hossam Shabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossam_Shabat) was killed in March 2025. The IDF confirmed that it had deliberately targeted Shabat.

Numerous Palestinian journalists in Gaza were killed by Israeli attacks while in the line of duty. Ibrahim Mohammad Lafi, a photographer for Ain Media, was fatally shot during the attack on the Erez crossing on 7 October, while Mohammad Jarghoun, a reporter with Smart Media, was killed east of Rafah on the same day. Freelance journalist Mohammad el-Salhi was also shot dead on the border east of [Bureij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureij) refugee camp on 7 October. On 9 October, Saeed al-Taweel, editor-in-chief of Al-Khamsa News website, Mohammed Subh and Hisham Alnwajha were killed by an airstrike while filming an anticipated attack in [Gaza City](55387.md).

On 10 October, two additional journalists were reported missing, and another was injured by shrapnel. The homes of two journalists were destroyed by shelling, and the offices of four media outlets were destroyed by airstrikes.[^cite_note-journo-184] On 22 October, Rushdi Sarraj was killed by an Israeli airstrike on his home.[^cite_note-185] On 24 October, reporter [Wael Al-Dahdouh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wael_Al-Dahdouh) lost his entire family due to an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-186] On 27 October, the IDF told [Reuters](18998750.md) and [Agence France Presse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agence_France_Presse) it would not guarantee their journalists' safety in Gaza.[^cite_note-187] On 30 October, Al Jazeera correspondent [Youmna El-Sayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youmna_El_Sayed) received a threat from Israeli forces, leading the spokesperson for the UN-Secretary General to remark on the "immense courage" of journalists in Gaza.[^cite_note-188] On 2 November, Mohammed Abu Hatab and 10 members of his family were killed by an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-189]

On 19 October, the committee to Protect Journalists stated 21 journalists were confirmed dead, eight were injured, and three were missing or detained.[^cite_note-190] A 29 October report by [Reporters Without Borders](526054.md) (RSF) said that Israel had targeted journalists who were clearly identifiable as press, in two 13 October missile strikes that killed a reporter and injured four.[^cite_note-191] On 31 October, RSF said that 34 journalists had been killed to date in the conflict, including 12 "in connection with their work", ten of whom were killed in Israel's attack on Gaza; they described the first two weeks of the conflict as the deadliest start of a war of the 21st century for journalists.[^cite_note-192] On 7 November, an Israeli airstrike killed journalist Mohammad Abu Hasira and 42 of his family members.[^cite_note-193] On 23 November, photojournalist Mohammad Moin Ayyash and his family were killed by an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-194]

#### Investigations

On 1 November, Reporters Without Borders asked the [International Criminal Court](14880.md) to begin a priority war crimes investigation into the killing of nine journalists.[^cite_note-195] RSF noted 41 journalists had been killed during the first month of the conflict, stating multiple journalists had been killed by Israel in their homes.[^cite_note-196] Israel maintains records of the place and residence of every person in Gaza.[^cite_note-197] RSF stated Israel had used targeted strikes to kill journalists in Gaza.[^cite_note-198]

We did what we could. Remember us."[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216] On 24 December, a [UNDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNDP) worker was killed in an airstrike, along with 70 members of his family.[^cite_note-217]

### Missing and buried

This section is an excerpt from [Israeli bombing of the Gaza Strip § Missing persons](76139149.md#Missing_persons).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Israeli_bombing_of_the_Gaza_Strip\&action=edit)]



Rescue teams searching for survivors under rubble, October 2023

On 11 February 2026, roughly 8,000 people were reported missing beneath rubble.[^cite_note-218] On 27 October 2023, the [World Health Organization](33583.md) stated more than 1,000 unidentified people were buried under rubble.[^cite_note-219] On 3 December 2023, the [Palestinian Civil Defence](49984885.md) stated the situation "beyond dire" as the organization was unable to rescue many people buried under rubble.[^cite_note-220]

Individuals were rescued by aid workers after reportedly surviving several days buried underneath rubble.[^cite_note-221] Emergency responders stated that part of what made rescue so difficult is that Israeli bombs tend to "flatten entire buildings".[^cite_note-222] On 24 February 2024, Dr. [Paul B. Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_B._Spiegel) stated that total death counts were undercounts due to the large number of people under rubble, stating, "We projected the number of deaths that may be missing, and it was probably up to about ten to fifteen per cent more."[^cite_note-223] On 26 February 2024, Israeli warplanes bombed and destroyed an emergency rescue machinery in [Beit Lahia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Lahia).[^cite_note-224]

According to *The New York Times*, "The buried make up a shadow death toll in Gaza, a leaden asterisk to the health ministry's official tally of more than 31,000 dead".[^cite_note-225] In May 2024, a U.S. doctor described the deterioration of rescue operations in Gaza, stating, "We hear bombs and before my thought used to be 'what patients are we going to meet tomorrow?' And now we hear bombs and no one comes."[^cite_note-226] The United Nations stated that more than 10,000 people were estimated to be buried under the rubble.[^cite_note-227] Dr. Marwan al-Hams stated the number was so high because of the lack of heavy equipment or fuel to dig through concrete and steel to rescue them.[^cite_note-228]

In late-June 2024 it was reported by [Save the Children](436885.md) that up to 21,000 children are estimated to be missing due to the bombing and the ongoing war. While the organization acknowledged that it was difficult to collect and verify information in Gaza, it was believed that about 17,000 children were unaccompanied and separated, about 4,000 likely missing in the rubble and an unknown number in mass graves.[^cite_note-229] In July 2024, emergency crews stated there were many people trapped under debris in the [Tel al-Hawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_al-Hawa) neighborhood following heavy attacks by Israel.[^cite_note-230]

Saadi Hassan Sulieman Baraka, a 64-year-old undertaker in [Deir el-Balah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_el-Balah), told [Al Jazeera English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English) in February 2024 that he had personally buried 17,000 people since October 2023.[^cite_note-231] By August 2024, the dead were buried throughout the Gaza Strip, including in public spaces such as yards and streets.[^cite_note-232] [Scavenging animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scavenger) have made it difficult to for emergency services to identify the bodies of killed Palestinians.[^cite_note-233] In an October 2024 article, a returning IDF troop spoke about IDF procedures involving [armored bull dozers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IDF_Caterpillar_D9), that have made it more difficult in identifying and recovering bodies of deceased Palestinians. A former IDF soldier recounted to the Knesset that soldiers were instructed to "run over terrorists (in Gaza), dead and alive, in the hundreds" adding that "everything squirts out".[^cite_note-cnn20241021-234]

The casualties include approximately 70 dead or missing [Arab-Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab-Israeli) citizens, many of whom are [Negev Bedouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negev_Bedouin).[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252] 14 Israeli children under 10 and 36 adolescents aged between 10 and 19 were initially believed to have been killed in the 7 October attack.[^cite_note-toi_casulties-237]



Collection and identification of remains after the [Kfar Aza massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kfar_Aza_massacre) on 15 October 2023

On 7 October, there were massacres at more than nine [kibbutzim](106200.md) where civilians resided and at an outdoor dance music festival. Over 260 attendees were killed at the [psychedelic trance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_trance) open-air ["Supernova Sukkot Gathering"](75007485.md) music festival near the [Re'im](17756866.md) kibbutz. It became the deadliest concert attack ever and the worst Israeli civilian massacre in its history.[^cite_note-RS-Browne-253] Over 100 civilians were killed in the [Be'eri massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be'eri_massacre), including children. Many of the kibbutz residents among the dead or missing were peace activists,[^cite_note-NYT-Rabin-254] such as the 74-year-old [Vivian Silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivian_Silver), a former board member of the human rights organisation [B'Tselem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B'Tselem).[^cite_note-255] At least 50–100 people have been reported killed in the [Kfar Aza massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kfar_Aza_massacre), with the total death toll unknown.[^cite_note-The_Times_of_Israel-256] Filmmaker [Yahav Winner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahav_Winner) was killed in Kfar Aza. Many civilians were also killed in the [Nahal Oz attack](75068288.md). Nine people were fatally shot at a bus shelter in Sderot,[^cite_note-ap1-257] and at least 14 were killed by gunfire and grenades at a roadside bomb shelter near Re'im.[^cite_note-Kershner_2023-11-11-258] At least four people were reported killed in [Kuseife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuseife).[^cite_note-aj7oct-204] At least 400 casualties were reported in Ashkelon,[^cite_note-259][^cite_note-cnn1-260] while 280 others were reported in Beer Sheva, 60 of whom were in serious condition.[^cite_note-ap1-257] In the north, injuries from rocket attacks were reported in Tel Aviv.[^cite_note-261]

Former [Hapoel Tel Aviv F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hapoel_Tel_Aviv_F.C.) striker [Lior Asulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lior_Asulin) was among those killed in the Re'im music festival massacre.[^cite_note-262] The head of the [Sha'ar HaNegev Regional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sha'ar_HaNegev_Regional_Council), [Ofir Libstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofir_Libstein), was killed in an exchange of fire with the militants.[^cite_note-263] The police commander of [Rahat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahat), [Jayar Davidov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayar_Davidov), was also killed.[^cite_note-264] [Izhar Peled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izhar_Peled), a police officer, was killed in Kfar Aza. The IDF confirmed that 258 of its soldiers had been killed.[^cite_note-idf258-265] Among their confirmed dead were Colonel [Yonatan Steinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonatan_Steinberg), the commander of the [Nahal Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahal_Brigade), who was killed near [Kerem Shalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerem_Shalom); Colonel [Roi Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roi_Levy), commander of the [Refaim United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refaim_Unit), who was killed near Kibbutz Re'im;[^cite_note-266][^cite_note-267] and Lieutenant Colonel [Eli Ginsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Ginsberg), commander of the [LOTAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LOTAR) Counter-terrorism Unit School.[^cite_note-268] The [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze_in_Israel) deputy commander of the 300th "Baram" Regional Brigade, Lieutenant Colonel [Alim Abdallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alim_Abdallah), was killed in action along with two other soldiers while responding to an infiltration from southern Lebanon on 9 October.[^cite_note-alim-269] Israeli peace activist [Hayim Katsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayim_Katsman) was killed in Holit.

### Invasion of Gaza

It was reported in April 2024 that since 27 October 2023, when Israel invaded the Gaza Strip, 260 Israeli soldiers had been killed, mainly during ground operations.[^cite_note-Aderet_2024_i232-270] On 15 June 2024 it was reported that eight IDF troops were killed while returning from an overnight operation near the [Tal al-Sultan refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_al-Sultan_refugee_camp) in [Rafah](584373.md) during the ongoing [offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafah_offensive). Per a statement, Hamas had fired a rocket at an [Israeli army bulldozer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armored_bulldozer) and fired a second one as reinforcements arrived, reportedly causing the casualties.[^cite_note-271][^cite_note-272]

The IDF said in July 2024 that a large number of tanks had been incapacitated in the course of the war, limiting the number of tanks available for training exercises.[^cite_note-273] On 4 November, Hamas claimed that since 2 November they had destroyed 24 Israeli vehicles, including a tank, an APC, and a bulldozer with anti-armour weapons.[^cite_note-274]

In September 2025, the [Israeli Ministry of Defense](3226446.md)'s rehabilitation department reported that around 20,000 soldiers were treated since 7 October 2023, with around 1,000 being hospitalized per month. Around 45% had physical injuries, with 9% in moderate-to-severe condition, 35% experienced mental health conditions, and 20% experienced both physical wounds and mental health issues.[^cite_note-275]

#### Mental health

In January 2024, it was reported that at least 1,600 IDF troops had been identified as showing symptoms of combat-related [PTSD](82974.md) since the start of the ground invasion of Gaza and about 250 soldiers were discharged due to prolonged combat stress symptoms.[^cite_note-276] According to IDF officials in February 2024, around 3,000 troops have been checked by the IDF's mental health system with 82% returning to active duty.[^cite_note-277] While the IDF has not officially reported how many IDF troops returning from fighting in Gaza have committed suicide there was a noticed increase a year after the war began, with many indicating that they were not given appropriate care by medical and mental health officials.[^cite_note-278][^cite_note-cnn20241021-234]

### Journalists in Israel

According to the [Committee to Protect Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_to_Protect_Journalists), two Israeli journalists, [Roee Idan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roee_Idan) and [Yaniv Zohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaniv_Zohar), have been killed.[^cite_note-279]

### Migrant workers

At least 50 migrant workers, primarily from Thailand and Nepal, were killed during Hamas' attack on 7 October and around 100,000 migrant workers are trapped in Israel during the conflict due to debt from large fees they had to pay to recruitment agencies to obtain the jobs.[^cite_note-280]

## West Bank

[OCHA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OCHA) reported that Israel killed 370 Palestinians in the West Bank between 7 October 2023 and 29 January 2024, including 94 children.[^cite_note-wb_casualties-281] 97% of these were killed by [Israeli security forces](315904.md) and the remainder by Israeli settlers.[^cite_note-wb_casualties-281]

Several thousand Gazan workers were in Israel at the time when the conflict started. As of 16 October some of them were detained at a "holding facility" in the West Bank while others sought refuge in the Palestinian communities of the West Bank.[^cite_note-282] The Minister of Labor for the Palestinian Authority estimated 4,500 workers are unaccounted for while Israeli media outlet N12 reported 4,000 Gazans were in Israeli holding facilities. The Palestinian Prisoners Society said that Israeli forces had [arrested](75234504.md) over 1,450 West Bank Palestinians since 7 October.[^cite_note-aj2-283] On 29 October 30 Israeli human rights organizations addressed [settler violence](47246246.md) in the West Bank, asking the international community to "act urgently" to end it.[^cite_note-284] On 30 October, the German government called on Israel to protect Palestinians in the West Bank.[^cite_note-285] On 31 October, EU chief diplomat [Josep Borrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josep_Borrell) "firmly condemned" settler attacks in the West Bank.[^cite_note-286] [Linda Thomas-Greenfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Thomas-Greenfield) stated the United States was "deeply concerned," and condemned the killings of Palestinians in the West Bank.[^cite_note-287]

### Further details

The Nepali ambassador to Israel, Kanta Rijal, said at least seven of its nationals in the country were injured in the attack, and that they along with ten others were held captive by Hamas at Kibbutz [Alumim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alumim).[^cite_note-theguardian20231020-307] The Nepalese embassy later confirmed that 10 Nepalese students were killed during the attack in the kibbutz.[^cite_note-WIONforeign-358] Israeli media also reported that migrant workers from [Thailand](30128.md) and the [Philippines](23440.md) were also taken captive by Palestinian militants.[^cite_note-ap1-257] The Philippine government confirmed that four Filipinos were killed[^cite_note-abs4-318] while two others were injured in the attacks, with authorities verifying reports of Filipinos being held captive by Hamas. 26 Filipinos were rescued by Israeli security forces,[^cite_note-359][^cite_note-360][^cite_note-361] while two Filipinos were unaccounted for.[^cite_note-abs4-318] At least 28 Thais were killed and 17 were captured by Hamas at Kibbutz Alumim. The reason for Hamas attacking the foreign workers' living quarters was because security guards successfully defended the main kibbutz residential area from invasion so they attacked softer targets. There were no guards stationed at the mostly Asian-inhabited living quarters.[^cite_note-362][^cite_note-363]

A German-Israeli national, [Shani Louk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shani_Louk), was killed while attending the Re'im music festival; a video of Palestinians parading her near-naked body in a car was circulated on the internet.[^cite_note-364][^cite_note-businessinsider20231008-365][^cite_note-zdf20231010-341][^cite_note-366] Several other German citizens were reported to be among those kidnapped by militants.[^cite_note-WIONforeign-358] At least 17 British citizens were reported as dead or missing,[^cite_note-McNamee-367] including one attendee of the music festival.[^cite_note-368][^cite_note-369] 18 Ukrainians,[^cite_note-kyiv-300] a Cambodian student,[^cite_note-phnom-338] and a Chilean woman were confirmed to have been killed by Hamas.[^cite_note-chile1-370][^cite_note-chile2-371] 13 French citizens were killed, with an additional 17 missing, including four children.[^cite_note-372]

## Hostages in Gaza

See also: [Gaza war hostage crisis](75025451.md)



Posters in Tel Aviv calling for the return of Israeli hostages in Gaza

248 people were [taken hostage during the](75025451.md) [October 7 attacks](74998317.md), mostly civilians.[^cite_note-NYT-Vinograd-379][^cite_note-Atlantic-Kidnapping-380][^cite_note-Guardian-Jones-381] On 8 October, [Palestinian Islamic Jihad](13093253.md) claimed to be holding at least 30 captives.[^cite_note-382] At least four people were reported taken from Kfar Aza.[^cite_note-ap3-383] Videos from Gaza appeared to show captured people, with Gazan residents cheering trucks carrying dead bodies.[^cite_note-nyt7oct-384] Israel reported four captives were killed in Be'eri,[^cite_note-385] while Hamas indicated that an IDF airstrike on Gaza on 9 October killed four captives.[^cite_note-386]

Civilians believed to be held captive in Gaza include families, children, festival-goers, peace activists, caregivers, and elders such as 75-year-old historian Alex Dancyg, who has written books on [Poland's Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Poland) and [The Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust), was taken from Nir Oz.[^cite_note-Guardian-Jones-381] Also at Nir Oz, six members of the [Silberman-Bibas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidnapping_and_killing_of_the_Bibas_family) family were caught on video being taken from their home;[^cite_note-387][^cite_note-388] on 11 October, Hamas released a video showing three of them being let go near the border fence.[^cite_note-389] On 16 October, Hamas released a video of one of its hostages, a 21-year-old French Israeli woman who had sustained injuries to her arm and a scar.[^cite_note-cnnhostage-390] On 20 October, Hamas released an American woman and her 17-year-old daughter who were taken while visiting relatives in Nahal Oz.[^cite_note-391][^cite_note-392] According to a report sent to the [International Committee of the Red Cross](5023832.md) by the Israeli organization [Hostages and Missing Families Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hostages_and_Missing_Families_Forum), hostages include people with [Parkinson's disease](22228064.md), [multiple sclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_sclerosis), [cardiovascular disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease), [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes), cancer, [dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dementia), [autism](29113700.md) and [psychiatric disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_disorder), who are "in urgent need of treatment and lifesaving medication", and are "prone to immediate mortality \[without] essential medications and treatment." The report also expressed concern about untreated injuries induced during the attack.[^cite_note-393][^cite_note-394]

An open letter published in *[The Lancet](463899.md)* by a group of 1,500 Israeli health-care professionals expressed shock at "the greatest loss of civilian life since the establishment of the state of Israel", and the indiscriminate "barbaric rampage" through "entire villages in the south of Israel", which it termed a "[crime against humanity](61099.md)". The letter called on the international medical community to "condemn the savage massacre, to immediately call for guarantees for the safety and health of all those being kept hostage, and to unequivocally call for the immediate and unconditional return of our families and friends who have been cruelly taken hostage".[^cite_note-395]

American-Israeli author Robby Berman set up a fund offering a reward of 1 million [Israeli shekels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_shekel) for the release of hostages in Gaza, specifically aimed at encouraging Palestinians to aid in the rescue of Jewish prisoners.[^cite_note-396]

## Palestinians imprisoned in Israel

Main article: [Mass detentions in the Gaza war](75234504.md)

Thousands of Palestinians working in Israel on the eve of the war have gone missing. Human rights groups believe they have subjected to [mass arrests](28079790.md) by Israel, but Israel has refused to release the names of those whom they are holding.[^cite_note-aj2-283] According to testimonies obtained by [HaMoked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HaMoked) and *Al-Jazeera* some of these prisoners have been beaten by Israeli soldiers and denied access to contact the Red Cross.[^cite_note-aj2-283] Eight of the workers were interviewed by [CNN](62028.md) made claims of torture, including being stripped naked, "viciously" beaten, including one account of electrocution. One prisoner reported; “They broke us and beat us with batons and metal sticks... they humiliated us... they have made us starve without food or water,” whilst another claimed "some people died on the way here because they were beaten and subjected to electric shocks." The interviewed workers were eventually returned to Gaza on 4 November. At least six human rights organizations in Israel have filed a petition to Israel's High Court arguing these detentions were "without legal authority and without legal grounds." Amani Sarahneh of Palestinian Prisoners Society and Dror Sadot of [B’Tselem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B'Tselem) both described the issue as systemic, with Sadot stating in response; “We've been investigating this for so many years – the military enforcement system works as a whitewash mechanism with almost no indictments,” she said. “So they will say ‘those are the exception, not the rule,’ but if the impunity for soldiers continues – and not just the soldiers but also the policy itself – when no one's being held accountable, of course, things will just continue,”[^cite_note-397]

## Reactions and analysis

Both Israel and Hamas have been accused of committing [war crimes](75113830.md).[^cite_note-398][^cite_note-399] South Africa accused Israel of committing [genocide](75668427.md) in a case it brought before the [International Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_v._Israel_\(Genocide_Convention\)).[^cite_note-400] In a set of preliminary rulings, the court found that the Palestinians' rights under the genocide convention were plausibly in danger but did not rule on whether genocide was plausibly happening.[^cite_note-401][^cite_note-402] In June 2024, a [UN Commission of Inquiry](69971286.md) found the scale of Israel's killing of Palestinians constituted a [crime against humanity](61099.md).[^cite_note-403] [Marwan Bishara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwan_Bishara), the senior political analyst at [Al Jazeera English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English), argued that Israel's military campaign aimed to "eliminate anything that walks or breathes in Gaza".[^cite_note-404] Hani Mahmoud, a journalist on the ground in Gaza, wrote, "It's difficult to imagine that the Israeli military has any clear objective other than the mass murder of civilians inside their homes."[^cite_note-405]

In August 2024, the UNRWA Chief of Staff, Ben Majekodunmi, stated that the reported killing of 40,000 people since October 2023 was an "unspeakable tragedy".[^cite_note-406] [Islamic Relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Relief), a UK-based humanitarian organization, stated that the killing of 40,000 should be a "source of eternal global shame".[^cite_note-407] UN rights chief [Volker Türk](71721726.md) stated, "This unimaginable situation is overwhelmingly due to recurring failures by the Israeli Defense Forces to comply with the rules of war".[^cite_note-408] UNRWA spokesperson Louise Wateridge stated, "It does feel like people are waiting for death. Death seems to be the only certainty in this situation".[^cite_note-409]

In July 2024, correspondence published in British medical journal *[The Lancet](463899.md)* issued a warning that casualty figures based on direct death tolls alone significantly underestimated the total, and conservatively estimated that the conflict up to June would in the coming months and years result in up to 186,000 deaths when direct and indirect deaths were accounted for.[^cite_note-LancetPII-410][^cite_note-thenation20240530-9] Jean-François Corty, a humanitarian doctor and president of the NGO *[Doctors of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9decins_du_Monde)*, said that the Gaza Health Ministry's figures take into account the identified dead, "without taking into account all the dead left under the rubble of the bombardments, or the indirect victims who died because of a [lack of care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care) or access to care, or from being transported to a health centre. If you add those who are likely to die of [malnutrition](258979.md) or as a result of [wounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wound) inflicted by Israeli bombardments in the weeks and months to come, because of the risks of [superinfection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superinfection) and because their pathology will be treated late, then yes, the figure of 186,000 deaths mentioned in The Lancet is credible."[^cite_note-411] The Lancet correspondence has been criticized by the Chair of Every Casualty Counts network [Michael Spagat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Spagat), who wrote that it "lacks a solid foundation and is implausible".[^cite_note-aoav-spagat-412]

In January 2025, a peer-reviewed analysis was published in *The Lancet* regarding mortality due to traumatic injury in the Gaza Strip between 7 October 2023 and 30 June 2024. The research was conducted by scholars from the [University of Cambridge](25978572.md), [Yale University](34273.md), [London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Hygiene_and_Tropical_Medicine) and [Nagasaki University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki_University). The paper utilized a model with "three-list capture–recapture analysis using data from Palestinian Ministry of Health (MoH) hospital lists, an MoH online survey, and social media obituaries", and estimated 64,260 deaths from traumatic injury during this period, likely exceeding 70,000 by October 2024, with 59.1% of them being women, children and the elderly. It concluded the GHM undercounted trauma-related deaths by 41% in its reports. It also noted that its findings "underestimate the full impact of the military operation in Gaza, as they do not account for non-trauma-related deaths resulting from health service disruption, food insecurity, and inadequate water and sanitation."[^cite_note-jamaluddine_et_al-413]
