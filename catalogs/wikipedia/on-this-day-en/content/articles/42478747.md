**Jorhat** ([/ˈdʒɔːrhɑːt/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/11/Jorhat_Pronunciation.wav/Jorhat_Pronunciation.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/11/Jorhat_Pronunciation.wav/Jorhat_Pronunciation.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*JOR-haht*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)/Assamese: [\[zʊ(ɹ).ɦat\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Assamese)) is a city in the Indian state of [Assam](186162.md), located 300 km east of state capital [Dispur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispur). It is the administrative headquarters of the [Jorhat District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorhat_district). Jorhat is also home to [Asia](689.md)'s oldest [golf course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf_course), the [Jorhat Gymkhana Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorhat_Gymkhana_Club).[^cite_note-3] Located on the banks of the [Bhogdoi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhogdoi_River), Jorhat continues to be amongst the fastest growing urban centers in the state of Assam.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## Etymology

Jorhat ("jor" means twin and "hat" means market) means two hats or mandis - "Masorhaat" and "Sowkihat" which existed on the opposite banks of the [Bhugdoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhogdoi_River) river.

## History

See also: [Timeline of Jorhat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Jorhat)

Main article: [Colonial Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Assam)

### Under Konbaung empire

Main article: [Konbaung dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konbaung_dynasty)



[Maha Bandula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maha_Bandula) who led the [Burmese forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Burmese_armed_forces) to Jorhat

The town was occupied by the Burmese [Konbaung dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konbaung_dynasty) between 1817 - 1825. The Burmese left the commander Mingimaha Tilwa in charge of the area who appointed [Jogeswar Singha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogeswar_Singha) as the new puppet King in 1821 in Jorhat.[^cite_note-8] Later when the deposed king [Chandrakanta Singha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandrakanta_Singha) tried to fight back, [Bagyidaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagyidaw) (1819 – 1837), the seventh King of Burma sent the greatest of his generals [Mingimaha Bandula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maha_Bandula) with 20,000 troops[^cite_note-harvcol|Nag|2018|pp=13–14-9] (including 10,000 [Kamti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khamti_people) [Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_States) and [Kachin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingpo_people) [levies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudal_levies) who were chieftains of [Mongkawng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongkawng) and [Hukawng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hukawng_Valley)) who defeated Chandrakanta Singha at Mahgarh near Jorhat and reestablished Burmese authority in Assam. The defeat of Chandrakanta on 12 June 1822 marks the start of the Burmese rule in Assam.[^cite_note-10] and Burmese commander Mingimaha Tilwa was made the king. Maha Bandula then returned to [Ava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inwa) in [Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma).

### Under British Empire

In 1885, a narrow-gauge railway, [Jorehaut Provincial Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorehaut_Provincial_Railway), became operational. In time, this contributed to the rapid growth of the tea industry around the whole region.

## Geography

Jorhat is located at [26°45′N 94°13′E  /  26.75°N 94.22°E  / 26.75; 94.22](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Jorhat\&params=26.75_N_94.22_E_). It has an average elevation of 116 metres (381 feet).

The municipality covers an area of 72.8 square kilometres (28.1 sq mi),[^cite_note-jorhatonline.in-2] has 19 wards with a population of 71,398 as per 2011 census.The district spreads over 2,859 square kilometres (1,104 sq mi) and had a population of 870,000 according to a 1991 census. Population density at that time was 306 persons per square kilometre (793/sq mi). The sex ratio is 913 (913 females per 1000 males).

The average literacy rate of Jorhat in 2011 was 91.39%. Gender-wise, male and female literacy were 93.63% and 88.99% respectively, which is one of the highest in the state.

[Assamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_language) is the predominant language and is spoken by 70.08% of the population, while [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language) was spoken by 12.27%. [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) (11.60%) and [Bhojpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_language) (1.01%) are spoken by migrants from the Gangetic plains, such as traders and labourers. Ethnic languages like [Mising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mising_language) and [Deori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deori_language) are also spoken by the respective ethnicity. Other small languages in the city include [Marwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwari_language), [Sadri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadri_language), [Santali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santali_language), [Sora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sora_language) and [Odia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language) which in total are spoken by 5.04% of the population.[^cite_note-15]

## Culture

Jorhat has rich contributions in the fields of arts, culture and tradition of the contemporary Assamese society. In 1896, [Jorhat Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorhat_Theatre) was established to perform cultural activities and dramas by some renowned people of the town. The Chandrakanta Handique Bhawan, the headquarters of [Asam Sahitya Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asam_Sahitya_Sabha) was established in 1926. Jorhat has produced creative writers, historians and journalists. [Birendra Kumar Bhattacharya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birendra_Kumar_Bhattacharya), the first Assamese to win India's highest literature award, the [Jnanpith Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jnanpith_Award), was from Jorhat.[^cite_note-16]

## Media

In 1935, the first Assamese daily newspaper *Dainik Batori* was published from Jorhat by Raibahadur [Siva Prasad Barooah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siva_Prasad_Barooah).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] The daily newspapers which have Jorhat editions include *[Dainik Janambhumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dainik_Janambhumi)*, *[Amar Asom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amar_Asom)*, *[Dainik Agradoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dainik_Agradoot)* and *[Asomiya Khabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asomiya_Khabar)* in [Assamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_language), the *Purbanchal Prohori* in Hindi and *[The Telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Telegraph_\(Calcutta\))* in English. *The Eastern Clarion* was the first English daily published from the city, but it was ceased. Besides the dailies, a weekly newspaper *Saptahik Janambhumi* is also published from Jorhat. *City Guide of Jorhat* was the first yellow pages, published in July 1987. Jorhat has a radio broadcast station of [All India Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Radio) (AIR) located on the outskirts of the city at Garmur.

## District Court



District & Sessions Judge Court, Jorhat

The District & Sessions Judge of [Upper Assam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Assam) District, Jorhat, was established on November 15, 1948, and became permanent on April 1, 1953. The first District & Sessions Judge of Assam Valley, Upper Assam Districts, Jorhat, was Mr. S.K. Das, M.A., B.L. Currently, the district's territorial jurisdiction extends over two sub-divisions: Majuli Sub-Division and Titabar Sub-Division. Altogether, the District & Sessions Judge and the Chief Judicial Magistrate comprise 15 courts staffed by Judicial Officers of various grades. The District & Sessions Judge together with the Chief Judicial Magistrate Court Complex, Jorhat is situated in the heart of the city. The Present District & Sessions Judge as the head of the establishment is Smti. Barnali Mahanta. The present Chief Judicial Magistrate, Jorhat, the head of all the Criminal Courts is Sri Arup Jyoti Baishya.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

## Transport

### Air

The [Jorhat Airport](7492180.md), commonly known as Rowriah Airport, is located at Rowriah, a suburb of Jorhat, about 7 kilometres (4.3 mi) from the city centre.[^cite_note-21] The airport has flight connectivity to [Kolkata](47905.md) and [Delhi](37756.md) operated by [IndiGo](2085799.md). It is controlled by the [Airports Authority of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airports_Authority_of_India).

### Rail

The first rail connectivity of Jorhat began during British era in 1885, when [Jorehaut Provincial Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorehaut_Provincial_Railway), a narrow-gauge railway services became operational.

Jorhat is served by [Jorhat Town railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorhat_Town_railway_station)[^cite_note-22] which lies on the Furkating-Jorhat-Mariani branch line of [Tinsukia railway division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinsukia_railway_division). [Mariani Junction railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariani_Junction_railway_station),[^cite_note-23] the major railway junction of the district is about 18 km (11 mi) from Jorhat. It falls in the [Lumding-Dibrugarh section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumding-Dibrugarh_section) and is well connected to all the large cities of the country by long-distance express trains.

### Road

The [Inter State Bus Terminus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_State_Bus_Terminals) (ISBT) of Jorhat is at Kotoki Pukhuri, Tarajan. It operates daily bus services from [ASTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_State_Transport_Corporation) and private operators to other towns and cities in the state and few destinations of the [Northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_India). [Auto-rickshaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auto-rickshaw), local cabs and [rickshaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickshaw) are the main modes of public transport within the city.

### Water

The daily ferry services connect the Nimati ghat with Kamalabari and Aphalamukh in [Majuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majuli). The *Neamati multimodal waterways terminal* on [Brahmaputra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmaputra) [National Waterway 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Waterway_2) in Jorhat district is part of the [Bharatmala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatmala) and [Sagarmala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagarmala) projects.[^cite_note-mala1-24]

## Sports

Established in 1950, the multi-purpose [Jorhat Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorhat_Stadium) is the oldest stadium of Assam, which is mainly used for [cricket](25675557.md) and [football](10568.md).[^cite_note-57] It has hosted few [Ranji Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranji_Trophy) matches. The prominent football tournament [ATPA Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATPA_Shield) held every year in this venue since 1955. [Jorhat Town Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorhat_Town_Club) used the stadium as home ground for their home matches of [Assam State Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assam_State_Premier_League). Other sporting venues near the stadium include [JDSA Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorhat_District_Sports_Association_Ground), Kushal Konwar Indoor Stadium, Jorhat Swimming Society and Jorhat Tennis Club.[^cite_note-58] The historic sporting venue [Jorhat Gymkhana Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorhat_Gymkhana_Club) known for the century old annual horse race called Jorhat Races, which began in 1877. It has a golf course and a cricket ground.[^cite_note-59]
