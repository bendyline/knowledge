**Gibraltar** ([/dʒɪˈbrɔːltər, -ˈbrɒl-/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/88/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-Gibraltar.wav/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-Gibraltar.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/88/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-Gibraltar.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-Gibraltar.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*jih-BRAWL-tər, -⁠BROL-*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key),[^cite_note-9] Spanish: [\[xiβɾalˈtaɾ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)) is a [British Overseas Territory](207114.md)[^cite_note-11] and [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_overseas_cities)[^cite_note-12] located at the southern tip of the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula), on the [Bay of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Gibraltar), near the exit of the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) into the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) ([Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar)).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] It has an area of 6.8 km<sup>2</sup> (2.6 sq mi)[^cite_note-gibraltar.gov.gi-6] and is [bordered to the north](36577119.md) by [Spain](26667.md) ([Campo de Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campo_de_Gibraltar)). The landscape is dominated by the [Rock of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_of_Gibraltar), at the foot of which is a densely populated town area. Gibraltar is home to around 38,000 people,[^cite_note-CensusNov22-7] primarily [Gibraltarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltarians).[^cite_note-15]

Gibraltar was founded as a permanent watchtower by the [Almohads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad_Caliphate) in 1160. It switched control between the [Nasrids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasrids), [Castilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile) and [Marinids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinids) in the [Late Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages), acquiring larger strategic clout upon the destruction of nearby [Algeciras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeciras) c. 1375. It became again part of the Crown of Castile in 1462. In 1704, Anglo-Dutch forces [captured Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Gibraltar) from Spain during the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession), and it was ceded to [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) in perpetuity under the [Treaty of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Utrecht) in 1713. It became an important base for the [Royal Navy](26061.md), particularly during the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars) and [World War II](32927.md), as it controlled the [narrow entrance and exit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choke_point) to the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar), with half the world's seaborne trade passing through it.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

The sovereignty of Gibraltar is a point of contention in [Anglo-Spanish relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain%E2%80%93United_Kingdom_relations) as Spain asserts [a claim to the territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_Gibraltar).[^cite_note-Maec-19][^cite_note-20] Gibraltarians overwhelmingly rejected proposals for Spanish sovereignty in a [1967 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Gibraltar_sovereignty_referendum), and for shared sovereignty in a [2002 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Gibraltar_sovereignty_referendum).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-Hills-13-22] Nevertheless, Gibraltar maintains close economic and cultural links with Spain, with many Gibraltarians speaking Spanish as well as a local dialect known as [Llanito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanito).[^cite_note-Levey2008-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-Gibraltar_Chronicle-25]

Gibraltar's economy rests on [online gambling](2213921.md),[^cite_note-GovGib2022-26] financial services, tourism and the port.[^cite_note-SavillsGib-27] With one of the world's lowest unemployment rates, the largest part of the labour force are resident in Spain or non-Gibraltarians, especially in the private sector.

Since [Brexit](41688778.md), Gibraltar is not a member of the [European Union](9317.md): a draft treaty was negotiated in June 2025 that would allow the territory to participate in the [Schengen Agreement](179592.md) and the [European Union customs union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_customs_union).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] In July 2026, the UK, Spain, Gibraltar and the EU signed the treaty, removing border controls with Spain.[^cite_note-30]

## Name

The name is derived from [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): [جبل طارق](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D8%AC%D8%A8%D9%84%20%D8%B7%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%82), [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Jabal Ṭāriq*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Mount of Tariq' (named after the 8th-century North African military leader [Tariq ibn Ziyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariq_ibn_Ziyad), who began the [Muslim conquest of the Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Iberian_Peninsula) via the Strait of Gibraltar in 711).[^cite_note-WattCachia2007-31]

## History

Main article: [History of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Gibraltar)



View of the northern face of the [Moorish Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorish_Castle)'s Tower of Homage

### Prehistory and ancient history

Evidence of [Neanderthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal) habitation in Gibraltar from around 50,000 years ago has been discovered at [Gorham's Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorham's_Cave).[^cite_note-32] The caves of Gibraltar continued to be used by *[Homo sapiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens)* after the final extinction of the Neanderthals. Stone tools, ancient hearths and animal bones dating from around 40,000 years ago to about 5,000 years ago have been found in deposits left in Gorham's Cave.[^cite_note-33]

Numerous [potsherds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherd) dating from the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) period have been found in Gibraltar's caves, mostly of types typical of the [Almerian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Iberia) found elsewhere in [Andalusia](2736.md), especially around the town of [Almería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almer%C3%ADa), from which it takes its name.[^cite_note-34] There is little evidence of habitation in the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) when people had largely stopped living in caves.[^cite_note-35]

During ancient times, Gibraltar was regarded by the peoples of the Mediterranean as a place of religious and symbolic importance. The [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicians) were present for several centuries since around 950 BC, apparently using Gorham's Cave as a shrine to the *[genius loci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genius_loci)*,[^cite_note-36] as did the [Carthaginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginians) and [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) after them. Gibraltar was known as *[Mons Calpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mons_Calpe)*, a name perhaps of Phoenician origin.[^cite_note-Hills-13-22] *Mons Calpe* was considered by the ancient Greeks and Romans as one of the [Pillars of Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillars_of_Hercules), after the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) legend of the creation of the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar) by [Heracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heracles). There is no known archaeological evidence of permanent settlements from the ancient period.[^cite_note-37] They settled at the head of the bay in the [*Campo* (hinterland) of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campo_de_Gibraltar).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJackson22-38] The town of [Carteia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carteia), near the location of the modern Spanish town of [San Roque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Roque,_C%C3%A1diz), was founded by the Phoenicians around 950 BC on the site of an early settlement of the native [Turdetani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turdetani) people.[^cite_note-39]

### Middle Ages

The area of Gibraltar later formed part of the [Visigothic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigothic_Kingdom) of [Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania) for almost 300 years, from 418 until 720 AD.[^cite_note-40]

Following a raid in 710, a predominantly [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) army under the command of [Tariq ibn Ziyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariq_ibn_Ziyad) crossed from North Africa in April 711 and landed somewhere in the vicinity of Gibraltar (though most likely not in the bay or at the Rock itself).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJackson21–5-42] Tariq's expedition led to the [Islamic conquest of most of the Iberian peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_conquest_of_Hispania).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] *[Mons Calpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_of_Gibraltar)* was renamed *Jabal Ṭāriq* (جبل طارق), "the Mount of Tariq", subsequently corrupted into *Gibraltar*.[^cite_note-Hills-13-22][^cite_note-45]

In 1160 the [Almohad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad_Caliphate) Sultan [Abd al-Mu'min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al-Mu'min) ordered that a permanent settlement, including a castle, be built. It received the name of Medinat al-Fath (City of the Victory).[^cite_note-norris-46] The Tower of Homage of the [Moorish Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorish_Castle) remains standing.

From 1274 onwards, the town was fought over and captured by the [Nasrids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasrid_dynasty) of [Granada](38264.md) (in 1237 and 1374), the [Marinids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinids) of [Fez](39760328.md) (in 1274 and 1333) and the kings of [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile) (in 1309). Upon the Nasrid destruction and abandonment of [Algeciras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeciras) c. 1375 and Nasrids' procurement of Gibraltar away from Marinids in 1375, the Nasrids favoured Gibraltar (a worse natural harbor than Algeciras but featuring better defence capabilities) as a military and urban outpost in the Strait, although Gibraltar did not ever reach a large population during this period.[^cite_note-47]

### Modern era



[Baedeker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baedeker) map of Gibraltar, 1901

In 1462, Gibraltar was [captured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Siege_of_Gibraltar) by [Juan Alonso de Guzmán, 1st Duke of Medina Sidonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Alonso_de_Guzm%C3%A1n,_1st_Duke_of_Medina_Sidonia), from the [Emirate of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Granada).[^cite_note-48]

After the conquest, [Henry IV of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_IV_of_Castile) assumed the additional title of [King of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Gibraltar), establishing it as part of the *[comarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comarca)* of the [Campo Llano de Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campo_de_Gibraltar).[^cite_note-49] Six years later, Gibraltar was restored to the Duke of Medina Sidonia, who sold it in 1474 to a group of 4,350 *[conversos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Converso)* (Christian converts from Judaism) from [Cordova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain) and [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville) and in exchange for maintaining the garrison of the town for two years, after which time they were expelled, returning to their home towns or moving on to other parts of Spain.[^cite_note-50] In 1501, Gibraltar passed back to the Spanish Crown, and [Isabella I of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabella_I_of_Castile) issued a [Royal Warrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_warrant_\(document\)) granting Gibraltar the coat of arms that it still uses.

In 1540 Gibraltar was captured and ransacked by [Ottoman](22278.md) admiral [Hayreddin Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayreddin_Barbarossa).[^cite_note-Hernandez-51][^cite_note-52] The raid of the small town eliminated nearly an entire generation of Gibraltarians.[^cite_note-Hernandez-51]

In 1704, during the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession), a combined [Anglo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England)-[Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) fleet, representing the [Grand Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Alliance_\(League_of_Augsburg\)), [captured the town of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Gibraltar) on behalf of the [Archduke Charles of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) in his campaign to become King of Spain. Subsequently, the vast majority of the approximately 4,000 to 5,000 Spanish inhabitants left the town, with only about 70 people, mostly neutral Genoese traders, choosing to remain.[^cite_note-Jackson-53][^cite_note-Harvey-54] Many of the displaced residents settled nearby.[^cite_note-Jackson-53] As the Alliance's campaign faltered, the 1713 [Treaty of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Utrecht) was negotiated, which ceded control of Gibraltar to Britain to secure Britain's withdrawal from the war. Unsuccessful attempts by Spanish monarchs to regain Gibraltar were made, with the [siege of 1727](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Gibraltar_\(1727\)), and again with the [Great Siege of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Siege_of_Gibraltar) (1779 to 1783), during the [American War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_War_of_Independence).

After the destructive Great Siege, the town was almost entirely rebuilt.[^cite_note-At_an_architectural_crossroads-55] Giovanni Maria Boschetti, who arrived in Gibraltar in 1784 as a 25-year-old from Milan, where he is thought to have been a stonemason or engineer, built the Victualling Yard (completed in 1812) and many other buildings. Boschetti is regarded as having been responsible for setting the old town's style, described by Claire Montado, chief executive of the Gibraltar Heritage Trust, as "military-ordnance-style arched doorways, Italianate stucco relief, Genoese shutters, English Regency ironwork balconies, Spanish stained glass and Georgian sash and casement windows."[^cite_note-At_an_architectural_crossroads-55]

During the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), Gibraltar became a key base for the [Royal Navy](26061.md) and played an important role leading up to the [Battle of Trafalgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Trafalgar) (21 October 1805). Designated one of four [Imperial fortresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_fortress) (along with [Bermuda](3460.md), [Halifax (Nova Scotia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia) and [Malta](19137.md)),[^cite_note-56] its strategic location made it a key base during the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) of 1854–1856. In the 18th century, the peacetime military garrison fluctuated in numbers from a minimum of 1,100 to a maximum of 5,000. The first half of the 19th century saw a significant increase of population to more than 17,000 in 1860, as people from Britain and all around the Mediterranean – Italian, Portuguese, Maltese, Jewish and French – took up residence in the town.[^cite_note-57]

Its strategic value increased with the opening of the [Suez Canal](29323.md), as it lay on the sea route between the UK and the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) east of Suez. In the later 19th century, major investments were made to improve the fortifications and the port.[^cite_note-58]

### Contemporary history

See also: [Military history of Gibraltar during World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Gibraltar_during_World_War_II) and [Effect of Brexit on Gibraltar](53720900.md)



Shown here during the Second World War, a [Douglas Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Dakota) of [BOAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BOAC) is silhouetted at Gibraltar by the batteries of searchlights on the Rock, as crews prepare it for a night flight to the United Kingdom.

[During the Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Gibraltar_during_World_War_II), most of Gibraltar's [civilian population was evacuated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evacuation_of_the_Gibraltarian_civilian_population_during_World_War_II), mainly to London, but also to parts of [Morocco](19291.md) and [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira) and to [Gibraltar Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Camp) in [Jamaica](15660.md). The Rock was strengthened as a [fortress](204118.md). On 18 July 1940, the [Vichy French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France) [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_French_Air_Force) attacked Gibraltar in retaliation for the [British bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Mers-el-K%C3%A9bir) of the Vichy navy. The naval base and the ships based there played a key role in the provisioning and supply of the island of [Malta](19137.md) during [its long siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Malta_\(World_War_II\)). As well as frequent short runs, known as "Club Runs", towards Malta to fly off aircraft reinforcements (initially [Hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_hurricane), but later, notably from the USN aircraft carrier *[Wasp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Wasp_\(CV-7\))*, [Spitfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermarine_Spitfire)), the critical [Operation Pedestal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pedestal) convoy was run from Gibraltar in August 1942. This resupplied the island at a critical time in the face of concentrated air attacks from German and Italian forces. Spanish dictator [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco)'s reluctance to allow the [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) onto Spanish soil frustrated a German plan to capture the Rock, codenamed [Operation Felix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Felix).



Buildings of the former [HM Dockyard, Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Dockyard,_Gibraltar), dating from the 1895 expansion

In the 1950s, Franco renewed Spain's claim to sovereignty over Gibraltar and restricted movement between Gibraltar and Spain. Gibraltarians voted overwhelmingly to remain under British sovereignty in the [1967 Gibraltar sovereignty referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Gibraltar_sovereignty_referendum), which led to the passing of the [Gibraltar Constitution Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Constitution_Order_1969) in 1969. In response, Spain completely closed the border with Gibraltar and severed all communication links.[^cite_note-Jordine2009-59] The border with Spain was partially reopened in 1982 and fully reopened in 1985 before Spain's accession to the [European Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Community).

In the early 2000s, Britain and Spain were in negotiations over a potential agreement that would see them sharing sovereignty over Gibraltar. The government of Gibraltar organised a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Gibraltar_sovereignty_referendum) on the plan, and 99% of the population voted to reject it.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] In 2008, the British government committed to respecting the Gibraltarians' wishes.[^cite_note-62] A [new Constitution Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Constitution_Order_2006) was approved in referendum in 2006. A process of tripartite negotiations started in 2006 between Spain, Gibraltar and the UK, ending some restrictions and dealing with disputes in some specific areas such as air movements, customs procedures, telecommunications, pensions and cultural exchange.[^cite_note-63]

In the [British referendum on membership of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_referendum_on_membership_of_the_European_Union) 96% of [Gibraltarians voted to remain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltarians_voted_to_remain) on an 84% turnout.[^cite_note-64] Spain renewed calls for joint Spanish–British control of the peninsula;[^cite_note-65] these were strongly rebuffed by Gibraltar's Chief Minister.[^cite_note-66] On 18 October 2018, however, Spain seemed to have reached an agreement with the United Kingdom in relation to its objections to Gibraltar leaving the EU with the UK, with Spain's prime minister [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md) stating, "Gibraltar will no longer be a problem in arriving at a Brexit deal."[^cite_note-67]

On 31 January 2020, the [UK left the European Union](41688778.md), and Gibraltar consequently did the same. During the transition phase set out in the [Brexit withdrawal agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit_withdrawal_agreement), Gibraltar's relationship with the EU remained unchanged until the end of 2020, when it was replaced by the [EU–UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU%E2%80%93UK_Trade_and_Cooperation_Agreement). On 31 December 2020, the UK and Spain agreed in principle on a framework for Gibraltar to participate in the [Schengen Area](16565139.md)[^cite_note-GibStatement-68] to avoid a hard border with Spain after years of negotiations.[^cite_note-BBC_News-69][^cite_note-Ashifa_Kassam-70][^cite_note-71]

In 2022, Gibraltar launched a bid for city status as part of the [Platinum Jubilee Civic Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum_Jubilee_Civic_Honours). The bid was refused, but when researchers looked through [the National Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_National_Archives_\(United_Kingdom\)), they found that it had already been recognised as a city by [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria) in 1842.[^cite_note-\:0-72] The status came into force on 29 August 2022.[^cite_note-\:0-72]

On 11 June 2025, a deal was signed which established an [open border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_border) and joint passport controls at [Gibraltar International Airport](2120898.md), measures designed to facilitate easier travel and daily crossings. Despite these arrangements, the UK reaffirmed that Gibraltar remains British sovereign territory, preserving the existing status despite practical changes on the ground.[^cite_note-OBUK1-73][^cite_note-OBUK2-74] The agreement was later formalised on 14 July 2026, when the UK, Spain, Gibraltar and the European Union signed a treaty in [Brussels](3708.md) removing routine border controls at the Gibraltar–Spain frontier and enabling Gibraltar's participation in the Schengen Area and the European Union Customs Union.[^cite_note-75]

## Governance

Main article: [Politics of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Gibraltar)

See also: [Disputed status of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disputed_status_of_Gibraltar) and [Political development in modern Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_development_in_modern_Gibraltar)



Main Street entrance to the Governor's Residence, [The Convent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Convent_\(Gibraltar\))



[John Mackintosh Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mackintosh_Square) entrance to the [Gibraltar Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Parliament)

Under its current [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Constitution_Order_2006), Gibraltar has almost complete internal [self-governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance) through a [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Parliament)[^cite_note-publications.parliament.uk-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] elected for a term of up to four years. The [unicameral](204304.md) parliament presently consists of 17 elected members, and the [Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Speakers_of_the_Gibraltar_Parliament) who is not elected but appointed by a resolution of the parliament.[^cite_note-79] The [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Gibraltar) consists of 10 elected members. The head of state is the British monarch King [Charles III](125248.md), who is represented by the [Governor of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Gibraltar). The governor enacts day-to-day matters on the advice of the Gibraltar Parliament but is responsible to the British government in respect of defence, foreign policy, internal security and general good governance. Judicial and other appointments are made on behalf of the monarch in consultation with the [head of the elected government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Gibraltar).[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-news.bbc.co.uk-82]

The [2011 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Gibraltar_general_election) was contested by the [Gibraltar Social Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Social_Democrats) (GSD), [Gibraltar Socialist Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Socialist_Labour_Party) (GSLP)-[Liberal Party of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Gibraltar) (LPG) Alliance and the [Progressive Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Democratic_Party_\(Gibraltar\)) (PDP). The PDP was a new party, formed in 2006 and fielded candidates in the 2007 election, but none were elected. The head of government is the [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Gibraltar) (as of December 2011, [Fabian Picardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabian_Picardo)). All local political parties oppose any transfer of sovereignty to Spain, instead supporting [self-determination](29269.md). It is British government policy not to engage in talks about the sovereignty of Gibraltar without the consent of the people of Gibraltar.[^cite_note-83]

Gibraltar was part of the [European Union](9317.md), having joined through the [European Communities Act 1972 (UK)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Communities_Act_1972_\(UK\)), which gave effect to the [Treaty of Accession 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Accession_1972), as a dependent territory of the United Kingdom under what was then article 227(4) of the [Treaty Establishing the European Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_Establishing_the_European_Community) covering [special member state territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_and_the_European_Union), with exemption from some areas such as the [European Union Customs Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Customs_Union), [Common Agricultural Policy](141924.md) and the [Schengen Area](16565139.md). It is the only [British Overseas Territory](207114.md) which was part of the European Union. After a 10-year campaign for the right to vote in [European elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_the_European_Union), from 2004 to 2019 the people of Gibraltar participated in elections for the [European Parliament](9581.md) as part of the [South West England constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_England_\(European_Parliament_constituency\)).[^cite_note-84] On 23 June 2016 Gibraltar voted along with the United Kingdom in the [EU referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum); 96% of its population voted to remain, but the overall United Kingdom result gave a 51.9% majority to leaving the EU.[^cite_note-85] Nevertheless, Spanish Prime Minister [Pedro Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_S%C3%A1nchez_\(politician\)) stated on 18 October 2018 that the Gibraltar protocol had been "resolved" and that Spain will hold no objection when Gibraltar leaves the EU with Britain.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

Gibraltar was nominated to be included on the [United Nations list of non-self-governing territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_list_of_non-self-governing_territories) by the United Kingdom when the list was created in 1946[^cite_note-88] and has been listed ever since.[^cite_note-89] The government of Gibraltar has actively worked to have Gibraltar removed from the list,[^cite_note-90] and in 2008 the British government declared Gibraltar's continued presence on the list an anachronism.[^cite_note-91]

Gibraltar is not a member of the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) in its own right and is represented by the [United Kingdom](31717.md) but was granted Associate Membership of the [Commonwealth Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Foundation) in 2004. Gibraltar has competed in the [Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Games) since 1958.[^cite_note-92]

### Citizenship

Further information: [British passport (Gibraltar)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_passport_\(Gibraltar\))

As a result of the [British Nationality Act 1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Nationality_Act_1981), Gibraltarians were made [British Overseas Territories citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Overseas_Territories_citizen) by default, but could apply for registration as a [British citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_citizen) ("an entitlement that cannot be refused") under section 5 of the Act. Under the subsequent [British Overseas Territories Act 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Overseas_Territories_Act_2002), all British Overseas Territories citizens, except of those of the Sovereign Base Areas of [Akrotiri and Dhekelia](1025144.md), and [Episkopi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episkopi_Cantonment) in [Cyprus](5593.md), became British citizens on 21 May 2002.[^cite_note-93]

## International relations

Further information: [Status of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_Gibraltar) and [Effect of Brexit on Gibraltar](53720900.md)

Gibraltar is not a [sovereign state](23604120.md) but a dependent territory of the United Kingdom, with its formal international relations managed by the UK Government. Since [Brexit](41688778.md), Gibraltar is no longer part of the [European Union](9317.md) (EU) but participates in the [EU–UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU%E2%80%93UK_Trade_and_Cooperation_Agreement). On 31 December 2020, the UK and [Spain](26667.md) agreed in principle on a framework for the EU and the UK to negotiate an agreement allowing Gibraltar to participate in the [Schengen Area](16565139.md)[^cite_note-GibStatement-68] to avoid a hard border with Spain. Although these arrangements did not enter into force,[^cite_note-GibStatement-68][^cite_note-BBC_News-69] both sides aimed to minimise delays at the border in the meantime.[^cite_note-Ashifa_Kassam-70][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] However, talks stalled for several years due to Spain's insistence that its *[Policía Nacional](1608789.md)* control entry into the Schengen area, while the UK demanded that the European Union agency [Frontex](3072700.md) perform this role.

In June 2025, a preliminary political agreement was reached between the United Kingdom, Spain and the European Union to remove all physical border and customs checks between Gibraltar and Spain. Under the agreement, The *Policía Nacional* will conduct Schengen passport controls at Gibraltar's port and airport alongside Gibraltar authorities, and the [land border will be fully open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_border) for people and goods. The agreement also establishes joint Gibraltarian–Spanish cooperation on customs, indirect taxation including tobacco and anti-money laundering, with commitments to fair competition in taxation, state aid and labour. It explicitly states that British sovereignty remains legally unaffected despite *de facto* Spanish control over Gibraltar's borders.[^cite_note-OBUK1-73][^cite_note-OBUK2-74][^cite_note-96] In July 2026, the United Kingdom and the European Union removed the land-border fence between Gibraltar and Spain under a post-Brexit treaty, allowing free movement while introducing joint British-Spanish checks at Gibraltar's airport and port.[^cite_note-97]

## Geography

See also: [Rock of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_of_Gibraltar), [Bay of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Gibraltar), [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar), and [Gibraltar Artificial Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Artificial_Reef)



View of the Rock of Gibraltar from the [Mediterranean Steps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Steps)



Enlargeable, detailed map of Gibraltar

Gibraltar's territory covers 6.8 km2 (2.6 sqmi)[^cite_note-gibraltar.gov.gi-6] and shares a 1.2 km (0.75 mi) land border with Spain. The town of [La Línea de la Concepción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_L%C3%ADnea_de_la_Concepci%C3%B3n), a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_C%C3%A1diz) of the [province of Cádiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_C%C3%A1diz), lies on the Spanish side of the border. The Spanish hinterland forms the [comarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comarca) of [Campo de Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campo_de_Gibraltar) (literally "Countryside of Gibraltar"). The shoreline measures 12 km (7.5 mi) in length. There are two coasts ("Sides") of Gibraltar: the East Side, which contains the settlements of [Sandy Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Bay,_Gibraltar) and [Catalan Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_Bay); and the [Westside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westside_\(Gibraltar\)), where the vast majority of the population lives. Gibraltar has no administrative divisions but is divided into seven [Major Residential Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Major_Residential_Areas).

Having negligible [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource) and few natural [freshwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater) resources, limited to natural wells in the north, until recently Gibraltar used large concrete or natural rock [water catchments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) to collect rainwater. Fresh water from the boreholes is nowadays supplemented by two [desalination](156787.md) plants: a [reverse osmosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_osmosis) plant, constructed in a tunnel within the rock, and a [multi-stage flash distillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-stage_flash_distillation) plant at North Mole.[^cite_note-98]

Gibraltar's terrain consists of the 426 m-high (1,398 ft) [Rock of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_of_Gibraltar)[^cite_note-99] made of [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic) [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), and the narrow coastal lowland surrounding it. It contains many tunnelled roads, most of which are still operated by the military and closed to the general public.

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Gibraltar)

Gibraltar has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Csa*),[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] with mild, rainy winters and summers that are very warm to hot and humid, but with very little rainfall. As is the case for nearby [Algeciras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeciras) and [Tarifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarifa), summers are significantly cooler and annual temperature more constant than other cities on the southern coast of the Iberian peninsula because of its position on the Strait of Gibraltar. Rain occurs mainly in winter, with summer being generally dry. Its average annual temperature is 21.7 °C (71.1 °F) as a daily high and 15.8 °C (60.4 °F) as the overnight low. In the coldest month, January, the high temperature averages 16.3 °C (61.3 °F) and the overnight low averages 11.2 °C (52.2 °F) and the average sea temperature is 16 °C (61 °F). In the warmest month, August, the daily high temperature averages 28.4 °C (83.1 °F), the overnight low averages 21.2 °C (70.2 °F), and the average sea temperature is 22 °C (72 °F).[^cite_note-DWD-102] [^cite_note-weather2travel-103]

### Flora and fauna

See also: [List of mammals of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mammals_of_Gibraltar), [List of birds of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_birds_of_Gibraltar), and [List of amphibians and reptiles of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_amphibians_and_reptiles_of_Gibraltar)



[Gibraltar candytuft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberis_gibraltarica) growing at the [Gibraltar Botanic Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Botanic_Gardens)



A [common dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_dolphin) in the [Bay of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Gibraltar)

Over 500 different species of [flowering plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flowering_plant) grow on the Rock. Gibraltar is the only place in Europe where Gibraltar candytuft (*[Iberis gibraltarica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberis_gibraltarica)*) is found growing in the wild; the plant is otherwise native to North Africa. It is the symbol of the [Upper Rock nature reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_of_Gibraltar#Upper_Rock_Nature_Reserve). [Olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive) and [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine) trees are among the most common of those growing around the Rock.

Most of the Rock's upper area is covered by a [nature reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_reserve) which is home to around 230 [Barbary macaques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Barbary_macaques), the famous "apes" of Gibraltar, which are actually [monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkey). These are the only wild apes or monkeys found in Europe.[^cite_note-111] This species, known scientifically as *[Macaca sylvanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaca_sylvanus)*, is listed as endangered by the [IUCN Red List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUCN_Red_List) and is declining. Three-quarters of the world population live in the [Middle Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Atlas) mountains of [Morocco](19291.md). Recent genetic studies and historical documents point to their presence on the Rock before British control, having possibly been introduced during the Islamic period. A superstition analogous to that of the [ravens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raven) at the [Tower of London](31165.md) states that if the apes ever leave, so will the British. In 1944, [British Prime Minister](24150.md) [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill) was so concerned about the dwindling population of apes that he sent a message to the [Colonial Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Secretary_\(British_Empire\)) requesting that something be done about the situation.[^cite_note-112]

Other mammals found in Gibraltar include rabbits, foxes and bats. Dolphins and whales are frequently seen in the [Bay of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Gibraltar). Migrating birds are very common and Gibraltar is home to the only [Barbary partridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_partridge) found on the European continent.

In 1991, Graham Watson, Gibraltar's [MEP](258909.md), highlighted [conservationists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservationism)' fears that [urban development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_development), tourism and [invasive plant species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasive_plant_species) were threatening Gibraltar's own plants as well as birds and [bat species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat_species).[^cite_note-113]

### Environment



The Rock of Gibraltar (2010)

In May 2016, a report by the [World Health Organization](33583.md) showed that Gibraltar had the worst air quality in any British territory. The report concentrated on [PM10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PM10) and [PM2.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PM2.5) pollutants in the air.[^cite_note-114]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Gibraltar)



The semi-wild [Barbary macaques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Barbary_macaques) are an integral feature in [Gibraltar's tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Gibraltar).

Gibraltar's economy is dominated by four main sectors: [financial services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_financial_centre), [online gambling](2213921.md), maritime services (including ship repairs and fuel supply services), and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism), which includes duty-free retail sales to visitors.[^cite_note-115] The [British military](203793.md) traditionally dominated [Gibraltar's economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Gibraltar), with the [naval dockyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_dockyard) providing the bulk of economic activity. This, however, has diminished over the last 20 years and is estimated to account for only seven per cent of the local economy, compared to over 60 per cent in 1984. The territory also has a small manufacturing sector, representing a bit less than two per cent of the economy.

Gibraltar's [labour market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_market) employs around thirty thousand workers, 80% in the private sector and 20% in the public sector. The unemployment rate is extremely low, at around one per cent.

More than half (53 per cent) of the labour force are resident in Spain or are non Gibraltarians. According to the Government of Gibraltar's statistics, around 45 per cent of the total employee jobs, and 58% of the private sector jobs, are held by frontier workers (employees who are normally resident in Spain but are employed in Gibraltar). Around 63 per cent of the frontier workers are Spanish nationals. More than half (55 per cent) of the private sector employee jobs are held by persons who are not Gibraltarians or Other British nationalities. The public sector, on the other hand, employs mainly Gibraltarians and other British (90 per cent).[^cite_note-116] As a consequence, according to [Fabian Picardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabian_Picardo), Chief Minister of Gibraltar, during Brexit negotiations, a frontier which lacked the necessary fluidity for people to be able to access their places of work would put directly at risk nearly half of the jobs of the Gibraltar workforce.[^cite_note-Gibraltar_Chronicle-25][^cite_note-117]

In the early 2000s, many [bookmakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookmaker) and online gaming operators moved to Gibraltar to benefit from operating in a regulated jurisdiction with a favourable [corporate tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_tax) regime. This corporate tax regime for non-resident controlled companies was phased out by January 2011 and replaced by a still favourable fixed corporate tax rate of ten per cent.[^cite_note-118]

Tourism is also a significant industry. Gibraltar is a popular port for cruise ships and attracts day visitors from resorts in Spain. The Rock is a popular tourist attraction, particularly among British tourists and residents in the southern coast of Spain. It is also a popular shopping destination, and all goods and services are [VAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_added_tax) free, but may be subject to Gibraltar taxes. Many of the large British high street chains have branches or franchises in Gibraltar including [Morrisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrisons), [Marks & Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marks_&_Spencer) and [Mothercare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mothercare). Branches and franchises of international retailers such as [Tommy Hilfiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Hilfiger) and [Sunglass Hut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunglass_Hut_International) are also present in Gibraltar, as is the Spanish clothing company [Mango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango_\(clothing\)).



[Queensway Quay Marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensway_Quay_Marina), a residential district

A number of [British and international banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_banks_in_Gibraltar) have operations based in Gibraltar. [Jyske Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyske_Bank) claims to be the oldest bank in the country, based on Jyske's acquisition in 1987 of [Banco Galliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_Galliano), which began operations in Gibraltar in 1855. An ancestor of [Barclays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barclays), the [Anglo-Egyptian Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_Bank), entered in 1888, and Credit Foncier (now [Crédit Agricole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%A9dit_Agricole)) entered in 1920.

There is some manufacturing activity, representing around 2% of the total employment. One company (Bassadone Automotive Group) supplies ambulances and other project vehicles converted locally from [SUV](47696.md) vehicles to the United Nations and other agencies, employing some 320 staff across its range of activities.[^cite_note-119]

In 1967, Gibraltar enacted the Companies (Taxation and Concessions) Ordinance (now an Act), which provided for special tax treatment for international business.[^cite_note-120] This was one of the factors leading to the growth of professional services such as [private banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_banking) and captive insurance management. Gibraltar has several attractive attributes as a [financial centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_centre), including a [common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law) [legal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Gibraltar) and access to the EU single market in financial services. Gibraltar is considered a high class jurisdiction and is listed on the "white list" by the [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD)](33853117.md) due to its highly regulated financial and e-gaming sectors, sharing the same status as the UK, USA and Germany.[^cite_note-Kunar_et_al-121] It is internationally collaborative, including in the exchange of information on tax matters.[^cite_note-Kunar_et_al-121]

The [Gibraltar Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Stock_Exchange) was established in 2014.

In January 2018, Gibraltar introduced a regulatory framework for Distributed Ledger Technology, with the aim of pursuing a "more flexible, adaptive approach... in the case of novel business activities, products, and business models".[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123] The Financial Services Commission (FSC),[^cite_note-124] which was established by an ordinance in 1989 (now an Act) that took effect in 1991, regulates the finance sector.[^cite_note-125] In 1997, the Department of Trade and Industry established its Gibraltar Finance Centre (GFC) Division to facilitate the development the [financial sector development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_sector_development). As of 2012, Gibraltar has 0.103 [Big Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Four_\(audit_firms\)) accounting firm offices per 1,000 population, the second highest in the world after the [British Virgin Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Virgin_Islands), and 0.6 banks per 1,000 people, the fifth most banks per capita in the world.[^cite_note-126] As of 2017, there is [very significant uncertainty](53720900.md) on continuing access to the EU single market after the forthcoming [Brexit](41688778.md).[^cite_note-127]

The currency of Gibraltar is the [Gibraltar pound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_pound), issued by the [Government of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Gibraltar) under the terms of the 1934 *Currency Notes Act*. These banknotes are [legal tender](267793.md) in Gibraltar alongside Bank of England banknotes.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-currnotesact-129] In a [currency board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_board) arrangement, these notes are issued against reserves of [sterling](270673.md).[^cite_note-currnotesact-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] Clearing and settlement of funds is conducted in sterling.[^cite_note-132] [Coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coins_of_the_Gibraltar_pound) in circulation follow British denominations but have separate designs. Unofficially, most retail outlets in Gibraltar accept the [euro](9472.md), though some [payphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payphone) and the [Royal Gibraltar Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Gibraltar_Post_Office), along with all other government offices, do not.[^cite_note-133]

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Gibraltar)



The Roman Catholic [Cathedral of St. Mary the Crowned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_St._Mary_the_Crowned) was built in 1462 and is the territory's oldest Catholic church.



The [Gibraltar Hindu Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Hindu_Temple) opened in 2000.



The [Ibrahim-al-Ibrahim Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim-al-Ibrahim_Mosque) was a gift from [King Fahd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahd_of_Saudi_Arabia) of [Saudi Arabia](349303.md).

Gibraltar is one of the most densely populated territories in the world, with a usually-resident population in 2012 of 32,194[^cite_note-134] equivalent to approximately 4,959/km<sup>2</sup> (12,840/sq mi). The growing demand for space is being increasingly met by [land reclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reclamation); reclaimed land makes up approximately one-tenth of the territory's total area, but houses over 40% of its population (2012 Census).

### Language

Main article: [Languages of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Gibraltar)

The [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) of Gibraltar is English and is used by the government and in schools. Most locals are [bilingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilingualism), also speaking Spanish. However, because of the varied mix of ethnic groups which reside there, other languages are also spoken on the Rock. [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages) and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) are spoken by the [Moroccan](19291.md) community, as are [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) and [Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_language) by the Indian community. [Maltese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_language) is spoken by some families of [Maltese descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Maltese_in_Gibraltar).[^cite_note-Archer2013-137]

Gibraltarians often converse in *[Llanito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanito)* (pronounced [\[ʎaˈnito\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)), a [vernacular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernacular) unique to Gibraltar. It is based on [Andalusian Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andalusian_Spanish) with a strong mixture of [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English) and elements from languages such as Maltese, [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language), [Genoese Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese_dialect) and [Haketia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haketia) (a [Judaeo-Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaeo-Spanish) dialect). Llanito also often involves [code-switching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code-switching) to English and Spanish.

The English language is becoming increasingly dominant in Gibraltar, with the younger generation speaking little or no Llanito despite learning Spanish in school.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

Gibraltarians often call themselves *Llanitos*.[^cite_note-Kellermann2001-140]

## Education

Main article: [Education in Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Gibraltar)

[Education in Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Gibraltar) generally follows the [English model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_England), operating within a [three tier system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-tier_education). Schools in Gibraltar use the [Key Stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_Stage) modular approach to teach the [National Curriculum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Curriculum_\(England,_Wales_and_Northern_Ireland\)). Gibraltar has 15 [state schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_school), two private schools and a college of further education, [Gibraltar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_College). Government secondary schools are [Bayside Comprehensive School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayside_Comprehensive_School_\(Gibraltar\)) and [Westside School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westside_School_\(Gibraltar\)), both of which are [coeducational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-sex_education), and Prior Park School Gibraltar is an independent co-ed secondary school.[^cite_note-143]

On 31 March 2015, the government of Gibraltar announced the adoption of the University of Gibraltar Act and the [University of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Gibraltar) opened in September 2015.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145] Previously, there were no facilities in Gibraltar for full-time higher education, and consequently, all Gibraltarian students studied elsewhere at [degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_degree) level or its equivalent and also for certain non-degree courses.[^cite_note-146]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Gibraltar)



[Tercentenary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tercentenary) celebrations in Gibraltar

The [culture of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Gibraltar) reflects Gibraltarians' diverse origins. While there are Spanish (mostly from nearby [Andalusia](2736.md)) and British influences, the ethnic origins of most Gibraltarians are not confined to these ethnicities. Other ethnicities include [Genoese](47332321.md), [Maltese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_people), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people), [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalans), [Sephardic Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jewish), [Sicilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilians), [Moroccan](19291.md), and [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-resident_Indian_and_person_of_Indian_origin). British influence remains strong, with English being the language of government, commerce, education and the media.

[Gibraltar's first sovereignty referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Gibraltar_sovereignty_referendum) is celebrated annually on [Gibraltar National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_National_Day) (10 September). It is a public holiday, during which most Gibraltarians dress in their [national colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_colours) of red and white. Until 2016, the tradition had been to also release 30,000 similarly coloured balloons, which represented the people of Gibraltar. However, this tradition has now been ended because of the threat that it poses to wildlife, particularly marine.[^cite_note-147] The 300th anniversary of [Gibraltar's capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Gibraltar) was celebrated in 2004 on Tercentenary Day (4 August), when in recognition of and with thanks for its long association with Gibraltar, the [Royal Navy](26061.md) was given the [Freedom of the City of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_the_City_of_Gibraltar?action=edit\&redlink=1) and a human chain of Gibraltarians dressed in red, white and blue, linked hands to encircle the Rock. On 4 June 2012, the [Gibraltar Diamond Jubilee Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Diamond_Jubilee_Flotilla), inspired by the [Thames Diamond Jubilee Pageant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thames_Diamond_Jubilee_Pageant), celebrated sixty years of the [Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II)'s reign.[^cite_note-148]

The [Gibraltar Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Broadcasting_Corporation) operates a television and radio station on UHF, VHF and medium-wave. The radio service is also internet-streamed. Special events and the daily news bulletin are streamed in video. The other local radio service is operated by the [British Forces Broadcasting Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Forces_Broadcasting_Service) which also provides a limited cable television network to HM Forces. The largest and most frequently published newspaper is the *[Gibraltar Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Chronicle)*, Gibraltar's oldest established daily newspaper and the world's second-oldest English language newspaper to have been in print continuously[^cite_note-149] with daily editions six days a week. *[Panorama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panorama_\(Gibraltar\))* is published on weekdays, and *7 Days*, *The New People*, and *Gibsport* are weekly.



Thousands of [Gibraltarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltarian_people) dress in their national colours of red and white during the 2013 [Gibraltar National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_National_Day) celebrations.

Native Gibraltarians have produced some literature of note. The first in fiction was probably [Héctor Licudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ctor_Licudi)'s 1929 novel *Barbarita*, written in Spanish,[^cite_note-150] chronicling the largely autobiographical adventures of a young Gibraltarian man. Throughout the 1940s and 1950s, several anthologies of poetry were published by [Leopoldo Sanguinetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldo_Sanguinetti), [Albert Joseph Patron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Joseph_Patron?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Alberto Pizzarello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Pizzarello). The 1960s were largely dominated by the theatrical works of [Elio Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elio_Cruz) and his two highly acclaimed Spanish language plays *La Lola se va pá Londre* and *Connie con cama camera en el comedor*. In the 1990s, the Gibraltarian [man-of-letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual) [Mario Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Arroyo) published *Profiles* (1994), a series of bilingual meditations on love, loneliness and death. [Trino Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trino_Cruz) is a bilingual poet originally writing English but now mainly in Spanish, who also translates [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb) poetry.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-ElPais-152][^cite_note-Téllez2013-153] Of late there have been works by the essayist [Mary Chiappe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Chiappe), such as her volume of essays *Cabbages and Kings* (2006) and by [M. G. Sanchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._G._Sanchez), author of the books *Rock Black: Ten Gibraltarian Stories* (2008) and *Diary of a Victorian Colonial* (2009). Mary Chiappe and [Sam Benady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Benady) have also published a series of detective books centred on the character of the nineteenth-century Gibraltarian sleuth Bresciano.

Musicians from Gibraltar include [Charles Ramirez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Ramirez), the first guitarist invited to play with the [Royal College of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Music) Orchestra,[^cite_note-154] successful rock bands like [Breed 77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breed_77), [Melon Diesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melon_Diesel) and [Taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxi_\(Gibraltar_band\)), while Gibraltarian bassist Glen Diani played for Irish/British [nu metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nu_metal) group [One Minute Silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Minute_Silence). [Albert Hammond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Hammond) had top 10 hits in the UK and US and has written many songs for international artists such as [Whitney Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Houston), [Tina Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Turner) and [Julio Iglesias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Iglesias).[^cite_note-155]

[Gibraltarian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltarian_cuisine) is the result of a long relationship between the Andalusian Spaniards and the British, as well as the many foreigners who made Gibraltar their home over the past three centuries. The culinary influences include those from Malta, Genoa, Portugal, Andalusia and Britain. This marriage of tastes has given Gibraltar an eclectic mix of Mediterranean and British cuisine, such as [calentita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calentita), a baked bread-like dish made with [chickpea flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_flour), water, olive oil, salt and pepper.

## Sport

Main article: [Sport in Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Gibraltar)



The [Gibraltar national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_national_football_team) lining up in their first official match, against [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_national_football_team), in 2013

[Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Gibraltar) is a popular sport in Gibraltar. The [Gibraltar Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Football_Association) applied for full membership of [UEFA](32332.md), but their bid was declined in 2007 in a contentious decision.[^cite_note-BBC_Sport-166] Gibraltar was confirmed as UEFA's 54th member on 24 May 2013 as a result of [Court of Arbitration for Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Arbitration_for_Sport) (CAS) arbitration and played in [Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016) qualifications.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-autogenerated1-168] Their first match was a 0–0 draw against Slovakia. Gibraltar's national team won its first-ever match in UEFA competition on 13 October 2018, defeating Armenia 1–0 in the [2018–19 UEFA Nations League D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Nations_League_D).[^cite_note-169]

Subsequently, Gibraltar applied for FIFA membership but this bid was also declined. On 2 May 2016, the CAS upheld the appeal filed by the Gibraltar Football Association regarding its request to become a full-time member of FIFA. CAS ordered FIFA to stop blocking Gibraltar's application for membership and allow it "without delay".[^cite_note-170]

[Rugby union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_union_in_Gibraltar) is fairly popular and one of the fastest-growing team sports. [Gibraltar Rugby Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Rugby_Football_Union) applied for membership of Europe's governing body for rugby. Gibraltar is believed to be the birthplace of the rugby variant [Tag Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tag_Rugby).[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]

## Communications

Main article: [Communications in Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_in_Gibraltar)



A plaque in City Mill Lane marking the site of Gibraltar's first telephone exchange



A Victorian post box of standard 1887 UK design in use in Gibraltar's Main Street (2008)

Gibraltar has a digital [telephone exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_exchange) supported by a [fibre optic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_fibre) and copper infrastructure; the telephone operator [Gibtelecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibtelecom) also operates a [GSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSM) network. Internet connectivity is available across the fixed network. Gibraltar's top-level domain code is [.gi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.gi).

[International Direct Dialling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Direct_Dialling) (IDD) is provided, and Gibraltar was allocated the access code [+350](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/+350) by the [International Telecommunication Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Telecommunication_Union). This has been finally accepted by Spain since 10 February 2007, when the [telecom dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecom_dispute_between_Gibraltar_and_Spain) was resolved.

## Transport

Main article: [Transport in Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Gibraltar)



The [Gibraltar Cable Car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Cable_Car) runs from outside the [Gibraltar Botanic Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Botanic_Gardens) to the top of the Rock, with an intermediate station at Apes' Den.

### Road

Within Gibraltar, the main form of transport is the car. [Motorcycles](19876.md) are also very popular and there is a good modern bus service. Unlike in the UK and other British territories, traffic [drives on the right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-_and_left-hand_traffic) and speed limits are in km/h, as the territory shares a land border with Spain. The [E15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E15) route connecting with Spain, France, England and Scotland is accessible from the Spanish side using the [CA-34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa_CA-34) [autovía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa).

Restrictions on transport introduced by Spanish dictator [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco) closed the land frontier in 1969 and also prohibited any air or ferry connections. In 1982, the land border was reopened. As the result of an agreement signed in [Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Spain) on 18 September 2006 between Gibraltar, the United Kingdom and Spain,[^cite_note-cordoba-173] the Spanish government agreed to relax [border controls](247927.md) at the frontier that have plagued locals for decades; in return, Britain paid increased pensions to Spanish workers who lost their jobs when Franco closed the border.[^cite_note-pensions-174] Telecommunication restrictions were lifted in February 2007 and air links with Spain were restored in December 2006.[^cite_note-airport-175][^cite_note-176]

Motorists and pedestrians crossing the border with Spain are occasionally subjected to very long delays.[^cite_note-177] Spain has occasionally closed the border during disputes or incidents involving the Gibraltar authorities, such as the [*Aurora* cruise ship incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Aurora_\(2000\)#Norovirus_breakout)[^cite_note-178] and when fishermen from the Spanish fishing vessel *Piraña* were arrested for [illegal fishing](6346579.md) in Gibraltar waters.[^cite_note-179]

Proposals exist for a [Strait of Gibraltar crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar_crossing) by bridge or tunnel.

### Air

Main article: [Gibraltar International Airport](2120898.md)

As of 2017, Gibraltar maintains regular flight connections with London (Heathrow & Gatwick), [Manchester](20206.md), [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham) and [Bristol](36741.md) in the UK.[^cite_note-180] This is via the [Royal Air Force](25679.md)'s military aerodrome in Gibraltar, which also serves as [the territory's civilian airport](2120898.md).[^cite_note-181]

[GB Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GB_Airways) operated a service between Gibraltar and London and other cities for many years. The airline initially flew under the name "Gibraltar Airways". In 1989, and in anticipation of service to cities outside the UK, Gibraltar Airways changed its name to GB Airways with the belief that a new name would incur fewer political problems. As a franchise, the airline operated flights in full [British Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Airways) livery. In 2007, GB Airways was purchased by [easyJet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EasyJet),[^cite_note-182] which began operating flights under their name in April 2008 when British Airways re-introduced flights to Gibraltar under their name. EasyJet have since added [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Airport) and [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Airport) and also operated flights to [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_Airport) between 2011 and 2012. Until entering administration in October 2017, [Monarch Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch_Airlines) operated the largest number of flights between the United Kingdom and Gibraltar, with scheduled services between Gibraltar and [Luton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Luton_Airport), [London Gatwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatwick_Airport), [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_Airport) and [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Airport). The Spanish [national airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_airline), [Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberia_Airlines), operated a daily service to [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barajas_Airport) which ceased for lack of demand. In May 2009, [Ándalus Líneas Aéreas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ndalus_L%C3%ADneas_A%C3%A9reas) opened a Spanish service,[^cite_note-183] which also ceased operations in March 2010.[^cite_note-184] An annual return [charter flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_flight) to [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_International_Airport) is operated by [Maltese](19137.md) national airline, [Air Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Malta).



The main road that crosses Gibraltar Airport



The new terminal at Gibraltar Airport

[Gibraltar International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Airport) is unusual not only because of its proximity to the city centre resulting in the airport terminal being within walking distance of much of Gibraltar but also because the runway intersects [Winston Churchill Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill_Avenue,_Gibraltar), formerly the main road into Spain, requiring movable barricades to close when aircraft land or depart. New roads and a tunnel, ending the need to stop road traffic when aircraft use the runway, were planned to coincide with the building of a new [airport terminal building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Airport#New_terminal) with an originally estimated completion date of 2009,[^cite_note-pressnote-185][^cite_note-186] although delays pushed back its official opening until 31 March 2023.[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188] The new road and tunnel was named Kingsway with the approval of [Charles III](125248.md)[^cite_note-189] and passes under the terminal and the eastern edge of the runway before connecting with [Devil's Tower Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil's_Tower_Road). Runway access is closed to everyday road traffic but is still available for exceptional, specific, or emergency use as well as pedestrians, cyclists and mobility scooters although an alternative subway is provided.[^cite_note-190]

The most popular alternative airport for Gibraltar is [Málaga Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga_Airport) in Spain, some 120 km (75 mi) to the east, which offers a wide range of destinations, second to [Jerez Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerez_Airport) which is closer to Gibraltar. In addition, the [Algeciras Heliport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeciras_Heliport) across the bay offers scheduled services to [Ceuta](6443.md).

## Water supply and sanitation

Main article: [Water supply and sanitation in Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_supply_and_sanitation_in_Gibraltar)

Gibraltar's supply of drinking water comes entirely from desalination, with a separate supply of saltwater for sanitary purposes. Both supplies are delivered from huge underground reservoirs excavated under the [Rock of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_of_Gibraltar).

[Water supply](684928.md) and [sanitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitation) in Gibraltar have been major concerns for its inhabitants throughout its history. There are no rivers, streams, or large bodies of water on the peninsula. Gibraltar's water supply was formerly provided by a combination of an aqueduct, wells, and the use of cisterns, barrels and earthenware pots to capture rainwater. This became increasingly inadequate as Gibraltar's population grew in the 18th and 19th centuries and lethal diseases such as [cholera](7591.md) and [yellow fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_fever) began to spread. In the late 19th century, a Sanitary Commission instigated major improvements which saw the introduction of large-scale [desalination](156787.md) and the use of giant water catchments covering over 2.5 million square feet (nearly 250,000 m<sup>2</sup>).

## Police

Further information: [Royal Gibraltar Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Gibraltar_Police) and [Gibraltar Defence Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Defence_Police)



Royal Gibraltar Police car, 2012



[Royal Gibraltar Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Gibraltar_Police), Marine Section

The [Royal Gibraltar Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Gibraltar_Police) (RGP), [Gibraltar Defence Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Defence_Police) (GDP) and [His Majesty's Customs (Gibraltar)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Majesty's_Customs_\(Gibraltar\)) are Gibraltar's principal civilian [law enforcement agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_agency). Outside the [United Kingdom](31717.md), the RGP is the oldest police force of the former [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), formed shortly after the creation of London's [Metropolitan Police](192450.md) in 1829 when Gibraltar was declared a [crown colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_colony) on 25 June 1830.[^cite_note-197]

In general, the Gibraltar force follows British police models in its dress and its mostly male constables and sergeants on foot patrol wear the traditional [custodian helmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Custodian_helmet), the headgear of the British "bobby on the beat". The helmet is traditionally made of cork covered outside by felt or [serge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serge_\(fabric\))-like material that matches the tunic. The vehicles also appear virtually identical to typical UK police vehicles, but are [left hand drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_hand_drive).

The force, whose name received the prefix "Royal" in 1992, numbers over 220 officers divided into a number of units. These include the [CID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Investigation_Department), drug squad, [special branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_branch), firearms, [scene of crime examiners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scenes_of_Crime_Officer), traffic, marine and operations units, sections or departments.

On 24 September 2015, the Freedom of the City of Gibraltar was conferred upon the RGP by the Mayor, Adolfo Canepa.

## Armed forces

The defence of Gibraltar, as a British territory, is the responsibility of the *national* (i.e., British) government, with its tri-services [British Forces Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Forces_Gibraltar):

- The [Royal Gibraltar Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Gibraltar_Regiment) provides the army garrison with a detachment of the [British Army](4887.md), based at [Devil's Tower Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil's_Tower_Camp).

  [^cite_note-198]

  &#x20;The regiment was originally a part-time reserve force until the British Army placed it on a permanent footing in 1990. The regiment includes full-time and part-time soldiers recruited from Gibraltar as well as British Army regulars posted from other regiments.

- The [Royal Navy](26061.md) maintains a [squadron of several patrol vessels and craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Squadron) at the Rock. The squadron is responsible for the security and integrity of British Gibraltar Territorial Waters (BGTW). The shore establishment at Gibraltar is called [HMS *Rooke*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Rooke) after [Sir George Rooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_George_Rooke), who captured the Rock for [Archduke Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_VI,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) (pretender to the Spanish throne) in 1704. The naval air base was named HMS *Cormorant.* Gibraltar's strategic position provides an important facility for the Royal Navy and Britain's allies. British and US nuclear submarines frequently visit the *Z berths* at Gibraltar.

  [^cite_note-199]

  &#x20;A [Z berth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berth_\(moorings\)) provides the facility for nuclear submarines to visit for operational or recreational purposes and for non-nuclear repairs. During the [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War), an Argentine plan to attack British shipping in the harbour using frogmen ([Operation Algeciras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Algeciras)) was foiled.

  [^cite_note-200]

  &#x20;The naval base also played a part in supporting the task force sent by Britain to recover the Falklands.

- The [Royal Air Force station](2563239.md) at Gibraltar forms part of Headquarters British Forces Gibraltar. Although aircraft are no longer permanently stationed at [RAF Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Gibraltar), a variety of RAF aircraft make regular visits and the airfield also houses a section from the [Met Office](319671.md). The [Gibraltar Air Cadets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Air_Cadets) is an active squadron.

  [^cite_note-201]

In January 2007, the Ministry of Defence announced that the private company [Serco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serco) would provide services to the base. The announcement resulted in extensive negotiations with the relevant trade unions.[^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203]

Gibraltar has an important role in [signals intelligence gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence), serving as a vital strategic part of the United Kingdom communications gathering and monitoring network in the Mediterranean and North Africa.[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205]



The [Royal Navy](26061.md)'s base in Gibraltar

Historically, Gibraltar was one of four [Imperial fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_fortress) colonies, along with [Bermuda](3460.md), [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia) , [Nova Scotia](21184.md) and [Malta](19137.md).
