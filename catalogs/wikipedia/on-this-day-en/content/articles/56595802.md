The **Embassy of the United States in Pretoria** is the [diplomatic mission](8970.md) of the United States of America in [South Africa](17416221.md).

## History

The United States recognized the Union of South Africa on November 5, 1929, with President [Herbert Hoover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Hoover) accepting the credentials of [Eric Louw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Louw) as diplomat of the Union of South Africa.[^cite_note-history.state.gov-1]

On December 21, 1948, the legations of South Africa and the United States were elevated to embassy status.[^cite_note-history.state.gov-1] This took effect on March 3, 1949, when H. T. Andrews presented his credentials as Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary of South Africa to President [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman), and on March 23, 1949, when North Winship presented his credentials in a similar capacity for the United States to the Government of the Union of South Africa.[^cite_note-history.state.gov-1]

The Republic of South Africa was established on May 31, 1961, and that day, U.S. Ambassador [Joseph C. Satterthwaite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_C._Satterthwaite) was reaccredited to the new republic.[^cite_note-history.state.gov-1]

During the subsequent decades and especially from the 1960s through early 1990s, relations between the United States and South Africa were significantly affected by South Africa's policy of apartheid.[^cite_note-diplomacy.state.gov-2] Following the [Sharpeville massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharpeville_massacre), President [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy)'s [administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administration_of_John_F._Kennedy) was taking a renewed look at the country.[^cite_note-3]
