**Same-sex marriage**, also known as **gay marriage** or **same-gender marriage**, is the [marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage) of two people of the same legal sex or [gender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_gender). As of 2026, marriage between [same-sex](5488304.md) couples is legally performed and recognized in 38 countries, with a total population of 1.5 billion people (20% of the world's population). The most recent jurisdiction to legalize same-sex marriage is [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Thailand); legalization has also been mandated for [Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Nepal).

Same-sex marriage is legally recognized in a majority of the world's [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country) and across much of the [Western world](21208200.md). Most jurisdictions which allow same-sex marriage also grant [adoption rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_adoption) to those couples. Some countries, such as [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_in_Nigeria) and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_in_Russia), restrict advocacy for same-sex marriage.[^cite_note-15] A few of these are among the 35 countries (as of 2023) that constitutionally define marriage as being between couples of the opposite sex, with most of those provisions enacted in recent decades as a preventative measure.&#x20;

In Europe, there are records of marriage between men dating back to the [first century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_century).[^cite_note-WilliamsRoman2-16] [Jack Baker and Michael McConnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Baker_and_Michael_McConnell)[^cite_note-auto-17][^cite_note-auto1-18] are the first same-sex couple in modern recorded history[^cite_note-auto2-19] known to obtain a [marriage license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage_license),[^cite_note-National_Archives-20] have their marriage solemnized, which occurred on September 3, 1971, in [Minnesota](19590.md),[^cite_note-3Sept1971-21] and have it legally recognized by any form of government.[^cite_note-ruling-22][^cite_note-epilogue-23] The first law providing for marriage equality between same-sex and opposite-sex couples was [passed in the continental Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_the_Netherlands) in 2000 and took effect on 1 April 2001.[^cite_note-bloomberg-2014-12-04-24] The application of [marriage law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage_law) equally to same-sex and opposite-sex couples has varied by jurisdiction, and has come about through legislative change to marriage law, court rulings based on constitutional guarantees of equality, recognition that marriage of same-sex couples is allowed by existing marriage law, and by direct popular vote, such as through [referendums](51582.md) and [initiatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_initiative).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] The most prominent supporters of same-sex marriage are the world's major medical and [scientific communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_community),[^cite_note-science-27][^cite_note-amici-28][^cite_note-cpa2006-29] [human rights](13831.md) and [civil rights](37071.md) organizations, and some progressive religious groups,[^cite_note-bbc-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] while its most prominent opponents are from conservative religious groups (some of which nonetheless support same-sex [civil unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_unions) providing legal protections for same-sex couples).[^cite_note-\:37-33][^cite_note-religion-34] [Polls consistently show](#Public_opinion) continually rising support for the recognition of same-sex marriage in most developed democracies and in many developing countries.&#x20;

Scientific studies show that the financial, psychological, and physical well-being of gay people is enhanced by marriage, and that the children of same-sex parents benefit from being raised by married same-sex couples within a marital union that is recognized by law and supported by societal institutions. At the same time, no harm is done to the institution of marriage among heterosexuals.[^cite_note-35] Social science research indicates that the exclusion of same-sex couples from marriage stigmatizes and invites public discrimination against gay and lesbian people, with research repudiating the notion that either civilization or viable social orders depend upon restricting marriage to heterosexuals.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-aaa-38] Same-sex marriage can provide those in committed same-sex relationships with relevant government services and make financial demands on them comparable to that required of those in opposite-sex marriages, and also gives them legal protections such as inheritance and hospital visitation rights.[^cite_note-39] Opposition is often based on [religious teachings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexuality_and_religion), such as the view that marriage is meant to be between one man and one woman, and that procreation is the natural goal of marriage.[^cite_note-CCC2357-40][^cite_note-religion-34] Other forms of opposition are based on claims such as that homosexuality is unnatural and abnormal, that the recognition of same-sex unions will promote homosexuality in society, and that children are better off when raised by opposite-sex couples. These claims are refuted by [scientific studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biology_and_sexual_orientation), which show that homosexuality is a natural and normal variation in human sexuality, that [sexual orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation) is not a choice, and that children of same-sex couples fare just as well as the children of opposite-sex couples.[^cite_note-science-27]

## Terminology

### Alternative terms



Wedding cake of a same sex marriage

Some proponents of the legal recognition of same-sex marriage—such as [Marriage Equality USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage_Equality_USA) (founded in 1998), [Freedom to Marry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_to_Marry) (founded in 2003), [Canadians for Equal Marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadians_for_Equal_Marriage), and [Marriage for All Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_LGBT-related_organizations_and_conferences#Japan)—used the terms *marriage equality* and *equal marriage* to signal that their goal was for same-sex marriage to be recognized on equal ground with opposite-sex marriage.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-Pratt-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-Ring-46] The [Associated Press](18935732.md) recommends the use of *same-sex marriage* over *gay marriage*.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] A further move to more inclusive language has been the shift from [same-sex relationship](19771642.md) to *same-gender relationship*, and similarly to *same-gender marriage* to not restrict individuals within the relationship by sexual orientation, as gender identity is a spectrum and noting that historical terms excluded members of the [LGBTQ+ community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ+_community) and were forms of [bisexual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisexual_erasure) or [transgender erasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_erasure).[^cite_note-SageEncyclopedia-50][^cite_note-51]

### Use of the term *marriage*

Anthropologists have struggled to determine a definition of [marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage) that absorbs commonalities of the [social construct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_constructionism) across cultures around the world.[^cite_note-Fedorak-52][^cite_note-Gough-53] Many proposed definitions have been criticized for failing to recognize the existence of same-sex marriage in some cultures, including those of more than 30 [African peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Africa), such as the [Kikuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikuyu_people) and [Nuer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuer_people).[^cite_note-Gough-53][^cite_note-Africa-54][^cite_note-Kikuyu-55]

With several countries revising their marriage laws to recognize same-sex couples in the 21st century, all major English dictionaries have revised their definition of the word marriage to either drop gender specifications or supplement them with secondary definitions to include gender-neutral language or explicit recognition of same-sex unions.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-ABA-57] The *[Oxford English Dictionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary)* has recognized same-sex marriage since 2000.[^cite_note-slate-58]

Opponents of same-sex marriage who want marriage to be restricted to pairings of one man and one woman, such as [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints), the [Catholic Church](606848.md), and the [Southern Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Baptist_Convention), use the term *traditional marriage* to mean opposite-sex marriage.[^cite_note-religion-34]

### Ancient

Further information: [Homosexuality in ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexuality_in_ancient_Rome)

The [Book of Leviticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Leviticus) prohibited homosexual relations, and the Hebrews were warned not to "follow the acts of the land of Egypt or the acts of the land of Canaan" (Lev. 18\:22, 20\:13). A reference to marriage between same-sex couples appears in the Rabbinic literature [Sifra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sifra), which was written between 200 to 300 CE. The Sifra clarifies what it calls ambiguous acts, and that they included marriage between same-sex couples: "A man would marry a man and a woman a woman, a man would marry a woman and her daughter, and a woman would be married to two men."[^cite_note-59]

A few scholars believe that in the early [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) some male couples were celebrating [traditional marriage rites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage_in_ancient_Rome) in the presence of friends. Male–male weddings are reported by sources that mock them; the feelings of the participants are not recorded.[^cite_note-60] Various ancient sources state that the emperor [Nero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nero) celebrated two public weddings with males, once taking the role of the bride (with a [freedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedman) [Pythagoras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagoras_\(freedman\))), and once the groom (with [Sporus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporus)); there may have been a third in which he was the bride.[^cite_note-61] In the early 3rd century AD, the emperor [Elagabalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elagabalus) is reported to have been the bride in a wedding to his male partner. Other mature men at his court had husbands, or said they had husbands in imitation of the emperor.[^cite_note-62] [Roman law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_law) did not recognize marriage between males, but one of the grounds for disapproval expressed in Juvenal's satire is that celebrating the rites would lead to expectations for such marriages to be registered officially.[^cite_note-auto4-63] As the empire was becoming Christianized in the 4th century, legal prohibitions against marriage between males began to appear.[^cite_note-auto4-63]

In 17th century China, writer Li Yu and scholar-bureaucrat Shen Defu wrote about a system of male marriages in the Fujian region. The older man in the union would play the masculine role as a qixiong or "adoptive older brother", paying a "[bride price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bride_price)" to the family of the younger man—it was said virgins fetched higher prices—who became the qidi, or "adoptive younger brother". Li Yu described the ceremony, "They do not skip the three cups of tea or the six wedding rituals- it is just like a proper marriage with a formal wedding."[^cite_note-64]

### Contemporary



Newly married couple in [Minnesota](19590.md) shortly after the federal legalization of [same-sex marriage in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_the_United_States), 2015

[Michael McConnell and Jack Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_McConnell_and_Jack_Baker)[^cite_note-auto-17][^cite_note-auto1-18] are the first same sex couple in modern recorded history[^cite_note-auto2-19] known to obtain a [marriage license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage_license),[^cite_note-National_Archives-20] have their marriage solemnized, which occurred on September 3, 1971, in [Minnesota](19590.md),[^cite_note-3Sept1971-21] and have it legally recognized by any form of government.[^cite_note-ruling-22][^cite_note-epilogue-23] Historians variously trace the beginning of the modern movement in support of same-sex marriage to anywhere from around the 1980s to the 1990s. During the 1980s in the [United States](3434750.md), the [AIDS epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS_in_the_United_States) led to increased attention on the legal aspects of same-sex relationships.[^cite_note-65] [Andrew Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Sullivan) made the first case for same sex marriage in a major American journal in 1989,[^cite_note-intelligent-66] published in *The New Republic*.[^cite_note-groom-67]

In 1989, Denmark became the first country to legally recognize a relationship for same-sex couples, establishing [registered partnerships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Registered_partnership), which gave those in same-sex relationships "most rights of married heterosexuals, but not the right to adopt or obtain joint custody of a child".[^cite_note-68] In 2001, the [continental Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_the_Netherlands) became the first country to broaden marriage laws to include same-sex couples.[^cite_note-bloomberg-2014-12-04-24][^cite_note-69] Since then, same-sex marriage has been established by law in 38 other countries, including most of the [Americas](29833.md) and [Western Europe](33800.md). However, its spread has been uneven — [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_South_Africa) is the only country in [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Africa) to take the step; [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Taiwan) and [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_Thailand) are the only ones in [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Asia).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

As of 2026, Bermuda is the first and only jurisdiction in the world that formally legalized same-sex marriage and where that legalization is now repealed.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

[Civil unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_union) are being considered in the [Philippines](71592773.md)[^cite_note-88] and [Poland](1238328.md).[^cite_note-89]

On 12 March 2015, the [European Parliament](9581.md) passed a non-binding resolution encouraging EU institutions and member states to "\[reflect] on the recognition of same-sex marriage or same-sex civil union as a political, social and human and civil rights issue".[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

In response to the international spread of same-sex marriage, a number of countries have enacted preventative [constitutional bans](23712287.md#Bans), with the most recent being [Gabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Gabonese_constitutional_referendum) in 2024. In other countries, such restrictions and limitations are effected through legislation. Even before same-sex marriage was first legislated, some countries had constitutions that specified that marriage was between one man and one woman.

### International court rulings

#### European Court of Human Rights

In 2010, the [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights) (ECHR) ruled in *[Schalk and Kopf v Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schalk_and_Kopf_v_Austria)*, a case involving an Austrian same-sex couple who were denied the right to marry.[^cite_note-CASE_OF_SCHALK_AND_KOPF_v._AUSTRIA-92] The court found, by a vote of 4 to 3, that their human rights had not been violated.[^cite_note-93] The court further stated that same-sex unions are not protected under art. 12 of ECHR ("Right to marry"), which exclusively protects the right to marry of opposite-sex couples (without regard if the sex of the partners is the result of birth or of sex change), but they are protected under art. 8 of ECHR ("Right to respect for private and family life") and art. 14 ("Prohibition of discrimination").[^cite_note-94]

[Article 12 of the European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_12_of_the_European_Convention_on_Human_Rights) states that: "Men and women of marriageable age have the right to marry and to found a family, according to the national laws governing the exercise of this right",[^cite_note-95] not limiting marriage to those in a heterosexual relationship. However, the ECHR stated in *Schalk and Kopf v Austria* that this provision was intended to limit marriage to heterosexual relationships, as it used the term "men and women" instead of "everyone".[^cite_note-CASE_OF_SCHALK_AND_KOPF_v._AUSTRIA-92] Nevertheless, the court accepted and is considering cases concerning same-sex marriage recognition, e.g. *Andersen v Poland*.[^cite_note-96] In 2021, the court ruled in *[Fedotova and Others v. Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fedotova_and_Others_v._Russia)*—followed by later judgements concerning other member states—that countries must provide some sort of legal recognition to same-sex couples, although not necessarily marriage.[^cite_note-97]

#### European Union

On 5 June 2018, the [European Court of Justice](87187.md) ruled, in [Coman and Others v General Inspectorate for Immigration and Ministry of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coman_and_Others_v_General_Inspectorate_for_Immigration_and_Ministry_of_the_Interior), that, under the specific conditions of the couple in question, married same-sex couples have the same residency rights as other married couples in an EU country, even if that country does not permit or recognize same-sex marriage.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] However, the ruling was not implemented in Romania, and on 14 September 2021 the [European Parliament](9581.md) passed a resolution calling on the [European Commission](9974.md) to ensure that the ruling is respected across the EU.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

On 25 November 2025, the [European Court of Justice](87187.md) established that all EU Member States must recognize same-sex marriages legally performed in other EU countries for other purposes, expanding on the Coman case.[^cite_note-102] The key provisions of the ruling are:

- Member States are required to recognize a marriage lawfully contracted in another Member State where at least one spouse is an EU citizen exercising their right to freedom of movement and residence.

- This recognition is mandatory for the purpose of exercising rights conferred by EU law, such as residency, tax benefits, inheritance, and social security.

- If a Member State provides a procedure for recognizing foreign marriages through registration (e.g., in Poland the term transcription, or transkrypcja, is used to enter a marriage contracted abroad into a civil register), it must apply that procedure equally to same-sex couples. It cannot refuse transcription solely because domestic law does not permit same-sex marriage.

However, the ruling does not require Member States to legalize same-sex marriage within their own domestic legal systems. It only mandates the recognition of marriages validly concluded elsewhere in the EU. The following EU countries, which previously did not recognize same-sex marriages, are directly affected by this ruling:[^cite_note-103] Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Czechia, Hungary, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Romania and Slovakia.

#### Inter-American Court of Human Rights



Alexandra Chávez and Michelle Avilés, the first same-sex couple to marry in Ecuador

On 8 January 2018, the [Inter-American Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-American_Court_of_Human_Rights) (IACHR) issued an advisory opinion that states party to the American Convention on Human Rights should grant same-sex couples accession to all existing domestic legal systems of family registration, including marriage, along with all rights that derive from marriage. The Court recommended that governments issue temporary decrees recognizing same-sex marriage until new legislation is brought in. They also said that it was inadmissible and discriminatory for a separate legal provision to be established (such as [civil unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_union)) instead of same-sex marriage.[^cite_note-104]

## Studies

&#x20;The [American Anthropological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Anthropological_Association) stated on 26 February 2004:

> The results of more than a century of anthropological research on households, kinship relationships, and families, across cultures and through time, provide no support whatsoever for the view that either civilization or viable social orders depend upon marriage as an exclusively heterosexual institution. Rather, anthropological research supports the conclusion that a vast array of family types, including families built upon same-sex partnerships, can contribute to stable and humane societies.[^cite_note-aaa-38]

Research findings from 1998 to 2015 from the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia), [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University), [Florida State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_State_University), the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam), the [New York State Psychiatric Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Psychiatric_Institute), [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University), the [University of California-San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California-San_Francisco), the [University of California-Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California-Los_Angeles), [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University), [Boston Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Medical_Center), the Committee on Psychosocial Aspects of Child and Family Health, and independent researchers also support the findings of this study.[^cite_note-120]

The overall socio-economic and health effects of legal access to same-sex marriage around the world have been summarized by Badgett and co-authors.[^cite_note-121] The review found that sexual minority individuals took-up legal marriage when it became available to them (but at lower rates than different-sex couples). There is instead no evidence that same-sex marriage legalization affected different-sex marriages. On the health side, same-sex marriage legalization increased health insurance coverage for individuals in same-sex couples (in the US), and it led to improvements in sexual health among men who have sex with men, while there is mixed evidence on mental health effects among sexual minorities. In addition, the study found mixed evidence on a range of downstream social outcomes such as attitudes toward LGBTQ+ people and employment choices of sexual minorities.

### Health

As of 2006, the data of current psychological and other social science studies on same-sex marriage in comparison to mixed-sex marriage indicate that same-sex and mixed-sex relationships do not differ in their essential psychosocial dimensions; that a parent's sexual orientation is unrelated to their ability to provide a healthy and nurturing family environment; and that marriage bestows substantial psychological, social, and health benefits. Same-sex parents and carers and their children are likely to benefit in numerous ways from legal recognition of their families, and providing such recognition through marriage will bestow greater benefit than civil unions or domestic partnerships.[^cite_note-aap2006-122][^cite_note-autogenerated4-123] Studies in the United States have correlated legalization of same-sex marriage to lower rates of HIV infection,[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125] psychiatric disorders,[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-psychtoday-127] and [suicide rate in the LGBT population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_among_LGBT_youth).[^cite_note-JAMA-128][^cite_note-129]

## Issues

See also: [LGBT rights opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_opposition)

While few societies have recognized same-sex unions as marriages, the [historical and anthropological record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_same-sex_unions) reveals a large range of attitudes towards same-sex unions ranging from praise, through full acceptance and integration, sympathetic toleration, indifference, prohibition and discrimination, to persecution and physical annihilation. Opponents of same-sex marriages have argued that same-sex marriage, while doing good for the couples that participate in them and the children they are raising,[^cite_note-Laurie-130] undermines a right of children to be raised by their biological mother and father.[^cite_note-131] Some supporters of same-sex marriages take the view that the government should have no role in regulating personal relationships,[^cite_note-findlaw1-132] while others argue that same-sex marriages would provide social benefits to same-sex couples.[^cite_note-133] The debate regarding same-sex marriages includes debate based upon social viewpoints as well as debate based on majority rules, religious convictions, economic arguments, health-related concerns, and a variety of other issues.

### Parenting

Main articles: [LGBT parenting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_parenting) and [Same-sex marriage and the family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_and_the_family)



Same-sex couple with a child

Scientific literature indicates that parents' financial, psychological and physical well-being is enhanced by marriage and that children benefit from being raised by two parents within a legally recognized union (either a mixed-sex or same-sex union). As a result, professional scientific associations have argued for same-sex marriage to be legally recognized as it will be beneficial to the children of same-sex parents or carers.[^cite_note-amici-28][^cite_note-cpa2006-29][^cite_note-aap2006-122][^cite_note-134][^cite_note-AAP-Discovery-135]

Scientific research has been generally consistent in showing that lesbian and gay parents are as fit and capable as heterosexual parents, and their children are as psychologically healthy and well-adjusted as children reared by heterosexual parents.[^cite_note-cpa2006-29][^cite_note-AAP-Discovery-135][^cite_note-apsp-136][^cite_note-amici2010-137] According to scientific literature reviews, there is no evidence to the contrary.[^cite_note-aap2006-122][^cite_note-herek-138][^cite_note-How_Does_the_Gender_of_Parents_Matter?-139][^cite_note-cpa2005-140]

Compared to heterosexual couples, same-sex couples have a greater need for [adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adoption) or [assisted reproductive technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_reproductive_technology) to become parents. Lesbian couples often use [artificial insemination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_insemination) to achieve pregnancy, and [reciprocal in vitro fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocal_in_vitro_fertilization) (where one woman provides the egg and the other gestates the child) is becoming more popular in the 2020s, although many couples cannot afford it. [Surrogacy](1514981.md) is an option for wealthier gay male couples, but the cost is prohibitive. Other same-sex couples adopt children or raise the children from earlier opposite-sex relationships.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

All states that allow same-sex marriage also allow the joint [adoption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adoption) of children by those couples with the exception of Ecuador and a third of states in Mexico, though such restrictions have been ruled unconstitutional in Mexico. Some additional states do not recognize same-sex marriage but allow stepchild adoption by couples in civil unions, namely the Czech Republic and San Marino.

See also: [Transgender rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_rights) and [Intersex human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersex_human_rights)

The legal status of same-sex marriage may have implications for the marriages of couples in which one or both parties are [transgender](19904525.md), depending on how sex is defined within a jurisdiction. Transgender and [intersex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersex) individuals may be prohibited from marrying partners of the "opposite" sex or permitted to marry partners of the "same" sex due to legal distinctions. In any legal jurisdiction where marriages are defined without distinction of a requirement of a male and female, these complications do not occur. In addition, some legal jurisdictions recognize a legal and official change of gender, which would allow a transgender male or female to be legally married in accordance with an adopted gender identity.[^cite_note-143]

In the United Kingdom, the *[Gender Recognition Act 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_Recognition_Act_2004)* allows a person who has lived in their chosen gender for at least two years to receive a gender recognition certificate officially recognizing their new gender. Because in the United Kingdom marriages were until recently only for mixed-sex couples and civil partnerships are only for same-sex couples, a person had to dissolve their civil partnership before obtaining a gender recognition certificate, and the same was formerly true for marriages in England and Wales, and still is in other territories. Such people are then free to enter or re-enter civil partnerships or marriages in accordance with their newly recognized gender identity. In Austria, a similar provision requiring transsexual people to divorce before having their [legal sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_sex) marker corrected was found to be unconstitutional in 2006.[^cite_note-144] In Quebec, prior to the legalization of same-sex marriage, only unmarried people could apply for legal change of gender. With the advent of same-sex marriage, this restriction was dropped. A similar provision including sterilization also existed in Sweden, but was phased out in 2013.[^cite_note-145] In the United States, transgender and intersex marriages was subject to legal complications.[^cite_note-146] As definitions and enforcement of marriage are defined by the states, these complications vary from state to state,[^cite_note-147] as some of them prohibit legal changes of gender.[^cite_note-148]

### Divorce

Main article: [Divorce of same-sex couples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorce_of_same-sex_couples)

In the United States before the case of *[Obergefell v. Hodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obergefell_v._Hodges)*, couples in same-sex marriages could only obtain a divorce in jurisdictions that recognized same-sex marriages, with some exceptions.[^cite_note-149]

### Judicial and legislative

Main article: [Conflict of marriage laws § Same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_marriage_laws#Same-sex_marriage)

There are differing positions regarding the manner in which same-sex marriage has been introduced into democratic jurisdictions. A "[majority rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_democracy)" position holds that same-sex marriage is valid, or void and illegal, based upon whether it has been accepted by a simple majority of voters or of their elected representatives.[^cite_note-USA_Today-150]

In contrast, a [civil rights](37071.md) view holds that the institution can be validly created through the ruling of an impartial judiciary carefully examining the questioning and finding that the right to marry regardless of the gender of the participants is guaranteed under the civil rights laws of the jurisdiction.[^cite_note-bbc-30]
