**Kristi Lynn Arnold Noem**[^cite_note-1] ([/noʊm/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*NOHM*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-2] [née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9e) **Arnold**; born November 30, 1971) is an American politician serving as the [United States special envoy](48752185.md) for the [Shield of the Americas](82587150.md) since 2026.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-ReutersNoemEnvoy-4] From 2025 to 2026, she served as the eighth [United States secretary of homeland security](212926.md). A member of the [Republican Party](32070.md), she served from 2019 to 2025 as the 33rd [governor of South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_South_Dakota) and represented [South Dakota's at-large congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota's_at-large_congressional_district) in the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md) from 2011 to 2019. During her tenure at the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) (DHS), her immigration policies generated controversies, particularly following the actions of [Immigration and Customs Enforcement](216537.md) (ICE).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

Born in [Watertown, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watertown,_South_Dakota), Noem began her political career in the [South Dakota House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_House_of_Representatives), serving from 2007 to 2011. She was elected as the first [female governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_female_governors_in_the_United_States) of South Dakota in 2018 with the endorsement of President [Donald Trump](4848272.md). She gained national attention during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) for opposing statewide [mask mandates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mask_mandates) in favor of voluntary measures. Noem has [conservative](6675.md) positions on most domestic issues, particularly gun rights, [abortion](765.md), and immigration.

In January 2026, Noem defended the killings of [Renée Good](82055905.md) and [Alex Pretti](82203651.md) by federal ICE agents, before any investigation, which led some lawmakers to call for her resignation or impeachment.[^cite_note-7] In March 2026, Noem appeared before the [Senate Committee on the Judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_the_Judiciary) after reports emerged of her alleged relationship with her subordinate, [Corey Lewandowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Lewandowski),[^cite_note-8] and of her misuse of government funds on television advertisements and private luxury jets,[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] as well as inappropriate use of U.S. Coast Guard assets and housing.

Her testimony before the committee intensified criticism from Congress. After the congressional hearing, Trump announced his decision to remove Noem from DHS.[^cite_note-CBS-Noem-Mullin-11] In March 2026, Noem was appointed special envoy for the Shield of the Americas, a US-led regional security organization.

## Early life and education

Noem was born Kristi Lynn Arnold to Corinne and Ron Arnold on November 30, 1971, in [Watertown, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watertown,_South_Dakota),[^cite_note-12] and raised with her siblings on the family ranch and farm near the town of [Hazel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazel,_South_Dakota).[^cite_note-HeadOfClass-13] She has [Norwegian ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Americans) and is a descendant of Ephraim Wilson, who fought in the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-plainsman-15] Noem was crowned South Dakota Snow Queen on January 13, 1990,[^cite_note-title_meant-16][^cite_note-17] when she was a senior at Hamlin High School in [Hayti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayti,_South_Dakota).

Noem attended [Northern State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_State_University) from 1990 to 1994 but did not graduate. In March 1994, her father was killed in a [grain bin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain_bin) accident and Noem left college early to run the family farm.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-HeadOfClass-13][^cite_note-poignant-19] Her daughter, Kassidy, was born weeks later, on April 21, 1994. She added a [hunting lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting_cabin) and restaurant to the family property. Her siblings also moved back to help expand the businesses.[^cite_note-HeadOfClass-13]

Noem subsequently took classes at the Watertown campus of [Mount Marty College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Marty_College) and at [South Dakota State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_State_University), and online classes from the [University of South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Dakota).[^cite_note-HeadOfClass-13][^cite_note-title_meant-16][^cite_note-20] She earned a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) degree with a major in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) from [South Dakota State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_State_University#Political_Science_Department) in 2012[^cite_note-degree-21] while serving as a U.S. representative.[^cite_note-22] *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* dubbed her Capitol Hill's "most powerful intern" for receiving college intern credits from her position as a member of Congress.[^cite_note-23]

## South Dakota House of Representatives (2007–2011)

In 2006, Noem won a seat as a Republican in the [South Dakota House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_House_of_Representatives), representing the 6th district, comprising parts of [Beadle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beadle_County,_South_Dakota), [Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County,_South_Dakota), [Codington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codington_County,_South_Dakota), [Hamlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlin_County,_South_Dakota), and [Kingsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsbury_County,_South_Dakota) counties. In 2006, she won with 39% of the vote,[^cite_note-24] and received \$6330 in direct contributions to her campaign.[^cite_note-25] In 2008, she was reelected with 41% of the vote.[^cite_note-26]

Noem served for four years, from 2007 to 2010. She was an assistant majority leader during her second term.[^cite_note-argusleader.com-27][^cite_note-historical_listing-28] During her tenure, Noem was the prime sponsor of 11 bills that became law, including several property tax reforms and two bills to increase gun rights in South Dakota.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] In 2009, she served as vice chair of the Agriculture Land Assessment Advisory Task Force. Senator [Larry Rhoden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Rhoden) chaired the task force, and later served as her lieutenant governor.[^cite_note-32] During her tenure, she joined the [Civil Air Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Air_Patrol) as a "state legislative member".[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

## U.S. House of Representatives (2011–2019)

See also: [2010 United States House of Representatives election in South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_House_of_Representatives_election_in_South_Dakota)

In 2010, Noem ran for [South Dakota's at-large seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota's_at-large_congressional_district) in the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md).[^cite_note-wins_primary-35] She won the Republican primary[^cite_note-scb20100610-36] and defeated incumbent Democrat [Stephanie Herseth Sandlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Herseth_Sandlin) in the general election. Noem was reelected three times, serving in Congress until 2019.[^cite_note-argusleader.com-27]

### Tenure



Official [112th United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/112th_United_States_Congress) portrait, 2011

The 2011 House Republican 87-member freshman class elected Noem as liaison to the House Republican leadership, making her the second woman member of the House GOP leadership.[^cite_note-TheHill111710-37] According to *[The Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hill_\(newspaper\))*, her role was to push the leadership to make significant cuts to federal government spending and to help Speaker [John Boehner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Boehner) manage the expectations of the freshman class.[^cite_note-TheHill01012011-38] In March 2011, Republican Representative [Pete Sessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Sessions) of Texas named Noem one of the 12 regional directors for the [National Republican Congressional Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Republican_Congressional_Committee) during the 2012 election campaign.[^cite_note-HeadOfClass-13][^cite_note-RegDirector-39]

On March 8, 2011, she announced the formation of a leadership [political action committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_action_committee), KRISTI PAC.[^cite_note-backronym-40] Former South Dakota [Lieutenant Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_South_Dakota) [Steve Kirby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_T._Kirby) is its treasurer.[^cite_note-PACFormed-41][^cite_note-freshman_forming-42][^cite_note-PoliticoPAC-43] Noem was among the top freshman Republicans in PAC fundraising in the first quarter of 2011, raising \$169,000 from PACs.[^cite_note-influence_industry-44]

#### Abortion and stem cells

In August 2010, while running for Congress, Noem indicated on a questionnaire from the [Christian Coalition of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Coalition_of_America) that she would vote to ban [in-vitro fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-vitro_fertilization) and embryonic stem-cell research.[^cite_note-ab-45]

Noem co-sponsored legislation that would federally ban abortion.[^cite_note-46] In 2015, she co-sponsored a bill to amend the [14th Amendment](31666.md) to define human life and personhood as beginning at [fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilisation), federally banning abortion from the moment of fertilization, without exceptions. She also voted for a bill to ban abortion after 20 weeks of pregnancy.[^cite_note-ab-45]

#### Energy and environment

Noem has expressed skepticism about the [scientific consensus on climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_consensus_on_climate_change). In 2022 she said she believes "the science has been varied on it, and it hasn't been proven to me that what we're doing is affecting the climate."[^cite_note-47]

Noem has said that the U.S. needs an "all-of-the-above energy approach" that includes renewables like wind and ethanol while still realizing the need for a "balanced energy mix" that ends American dependence on [foreign oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_energy_independence).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-NoemJoinsThune-50][^cite_note-51]

Noem supported the [Keystone XL Pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_Pipeline)[^cite_note-LarsenKCSR11182014-52] and supports [offshore oil drilling](9969688.md).[^cite_note-JournalOffshore-53] She co-sponsored three bills that she argued would reduce American dependence on foreign oil by ending the [2010 United States deepwater drilling moratorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_deepwater_drilling_moratorium) in the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md) and reopening sales on [oil leases in the Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_oil_and_gas_in_the_Gulf_of_Mexico_\(United_States\)) and [off the coast of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_drilling_on_the_Atlantic_coast_of_the_United_States).[^cite_note-FoxTVOffshore-54] In 2011, she sponsored a measure to block [Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md) funding for tighter air pollution standards for [coarse particulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulate_matter).[^cite_note-55]

Noem opposed a bill introduced by South Dakota Senator [Tim Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Johnson_\(U.S._Senator\)) that would designate over 48,000 acres (190 km<sup>2</sup>) of the [Buffalo Gap National Grassland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Gap_National_Grassland) as [protected wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Wilderness_Preservation_System).[^cite_note-56] She supports the designation of the land as a [national grassland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_National_Grassland).[^cite_note-BuffaloGap03202011-57] She said the land is already managed as roadless areas similar to wilderness[^cite_note-BuffaloGapAL09102011-58] and argued that changing the land's designation to wilderness would further limit [leaseholder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaseholder) access to the land and imperil [grazing rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazing_rights).[^cite_note-BuffaloGap03202011-57][^cite_note-BuffaloGapAL09102011-58]

#### Foreign affairs

From 2013 to 2015, Noem served on the House Armed Services Committee, where she worked on the 2014 National Defense Authorization Act.[^cite_note-59] Her appointment to the committee was seen as a benefit to South Dakota's [Ellsworth Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellsworth_Air_Force_Base).[^cite_note-60] In March 2011, Noem was critical of President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama)'s approach to the [NATO-led military intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_military_intervention_in_Libya) in the [2011 Libyan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Libyan_civil_war), calling on him to provide more information about the U.S.'s role in the conflict, and characterizing his statements as vague and ambiguous.[^cite_note-Gaddafi-61][^cite_note-WischmeyerLibya-62]

#### Health care

Noem opposes the [Affordable Care Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_Care_Act) (Obamacare) and has voted to repeal it.[^cite_note-NYTObamaCare-63][^cite_note-wants_to_stop-64] Having unsuccessfully sought to repeal it, she sought to defund it while retaining measures such as the Indian Health Care Improvement Act, the provision allowing parents to keep their children on their health insurance plan into their 20s, and the high-risk pools.[^cite_note-NoemHealthCareAlt-65] Noem wanted to add such provisions to federal law as limits on [medical malpractice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_malpractice) lawsuits and allowing patients to buy health insurance plans from other states.[^cite_note-NoemHealthCareAlt-65] She supported cuts to [Medicaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicaid) funding proposed by Republican Budget Committee chairman [Paul Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ryan). A study found that this action would reduce benefits for South Dakota Medicaid recipients by 55 percent.[^cite_note-NoemJoinsThune-50]

#### Immigrants and refugees

Noem supported President [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s 2017 [Executive Order 13769](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_13769), which suspended the U.S. refugee program for 120 days and banned all travel to the U.S. by nationals of seven [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim)-majority countries for 90 days.[^cite_note-Ferguson-66] She said she supported a temporary ban on accepting refugees from "terrorist-held" areas,[^cite_note-Blake1-67] but "did not address whether she supports other aspects of the order, which led to the detention of legal U.S. residents such as green-card holders, and people with dual citizenship as they reentered the country" in the aftermath of the order's issuance.[^cite_note-Ferguson-66]

In 2019, Noem consented to South Dakota's participation in the [U.S. Refugee Resettlement Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Refugee_Admissions_Program) following a Trump executive order that allowed state and local governments to opt out.[^cite_note-68]

#### Taxes

In 2017, Noem was on the conference committee that negotiated the passage of the [Tax Cuts and Jobs Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_Cuts_and_Jobs_Act_of_2017), which she touted as giving the average South Dakota family a \$1,200 tax cut.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

In 2018, Noem was reported to have "pitched the idea to members of the conservative [House Freedom Caucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Freedom_Caucus)" to attach her online sales tax bill to the government funding package as part of an omnibus. A court case under consideration in the [South Dakota Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_Supreme_Court) involved requiring "certain out-of-state retailers to collect its sales taxes." Noem said that South Dakota businesses (and by extension businesses nationwide) "could be forced to comply with 1,000 different tax structures nationwide without the tools necessary to do so", adding that her legislation "provides a necessary fix."[^cite_note-71]

Noem has called the [budget deficit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_budget_deficit) one of the most important issues facing Congress. She cosponsored H. J. Res. 2, which would require that total spending for any [fiscal year](5752099.md) not exceed total receipts.[^cite_note-LawrenceNoemPushes-72][^cite_note-under_40-73] She cited the [Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md), the [Department of Veterans Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Veterans_Affairs), [Medicaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicaid), [high-speed rail projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail), [cap-and-trade](37104.md) technical assistance, and subsidies for the [Washington Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Metro) rapid transit system as examples of federal programs where she would like to see cuts.[^cite_note-NoemJoinsThune-50][^cite_note-LawrenceNoemPushes-72][^cite_note-does_not_name-74][^cite_note-RCJVets-75]

In 2011, Noem indicated that she would vote to raise the federal [debt ceiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_debt_ceiling), but only if "tied to budget reforms that change the way we spend our dollars and how Washington, D.C., does business. It won't just be a one-time spending cut."[^cite_note-Miller2011-76] She ultimately voted for S. 365, The Budget Control Act of 2011, which allowed Obama to raise the debt ceiling in exchange for spending cuts to be decided by a bipartisan committee.[^cite_note-FWscorecard-77] She also said she wanted to eliminate the [estate tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estate_tax),[^cite_note-BendavidWSJ-78] lower the corporate tax rate, and simplify the [tax code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Revenue_Code).[^cite_note-HeadOfClass-13] She said she would not raise taxes to balance the budget.[^cite_note-ArgusLead-79]

## Governor of South Dakota (2019–2025)



Official gubernatorial portrait, 2023

### Elections

#### 2018

Main article: [2018 South Dakota gubernatorial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_South_Dakota_gubernatorial_election)

In November 2016, Noem announced she would run for [governor of South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_South_Dakota) [in 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_South_Dakota_gubernatorial_election) rather than seek reelection to Congress.[^cite_note-84] She defeated South Dakota Attorney General [Marty Jackley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_Jackley) in the June Republican primary, 56 to 44 percent,[^cite_note-SDSOS06062018-85][^cite_note-86] and [Democratic](5043544.md) nominee [Billie Sutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Sutton) in the general election, 51 to 48 percent.[^cite_note-South_Dakota_Election_Results-87]

#### 2022

Main article: [2022 South Dakota gubernatorial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_South_Dakota_gubernatorial_election)

In November 2021, Noem announced she was running for reelection as governor.[^cite_note-88] State Representative [Steven Haugaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Haugaard), a Republican, announced he was running against Noem.[^cite_note-89] In February 2022, House Democratic Minority Leader [Jamie Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Smith_\(politician\)) announced he was seeking the Democratic nomination.[^cite_note-90]

In the Republican primary in June, Noem defeated Haugaard, 76% to 24%. In the general election, she defeated Smith, 62% to 35%. Despite predictions of a competitive race, Noem flipped 17 counties that had previously voted Democratic and set a record for the most votes received by a candidate for governor in South Dakota.

### Tenure

Noem was sworn in as South Dakota governor on January 5, 2019. She is the first woman to hold that office.[^cite_note-91]

#### Abortion

Noem is [anti-abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-abortion).[^cite_note-long_after-92] She has been lauded by the anti-abortion group [Susan B. Anthony List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_B._Anthony_List)[^cite_note-93] and said she intends to maintain her 100% anti-abortion voting record.[^cite_note-BendavidWSJ-78][^cite_note-94]

In 2019, Noem signed bills restricting abortion, saying they would "crack down on abortion providers in South Dakota" and that a "strong and growing body of medical research provides evidence that unborn babies can feel, think, and recognize sounds in the womb. These are people, they must be given the same basic dignities as anyone else."[^cite_note-95]

Following the [overturning of *Roe v. Wade*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobbs_v._Jackson_Women's_Health_Organization), South Dakota became one of the first states to enact [trigger laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigger_law) banning abortions.[^cite_note-Iyer-96] Noem defended South Dakota's abortion ban, which only allows exceptions in cases in which the mother's life is in danger. When asked about [the case of the 10-year-old child abuse victim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ohio_child-rape_and_Indiana_abortion_case) who traveled from Ohio to Indiana to receive an abortion, Noem said she would not support changing the law to allow exceptions for rape victims, explaining that she did not "believe a tragic situation should be perpetuated by another tragedy".[^cite_note-Iyer-96]

Noem proclaimed 2024 the "Freedom for Life Year", promoting anti-abortion laws.[^cite_note-97] In April 2024, she announced that she had reversed her support for a federal ban on abortion, saying she believed abortion law should be determined at the state level, and continued to support South Dakota's law banning abortion except to save the life of the pregnant patient, without exceptions for rape or incest.[^cite_note-98]

#### Early childhood education

Noem is a vocal opponent of subsidized child care. Her administration rejected \$7.5 million in federal funding for free summer meal programs for low-income residents and defeated multiple attempts to provide school lunches for eligible students. In 2023, Noem said, "I just don't think it's the government's job to pay or to raise people's children for them".[^cite_note-99]

#### Access to public records

While running for governor in 2018, Noem made government transparency part of her platform.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] In her first State of the State address she pledged to "work toward building the most transparent administration South Dakota has ever seen".[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

Throughout her tenure, news outlets and government transparency advocates sued Noem for failing to provide the transparency she had advocated for.[^cite_note-Tupper-104][^cite_note-105] Complaints included denial of immediate access to a state-funded report about the alleged presence of [critical race theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020s_controversies_around_critical_race_theory) and "divisive concepts" in South Dakota schools;[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] denial of access to pardon records;[^cite_note-Tupper-104] not releasing the cost of the governor's security team;[^cite_note-apnews.com-109][^cite_note-110] whipping votes against a bill to make public records of the cost of the governor's security;[^cite_note-apnews.com-109][^cite_note-111] and attempts to seal records on an ethics investigation involving her daughter.[^cite_note-112]

#### Anti-protest legislation

In response to protests against the [Keystone Pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_Pipeline), Noem's office collaborated with the energy company [TransCanada Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransCanada_Corporation) to develop anti-protest legislation, which Noem signed into law in 2019. The law created a fund to cover the costs of policing pipeline protests. Another law was passed to raise revenue for the fund by creating [civil penalties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_penalties) for advising, directing, or encouraging participation in rioting. The [Pine Ridge Indian Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Ridge_Indian_Reservation) banned Noem from their grounds as a result. The [Indigenous Environmental Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_Environmental_Network), [Sierra Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Club), and other groups challenged the laws in suits, arguing that they violated [First Amendment rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Amendment_rights) by incentivizing the state to sue protesters.[^cite_note-113] In 2020, after a federal court struck down sections of the legislation as unconstitutional, Noem brought legislation to repeal sections of the previous bill and clarify the definition of "incitement to riot".[^cite_note-114]

#### China

Noem has called China "an enemy" of the U.S.[^cite_note-115] In 2022, she issued an order banning [TikTok](56822861.md) from state-owned devices, saying the "[Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) uses information it gathers on TikTok to manipulate the American people".[^cite_note-116] In 2023, she signed an order prohibiting the downloading or use of any application or visiting of any site owned by the Chinese company [Tencent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tencent), including [WeChat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WeChat), on state-owned devices.[^cite_note-117] In 2024, she signed a bill prohibiting the governments of six countries—China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia, and Venezuela—and entities from those countries from buying agricultural land in South Dakota.[^cite_note-118]

#### Conflict of interest action to professionally benefit daughter

In 2020, after Noem's 26-year-old daughter,[^cite_note-hypocrisy-119] Kassidy Peters, was denied a real estate appraisal license, Noem summoned to her office Sherry Bren, a state employee who had directed South Dakota's Appraiser Certification Program for 30 years.[^cite_note-summoned-120] Attendees included Peters, Noem's chief of staff Tony Venhuizen,[^cite_note-121] Department of Labor Attorney Amber Mulder and Labor Secretary Marcia Hultman.[^cite_note-goa12142021-122]

By telephone, the group was joined by the governor's general counsel, Tom Hart, and a lawyer from the state's Department of Labor and Regulation, Graham Oey.[^cite_note-summoned-120] A week later, Hultman demanded Bren's resignation. Bren repeatedly tried to resolve the issues short of resigning, but unsuccessfully, eventually filing an age discrimination complaint.[^cite_note-summoned-120] She received a \$200,000 settlement as part of a nondisclosure agreement to withdraw her complaint and leave her position.[^cite_note-123] Noem's spokesperson characterized the allegations as an example of how Noem cut through "bureaucratic red tape".[^cite_note-summoned-120]

After the [Associated Press](18935732.md) published a story about the incident, the [State Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_Senate)'s Government Operations and Audit Committee was delegated to investigate.[^cite_note-Max-124] In October 2021, the Committee invited Hultman and Bren to come before it to discuss the appraisal program in light of the controversy.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126] On December 14, 2021, Bren testified before the Government Operations and Audit Committee.[^cite_note-goa12142021-122] She said that Peters received an Agreed Disposition around March/April 2020. Around July 20, 2020, Peters received a letter and/or Findings of Fact and Conclusions of Law when she failed to meet the requirements of the Agreed Disposition. Bren said that on July 26, Department of Labor attorney Amber Mulder told her to be prepared to discuss "what is the definition of a serious deficiency; what criteria do you use for denials; how many are denied each year; how many are approved; are we saying that Kassidy can take certain classes and resubmit".[^cite_note-goa12142021-122]

Bren said she felt "very nervous" and "intimidated" when meeting with Noem and attorneys and Labor Secretary Hultman.[^cite_note-127] Bren mentioned during the meeting at the [mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_Governor's_Residence) some appraisal classes that she thought would be helpful to Peters. Bren said that Noem was upset that she was just now hearing about the classes. Bren testified that the decision to depart from recognized upgrade procedures and offer a third opportunity would be Hultman's. Bren said this was beyond the recognized procedures and "not normal."[^cite_note-goa12142021-122]

On November 1, 2021, the Government Accountability Board set an agenda to discuss this issue and another issue based on complaints brought by Ravnsborg.[^cite_note-128] On December 15, 2021, the Government Accountability Board referred one of the two complaints to Noem for a response and sent the other back to the complainant for further information.[^cite_note-129] On February 3, 2022, the Government Accountability Board referred the second complaint to Noem for a response and gave her until April 15, 2022, to answer both pending complaints.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

On February 24, 2022, Republican State Representative [John Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mills_\(South_Dakota_politician\)) introduced House Resolution 7004, "Addressing the Governor's unacceptable actions in matters related to the appraiser certification program", against Noem.[^cite_note-132] On March 1, the resolution was debated and failed by a margin of 29 to 38 with three excused, including Noem's primary opponent [Steven Haugaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Haugaard) and U.S. House candidate [Taffy Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taffy_Howard).[^cite_note-133]

#### Conflict with Native American tribes

In 2024, it was reported that all nine tribes of South Dakota banned Noem from entering any tribal lands, prohibiting her from entering almost 20% of South Dakota.[^cite_note-Robertson-134] Other media reported that one of the nine tribes, the [Yankton Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankton_Sioux), had not officially banned Noem.[^cite_note-Terrall-135] The [Oglala Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oglala_Sioux) banned Noem in February, followed by the [Cheyenne River Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheyenne_River_Sioux), the [Standing Rock Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Rock_Sioux), and the [Rosebud Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosebud_Sioux) in April, and the [Sisseton Wahpeton Oyate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisseton_Wahpeton_Oyate), the [Crow Creek Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crow_Creek_Sioux), and the [Flandreau Santee Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flandreau_Santee_Sioux) in May.[^cite_note-Terrall-135]

The tribes took action after demanding that Noem apologize for her comments about them.[^cite_note-Schafer-136] In January 2024, Noem said that an "invasion is coming over the southern border" of the United States, and the "enemy is the Mexican drug cartels", which are "perpetrating violence in each of our states, even here in South Dakota ... The cartels are using our reservations to facilitate the spread of drugs throughout the Midwest."[^cite_note-137] In March 2024, Noem said there were "some tribal leaders that I believe are personally benefiting from the cartels being there", but gave no evidence, and that there were people "who actually live in those situations, who call me and text me every day and say, 'Please, dear governor, please come help us in [Pine Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Ridge_Indian_Reservation). We are scared.'"[^cite_note-Funk-138][^cite_note-139] She added: "they live with 80% to 90% unemployment. Their kids don't have any hope. They don't have parents who show up and help them."[^cite_note-Schafer-136]

Around January 2025, Noem apologized to the tribes for the misunderstanding between them, and the Flandreau Santee Sioux tribe dissolved its order banning Noem from its land.[^cite_note-PBS-Noem-140] The tribal leaders said Noem had committed to protecting the people of South Dakota, including the citizens of the nine Tribal Nations, and expressed support for her nomination as Secretary of Homeland Security.[^cite_note-PBS-Noem-140] In January 2026, amid reports of ICE agents targeting Indigenous people in Minnesota, tribal leaders from the Dakotas expressed their displeasure and concern with Noem and ICE policies that caused fear among tribal citizens.[^cite_note-IC-Dakota-Natives-141][^cite_note-IC-South_Dakota-142]

#### COVID-19 pandemic

During the [COVID-19 pandemic in South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_South_Dakota), Noem initially supported some containment measures before adopting a largely hands‑off approach.[^cite_note-closures-143][^cite_note-resistance-144] In November 2020, she used pandemic relief funds to promote tourism during a surge in cases.[^cite_note-ad-145] She was one of few governors who did not maintain statewide [stay-at-home orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stay-at-home_orders) or mask mandates; her response broadly mirrored Donald Trump's approach, and she was given a speaking slot at the [2020 Republican National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Republican_National_Convention), which raised her national profile.[^cite_note-AP200505-146][^cite_note-ABC201116-147][^cite_note-denial-148][^cite_note-AP200806-149][^cite_note-PBS200826-150][^cite_note-151]

Early in the pandemic, Noem asked the legislature to give the state health secretary and county officials authority to close businesses; the House rejected the bill.[^cite_note-closures-143][^cite_note-resistance-144] On March 13, 2020, she ordered K‑12 schools to close, and on April 6 she extended that order for the rest of the school year; she also directed businesses and local governments to follow CDC social‑distancing guidance.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-NoemApril62020-153]

Noem emphasized South Dakota's role in evaluating [hydroxychloroquine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroxychloroquine), which was later found to be ineffective against COVID-19 and to cause fatal cardiac [arrhythmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrhythmia).[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-pork-155]

In early 2020, one of the largest COVID [outbreaks in the US](63136490.md) occurred in South Dakota.[^cite_note-156] The [Smithfield Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithfield_Foods) plant in [Sioux Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux_Falls,_South_Dakota) reported four deaths and nearly 1,300 infections among workers and family members.[^cite_note-157] [Secretary of Health and Human Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_Health_and_Human_Services) [Alex Azar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Azar) told legislators that infections were likely due to workers' "home and social" habits; on April 13, Noem said, "We believe that 99 percent of what's going on today wasn't happening inside" the plant.[^cite_note-USDA-158] The Food Safety and Inspection Service initially did not give inspectors PPE and discouraged masks. It later permitted and then supplied them.[^cite_note-USDA-158] Noem said the plant was operating as an essential facility;[^cite_note-159] 48 workers were hospitalized.[^cite_note-Salon-160]

Noem did not mandate social distancing or [mask‑wearing](63631542.md) at the July 3, 2020, event at [Mount Rushmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Rushmore) with Trump present.[^cite_note-161] She publicly doubted mask effectiveness, citing the [Association of American Physicians and Surgeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Physicians_and_Surgeons) in a *[Rapid City Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_City_Journal)* opinion piece.[^cite_note-good-162]

COVID cases rose sharply after the 2020 [Sturgis Motorcycle Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturgis_Motorcycle_Rally), in which Noem participated; hospitalizations reached a then‑record 355 on October 22 (75 in ICUs). Health systems postponed elective procedures and urged masking; [Sioux Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux_Falls) Mayor [Paul TenHaken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_TenHaken) told residents, "Wear a dang mask."[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164][^cite_note-good-162]

Sixteen weeks after Trump's 2020 executive order providing a \$300 weekly unemployment supplement, Noem declined the money, citing low unemployment; South Dakota was the only state to do so.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-UI-166] The jobless rate in June was 7.2%, up from 3.1% in March and down from 11% in April.[^cite_note-Salon-160]

#### Department of Corrections

In July 2021, Noem placed Secretary of the [Department of Corrections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_Department_of_Corrections) Mike Liedholt on administrative leave, and fired [South Dakota State Penitentiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_State_Penitentiary) Warden Darin Young and Deputy Warden Jennifer Dreiske, after receiving an anonymous note with complaints regarding pay, medical coverage and instances of sexual harassment.[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177] Liedholt later announced his retirement.[^cite_note-178] Later that month, after meeting with prison employees, despite lingering COVID-19 cases, Noem ended the prison's mask mandate.[^cite_note-179]

In August 2021, Noem announced that the CGL Group, a California-based company, was hired for \$166,410 to conduct a comprehensive review of the Department of Corrections' operations.[^cite_note-180] At the same time, the director of the prison work program was fired, and two other DOC employees relieved of their duties.

The prison work program director, Stephany Bawek, subsequently filed a complaint with the [U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Equal_Employment_Opportunity_Commission) (EEOC), alleging that she was retaliated against for reporting sexual harassment by Young.[^cite_note-181] On March 14, 2022, Bawek filed a lawsuit in federal district court alleging that she was fired for reporting incidents of sexual harassment in the workplace.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183]

#### Deployment of South Dakota National Guard to southern border (2021)

In June 2021, Noem announced that she was sending members of the [South Dakota National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_National_Guard) to Texas's border with Mexico.[^cite_note-184] Tennessee billionaire [Willis Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willis_Johnson) said he would donate the money necessary for the deployment.[^cite_note-185] On September 22, 2021, the [Center for Public Integrity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Public_Integrity) sued the South Dakota National Guard and the U.S. Department of Defense in the federal district court in the District of Columbia to obtain documents about the deployment and the donation.[^cite_note-186] The [2022 National Defense Authorization Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2022) banned National Guard members from crossing state borders to perform duties paid for by private donors.[^cite_note-187]

#### Fireworks at Mount Rushmore lawsuit (2021)

In 2021, Noem sued U.S. secretary of the interior [Deb Haaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deb_Haaland), seeking to have fireworks at [Mount Rushmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Rushmore) for [Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(United_States\)). Fireworks displays had been halted at the site in 2009 by the [National Park Service](161535.md) due to fire risks and other reasons.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189] Noem hired the private Washington D.C. law firm Consovoy McCarthy to bring the case, with South Dakota state taxpayer money paying for the suit.[^cite_note-190] The U.S. District Court dismissed the suit, with Judge [Roberto Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Lange) finding that four of the five reasons given by the NPS and Secretary Haaland were valid.[^cite_note-191] On July 13, Noem filed an appeal with the [8th Circuit Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Circuit_Court_of_Appeals).[^cite_note-192]

On March 14, 2022, the National Park Service again denied Noem's application for a permit to have fireworks at Mount Rushmore for the 4th of July, citing opposition from Native American groups and the possibility of wildfires.[^cite_note-193]

In May 2019, Noem proposed to build a fence around the governor's mansion, estimated to cost approximately \$400,000, but retracted the proposal.[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195] In 2020, the 2019 project was revived; a senior Noem advisor told the media that the decision was based on the recommendations of Noem's security team.[^cite_note-196] In late November 2021, it was reported that Noem spent \$68,000 of taxpayer dollars on imported rugs from India, chandeliers and a sauna for the mansion.[^cite_note-197]

In 2019, Noem signed a bill into law abolishing South Dakota's permit requirement to carry a concealed handgun.[^cite_note-198][^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200] In 2022, she sought to build a gun range in [Meade County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meade_County,_South_Dakota) with government funds, but the legislature rejected it.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203]

#### LGBTQ rights

Noem opposes [same-sex marriage](92656.md). In 2015, she said she disagreed with *[Obergefell v. Hodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obergefell_v._Hodges)*, the Supreme Court's ruling that same-sex marriage bans are unconstitutional.[^cite_note-205]

On March 8, 2021, Noem announced on Twitter that she would sign into law H.B. 1217, the [Women's Fairness in Sports Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairness_in_Women's_Sports_Act),[^cite_note-206] which bans [transgender](19904525.md) athletes from playing on or against women's school and college sports teams. Some critics of the bill said they were worried it might turn away business and cost the state money.[^cite_note-207] On March 19, Noem issued a style and form veto to H.B. 1217 that substantially altered the bill, not just correcting grammar and spelling mistakes.[^cite_note-208] She defended her position on *[Tucker Carlson Tonight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson_Tonight)*.[^cite_note-209]

On March 29, the [South Dakota House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_House_of_Representatives) rejected Noem's veto, 67–2.[^cite_note-210] The bill was returned to Noem for reconsideration, and she vetoed it again.[^cite_note-211] The House failed to override her veto, by a vote of 45–24. 47 votes were needed to override.[^cite_note-212] Many conservative commentators criticized Noem for vetoing the bill.[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214]

In December 2021, Noem and her office signaled their support for a bill called "An Act to Protect Fairness in Women's Sports." The bill would require young athletes to join teams that align with their sex assigned at birth.[^cite_note-215]

In 2021, Noem signed a religious refusal bill into law. The legislation amended the [state RFRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Religious_Freedom_Restoration_Acts) to allow business owners to cite religious beliefs as a basis to deny products or services to people based on sexual orientation or gender identity.[^cite_note-216] The legislation, S.B. 124, was criticized by civil rights groups who said it would enable discrimination against [LGBTQ+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ+) people, women, and members of minority faiths.[^cite_note-lgbtq-217][^cite_note-218] This bill was the first major state RFRA law signed into law in six years, and resembles the 2015 bill signed into law by Indiana Governor [Mike Pence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pence).[^cite_note-pence-219]

#### "Meth. We're on It" campaign

On November 18, 2019, Noem released a [meth](11038318.md) awareness campaign named "Meth. We're on It". The campaign was widely mocked and Noem was criticized for spending \$449,000 of [public funds](1203070.md) while hiring an out-of-state advertising agency from Minnesota to lead the project.[^cite_note-220] She defended the campaign as successful in raising awareness.[^cite_note-221]

#### Opposition to cannabis legalization

In 2020, Noem opposed two ballot measures to legalize cannabis for [medical use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_cannabis_in_the_United_States) and [recreational use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legalization_of_non-medical_cannabis_in_the_United_States) in South Dakota,[^cite_note-222] saying, "The fact is, I've never met someone who got smarter from smoking pot. It's not good for our kids. And it's not going to improve our communities."[^cite_note-223] After both measures passed, she and two police officers filed a lawsuit seeking a court decision against the measure legalizing recreational use, [Amendment A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_South_Dakota_Amendment_A).[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225]

On February 8, 2021, circuit court judge Christina Klinger struck down the amendment as unconstitutional.[^cite_note-AP210208-226] After the ruling, she also sought to delay the implementation of the medical marijuana initiative for a year.[^cite_note-227] Ultimately, her efforts failed and medical marijuana became legal on July 1, 2021.[^cite_note-228]

Noem has opposed the cultivation of [industrial hemp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_hemp), vetoing a bill that passed the South Dakota House and Senate in 2019 to legalize hemp cultivation. She said, "There is no question in my mind that normalizing hemp, like legalizing medical marijuana, is part of a larger strategy to undermine enforcement of the drug laws and make legalized marijuana inevitable."[^cite_note-229]

#### Release of prisoners without review by the state Board of Pardons and Paroles

Noem ordered release of 19 convicted criminals without the customary review by the state Board of Pardons and Paroles. As of May 2026, 12 of them had been charged with new crimes.[^cite_note-230]

#### RV park in Custer State Park proposal

In 2022, Noem sought to locate a government-paid RV park in Custer State Park.[^cite_note-231] The proposal was met with significant opposition to include government competing with private business and disturbing the pristine nature of the park.[^cite_note-232] The House Agricultural and Natural Resources deferred the bill to the 41st day, effectively killing it, by a vote of 9–3.

#### School prayer bill

In 2022, Noem sought to have [prayer put back in school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_prayer_in_the_United_States) after mentioning it in a speech in Iowa. On January 21, 2022, the "prayer bill", HB 1015, was defeated in the House Education Committee by a vote of 9–6. An aide to Noem admitted to the committee that no schools were consulted about the proposal.[^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234]

Noem's Senate confirmation hearing to become Secretary of Homeland Security on January 17, 2025

### Deportation of U.S. service members and veterans



Decertification letter sent by Noem on May 22, 2025

During a December 12, 2025, committee hearing, U.S. Representative [Seth Magaziner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Magaziner) asked Noem how many U.S. veterans DHS had deported. She replied that they had not deported any. He then showed, via [Zoom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoom_\(software\)), [Purple Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Heart) recipient and green-card holder Sae Joon Park, who had been deported during her tenure as Secretary. Park legally immigrated to the U.S. from South Korea when he was 7, grew up in Los Angeles and the [San Fernando Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley), and enlisted in the U.S. Army after graduating from Notre Dame High School in [Sherman Oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_Oaks,_Los_Angeles) in 1988.[^cite_note-ph-280] He was deployed to Panama in 1989 during Operation Just Cause and wounded by enemy gunfire. He moved to Hawaii, and in 2009, he was arrested for attempting to buy cocaine.[^cite_note-ka-281] Federal authorities allowed Park to stay in the U.S. as long as he made regular check-ins, which he did until June 2025,[^cite_note-ka-281] when he was told to self-deport or be deported by DHS officials.[^cite_note-ph-280] DHS Assistant Secretary Tricia McLaughlin said Park had an "extensive criminal history" and had been given a final removal order, with the option to self-deport. ### Criticism over use of Coast Guard resources



Noem speaking to law enforcement agents during an [ICE](216537.md) raid in January 2025



Noem at the [CECOT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_Confinement_Center) security prison in [El Salvador](9356.md) in March 2025

The Coast Guard has said that several of Noem's actions and directives have caused strain between it and DHS, especially downgrading search and rescue, a core USCG mission to secondary to ICE deportation missions.[^cite_note-mb-282] This began in February 2025, when Noem told the National Command Center to divert a USCG [C-130 Hercules](7697.md) from a search and rescue mission for a coast guardsman who had gone overboard.[^cite_note-cg-283] The USCG commander in San Diego sent two [C-27 Spartans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alenia_C-27J_Spartan) to Texas for the deportation mission and kept the C-130 on the search.[^cite_note-284] DHS has denied that it ordered the aircraft to suspend the search.[^cite_note-mb-282]

During Noem's tenure, more than 886 USCG flights were redirected to deportation missions transporting 9,805 migrants in fiscal year 2025.[^cite_note-285] The Coast Guard called the flights routine and a typical use of multi-mission aircraft.

Noem's advisor [Corey Lewandowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Lewandowski) also fired a USCG pilot during a mission because Noem's blanket had been left on another aircraft.[^cite_note-cg-283] DHS assistant secretary [Tricia McLaughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricia_McLaughlin_\(spokeswoman\)) denied that the incident took place.[^cite_note-286] Lewandowski reportedly entered the aircraft flight deck while the plane was still climbing but not yet at 10,000 feet, which is widely considered unsafe behavior.[^cite_note-287]

Noem was criticized for living rent-free in the Coast Guard Commandant's residence at [Joint Base Anacostia–Bolling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Base_Anacostia%E2%80%93Bolling).[^cite_note-288] She had taken occupancy of the waterfront home after the previous resident, Coast Guard Commandant and Four Star Admiral [Linda Fagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Fagan), recently fired by President Trump, had been given only three hours' notice on February 4, 2025, to vacate the residence.[^cite_note-289] DHS officials said that Noem was paying fair market value for the temporary use of the residence but provided no documentation to substantiate that claim.[^cite_note-mb-282]

### Criticism over ICE-related actions

In January 2026, there were widespread protests over [United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement](216537.md) (ICE) actions in Minneapolis, actions that then DHS Secretary Noem strongly supported.[^cite_note-IC-Dakota-Natives-141][^cite_note-IC-South_Dakota-142] On January 7, 2026, [Renée Good](82055905.md), a U.S. citizen, was shot and killed by an ICE agent in Minneapolis. The killing sparked massive protests in Minneapolis[^cite_note-Mitchell-2026-290] and [in cities across the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_U.S._immigration_enforcement_protests), including Chicago, [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICE_and_New_York_City_during_the_second_Trump_presidency#January_7,_2026), Los Angeles, San Francisco, Seattle, and Washington, D.C. Marches in Minneapolis prompted the closing of public schools and the deployment of more police officers. Federal agents used [tear gas](1202646.md) and [pepper spray](157302.md) against protesters;[^cite_note-Luscombe-2026b-291] an eyewitness to the shooting said, "People in our neighborhood have been terrorized by ICE for six weeks."[^cite_note-Colorado_Public_Radio-2026-292]

Minnesota Attorney General [Keith Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Ellison), along with the cities of Minneapolis and [Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul,_Minnesota), sued the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) to halt ICE deployments. The incident intensified national debate over immigration enforcement and renewed calls to [abolish ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolish_ICE).[^cite_note-Mitchell-2026-290][^cite_note-Luscombe-2026b-291] Dakota tribal leaders protested ICE's questionable detention of five Dakota citizens in Minneapolis.[^cite_note-IC-Dakota-Natives-141][^cite_note-IC-Dakota-MN-293] On January 24, during a protest demonstration, [two ICE agents killed Alex Pretti](82203651.md), a U.S. citizen and ICU nurse for the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs.[^cite_note-294]

Noem defended the killings of Good and Pretti by federal agents before any investigation began, which led some lawmakers to call for her resignation or impeachment,[^cite_note-IC-Dakota-Natives-141][^cite_note-IC-Dakota-MN-293][^cite_note-Luscombe-2026b-291] and caused several political analysts to question the blaming of victims and the exoneration of federal agents.[^cite_note-Guard-ICE-295][^cite_note-CBC-ICE-296] Vice President [JD Vance](53396477.md) strongly supported Noem and the aggressive immigration enforcement operations by ICE.[^cite_note-Reuters-ICE-297]

#### Impeachment proceedings

In January 2026, Noem made "unfounded claims" that Alex Pretti, the U.S. citizen shot dead by Customs and Border Protection (CBP) officers during protests against ICE in Minneapolis, had committed an act of "domestic terrorism".[^cite_note-NYT-Noem-3-26-298] On January 14, Representative [Robin Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Kelly) introduced three [articles of impeachment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_of_impeachment) against Noem. She alleged Noem had [obstructed congressional oversight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstruction_of_Congress) of ICE facilities; violated public trust regarding arrests and the use of force; and engaged in [self-dealing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-dealing) by awarding the contract for a \$200 million taxpayer-funded recruitment campaign to the husband of [Tricia McLaughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricia_McLaughlin_\(government_official\)). As of the filing, 70 representatives had signaled their support for the proceedings.[^cite_note-299][^cite_note-300]

## Special Envoy for the Shield of the Americas (2026–)



Kristi Noem and President Daniel Noboa, July 2025

In March 2026, Noem was appointed Special Envoy for the [Shield of the Americas](82587150.md), a US-led regional security organization focused on coordinating efforts to combat organized crime, drug trafficking, and illegal immigration throughout the western hemisphere.[^cite_note-310] She is responsible for coordination between US agencies and foreign counterparts on intelligence sharing, border security, and [drug trade operations](218449.md), and has also represented the US in several multilateral discussions and bilateral engagements across the region.[^cite_note-ReutersNoemEnvoy-4]

### Relations with Ecuador

As Secretary of Homeland Security, Noem made multiple visits to [Ecuador](9334.md) and developed a working relationship with President [Daniel Noboa](73980059.md).[^cite_note-311] Agreements were made to cooperate on efforts against transnational organized crime, and intelligence sharing was discussed.[^cite_note-312]

Since her appointment as Special Envoy, Noem has prioritized Ecuador as a regional partner, visiting it for the first time in her new capacity on March 25, 2026. During this visit, Noboa awarded Noem the [National Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Order_of_Merit_\(Ecuador\)) in the grade of Grand Cross, one of Ecuador's highest civilian honors, in recognition of her role in strengthening cooperation between the two nations.[^cite_note-313]

Through her ties with Noboa, Noem expanded US operational involvement in Ecuador as part of a wider initiative targeting Latin American drug trafficking.[^cite_note-314] In March, [United States Southern Command](1590932.md) initiated joint operations with the [Ecuadorian Armed Forces](9342.md) to attack "narco-terrorist" organizations operating in the country, including bombing operations against alleged cartel training camps near the Colombian border associated with the [Comandos de la Frontera](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comandos%20de%20la%20Frontera) (a [dissident FARC group](26100.md)).[^cite_note-315] These operations used aircraft, drones, and ground assets. On March 19, Noem introduced the first permanent US [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) office in Ecuador. Agents work alongside Ecuadorian authorities on investigations of drug trafficking and money laundering.[^cite_note-316]

### 2024 presidential endorsement

Noem endorsed Trump in the [2024 Republican Party presidential primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries) in September 2023, at a rally hosted for him in [Rapid City, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_City,_South_Dakota).[^cite_note-Five38-327][^cite_note-328] Trump invited her to appear with him at a March 2024 rally in [Vandalia, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandalia,_Ohio).[^cite_note-329][^cite_note-330]

## Autobiographical debates

In April 2024, pre-release excerpts of Noem's second autobiography, *No Going Back*, were widely debated, criticized, and condemned.[^cite_note-341] In the chapter "Bad Day to Be a Goat",[^cite_note-Martin-342][^cite_note-Pengelly2-343] Noem recounts that she brought her family's 14-month-old female [wirehaired pointer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Wirehaired_Pointer), Cricket, along for a [pheasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheasant) hunt with guests at her family's hunting lodge.[^cite_note-Hartmann-344] Expecting Cricket to emulate the older, trained, dogs on the hunt,[^cite_note-Martin-342][^cite_note-345] Noem instead felt that Cricket ruined the hunt by "chasing all those birds and having the time of her life".[^cite_note-Hartmann-344][^cite_note-Martin-342] After the dog killed several chickens that day, Noem decided Cricket was "dangerous" and "untrainable", and shot the dog dead in a [gravel pit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravel_pit).[^cite_note-Martin-342][^cite_note-346] She then killed her family's male goat, which she said was "disgusting, musky, rancid".[^cite_note-Martin-342][^cite_note-347][^cite_note-AP_4-30-348]

Noem initially responded that "tough decisions like this happen all the time on a farm", and later said the incident occurred 20 years ago and that "the fake news ... put the worst spin" on the story, as Cricket was a "[working dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_dog)" that "came to us from a family who had found her way too aggressive ... a responsible owner does what they need to do".[^cite_note-stop-349][^cite_note-Pengelly2-343][^cite_note-350] The story led to bipartisan criticism of Noem, and doubt that Trump would choose her as his [vice-presidential running mate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Republican_Party_vice_presidential_candidate_selection) intensified.[^cite_note-351][^cite_note-WPDog-352] A fundraising dinner for Noem in Colorado scheduled for May 4 was canceled after the group and the hotel hosting the event received [death threats](13262036.md).[^cite_note-353]

Later in the memoir, Noem wrote of imagining herself becoming president in 2025, taking over from Biden, and that the first thing she would do would be to "make sure Joe Biden's dog was nowhere on the grounds ('[Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_\(dog\)), say hello to Cricket for me')", in an apparent suggestion that Commander be killed.[^cite_note-354][^cite_note-355] Months earlier, Commander had been moved out of the White House after having bitten Secret Service agents and others on over a dozen occasions. In an interview, Noem said that Biden was "accountable" and called for him to "make a decision" on "what to do" about Commander.[^cite_note-356][^cite_note-357]

Noem also falsely claimed in her memoir to have met the North Korean dictator [Kim Jong Un](5962511.md), writing, "I'm sure he underestimated me, having no clue about my experience staring down little tyrants (I'd been a children's pastor, after all). Dealing with foreign leaders takes resolve, preparation, and determination."[^cite_note-358] Her spokesperson said the claim was an error and would be expunged from the book's future editions.[^cite_note-359] Noem also wrote in the book that she was once "slated to meet with French president [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md)", but called off the meeting because he made a "very pro-[Hamas](13913.md) and anti-Israel comment to the press"; the French government responded that it had neither invited Noem nor had any record of a scheduled meeting with her.[^cite_note-360]

*The Washington Post*'s literary critic [Ron Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Charles_\(critic\)) wrote that the "description of Cricket's Last Stand is the one time in this howlingly dull book that Noem demonstrates any sense of setting, character, plot and emotional honesty. Otherwise, it's mostly a hodgepodge of worn chestnuts and conservative maxims".[^cite_note-361]

Noem is a [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant).[^cite_note-377] As of 2018, her family attended a [Foursquare Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foursquare_Church) in [Watertown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watertown,_South_Dakota).[^cite_note-378]

In August 2024, at the [South Dakota State Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota_State_Fair), Noem and her sister, Cindy Grantham, were inducted into the [Daughters of the American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daughters_of_the_American_Revolution) by State Regent Katherine Tarrell.[^cite_note-plainsman-15]

In March 2024, Noem shared a video in which she identified herself as South Dakota's governor and promoted a cosmetic dentist business that she said helped her after she lost her front teeth in a biking accident years before: "I love my new family at Smile Texas!"[^cite_note-379] Noem has since become one of the most prominent examples of so-called "[Mar-a-Lago face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar-a-Lago_face)", a cosmetic surgery trend among conservative women,[^cite_note-380][^cite_note-381] also known as "Republican makeup".[^cite_note-382]

#### Other activities

In June 2026, Noem joined the Canadian mineral exploration firm NovaRed Mining as an adviser.[^cite_note-383]
