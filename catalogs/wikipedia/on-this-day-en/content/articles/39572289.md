The **2014 NBA Finals** was the [championship series](890793.md) of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md)'s (NBA) [2013–14 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_NBA_season) and the conclusion of [the season's playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_NBA_Playoffs), played from June 5 to 15, 2014.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] It was contested between two-time defending NBA champion and [Eastern Conference](1305847.md) champion [Miami Heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Miami_Heat_season) and the [Western Conference](28141073.md) champion [San Antonio Spurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_San_Antonio_Spurs_season). In a rematch, the Spurs defeated the Heat 4–1 for their 5th title overall. [Kawhi Leonard](31163849.md) was named the [Finals Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Russell_NBA_Finals_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) (MVP), receiving 10 out of 11 votes.[^cite_note-3]

The series served as a rematch from the [previous NBA season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_NBA_Finals), the 12th in Finals history, but only the fifth since the [ABA–NBA merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABA%E2%80%93NBA_merger) in 1976.[^cite_note-adi_06102104-4] This was the first NBA Finals since [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_NBA_Finals) to use the 2–2–1–1–1 format after the Board of Governors agreed to change the format from 2–3–2, which was used from [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_NBA_Finals) to 2013. This was due to competitive disadvantages of a team with home court advantage having to play the middle three games on the road, as well as the increased use of [chartered flights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_charter) as opposed to [commercial flights](164468.md).[^cite_note-5]

At the conclusion of this series, both teams would fall into a Finals appearance drought. The Heat would fail to advance to the NBA Finals until the shortened [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_NBA_Finals) season, where they would lose to the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season) in six games. The Spurs, who had strengthened their dynasty by winning their fifth title in 15 seasons, would fail to advance to the Finals until [2026](82427430.md), where they would face the [New York Knicks](80108544.md) in a losing effort but a rematch of the [1999 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NBA_Finals), the series that saw the Spurs win their first title and begin their dynasty.

## Background

### Miami Heat

Main article: [2013–14 Miami Heat season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Miami_Heat_season)

Led by their [Big Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_\(Miami_Heat\)) of [LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James), [Dwyane Wade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwyane_Wade), and [Chris Bosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Bosh), the Miami Heat made their fourth straight appearance in the NBA Finals, following two back-to-back wins in the 2012 and 2013 playoffs.

They were the first team since the [1987 Boston Celtics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_Boston_Celtics_season) to make it to four straight NBA Finals, and only the fourth team in NBA history to achieve that goal, besides the [1966 Boston Celtics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965%E2%80%9366_Boston_Celtics_season) (as a matter of fact, ten straight appearances), [1985 Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season) and the 1986-87 Boston Celtics. From 2015 to 2018 the [Cleveland Cavaliers](72868.md) led by [LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James) also achieved the same feat, along with the [Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors) those same years. They were seeking to become the first NBA team to [three-peat](345955.md) since the [2002 Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season). Heading into the postseason, the Heat had an 11–14 record in the last 25 games. In the first round, they eliminated the [Charlotte Bobcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Charlotte_Bobcats_season) and won 4–0. In the Conference semifinals, they eliminated the [Brooklyn Nets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Nets) and won 4–1, despite being swept by Brooklyn in the regular season. In the Eastern Conference finals, they again played the [Indiana Pacers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Pacers) in a rematch of the previous year's Conference finals. Miami won the series 4–2, eliminating the Indiana Pacers from the playoffs for the third straight year.

### San Antonio Spurs

Main article: [2013–14 San Antonio Spurs season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_San_Antonio_Spurs_season)

The Spurs had a deep roster, with no player averaging 30 minutes during the regular season. Their offense relied on ball movement, being called "one of the most beautiful-to-watch teams in the NBA" by *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today)*.[^cite_note-adi_06102104-4]

This was the [San Antonio Spurs](72889.md)'s sixth appearance in the NBA Finals, and they headed to the postseason with the best record in the NBA and a franchise record 19-game winning streak, ending with a 22–4 run in their last 26 games. In the first round, they faced their Texas rivals, the [Dallas Mavericks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Mavericks), who surprised the Spurs by taking the series to seven games despite the Spurs sweeping the Mavericks in the regular season for 2 consecutive years. San Antonio won 4–3. In the Conference semifinals, they eliminated the [Portland Trail Blazers](72897.md) and won 4–1. In their third consecutive Conference finals, they eliminated the [Oklahoma City Thunder](18256220.md) and won the series 4–2, despite being swept by Oklahoma City in the regular season, and for the first time, they qualified for back-to-back Finals appearances.

## Game summaries

*All times are in [Eastern Daylight Time](496028.md) ([UTC−4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC%E2%88%924))*

The Spurs rebounded from their seven-game loss to the Heat in the [2013 Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_NBA_Finals) to win the series, 4–1, for the franchise's fifth NBA championship. After winning their first four over nine seasons, this was their first since [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NBA_Finals). They defeated Miami by margins of at least 15 points in each of their four wins[^cite_note-mahoney_06152014-6] and also outscored them by an average of 14.0 points per game in the series, which was the largest differential in Finals history at the time.[^cite_note-ap_06152014-7][^cite_note-8] The Spurs finished the playoffs with 12 wins by 15 points or more, the most ever in the postseason. Miami had won 11 straight playoffs series, which was the fifth longest in league history.[^cite_note-mahoney_06152014-6]

The Spurs' [Kawhi Leonard](31163849.md) was named the [Finals MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Russell_NBA_Finals_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) after leading the team in scoring in each of the final three games, averaging 23.7 points and shooting 68.5 percent, after scoring just nine in each of the first two games[^cite_note-mahoney_06152014-6][^cite_note-MVP-9] Overall, he averaged 17.8 points on 61.2 percent shooting in the series,[^cite_note-10] the highest field goal percentage of any Finals MVP,[^cite_note-11] and shot 65 percent when guarded by LeBron James in the series.[^cite_note-12] Leonard also led the team with a 57.9 [three-point field goal percentage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-point_field_goal_percentage).[^cite_note-ap_06152014-7] Leonard was the third-youngest recipient of the award (22 years and 351 days old) since its inception in 1969,[^cite_note-MVP-9] and the youngest since [Magic Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Johnson) in [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_NBA_Finals).[^cite_note-13]

[Tim Duncan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Duncan) of the Spurs led all players in the series with 50 rebounds. He was followed by teammate [Boris Diaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Diaw) (43), who was inserted into the starting lineup beginning in Game 3. Diaw led all players in the series in assists (29).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

[Tim Duncan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Duncan) scored 21 points and had 10 [rebounds](3521142.md) to lead the Spurs to a 110–95 win in Game 1. The game featured the [AT\&T Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Center)'s malfunctioning air-conditioning system, which caused temperatures in the arena to exceed 90 °F (32 °C) in the second half. The conditions caused Miami's [LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James) to dehydrate and experience cramps, limiting him to just five minutes of playing time in the fourth quarter.[^cite_note-ap_06062014-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] With James on the bench, San Antonio went on a 16–3 run in the game's final four minutes, and outscored the Heat 36–17 in the fourth quarter.[^cite_note-ap_06062014-16]

James, who also had cramps in the [finals two years earlier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_NBA_Finals), finished the game with 25 points while playing only 33 minutes. [Manu Ginóbili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manu_Gin%C3%B3bili) had 16 points and 11 [assists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assist_\(basketball\)) and [Tony Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Parker) contributed 19 points and eight assists for the Spurs, who shot 59 percent for the game despite committing 23 turnovers.[^cite_note-ap_06062014-16]

The Spurs shot 14/16 in the 4th quarter. The Spurs' 87.5% conversion rating in the 4th quarter was the most efficient field goal conversion rating for any team in any quarter in NBA Finals history.

James rebounded from cramps in Game 1 with 35 points and 10 rebounds to lead Miami to a 98–96 win to tie the series. Bosh made the go-ahead [three-point field goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-point_field_goal) on a pass from James with 1\:18 remaining in the game, as the Heat won their 13th straight following a postseason loss. Temperatures in the AT\&T Center were comfortable for the game after a broken circuit breaker was repaired following Game 1.[^cite_note-ap_06082014-19]

After enduring criticism for not finishing the previous game, James started slowly in the first quarter, shooting 1-for-4 with three [turnovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turnover_\(basketball\)). Meanwhile, the Spurs began the game making 10 of their first 15 shots. James then made 11 of his next 13, and finished 14-for-22 while playing 38 minutes.[^cite_note-ap_06082014-19][^cite_note-costa_060914-20][^cite_note-21] He had 11 points in the second quarter, when the Heat overcome an early 11-point deficit. The score remained close through the remainder of the game. The Spurs held a two-point lead with 6\:43 remaining in the fourth quarter, when Miami's [Mario Chalmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Chalmers) elbowed Parker in the midsection for a [flagrant foul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagrant_foul). Tony Parker and Tim Duncan then combined to miss four straight [free throws](1098087.md).[^cite_note-ap_06082014-19] James scored 33 in the final three quarters; he had 22 in the second half, when every shot he made was from 18 feet (5.5 m) or further.[^cite_note-costa_060914-20] He also made a key strip of Parker late in the game.[^cite_note-ap_06082014-19]

Bosh finished with 18 points, and Wade and [Rashard Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashard_Lewis) added 14 for Miami. Parker had 21 points and Duncan scored 18 points with 15 rebounds for the Spurs, who had won eight consecutive home games by at least 15 points. Parker passed [Michael Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jordan) for eighth place on the [NBA's all-time playoff assist list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Basketball_Association_career_playoff_assists_leaders).[^cite_note-ap_06082014-19]

The Spurs took a 2–1 lead in the series after a career-high 29 points from Leonard and a Finals-record 75.8% shooting effort from the team during the first half. Leonard, limited to only 18 points in the first two games, made his first six shots and was 10-of-13 for the game. San Antonio led by as many as 25 and were comfortably ahead most of the game, including 71–50 at the half.[^cite_note-ap_06112014-22] The 21-point margin was the largest halftime lead in the Finals by a road team since Game 3 in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_NBA_Finals) by the [Chicago Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Bulls) against the [Seattle SuperSonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_SuperSonics).[^cite_note-23] The Heat scored 10-straight points in the third quarter to bring the score to 81–74, the closest they would get to the Spurs the rest of the game.[^cite_note-ap_06112014-22]

San Antonio's insertion of [Boris Diaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Diaw) into the starting lineup created more ball movement, as the Spurs achieved the first 70-point first half in the Finals since the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers)' 75 from Game 2 in [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_NBA_Finals) against the [Boston Celtics](43376.md). The Heat, who had been 8–0 at home in the playoffs, were led by James and Wade with 22 points apiece.[^cite_note-ap_06112014-22] Miami's 20 turnovers were their playoff-high, with James committing his Finals career-high of 7.[^cite_note-24]

Leonard had 20 points and 14 rebounds in another rout of the Heat, as the Spurs won 107–86 to take a 3–1 lead in the series; no team had ever come back from a 3–1 deficit in the Finals until [two years later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NBA_Finals). San Antonio again built a large lead on the road before halftime, taking a 55–33 lead in the second quarter after scoring seven consecutive points, culminated by a soaring dunk by Leonard. The Spurs defense held Miami to just 35 percent shooting in the first half after allowing the Heat to shoot 50 percent overall in the prior game. The Heat had followed their prior 13 playoff losses with a win.[^cite_note-ap_06122014-25]

The Heat struggled to defend the Spurs' crisp ball movement, orchestrated by Diaw and his game-high nine assists. San Antonio made 57 percent of its [field goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_goal_\(basketball\)), with Parker scoring 19 points, and Duncan adding 10 points and 11 rebounds to surpass [Magic Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Johnson) for the most [double-doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-doubles) in NBA Playoffs history (158). Duncan also eclipsed [Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar)'s previous mark (8,851) for [most postseason minutes played](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Basketball_Association_career_playoff_minutes_leaders).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-freeman_06132014-26] Miami was led by James, who had 28 points and eight rebounds, but their other starters combined for only 28 points on 32 percent shooting. Wade made only 1 of 10 from the field through three quarters, finishing with 10 points.[^cite_note-ap_06122014-25]

## Broadcast

In the United States, the NBA Finals aired on [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_on_ABC) (including for the second straight year local stations [KSAT-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSAT-TV) in San Antonio and [WPLG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WPLG) in Miami) with [Mike Breen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Breen) (play-by-play) and [Jeff Van Gundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Van_Gundy) (analyst) as commentators. Also beginning this Finals, and for the first time since [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NBA_Finals), [Mark Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Jackson) returned as analyst after being fired by the [Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors) early in the season.[^cite_note-28] [ESPN Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_on_ESPN_Radio) aired it as well and had [Kevin Calabro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Calabro) and [Hubie Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubie_Brown) as commentators.

## Aftermath



President Obama and Finals MVP [Kawhi Leonard](31163849.md)

This was an end of the [Heatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_\(Miami_Heat\))-era for Miami, who would lose [LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James) in free agency back to the [Cleveland Cavaliers](72868.md).[^cite_note-29] [Ray Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Allen) would not officially retire until years later, but he did not play in another game after the 2014 NBA Finals. In 2016, [Chris Bosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Bosh)'s career came to an abrupt end after a career-ending blood clotting condition. In that same summer, [Dwyane Wade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwyane_Wade)'s 12-year tenure with the Heat ended after a contract dispute with team president [Pat Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Riley). Wade signed with his hometown team, the [Chicago Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Bulls), but later reconciled with Riley and finished his career with the Heat. By the time of their next NBA Finals team in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_NBA_Finals), the only Miami Heat player from 2014 was [Udonis Haslem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udonis_Haslem). The Heat have lost their last three NBA Finals they have participated in (2014, 2020, and [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NBA_Finals)).

> "It's the [U.N.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.N.) of basketball teams. And it shows the way that this wonderful sport has become an international sport. Some people have, rightfully, started calling it -- the Spur’s style of basketball “the beautiful game.” And when you look at the passing and guys back-cutting the hoop and sharing the basketball, you see basketball the way it should be played."[^cite_note-30]

— President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) on the [2013–14 San Antonio Spurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013-14_San_Antonio_Spurs_season)

Although the Spurs remained a competitive team in the following years, even winning a team record 67 games in [2015–16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_San_Antonio_Spurs_season), this was the last NBA Finals for many of the team's players and personnel, such as [Tim Duncan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Duncan), [Manu Ginóbili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manu_Gin%C3%B3bili), [Tony Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Parker), and head coach [Gregg Popovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Popovich). After clashing with the team's medical staff, the Spurs sent [Kawhi Leonard](31163849.md) (and [Danny Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Green_\(basketball\))) to the [Toronto Raptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Raptors) just before the start of the 2018–2019 season.[^cite_note-31] With the Raptors, Kawhi would help lead the team to their first [franchise championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_NBA_Finals) and win another [NBA Finals MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Finals_MVP). The Spurs did not make their next NBA Finals appearance until [2026](82427430.md).[^cite_note-32]

This was the last NBA Finals to date to feature special on-court decals within the playing surface. Due to player safety concerns, the NBA opted not to place any special decals on the court beginning with the [2015 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_NBA_Finals), though starting with the [2018 Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NBA_Finals), the Finals logo returned on the court, albeit in front of the player benches. Starting with the [2026 Finals](82427430.md), specially designed courts will be used, using each team's primary court but with both the Finals logo and the Larry O'Brien Trophy painted at center court (as opposed to sticker decals or virtual ads during TV broadcasts).
