The [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), including the [closure of the Strait of Hormuz](82550763.md), has led to what the [International Energy Agency](285292.md) has characterized as the "largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market".[^cite_note-\:20-2][^cite_note-3] The conflict has echoed the [1970s energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970s_energy_crisis) through acute supply shortages, currency volatility, inflation and heightened risks of [stagflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagflation) and [recession](25382.md).[^cite_note-\:20-2][^cite_note-El_Safty-2026-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-Reuters-6] A 2026 market analysis by [MCB Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCB_Group) placed the current disruption alongside four earlier oil-market shocks, noting that in each case the initial price spike proved larger than the eventual sustained increase.[^cite_note-auto-7] The 1990–91 [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) saw oil prices decline within about nine months of the initial spike, according to a survey of historical oil shocks.[^cite_note-8] A similar pattern followed the [2003 Iraq War](201936.md). The [Congressional Research Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Research_Service) found that oil prices, after rising sharply at the outset of the conflict, returned to pre-crisis levels within a few months.[^cite_note-9] Brent crude's rise to nearly \$130 a barrel after [Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) but fell back over the following months.[^cite_note-10] MCB Group similarly noted that a \$10–15 per barrel risk premium associated with the June 2025 Twelve-Day War between Israel and Iran dissipated within days after the ceasefire.[^cite_note-auto-7]

As the result of the crisis, interest rate reductions were expected to be postponed or conversely increased in light of higher inflation caused by supply shortages and speculation.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-\:21-12] Stock markets experienced declines globally and there was a global bonds market sell-off.[^cite_note-\:21-12][^cite_note-NPR-13][^cite_note-\:22-14] Nevertheless, since the [S\&P 500](170992.md) hit 6,316.91 points on March 30, 2026, it increased 17.3% closing at 7,411.98 points on July 24, 2026.[^cite_note-\:32-15][^cite_note-\:33-16]

Nevertheless, as tension eased, [brent](1150532.md) prices fell from a pick of \$118.35 in March 31, 2026 to \$71.57 in July 01, 2026.[^cite_note-\:30-17] As tension in the gulf started to increase the brent price increased to \$100.69 in July 23, but in July 24, despite mounting threats by [U.S](3434750.md) president [Donald Trump](4848272.md),[^cite_note-\:34-18][^cite_note-\:35-19] the brent price closed at \$96.78.[^cite_note-\:30-17] Moreover, oil shipping index which picked to 3,737 in March 2026 has declined to 1,850 in July 2026.[^cite_note-\:36-20]

## Background

Main article: [2026 Iran war](82533582.md)

The 2026 Iran conflict began on 28 February, with joint US–Israeli airstrikes targeting Iranian leadership and military infrastructure, including a decapitation strike that [assassinated Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei](82537558.md).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-Smith-2026-22] The operation, codenamed "[Operation Epic Fury](82533582.md)" by the US, involved advanced military equipment costing millions of dollars, such as [precision-guided munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition) and [stealth aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stealth_aircraft).[^cite_note-23] Iran retaliated with missile and drone strikes on US and Israeli targets, as well as Gulf states hosting US forces, leading to airspace closures and attacks on infrastructure.[^cite_note-24] Prior to the conflict, Iran's economy was strained by sanctions, protests, and a depreciating rial, with inflation exceeding 40% in 2025.[^cite_note-25] The strikes intensified these pressures, while global markets reacted to fears of supply chain disruptions.

Moreover, some economists have argued that modern economies are less vulnerable to oil shocks that in the 1970s, citing lower oil intensity, more flexible labor markets, and more credible monetary policy.[^cite_note-26] A similar pattern has been found using geopolitical risk indices more broadly.[^cite_note-27] On July, the [IMF](15251.md) revised its world [GDP](12594.md) growth rate to 3% for 2026 and 3.4% in 2027.[^cite_note-\:31-28] The forecasted growth rate is higher than the actual growth rates in 2023–2025.[^cite_note-29] According to IMF the effect of the oil supply shock on the world economy is limited since the [AI](1164.md)-driven demand offsets the negative effect of the oil supply shock.[^cite_note-\:31-28] Moreover, according to the [*WSJ*](173070.md) July 2026 survey of 74 economists, the consensus remains that the U.S. economy will avoid a recession, with the average probability of a downturn over the next year falling to 25% from 33% in the previous survey.[^cite_note-\:37-30]

[Arab states of the Persian Gulf](3088555.md) as well as Iran itself rely on the Strait of Hormuz for their energy exports and grocery imports, with only Saudi Arabia and UAE having alternative, albeit limited, routes.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] The war has caused a systemic collapse of the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md) economic model. Following the closure of the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md) on 4 March 2026, oil and [LNG](832128.md) exports were stranded, causing [Brent Crude](1150532.md) to surge past \$120 per barrel and forcing [QatarEnergy](11292570.md) to declare [force majeure](709128.md) on all exports.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]The oil production of [Kuwait](15554971.md), [Iraq](39750625.md), Saudi Arabia, and the United Arab Emirates collectively dropped by a reported 6.7 million barrels per day by 10 March,[^cite_note-36] and by at least 10 million barrels per day as of 12 March.[^cite_note-37] The maritime blockade triggered a concurrent "grocery supply emergency" across Gulf Cooperation Council states, which rely on the Strait for over 80% of their caloric intake; by mid-March, 70% of the region's food imports were disrupted, forcing retailers like Lulu Retail to airlift staples, resulting in a 40–120% spike in consumer prices.[^cite_note-El_Safty-2026-4] The crisis has shifted from a fiscal contraction toward fears about a humanitarian crisis following Iranian strikes on [desalination](156787.md) plants—the source of 99% of drinking water in Kuwait and Qatar. The regional aviation sector, including [Emirates](142715.md) and [Qatar Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Airways), faced a near-total cessation of operations due to multi-national airspace closures, causing widespread disruption to global air travel.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-\:29-39] A [United Nations Development Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme) study released on 30 March estimated that the war could reduce economic growth in Arab nations by \$120–194 billion in GDP.[^cite_note-40]

Beyond immediate trade disruptions, analysts have noted a profound shift in the region's long-term economic narrative.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] [Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle) reported that the Gulf states are unlikely to sustain high levels of investment spending during or after the war.[^cite_note-\:9-43] The conflict has been described as the "end of the narrative" that the Gulf is a permanently safe destination for [expatriates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate), immigrants, and tourists; the Qatar-funded Middle East Council on Global Affairs suggested the war has "irreversibly shaken" the region's image, exposing a deep-seated fragility beneath the facade of the Gulf's rapid economic transformation.[^cite_note-\:29-39][^cite_note-\:10-44][^cite_note-\:12-45][^cite_note-\:13-46][^cite_note-\:14-47] Sinem Cengiz, a Kuwaiti journalist, conveyed in her article how the immeasurable social and psychological impact in the economic, political and security spheres were unlikely to fade.[^cite_note-\:15-48]

The war has precipitated a second major energy crisis and subsequently economic crisis for Europe, primarily through the suspension of Qatari [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) (LNG) and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz.[^cite_note-rinaldi-49] The conflict coincided with historically low European gas storage levels—estimated at just 30% capacity following a harsh 2025–2026 winter—causing Dutch [TTF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_Transfer_Facility) gas benchmarks to nearly double to over €60/MWh by mid-March.[^cite_note-rudinger_bouadica-50] Consequently, the [European Central Bank](9474.md) (ECB) postponed its planned interest rate reductions on 19 March, raising its 2026 inflation forecast and cutting GDP growth projections, with economists warning that energy-intensive economies face high risks of [technical recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_recession) if the maritime blockade persists through the summer refill season.[^cite_note-morningstar_19-03-26-51][^cite_note-maaal_20-03-26-52] The crisis has further impacted industrial output in the United Kingdom and the EU, where chemical and steel manufacturers have imposed surcharges of up to 30% to offset surging electricity and feedstock costs, potentially leading to permanent deindustrialization in some sectors.[^cite_note-tg_17-03-26-53][^cite_note-bbc_19-03-26-54] The economic impact on Europe has been characterized by a severe energy-supply shock and industrial strain, with the European Central Bank (ECB) warning that a prolonged conflict will likely trigger a period of stagflation, a period of low growth rates accompanied by inflation, and push major energy-dependent economies, including Germany and Italy, into technical recession by the end of 2026.[^cite_note-\:6-55][^cite_note-\:7-56][^cite_note-57] British company Shell had issued a warning that Europe could face a shortage of fuel as early as April.[^cite_note-58]

The rest of the world has been affected by panic buying and severe disruption to the distribution of petroleum, much of which is sourced from the Middle East and transits the Strait of Hormuz, the latter blockaded by Iran in early March 2026.[^cite_note-59] The Philippines declared a state of emergency on the 24th March due to a concurrent strike by transport workers,[^cite_note-60] while other countries such as Zimbabwe, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nigeria, and Vietnam face similar predicaments with severe shortages of fuel. However other countries such as Australia and India have more sufficient reserves but face challenges with panic buying and their "crisis is almost entirely man-made".[^cite_note-\:21-12][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] Economies most reliant on the strait for energy imports are in Asia, with Europe also viewing the strait as vital for its energy security. The initial disruption of petroleum is expected to affect Asia the most, but Europe is likely to be hit hard in the medium-to-long term.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

Due to related increased tensions in [Houthi Yemen](79583620.md), commercial vessels have been avoiding the [Bab-el-Mandeb](62138.md) strait and the [Suez Canal](29323.md) route in preference to the [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope) to reach the [Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_trade). The official entry of the Houthis into the war on 28 March has raised fears of disruption to global shipping and consequent supply shortages.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] Additionally, the [simultaneous war between Afghanistan and Pakistan](82482902.md) has disrupted airspace and trade routes through Central Asia.[^cite_note-thediplomat.com-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

The head of the [International Energy Agency](285292.md) described the situation caused by the war as the "greatest global energy security challenge in history".[^cite_note-75] The conflict caused immediate volatility in energy markets, with Brent crude oil prices surging 10–13% to around \$80–82 per barrel by 2 March 2026.[^cite_note-76] Iran's closure of the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md) disrupted 20% of global oil supplies and significant [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) (LNG) volumes.[^cite_note-77] Exports from the region are typically going to Asian countries, with China, India, Japan and South Korea accounting for 75% of oil and 59% of LNG exports.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] However, Singapore and Taiwan depend more on Qatari LNG, while Pakistan and Bangladesh are more price sensitive.[^cite_note-80]

QatarEnergy later announced that it was declaring [force majeure](709128.md) on its contracts with buyers, and internal sources, according to [Reuters](18998750.md), said that it would soon be shutting down gas liquefication as [LNG tankers](12378960.md) could not leave the Gulf, and that restarting it would take weeks.[^cite_note-81] These announcements caused increases in world gas prices, which analysts said was a part of the Iranian government's plan to apply pressure on the world to stop the war.[^cite_note-82] On 6 March, [al-Kaabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bani_Kaab) warned that if the war continues other Gulf energy producers may be forced to halt exports and declare Force Majeure, and that "this will bring down economies of the world".[^cite_note-83]

On 6 March, it was reported that according to satellite imagery analysis by both [Bloomberg](3057255.md) and the Energy Economics and Society Research Institute in Tokyo, Ras Laffan, the main gas facility in Qatar, appears to have not been damaged before the "unprecedented shutdown" which sent fuel prices higher.[^cite_note-84] The United States, buffered by domestic production, faced less direct impact but saw gasoline prices rise 5–10 cents per gallon daily.[^cite_note-NPR-13] The British think tank The Food Policy Institute has warned of long-term increases in food prices due to disruption in fuel and fertilizer markets.[^cite_note-85]

On March 18, Iran hit Qatar's inactive [Ras Laffan](24864449.md) Industrial City LNG complex, causing a 17% reduction in Qatar's LNG production capacity.[^cite_note-86] The damages from this attack were estimated to require 3–5 years to fully repair.[^cite_note-87] Consequently, LNG spot prices in Asia increased by over 140 %.[^cite_note-88]

The blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, the route of approximately 20% of the seaborne crude oil and LNG exports, keeps energy supply tight.[^cite_note-89]

By 28 March, analysts feared that another hard oil crisis would unfold that was expected to be very decisive to the global economy.[^cite_note-90] Three days later oil prices hit US\$4 per gallon, as the war with Iran caused a surge of 30% in gas prices.[^cite_note-91]

[Vitol](17518274.md) CEO [Russell Hardy](59003693.md) said on 21 April 2026 that one billion barrels of oil production would be lost because of the war, and that the current loss was between 600 and 700 million barrels.[^cite_note-92] The [International Energy Agency](285292.md) called the Iran war the greatest global energy security challenge in history by May, with oil prices hitting \$4.50 per barrel and heading swiftly towards \$5.00.[^cite_note-93]

Since reaching a peak of \$118.35 on March 31, 2026, [brent](1150532.md) prices fell \$71.57 in July 01, 2026.[^cite_note-\:302-94] As war tensions started to mount, the brent price increased to \$100.69 in July 23, but in July 24, the brent price closed at \$96.78.[^cite_note-\:302-94] Also, oil shipping index which picked to 3,737 in March 2026 has declined to 1,850 in July 2026.[^cite_note-\:362-95]

### Aviation



EU airlines' travel between Europe and Asia is restricted to two corridors to avoid war zones and [prohibited airspace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibited_airspace).

Aviation has been significantly disrupted due to the closure of airspace on key flight corridors between Africa, Asia and Europe. Airlines have been rerouting flights along longer flight paths that circumnavigate the Middle East, adding to journey time and fuel costs, whereas several major airports in the Middle East have also been closed which collectively handle around 15% of global air traffic. The war more than doubled the price of [kerosene](16992.md)-based products like diesel and [jet fuel](1205435.md), as refineries lack certain types of crude oil.[^cite_note-96] Many airlines have increased ticket prices or cancelled flights to maintain cashflow.[^cite_note-97] Thousands were left stranded globally and repatriation efforts were described as the biggest since the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) or [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-98]

Bahrain, Iraq, Israel, Kuwait, Qatar, Syria and the UAE closed their respective airspace following the attacks, with multiple airliners being redirected to other destinations.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] Iran's airspace was largely empty of civilian aircraft following the initial strikes on 28 February as regional states closed airspace.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] The major airlines of the Middle East, including [Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirates_Airlines), [Etihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etihad) and [Qatar Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Airways), suspended all operations, facing major disruptions, leading to losses estimated at in billions.[^cite_note-BBC-104] International airlines like [Air India](26457880.md), [Biman Bangladesh Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biman_Bangladesh_Airlines), [British Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Airways), [Cathay Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathay_Pacific), [IndiGo](2085799.md), [Lufthansa](97733.md), [Virgin Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Atlantic), and [Wizz Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizz_Air) also suspended services to the Middle East in view of the conflict.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] Airspace closures in the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, and other Gulf states have led to over 4,000 daily flight cancellations, stranding hundreds of thousands of passengers.[^cite_note-109] [Dubai International Airport](154742.md) and [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md) sustained damage from Iranian strikes, while planes in Kuwait were grounded after airport hits.[^cite_note-110]

Iran's airspace emptied of civilian aircraft during the conflict,[^cite_note-111] as Bahrain, Iraq, Israel, Kuwait, Qatar, Syria and the UAE closed their respective airspaces.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] Some carriers suspended service to the Middle East.[^cite_note-115] The disruption in global oil supply, caused by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz during the conflict, led to higher jet fuel prices, which increased operating costs for airlines and pushed up airfares on both domestic and international routes. Airlines responded by raising ticket prices and adjusting some flight schedules to cope with higher fuel costs. This impact was also visible in Europe, where continued disruptions in energy supply routes contributed to higher fuel prices and additional pressure on airlines. As a result, some carriers reduced capacity or cut flights, while others passed increased costs directly on to passengers.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] [Spirit Airlines](446433.md), an American [low-cost airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-cost_airline), ceased operations on 2 May 2026, after increased fuel costs from the war exacerbated its existing financial difficulties.[^cite_note-120]

### Financial markets

Global stock markets declined, with the Dow Jones falling over 400 points and the S\&P 500 dropping 0.7% on 2 March.[^cite_note-121] European and Asian indexes fell 1–2%, reflecting fears of inflation and supply chain issues.[^cite_note-122] Gold prices rose as a safe-haven asset, while airline stocks like United Airlines dropped 6%.[^cite_note-123] [Emerging markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_market) as a whole were adversely affected by the war, while the value of the US dollar rose in the war's first days.[^cite_note-124] Prolonged conflict risked a global recession, with higher energy costs eroding consumer spending and industrial competitiveness.[^cite_note-125] Developing economies faced currency pressures, while [central banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank) grappled with rate decisions amid inflation, limiting central banks' ability to cut [interest rates](163115.md).[^cite_note-Janes-126][^cite_note-127] Emerging markets experienced currency pressures from a stronger US dollar.[^cite_note-Janes-126]

Financial markets saw volatility, with stock indices like Japan's Nikkei dropping over 2%.[^cite_note-128] On 2 March, the [KSE 100 index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSE_100_index) witnessed a market halt and recorded its largest-ever single-day decline, losing 16,089 points, or 9.57%, to close at 151,973 points.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130] South Korea's [KOSPI](1352321.md) index suffered its biggest crash since the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), dropping up to 12% in a single day and triggering a [circuit breaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_curb) on 4 March. The [Thai Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_Exchange_of_Thailand) also imposed a trading curb after an 8% decline.[^cite_note-131]

Following the war, most flights to and from Dubai were grounded due to geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, disrupting gold shipments. According to some experts, the disruption caused significant price swings in target markets, including India, where gold prices went from a \$50 discount to the London price in a matter of days.[^cite_note-132] The expansion of military conflicts in the Middle East has posed a new shock to the US economy, which was already struggling with tariffs, persistent inflation, and declining employment. According to the Anadolu Agency, Iran's retaliatory attacks on US bases in Qatar, the UAE, and Bahrain have injected uncertainty into global markets and raised alarm bells for economic policymakers in Washington.[^cite_note-a-133]

A *[Financial Times](136566.md)* investigation found that US\$580 million bets on falling oil prices had been placed with the stock market by selling futures just 15 minutes before Donald Trump published his statement on postponing attacks on Iran for talks on 23 March 2026, which caused oil prices to fall for a while. This resulted in speculation about insider trading and calls for further investigation.[^cite_note-134] A second suspicious series of bets, worth US\$950 million, on falling oil prices happened on 7 April 2026, again shortly before a policy shift was announced by Donald Trump, this time a two-week cease fire with the opening of the Strait of Hormuz. A third series of suspicious bets on falling prices happened on 17 April 2026 with 7,990 lots of Brent crude futures, worth an estimated US\$750 million, being sold 20 minutes before a statement by Iran's foreign minister, announcing that the Strait of Hormuz was open for the rest of the ceasefire.[^cite_note-135]

The interest rates also surged. On 27 March the 10-year bonds yield jumped to 4.46%, its highest level since July 2025.[^cite_note-136] The 30-year mortgage rate climbed to 6.38% on 26 March.[^cite_note-137]

Yet, since the [S\&P 500](170992.md) hit 6,316.91 points on March 30 2026 it increased by 17.3% closing at 7,411.98 points on July 24 2026.[^cite_note-\:32-15][^cite_note-\:33-16]

### Commodity markets

The near-total halt of tanker traffic in the Strait of Hormuz has caused a significant disruption in the global supply of [sulfur](27127.md), with Gulf countries accounting for roughly 45% of the global commodity. As a major producer of sulfur and [urea](31734.md), the region's supply stoppage is projected to spike fertilizer costs, metal leaching in the copper industry, and [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid). The crisis has also constrained the supply of [helium](13256.md), crucial for [semiconductor manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_device_fabrication).[^cite_note-138] The UN [World Food Programme](33582.md) and various analysts warn that these disruptions are driving long-term increases in global food prices,[^cite_note-139] threatening a scenario similar to the [2022 food crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%932023_global_food_crises).[^cite_note-140] According to The Fertilizer Institute, roughly 50% of global urea and sulfur exports, along with 20% of global liquefied natural gas—a feedstock for nitrogen fertilizers—transit through the strait.[^cite_note-141] Disruption in the supply of essential [fertilizers](37401.md) due to the closure of the Strait threatened food production in many countries.[^cite_note-142] The Fertilizer Institute stated that nearly 50% of global [urea](31734.md) and [sulfur](27127.md) exports annually transited through the Strait of Hormuz, made worse by the natural gas shortage, which is a key feedstock for [nitrogen fertilizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_fertilizer).[^cite_note-Buchanan-2026-143]

Sulfuric acid prices have risen significantly since the beginning of the conflict, driven by tighter sulfur supply from disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz and export restrictions imposed by China on key industrial commodities.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145] According to projections by Morningstar analyst Seth Goldstein, nitrogen fertilizer prices could roughly double from 2024 levels, while [phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate) prices might increase by approximately 50% Data from Kpler shows that as of 2024, Asian nations are highly dependent on Middle Eastern supply, receiving 35% of urea, 53% of [sulfur](27127.md), and 64% of [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia) exports from the region. Analysts warn that supply constraints coinciding with the Northern Hemisphere's spring planting season could lead to decreased usage, lowering yields for staple crops such as [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), and [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize).[^cite_note-aljazeera-2026-3-18-146] From February 28 to March 27, 2026, [Brent](1150532.md) crude oil went up from \$72.48 to \$112.57 reflecting a 55.32% increase.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] But, as tension eased, [brent](1150532.md) prices fell from a pick of \$118.35 on March 31, 2026 to \$71.57 on July 1, 2026.[^cite_note-\:30-17] As tension in the gulf started to increase the brent price increased to \$100.69 on July 23, but on July 24, despite ongoing threats by [U.S](3434750.md) president [Donald Trump](4848272.md),[^cite_note-\:34-18][^cite_note-\:35-19] the brent price closed at \$96.78.[^cite_note-\:30-17] Also, the oil shipping index which picked to 3,737 in March 2026 declined to 1,850 by July 2026.[^cite_note-\:36-20]

The sector of fertilizers also took a hit. Fertilizers are produces using [urea](31734.md) and [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia), therefore, energy cost make up to 70% of the production costs.[^cite_note-149] From the beginning of the conflict until March 20 the price of fertilizers increased by up to 40%,[^cite_note-150] affecting the food prices all over the world.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152] [Helium](13256.md) is an essential production factor in semiconductors manufacturing due to its chemical inertness and high thermal conductivity, which allow it to prevent unwanted reactions and efficiently cool silicon wafers during production.[^cite_note-153] Helium is also used in medical imaging (such as MRI) because liquid helium can reach extremely low temperatures required to cool superconducting magnets.[^cite_note-154] The aerospace industry also uses helium since it is non-flammable, light, and remains as gas at very low temperatures, and also in scientific research.[^cite_note-155] Much of the world's helium is produced as a byproduct of liquefied natural gas (LNG). Therefore, Qatar, one of the world's largest LNG producers, is also responsible for helium production. Ras Laffan Industrial City in Qatar hosts some of the largest helium refining and liquefaction facilities, enabling the extraction of helium from natural gas processed for export. As of 2024 Qatar produces 35% percent of the world's helium production, second to the USA, with 44% of the world's production.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157]

Consequently, helium prices, which normally were about \$300 per thousand cubic feet, jumped since the start of the war to \$600 and even \$900 per thousand cubic feet.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] Sulfur is used in the fertilizers and pesticides industries.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] Qatar, Kuwait and Iran together produce and export roughly 45% of global traded sulfur.[^cite_note-162] Consequently, since the beginning of the war, the price of sulfuric acid is now 30% higher than it was prior to the war.[^cite_note-\:23-163] Tungsten is critical for armor‑piercing ammunition and other military applications. It is also an essential component in the production of semiconductors, photovoltaics, aerospace, drilling and high‑precision manufacturing die to its unique hardness and thermal stability.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] China, the largest producer of tungsten, with 80% of the world production,[^cite_note-166] have restricted export. Consequently, tungsten's price surged over 50% in Match 2026, and more than tripling in price since December 2025.[^cite_note-\:23-163]

The Gulf states produce approximately 9% of the world's [aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium) supply. Due to the war, the price of aluminum increased by 8% in March. Moreover, on March 28 Iran hit Emirates Global aluminum, causing massive production disruptions.[^cite_note-167] An aluminum shortage have a direct impact on automotive, aerospace, electronics, and construction industries, and also on packaging of consumer goods costs.[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-\:25-169] Last year, Gulf exports made up roughly 21% of US primary aluminum imports. The Gulf also accounted for 19% of EU imports, primarily serving Italy and the Netherlands, and about a quarter of Japan's total imports. In addition, countries such as Mexico, South Korea, India, Thailand, Taiwan, and the UK depend on Gulf aluminum. This dependence means that rising aluminum prices could cascade through major consuming industries.[^cite_note-\:25-169] On the other hand, gold, once considered a safeguard against economic instability, decreased in value by 28% since the beginning of the war.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] The instability has affected [gold embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldwork_\(embroidery\)) artisans serving the [Holy Week in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week_in_Spain).[^cite_note-Trashorras-172]

### Military costs

Defense Secretary [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md), Joint Chiefs of Staff Chair [Dan Caine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Caine), and Agriculture Secretary [Brooke Rollins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooke_Rollins) testifying before the [Senate Appropriations Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Appropriations_Committee) to testify on President Trump's \$87.6 billion funding request for the war in July 2026

As of early April, US military costs were estimated at \$25–35 billion, according to the [American Enterprise Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Enterprise_Institute),[^cite_note-173] and Israeli costs at \$11 billion, according to [Israel's Finance Ministry](7088294.md).[^cite_note-174] Costs to the Iranian economy were estimated at \$40–50 billion, around 10% of [GDP](12594.md).[^cite_note-175] According to a report by the [Center for International Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_International_Policy) in early May, two months into the war, the US had spent nearly \$72 billion on the conflict (\$1.2 billion per day on average)—nearly three times the amount of the Pentagon's official estimate.[^cite_note-176] In July, U.S. defense secretary Pete Hegseth told a Senate committee that the US had spent \$37.5 billion on the war; he requested an additional \$67 billion to continue the war.[^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178] Iran reportedly spent less on weapons than the US and Israel. For example, Iran could pay \$50,000 for a [Shahed drone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahed_drone), while [PATRIOT](52024.md) and [THAAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/THAAD) interceptors to neutralize the drone could cost \$4 million and \$12 million respectively.[^cite_note-ndtv10march-179]

The war has also increased indirect military costs due to the rapid depletion of US munitions stockpiles. According to reports, the United States has used large quantities of high-cost interceptors such as Patriot and [THAAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_High_Altitude_Area_Defense) missiles, further increasing replacement expenses and strain on defense budgets. The depletion of stockpiles has also affected US arms transfers and procurement decisions, including reports that Washington is reconsidering or delaying the transfer of Tomahawk cruise missiles to Germany and reviewing Germany's request for [Typhon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhon_missile_system) launchers due to limited availability.[^cite_note-nbcnews14may-180][^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183] Reports also indicate delays in missile deliveries to Japan and new procurement efforts, including an \$842 million agreement for [JASSM-ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-158_JASSM) cruise missiles from Denmark. US officials have raised concerns that existing stockpiles may be insufficient for sustained operations in multiple theaters, including in the event of a future conflict in Asia. The Pentagon has also expanded efforts to increase production capacity through programs aimed at scaling lower-cost "affordable mass" munitions and involving non-traditional defense contractors.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185][^cite_note-nbcnews14may-180] The US defence industry saw record stock surges during the war.[^cite_note-186] As of August 2026, the U.S. Army has used "virtually all" of their stocks of Army Tactical Missile Systems ([ATACMS](147015.md)) and Precision Strike Missiles ([PrSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PrSM)), two surface-to-surface missiles that cost \$1 million each.[^cite_note-187]

### Real estate

Several economists and market analysts have suggested that the conflict could also have indirect effects on global real estate investment patterns. Previous geopolitical crises in the Middle East, most notably the [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis) and the [1979 Iranian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Iranian_Revolution), were associated with significant increases in [petrodollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrodollar) wealth and capital outflows from the region, part of which was invested in overseas property markets, notably prime real estate in [London](17867.md). Scholars and property market analysts have documented the role of Middle Eastern investment in the internationalization of London's housing market from the late twentieth century onward, and noted the correlation between crises in the Middle East and strong price increases in London's real estate.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190]

During the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), [disruptions to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz](82550763.md) and heightened geopolitical risk in the [Gulf](220950.md) led some investment strategists to speculate that similar "safe-haven" [capital flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_flows) could emerge if regional instability persisted. Analysts noted that London has historically attracted international [wealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth) during periods of geopolitical uncertainty due to its deep financial markets, strong legal protections for [property rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_rights) and liquid real estate sector. However, economists cautioned that the overall impact would depend on the duration of the conflict and broader global economic conditions, including [interest rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rates) and [energy prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_economics).

## Gulf

The war is assessed to have caused the biggest shock to Middle East's economy in five decades.[^cite_note-191]

### Iran

Iran's economy, already contracting under sanctions, faced further infrastructure damage and revenue losses.[^cite_note-192] Consequently, analysts expect that Iran's economy will shrink by 10% due to the war.[^cite_note-193] Iran imports [grain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain) mainly through its Persian Gulf harbours, but struggled to fund it even before the war. Most feed-grain (mainly [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) from Brazil) is imported via the Strait of Hormuz to the [Bandar-e Emam Khomeini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar-e_Emam_Khomeini) harbour. 30% of wheat is imported. By 6 March nine grain ships were outside the Strait of Hormuz, inbound for Iran.[^cite_note-194] By 6 March, 4,000 civilian buildings had already been damaged by the US-Israeli strikes.[^cite_note-195] Despite the economic harm by the US-Israel attacks, intelligence reports considered it "unlikely" that such damage would topple [Iran's military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran's_military) and [clerical structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardianship_of_the_Islamic_Jurist) in the short term, and that it would not help the fragmented [Iranian opposition](80207658.md) to seize power on its own.[^cite_note-196] [Russia](25391.md), despite denying providing the alleged assistance,[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198] clearly benefits from the conflict's continuation, particularly the rise in oil prices.[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200] Some countries that have being receiving [Iranian drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_drone) for their military development, like [Sudan](27421.md), have lost their continuous supply due to the damage inflicted to Iran's military capabilities. However, Iran has not necessarily cut them off completely and has only constrained how many drones they can spare.[^cite_note-201]

Many of Iran's military investments were obliterated by the heavy bombings. [CENTCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CENTCOM) said that more than 66% of Iran's missiles drone, naval production sites and shipyards has been hit, and 92% of Iran's large naval vessels had been sunk.[^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203][^cite_note-204]

[Natanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natanz) [enrichment](37555.md) facility was bombed and hit on 3 March. On 27 March the Shahid Rezayee Nejad [yellowcake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowcake) production facility in [Yazd](451385.md) had been attacked, as well as the [Khondab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khondab) [heavy water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_water) complex near [Arak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arak,_Iran).[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207]

On March 27, Iran's economy took another hit as Israel struck Mobarakeh [Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel) in [Isfahan](223149.md) and [Khuzestan](448079.md) Steel in [Ahvaz](599079.md). A company operations director at Khuzestan Steel said "all modules and steelmaking furnaces at the complex in Ahvaz had been damaged", and that it would require at least six months to repair.[^cite_note-208] Damages was inflicted to storage facilities and power infrastructure at Mobarakeh Steel.[^cite_note-209]

The inflation in Iran surged to new heights, reflected by the fact that the Iranian central bank issued the ten-million-rial note, the largest ever.[^cite_note-\:19-210][^cite_note-211] Food inflation surged to 105%.[^cite_note-\:19-210]

From March 2025 to March 2026, the price of bread and cereals increase by 140%, the price of red meat and poultry rose by 135%, the price of oil and fats increase by 219%, the price of fruits and nuts went up by 104.2%, and the price of dairy products (milk, cheese, eggs) has climbed by 116.8%.[^cite_note-\:24-212] The price of furniture and household maintenance increased by 72.8%, and the price of transportation increased by 67.5%.[^cite_note-\:24-212] Prices surged unequally: rural areas experienced year-on-year inflation of 86.5%, while urban year-on-year inflation is only 69.3%.[^cite_note-\:24-212]

On the labor market, the war has led to widespread [layoffs](507285.md) in Iran as many domestic suppliers have been either attacked or disrupted, and many foreign suppliers are unable to transport products due to the [Strait of Hormuz crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Hormuz_crisis). Additionally, the [2026 internet blackout](82063825.md) has caused many self-employed Iranians, many of them women, to lose access to their online platforms.[^cite_note-213]

Overall, the economic toll on Iran is a catastrophic one: The Iranian regime's spokesperson, Fatemeh Mohajerani, estimated the direct and indirect cost of 40 days of war at \$270bn, or 90% of the estimated Iranian GDP in 2026.[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-215]

### Arab nations

Many [Middle Eastern](19323.md) countries have shut down their liquefied natural gas facilities, suspended production of oil refineries and chunks of their gas and oil exports, including strategic commercial ports were hit in the crossfire.[^cite_note-216] Damage to these facilities in Middle Eastern countries could cost \$25 billion to repair.[^cite_note-217] Arab economists, like [Qatar](8238258.md)'s Energy Minister Al-Kaabi, have adverted that Gulf energy exports may halt if war continues as the continued fighting could drive oil prices to \$150 per barrel and gas prices to \$40 per million thermal units, caused by the current increase in exporters declaring a situation of force majeure and suspending contractual obligations which could lead in the long term to the "collapse of world economies", but in the short term to stall the recovery of [Gulf Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Countries) for months if the war ended soon.[^cite_note-218] Asia's importers, like China (a main economic partner of many countries in the war zone), saw heightened costs, while Middle Eastern economies suffered from suspended aviation and tourism.[^cite_note-219]

> Energy prices will rise for everyone, there will be shortages of some products, and there will be a chain of negative reactions for factories that will not be able to secure supplies

— Al-Kaabi, Qatar's energy minister

The economic impact of the conflict caused the [Saudi Vision 2030](50299103.md) plan to undergo major changes.[^cite_note-220]

According to a *[Wirtschaftswoche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirtschaftswoche)* analysis, prolonging the conflict would mean a "catastrophe" for the [Gulf states](3088555.md) such as Qatar and the UAE.[^cite_note-Stölzel-2026-221] Iraq is even worse hit than the other Gulf countries, as oil and gas is 90% of income for Iraq's state budget, and 90% of Iraq's import of food, goods and medicine comes through the Strait of Hormuz.[^cite_note-222]

Following the Iranian blockade of the straights of Hormuz Gulf countries revive of US-led plans of IMEC, a corridor that would run from India through the Gulf and then to the Israeli port of Haifa and from there to Europe.[^cite_note-\:26-223] Saudi Arabia is currently considering to expand its 1,200 kilometres (750 mi) East-West pipeline built in the 1980s after fears that the Iran-Iraq "tanker war" would close the strait. The pipeline currently delivers seven million barrels of oil a day to the Red Sea port of Yanbu, bypassing Hormuz entirely.[^cite_note-\:26-223]

Since the war started, oil production in southern Iraq has dropped by more than 70% and the volume of imported goods reaching the Iraq's ports has been cut in half. Zubair oil field in [Basra](57580.md), that produced around 400,000 barrels per day, has seen output drop to roughly 250,000 as a result of continuous attacks by Iran.[^cite_note-\:27-224] This is a heavy economic toll on the Iraqi's economy since 90% of its GDP come from oil export.[^cite_note-\:27-224]

#### Food and water supply shortages

The [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md) imports the vast majority of its food. Around 70% of their food is imported through the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's blockade of the straits has caused a severe food security challenge in the region, resulting in shortages and severe price increases on groceries.[^cite_note-229]

Desalination supplies 77.3% of total water demand in Qatar, 67.5% in Bahrain, 52.1% in the UAE, 42.2% in Kuwait, 31% in Oman, and 18.1% in Saudi Arabia. Qatar obtains 99% of its potable supply from desalination, while Bahrain more than 90%. In Kuwait, Oman, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE, desalination accounts for roughly 90%, 86%, 70%, and 42% of drinking water, respectively. Major cities, like Doha, Dubai, Manama, and Kuwait City, depend on desalination for their very existence. Qatar and Bahrain also rely heavily on desalinated water for industrial use, allocating more than half of their production to sectors such as petrochemicals and data centers. As a result, any damage to or disruption of desalination infrastructure would pose a serious threat to water security, affecting businesses, industry, and potentially millions of people across the Persian Gulf.[^cite_note-230] the attacks by Iran on desalination plants, has raised fears of a disastrous disruption to water supplies.[^cite_note-231]

#### Exodus of talent and tourists

There has been a major exodus of expats and immigrants from the major Gulf states as a result of the war.[^cite_note-\:9-43][^cite_note-232] Several academics and journalists from the region have written about the long-term socioeconomic impact on the region and its consequences for immigration.[^cite_note-\:10-44][^cite_note-\:12-45][^cite_note-\:13-46][^cite_note-\:14-47][^cite_note-\:15-48][^cite_note-\:11-233]

There is likely to be a long term impact in the westward movement of Indians to the Middle East. Hundreds of thousands of Indian nationals have been repatriated from the region,[^cite_note-\:16-234] including a high percentage of the skilled professionals and business owners, driving a 14% growth in secondary real estate markets of India.[^cite_note-\:17-235] This suggests that the movement is long term in nature and policy makers in India have taken note of this;[^cite_note-\:18-236] interviews of Indians who lived in the region suggests the impact of shock and trauma of the war may be long term.[^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238][^cite_note-239]

Tourism in the UAE and Qatar suffered, with hotel bookings plummeting and economic hubs like Dubai facing paralysis.[^cite_note-240] According to a *[Wirtschaftswoche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wirtschaftswoche)* analysis, prolonging the conflict would mean an economic "catastrophe" for the [Gulf states](3088555.md) such as Qatar and the UAE.[^cite_note-Stölzel-2026-221] Major sporting events in the Middle East have been canceled or rescheduled in multiple countries due to the war including the 2026 [Bahrain Grand Prix](511204.md) and [Saudi Grand Prix](65766701.md) Formula One races.[^cite_note-241] The 2026 [Dubai World Cup Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_World_Cup_Night), while still scheduled to take place on 28 March, has also been affected as many participants, most notably 2025 [Dubai Sheema Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_Sheema_Classic) winner [Danon Decile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danon_Decile), have cancelled their plans to compete in the scheduled races citing the ongoing tension as well as disruption of air travel.[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243] The [2026 Esports World Cup](82191810.md), originally scheduled to take place in Riyadh, would move to [Paris](22989.md) whilst still being scheduled for July and August 2026.[^cite_note-244]

#### Real estate

In July 2026, Iran, in response to US attacks, plans to blockade both the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md) and the [Bab-el-Mandeb](62138.md) with the help of its allies in a pincer movement to create a crisis in world trade and the global energy sector, which could lead to a recession in the [Gulf countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_countries). Since March 2026, Gulf real estate has suffered a 30% loss in value. The countries most exposed to this loss are those bordering the Gulf, such as [Bahrain](18933277.md), [Qatar](8238258.md), and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), less so [Saudi Arabia](349303.md). As of July 2026, the decline in real estate value is accelerating.[^cite_note-245] While the United States aims to weaken Iran's offensive military capacity and consequently provoke a serious internal crisis against the regime, Iran aims to destabilize the monarchies and rulers of the Gulf countries. Iran wants to lead its adversaries into a war of attrition, undermining the Gulf economies in order to dissuade the United States from continuing the conflict and obtain revenge and reparations from its enemies. The United States escalated the conflict in July to push Iran into unconditional surrender without compensation.[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247][^cite_note-248] The conflict is escalating towards civilian targets, worsening the crisis; not only are oil and gas facilities hit, but also industrial plants and desalination plants, especially in [Kuwait](7515890.md) and [Bahrain](18933277.md).[^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252]

In August 2026, with the continuation of the war,[^cite_note-253] the real estate market in the Gulf countries lost another 10% in value. While Iran continued to attack Bahrain, Qatar, and the United Arab Emirates, the [Houthis](17240332.md) from Yemen continued to attack Saudi Arabia, particularly its [Saudi Aramco](290527.md) refineries. On 20 August 2026, the United States launched the [Economic D-Day](84050791.md) against Iran; however, some analysts are very skeptical about the effectiveness of this initiative, and suggest that it might instead further strengthen Iran's position, since Iran is already de facto an isolated country, accustomed for decades to economic sanctions and skilled, with China's complicity, in circumventing them.[^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255] China has declared that the sanctions are illegal and that it will merely act in its own interests.[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258] Russia and China are supporting Iran militarily and financially in the conflict.[^cite_note-259][^cite_note-260][^cite_note-261]

## Near East

The countries along the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) ([Armenia](10918072.md), [Azerbaijan](746.md) and [Georgia](48768.md)) have been [diplomatically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic) cautious due to reasons linked to trade and national security. The main preoccupation is related to the high risk of being merged the [Armenia–Azerbaijan conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93Azerbaijan_conflict) with the [Iran–Israel proxy conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Israel_proxy_conflict) due to the informal pro-US [Azerbaijan–Israel](11716339.md) and pro-Russia [Armenia–Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93Iran_relations) geo-strategic alliances (which also could affect the [Georgian–Ossetian conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian%E2%80%93Ossetian_conflict)), as also the fear of an uprising by [Azerbaijani separatists in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_separatism_in_Iran) backed by the US and Israel that could destabilize the local geopolitical calculations in the zone (currently based in the [2025 Armenia–Azerbaijan peace negotiations](79518584.md)). Another preoccupation has been the exodus of foreign nationals seeking to escape the war zone through the [Azerbaijan–Iran border](63622217.md) and [Armenia–Iran border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93Iran_border). The three Caucasian countries have been calling for a quick cessation of hostilities, and the [Armenian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Government) since 28 February established a working group (from the [Security Council of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council_of_Armenia)) to assess the US-Israel War on Iran potential impact on the Caucasus geopolitics.[^cite_note-OC_Media-2026a-262][^cite_note-263] For Azerbaijan there are fears that exist about the possibility of an independent [Iranian Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Azerbaijan) or a post-Islamic Republic [Iran](14653.md) that could harm its geopolitical privileges with the [Western Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Bloc).[^cite_note-264]

Since 3 March, [Azerbaijan](746.md)'s troops have already begun deploying toward the [Iranian border](63622217.md) as the conflict rages. This heightened military presence was justified in the fact that the Iran conflict severely escalates the risk of border skirmishes, especially if Iranian internal security collapses or if Israel uses Azerbaijani territory to launch attacks against Iran.[^cite_note-265][^cite_note-OC_Media-2026b-266] However, [Jeyhun Bayramov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeyhun_Bayramov) ([Foreign Ministry](11712667.md)) stated that "it is impossible for any country to use the territory of Azerbaijan against neighboring and friendly Iran."[^cite_note-OC_Media-2026b-266][^cite_note-OC_Media-2026a-262] China warned that is highly possible that the Iran War will have spillovers in the Caucasus and spread beyond [Middle East](19323.md) if peace isn't reached sooner with respect of Iran's territorial integrity.[^cite_note-267] The [Kadyrovites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadyrovites) ([Chechnya](6095.md)'s Akhmat special forces who fought in the [Islamic State insurgency in the North Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_insurgency_in_the_North_Caucasus)) have stated that they're prepared to intervene in the side of the [Axis of Resistance](36658251.md) in case the Russian interests in the [South Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Caucasus) and [Ukraine](70149799.md) are threatened by a total war instigated by US-Israel War on Iran.[^cite_note-268]

In [Asia minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_minor), [Turkey](11125639.md) strengthened the military presence in the [Iran–Turkey border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Turkey_border) to safeguard its territory and airspace from the risk of spillover due to the increase of regional tensions at a "terrifying level" due to the impact of the US-Israel war on Iran.[^cite_note-269] Simultaneously, [Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan) declared that its [government](3680197.md) is closely following the development of the Iranian conflict, trying to approach to the [international community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_community) in diplomatic efforts to advise the belligerent parties to refrain from steps that can increase instability and danger the global security.[^cite_note-270] Turkey and [Iraqi Kurdistan government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Kurdistan_government) discussed the regional tensions and considered to develop a common policy to end regional conflicts and Iran-backed terrorism as also the one of the [Kurdistan Workers' Party](69680.md).[^cite_note-271] On Cyprus, protests have started against the existence of [British Cyprus Bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Cyprus_Bases), as also the [Cyprus–United Kingdom relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus%E2%80%93United_Kingdom_relations) chilled, both due to the [Cypriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypriots) fearing that the country could be dragged to the Iranian conflict if the bases are used for offensive instead of defensive purposes, which would have catastrophic consequences for their economy and foreign policy.[^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273][^cite_note-274]

[Italian intelligence agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_intelligence_agencies) have been reporting that it is highly possible that the entire [Eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean) region will be affected by the growing instability in the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) and the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md), mainly suffering impacts on energy markets and supply security, which will impact the Mediterranean countries and Europe as a whole if the [Indo-Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Mediterranean) trade route through [Suez Canal](29323.md)-[Red Sea](26191.md) is damaged by the conflict (especially if [Houthis](17240332.md) do a more active aid to Iran at the [Gulf of Aden](58716.md)), generating a widening arc of instability across the multiple regions of [Afro-Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Eurasia). [Europe](9239.md) is expected to suffer a second energy crisis and consequent economic crisis, primarily as a result of the suspension of Qatari [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) (LNG) and the closure of the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md).[^cite_note-rinaldi-49] The conflict coincided with historically low European gas storage levels—estimated at just 30% capacity following a harsh 2025–2026 winter—causing Dutch [TTF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_Transfer_Facility) gas benchmarks to nearly double to over €60/MWh by mid-March.[^cite_note-rudinger_bouadica-50]

> "Just like the crisis after Russia's fullscale invasion of Ukraine. Different conflict. Same European divisions; same dilemmas over energy. We can't keep going round in these circles. Something's got to give."

— A quote of a European MEP by Katya Adler of the BBC. She writes that "You'd be hard-pushed to find a policy-maker in Europe who didn't agree with that last statement.".

[European natural gas prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_Transfer_Facility) nearly doubled after, on 2 March, the Qatari Ministry of Defense announced that two Iranian drones [attacked Qatari gas facilities](82557695.md), followed closely by an announcement from QatarEnergy that all gas production in the country has been halted. This raised concerns over energy security and fertilizer costs.[^cite_note-Guardian-297][^cite_note-298] EU natural gas prices decreased again to a less-high €48/MWh on Wednesday 4 March.[^cite_note-299]

Shipping disruption contributed to volatility in UK energy markets, with analysts warning that wholesale gas price increases could raise household energy bills and expose the country's reliance on global fuel markets.[^cite_note-300] The situation also renewed political debate over the role of domestic production in the [North Sea](21179.md), including potential reforms to the UK's Energy Profits Levy ([windfall tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windfall_tax_\(United_Kingdom\))) on oil and gas producers and the future of investment in the sector.[^cite_note-301] However, analysts noted that additional drilling in the North Sea would be unlikely to significantly reduce UK energy bills in the short term, as most oil and gas produced there is sold on international markets at global prices.[^cite_note-302][^cite_note-303] Iran conflicts also bolstered the necessity for renewable energy, as solar and wind power can reduce vulnerability to external supply and decentralized power generation that offers greater autonomy from global energy markets. With fluctuating oil prices, renewable energy has become significantly more cost-competitive.[^cite_note-304][^cite_note-305] British supermarket Asda also warned that it was already facing shortages of fuel at their fuel stations.[^cite_note-306]

In September 2026, [Isabel Schnabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabel_Schnabel), a member of the European Central Bank's Executive Board, said that the energy price shock was proving "much more persistent" than initially expected. Speaking at an event in [Salsomaggiore Terme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salsomaggiore_Terme), [Italy](14532.md), she noted that price increases were no longer confined to oil but were also affecting diesel and gas. "Initially, there was still the question: 'Well, maybe this is a short-lived event,' but we unfortunately had to learn that this is much more persistent," Schnabel said. She added that the [European Central Bank](9474.md) had raised interest rates twice, in June and September, in response to inflation concerns.[^cite_note-307] On 20 September 2026, it was reported that 15% of French service stations had already run out of either diesel or gas.[^cite_note-308]

### Inflation

Economists have increased projected inflation rates for 2026 as a result of the war, with many citing an increased risk of stagflation.[^cite_note-309]

The [European Central Bank](9474.md) (ECB) postponed its planned interest rate reductions on 19 March, raising its 2026 inflation forecast and cutting GDP growth projections, with economists warning that energy-intensive economies face high risks of technical [recession](25382.md) if the maritime blockade persists through the summer refill season. The European Union has increased its inflation forecast to between 2.6% and 4.4% depending of the severity of the war.[^cite_note-310]

UK inflation is expected to breach 5% in 2026, the highest prediction for Europe.[^cite_note-morningstar_19-03-26-51][^cite_note-maaal_20-03-26-52]

The OECD forecasts that due to the war, the inflation in the US will be 4.2% this year, higher by 1.2% than previous predictions.[^cite_note-311][^cite_note-312] Similar prediction were issued by the US [Federal Reserve](10819.md).[^cite_note-313] Nevertheless, a 2026 [Dallas Federal Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank_of_Dallas) working paper projected using [DSGE model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_stochastic_general_equilibrium) a 0.6 [percentage point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percentage_point) rise in US [headline inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headline_inflation) under one-quarter closure of the [strait of Hormoz](29005.md), while the longer-term inflation is projected to barley move.[^cite_note-314] As of June 2026, the June 2025 - June 2026 inflation rate is 3.53%, the [consumer price index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_price_index) decreased by 0.42% since May 2026, while the weekly earnings increased by 0.77% in the same period.[^cite_note-315]

In March the inflation rate in the EU reached to 2.5% as energy prices increased by 4.9%.[^cite_note-316] The yearly growth forecast for Germany was cut to 0.6%.[^cite_note-317]

The heavy economic toll caused by the war have urged more than 40 countries to discuss how to open the Hormuz straits.[^cite_note-318]

Europe is facing elevated risks of falling into a recession. The crisis has further impacted industrial output in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and the EU, where chemical and steel manufacturers have imposed surcharges of up to 30% to offset surging electricity and feedstock costs, potentially leading to permanent deindustrialization in some sectors.[^cite_note-tg_17-03-26-53][^cite_note-bbc_19-03-26-54] The economic impact on Europe has been characterized by a severe energy-supply shock and industrial strain, with the European Central Bank (ECB) warning that a prolonged conflict will likely trigger a period of [stagflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagflation) and push major energy-dependent economies, including Germany and Italy, into technical [recession](25382.md) by the end of 2026.[^cite_note-\:6-55] The UK was the worst hit by the war-induced global bond market sell-off, signalling weakness in the UK gilts market.[^cite_note-320]

### War in Ukraine

The EU defence commissioner [Andrius Kubilius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrius_Kubilius) said on 6 March that US military costs were overstretched in having a shortage of key missile stocks, and the US was becoming incapable of providing enough of both military aid to its Gulf allies and [military aid to Ukraine](70196881.md) in its [war against Russia](42085878.md).[^cite_note-322] In France, the main military bases at [Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest,_France) and [Toulon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulon) have become nearly empty due to its mobilization of forces towards the Middle East.[^cite_note-323]

Ukraine had gained experience in dealing with and producing drones in the four years between the [2022 invasion](79400268.md) and the start of the Iran War. Following the Iranian drone attacks on the Gulf states, Ukrainian president [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) concluded 10-year defense cooperation agreements with Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and the United Arab Amirates on 27 March. In response to Russia's reengineered [Shahed](75153890.md) drone threat, Ukraine has developed and now exports low‑cost counter‑drone systems such as [Sting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_\(drone\)). These systems might support Gulf countries in defending essential infrastructure and civilian zones outside the range of US‑provided point‑defense systems, which are largely positioned to protect American bases.[^cite_note-324] A piece for *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* by war technology journalist David Kirichenko noted that the sudden need for drone systems had provoked international interest in Ukrainian technology for its lower cost and high effectiveness, however, he noted that the country would need to strike a balance between its burgeoning defence export market, and technology needed on the front lines at home.[^cite_note-325]

#### Brazil

[Brazil](3383.md) is almost entirely dependent on imported fertilizers, with nearly half of its supply transiting the Strait of Hormuz. Brazilian beef and chicken exporters have adapted to the conflict's disruption by rerouting their shipments to the [Red Sea](26191.md) and [Suez Canal](29323.md) to reach [Middle Eastern](19323.md) markets like Iraq, Qatar, markets that account for more than 30% of their export. According to *The New York Times*, the suspension of tanker traffic in the Strait of Hormuz (through which 20% of the world's oil passes) and the wandering of about 200 ships in the region have disrupted the supply chain. Saudi Arabia's largest refinery and Qatar's export facilities have also been targeted by drone attacks. These disruptions have been directly transmitted to US markets, with Brent crude jumping 15% to \$83 per barrel by 5 March 2026.[^cite_note-b-352] Gasoline prices in the US rose 7.5% to \$3.20 per gallon.[^cite_note-a-133] [Gasoline](23639.md) prices rose above \$4 per gallon, the highest since late 2023.[^cite_note-b-352] [ABC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_News_\(United_States\)) reports that rising energy prices have complicated the inflation outlook. The consumer price index rose 2.4% in January, but the oil shock caused by the war could wipe out those gains.[^cite_note-c-353] Former Federal Reserve Chair Janet Yellen has warned that, depending on the impact of the war on the oil market, economic growth will suffer and the Fed's job of containing inflation will become more difficult.[^cite_note-c-353]

US stock markets have also not been immune to this tension. Rising oil and gasoline prices have reduced the purchasing power of American consumers. "Rising oil prices weigh on economic growth and push up inflation," emphasize some economists,[^cite_note-Guardian-297][^cite_note-354] according to the Turkish [Anadolu Agency](43356543.md).[^cite_note-a-133] ABC News also points to the risk of reduced investment and employment: "As new uncertainty is injected into the business environment, confidence will decline, and companies will invest and hire less." If the war continues and the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md) remains closed for weeks, oil prices will cross \$100. In that scenario, [Goldman Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Sachs) predicts that gasoline in the United States will reach \$3.50 per gallon and inflation will become a permanent problem.[^cite_note-c-353]

[California](5407.md)'s gasoline prices surged above \$5 per gallon during the second week of March.[^cite_note-355] [Goldman Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Sachs) says the risk of a downturn over the next 12 months has risen to 30%, driven by the surge in oil prices. The bank expects the unemployment rate to rise to 4.6% by the end of 2026, up from 4.4% in February, as hiring slows. Several firms now see inflation running closer to 3% this year rather than 2%, eroding disposable incomes and weighing on job creation.[^cite_note-356] Nevertheless, Goldman Sachs also says that the Iran war does not a broad drive supply chain crisis.[^cite_note-357] Moreover, some economists have argued that modern economies are less vulnerable to oil shocks that in the 1970s, citing lower oil intensity, more flexible labor markets, and more credible monetary policy. A similar pattern has been found using geopolitical risk indices more broadly. According to the [WSJ](173070.md) July 2026 survey of 74 economists, the consensus remains that the U.S. economy will not experience a recession, with the average probability of a downturn over the next year falling to 25% from 33% in the previous survey.[^cite_note-\:37-30] From the [cost-benefit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost%E2%80%93benefit_analysis) perspective, and despite estimating a war cost of more than \$890 million a day, [Kent Smetters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_Smetters), the faculty director of the [Penn Wharton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_Wharton) Budget Model, estimated that the cost pales in comparison to the cost of a nuclear Iran, a cost that would mount to trillions upon trillions of dollars.[^cite_note-358]

#### Venezuela

After the [US intervention](82021123.md) earlier in the year, Venezuela was forecast to increase oil production by one million barrels a day by 2035, with an apparent oil boom coming at a beneficial time when key economies aimed to diverge oil imports from the Middle East.[^cite_note-\:28-331] Europe's rise in jet fuel shipments from the US appeared to come from oil imported by US refineries that heeded Donald Trump's call to invest in the region.[^cite_note-359] However, [Wood Mackenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_Mackenzie) director Dylan White suggested that Venezuela's output, despite having grown impressively between the US intervention and the start of the war, would not be enough to compensate for the disruption caused by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, and the rise in production in Venezuela and Brazil could be stopped if the Strait reopened quickly.[^cite_note-\:28-331]

Since the beginning of the year, Venezuela's oil production have surged by 28.5% from 924,000 [b/d](594043.md) in January to 1,187,000 b/d in June.[^cite_note-360]

### Asia-Pacific

The Middle East is the primary source of petroleum products for the majority of Asia, including up to 60% of crude oil.[^cite_note-361] The conflict has exposed Asian economies' vulnerability to a prolonged closure of the Strait of Hormuz.[^cite_note-362] On 4 March LNG spot prices in Asia more than doubled to three-year highs, reaching \$25.40 per million [British thermal units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_thermal_unit) (MMBtu) as [QatarEnergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QatarEnergy_LNG) declared force majeure at its giant [Ras Laffan](24864449.md) LNG plant.[^cite_note-363][^cite_note-364] Ras Laffan is the world's largest liquefaction facility and is responsible for 20% of global LNG production. The majority (around 80%) of its volumes serve customers in Asia, mostly in China, Japan, India, and South Korea.[^cite_note-365]

According to analysts, growth is expected to be cut up to 1.3% in developing Asia and the Pacific countries.[^cite_note-366]

#### Australia

Prices have risen at local petrol stations, and on 12 March, Australian energy minister [Chris Bowen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Bowen) authorised a 60-day suspension of national fuel quality standards, allowing domestic refineries to produce and sell petrol with higher levels of sulphur, which was normally reserved for export. The action aimed to introduce an additional 100 million litres of fuel per month over a 60-day period.[^cite_note-367] The wholesale diesel price rose to A\$2.45 per litre by 23 March, and it was feared that the shortage of fertiliser ([urea](31734.md)) could threaten the autumn planting season for the [agricultural sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Australia).[^cite_note-368] On 24 March, the fuel quality standards were also relaxed for diesel.[^cite_note-369][^cite_note-r800-370] Although local prices had increased, Australia had not yet felt the full impact of oil supply shortages caused by the war, as there is a lag in the supply chain from Asia, and physical shortages have not yet reached Australia's shores. Hundreds of petrol stations reported fuel shortages.[^cite_note-371] The government said that fuel deliveries are assured until mid-April.[^cite_note-jackson-372]

#### China

[China](5405.md) has been affected by disruption to the trade routes along the [Belt and Road initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_and_Road_initiative) that import petroleum and export manufactured goods. However, China has taken measures to mitigate potential petroleum shortages with reserves and investment in renewable energy.[^cite_note-373][^cite_note-374] China has engaged in mediation efforts in several regional conflicts including [Iran–Saudi Arabia proxy war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Saudi_Arabia_proxy_war), [Afghan conflict](27576770.md) and the [2026 Afghanistan–Pakistan war](82482902.md).[^cite_note-375][^cite_note-376] China officially declared itself [neutral country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_country), while seeking to maintain relations with potential future governments in Iran, as Iran is China's third-largest supplier of crude oil (being expected an increase in China's energy import costs and overall inflationary pressures) and China has invested over US\$100 billion in energy and infrastructure projects in Iran (which could be halted by the fighting or by sanctions from the US against the renminbi settlement system between China and Iran).[^cite_note-377] Some surveys have reported a relatively positive public outlook toward the war in China.[^cite_note-378] China restricted exports of fertilizers, including urea, to prioritize domestic [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_China) needs amid rising global prices and supply chain disruptions.[^cite_note-aljazeera-2026-3-18-146]

#### India

[India](14533.md) maintained significant economic and energy interests in the Persian Gulf, relying on the region for nearly 60% of its petroleum imports and as the primary source for over \$125 billion in annual [remittances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance) sent by a diaspora of primarily low-income workers whose earnings support millions of families back home.[^cite_note-379] This dependency is especially notable in the fertilizer sector, with over 40% of India's [urea](31734.md) and [phosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate) sourced from the region. Following a drop in LNG output from Qatar, India reduced production at three urea plants. As a leading global food producer, accounting for approximately 25% of [rice exports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_rice_exports) in 2024, this dependence may affect global supply chains during periods of regional instability.[^cite_note-aljazeera-2026-3-18-146]

While India was a major ally of [Iran](14653.md) during the anti-colonial era, New Delhi has strengthened its relationship with [Israel](9282173.md), including Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md)'s visit to the [Knesset](74656.md) in February 2026, which some analysts argue contributed to reduced engagement with Iran, potentially contributing to closer military and economic ties between Iran, [China](5405.md) and [Russia](25391.md).[^cite_note-380] To mitigate the energy shock caused by the closure of the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md), the [US Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Treasury) granted India a temporary 30-day emergency waiver on 6 March 2026, authorizing the purchase of stranded [Russian oil](10450931.md) cargoes to stabilize domestic fuel prices.[^cite_note-381] However, the conflict has had significant impacts on the West Asian diaspora; a large-scale departure of foreign residents has followed strikes on civilian infrastructure, leading analysts to conclude that the war has challenged perceptions of cities like [Dubai](211583.md) as stable destinations for migrant workers.[^cite_note-\:18-236][^cite_note-382][^cite_note-383][^cite_note-384]

The term 'returnees' refers to a socio-economic trend in [India](14533.md) triggered by the war. As of March 2026, over 220,000 Indian nationals have been repatriated from the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md) region and [Iran](14653.md) due to the escalating conflict and the blockade of the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md).[^cite_note-\:16-234] Unlike the temporary labor migrations of the past, this reverse migration involves a high percentage of skilled professionals and business owners. Economic data from March 2026 indicates that this demographic is bypassing over-congested Tier-1 metros in favor of Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities, driving a 14% growth in these secondary real estate markets.[^cite_note-\:17-235] The influx of "Returnee" capital is described by some analysts as a strategy to offset multiple economic pressures, as the Indian economy grapples with falling remittances and rising energy costs (\$100+ per barrel) caused by the ongoing war.[^cite_note-\:18-236]

#### Japan

In Japan, disruptions in energy supplies contributed to a significant rise in domestic fuel prices. On 16 March 2026, the national average retail price for regular gasoline reached a record ¥190.8 per liter, an increase of ¥29 from the previous week, according to the Agency for Natural Resources and Energy.[^cite_note-japan-385] In response, the government introduced subsidies aimed at keeping gasoline prices at around ¥170 per liter.[^cite_note-japan-385] In June, it approved a supplementary budget of ¥3.11 trillion to cushion households and businesses against higher energy costs, including a ¥2.5 trillion reserve fund to respond to developments in the Middle East.[^cite_note-386] By the end of July, the balance of the fund for gasoline subsidies had fallen to about ¥210 billion, while the cumulative cost of the subsidy program had reached approximately ¥9 trillion.[^cite_note-387] On 31 July 2026, as a result of the weakening yen when it hit a 40-year low, the United States launched a rare [intervention to prop up the yen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_U.S.%E2%80%93Japan_yen_intervention). This depreciation had been exacerbated by increasing energy costs as a result of the Iran war.[^cite_note-388]

The [Philippines](23440.md)' overall supply of oil has been among the most affected, more so than Thailand and Vietnam, causing the [Philippine Peso](227672.md) (PHP) to drop to a record low of 62.86 PHP per [USD](18717338.md) on 14 September 2026.[^cite_note-389] The Philippines importing 95% of crude oil needs as well as a sluggish economy before the war was expected to push up the peso's inflation rate.[^cite_note-390] Prior to that, the prices of [petroleum products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_product) also increased with diesel prices in the country hiking up by 38.6%.[^cite_note-391] Alongside this, electricity prices all throughout the country have also been slated to go up in response to the increase in usage during the summer months accompanied by high oil prices.[^cite_note-392] In response to rising prices, several government agencies have implemented a four-day work week to reduce electricity costs.[^cite_note-393] On 24 March 2026, President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) declared [a state of national energy emergency](82777095.md) due to the rising oil prices.[^cite_note-394]

### Africa

Since the start of the US–Israel war on Iran, fears have developed that it will spill over into [Egypt](8087628.md) through the [Gaza theater](74998519.md) or the [Red Sea crisis](75207766.md). The [Egyptian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_government) has been considering the conflict as both a general threat to the security of the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world) and a question of economic [national security](240468.md), due to the effects of the higher oil prices that increase the cost of imports for Egypt and place pressures on the [Egyptian economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_economy) (specially by straining the public finances and complicating the efforts to stabilise the economy after the [Egyptian Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Crisis_\(2011%E2%80%932014\))).[^cite_note-402][^cite_note-403] Another preoccupation for Egypt is that the conflict reduced the traffic through the [Suez Canal](29323.md), which is declining vital shipping activity and costing approximately \$10 billion in losses according to the [World Bank Group](36752.md).[^cite_note-404][^cite_note-405] This has led to a situation of near [state of emergency](298608.md) directed against the price-gougers, who could be processed in military courts by [treason](31292.md) due to harming the traffic of shipping companies in Suez and the national interests of Egypt as an import-dependent economy.[^cite_note-406] Another indirect economical effect is that the Iran conflict quietly stalled the [GERD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ethiopian_Renaissance_Dam) negotiations between Egypt and [Sudan](27421.md) with [Ethiopia](187749.md), as international mediators such as the US, [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN) and [African Union](10634933.md) diverted their diplomatic efforts to the [2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis](82550763.md), so Ethiopia has been operating the dam without a binding agreement with the other Nile river states.[^cite_note-407]

The [Ethiopia economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Ethiopia) has been projected severe [price shocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_\(economics\)) as their economy relies heavily on imported refined petroleum (Ethiopia sources the vast majority of its fuel from the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Kuwait) due to not producing commercial crude oil. The Strait of Hormuz crisis has provoked skyrocketing risk premiums and maritime insurance costs, which translate immediately into [inflated](38286.md) import prices for Ethiopia, while the Red Sea crisis is threatening the logistical risks due to the fact that 90–95% of its petroleum imports pass through a single chokepoint at the [Port of Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Djibouti), which is provoking speculation due the fears of renewed Houthis attacks that will be elevating freight costs. Despite this, economists have proposed that Ethiopia could avoid the worst part of a global recession if they capitalize on its 95% renewable electricity generation (driven by projects like the [Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ethiopian_Renaissance_Dam)) to accelerate a shift toward electric mobility and so reducing the transport sector's demand for imported petroleum.[^cite_note-408]

The [Port of Berbera](41240501.md) in [Somaliland](51425.md) (an ally of [Israel](9282173.md) and of the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), who have a military presence in the area)[^cite_note-409] has been considered a military target by [Houthis](17240332.md) (and their [Al-Shabaab](8726463.md) allies). The Israel recognition of Somaliland included discussions of Israel having a military base in Berbera, with Houthis warning after the recognition that "Israeli assets in Somaliland would be legitimate military targets", risking regional stability in the [Horn of Africa](62142.md).[^cite_note-410][^cite_note-411] Similar notices has been sent by the Houthis to the rival [Presidential Leadership Council](70488867.md), threatening to attack any US or [Saudi](46230181.md) military facilities in [Yemen](350939.md) on a [current civil war](46215853.md) (as also Iran has reportedly increased diplomatic engagement with Ethiopia in the context of the [Red Sea crisis](75207766.md)).[^cite_note-412] [Ismaïl Omar Guelleh](392341.md), president of [Djibouti](17207794.md) and ally of [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), denounced that both Israel and United Arab Emirates are driving strategic realignments across [Northeast Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Africa) that risk intensifying current conflicts (like the [Somali Civil War](3070332.md), [Sudanese Civil War](1141082.md), [insurgency in Chad](67450724.md), [Libyan crisis](44791215.md), etc.) and a possible merging of them with the [Iran–Israel proxy conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Israel_proxy_conflict).[^cite_note-413]

In Kenya, the [Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_and_Petroleum_Regulatory_Authority) announced record high fuel surges in mid-April.[^cite_note-414] This resulted in public transport operators hiking their fares by roughly 25% dependent on various factors.[^cite_note-415][^cite_note-416] These actions are expected to raise inflation and increase prices for basic goods and important services.[^cite_note-417] Increased oil prices led to protests including on 21 April[^cite_note-418] and 18 May where four people died.[^cite_note-419]
