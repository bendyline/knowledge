A constitutional referendum on expanding [presidential](392259.md) powers was held on 30 August 2026 by the [ruling military junta](81695873.md) in [Guinea-Bissau](12186.md).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] The proposal was accepted with a majority of 70%.

## Background

One month and some weeks after the [coup d'état on 26 November 2025](81695397.md), the [National Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council_\(Guinea-Bissau\)) approved constitutional changes on 14 January 2026, in which the [president would also become the head of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system).[^cite_note-5] [Horta Inta-A Na Man](81702722.md) announced on 6 July 2026,[^cite_note-6] that there will be a constitutional referendum in which the people can endorse the approved planned constitutional changes by the NTC.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## Proposals

- Allowing the [president](392259.md) to appoint and dismiss the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_Guinea-Bissau) and cabinet, to ⁠create or abolish government ministries, chair the Council of Ministers, dissolve parliament in the event of a grave political crisis and dismiss the government under certain circumstances.

  [^cite_note-9]

- Shrink [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_People's_Assembly_\(Guinea-Bissau\)) from 102 to 65 seats and cut the number of electoral constituencies from 29 to 12.

  [^cite_note-10]

- Require candidates for president to have lived in Guinea-Bissau for the previous five years and pay a 50 million West African CFA franc (\$90,000) deposit.

  [^cite_note-11]

  [^cite_note-aljazeera-12]

- Require political parties to have a minimum of 5,000 members, including at least 300 in each region, before they can sponsor a presidential candidate.

  [^cite_note-aljazeera-12]

- Establishment of a Constitutional Court.

  [^cite_note-aljazeera-12]

## Campaign

Around 915,000 people were eligible to vote in the referendum.[^cite_note-euronews-13]

Opposition parties called for a boycott of the referendum, with the [PAIGC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAIGC) warning that the proposed constitution would weaken the prime minister, the government, political parties and the legislature.[^cite_note-14] The head of the Guinea-Bissau Human Rights League, Bubacar Turé, said "no" supporters were not authorised to campaign, and that the draft constitution was made without consultations from political parties, civil-society organisations, or even traditional and religious leaders.[^cite_note-euronews-13]

## Results

The National Electoral Commission announced the results on September 1, were 70% of voters voted in favour of the new constitution and 30% opposed.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] With the majority accepting it, Guinea-Bissau has a new constitution.[^cite_note-17]
