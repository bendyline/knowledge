**Ceuta** ([UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English): [/ˈsjuːtə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/ˈseɪuːtə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Spanish: [\[ˈθewta, ˈsewta\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/52/Pronunciation_of_Ceuta_in_Spanish.ogg/Pronunciation_of_Ceuta_in_Spanish.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/52/Pronunciation_of_Ceuta_in_Spanish.ogg/Pronunciation_of_Ceuta_in_Spanish.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Moroccan Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_Arabic_language): سَبْتَة, romanized: *Sabtah*) is an [autonomous city](55002.md#Autonomous_cities) of [Spain](26667.md) on the [North African](21714.md) coast. Bordered by [Morocco](19291.md), it lies between the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) and the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md). Ceuta is one of the [special territories of members of the European Economic Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_territories_of_members_of_the_European_Economic_Area): it is part of the [Schengen area](16565139.md), but with specific provisions such as border checks between Ceuta and European Spain.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

[Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) founded a settlement on the [La Almina Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Almina_Peninsula), which had continuity under Roman and Byzantine rule (*Septem Fratres*). It was annexed to the early caliphates upon the [Islamic conquest of the Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Maghreb), only to be destroyed during the [Berber Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_Revolt). It was rebuilt in the ninth century by Majkasa [Ghomaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghomaras). For much of the Middle Ages, regional powers north and south of the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar) vied for control over Ceuta, which was a key contested port in the so-called [Battle of the Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Strait). In 1415, it was annexed to the [Kingdom of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal). In 1580, it was annexed by the [Hispanic Monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_Monarchy_\(political_entity\)), and the city chose to remain under Spanish authority after 1640. Ceuta belonged to the Spanish [province of Cádiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_C%C3%A1diz) as a regular [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_in_Spain) until the passage of its [Statute of Autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Autonomy) in March 1995,[^cite_note-11] when it became an autonomous city.

Along with the [Canary Islands](5717.md), Ceuta was classified as a [free port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_port) before Spain joined the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-12] Its population is predominantly made up of [Spanish Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Spain) and [Arab Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Muslims), with a small minority of [Sephardic Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jews), and [Sindhi Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_Hindus) from [pre-partition India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India).[^cite_note-13] [Spanish](26825.md) is the official language of Ceuta, while [Darija Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_Arabic) (in the [Ceuta Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceuta_Arabic) dialect) is also widely spoken.

## Names

The name **Abyla** has been said to have been a [Punic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_language) name ("Lofty Mountain"[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECauvin_&_al.1843-14] or "Mountain of [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_\(deity\))") for [Jebel Musa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebel_Musa_\(Morocco\)),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBonney_&_al.190726-15] the southern [Pillar of Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillars_of_Hercules).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESmith1854-16] The name of the mountain was in fact *Habenna* ([Punic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_language): 𐤀𐤁‬𐤍‬, *ʾbn*, "Stone" or "[Stele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stele)") or *ʾAbin-ḥīq* (𐤀𐤁‬𐤍𐤇‬𐤒, *ʾbnḥq*, "Rock of the Bay"), about the nearby [Bay of Benzú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benz%C3%BA).[^cite_note-lip-17] The name was [hellenized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenization) variously as *Ápini* ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Ἄπινι),[^cite_note-lip-17] *Abýla* (Ἀβύλα), *Abýlē* (Ἀβύλη), *Ablýx* (Ἀβλύξ), and *Abilē Stḗlē* (Ἀβίλη Στήλη, "Pillar of Abyla")[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESmith1854-16] and in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) as *_Abyla Mons_* ("Mount Abyla") or *_Abyla Columna_* ("the Pillar of Abyla").

The settlement below Jebel Musa was later renamed for the seven hills surrounding the site, collectively referred to as the "Seven Brothers"[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESmedley_&_al.184549-18] ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Ἑπτάδελφοι, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ancient_Greek): *Heptádelphoi*;[^cite_note-19] [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Septem Fratres*).[^cite_note-20] In particular, the Roman stronghold at the site took the name "Fort at the Seven Brothers" (*Castellum ad Septem Fratres*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESmith1854-16] This was gradually shortened to Septem[^cite_note-Kaegi-21] (Σέπτον *Sépton*) or, occasionally, **Septum**[^cite_note-Kitto-22] or **Septa**.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDyer1873-23] These clipped forms continued as [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_language) *Sebta* and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) *Sabtan*[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESmedley_&_al.184549-18] or *Sabtah* (سبتة‎), which themselves became *Ceuta* in [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language) (pronounced [\[ˈseu̯tɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese)) and [Spanish](26825.md) (locally pronounced [\[ˈseu̯ta\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)).

## History

### Ancient

Main article: [Abyla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abyla)



A Phoenician archeological site, dated to the 7th century BC, next to the [Cathedral of Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Ceuta)

Controlling access between the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) and the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar) is an important military and commercial [chokepoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chokepoint). The [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicians) realized the extremely narrow isthmus joining the [La Almina Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Almina_Peninsula) to the African mainland made Ceuta eminently defensible and established an outpost there early in the 1st millennium BC.&#x20;

The [Greek geographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_geography) record it by variations of *Abyla*, the ancient name of nearby [Jebel Musa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebel_Musa_\(Morocco\)). Beside [Calpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calpe), the other [Pillar of Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillars_of_Hercules) now known as the [Rock of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_of_Gibraltar), the Phoenicians established [Kart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carteia) at what is now [San Roque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Roque,_C%C3%A1diz), [Spain](26667.md). Other good anchorages nearby became [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenician_colonies) and then [Carthaginian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginian_Empire) ports at what are now [Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier), Morocco and [Cádiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1diz).

After [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Carthage)'s [destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Carthage_\(c._149_BC\)) in the [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars), most of the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb) was left to the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) [client states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Client_state) of [Numidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numidia) and [Mauretania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauretania). [Punic culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punics) continued to thrive in what the Romans knew as "Septem". After the [Battle of Thapsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Thapsus) in 46 BC, [Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar) and his heirs began annexing North Africa directly as [Roman provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province). As late as [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus), most of Septem's [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) residents continued to speak and write in [Punic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_language).

[Caligula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caligula) executed the Mauretanian king [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_of_Mauretania) in AD 40 and seized his kingdom, which [Claudius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius) organised in AD 42, placing Septem in the [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of [Tingitana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tingitana) and raising it to the level of a [colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_colonia). It was [Romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_\(cultural\)) and thrived into the late 3rd century, trading heavily with [Roman Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania) and becoming well known for its [salted fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salted_fish). [Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_road) connected it overland with [Tingis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tingis) (Tangiers) and [Volubilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volubilis). Under [Theodosius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_I) in the late 4th century, Septem still had 10,000 inhabitants, nearly all [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christianity) [citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_citizenship) speaking [African Romance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Romance), a local dialect of Latin.

### Medieval



The [Marinid Walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinid_Walls_of_Ceuta), built by order of [Abu Sa'id Uthman II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Sa'id_Uthman_II) in 1328

[Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandal_Kingdom), probably invited by [Count Boniface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonifacius) as protection against the empress dowager [Galla Placidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galla_Placidia), crossed the strait near Tingis around 425 and swiftly overran [Roman North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_\(Roman_province\)). Their king, [Gaiseric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaiseric), focused his attention on the rich lands around [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage). Although the Romans eventually accepted his conquests and he continued to raid them anyway, he soon lost control of Tingis and Septem in a series of Berber revolts. When [Justinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I) decided to [reconquer the Vandal lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandalic_War), his victorious general [Belisarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belisarius) continued along the coast, making Septem a westernmost outpost of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) around 533. Unlike the former ancient Roman administration, Eastern Rome did not push far into the [hinterland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinterland) and made the more defensible Septem their regional capital in place of Tingis.

The [plague of Justinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_of_Justinian), less capable successors, and overstretched supply lines forced a retrenchment and left Septem isolated. It is likely that its [count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comes) (*_comes_*) was obliged to pay homage to the [Visigoth Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoth_Kingdom) in Spain in the early 7th century. There are no reliable contemporary accounts of the end of the [Islamic conquest of the Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_conquest_of_the_Maghreb) around 710. Instead, the rapid [Muslim conquest of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Spain) produced [romances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_romance) concerning [Count Julian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_Julian) of Septem and his betrayal of Christendom in revenge for the dishonor that befell his daughter, [Florinda la Cava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florinda_la_Cava), at [King Roderick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roderic)'s court. Allegedly with Julian's encouragement and instructions, the Berber convert and freedman [Tariq ibn Ziyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariq_ibn_Ziyad) took his garrison from Tangiers across the strait and overran the Spanish so swiftly that both he and his master [Musa bin Nusayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_bin_Nusayr) fell afoul of [a jealous caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulayman_ibn_Abd_al-Malik), who stripped them of their wealth and titles.

After the death of Julian, sometimes also described as a king of the [Ghomara Berbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghomara_people), Berber converts to Islam took direct control of what they called Sebta. It was then destroyed during [their great revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_Revolt) against the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) around 740. Sebta remained a small village of Muslims and Christians surrounded by ruins until its resettlement in the 9th century by Mâjakas, chief of the Majkasa Berber tribe, who started the short-lived [Banu Isam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Isam) dynasty.[^cite_note-GibbKramers1994-24] His great-grandson briefly allied his tribe with the [Idrisids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idrisid).[^cite_note-auto-25]

Banu Isam rule ended in 931,[^cite_note-auto-25] when he abdicated in favour of [Abd ar-Rahman III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_ar-Rahman_III), the Umayyad emir of Córdoba who had self-proclaimed as a caliph in 929. Through the overseas conquests of Ceuta in 931 and Melilla in 927 that allowed enforcement of direct political and military influence in the fragmented landscape of the north-African coast, crowned by the skillful political subversion resulting in the 944 revolt in eastern Berbery, the power exerted by the Umayyad Caliphate (engaged in struggle against the Fatimids) in the Western Mediterranean took hold.[^cite_note-26]



The [Arab Baths of Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Baths_\(Ceuta\)), built between the 11th and 13th centuries

Chaos ensued with [the collapse of the Caliphate of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitna_of_al-Andalus) in the early 11th century. In the wake of this, the [Banū Hammūd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammudid_dynasty) established a petty kingdom, and nominal caliphate, centered in [Málaga](184763.md) and Ceuta—the so-called [Taifa of Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_M%C3%A1laga)—with Málaga as a capital and Ceuta hosting the heir's residence.[^cite_note-27] In 1056, the link with Málaga was severed as it was conquered by the [Banu Ziri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zirid_dynasty), while Suqut al-Bargawati remained in power in Ceuta, styling as a caliph after 1061.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalero_Secall199447-28]

Starting in 1084, the [Almoravid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravid) Berbers ruled the region until 1147, when the [Almohads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad) conquered the land. Apart from [Ibn Hud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Hud)'s rebellion in 1232, they ruled until the Tunisian [Hafsids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafsid) established control. The Hafsids' influence in the west rapidly waned, and Ceuta's inhabitants eventually expelled them in 1249. After this, a period of political instability persisted, under competing interests from the [Marinids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinids) and [Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Granada) as well as autonomous rule under the native [Banu al-Azafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_al-Azafi). The Fez-based Marinid sultanate finally conquered the region in 1387, with assistance from [Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon).

### Portuguese



A representation of [Prince Henry the Navigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_the_Navigator) during the [Conquest of Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Ceuta) in [*azulejos*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azulejo) at the [São Bento railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Bento_railway_station)



A 1572 depiction of Ceuta



The [Royal Walls of Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Walls_of_Ceuta), built from 962 to the 18th century, and navigable moats

On the morning of 21 August 1415, King [John I of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_I_of_Portugal) led his sons and their assembled forces in a surprise assault that would come to be known as the [Conquest of Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Ceuta). The 45,000 Portuguese who traveled on 200 ships caught the defenders of Ceuta off guard and suffered only eight casualties. By nightfall the town was captured. On the morning of 22 August, Ceuta was in Portuguese hands.&#x20;

[Álvaro Vaz de Almada, 1st Count of Avranches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_Vaz_de_Almada,_1st_Count_of_Avranches) was asked to hoist what was to become the [flag of Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Ceuta), which is identical to the [flag of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Lisbon), but in which the coat of arms derived from that of the [Kingdom of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal) was added to the center. The original Portuguese flag and [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms) of Ceuta remained unchanged, and the modern-day Ceuta flag features the configuration of the [Portuguese shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Portugal#Portuguese_shield).

John's son [Henry the Navigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_the_Navigator) distinguished himself in the battle, being wounded during the conquest. The looting of the city proved to be less profitable than expected for John I, so he decided to keep the city to pursue further enterprises in the area.[^cite_note-29] From 1415 to 1437, [Pedro de Meneses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_de_Meneses,_1st_Count_of_Vila_Real) was the first governor of Ceuta. The [Marinid Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinid_Sultanate) started the [1419 siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Ceuta_\(1419\)). They were defeated by the first governor of Ceuta, before reinforcements arrived in the form of [John, Constable of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John,_Constable_of_Portugal) and his brother Henry the Navigator, who were sent with troops to defend Ceuta.

Under [King John I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_I_of_Portugal)'s son, [Duarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward,_King_of_Portugal), the city of Ceuta rapidly became a drain on the Portuguese treasury. [Trans-Saharan trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_trade) journeyed instead to [Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier). It was soon realized that without the city of Tangier, possession of Ceuta was worthless. In 1437, [Duarte's brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illustrious_Generation_\(Portugal\)) [Henry the Navigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_the_Navigator) and [Fernando, the Saint Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando,_the_Saint_Prince) persuaded him to launch an attack on the [Marinid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinid) sultanate. The resulting [Battle of Tangier (1437)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tangier_\(1437\)), led by Henry, was a total failure. In the resulting treaty, Henry promised to deliver Ceuta back to the Marinids in return for allowing the Portuguese army to depart unmolested, which he reneged on.

The Portuguese conquest marked Portugal's first hold in the strategic area of the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar). The development had a mixed reception from [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile), which had to the same in 1292 in the wake of the [Conquest of Tarifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Tarifa) on the north side, as it marked the arrival of a potential challenger for hegemony in the area.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECriado_Atalay202468,_73–74-30] Whatever the case, the authorities soon established collaboration with [Tarifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarifa) across the Strait, and throughout the 15th century Ceuta relied on support from coastal Andalusian towns.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECriado_Atalay202477-31] Cross strait collaboration with Ceuta and other Portuguese outposts in the area ([Ksar es-Seghir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ksar_es-Seghir), [Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier), and [Asilah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Asilah)) from [Gibraltar](7607314.md) and Málaga or Cádiz and Puerto de Santa María (depending on prevailing winds) was sustained in the 1520s.[^cite_note-32] In the case of Ceuta, this collaboration strengthened after 1580.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECriado_Atalay202486-33]

[Portuguese expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Portugal_\(1415%E2%80%931578\)) throughout the coasts of the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb) took place on the basis of the presence of grain, cattle, sugar, and textiles, as well as fish, hides, wax, and honey.[^cite_note-Payne-34] The city was recognized as a Portuguese possession by the [Treaty of Alcáçovas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Alc%C3%A1%C3%A7ovas) (1479) and by the [Treaty of Tordesillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tordesillas) (1494). In the 1540s, the Portuguese began building the [Royal Walls of Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Walls_of_Ceuta) as they are today including [bastions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastion), a navigable moat and a drawbridge. Some of these bastions are still standing, like the bastions of Coraza Alta, Bandera and Mallorquines.[^cite_note-fortified-places-35] [Luís de Camões](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_de_Cam%C3%B5es) lived in Ceuta between 1549 and 1551, losing his right eye in battle, which influenced his work of poetry *[Os Lusíadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Os_Lus%C3%ADadas)*.

### Hispanic Monarchy

In August 1578 King [Sebastian of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_of_Portugal) died at the [Battle of Alcácer Quibir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Alc%C3%A1cer_Quibir) (known as the Battle of Three Kings) in what is today northern Morocco, without descendants, triggering the [1580 Portuguese succession crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1580_Portuguese_succession_crisis). His grand-uncle, the elderly [Cardinal Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry,_King_of_Portugal), succeeded him as king, but also had no descendants, having taken [holy orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_orders). When the he died a year and a half later, in January 1580, three grandchildren of King [Manuel I of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_I_of_Portugal) claimed the throne: [Infanta Catarina, Duchess of Braganza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infanta_Catarina,_Duchess_of_Braganza), [António, Prior of Crato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio,_Prior_of_Crato), and [Philip II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain), the uncle of former King Sebastian of Portugal. In the [ensuing war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Portuguese_Succession), which lasted for three years, Philip eventually prevailed and was crowned King [Philip I of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_I_of_Portugal) in April 1581 at the [Cortes](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortes%20de%20Tomar%20de%201581) meeting in [Tomar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomar), thus [uniting under a dynastic union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Union) the crown of [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Portugal_\(1580%E2%80%931640\)) (along with its overseas empire) with the crowns of [Castille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castille) and [Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon) (which were already under a dynastic union since 1479).[^cite_note-Kamen177-36]

During the Hispanic Monarchy, which lasted from 1580 to 1640, Ceuta attracted many residents of mainland Spanish origin[^cite_note-Griffinh-37] and eventually became the only city in the [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) that sided with Madrid when Portugal regained its independence in the [Portuguese Restoration War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Restoration_War) of 1640–1668.

During the early modern period, Ceuta served as a defensive outpost against [Barbary corsairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_corsairs) from the [Barbary Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_Coast) of North Africa who [raided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_slave_trade) the Spanish coast.[^cite_note-38]

On 1 January 1668, King [Afonso VI of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afonso_VI_of_Portugal) recognised the formal allegiance of Ceuta to Spain and ceded Ceuta to King [Carlos II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_II_of_Spain) by the [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon_\(1668\)).[^cite_note-39] The city was attacked by Moroccan forces under [Moulay Ismail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulay_Ismail) during the [Siege of Ceuta (1694–1727)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Ceuta_\(1694%E2%80%931727\)), the longest siege in history.[^cite_note-40] During these years, the city underwent changes, leading to the loss of its "Portuguese" character. While most of the military operations took place around the [Royal Walls of Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Walls_of_Ceuta), there were also small-scale penetrations by Spanish forces at various points on the Moroccan coast, and ships were seized in the Strait of Gibraltar. In 1790–1791, Ceuta was [besieged by the Moroccan army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Ceuta_\(1790%E2%80%931791\)), but the siege was unsuccessful and the city remained under Spanish control. From 1810 to 1815, during the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), British forces occupied Ceuta.[^cite_note-41] Disagreements regarding the border of Ceuta resulted in the [War between Spain and Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispano-Moroccan_War_\(1859%E2%80%931860\)) of 1859–1860, which ended at the [Battle of Tetuán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_T%C3%A9touan). The subsequent 1860 [Treaty of Wad Ras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Wad_Ras) expanded the borders of the Spanish *[plazas de soberanía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plazas_de_soberan%C3%ADa)*.[^cite_note-42]

In July 1936, General [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco) took command of the [Spanish Army of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Army_of_Africa) and rebelled against the Spanish republican government; his military uprising led to the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War) of 1936–1939. Franco transported troops to mainland Spain in an airlift using transport aircraft supplied by [Germany](21212.md) and [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy). Ceuta became one of the first battlegrounds of the uprising: General Franco's rebel nationalist forces seized Ceuta, while at the same time the city came under fire from the air and sea forces of the republican government.[^cite_note-43] The [Llano Amarillo monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumento_del_Llano_Amarillo) was erected to honor Francisco Franco and inaugurated on 13 July 1940. The tall obelisk has since been abandoned, but the shield symbols of the [Falange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falangism) and Imperial Eagle remain visible.[^cite_note-44]

Following the 1947 [partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India), a number of [Sindhi Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_Hindus) from current-day Pakistan settled in Ceuta, adding to a small Hindu community that had existed in Ceuta since 1893, connected to Gibraltar's Hindu community.[^cite_note-45] When Spain recognized the independence of [Spanish Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_protectorate_in_Morocco) in 1956, Ceuta and the other *[plazas de soberanía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plazas_de_soberan%C3%ADa)* remained under Spanish rule. Spain considered them integral parts of the Spanish state, but the Moroccan state has disputed this claim.

Culturally, modern Ceuta is part of the Spanish region of [Andalusia](2736.md). It was attached to the [province of Cádiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_C%C3%A1diz) until 1995. The Iberian coast is 20 km (12.5 miles) away. It is a cosmopolitan city, with a large ethnic [Arab](2185.md)-[Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) [Muslim](6037917.md) population (although the Berber presence is smaller in Ceuta than in [Melilla](20226.md), Arab Muslims comprise approximately half of Ceuta's population).[^cite_note-46] Small minorities of [Sephardic Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jews) and [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) are also present.[^cite_note-47]

On 5 November 2007, King [Juan Carlos I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_I) and Queen [Sofía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Sof%C3%ADa_of_Spain) visited Ceuta and Melilla, sparking enthusiasm from the local population and protests from the Moroccan government, which led to a brief [diplomatic conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Morocco%E2%80%93Spain_diplomatic_conflict).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] It was the first time a Spanish head of state had visited the two cities since 1927.[^cite_note-50] Since 2010, Ceuta and Melilla have declared the [Muslim holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_holiday) of [Eid al-Adha](358226.md), or Feast of the Sacrifice, an official public holiday, the first time a non-Christian [religious festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_festival) has been officially celebrated in Spanish ruled territory since the [Reconquista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconquista).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

In summer 2026, [unrest](83824713.md) occurred in Ceuta after approximately 70,000 migrants breached the border with Morocco.

## Geography

Ceuta is separated by 17 km (11 mi)[^cite_note-53] from the [province of Cádiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_C%C3%A1diz) on the Spanish mainland by the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar). It shares a 6.4 km (4 mi) land [border](48209107.md) with [M'diq-Fnideq Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M'diq-Fnideq_Prefecture) of the [Kingdom of Morocco](19291.md). It has an area of 18.5 km<sup>2</sup> (7 sq mi; 4,571 acres).[^cite_note-TarverSlape2016-54] Ceuta is dominated by Monte Anyera, a hill along its western frontier with Morocco, which is guarded by a Spanish [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_army) fort. [Monte Hacho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Hacho) on the [La Almina Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Almina_Peninsula) overlooking [the port](60033690.md) is one of the possible locations of the southern pillar of the [Pillars of Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillars_of_Hercules) of Greek legend. The other possibility is [Jebel Musa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebel_Musa_\(Morocco\)).[^cite_note-TarverSlape2016-54]

### Important Bird Area

The Ceuta Peninsula has been recognised as an [Important Bird Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Important_Bird_Area) (IBA) by [BirdLife International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BirdLife_International) because the site is part of a migratory bottleneck, or choke point, at the western end of the Mediterranean for large numbers of [raptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_of_prey), [storks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stork) and other birds flying between the Iberian Peninsula and the Maghreb. These include [European honey buzzards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_honey_buzzard), [black kites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_kite), [short-toed snake eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-toed_snake_eagle), [Egyptian vultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_vulture), [griffon vultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffon_vulture), [black storks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_stork), [white storks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_stork) and [Audouin's gulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audouin's_gull).[^cite_note-bli-55]

### Climate

Ceuta has a maritime-influenced [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate), similar to such nearby Spanish and Moroccan cities as [Tarifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarifa), [Algeciras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeciras), [Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier), and [Tétouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A9touan).[^cite_note-56] The average [diurnal temperature variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnal_temperature_variation) is relatively low. The average annual temperature is 18.8 °C (65.8 °F) with average yearly highs of 21.4 °C (70.5 °F) and lows of 15.7 °C (60.3 °F), though the Ceuta weather station has only been in operation since 2003.[^cite_note-Standard_climate_values_for_Ceuta-57] Ceuta has relatively mild winters for the latitude. Summers are warm yet milder than in the interior of Southern Spain, due to the moderating effect of the Straits of Gibraltar. Summers are very dry. Yearly precipitation is still at 849 mm (33.4 in),[^cite_note-Standard_climate_values_for_Ceuta-57] which could be considered a humid climate if the summers were not so arid.

In 2023, Ceuta experienced above average temperatures, with a maximum of 105.4 °F (40.8 °C) recorded on the 19th of July 2023.[^cite_note-58]

## Government and administration



The Palacio de la Asamblea de Ceuta is the seat of the [Assembly of Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_Ceuta).

Since 1995, Ceuta has been one of the two [autonomous cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_community#autonomous_cities) of Spain (along with [Melilla](20226.md)).[^cite_note-60] Ceuta is known officially in Spanish as *Ciudad Autónoma de Ceuta* (English: *Autonomous City of Ceuta*), with a rank between a standard [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_in_Spain) and an [autonomous community](55002.md). Ceuta is part of the territory of the [European Union](9317.md). The city was a [free port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_port) before Spain joined the European Union in 1986. Today, Ceuta has a low-tax system within the [Economic and Monetary Union of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_and_Monetary_Union_of_the_European_Union).

Since 1979, Ceuta has held elections to its 25-seat [assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_Ceuta) every four years. The leader of its government was the Mayor until the Autonomy Statute provided for the new title of [Mayor-President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor-President). As of 2011, the [People's Party](404727.md) (PP) won 18 seats, keeping [Juan Jesús Vivas](689977.md) as Mayor-President, which he has been since 2001. The remaining seats are held by the regionalist [Caballas Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caballas_Coalition) (4) and the [Socialist Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Workers'_Party_\(Spain\)) (PSOE, 3).[^cite_note-61]

Owing to its small population, Ceuta elects only one member of the [Congress of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Deputies_\(Spain\)), the lower house of the *[Cortes Generales](519703.md)* (the Spanish Parliament). As of the November 2023 general election, this post is held by Javier Celaya Brey of the PP, who gained it from [Vox](41928488.md).[^cite_note-62] Ceuta is subdivided into 63 *barriadas* ("neighborhoods"), such as Barriada de Berizu, Barriada de P. Alfonso, Barriada del Sarchal, and El Hacho.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] Ceuta maintains its own police force.

## Defence and Civil Guard

Defence of the territory is the responsibility of the [Spanish Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Armed_Forces)'s General Command of Ceuta (COMGECEU).[^cite_note-Contenido_-_Army-66] The [Spanish Army](2142325.md)'s combat components of the command include:

- [54th *Regulares* Infantry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulares) based in *González Tablas* barracks;

- [2nd Tercio *Duke of Alba* Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tercio_%22Duque_de_Alba%22_No._2_of_the_Legion) of the [Spanish Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Legion) based in the Seraglio-Recarga cantonment;

- 3rd "Montesa" Cavalry Regiment (RC-3) located in the *Colonel Galindo* barracks and equipped with [Leopard 2 main battle tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard_2) and [Pizarro infantry fighting vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASCOD);

- 30th Mixed Artillery Regiment, one group equipped with [155/52mm towed howitzers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_B%C3%A1rbara_Sistemas_155/52) and the other with [Mistral short-range SAMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral_\(missile\)) and [35/90 SKYDOR/35/90 GDF-007 anti-aircraft guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oerlikon_GDF) fulfilling an air defence role;

  [^cite_note-68]

  &#x20;and

- 7th Engineer Regiment.

The command also includes its headquarters battalion as well as logistics elements.[^cite_note-Contenido_-_Army-66]

In 2023, the [Spanish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Navy) replaced the *Aresa*-class patrol boat *P-114* in the territory with the *Rodman*-class patrol boat *Isla de León*.[^cite_note-69]

Ceuta itself is only 113 km (70 mi) distant from the main Spanish naval base at [Rota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Station_Rota) on the Spanish mainland. The [Spanish Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Air_Force)'s [Morón Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mor%C3%B3n_Air_Base) is also within 135 km (84 mi) proximity.

The [Civil Guard](325008.md) is responsible for border security and protects both the territory's [fortified land border](859281.md) as well as its maritime approaches against frequent, and sometimes significant, migrant incursions.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-NPR_1_Aug_26-71]

## Economy

The official currency of Ceuta is the [euro](9472.md). It is part of a special low tax zone in Spain.[^cite_note-Ceuta.es-72] Ceuta is one of two Spanish port cities on the northern shore of Africa, along with [Melilla](20226.md). They are historically military strongholds, [free ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_port), oil ports, and also fishing ports.[^cite_note-73] Today, Ceuta's economy heavily depends on its port (now in expansion), and its industrial and retail centres.[^cite_note-Ceuta.es-72]

[Lidl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidl), [Decathlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decathlon_\(retailer\)) and [El Corte Inglés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Corte_Ingl%C3%A9s) have branches in Ceuta. There is also a [casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casino). Border trade between Ceuta and Morocco is active because of advantage of tax-free status. Thousands of Moroccan women are involved in the cross-border porter trade daily, as [porteadoras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porteadoras). The [Moroccan dirham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_dirham) is used in such trade, although prices are marked in euros.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

### Transport



Ceuta Heliport

The city's [Port of Ceuta](60033690.md) is connected to the [Port of Algeciras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Algeciras) across the Strait of Gibraltar by multiple daily sailings of ferries. A single road border checkpoint, near [Fnideq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fnideq), allows for cars and pedestrians to travel between Morocco and Spain. An additional border crossing for pedestrians exists between Benzú and [Belyounech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belyounech) on the northern coast. The rest of the border is closed and inaccessible. There is a bus service throughout the city, and while it does not pass into neighbouring Morocco, it services both frontier crossings.[^cite_note-77]

The [Ceuta Heliport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceuta_Heliport) is now used to connect the city to mainland Spain by air.&#x20;

In 2024, its population was 83,299.[^cite_note-84]

Due to its location, Ceuta is home to a mixed ethnic and religious population. The two main religious groups are Christians and Muslims. In 2006, 51% of the population was Christian and 48% was Muslim.[^cite_note-85] In a 2018 estimate, approximately 68% of the city's population was born in Ceuta.[^cite_note-86] [Spanish](26825.md) is the official language of Ceuta.[^cite_note-87] [Moroccan Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_Arabic) ([Ceuta Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceuta_Arabic) dialect) is also widely spoken.[^cite_note-88] In 2025, Spanish speakers were estimated to number nearly 60%, [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) speakers 40–42%, and another 1% were [Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_language) speakers.[^cite_note-chapter4-5]

Remains of the Late Roman Christian Basilica and Necropolis of Ceuta, dated to the mid-4th century AD or the beginning of the 5th century AD



The [Cathedral of St. Mary of the Assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_St_Mary_of_the_Assumption_\(Ceuta\)), completed in 1726

[Christianity](5211.md) has had a continuous presence in Ceuta from [late antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_antiquity), as evidenced by the ruins of a basilica in Ceuta's city-centre,[^cite_note-89] and by accounts of the 1227 [martyrdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_martyrdom) of [St. Daniel Fasanella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_and_companions) and his [Franciscans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan_Order) during the [Almohad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad_Caliphate). The town's medieval Grand Mosque had been built over a [Byzantine-era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_architecture) church. In 1415, the year of the city's conquest, the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) converted the Grand Mosque into [Ceuta Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceuta_Cathedral). The present form of the cathedral dates to late-17th century renovations, combining [baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) and [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) elements. It was [dedicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedication_\(ritual\)) to [St Mary of the Assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Mary_of_the_Assumption) in 1726.

The [Roman Catholic](606848.md) [Diocese of Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Ceuta) was established in 1417. It incorporated the [Diocese of Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Tangier) in 1570.[^cite_note-90] The Diocese of Ceuta was a [suffragan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragan) of [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Lisbon) until 1675, when it became a suffragan of [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Seville).[^cite_note-91] In 1851, Ceuta's administration was notionally merged into the [Diocese of Cádiz and Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Cadiz_y_Ceuta) as part of [a concordat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordat_of_1851) between Spain and the [Holy See](13393.md).[^cite_note-92] The union was finalised in 1879. Small [Jewish](15624.md) and [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) minorities are also present.[^cite_note-93]

## Border control

Main article: [Ceuta border fence](859281.md)

Like [Melilla](20226.md#Immigration), Ceuta's position on the [Maghreb's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb) [Mediterranean](19006.md) poses a challenge both in terms of curbing illegal human and [hashish](14415.md) trafficking (of which neighboring Morocco is the world's leading producer and exporter).[^cite_note-94]

Regarding [irregular migration](32828476.md), people breach the double fence separating Ceuta from Morocco in groups or use the sea to skirt around the Tarajal or Benzú border points.[^cite_note-95] Notable large-scale breaches occurred [in 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Morocco%E2%80%93Spain_border_incident) (approximately 8,000 people) and [in 2026](83824713.md) (approximately 60,000 people).[^cite_note-NPR_1_Aug_26-71] The Moroccan position towards Ceuta (and Melilla) displays many characteristics of a [grey-zone strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey-zone_\(international_relations\)), of which the mobilisation of civilians is a key instrument.[^cite_note-96]

Regarding drug trafficking, in addition to the use of [hidden compartments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_compartment) in vehicles,[^cite_note-97] traffickers have used [narco-tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smuggling_tunnel) dug from Morocco.[^cite_note-98]

## Education

The [University of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Granada#Campus_located_in_Ceuta) has undergraduate programmes at its campus in Ceuta. Like all areas of Spain, Ceuta is also served by the [National University of Distance Education (UNED)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Distance_Education). While primary and secondary education are generally offered in Spanish only, a growing number of schools are entering the Bilingual Education Programme.

## Dispute with Morocco

See also: [Greater Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Morocco)

The [Moroccan government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Morocco) has repeatedly called for Spain to transfer the sovereignty of Ceuta, [Melilla](20226.md) and the *[plazas de soberanía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plazas_de_soberan%C3%ADa)* to Morocco, with Spain's refusal to do so serving as a major source of tension in [Morocco–Spain relations](32865910.md). In Morocco, Ceuta is frequently referred to as "[occupied](816214.md)". The Moroccan government has argued that Ceuta, along with other Spanish territories in the region, are [colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony).[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]

One of the major arguments used by Morocco in attempts to acquire sovereignty over Ceuta refers to the geographical position of the city, as Ceuta is an [exclave](4848945.md) surrounded by Moroccan territory and the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md); it has no territorial continuity with the rest of Spain.[^cite_note-111] This argument was originally developed by one of the founders of the Moroccan [Istiqlal Party](1790362.md), Alal-El Faasi, who openly advocated for Morocco to occupy Ceuta and other North African territories under Spanish administration.[^cite_note-112] Spain, in line with the majority of the world's states, has never recognized Morocco's claim over Ceuta. The official position of the [Spanish government](1306113.md) is that Ceuta is an integral part of Spain, and has been since the 16th century.[^cite_note-113] A 2012 French doctoral thesis claims that the "majority" of Ceuta's population support continued Spanish sovereignty and oppose Moroccan control over the territory.[^cite_note-114]

In 1986, Spain joined [NATO](21133.md). Article 6 of the [North Atlantic Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Treaty) limits such coverage to continental Europe, North America, and islands north of the [Tropic of Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_of_Cancer). Decades earlier, [French Algeria](292341.md) was included in the treaty (upon France's NATO entry). Until 1962, Algeria was considered an integral part of territorial France. Some legal experts have claimed that other articles of the treaty could cover Spanish territories in North Africa; however, this interpretation has not been tested in practice.[^cite_note-Newtral-115]

During the [2022 Madrid summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Madrid_summit), Spain raised the issue of Ceuta's protection, with NATO Secretary General [Jens Stoltenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Stoltenberg) stating: "On which territories NATO protects, and Ceuta and Melilla, NATO is there to protect all Allies against any threats. At the end of the day, it will always be a political decision to invoke Article 5, but rest assured NATO is there to protect and defend all Allies".[^cite_note-116] In December 2020, following statements made by Morocco's then-Prime Minister [Saadeddine Othmani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadeddine_Othmani) that Ceuta is "Moroccan as the [Sahara](325363.md)," the Spanish government summoned the Moroccan ambassador, Karima Benyaich, to convey that Spain expects all its partners to respect the sovereignty and territorial integrity of its territory, asking for an explanation for Othmani's words.[^cite_note-117]
