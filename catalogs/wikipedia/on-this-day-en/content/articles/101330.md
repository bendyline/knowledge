**Khan Yunis** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): خان يونس, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) '[khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravanserai#Khan) (caravanserai) \[of] Yunis'[^cite_note-name-ref-anera.org-2]), also spelled **Khan Younis** or **Khan Yunus**, is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) in the southern [Gaza Strip](12047.md), Palestine, and serves as the capital of the [Khan Yunis Governorate](13900254.md). It has been largely destroyed during the [Gaza war](74998519.md), and is currently split by the yellow line with [Hamas](13913.md) controlling its north and Israel controlling its south.&#x20;

Before the 14th century, Khan Yunis was a village known as "Salqah". A vast [caravanserai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravanserai), today known as [Barquq Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barquq_Castle), was constructed there by the [emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) Yunis al-Nawruzi[^cite_note-abukhalaf-3][^cite_note-museumwnf-4] in 1387–88 to protect caravans, pilgrims, and travellers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESharon1999228-5]

According to the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics), Khan Yunis had a population of 205,125 in 2017.[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1] Khan Yunis, which lies only four kilometres (2+1⁄2 miles) east of the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), has a [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) with temperature of 30 °C maximum in summer and 10 °C minimum in winter, with an annual rainfall of approximately 260 mm (10.2 in).

## History

### Ancient period

[Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus) describes a city named *Ienysos* ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Ιηνυσος) located between [Lake Serbonis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Bardawil) and [Kadytis](55387.md) (modern Gaza city). He talks about how the Persian military marched through the location on its way to Egypt. He also describes how the coastal area between [Kadytis](55387.md) and Ienysos was inhabited by [local Arab tribes](61770.md). Some sources, due to phonological resemblance of the names and due to the general matching of the geographic locations, associate this site with modern Khan Yunis.[^cite_note-abukhalaf-3][^cite_note-Retsö-6]

Other sources have suggested a further inland location of "Khirbet Ma'in Abu Sitta" (Palestinian village depopulated in 1949, near modern kibbutz of [Nir Oz](17751040.md))[^cite_note-7] or the Egyptian town of [Arish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arish) as possible locations of Ienysos, but there is no clear evidence to support this identification.[^cite_note-Retsö-6][^cite_note-8]

Ancient discoveries in Khan Yunis feature a [lintel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lintel) with a [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) inscription, discovered repurposed in the tomb of Sheikh Hamada. The inscription translates to: '*Hilarion - giving thanks to [St. Georgius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_George)*.' Originally housed in the Musée de Notre Dame de France in Jerusalem, the lintel is currently lost.[^cite_note-9]

### Establishment by Mamluks

Main article: [Barquq Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barquq_Castle)



The southern part of the historic *[khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_\(inn\))* ([Barquq Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barquq_Castle)) at Khan Yunis, 1930s

Before the 14th century, Khan Yunis was a village known as "Salqah".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESharon1999228-5] A vast [caravanserai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravanserai), today known as [Barquq Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barquq_Castle), was constructed there by the [emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) [Yunus al-Nawruzi ad-Dawadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunus_al-Nawruzi_ad-Dawadar?action=edit\&redlink=1)[^cite_note-abukhalaf-3][^cite_note-museumwnf-4] in 1387–88, an official of the [Mamluk Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Empire) to protect caravans, pilgrims, and travellers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESharon1999228-5] The growing town surrounding it was named "Khan Yunis" after him.[^cite_note-abukhalaf-3] Yunus was killed in battle in 1389.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESharon1999229-10] Yunus was the executive secretary (*[dawadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawadar)*), one of the high-ranking officials of the [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate_\(Cairo\)) [Circassian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians) sultan [Barquq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barquq). The town became an important center for trade and its weekly Thursday market drew traders from neighboring regions.[^cite_note-abukhalaf-3][^cite_note-11]

The khan served as resting stop for couriers of the *[barid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barid)*, the Mamluk [postal network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_history_of_Palestine) in Palestine and Syria.

### Ottoman period

Khan Yunis was the site of a minor [battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Yaunis_Khan) in late 1516, when the Egypt-based Mamluks were defeated by [Ottoman](22278.md) forces under the leadership of [Hadim Sinan Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadim_Sinan_Pasha). The Ottoman sultan [Selim I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selim_I) then arrived in the area where he led the Ottoman army across the [Sinai Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula) to conquer Egypt.[^cite_note-12] During the 17th and 18th centuries the Ottomans assigned an [Asappes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azeb) garrison associated with the [Cairo Citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_Citadel) to guard the fortress at Khan Yunis.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHathaway200238-13] The village and its fortified caravenserai continued to function as an important stop on the Cairo-Damascus road.[^cite_note-14]

[Pierre Jacotin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Jacotin) named the village *Kan Jounes* on his map from 1799,[^cite_note-15] while in 1838, [Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Robinson_\(scholar\)) noted *Khan Yunas* as a [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) village located in the Gaza district.[^cite_note-16] In 1863 French explorer [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) visited Khan Yunis. He found it had about a thousand inhabitants, and that many fruit trees, especially [apricots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apricot) were planted in the vicinity.[^cite_note-17]

During the [Ottoman period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_period), the [Bedouin](61770.md) [tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe) of **'Arab al-Wahidat** (عرب الوحيدات) were among the residents of Khan Yunis.[^cite_note-18]

At the end of the 19th century, the Ottomans established a municipal council to administer the affairs of Khan Yunis, which had become the second largest town in the Gaza District after [Gaza](55387.md) itself.[^cite_note-19]

### British Mandate



Khan Yunis 1931 1\:20,000

In the [1922 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine) conducted by the [British Mandate authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), Khan Yunis had a population of 3890 inhabitants (3866 Muslims, 23 Christians, and one Jew),[^cite_note-Census1922-20] decreasing in the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine) to 3811 (3767 Muslims, 41 Christians, and three Jews), in 717 houses in the urban area[^cite_note-censusP4-21] and 3440 (3434 Muslims and 6 Christians) in 566 houses in the suburbs.[^cite_note-censusP5-22]

In the 1938 village statistics, the population is listed as 4,379 (including three Jews) with 3,953 in nearby suburbs.[^cite_note-23] In the [1945 statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945), Khan Yunis had a population of 11,220 (11,180 Muslims and 40 Christians),[^cite_note-1945p31-24] with 2,302 (urban) and 53,820 (rural) [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam) of land, according to an official land and population survey.[^cite_note-25] Of this, 4,172 dunams were plantations and irrigable land, 23,656 used for cereals,[^cite_note-26] while 1,847 dunams were built-up land.[^cite_note-27]

### 1948–1967



Khan Yunis 1945 1\:250,000

Khan Yunis was one of the towns where [Palestinian refugees](74681.md) who were forcibly expelled from areas of Palestine to the east and north of the Gaza Strip gathered, to wait for a resolution that would allow then to return to their ancestral villages and homes.&#x20;



A mother and child stand next to their tent in a newly formed Khan Younis refugee camp in the wake of the war in 1948 (courtesy of [UNRWA](32270.md))

During the night of 31 August 1955, three Israeli [paratroop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paratroopers_Brigade) companies attacked the British-built [Tegart fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tegart_fort) in Khan Yunis from where attacks had been carried out against Israelis.[^cite_note-Katz-28] The police station, a petrol station and several buildings in the village of [Abasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abasan_al-Kabera) were destroyed, as well as railway tracks and [telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegraph) poles. In heavy fighting, 72 Egyptian soldiers were killed. One Israeli soldier was killed and 17 were wounded. The operation led to a ceasefire on September 4, forcing President [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser) and the Egyptian government to halt [Palestinian fedayeen operations](14068131.md#Suez_Crisis) against Israel.[^cite_note-29] One of the mechanized companies was commanded by [Rafael Eitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Eitan).[^cite_note-Katz-28][^cite_note-30]

Before the [Suez War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_War), Khan Yunis was officially administered by the [All-Palestine Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Palestine_Government), seated in Gaza and later in Cairo. After a fierce firefight, the [Sherman tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_tank) of the IDF 37th Armored Brigade broke through the heavily fortified lines outside of Khan Yunis held by the 86th Palestinian Brigade.[^cite_note-Varble,_Derek_page_46-31] It was the only site in the Gaza strip where the Egyptian army put up any resistance to the Israeli invasion of Gaza, but it surrendered on 3 November 1956.

There are conflicting reports of what happened. Israel said that Palestinians were killed when Israeli forces were still facing armed resistance, while the Palestinians said all resistance had ceased by then, and that many unarmed civilians were killed as the Israel troops went through the town and camp, seeking men in possession of arms.[^cite_note-un-32]

The killings, dubbed the [Khan Yunis massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Yunis_massacre), were reported to the UN General Assembly on 15 December 1956 by the Director of the [United Nations Relief and Works Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Relief_and_Works_Agency), Henry Labouisse. According to the report, the exact number of dead and wounded is not known, but the director received lists of names of persons allegedly killed from a trustworthy source, including 275 people, of which 140 were refugees and 135 local residents.[^cite_note-un-32][^cite_note-ap-33]

After 1959, the All-Palestine Government of Gaza Strip was abolished and the city was included in the [United Arab Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Republic), which was shortly disestablished and the Gaza Strip came under the direct [Egyptian military occupation rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Gaza_Strip_by_Egypt).

### 1967 and aftermath



The Kaware house after bombing in 2014, see [Kaware family home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_civilian_casualties_of_Israeli_bombing,_shelling_and_rocket_attacks_on_the_Gaza_Strip#Kaware_family_home)

In 1967, during the [Six-Day War](29328.md), [Israel](9282173.md) occupied Khan Yunis again.

As a result of the 1993-1995 [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords), Khan Yunis and most of the Gaza Strip (excluding Israeli settlements and military areas) were placed under the control of the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md).

Khan Yunis was the site of [Israeli](9282173.md) helicopter attacks in August 2001 and October 2002 that left several civilians killed, hundreds wounded and civilian buildings within the vicinity destroyed.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

The Palestinian Authority came into control of the entirety of the Strip following the [2005 Israeli disengagement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_disengagement_from_Gaza). However, following the [Battle of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gaza_\(2007\)) in 2007, [Hamas](13913.md) took over the Gaza Strip and established [its own government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas_government_in_the_Gaza_Strip) in the region.

### Israel–Gaza war

Main articles: [Siege of Khan Yunis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Khan_Yunis), [July 2024 Khan Yunis incursion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2024_Khan_Yunis_incursion), [August 2024 Khan Yunis incursion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2024_Khan_Yunis_incursion), and [Battle of Khan Yunis (2025)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khan_Yunis_\(2025\))



Ruins of a mosque destroyed in Israeli airstrikes in Khan Yunis, on October 8, 2023



Damage in Khan Yunis in 2025

During the ongoing Israel–Gaza war, Israel has bombed Khan Yunis along with other cities in the Gaza Strip as part of an offensive against Hamas. The [Israeli Air Force](177619.md) extensively bombed much of the city, including the [Hamad City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamad_City) apartment complex.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] Local sources have reported numerous civilian casualties in Khan Yunis as a result of Israeli bombings,[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] which Palestinian news agency [Wafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wafa) put at "at least 70" as of December 3.[^cite_note-41] The [Al Qarara Cultural Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Qarara_Cultural_Museum) was destroyed in an explosion as a result of an Israeli attack in October 2023, part of an offensive that reportedly targeted civilian homes and mosques in the vicinity.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Israeli armored units began entering the outskirts of the city [in December 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Khan_Yunis). After several months of fighting, Israeli forces ended up withdrawing from Khan Yunis and most of the southern Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] Having failed to root out Hamas from Khan Yunis,[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] Israeli forces began [a second invasion of the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Khan_Yunis) in late July 2024, which also ended in an Israeli withdrawal.[^cite_note-49] Israeli forces [invaded Khan Yunis yet again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Khan_Yunis) in August 2024, and withdrew once more at the end of the month.[^cite_note-50] Following [the January 2025 ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2025_Gaza_war_ceasefire), Hamas forces were seen parading through Khan Yunis.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] Following the resumption of the war in March, Israeli forces launched [a fourth invasion of the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khan_Yunis_\(2025\)) in May.

## Economy

Khan Yunis is the second largest urban area in the Gaza Strip after [Gaza City](55387.md). It serves as the principal market center of the territory's southern half and hosts a weekly Bedouin *[souk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souk)* ("open-air market") mostly involving local commodities.[^cite_note-53] As of 2012 Khan Yunis had the highest unemployment rate in the Palestinian territories.[^cite_note-54]

### Al Qarara Cultural Museum

Located in [Al-Qarara](15614535.md), on the outskirts of Khan Yunis, the [cultural museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Qarara_Cultural_Museum) tells the story of the history of Palestine. Key objects included [Byzantine mosaics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_mosaics), swords dating to the [Crusader period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades), [thobes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_thobe), as well as jewellery worn by women prior to the [Nakba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakba).[^cite_note-jinha-57] It also included military items dating to the [British Mandate in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine).[^cite_note-\:2-58]

The museum's structure, display cases, and collection were destroyed in October 2023 by [Israeli forces](39237.md).[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-\:3-61]
