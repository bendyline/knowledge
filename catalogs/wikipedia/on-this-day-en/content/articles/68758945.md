**Kenneth Walker III** (born October 20, 2000) is an American professional [football](18951490.md) [running back](295079.md) for the [Kansas City Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs) of the [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL). Nicknamed "**K9**",[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] he played [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football) for the [Wake Forest Demon Deacons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_Demon_Deacons_football) and [Michigan State Spartans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_Spartans_football), winning the [Walter Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Camp_Award) and [Doak Walker Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doak_Walker_Award) in 2021. Walker was selected by the [Seattle Seahawks](28390.md) in the second round of the [2022 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NFL_draft). He spent four seasons in Seattle, being named the [MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) of [Super Bowl LX](63727016.md) in 2025. The following offseason, he signed with the Chiefs.

## Early life

Walker was born on October 20, 2000, in [Memphis, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee).[^cite_note-4] He attended [Arlington High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_High_School_\(Tennessee\)) and played for their football team,[^cite_note-5] where he rushed for 3,485 yards with 41 [touchdowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touchdowns). He committed to [Wake Forest University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_University) to play [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football).[^cite_note-6]

## College career

Walker played at Wake Forest in 2019 and 2020. During those two years he rushed for 1,158 yards on 217 carries with 17 touchdowns.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] After the 2020 season, Walker transferred to [Michigan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_State_University).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] He became the starter his first year at Michigan State.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] In his debut with the Spartans, he rushed for 264 yards and four touchdowns.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] His four touchdowns doubled the Michigan State 2020 season total, and was the first time for a Spartan running back, in a single game, since the 2019 season.[^cite_note-15] Walker III was awarded the [Doak Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doak_Walker_Award) and [Walter Camp Player of the Year Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Camp_Award) and finished 6th in the [Heisman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisman_Trophy) for the [2021 NCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NCAA_Division_I_FBS_football_season) season.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] He earned Consensus [All-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-American) honors for the 2021 season.[^cite_note-18] Following the season, he announced that he would forgo his senior year and declared for the [2022 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NFL_draft).[^cite_note-19]

### Seattle Seahawks

#### 2022

Walker was drafted by the [Seattle Seahawks](28390.md) in the second round with the 41st overall pick in the [2022 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NFL_draft).[^cite_note-23]

Walker made his NFL debut in Week 2 against the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers).[^cite_note-24] In Week 5, against the [New Orleans Saints](21720.md), he had a 69-yard rushing touchdown in the 32–39 loss.[^cite_note-25] He was named the starting running back for the Seattle Seahawks after [Rashaad Penny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashaad_Penny) was placed on [injured reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injured_reserve).[^cite_note-26] In Week 7, against the [Los Angeles Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Chargers), Walker rushed for 167 yards and two touchdowns in the 37–23 victory.[^cite_note-27] On November 3, Walker was named the NFL's Offensive Rookie of the Month for October.[^cite_note-28] In Week 9, he rushed for 109 yards and two touchdowns in a 31–21 victory over the [Arizona Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Cardinals).[^cite_note-29] In Week 12, he had another game with two rushing touchdowns in the 40–34 loss to the [Las Vegas Raiders](22312.md).[^cite_note-30] He closed out the regular season with three consecutive games with over 100 rushing yards. He finished his rookie season with 228 carries for 1,050 rushing yards and nine rushing touchdowns to go along with 27 receptions for 165 receiving yards.[^cite_note-31] He joined [Curt Warner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curt_Warner) as the only players in franchise history to rush for at least 1,000 yards as a rookie.[^cite_note-32] On January 14, 2023, during the [2022–23 NFL playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_NFL_playoffs), Walker rushed the ball 15 times for 63 yards and a touchdown, during a 41–23 [Wild Card Round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_NFL_playoffs#NFC:_San_Francisco_49ers_41,_Seattle_Seahawks_23) loss to the 49ers.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] He was named to the [2022 Pro Football Writers of America All-Rookie Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Football_Writers_of_America_NFL_All-Rookie_Team).[^cite_note-35]

#### 2023

In Week 3, Walker totaled 156 scrimmage yards with a pair of rushing touchdowns in a 37–27 win over the [Carolina Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Panthers), earning [National Football Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_Conference) Offensive Player of the Week.[^cite_note-36] Walker finished the 2023 season with 219 carries for 905 rushing yards and eight rushing touchdowns to go with 29 receptions for 259 receiving yards and one receiving touchdown in 15 games.[^cite_note-37]

#### 2024

Walker dealt with injury throughout the 2024 campaign, making 11 total starts and compiling 573 rushing yards, seven rushing touchdowns, 299 receiving yards, and one receiving touchdown.[^cite_note-38] On December 26, 2024, Walker was placed on injured reserve with an ankle injury, ending his season.[^cite_note-39]

#### 2025: Super Bowl MVP



Walker holding the [Vince Lombardi Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vince_Lombardi_Trophy) in the [Super Bowl LX](63727016.md) parade

Walker started all 17 games for the Seahawks for the first time in his career, finishing with 1,027 rushing yards on 221 carries, averaging 4.6 yards per attempt. His rushing touchdown total was lower than in previous years, at five, as fellow running back [Zach Charbonnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Charbonnet) handled the majority of Seattle’s goal-line carries. Walker also tallied 31 receptions for 282 receiving yards.[^cite_note-40]

Walker's workload increased during the postseason after Charbonnet tore his ACL in the first half of the Seahawks' Divisional round game against the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_San_Francisco_49ers_season).[^cite_note-41] Walker finished the game with 116 yards and three touchdowns on 19 carries as Seattle won 41–6.[^cite_note-42] Against the [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Los_Angeles_Rams_season) in the NFC Championship game, Walker totaled 111 yards from scrimmage and a touchdown, helping the Seahawks secure a 31–27 win and a berth in [Super Bowl LX](63727016.md).[^cite_note-43] In Super Bowl LX, Walker amassed 27 carries for a season-high 135 yards and 26 receiving yards, leading the Seahawks to a 29–13 victory over the [New England Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_New_England_Patriots_season) and earning [Super Bowl MVP](291253.md) honors.[^cite_note-NickWagoner-44] Walker's 135 rush yards were the most in a Super Bowl since [Terrell Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrell_Davis) in [Super Bowl XXXII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXII), who was also the last running back named Super Bowl MVP before Walker.[^cite_note-NickWagoner-44]

### Kansas City Chiefs

On March 12, 2026, Walker signed a three-year, \$45 million contract with the [Kansas City Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs).[^cite_note-45] In his first game with the team, against the [Denver Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Broncos), he rushed for a career-high 173 yards, plus 18 receiving yards and two touchdowns, earning AFC Offensive Player of the Week honors.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]
