The **Transitional Presidential Council** (**TPC**; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Conseil présidentiel de transition* [\[kɔ̃sɛj pʁezidɑ̃sjɛl də tʁɑ̃zisjɔ̃\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); [Haitian Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Creole_language): *Konsèy Prezidansyèl Tranzisyon*) was a temporary body constituted by the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Haiti#Cabinet) on 12 April 2024 and sworn in at the [National Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Palace_\(Haiti\)) on 25 April to exercise the powers and duties of the [president of Haiti](516058.md) either until an elected president is inaugurated or until 7 February 2026, whichever came first. The council dissolved as scheduled after no presidential election was held.

## Background



[Jimmy "Barbecue" Chérizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_%22Barbecue%22_Ch%C3%A9rizier) (*pictured*) led demands for change of the government,

[^cite_note-demand1-4]

&#x20;but later threatened to attack any person who accepted appointment to the newly-formed TPC.

The gradual disintegration of Haitian state institutions during the [Haitian crisis](59942434.md) led to calls for acting prime minister [Ariel Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Henry) to step aside and surrender the *de facto* head of state functions.[^cite_note-economist-5] The demands were led by [Jimmy "Barbecue" Chérizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Ch%C3%A9rizier), a former policeman who heads a coalition of [gangs in Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_war_in_Haiti),[^cite_note-demand1-4] and [Guy Philippe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Philippe), a former senator and convict.[^cite_note-HL-6][^cite_note-politico-washington-7] Henry was seen as illegitimate since he took over after the 7 July 2021 [assassination](68178591.md) of president [Jovenel Moïse](48475064.md), and had repeatedly postponed the [general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Haitian_general_election) which he had promised to organize.[^cite_note-demand2-8] On 11 March 2024, Henry announced that he would resign and that a transitional council (whose membership would be determined in [Jamaica](15660.md) at an emergency [CARICOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CARICOM) meeting) would select an interim prime minister.[^cite_note-ahresign-9]

On 3 April 2024, the transitional presidential council set up at the [CARICOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CARICOM) meeting on 11 March had finalized its provisional list of representatives, who were appointed by the Council of Ministers on 16 April.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoniteur2024a-10]

After weeks of negotiation, a deal was sent to CARICOM on 7 April for a temporary government whose mandate will end on 7 February 2026.[^cite_note-f24-11] One of the council's tasks is to elect an acting prime minister, who cannot already be a member of the transitional council or the provisional government.[^cite_note-f24-11] The transitional council was officially created by a governmental decree published in *[Le Moniteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Moniteur_\(Haiti\))* on 12 April.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoniteur2024-3] The names of the nine members of the TPC were published on 16 April,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoniteur2024a-10] and they were sworn in at the [National Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Palace_\(Haiti\)) on 25 April amidst the sounds of automatic gunfire.[^cite_note-sworn_in-13] Prior to the announcement of Ariel Henry's resignation and the inauguration of the TPC, [Michel Patrick Boisvert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Patrick_Boisvert) was named acting prime minister by the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Haiti#Cabinet).[^cite_note-sworn_in-13]

## History

On 30 April 2024, [Edgard Leblanc Fils](76434292.md) was named chairman of the TPC, and [Fritz Bélizaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_B%C3%A9lizaire) was designated as the prime minister by a majority of four votes, although the latter choice was contested within the council.[^cite_note-14] As a result, the council members agreed to have the chairmanship being rotated every five months among Fils, Louis Gérald Gilles, Leslie Voltaire and Smith Augustin. In addition, it also set up a requirement of five votes instead of four to form a majority.[^cite_note-15] The council later started accepting submissions for the position of prime minister from 13–17 May.[^cite_note-16] In late May 2024, the council extended the tenure of acting prime minister Michel Patrick Boisvert for another month, and on 27 May six of the seven members with voting rights chose former UN special envoy and former prime minister [Garry Conille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Conille) as his successor, discarding the earlier vote that selected Bélizaire.[^cite_note-17] Conille and members of the council met several times after his swearing-in, agreeing on names of 13 ministers to be a part of his cabinet, with the ministers being named on 11 June 2024.[^cite_note-18] The council and the new government later agreed to replace the director-general of the [Haitian National Police](8017603.md).[^cite_note-19] On 7 October 2024, Voltaire was named to replace Fils as president of the council.[^cite_note-20]

On 2 October 2024, an official anti-corruption unit of the government recommended judicial review of three members of the Transitional Council—Smith Augustin, Gérald Gilles, and Emmanuel Vertilaire—for abuse of function, bribery, and passive corruption.[^cite_note-21]

The council removed Conille as acting prime minister in November 2024, replacing him with businessman [Alix Didier Fils-Aimé](78331060.md).[^cite_note-22] Several council members unsuccessfully attempted to remove Fils-Aimé in January 2026, drawing condemnation from the U.S.[^cite_note-23]

The council dissolved on 7 February 2026, with no successor. *[Radio France Internationale](3866201.md)* remarked that the prospects of a political transition were remote.[^cite_note-24] The council transferred all remaining powers to prime minister Fils-Aimé, who was left as the only politician with executive power in Haiti.[^cite_note-25]

## Composition

Under the terms of the decree of 12 April 2024, the Transitional Presidential Council consists of seven voting and two non-voting members.[^cite_note-can-26] The voting members include one representative from each of four political party coalitions (Accord du 30 août 2021, Accord du 21 décembre 2022, Collectif des Partis politiques, and Compromis Historique/RED/EDE), two political parties ([Fanmi Lavalas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanmi_Lavalas) and [Platfòm Pitit Desalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platf%C3%B2m_Pitit_Desalin)), and "the private sector". The non-voting members include one representative from [civil society](209443.md) and one representative from "the interfaith community".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoniteur2024-3] On 16 April, the Primature confirmed the appointment of the nine members nominated by the Transitional Presidential Council in Kingston.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoniteur2024a-10]

### Qualifications

Members of the TPC needed to be eligible for the presidency as established under Article 135 of the [Constitution of Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Haiti) and were disqualified from standing for the presidency in the planned election.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoniteur2024-3] Differences introduced in the decree include that individuals are ineligible for appointment to the council if they have been sanctioned by the [United Nations](31769.md), if they are under criminal indictment or have been found guilty of a crime in any jurisdiction, or if they oppose the introduction of the Multinational Security Support Mission in Haiti. Similarly, the government added in article 5 that all members must pursue the accelerated deployment of the international security force.[^cite_note-hl-27][^cite_note-28]

## Authority and mandate

Under the terms of the decree of 12 April, the Transitional Presidential Council exercised functions of the office of president until such time as a new president was elected and inaugurated.[^cite_note-can-26] Its mandate to act concluded on 7 February 2026.[^cite_note-can-26] The TPC's authority extends to the appointment of an acting prime minister and government and Henry has agreed to resign from the office of prime minister at such time as the TPC designates someone else to hold the office.[^cite_note-can-26]

## Reactions to establishment

### Domestic

The drafters of the agreement rejected the governmental decree on 13 April and called upon the members of the resigning government to publish the original agreement without modification.[^cite_note-nouv-35] Reactions within Haiti to the announcement of the TPC were mixed, with some Haitians questioning its constitutional legitimacy.[^cite_note-nyt-36] Shortly after the council's announcement, Jacky Lumarque, rector of [Université Quisqueya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A9_Quisqueya), described the council as not being a "Haitian-led" solution, calling it a "snake of seven heads".[^cite_note-cepr-37] He called for a judge of the Court of Cassation to be empowered as unitary acting president, instead.[^cite_note-cepr-37]

Jimmy "Barbecue" Chérizier said his forces would attack any person who accepted appointment to the council. Chérizier also invoked a traditional Haitian battle cry to describe the fate that would befall councilors: "Cut off their heads and burn down their houses".[^cite_note-nyt-36] Chérizier earlier objected to the lack of input his federation, the [Revolutionary Forces of the G9 Family and Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Forces_of_the_G9_Family_and_Allies), had had in governance transition talks. He also entered into an alliance with another federation of gangs, G-Pèp, to organize plans for armed resistance to any entry of foreign peacekeepers into the country as part of a future transitional arrangement.[^cite_note-ahresign-9][^cite_note-38]

Camille LeBlanc, former Haitian minister of justice, said he welcomed the creation of the council but doubted the ability of such a body to resolve Haiti's political impasse.[^cite_note-reuters-39]

### Foreign







UN secretary-general [António Guterres](471806.md), U.S. State Department spokesman [Matthew Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Miller_\(spokesperson\)), and President of Kenya [William Ruto](2577967.md) each welcomed the creation of the TPC on behalf of their respective nations and organizations.

In an official statement, [United Nations secretary-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretary-General) [António Guterres](471806.md) said he "welcomed" the creation of the Transitional Presidential Council and urged "all Haitian stakeholders to continue making progress in putting in place transitional governance arrangements".[^cite_note-un-40] The Caribbean Community welcomed the creation of the TPC saying it was a "Haitian owned formula for governance that will take the troubled country through elections to the restoration of the lapsed state institutions and constitutional government".[^cite_note-41]

The [United States Department of State](31975.md) stated the U.S. "welcomes" the creation of the TPC and "commends Haitian leaders for making tough compromises to move toward democratic governance via free and fair elections".[^cite_note-42] [President of Kenya](392265.md) [William Ruto](2577967.md) welcomed the creation of the TPC, writing on social media that "Kenya expresses confidence that the new political leadership will lay a strong foundation for resolution of the crisis in Haiti, restoration of security, afford the Haitian people a political transition and usher in sustainable peace and development".[^cite_note-43]

In a press release issued 14 April, Louise Mushikiwabo of *l'[Organisation internationale de la Francophonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_internationale_de_la_Francophonie)* "commended" the creation of the Transitional Presidential Council.[^cite_note-44] On 15 April, the [European Union](9317.md), through the [European External Action Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_External_Action_Service), said it was "crucial that the TPC is formally appointed without further delay by the outgoing government of Prime Minister Ariel Henry".[^cite_note-45]
