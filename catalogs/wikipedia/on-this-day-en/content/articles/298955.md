**Cotabato City**, officially the **City of Cotabato**,[^cite_note-6] is an [independent component city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Legal_classification) in the [Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](37260549.md), Philippines. According to the 2024 census, it has a population of 383,383 people,[^cite_note-PSA20–12-7] making it the most populated city under the independent component city status.

Cotabato City was formerly a part and the regional center of [Region XII](298888.md), but due to the ratification of the [Bangsamoro Organic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Organic_Law), it is now part of Bangsamoro and serves as the regional center. Being an independent component city, it is not subject to regulation from the Provincial Government of [Maguindanao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_del_Norte) where it is geographically located. The [Philippine Statistics Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Statistics_Authority) also lists Cotabato City as statistically independent.[^cite_note-info_page-8] It was the capital of the [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao).

## History



Territory of the Sultanate of Maguindanao in 1521 and Cotabato City serving as its capital.

Prior to the arrival of Hindus and Muslims, the city was a vast swamp and rainforest landscape where numerous ethno-linguistic groups lived. Maguindanao vernacular architecture developed during this era, which included the architectural techniques of at least 10 ethno-linguistic groups. Later on, Hindu traders arrived and the people of the area embraced the practice of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism). The set of moral standards and culture of present-day people of Maguindanao are seen due to this Hindu influence.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

### Sultanate of Maguindanao



Datu Piang (4th from left) with American officers circa 1899. He was the first governor of the [Empire Province of Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_\(historical_province\)); Cotabato City was once the capital of the province from 1920 to 1967.

By 1515, after a successful Muslim establishment in Sulu, Muslim merchants went to Maguindanao and converted many Maguindanaoans to [Islam](6037917.md). Those that did not accept the arrival of the Muslims went into higher ground or the interior of the island and became the [Teduray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teduray), as well as other [lumad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad) groups. During the same year, the [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao) was formally established, with Tubok as its initial capital. However, the sultan's seat would eventually be transferred in 1711 at around Tamontaka, a barangay within modern-day Cotabato. In 1861, Spain would eventually establish a garrison in Maguindanao and establish Kuta Watu, which was eventually renamed as Cotabato, as its new capital.[^cite_note-\:7-11]

### Spanish and American occupation



Aerial view of Cotabato, circa pre-1944

The [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) had defeated the [Sulu Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sultanate) in war and forced it to sign an unfavorable peace treaty in 1851.[^cite_note-12] With the [Moro Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Gulf) and [Illana Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illana_Bay) now open to Spanish traffic as a result of their victory over the Sulu Moros, this paved the way for the first steps for the conquest of the [Maguindanao Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_Sultanate) several years later. To that end, Spanish [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) missionaries have already made their way as early as 1859 at [Polloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parang,_Maguindanao_del_Norte) where they earned several hundred converts, but the place was deemed unsuitable on account of its less than optimal ecology. And so the Spanish district governor of Mindanao based in [Zamboanga](25167165.md) requested Datu Amirul, the father of present Sultan of Maguindanao Muhammad Makakua, for them to establish a military base at the stone fortress at Kota Wato in what is now Barangay Tamontaka, and Datu Amirul gave the consent. The Spanish then established themselves at the said fort, raising the Spanish flag there and renamed it Cotabato, the Hispanized form of its name, on April 30, 1861. The Spanish and the Moro chiefs and locals then had a conference later the same day which stipulated the Maguindanaoan locals of Cotabato are now subjects of the Spanish Crown but their Islamic beliefs would be respected. Thus, on April 30, 1861, in the flood plains of Tamontaka, the town of Cotabato was officially born.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

Under the reign of Sultan Muhammad Makakua, who while being the nominal sultan of Maguindanao was at this point under complete Spanish [vassalage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassalage), roads and wharves were built in Cotabato, specifically where the [Tamontaka River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao) was situated. Forests were felled and cleared by the new Catholic [Tiruray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teduray_people) converts of Tamontaka to make way for the expansion of the newly established town.[^cite_note-15] In 1871, the capital of the Spanish military district of Mindanao was moved from Zamboanga to Cotabato, only to be moved back previously the next year when a destructive earthquake ravaged the town.[^cite_note-16]

Following the Spanish evacuation in Jan. 1899, Datu Piang led the Moro's in a massacre of the remaining Christian community, enslaving those they did not kill.[^cite_note-Foreman-17]<sup>: 529–530 </sup> Americans arrived in Mindanao in 1900 after the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War) ended in 1898. Cotabato town was part of [Moro Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Province) and of [Department of Mindanao and Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Mindanao_and_Sulu) from 1903 to 1920, when the [Empire Province of Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_\(historical_province\)), referred to as "Moroland" by the Americans, was founded with the town as the capital, with [Datu Piang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu_Piang) as its first governor.[^cite_note-info_page-8]

### Cotabato

Several towns were carved off from Cotabato town since the year 1913, with [Pikit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pikit,_Cotabato) being the first one founded by Cebuano Christian colonists. Dulawan (now [Datu Piang, Maguindanao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu_Piang,_Maguindanao_del_Sur)) and [Midsayap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midsayap,_Cotabato) were incorporated as regular municipalities in 1936. In 1942, at the beginning of the [Pacific Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War) of [World War II](32927.md), the Japanese Imperial forces entered what is now the Maguindanao del Norte and Maguindanao del Sur provinces. In 1945, Maguindanao was liberated by allied Philippine Commonwealth troops and Muslim Maguindanaoan guerrilla units after defeating the Japanese Imperial forces in the [Battle of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Maguindanao) during the Second World War.[^cite_note-info_page-8] On August 18, 1947, just two years after the Second World War and a year after the official inauguration of Philippine independence, the number of towns in the gigantic Cotabato province were multiplied by Executive Order No. 82 signed by President [Manuel Roxas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Roxas), namely: [Kidapawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidapawan), [Pagalungan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagalungan), [Buayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Santos), [Marbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koronadal), [Parang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parang,_Maguindanao_del_Norte), [Nuling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Kudarat,_Maguindanao_del_Norte), [Dinaig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu_Odin_Sinsuat), [Salaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebak,_Sultan_Kudarat), [Buluan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buluan), [Kiamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiamba,_Sarangani), and [Cabacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabacan,_Cotabato), a total of eleven (11) towns added to the previous four towns; the newly founded towns of [Kabuntalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabuntalan), Pikit (conversion as regular municipality), and [Glan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glan,_Sarangani) added up on September 30, 1949. More and more newly created towns added up in the province's number of towns as the province entered the second half of the 20th century.[^cite_note-info_page-8]

### Cityhood

Main article: [Cities of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines)



Former flag of Cotabato City

The city was chartered when President [Carlos P. Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_P._Garcia) signed into law the *Republic Act No. 2364* on June 20, 1959. Authored then by Senator [Salipada K. Pendatun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salipada_K._Pendatun).[^cite_note-18] Under the said charter, the city was politically sibdivided into five (5) Barangays namely Poblacion, Rosary Heights, Bagua, Kalanganan, and Tamontaka. In 1989, the existing Barangays were split up which resulted into 32 more Barangays, presently the City has 37 Barangays.

The city used to be part of the [original Province of Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_\(historical_province\)) and was its capital from 1920 until 1967, a year after the separation of [South Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Cotabato); since then the city was the administrative center of the ARMM when [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao) was carved out in 1973.

However, the city broke off administratively from Maguindanao as it rejoined [Soccsksargen](298888.md) in the 1990s. Now many sources consider the city as part of the present [Cotabato province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotabato_province), although geographically it is still considered part of Maguindanao del Norte.[^cite_note-info_page-8]

### Inclusion in the Bangsamoro region

The city has traditionally resisted efforts for its inclusion to the [Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Region_in_Muslim_Mindanao) despite serving as the government center of the region. Despite this, the city's residents voted for their locality's inclusion in the new [Bangsamoro Autonomous Region](37260549.md) by voting to ratify the [Bangsamoro Organic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Organic_Law) in the January 21, [2019 plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Bangsamoro_Autonomous_Region_creation_plebiscite).[^cite_note-19] The city became *de jure* part of Bangsamoro following the plebiscite, as well as the government center of the new region. It became *official* part of the region after its formal turnover to the Bangsamoro regional government on December 15, 2020.[^cite_note-20]

## Geography

Cotabato is approximately 698.9 nautical miles (1,294.4 kilometres) from [Manila](184334.md), the country's capital, and is bounded by the [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_municipality) of [Sultan Kudarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Kudarat,_Maguindanao_del_Norte) to the north—with [Rio Grande de Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao)/[Pulangi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulangi_River) separating the two—[Kabuntalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabuntalan) to the east, and [Datu Odin Sinsuat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu_Odin_Sinsuat) to the south. The city faces [Illana Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illana_Bay), part of the [Moro Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Gulf), to the west.

Cotabato City has a total land area of 176.0 square kilometres (68.0 sq mi), located at the mouth of the [Rio Grande de Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_de_Mindanao) and [Pulangi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulangi_River).[^cite_note-21]

### Urban Areas



[Pedro Colina Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Colina_Hill)

Cotabato City has 27 urban barangays that are grouped into two major urban areas, the Down Town Area and the Upper Town Area.

**Down Town Area** The Downtown Area encompasses the barangays of Poblacion and Bagua, with a population exceeding 150,000. Situated below sea level, it faces constant flooding risks. Serving as Cotabato City's central hub for banking, trading, and commerce, it hosts over 20 banks and major wet markets like Mega Market and City Arcade. Additionally, prominent malls and supermarkets such as South Seas Mall, Puregold Main Branch, Sugni Super Store, and Superama (with 3 branches) are situated here, along with KCC Mall of Cotabato. Notable educational institutions include Notre Dame University, Cotabato City Central Pilot School (the largest elementary school), STI Cotabato, and Notre Dame of Cotabato.

**Upper Town Area** The Upper Town Area, situated in the elevated regions of Cotabato City, encompasses all barangays of Rosary Heights and is home to the Peoples Palace (City Hall). Serving as the service center of Cotabato City, it houses Regional Government Agencies from both the Bangsamoro Region and Region 12. Additionally, it is home to Cotabato Regional and Medical Center, along with other medical facilities. Educational institutions in this area include Cotabato State University, Notre Dame-RVM, and AMA Computer College, as well as the now-closed University of Mindanao. Major shopping destinations include CityMall, Mall of Alnor, and Fiesta Mall.

Two other emerging areas include Barangays of Kalanganan, where the Seaport and Grand Mosque are situated. The primary industry here revolves around aquaculture, with proposed developments for a Public Market and Public Terminal. Additionally, construction of a coastal road is underway in this area. Another burgeoning area is the Barangays of Tamontaka, slated for the establishment of an International Airport. Tamontaka also serves as the eastern diversion road of Cotabato City.

### Topography

The city is situated in the lowest portion of Maguindanao del Norte. The City of Cotabato with its 37 barangays spans an area with marked landscapes of flat, level to nearly level, very gently sloping to gently undulations to moderately sloping or rolling. It is basically a delta formed by two big rivers, the Tamontaka River and the Rio Grande de Mindanao. Basically 70% of its total land area is below sea level. There are only 2 existing elevated areas in the city, the [PC Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PC_Hill) and the Timako Hill with an altitude of 90 and 150 feet, respectively. At the foot of PC Hill is [Tantawan Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantawan_Park) and also hosts the [Kutang Bato Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutawato_Caves).

Concentration of settlements and other urban uses are in the central portion while the southwestern and southeastern portion have mixed uses of agricultural land settlements. The city is criss-crossed by meandering and braided creeks and rivers like the Matampay, Parang, Timako, Esteros and Miwaruy.

These water bodies serve as sources of both agricultural, industrial and domestic water requirements of some rural barangays. These rivers also serve as the natural drainage flow of the city's wastes.

### Climate

Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) system, Cotabato City features a [tropical rainforest climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate) (*Af*) with consistently hot, humid and wet weather year-round. There is a drier season from January to February, but unlike western Luzon rainfall is still over 80 millimetres or 3 inches in every month.

The majority of the inhabitants of Cotabato City are [Maguindanaons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_people), comprising about 50% of the city's population. There are sizable ethnic populations of [Cebuanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_people) (14%), [Tagalogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_people) (9.7%), [Iranuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranun_people) (7%), [Hiligaynons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_people) (5.6%), [Bisaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayans) (2.7%) and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) (2%). The remainder of the population belongs to other ethnicities (e.g. [Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taus%C5%ABg_people), [Teduray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiruray_people), [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people), [Maranao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao_people) and [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Filipino)).[^cite_note-auto-31]

### Language

[Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_language) is widely understood and spoken in Cotabato City, being the native language of the [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_people) and some other ethnicities in the city could also speak and understand it. The related [Danao languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danao_languages) of [Iranun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranun_language) and [Maranao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao_language) are spoken by residents of the corresponding ethnicities. Other [Moro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people) languages such as [Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tausug_language), [Sama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama_language) and [Yakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakan_language) are also spoken in the city.

The main [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca) is [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language), making Cotabato City the "Tagalog-speaking city of Mindanao". It is the only place outside Luzon that is predominantly Tagalog-speaking. Soccsksargen Tagalog, a Mindanao-based Tagalog dialect, is also spoken in the city.

[Chavacano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavacano), [Ilonggo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilonggo_language), [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language) and [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language) are spoken as well by the various Christian minorities of the city. Chavacano has its own dialect in the city called Cotabateño, but it was replaced by Tagalog as the lingua franca, which reduced the number of native Chavacano speakers.

[Classical Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Arabic_language) is often heard at [mosques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosques) and [madrasas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasa) as the [sacred language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_language) of [Islam](6037917.md).

### Religion



CRMC Mosque, displaying traditional Maguindanao architecture

As reported by Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) on 2015, 76% of the people of Cotabato City are adherents of [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) [Islam](6037917.md).[^cite_note-32] The followers of Islam are mainly [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_people), [Iranun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranun_people), [Maranao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao_people), and [Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tausug_people). The remaining proportion belong to non - Islamic belief such as [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism), Buddhism, and other sects.

Cotabato City also hosts the largest mosque in the Philippines, the [Sultan Haji Hassanal Bolkiah Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Haji_Hassanal_Bolkiah_Masjid) which can accommodate approximately 15,000 worshippers.[^cite_note-33] Cotabato City is also the seat of the [Archdiocese of Cotabato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Cotabato) which serves its Christian population.[^cite_note-34] The city also hosts the historic [Church of the Immaculate Concepcion Tamontaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamontaka_Church).

Festivals religious of origin are also held in the city annually, such as the Shariff Kabunsuan Festival which is dedicated to [Sharif Kabungsuwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_Kabungsuwan), a Muslim missionary who introduced Islam in the area.[^cite_note-35] The [Feast of the Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_the_Immaculate_Conception) is also observed, since the [Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Conception) is regarded as the patron saint of the city by its Catholic population.

Cotabato City serves as the commercial, industrial, financial, educational, healthcare, and regional center of the Bangsamoro Region and Central Mindanao. Situated strategically at the heart of Mindanao, the city boasts road links and public transport connectivity to all major cities on the island, including Davao, Cagayan de Oro, Zamboanga City, Dipolog, and General Santos.

In 2019, the city government reported approximately 1.2 billion pesos in new investments, resulting in the establishment of 1,368 new businesses. This robust economic growth contributed to the city's total annual income of 1.044 billion pesos for the same year. Over the past five years, Cotabato City has experienced an average annual income growth rate of 10.51 percent. Additionally, in 2019, Cotabato City was recognized as the second most competitive component city in Mindanao and the most competitive city in Region 12 for three consecutive years based on the annual ranking of the Cities Municipalities Competitiveness Index (CMCI).

The Gross Domestic Product (GDP) of Cotabato City in 2022 reached 72,091,571,637.36 pesos or 221,766.35 per Capita GDP comparable to [Tunisia](30188.md). Reports also indicate that Cotabato City ranks as the third city in Mindanao in terms of highest bank deposits, trailing behind Davao City and Cagayan De Oro City.

### Commercial Retail and Banking Center



Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas - Cotabato City Branch 2023

Cotabato City ranks as the third highest in bank deposits in Mindanao, totaling Php 88.66 billion as of June 30, 2021,[^cite_note-45] attributable to robust economic activity. Recognizing the city's significance, the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas established its Central Mindanao branch within Cotabato City.

The city boasts a mix of local and national shopping centers. Local establishments such as Superama, Sugni, Mall of Alnor, and Southseas Mall compete with national retailers like [CityMall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CityMall), [Puregold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puregold), Robinsons Supermarket and Department Store, SM Savemore, as well as Centro Department Store, Mi Department Store, and Fiesta Shopping Center, which are present in selected locations nationwide. Cotabato City stands out as one of the fastest-growing economies in the Soccsksargen region.[^cite_note-46]

[KCC Mall of Cotabato City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KCC_Mall_of_Cotabato_City) had started its full blown construction on July 1, 2020. It is located along Quezon Avenue corner Sinsuat Avenue, & the project is estimated to cost 11 billion pesos, the mall is four storey tall with a total lot area of 11 hectares; on the second phase of construction is the completion of eleven storey hotel with a convention center, probably upon completion this could be the tallest building in [Region XII](298888.md).[^cite_note-47] NCCC Malls, a Davao-based mall company have also confirmed their interest to build a mall within the city.[^cite_note-48] KCC Mall Of Cotabato will set to open by April 2, 2025.

### Agro-Industrial



City "Double A" Standard Halal Slaughterhouse

Cotabato City has a more or less 1,700 hectares of fishponds which has an annual production of 500,000 kg of [mangrove crabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scylla_serrata), [prawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prawn) and [milkfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milkfish).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

Aiming to be the [halal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halal_food) hub of the Philippines, the City Government and Malaysian Businessman built a Class AA halal slaughter house in Baranggay Kalangan II in the city primary serving the entire Central Mindanao, the Halal slaughter house generates a gross income of 4,642,135.00 pesos in 2018.[^cite_note-52]

The city has different factories for cooking oil, coffee, corn starch, processed food and furniture operating within the city.

### Festivals



2018 Kuyog street dancing competition

- **Feast of the Immaculate Conception** - a celebration of Our Lady of the Immaculate Conception, the patron saint of the city. The festival starts from December 1 to 8. The activities are mostly participated by different Catholic schools.

- **Shariff Kabunsuan Festival** – this festival is celebrated every December 15–19, which is celebrated in honor of Shariff Mohammad Kabunsuan, an Arab missionary from Johore who planted the seed of Islam in Central Mindanao. On this event, certain presentations are performed such as dances. There are also many recreational activities and sports. Highlights in this festival are Dance Parade, Banca Race and the re-enactment of Shariff's arrival.

- **Araw ng Kutabato** (Cotabato Day) – celebrated every June 12–25, this is the biggest celebration in all of the city's festivals. This festival is held to commemorate the city's charter day.

- **Festival of Lights** - A Christmas celebration organized by the Notre Dame schools in Cotabato City, celebrated every December of the year.

- **Ramadhan** - Being a Muslim majority, the city is one in celebrating the holy month of Ramadhan, both the city local government unit and the Bangsamoro Region are establishing their own Ramadhan Fair and different activities.

- **Layagan Festival** - is a Crabs Festival held and organized in the Barangay of Mother Kalanganan, the event is conducted every December, Barangay Kalanganan produces hundred of tons of crabs annually making it as the crabs capital of Soccsksargen.

## Education



Cariño Building, Notre Dame University

Cotabato City is home to 18 private colleges and two universities. According to a report from the Department of Education, the city had a total of 14,228 enrollees in higher education (colleges and universities) during the 2017–2018 school year. Among the higher education institutions in Central Mindanao, Notre Dame University (NDU) stands out. NDU is the first university in the Notre Dame system in Asia and was recognized as the best accountancy school in Mindanao in 2018. It also ranked as the 9th top-performing school in the May 2023 Nursing Board Exam, with a passing rate of 96.64%, as 115 out of 119 examinees successfully passed.

Additionally, the Cotabato City State Polytechnic College was converted into Cotabato State University by Republic Act 10585, becoming the second university in the city, with the change taking effect in April 2021.
