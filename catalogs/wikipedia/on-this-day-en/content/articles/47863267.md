**Central Visayas** ([Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language): *Tunga-tungang Kabisay-an*; [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Gitnang Kabisayaan*; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Gitnang Visayas*), designated as **Region VII**, is an [administrative region](216786.md) in the Philippines. With only two [provinces](165100.md): [Cebu](160278.md) and [Bohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol), as well as three [highly urbanized cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Independent_cities): [Cebu City](285868.md), [Lapu-Lapu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapu-Lapu_City), and [Mandaue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaue), it has the fewest provinces of any region in the country. Despite this, it is the most populous region in the [Visayas](72418.md), with a population of 6,545,603.

The regional center, as well as its largest city, is [Cebu City](285868.md). The [Cebuano language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language) is the region's [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca). The region is also dominated by native speakers of three [Visayan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisayan_languages): [Bantayanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantayanon_language), [Boholano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boholano_dialect), and [Porohanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porohanon_language).

In 2015, Central Visayas was redefined when the province of [Negros Oriental](261980.md) was transferred to the newly formed [Negros Island Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Island_Region). However, the Negros Island Region was dissolved in 2017, returning Negros Oriental to Central Visayas. After seven years, Negros Oriental was transferred again, along with the island province of [Siquijor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siquijor), when the Negros Island Region was re-established in 2024.

## Etymology

The name of the region, Central Visayas, was mostly chosen by American colonists to denote the centrality of the islands within the bigger Visayas area.

There have been proposals to rename the current Central Visayas region, which is dominated by the [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_people) (Sugbuanon) ethnic group, to *Sugbu*, the former name of the region prior to [Spanish colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) in the 16th century. The name refers to the former kingdom of the region, the [Rajahnate of Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajahnate_of_Cebu), or *Sugbu* in Cebuano.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

## History

Regions first came into existence on September 24, 1972, when the provinces of the Philippines were organized into 11 regions by *Presidential Decree No. 1* as part of the *Integrated Reorganization Plan* by President [Ferdinand Marcos Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos_Sr.) The provinces of [Cebu](160278.md), [Bohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol), and [Negros Oriental](261980.md) (including its then-subprovince of Siquijor) were grouped together to form the Central Visayas region.

By virtue of *Executive Order No. 183, s. 2015*, issued on May 29, 2015, by President [Benigno Aquino III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benigno_Aquino_III), moved Negros Oriental to the newly formed [Negros Island Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Island_Region).[^cite_note-7]

On August 9, 2017, President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) dissolved the Negros Island Region, revoking *Executive Order No. 183, s. 2015* through the signing of *Executive Order No. 38*.[^cite_note-8] This returned Negros Oriental to Central Visayas.

In 2024, Negros Oriental was again moved to the re-established Negros Island Region. Siquijor was also moved to the NIR through Republic Act No. 12000 signed by President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md).[^cite_note-9]

## Geography



Political map of Central Visayas (since June 2024)



Former map of Central Visayas prior to the revival of Negros Island Region in June 2024

See also: [Cebu § Geography](160278.md#Geography), and [Bohol § Geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol#Geography)

Central Visayas consists of the island provinces of Cebu and Bohol, as well as the three independent cities of [Cebu City](285868.md), [Lapu-Lapu City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapu-Lapu_City), and [Mandaue City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaue_City). The region also includes the straits of [Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_Strait) and parts of the [Tañon Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta%C3%B1on_Strait). Its land area is 10,144.52 km<sup>2</sup> (3,916.82 sq mi), 3.3% of the country's total land area.

Central Visayas is bounded on the north by the [Visayan Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayan_Sea), west by the provinces of [Negros Occidental](266813.md) and [Negros Oriental](261980.md) in [Negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_\(Philippines\)) island, south by the [Bohol Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol_Sea) and the province of [Siquijor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siquijor), and east by the [Camotes Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camotes_Sea) and the island of [Leyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyte_\(island\)) in [Eastern Visayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Visayas).

The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 510/km<sup>2</sup> (1,300/sq mi). The 2015 census showed an average annual population growth rate of 1.76% from 2010 to 2015, slightly higher than the national average of 1.72%.[^cite_note-PSA15–07-13]

### Languages

The native languages of Central Visayas are:

- [Bantayanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantayanon_language), spoken in [Bantayan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantayan_Island) of Cebu.

- [Boholano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boholano_dialect), a Cebuano dialect spoken in Bohol.

- [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language), spoken in Cebu and Bohol. It is the regional lingua franca.

- [Porohanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porohanon_language), spoken in [Camotes Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camotes_Islands) of Cebu.

## Transportation

### Ports



Fastcraft terminal for ferrying passengers from Cebu to Negros Island.

The [Port of Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Cebu) is the region's main gateway by sea. Other seaports in the region include the Liloan Port in [Santander, Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander,_Cebu) and the Port of [Tagbilaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagbilaran) in Bohol. Inter-island shipping is served by numerous shipping lines, including [ro-ro](1457484.md) companies [Montenegro Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegro_Lines) and [Lite Ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lite_Shipping_Corporation) and [fastcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fastcraft) companies [OceanJet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_Fast_Ferries) and [Supercat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercat_Fast_Ferry_Corporation).

### Airports



[Mactan–Cebu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mactan%E2%80%93Cebu_International_Airport).

[Mactan–Cebu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mactan%E2%80%93Cebu_International_Airport), located on [Mactan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mactan) in [Metro Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Cebu), is the country's second-busiest airport and a gateway to the region by air. It is the secondary hub of [Cebu Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_Pacific) and [Philippine Airlines](296889.md) (and their subsidiaries), with flights to locations throughout the country. It also serves international flights to other Asian and intercontinental destinations.[^cite_note-28]

[Bohol–Panglao International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol%E2%80%93Panglao_International_Airport) serves Bohol and is the region's newest airport.

## Mass media

Cebu City is the main media hub for the region. Large media networks – [ABS-CBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABS-CBN), [GMA Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMA_Network), [TV5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV5_\(Philippine_TV_channel\)), [People's Television Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Television_Network), [CNN Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN_Philippines), and [IBC 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_Broadcasting_Corporation) – maintain their respective local stations and branches for viewership, commercial and news coverage purposes. Most of these stations broadcast local news and public affairs as well as entertainment and dramas to cater to the local viewers.

Aside from the 24 national daily newspapers available, Cebu City also has 20 local newspapers. Among the widely read are *[SunStar Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SunStar_Cebu)*, *Cebu Daily News*, and *[The Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Freeman_\(newspaper\))*.
