# Bids for the 2036 Summer Olympics

| Olympic official emblem |

The **2036 Summer Olympics**, officially known as the **Games of the XXXVI Olympiad**, is a future international [multi-sport event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-sport_event). This edition celebrates the [centennial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centennial) of the [Olympic torch relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Olympic_torch_relays), a tradition that began in [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics_torch_relay). The host city will be selected in 2029.[^cite_note-hostFFTF-1]

## Bidding process

The new IOC bidding process was approved at the [134th IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/134th_IOC_Session) on 24 June 2019 in [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne), Switzerland. The key proposals, driven by the relevant recommendations from Olympic Agenda 2020, are:[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

- Establish a permanent, ongoing dialogue to explore and create interest among cities/regions/countries and National Olympic Committees for any Olympic event

- Create two Future Host Commissions (Summer and Winter Games) to oversee interest in future Olympic events and report to the IOC executive board

- Give the IOC Session more influence by having non-executive board members form part of the Future Host Commissions.

The IOC also modified the [Olympic Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Charter) to increase its flexibility by removing the date of election from 7 years before the games and changing the host as a city from a single city/region/country to multiple cities, regions, or countries.

### Reforms

The change in the bidding process was criticised by members of the German bid as "incomprehensible" and hard to surpass "in terms of non-transparency".[^cite_note-4] Following the election of [Kirsty Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsty_Coventry) as IOC president in 2025, she announced a review of issues facing the Olympics, including the new bidding process. Details on host city election reform were partly announced at the 145th IOC Session in 2026, which included more transparency and a proposed "short-list stage".[^cite_note-5] The short-list stage, referred to as "strategic dialogue", will begin in March 2027, with targeted dialogue to start in late 2028.[^cite_note-hostFFTF-1]

### Dialogue stages

According to Future Host Commission terms of reference with rules of conduct, the new IOC bidding system is divided into 3 dialogue stages are:[^cite_note-8]

- Continuous Dialogue: Non-committal discussions between the IOC and Interested Parties (City/Region/Country/NOC interested in hosting) concerning hosting future Olympic events.

- Strategic Dialogue: In-depth evaluation of detailed plans by shortlisted, interested parties in terms of alignment with IOC's strategic direction.

  [^cite_note-9]

  [^cite_note-10]

- Targeted Dialogue: Targeted discussions with one or more Interested Parties (called Preferred Host(s)), as instructed by the IOC Executive Board.

## Broadcasting rights

On 13 March 2025, [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast)/[NBCUniversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBCUniversal) announced that it had renewed its [long-running rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Olympic_broadcasts) to the Olympic Games in the United States through 2036, under an agreement valued at US\$3 billion. Aside from the extension, Comcast will be partnering with the IOC on providing the [Olympic Broadcasting Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Broadcasting_Services) with support for in-venue distribution as well as collaboration with the IOC on digital advertising opportunities.[^cite_note-202][^cite_note-203]
