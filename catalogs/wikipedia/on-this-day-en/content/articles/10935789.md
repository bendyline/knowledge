The **wildlife of [Cambodia](334751.md)** is very diverse with at least 162 [mammal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal) species,[^cite_note-\:0-1] 600 [bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird) species,[^cite_note-\:0-1] 176 [reptile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptile) species (including 89 subspecies),[^cite_note-2] 900 freshwater [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish) species,[^cite_note-\:0-1] 670 [invertebrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertebrate) species, and more than 3000 plant species.[^cite_note-\:0-1] A single protected area, [Keo Seima Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keo_Seima_Wildlife_Sanctuary), is known to support more than 950 total species, including 75 species that are listed as globally threatened on the [IUCN Red List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUCN_Red_List).[^cite_note-3] An unknown amount of species remains to be described by science, especially the [insect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect) group of butterflies and moths, collectively known as [lepidopterans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepidopterans).[^cite_note-REPORT4-4]

Many species in Cambodia, including several [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic) ones, are recognized by the [IUCN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUCN) or [World Conservation Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Conservation_Union) as threatened, endangered, or critically endangered due to [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) and [habitat destruction](1707053.md), [poaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaching), the illegal [wildlife trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_trade), and farming, fishing, and forestry concessions. Intensive poaching may have already driven Cambodia's national animal, the [kouprey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouprey), to extinction, wild tigers to [extirpation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extirpation), and [Eld's deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eld's_deer), wild water buffaloes and hog deer are at critically low numbers. The [giant ibis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_ibis), Cambodia's national bird, is also critically endangered.[^cite_note-5]

Wildlife in Cambodia includes [dholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhole), elephants, deer (sambar, Eld's deer, hog deer and [muntjac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muntjac)), wild oxen ([banteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banteng) and [gaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaur)), panthers, bears, and [tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger). Cormorants, cranes, ibises, parrots, green peafowl, pheasants, and wild ducks are also found, and species of venomous snakes and constrictors are numerous. [Deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation), mining activities, and unregulated hunting, have diminished the country's wildlife diversity rapidly.

Cambodia also has many endangered species, including the Asian elephant, [Siamese crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siamese_crocodile), wild water buffalo, and the Germain's silver langur.

Much work is being done in this area to help conserve and protect Cambodia's unique wildlife. Wildlife [conservation organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_organizations) operating in Cambodia include [Conservation International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_International), [World Wildlife Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wildlife_Fund), the [Wildlife Conservation Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_Conservation_Society), [Fauna and Flora International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_and_Flora_International), [BirdLife International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BirdLife_International), [Wildlife Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_Alliance), and many others. On 20 December 2016, 163 new animal species were reported in Southeast Asia including one known as the [Klingon newt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tylototriton_anguliceps) for its resemblance to a [Klingon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klingon) from *[Star Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Trek)*.[^cite_note-6]

### Tonlé Sap Lake

The [Tonlé Sap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonl%C3%A9_Sap) Great Lake is the largest natural freshwater lake in Southeast Asia and a key component of Cambodia's biodiversity.[^cite_note-\:1-7] Linked to the Mekong River by the 120-kilometre Tonlé Sap River, the lake expands dramatically during the annual monsoon flood pulse from around 2,500 km² in the dry season to as much as 10,000–16,000 km² in the wet season, generating exceptionally productive fish habitat.[^cite_note-\:1-7] The lake has recorded at least 149 fish species and hosts globally significant colonies of endangered waterbirds.[^cite_note-\:1-7] Prek Toal on the lake's northwestern shore is the only remaining breeding site in Southeast Asia for two globally threatened species, the Spot-billed Pelican and the Milky Stork.[^cite_note-\:1-7] The Tonlé Sap floodplain also supports more than half of the global population of the critically endangered Bengal Florican.[^cite_note-\:1-7]

Cambodia supports more than 3,000 identified plant species, many of which are endemic to unique local ecosystems such as the Tonlé Sap floodplain, forests of the Cardamom and Dâmrei Mountains, and elevated plains. These ecosystems provide diverse habitats that sustain a variety of plant species, contributing to the country's rich biodiversity.

In Cambodia [forest cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_cover) is around 46% of the total land area, equivalent to 8,068,370 hectares (ha) of forest in 2020, down from 11,004,790 hectares (ha) in 1990. In 2020, naturally regenerating forest covered 7,464,400 hectares (ha) and planted forest covered 603,970 hectares (ha). Of the naturally regenerating forest 4% was reported to be [primary forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_forest) (consisting of native tree species with no clearly visible indications of human activity). For the year 2015, 100% of the forest area was reported to be under [public ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_ownership).[^cite_note-16]

### National flower

- [Rumdul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphaerocoryne_lefevrei): Cambodia's national flower is the rumdul (*Mitrella mesnyi*). This flower is valued for its fragrant yellow-white blooms and is commonly found in rural areas across the country. It symbolizes elegance and is often used in traditional ceremonies and festivals.

### Rare and endangered species

Several plant species in Cambodia are considered rare and/or endangered, highlighting the importance of conservation efforts:

- *[Nepenthes bokorensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepenthes_bokorensis)*: One of two rare pitcher plants endemic to Cambodia, found growing in the Dâmrei Mountains. This species is notable for its insect-trapping mechanism and distinctive pitcher shape.

- *[Aquilaria crassna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquilaria_crassna)* (*chankreussna*): A critically endangered tree species valued for its aromatic resin, known as agarwood, used in perfume and traditional medicine. Overharvesting for its valuable resin has significantly reduced its population in the wild.

- *[Cinnamomum cambodianum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamomum_cambodianum)*: A non-scented species of cinnamon tree endemic to Cambodian forests, primarily in the Cardamom and Dâmrei Mountains. It is also possibly present in the border region between Cambodia and Thailand. This tree species faces threats from illegal logging and habitat destruction.

### Conservation efforts

Efforts to preserve Cambodia's flora include the establishment of protected areas and national parks, such as [Preah Monivong National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preah_Monivong_National_Park) and the Cardamom Mountains Protected Forests. Organizations and government initiatives are working towards sustainable management and conservation practices to protect these valuable plant species and their habitats.

## Threats

Sand mining in waterways, overfishing, illicit fishing methods, and the illegal wildlife trafficking are the three main threats to biodiversity in Cambodia.[^cite_note-17] Deforestation continues to be a severe and growing threat to Cambodia's wildlife habitats.[^cite_note-\:2-18] In 2024, Cambodia lost approximately 93,000 hectares of tree cover an area comparable in scale to a major urban centre with around 56% of that loss occurring inside the country's protected area network, according to an analysis of satellite data from the Global Land Analysis and Discovery laboratory at the University of Maryland in partnership with Global Forest Watch.[^cite_note-\:2-18] Major sites of loss included Prey Lang Wildlife Sanctuary, where approximately 9,346 hectares were cleared in 2024, partly due to the construction of a new high-voltage transmission line cutting through the lowland rainforest.[^cite_note-\:2-18] In the [Cardamom Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardamom_Mountains) region, poaching through snaring is another major factor in the decline in the population of [pileated gibbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pileated_gibbon), [dholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhole), serows, [spotted linsangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_linsang) and many more.[^cite_note-19]
