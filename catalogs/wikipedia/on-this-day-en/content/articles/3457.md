**Belarus**,[^cite_note-15] officially the **Republic of Belarus**,[^cite_note-16] is a [landlocked country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked_country) in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe). It is bordered by [Russia](25391.md) to the east and northeast, [Ukraine](31750.md) to the south, [Poland](22936.md) to the west, and [Lithuania](17675.md) and [Latvia](17514.md) to the northwest. Belarus spans an area of 207,600 square kilometres (80,200 sq mi) with a population of 9.1 million. The country has a [hemiboreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemiboreal) climate and is administratively divided into [six regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Belarus). [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk) is the capital and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_largest_towns_in_Belarus); it is administered separately as a city with special status.

For most of the medieval period, the lands of modern-day Belarus were ruled by independent city-states such as the [Principality of Polotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Polotsk). Around 1300 these lands came fully under the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania) and subsequently by the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth); this period lasted for 500 years until the [1792–1795 partitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland) of Poland-Lithuania placed Belarus within the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_history_in_the_Russian_Empire) for the first time. In the aftermath of the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution) in 1917, different states arose competing for legitimacy amid the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), ultimately ending in the rise of the [Byelorussian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byelorussian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), which became a founding [constituent republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_the_Soviet_Union) of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in 1922. After the [Polish-Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-Soviet_War) (1918–1921), Belarus lost almost [half of its territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Belarus) to Poland. Much of the borders of Belarus took their modern shape in 1939, when some lands of the [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic) were reintegrated into it after the [Soviet invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Poland), and were finalised after World War II. During World War II, military operations devastated Belarus, which lost about a quarter of its population and half of its economic resources. In 1945, the Byelorussian SSR became a founding member of the [United Nations](31769.md), along with the Soviet Union. The republic was home to a widespread and diverse [anti-Nazi insurgent movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_resistance_during_World_War_II) which dominated politics until well into the 1970s, overseeing Belarus's [transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_economic_miracle) from an agrarian to an industrial economy.

The parliament of the republic proclaimed the [sovereignty](28791.md) of Belarus on 27 July 1990, and during the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), Belarus gained independence on 25 August 1991. Following the adoption of a new [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Belarus) in 1994, [Alexander Lukashenko](156589.md) was elected Belarus's first president in the country's first and only free [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Belarusian_presidential_election) after independence, serving as president ever since. Lukashenko heads a highly centralised [authoritarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarian) government. Belarus [ranks low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_rankings_of_Belarus) in international measurements of [freedom of the press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_in_Belarus) and [civil liberties](21401671.md). It has continued several Soviet-era policies, such as [state ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_ownership) of large sections of the [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Belarus). In 2000, Belarus and Russia signed a treaty for greater cooperation, forming the [Union State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_State).

The country has been a member of the [United Nations](31769.md) since its founding and has joined the [CIS](36870.md), the [CSTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Security_Treaty_Organization), the [EAEU](27290106.md), the [OSCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_for_Security_and_Co-Operation_in_Europe), and the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement). It has shown no aspirations of joining the [European Union](9317.md) but maintains a [bilateral relationship with the bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus%E2%80%93European_Union_relations), and also participates in the [Baku Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_Initiative). Belarus is among the only three European countries (along with [Russia](25391.md) and [Kosovo](17391.md)) not a member of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe); it attempted to join in 1993 but was refused admission because of electoral malpractice and [serious human rights concerns](21401671.md) (Belarus is the only European country that continues to use [capital punishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Belarus)). Its limited relationship with the council was suspended after the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) from its territory [in April of that year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine_\(24_February_%E2%80%93_7_April_2022\)#24_February).

## Etymology

See also: [Etymology of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_Belarus)

The name *Belarus* is closely related with the term *Belaya Rus'*, i.e., *[White Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Rus')*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMinahan199835-17] There are several claims to the origin of the name *White Rus'*.[^cite_note-Zaprudnik_1993-18] An ethno-religious theory suggests that the name used to describe the part of old [Ruthenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenia) lands within the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania) that had been populated mostly by [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) who had been Christianised early, as opposed to [Black Ruthenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Ruthenia), which was predominantly inhabited by pagan [Balts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balts).[^cite_note-19] An alternative explanation for the name comments on the white clothing the local Slavic population wears.[^cite_note-Zaprudnik_1993-18] A third theory suggests that the old Rus' lands that were not conquered by the [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars) (i.e., Polotsk, Vitebsk, and Mogilev) had been referred to as *White Rus'*.[^cite_note-Zaprudnik_1993-18] A fourth theory suggests that the colour white was associated with the west, and Belarus was the western part of [Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') in the 9th to 13th centuries.[^cite_note-20]



Stamp with the [Cross of St. Euphrosyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_of_Saint_Euphrosyne) by [Lazar Bohsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazar_Bohsha) from 1992

The name *Rus'* is often conflated with its Latin forms *Russia* and *Ruthenia*, thus Belarus is often referred to as *White Russia* or *White Ruthenia*. The name first appeared in [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_High_German_literature) and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Latin_literature) [medieval literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_literature); the chronicles of [Jan of Czarnków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_of_Czarnk%C3%B3w) mention the imprisonment of Lithuanian grand duke [Jogaila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jogaila) and his mother at "*Albae Russiae, Poloczk dicto*" in 1381.[^cite_note-21] The first known use of *White Russia* to refer to Belarus was in the late-16th century by Englishman Sir [Jerome Horsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome_Horsey), who was known for his close contacts with the Russian royal court.[^cite_note-22] During the 17th century, the Russian [tsars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar) used the term to describe the lands added from the Grand Duchy of Lithuania.[^cite_note-23]

The term *Belorussia* (Russian: Белоруссия, the latter part similar but spelled and stressed differently from Россия, *Russia*) first rose in the days of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), and the Russian Tsar was usually styled "the Tsar of All the Russias", as *Russia* or the *Russian Empire* was formed by three parts of Russia—the [Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Russia), [Little](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Russia), and [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Ruthenia).[^cite_note-24] This asserted that the territories are all Russian and all the peoples are also Russian; in the case of the Belarusians, they were variants of the Russian people.[^cite_note-25]

After the [Bolshevik Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik_Revolution) in 1917, the term *White Russia* caused some confusion, as it was also the name of the military force that opposed the red Bolsheviks.[^cite_note-26] During the period of the Byelorussian SSR, the term *Byelorussia* was embraced as part of a national consciousness. In western Belarus under Polish control, *Byelorussia* became commonly used in the regions of [Białystok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bia%C5%82ystok) and [Grodno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grodno) during the interwar period.[^cite_note-27]

The term *Byelorussia* (its names in other languages such as English being based on the Russian form) was used officially only until 1991. Officially, the full name of the country is *Republic of Belarus* (Рэспубліка Беларусь, Республика Беларусь, *Respublika Belarus*).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-4-29] In Russia, the usage of *Belorussia* is still very common.[^cite_note-30]

In Lithuanian, besides *Baltarusija* (White Russia), Belarus is also called *Gudija*.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The etymology of the word *Gudija* is not clear. By one hypothesis the word derives from the [Old Prussian name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Prussian_language) *Gudwa*, which, in turn, is related to the form *Żudwa*, which is a distorted version of *Sudwa, Sudovia. Sudovia*, in its turn, is one of the names of the [Yotvingians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yotvingians). Another hypothesis connects the word with the [Gothic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oium) that occupied parts of the territory of modern Belarus and Ukraine in the 4th and 5th centuries. The self-naming of Goths was *Gutans* and *Gytos*, which are close to Gudija. Yet another hypothesis is based on the idea that *Gudija* in Lithuanian means "the other" and may have been used historically by Lithuanians to refer to any people who did not speak Lithuanian.[^cite_note-33]

## History

Main article: [History of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Belarus)

### Early history

Further information: [Early Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Slavs)

From 5000 to 2000 BC, the [Bandkeramik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandkeramik) predominated in what now constitutes Belarus, and the [Cimmerians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimmerians) as well as other pastoralists roamed through the area by 1,000 BC. The [Zarubintsy culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarubintsy_culture) later became widespread at the beginning of the [1st millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_millennium). In addition, remains from the [Dnieper–Donets culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper%E2%80%93Donets_culture) were found in Belarus and parts of Ukraine.[^cite_note-34] The region was first permanently settled by [Baltic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_peoples) tribes in the 3rd century. Around the 5th century, the area was taken over by the Slavs. The takeover was partially due to the lack of military coordination of the Balts, but their gradual assimilation into Slavic culture was peaceful.[^cite_note-35] Invaders from [Asia](689.md), among whom were the [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns) and [Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avars_\(Carpathians\)), swept through c. 400–600 AD, but were unable to dislodge the Slavic presence.[^cite_note-36]

### Kievan Rus'

Further information: [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus')



[Principalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_early_East_Slavic_states) in Eastern Europe before the Mongol and Lithuanian invasions

In the 9th century, the territory of modern Belarus became part of [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'), a vast East Slavic state ruled by the [Rurikids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rurikids). Upon the death of its ruler [Yaroslav the Wise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslav_the_Wise) in 1054, the state split into independent principalities.[^cite_note-37] The [Battle on the Nemiga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_on_the_Nemiga_River) in 1067 was one of the more notable events of the period, the date of which is considered the founding date of [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk).

Many early principalities were virtually razed or severely affected by a major [Mongol invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Kievan_Rus') in the 13th century, but the lands of modern-day Belarus avoided the brunt of the invasion and eventually joined the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania).[^cite_note-38] There are no sources of military seizure, but the annals affirm the alliance and united foreign policy of [Polotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Polotsk) and Lithuania for decades.[^cite_note-39]

Incorporation into the Grand Duchy of Lithuania resulted in an economic, political, and ethno-cultural unification of Belarusian lands.[^cite_note-40] Of the principalities held by the duchy, nine of them were settled by a population that would eventually become the Belarusians.[^cite_note-41] During this time, the duchy was involved in several military campaigns, including fighting on the side of [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland_\(1025%E2%80%931385\)) against the [Teutonic Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutonic_Knights) at the [Battle of Grunwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Grunwald) in 1410; the joint victory allowed the duchy to control the northwestern borderlands of [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe).[^cite_note-42]

The [Muscovites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow), led by [Ivan III of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_III_of_Russia), began military campaigns in 1486 in an attempt to incorporate the former lands of [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'), including the territories of modern-day Belarus and Ukraine.[^cite_note-43]

### Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth

Further information: [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth)



A map of the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania) in the 15th century prior to its union with the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland). Belarus was fully within its borders.

On 2 February 1386, the Grand Duchy of Lithuania and the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland_\(1025%E2%80%931385\)) were joined in a [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union) through a [marriage of their rulers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Krewo).[^cite_note-44] This union set in motion the developments that eventually resulted in the formation of the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth), created in 1569 by the [Union of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Lublin).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

In the years following the union, the process of gradual [Polonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonisation) of both Lithuanians and Ruthenians gained steady momentum. In culture and social life, both the [Polish language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language) and [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) became dominant, and in 1696, Polish replaced Ruthenian as the official language, with Ruthenian being banned from administrative use.[^cite_note-47] However, the Ruthenian peasants continued to speak their native language. Also, the [Belarusian Byzantine Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Byzantine_Catholic_Church) was formed by the Poles to bring Orthodox Christians into the [See of Rome](13393.md). The Belarusian church entered into a full [communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koinonia) with the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) through the [Union of Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Brest) in 1595, while keeping its Byzantine [liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgy) in the [Church Slavonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Slavonic) language.

### Russian Empire

Main article: [Belarusian history in the Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_history_in_the_Russian_Empire)

Further information: [Kościuszko Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%9Bciuszko_Uprising), [November Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_Uprising), and [January Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_Uprising)



Napoleon's *Grande Armée* retreating after [his invasion of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon's_invasion_of_Russia) and crossing the [Berezina river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berezina_river) (near [Barysaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barysaw), Belarus)

The union between Poland and Lithuania ended in 1795 with the [Third Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Partition_of_Poland) by Imperial Russia, [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia), and [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy).[^cite_note-48] The Belarusian territories acquired by the Russian Empire under the reign of [Catherine II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_II_of_Russia)[^cite_note-49] were included into the [Belarusian Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Governorate) ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Белорусское генерал-губернаторство) in 1796 and held until their occupation by the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) during [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-Olson-50]

Under [Nicholas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_I_of_Russia) and [Alexander III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_III_of_Russia) the national cultures were repressed with policies of [Polonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonisation)[^cite_note-51] replaced by [Russification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification)[^cite_note-52] which included the return to [Orthodox Christianity](10186.md) of Belarusian [Uniates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Brest). Belarusian language was banned in schools while in nearby [Samogitia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samogitia) primary school education with [Samogitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samogitian_dialect) literacy was allowed.[^cite_note-53]

In a [Russification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification) drive in the 1840s, [Nicholas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_I_of_Russia) prohibited the use of the Belarusian language in public schools, campaigned against Belarusian publications, and tried to pressure those who had converted to Catholicism under the Poles to reconvert to the Orthodox faith. In 1863, economic and cultural pressure exploded in a [revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_Uprising), led by [Konstanty Kalinowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstanty_Kalinowski) (also known as Kastus). After the failed revolt, the Russian government reintroduced the use of [Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic_script) to Belarusian in 1864 and no documents in Belarusian were permitted by the Russian government until 1905.[^cite_note-54]

During the negotiations of the [Treaty of Brest-Litovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brest-Litovsk_\(Russia%E2%80%93Central_Powers\)), Belarus first declared independence under German occupation on 25 March 1918, forming the [Belarusian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_People's_Republic).[^cite_note-Birgerson_2002-55][^cite_note-56] Immediately afterwards, the [Polish–Soviet War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Soviet_War) ignited, and the territory of Belarus was divided between Poland and Soviet Russia.[^cite_note-57] The Rada of the Belarusian Democratic Republic exists as a [government in exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_in_exile) ever since then; in fact, it is currently the world's longest serving government in exile.[^cite_note-58]

### Early states and interwar period



The first government ("activists") of the Belarusian People's Republic (BNR, Беларуская Народная Рэспубліка), 1918.\
Sitting, left to right:\
[Aliaksandar Burbis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliaksandar_Burbis), [Jan Sierada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Sierada), [Jazep Varonka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazep_Varonka), [Vasil Zacharka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasil_Zacharka).\
Standing, left to right:\
[Arkadź Smolič](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkad%C5%BA_Smoli%C4%8D), [Pyotra Krecheuski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotra_Krecheuski), [Kastuś Jezavitaŭ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastu%C5%9B_Jezavita%C5%AD), Anton Ausianik, Liavon Zayats.

The [Belarusian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_People's_Republic) was the first attempt to create an independent Belarusian state under the name "Belarus". Despite significant efforts, the state ceased to exist, primarily because the territory was continually dominated by the [Imperial German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_German_Army) and the [Imperial Russian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army) in [World War I](4764461.md), and then the [Bolshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik) [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army). It existed from only 1918 to 1919 but created prerequisites for the formation of a Belarusian state. The choice of name was probably based on the fact that core members of the newly formed government were educated in tsarist universities, with corresponding emphasis on the ideology of West-Russianism.[^cite_note-59]

The [Republic of Central Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Central_Lithuania) was a short-lived political entity, which was the last attempt to restore Lithuania to the historical confederacy state (it was also supposed to create Lithuania Upper and Lithuania Lower). The republic was created in 1920 following [the staged rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBeligowski's_Mutiny) of soldiers of the [1st Lithuanian–Belarusian Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Lithuanian%E2%80%93Belarusian_Division) of the [Polish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Army) under [Lucjan Żeligowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucjan_%C5%BBeligowski). Centred on the historical capital of the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania), [Vilna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilna) ([Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_language): *Vilnius*, [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Wilno*), for 18 months the entity served as a [buffer state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffer_state) between [Poland](22936.md), upon which it depended, and Lithuania, which claimed the area.[^cite_note-60] After a variety of delays, [a disputed election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_Republic_of_Central_Lithuania_general_election) took place on 8 January 1922, and the territory was annexed to Poland. Żeligowski later in his memoir which was published in London in 1943 condemned the annexation of the Republic by Poland, as well as the policy of closing Belarusian schools and general disregard of Marshal [Józef Piłsudski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3zef_Pi%C5%82sudski)'s confederation plans by Polish ally.[^cite_note-61]



Meeting in the [Kurapaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurapaty) woods, 1989, where between 1937 and 1941 from 30,000 to 250,000 people, including Belarusian [intelligentsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligentsia) members, were [murdered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_repressions_in_Belarus) by the [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD) during the [Great Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Purge)

In January 1919, a part of Belarus under Bolshevik Russian control was declared the [Socialist Soviet Republic of Byelorussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Soviet_Republic_of_Byelorussia) (SSRB) for just two months, but then merged with the [Lithuanian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) (LSSR) to form the [Socialist Soviet Republic of Lithuania and Belorussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Soviet_Republic_of_Lithuania_and_Belorussia) (SSR LiB), which lost control of its territories by August.

The [Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byelorussian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) (BSSR) was created in July 1920.[^cite_note-62]

The contested lands were divided between Poland and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) after the war ended in 1921, and the Byelorussian SSR became a founding member of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics in 1922.[^cite_note-Birgerson_2002-55][^cite_note-63] In the 1920s and 1930s, Soviet agricultural and economic policies, including [collectivisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivisation) and [five-year plans for the national economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-year_plans_of_the_Soviet_Union), led to famine and political repression.[^cite_note-64]

The [western part of modern Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Belorussia) remained part of the [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic).[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] After an early period of liberalisation, tensions between increasingly nationalistic Polish government and various increasingly separatist ethnic minorities started to grow, and the [Belarusian minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_minority_in_Poland) was no exception.[^cite_note-Norman_Davies-67][^cite_note-Stosunki_polsko-68] The [polonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonisation) drive was inspired and influenced by the Polish [National Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democracy_\(Poland\)), led by [Roman Dmowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Dmowski), who advocated refusing Belarusians and Ukrainians the right for a free national development.[^cite_note-69] A Belarusian organisation, the *[Belarusian Peasants' and Workers' Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Peasants'_and_Workers'_Union)*, was banned in 1927, and opposition to Polish government was met with state repressions.[^cite_note-Norman_Davies-67][^cite_note-Stosunki_polsko-68] Nonetheless, compared to the (larger) [Ukrainian minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_minority_in_Poland), Belarusians were much less politically aware and active, and thus suffered fewer repressions than the Ukrainians.[^cite_note-Norman_Davies-67][^cite_note-Stosunki_polsko-68] In 1935, after the death of Piłsudski, a new wave of repressions was released upon the minorities, with many [Orthodox churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Orthodox_Church) and Belarusian schools being closed.[^cite_note-Norman_Davies-67][^cite_note-Stosunki_polsko-68] Use of the [Belarusian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_language) was discouraged.[^cite_note-70] Belarusian leadership was sent to [Bereza Kartuska prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bereza_Kartuska_prison).[^cite_note-71]

### World War II

Main articles: [Byelorussia in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byelorussia_in_World_War_II) and [German occupation of Byelorussia during World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Byelorussia_during_World_War_II)



German soldiers in [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk), August 1941



[Khatyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khatyn) Memorial; during World War II the German Nazis murdered civilians in 5,295 different localities in [occupied Soviet Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Belarus_by_Nazi_Germany).

In September 1939, the Soviet Union invaded and occupied eastern Poland, following the German [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) two weeks earlier which marked the beginning of [World War II](32927.md). The territories of [Western Belorussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Belorussia) were [annexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_annexation_of_Western_Belorussia) and incorporated into the Byelorussian SSR.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] The Soviet-controlled Byelorussian People's Council officially took control of the territories, whose populations consisted of a mixture of Poles, Ukrainians, Belarusians and Jews, on 28 October 1939 in [Białystok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bia%C5%82ystok). Nazi Germany [invaded the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa) in 1941. The [defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_of_Brest_Fortress) of [Brest Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest_Fortress) was the first major battle of [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa).

The Byelorussian SSR was the hardest-hit Soviet republic in World War II; it remained under [German occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Byelorussia_during_World_War_II) until 1944. The German *[Generalplan Ost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalplan_Ost)* called for the extermination, expulsion, or enslavement of most or all Belarusians to provide more [living space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebensraum) in the [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drang_nach_Osten) for Germans.[^cite_note-76] Most of Western Belarus became part of the *[Reichskommissariat Ostland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissariat_Ostland)* in 1941, but in 1943 the German authorities allowed local [collaborators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byelorussian_collaboration_with_Nazi_Germany) to set up a client state, the [Belarusian Central Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Central_Council).[^cite_note-77]

During World War II, Belarus was home to a variety of [guerrilla movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_resistance_during_World_War_II), including Jewish, Polish, and Soviet partisans. Belarusian partisan formations formed a large part of the [Soviet partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_partisans),[^cite_note-78] and in the modern day these partisans have formed a core part of the Belarusian national identity, with Belarus continuing to refer to itself as the "partisan republic" since the 1970s.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] Following the war, many former Soviet partisans entered positions of government, among them [Pyotr Masherov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Masherov) and [Kirill Mazurov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirill_Mazurov), both of whom were First Secretary of the Communist Party of Byelorussia. Until the late 1970s, the Belarusian government was almost entirely composed of former partisans.[^cite_note-Ioffe-2003-81] Numerous pieces of media have been made about the Belarusian partisans, including the 1985 film *[Come and See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Come_and_See)* and the works of authors [Ales Adamovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ales_Adamovich) and [Vasil Bykaŭ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasil_Byka%C5%AD).

The German occupation in 1941–1944 and war on the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_\(World_War_II\)) devastated Belarus. During that time, 209 out of 290 towns and cities were destroyed, 85% of the republic's industry, and more than one million buildings. After the war, it was estimated that 2.2 million local inhabitants had died, and of those some 810,000 were combatants—some foreign. This figure represented [a staggering quarter of the prewar population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_casualties_of_the_Soviet_Union).[^cite_note-Axell-2002-82] In the 1990s some raised the estimate even higher, to 2.7 million.[^cite_note-83] The [Jewish population of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Belarus) was devastated during [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust) and never recovered.[^cite_note-Axell-2002-82][^cite_note-Fedor-1995b-84][^cite_note-85] The population of Belarus did not regain its pre-war level until 1971.[^cite_note-Fedor-1995b-84] Belarus was also hit hard economically, losing around half of its economic resources.[^cite_note-Axell-2002-82]

### Post-war



Belarusian poster where the text reads "Long live the Stalinist constitution of victorious socialism and true democracy!" (issued in 1940)

The borders of the Byelorussian SSR and Poland were redrawn, in accord with the 1919-proposed [Curzon Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curzon_Line).[^cite_note-Olson-50] Byelorussia gained territory to the west: the formerly Polish [Kresy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kresy).

[Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) implemented a policy of [Sovietisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovietization) to isolate the Byelorussian SSR from [Western influences](21208200.md).[^cite_note-Fedor-1995b-84] This policy involved sending Russians from various parts of the Soviet Union and placing them in key positions in the Byelorussian SSR government. After Stalin died in 1953, [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev) continued his predecessor's [cultural hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_hegemony) programme, stating, "The sooner we all start speaking Russian, the faster we shall build communism."[^cite_note-Fedor-1995b-84]

Between Stalin's death in 1953 and 1980, Belarusian politics was dominated by former members of the Soviet partisans, including First Secretaries Kirill Mazurov and Pyotr Masherov.[^cite_note-Ioffe-2003-81] Mazurov and Masherov oversaw Belarus's [rapid industrialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_economic_miracle) and transformation from one of the Soviet Union's poorest republics into one of its richest.[^cite_note-86] In 1986, the Byelorussian SSR was contaminated with most (70%) of the [nuclear fallout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fallout) from the explosion at the [Chernobyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster) power plant located 16 km beyond the border in the neighbouring [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

By the late 1980s, political liberalisation led to a national revival, with the [Belarusian Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Popular_Front) becoming a major pro-independence force.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

### Independence



Leaders of Russia, Ukraine and Belarus signing the [Belavezha Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belavezha_Accords), [dissolving the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), 8 December 1991

In March 1990, [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Belarusian_Supreme_Council) for seats in the [Supreme Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Soviet) of the Byelorussian SSR took place. Though the opposition candidates, mostly associated with the pro-independence [Belarusian Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Popular_Front), took only 10% of the seats,[^cite_note-Fedor-1995-91] Belarus declared itself sovereign on 27 July 1990 by issuing the [Declaration of State Sovereignty of the Belarusian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_State_Sovereignty_of_the_Belarusian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).[^cite_note-92]

[Wide-scale strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Belarusian_strikes) erupted in April 1991. With the support of the [Communist Party of Byelorussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Byelorussia), the country's name was changed to the Republic of Belarus on 25 August 1991.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-Fedor-1995-91] [Stanislav Shushkevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislav_Shushkevich), the chairman of the Supreme Soviet of Belarus, met with [Boris Yeltsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yeltsin) of Russia and [Leonid Kravchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Kravchuk) of Ukraine on 8 December 1991 in [Białowieża Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bia%C5%82owie%C5%BCa_Forest) to formally declare the dissolution of the Soviet Union and the formation of the [Commonwealth of Independent States](36870.md).[^cite_note-Fedor-1995-91]

In January 1992, the [Belarusian Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Popular_Front) campaigned for early elections later in the year, two years before they were scheduled. By May of that year, about 383,000 signatures had been collected for a petition to hold the referendum, which was 23,000 more than legally required to be put to a referendum at the time. Despite this, the meeting of the [Supreme Council of the Republic of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Council_of_the_Republic_of_Belarus) to ultimately decide the date for the referendum was delayed by six months. However, with no evidence to suggest such, the Supreme Council rejected the petition on the grounds of massive irregularities. Elections for the Supreme Council were set for March 1994. A new law on parliamentary elections failed to pass by 1993. Disputes over the referendum were accredited to the largely conservative [Party of Belarusian Communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Belarusian_Communists), which controlled the Supreme Council at the time and was largely opposed to political and economic reform, with allegations that some of the deputies opposed Belarusian independence.[^cite_note-94]

### Lukashenko era



1997 map of Belarus

A national constitution was adopted in March 1994 in which the functions of prime minister were given to the [President of Belarus](2631957.md). A two-round [election for the presidency on 24 June 1994 and 10 July 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Belarusian_presidential_election)[^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-4-29] catapulted the formerly unknown Alexander Lukashenko into national prominence. He garnered 45% of the vote in the first round and 80%[^cite_note-Fedor-1995-91] in the second, defeating [Vyacheslav Kebich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Kebich) who received 14% of the vote. The elections were the first and only free elections in Belarus after independence.[^cite_note-95]

The 2000s saw some economic disputes between Belarus and its primary economic partner, Russia. The first one was the [2004 Russia–Belarus energy dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Russia%E2%80%93Belarus_energy_dispute) when Russian energy giant [Gazprom](320489.md) ceased the import of gas into Belarus because of price disagreements. The [2007 Russia–Belarus energy dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Russia%E2%80%93Belarus_energy_dispute) centred on accusations by Gazprom that Belarus was siphoning oil from the [Druzhba pipeline](1950396.md) that runs through Belarus. Two years later the so-called [Milk War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_War), a trade dispute, started when Russia wanted Belarus to recognise the independence of [Abkhazia](18933375.md) and [South Ossetia](318381.md) and through a series of events ended up banning the import of dairy products from Belarus.

In 2011, Belarus suffered [a severe economic crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Belarus#Crisis_of_2011) attributed to Lukashenko's government's centralised control of the economy, with inflation reaching 108.7%.[^cite_note-96] Around the same time the [2011 Minsk Metro bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Minsk_Metro_bombing) occurred in which 15 people were killed and 204 were injured. Two suspects, who were arrested within two days, confessed to being the perpetrators and were executed by shooting in 2012. The official version of events as publicised by the Belarusian government was questioned in the unprecedented wording of the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council) statement condemning "the apparent terrorist attack" intimating the possibility that the Belarusian government itself was behind the bombing.[^cite_note-97]



[Alexander Lukashenko](156589.md) has ruled Belarus since 1994.

[Mass protests](64399515.md) erupted across the country following the disputed [2020 Belarusian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Belarusian_presidential_election),[^cite_note-98] in which Lukashenko sought a sixth term in office.[^cite_note-99] Neighbouring countries Poland and Lithuania do not recognise Lukashenko as the legitimate president of Belarus and the Lithuanian government has allotted a residence for main opposition candidate [Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviatlana_Tsikhanouskaya) and other members of the Belarusian opposition in [Vilnius](32597.md).[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] Neither is Lukashenko recognised as the legitimate president of Belarus by the European Union, Canada, the United Kingdom or the United States.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] The European Union, Canada, the United Kingdom, and the United States have all imposed sanctions against Belarus because of the rigged election and political oppression during the ongoing protests in the country.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] [Further sanctions](70323386.md) were imposed in 2022 following the [country's role and complicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_involvement_in_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md); Russian troops were allowed to stage part of the invasion from Belarusian territory.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] Sanctions were targeted towards not only corporate offices and individual officers of government, but also private individuals who work in the [state-owned enterprise](204768.md) industrial sector.[^cite_note-Ferris-2023-113] [Norway](21241.md) and [Japan](15573.md) have joined the sanctions regime which aims to isolate Belarus from the international supply chain. Most major Belarusian banks are also under restrictions.[^cite_note-Ferris-2023-113]

## Geography

Main articles: [Geography of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Belarus) and [Climate of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Belarus)

Belarus lies between latitudes [51°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51st_parallel_north) and [57° N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/57th_parallel_north), and longitudes [23°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_meridian_east) and [33° E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/33rd_meridian_east). Its extension from north to south is 560 km (350 mi), from west to east is 650 km (400 mi).[^cite_note-114] It is landlocked, relatively flat, and contains large tracts of [marshy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh) land.[^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-2-115] About 40% of Belarus is covered by forests.[^cite_note-Press_Service_of_the_President_of_the_Republic_of_Belarus-2015-116][^cite_note-117] The country lies within two ecoregions: [Sarmatic mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmatic_mixed_forests) and [Central European mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_mixed_forests).[^cite_note-118]

Many streams and 11,000 lakes are found in Belarus.[^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-2-115] Three major rivers run through the country: the [Neman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neman_River), the [Pripyat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pripyat_River), and the [Dnieper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_River). The Neman flows westward towards the Baltic Sea and the Pripyat flows eastward to the Dnieper; the Dnieper flows southward towards the [Black Sea](3386.md).[^cite_note-119]



[Strusta Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strusta_Lake) in the [Vitebsk Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitebsk_Region)

The highest point is [Dzyarzhynskaya Hara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzyarzhynskaya_Hara) (Dzyarzhynsk Hill) at 345 metres (1,132 ft), and the lowest point is on the Neman River at 90 m (295 ft).[^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-2-115] The average elevation of Belarus is 160 m (525 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level).[^cite_note-120] The climate features mild to cold winters, with January minimum temperatures ranging from −4 [°C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius) (24.8 [°F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit)) in southwest ([Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest,_Belarus)) to −8 °C (17.6 °F) in northeast ([Vitebsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitebsk)), and cool and moist summers with an average temperature of 18 °C (64.4 °F).[^cite_note-Fedor-1995a-121] Belarus has an average annual rainfall of 550 to 700 mm (21.7 to 27.6 in).[^cite_note-Fedor-1995a-121] The country is in the transitional zone between [continental climates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate) and [maritime climates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate).[^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-2-115]

Natural resources include [peat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peat) deposits, small quantities of oil and natural gas, [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite), dolomite ([limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone)), [marl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marl), chalk, sand, gravel, and clay.[^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-2-115] About 70% of the radiation from neighbouring Ukraine's 1986 [Chernobyl nuclear disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster) entered Belarusian territory, and about a fifth of Belarusian land (principally farmland and forests in the southeastern regions) was affected by radiation fallout.[^cite_note-122] The United Nations and other agencies have aimed to reduce the level of radiation in affected areas, especially through the use of [caesium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesium) binders and [rapeseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapeseed) cultivation, which are meant to decrease soil levels of [caesium-137](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesium-137).[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

In Belarus, [forest cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_cover) is around 43% of the total land area, equivalent to 8,767,600 hectares (ha) of forest in 2020, up from 7,780,000 hectares (ha) in 1990. In 2020, naturally regenerating forests covered 6,555,600 hectares (ha), and planted forests covered 2,212,000 hectares (ha). Of the naturally regenerating forest 2% was reported to be [primary forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_forest) (consisting of native tree species with no clearly visible indications of human activity) and around 16% of the forest area was found within protected areas. For the year 2015, 100% of the forest area was reported to be under [public ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_ownership).[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126]

Belarus borders five countries: [Latvia](17514.md) to the north, [Lithuania](17675.md) to the northwest, [Poland](22936.md) to the west, [Russia](25391.md) to the north and the east, and [Ukraine](31750.md) to the south. Treaties in 1995 and 1996 demarcated Belarus's borders with Latvia and Lithuania, and Belarus ratified a 1997 treaty establishing the Belarus-Ukraine border in 2009.[^cite_note-Titarenko-2011-127]<sup>: 14 </sup> Belarus and Lithuania ratified final border demarcation documents in February 2007.[^cite_note-Titarenko-2011-127]

## Government and politics

Main article: [Politics of Belarus](68948.md)

See also: [United Transitional Cabinet of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Transitional_Cabinet_of_Belarus)



[Alexander Lukashenko](156589.md)\
[President](2631957.md)



[Alexander Turchin](70679109.md)\
[Prime Minister](2599969.md)



[Government House, Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_House,_Minsk)

Belarus, by the constitution, is a [semi-presidential republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_republic) with [separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers), governed by a [president](2631957.md) and the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Belarus). However, Belarus has been led by a highly centralised and authoritarian government,[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-John_R._Short-2021-129] and has often been described as "Europe's last dictatorship" and president Alexander Lukashenko as "Europe's last dictator"[^cite_note-130] by some media outlets, politicians and authors.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134] Belarus has been considered an [autocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocracy) where power is ultimately concentrated in the hands of the president, elections are not free and [judicial independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_independence) is weak.[^cite_note-135] The [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) removed Belarus from its observer status since 1997 as a response for election irregularities in the November 1996 constitutional referendum and parliament by-elections.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137] Readmission of the country into the council is dependent on the completion of benchmarks set by the council, including the improvement of [human rights](21401671.md), [rule of law](25166191.md), and democracy.[^cite_note-138] A delegation still participates in the [Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Council_of_Europe) activities.[^cite_note-139]

The term for each presidency is five years. Under the 1994 constitution, the president could serve for only two terms as president, but a [change in the constitution in 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Belarusian_referendum) eliminated term limits.[^cite_note-140] Lukashenko has been President of Belarus since 1994. In 1996, Lukashenko called for a controversial vote to extend the presidential term from five to seven years, and as a result [the election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Belarusian_presidential_election) that was supposed to occur in 1999 was pushed back to 2001. The [referendum on the extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Belarusian_referendum) was denounced as a "fantastic" fake by the chief electoral officer, [Viktar Hanchar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktar_Hanchar), who was removed from the office for official matters only during the campaign.[^cite_note-141] The National Assembly is a [bicameral parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) comprising the 110-member [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Belarus\)) (the lower house) and the 64-member [Council of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_Republic_\(Belarus\)) (the upper house).[^cite_note-142]



[Victory Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Square,_Minsk) in Minsk

The House of Representatives has the power to appoint the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_Belarus), make constitutional amendments, call for a [vote of confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_of_Confidence) on the prime minister, and make suggestions on foreign and domestic policy.[^cite_note-143] The Council of the Republic has the power to select various government officials, conduct an impeachment trial of the president, and accept or reject the bills passed by the House of Representatives. Each chamber can veto any law passed by local officials if it is contrary to the Constitution.[^cite_note-Constitution_of_Belarus-2004-144]

The government includes a Council of Ministers, headed by the prime minister and five deputy prime ministers.[^cite_note-145] The members of this council need not be members of the legislature and are appointed by the president. The judiciary comprises the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Belarus) and specialised courts such as the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Belarus), which deals with specific issues related to constitutional and business law. The judges of national courts are appointed by the president and confirmed by the Council of the Republic. For criminal cases, the highest court of appeal is the Supreme Court. The Belarusian Constitution forbids the use of special extrajudicial courts.[^cite_note-Constitution_of_Belarus-2004-144]

### Elections

&#x20;Lukashenko was officially re-elected as president [in 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Belarusian_presidential_election), [in 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Belarusian_presidential_election), [in 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Belarusian_presidential_election), [in 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Belarusian_presidential_election), [in 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Belarusian_presidential_election) and again [in 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Belarusian_presidential_election), although none of those elections were considered free or fair nor democratic.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154]



The [former flag of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-red-white_flag), used in 1918, and then between 1991 and 1995, is widely used as a symbol of opposition to the government of Alexander Lukashenko.

Neither the pro-Lukashenko parties, such as the [Belarusian Social Sporting Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Social_Sporting_Party) and the [Republican Party of Labour and Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_of_Labour_and_Justice) (RPTS), nor the [People's Coalition 5 Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Coalition_5_Plus) opposition parties, such as the [BPF Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BPF_Party) and the [United Civic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Civic_Party), won any seats in the [2004 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Belarusian_parliamentary_election). The [Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe](22796.md) (OSCE) ruled that the elections were unfair because opposition candidates were arbitrarily denied registration and the election process was designed to favour the ruling party.[^cite_note-155]



Protests at [October Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Square,_Minsk) in Minsk in 2006 after the [2006 Belarusian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Belarusian_presidential_election)

In the [2006 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Belarusian_presidential_election), Lukashenko was opposed by [Alaksandar Milinkievič](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaksandar_Milinkievi%C4%8D), who represented a coalition of opposition parties, and by [Alyaksandr Kazulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alyaksandr_Kazulin) of the Social Democrats. Kazulin was detained and beaten by police during protests surrounding the [All Belarusian People's Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Belarusian_People's_Assembly). Lukashenko won the election with 80% of the vote; the Russian Federation and the [CIS](36870.md) deemed the vote open and fair[^cite_note-156] while the OSCE and other organisations called the election unfair.[^cite_note-157]

After the December completion of the [2010 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Belarusian_presidential_election), Lukashenko was elected to a fourth straight term with nearly 80% of the vote in elections. The runner-up opposition leader [Andrei Sannikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Sannikov) received less than 3% of the vote; independent observers criticised the election as fraudulent. When opposition protesters took to the streets in Minsk, many people, including some presidential candidates, were beaten and arrested by the riot police.[^cite_note-158] Many of the candidates, including Sannikov, were sentenced to prison or house arrest for terms which are mainly and typically over four years.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160] Six months later amid an unprecedented economic crisis, activists utilised social networking to initiate a fresh round of protests characterised by wordless hand-clapping.[^cite_note-161]

In the [2012 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Belarusian_parliamentary_election), 105 of the 110 members elected to the House of Representatives were not affiliated with any political party. The [Communist Party of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Belarus) won 3 seats, and the [Belarusian Agrarian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Agrarian_Party) and RPTS, one each.[^cite_note-162] Most non-partisans represent a wide scope of social organisations such as workers' collectives, public associations, and civil society organisations, similar to the composition of the Soviet legislature.[^cite_note-163]

In the [2020 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Belarusian_presidential_election), Lukashenko won again with official results giving him 80% of the vote, leading to [mass protests](64399515.md). The [European Union](9317.md) and the [United Kingdom](31717.md) did not recognise the result and the EU imposed sanctions.[^cite_note-164]

### Local government

[Local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government) in Belarus is administered by administrative-territorial units ([Belarusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_language): адміністрацыйна-тэрытарыяльныя адзінкі; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): административно-территориальные единицы), and occurs on two levels: basic and primary. At the basic level are 118 raions councils and 10 cities of oblast subordination councils, which are supervised by the governments of the oblasts.[^cite_note-rm.coe.int-165] At the primary level are 14 cities of raion subordination councils, 8 [urban-type settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban-type_settlements_in_Belarus) councils, and 1,151 village councils.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167] The councils are elected by their residents, and have executive committees appointed by their executive committee chairs. The chairs of executive committees for raions and city of oblast subordinations are appointed by the regional executive committees at the level above; the chairs of executive committees for towns of raion subordination, settlements, and villages are appointed by their councils, but upon the recommendation of the raion executive committees.[^cite_note-rm.coe.int-165] In either case, the councils have the power to approve or reject a nominee for executive committee chair.

[Settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_settlement) without their own local council and executive committee are called territorial units ([Belarusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_language): тэрытарыяльныя адзінкі; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): территориальные единицы). These territorial units may also be classified as a city of regional or raion subordination, urban-type settlement, or rural settlement, but whose government is administered by the council of another primary or basic unit.[^cite_note-168] In October 1995, a [presidential decree](322949.md) abolished the local governments of cities of raion subordination and urban-type settlements which served as the [administrative centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_centre) of raions, demoting them from administrative-territorial units to territorial units.[^cite_note-169]

As for 2019, the administrative-territorial and territorial units include 115 cities, 85 urban-type settlements, and 23,075 rural settlements.[^cite_note-170]

### Administrative divisions

Further information: [Regions of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Belarus) and [Districts of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Belarus)



Administrative divisions of Belarus

Belarus is divided into six regions called [oblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast) ([Belarusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_language): вобласць; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): область), which are named after the cities that serve as their administrative centres: [Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest,_Belarus), [Gomel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomel), [Grodno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grodno), [Mogilev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogilev), [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk), and [Vitebsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitebsk).[^cite_note-Constitution_of_Belarus-2004a-171] Each region has a provincial legislative authority, called a region council (Belarusian: абласны Савет Дэпутатаў; Russian: Областной Совет депутатов), which is elected by its residents, and a provincial executive authority called a region administration (Belarusian: абласны выканаўчы камітэт; Russian: областной исполнительный комитет), whose chairman is appointed by the president.[^cite_note-172] The regions are further subdivided into 118 [raions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raion), commonly translated as districts (Belarusian: раён; Russian: район).[^cite_note-Constitution_of_Belarus-2004a-171] Each raion has its own legislative authority, or raion council, (Belarusian: раённы Савет Дэпутатаў; Russian: районный Совет депутатов) elected by its residents, and an executive authority or raion administration appointed by oblast executive powers.[^cite_note-Press_Service_of_the_President_of_the_Republic_of_Belarus-2015-116] The city of Minsk is split into nine districts and enjoys special status as the nation's capital at the same administration level as the oblasts.[^cite_note-173] It is run by an executive committee and has been granted a charter of self-rule.[^cite_note-174]

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Belarus)



President [Alexander Lukashenko](156589.md) shaking hands with [Russian President](239646.md) [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), 2015

The Byelorussian SSR was one of the two Soviet republics that joined the [United Nations](31769.md) along with the Ukrainian SSR as one of the original 51 members in 1945.[^cite_note-175] Belarus and Russia have been close trading partners and diplomatic allies since the break-up of the Soviet Union. Belarus is dependent on Russia for imports of raw materials and for its export market.[^cite_note-United_States_Government-2007-176]

The [Union State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_State), a supranational confederation between Belarus and Russia, was established in a 1996–99 series of treaties that called for monetary union, equal rights, single citizenship, and a common foreign and defence policy. However, the future of the union has been placed in doubt because of Belarus's repeated delays of monetary union, the lack of a referendum date for the draft constitution, and a [dispute over the petroleum trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Russia%E2%80%93Belarus_energy_dispute).[^cite_note-United_States_Government-2007-176][^cite_note-177] Belarus was a founding member of the [Commonwealth of Independent States](36870.md) (CIS).[^cite_note-178] Belarus has trade agreements with several [European Union](9317.md) member states (despite other member states' travel ban on Lukashenko and top officials),[^cite_note-179] including neighbouring Latvia, Lithuania, and Poland.[^cite_note-Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_of_the_Republic_of_Belarus-2007-180] Travel bans imposed by the European Union have been lifted in the past in order to allow Lukashenko to attend diplomatic meetings and also to engage his government and opposition groups in dialogue.[^cite_note-181]



Leaders of Belarus, Russia, Germany, France, and Ukraine at the summit in [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk_II), 11–12 February 2015

Bilateral relations with the United States are strained; the United States has not had an ambassador in Minsk since 2007 and Belarus has not had an ambassador in Washington since 2008.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183] Diplomatic relations remained tense, and in 2004, the United States passed the [Belarus Democracy Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus_Democracy_Act_of_2004), which authorised funding for anti-government Belarusian NGOs, and prohibited loans to the Belarusian government, except for humanitarian purposes.[^cite_note-184]

Relations between [China and Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus%E2%80%93China_relations) are close,[^cite_note-185] with Lukashenko visiting China multiple times during his tenure.[^cite_note-186] Belarus also has strong ties with [Syria](7515849.md),[^cite_note-187] considered a key partner in the Middle East.[^cite_note-188] In addition to the CIS, Belarus is a member of the [Eurasian Economic Union](27290106.md) (previously the [Eurasian Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Economic_Community)), the [Collective Security Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Security_Treaty_Organization),[^cite_note-Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_of_the_Republic_of_Belarus-2007-180] the international [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement) since 1998,[^cite_note-189] and the [Organization on Security and Cooperation in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_on_Security_and_Cooperation_in_Europe) (OSCE). As an OSCE member state, Belarus's international commitments are subject to monitoring under the mandate of the [US Helsinki Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Helsinki_Commission?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-190] Belarus is included in the European Union's [Eastern Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Partnership) programme, part of the EU's [European Neighbourhood Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Neighbourhood_Policy) (ENP), which aims to bring the EU and its neighbours closer in economic and geopolitical terms.[^cite_note-191] However, Belarus suspended its participation in the Eastern Partnership programme on 28 June 2021, after the EU imposed more sanctions against the country.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193]

### Military

Main article: [Armed Forces of Belarus](3537.md)



Soldiers patrol in the [Białowieża Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bia%C5%82owie%C5%BCa_Forest) on the Belarusian border with Poland

Lieutenant General [Viktor Khrenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Khrenin) heads the Ministry of Defence,[^cite_note-194] and Alexander Lukashenko (as president) serves as Commander-in-Chief.[^cite_note-Constitution_of_Belarus-2004-144] The armed forces were formed in 1992 using parts of the former [Soviet Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Armed_Forces) on the new republic's territory. The transformation of the ex-Soviet forces into the Armed Forces of Belarus, which was completed in 1997, reduced the number of its soldiers by 30,000 and restructured its leadership and military formations.[^cite_note-195]

Most of Belarus's service members are [conscripts](5735.md), who serve for 12 months if they have higher education or 18 months if they do not.[^cite_note-196] Demographic decreases in the Belarusians of conscription age have increased the importance of contract soldiers, who numbered 12,000 in 2001.[^cite_note-197] In 2005, about 1.4% of Belarus's gross domestic product was devoted to military expenditure.[^cite_note-198]

Belarus has not expressed a desire to join [NATO](21133.md) but has participated in the Individual Partnership Program since 1997,[^cite_note-199] and Belarus provided refuelling and airspace support for the [International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force) mission in [Afghanistan](737.md).[^cite_note-200] Belarus first began to cooperate with NATO upon signing documents to participate in their Partnership for Peace Program in 1995.[^cite_note-201] However, Belarus cannot join NATO because it is a member of the CSTO. Tensions between NATO and Belarus peaked after the March 2006 presidential election in Belarus.[^cite_note-202]

### Human rights and corruption

Further information: [Human rights in Belarus](21401671.md)



Graffiti in [Gdańsk](12099.md) depicting Belarusian human rights activist [Ales Bialiatski](31971174.md)

[Amnesty International](18947898.md),[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-203] and [Human Rights Watch](46530.md)[^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-2005-204] have criticised Lukashenko's violations of human rights. Belarus's [Democracy Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Index) rating is the lowest in Europe, the country is labelled as "not free" by [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House),[^cite_note-205] as "repressed" in the [Index of Economic Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_of_Economic_Freedom), and in the [Press Freedom Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_Freedom_Index) published by [Reporters Without Borders](526054.md), Belarus is ranked 153rd out of 180 countries for 2022.[^cite_note-206] The Belarusian government is criticised for human rights violations and its persecution of non-governmental organisations, journalists, national minorities, and opposition politicians.[^cite_note-Amnesty_International-203][^cite_note-Human_Rights_Watch-2005-204] Lukashenko announced a law in 2014 that prohibits [kolkhoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkhoz) farm workers (9% of work force) from leaving their jobs at will—a change of job and living location will require permission from governors. Lukashenko himself compared the law with [serfdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serfdom).[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209] Belarus is the only European country still using [capital punishment](5902.md), having carried out executions in 2011.[^cite_note-210] [LGBTQ rights in Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights_in_Belarus) are ranked among the lowest in Europe.[^cite_note-211] In 2023, Lukashenko signed a law which allows using capital punishment against officials and soldiers convicted of high [treason](31292.md).[^cite_note-212]

The judicial system lacks independence and is subject to political interference.[^cite_note-213] Corrupt practices such as bribery often took place during tender processes, and [whistleblower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistleblower) protection and national [ombudsman](26289198.md) are lacking in Belarus's anti-corruption system.[^cite_note-214]



Rally against Lukashenko in Minsk, 23 August 2020

In 2020, the [Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights](22463.md) declared it had received reports of 450 documented cases of torture and ill-treatment of people who were arrested during the [2020–2021 Belarusian protests](64399515.md) following the presidential election. The reports included violence against women and children, including sexual abuse and rape with rubber batons.[^cite_note-215] At least three detainees suffered injuries indicative of sexual violence in [Okrestino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okrestino) prison in Minsk, or on the way there.[^cite_note-216] In an interview from September 2020 Lukashenko claimed that detainees faked their bruises, saying, "Some of the girls there had their butts painted in blue".[^cite_note-217]

On 23 May 2021, Belarusian authorities forcibly diverted [Ryanair Flight 4978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryanair_Flight_4978) from [Athens](1216.md) to [Vilnius](32597.md) to detain opposition activist and journalist [Roman Protasevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Protasevich) along with his girlfriend; in response, the [European Union](9317.md) imposed stricter sanctions on Belarus.[^cite_note-218] In May 2021, Lukashenko threatened that he would flood the European Union with migrants and drugs as a response to the sanctions.[^cite_note-219] In July 2021, Belarusian authorities launched [hybrid warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_warfare) by [human trafficking](19333662.md) of [migrants to the European Union](68384300.md).[^cite_note-220] [Lithuanian](17675.md) authorities and European officials [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md), [Josep Borrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josep_Borrell) condemned the usage of migrants as a weapon and suggested Belarus could be subject to further sanctions.[^cite_note-221] In August 2021, Belarusian officials, wearing uniforms, riot shields and helmets, were recorded on camera near the [Belarus–Lithuania border](41004652.md) pushing and urging the migrants to cross the EU border.[^cite_note-222] Following the granting of humanitarian visas to Olympic athlete [Krystsina Tsimanouskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krystsina_Tsimanouskaya) and her husband, [Poland](22936.md) accused Belarus of organising hybrid warfare as the number of migrants crossing the [Belarus–Poland border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus%E2%80%93Poland_border) sharply increased compared to 2020.[^cite_note-223][^cite_note-224] Illegal migrants numbers exceeded the previous annual numbers in [Latvia](17514.md).[^cite_note-225] On 2 December 2021, the United States, EU, UK and [Canada](5042916.md) imposed new sanctions on Belarus.[^cite_note-226]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Belarus)



Change in per capita GDP of Belarus, 1973–2018. Figures are inflation-adjusted to 2011 international dollars.



Belarus regions by gross regional product (GRP)

Belarus is [a developing country](78449.md), but at 60th place in the United Nations' [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index), it has "very high" human development.[^cite_note-227] In 2019, the share of manufacturing in GDP was 31%. Manufacturing employed 35% of the workforce.[^cite_note-228] Manufacturing growth is much smaller than for the economy as a whole—about 2.2% in 2021. Important agricultural products include potatoes and cattle byproducts, including meat.[^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-229]

### Trade

Belarus has trade relations with over 180 countries. As of 2024, its main trading partners were Russia, which accounted for 66% of Belarus' international trade, and EU countries, with 11% of the country's trade.[^cite_note-230]

In April 2022, as a result of its facilitation of the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), the EU imposed trade sanctions on Belarus.[^cite_note-231] The sanctions were extended and expanded in August 2023.[^cite_note-232] These sanctions are in addition to those imposed following the rigged 2020 "election" of Lukashenko.[^cite_note-233]

At the time of the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, Belarus was one of the world's most industrially developed states by proportion of GDP and the richest CIS member-state.[^cite_note-234] In 2015, 39.3% of Belarusians were employed by state-controlled companies, 57.2% by private companies (in which the government has a 21.1% stake) and 3.5% by foreign companies.\[^cite_note-[www.belstat.gov.by-2-235](http://www.belstat.gov.by-2-235/)] In 1994, Belarus's main exports included heavy machinery (especially [tractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus_\(tractor\))), agricultural products, and energy products.[^cite_note-236] Economically, Belarus involved itself in the [Commonwealth of Independent States](36870.md) (CIS), [Eurasian Economic Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Economic_Community), and [Union with Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_State).[^cite_note-237] In the 1990s, industrial production plunged due to decreases in imports, investment, and demand for Belarusian products from its trading partners.[^cite_note-238] GDP only began to rise in 1996;[^cite_note-239] the country was the fastest-recovering former Soviet republic in the terms of its economy.[^cite_note-240] In 2006, GDP amounted to US\$83.1 billion in [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) (PPP) dollars (estimate), or about \$8,100 per capita.[^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-229] In 2005, GDP increased by 9.9%; the inflation rate averaged 9.5%.[^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-229] Belarus was ranked 85th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2024,[^cite_note-241] and remained in the same rank in 2025.[^cite_note-242]

Since the dissolution of the Soviet Union, under Lukashenko's leadership, Belarus has maintained government control of key industries and eschewed the large-scale privatisations seen in other former Soviet republics.[^cite_note-243]

Belarus applied to become a member of the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) in 1993.[^cite_note-244] Due to its failure to protect labour rights, including passing laws forbidding unemployment or working outside state-controlled sectors,[^cite_note-245] Belarus lost its EU [Generalized System of Preferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_System_of_Preferences) status on 21 June 2007, which raised tariff rates to their prior [most favoured nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_favoured_nation) levels.[^cite_note-246]

### Employment

The labour force consists of more than 4 million people, of whom women are slightly more than men.\[^cite_note-[www.belstat.gov.by-2-235](http://www.belstat.gov.by-2-235/)] In 2005, nearly a quarter of the population was employed in industrial factories. Employment is also high in agriculture, manufacturing sales, trading goods, and education. The unemployment rate was 1.5% in 2005, according to government statistics. There were 679,000 unemployed Belarusians, of whom two-thirds were women. The unemployment rate has been declining since 2003, and the overall rate of employment is the highest since statistics were first compiled in 1995.\[^cite_note-[www.belstat.gov.by-2-235](http://www.belstat.gov.by-2-235/)]

### Currency



Belarusian annual [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) and [CPI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPI) rates 2001–2013

The currency of Belarus is the [Belarusian ruble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_ruble). The currency was introduced in May 1992 to replace the [Soviet ruble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_ruble) and it has undergone [redenomination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redenomination) twice since then. The first coins of the Republic of Belarus were issued on 27 December 1996.[^cite_note-247] The ruble was reintroduced with new values in 2000 and has been in use ever since.[^cite_note-248] In 2007, The [National Bank of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_the_Republic_of_Belarus) abandoned pegging the Belarusian ruble to the Russian ruble.[^cite_note-249] As part of the [Union of Russia and Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_State), the two states have discussed using a single currency analogous to the Euro. This led to a proposal that the Belarusian ruble be discontinued in favour of the [Russian ruble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_ruble) (RUB), starting as early as 1 January 2008.

On 23 May 2011, the ruble depreciated 56% against the United States dollar. The depreciation was even steeper on the black market and financial collapse seemed imminent as citizens rushed to exchange their rubles for dollars, euros, durable goods, and canned goods.[^cite_note-250] On 1 June 2011, Belarus requested an economic rescue package from the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md).[^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252] A new currency, the new Belarusian ruble ([ISO 4217](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_4217) code: BYN)[^cite_note-Currency-iso.org-253] was introduced in July 2016, replacing the [Belarusian ruble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_ruble) in a rate of 1\:10,000 (10,000 old ruble = 1 new ruble). From 1 July until 31 December 2016, the old and new currencies were in parallel circulation, and series 2000 notes and coins could be exchanged for series 2009 from 1 January 2017 to 31 December 2021.[^cite_note-Currency-iso.org-253] This [redenomination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redenomination) can be considered an effort to fight the high inflation rate.[^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255] On 6 October 2022, Lukashenko banned price increases, to combat food inflation.[^cite_note-256] In January 2023, Belarus legalised [copyright infringement](18948365.md) of [media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_media) and [intellectual property](14724.md) created by "unfriendly" foreign nations.[^cite_note-257]

The banking system of Belarus consists of two levels: the Central Bank (National Bank of the Republic of Belarus) and 25 commercial banks.[^cite_note-258]

Beltelecom owns all the backbone channels that linked to the Lattelecom, TEO LT, [Tata Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Communications) (former [Teleglobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleglobe)), Synterra, [Rostelecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostelecom), Transtelekom and MTS ISPs. ## Demographics

Main articles: [Demographics of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Belarus) and [Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians)

According to the 2019 census the population was 9.41 million\[^cite_note-[www.belstat.gov.by-261](http://www.belstat.gov.by-261/)] with ethnic [Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians) constituting 84.9% of Belarus's total population.\[^cite_note-[www.belstat.gov.by-261](http://www.belstat.gov.by-261/)] Minority groups include: [Russians](59510.md) (7.5%), [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) (3.1%), and [Ukrainians](145144.md) (1.7%).\[^cite_note-[www.belstat.gov.by-261](http://www.belstat.gov.by-261/)] Belarus has a population density of about 50 people per square kilometre (127 per sq mi); 70% of its total population is concentrated in urban areas.[^cite_note-262] [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk), the nation's capital and largest city, was home to 1,937,900 residents in 2015.[^cite_note-263] [Gomel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomel), with a population of 481,000, is the second-largest city and serves as the capital of the [Gomel Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomel_Region). Other large cities are [Mogilev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogilev) (365,100), [Vitebsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitebsk) (342,400), [Grodno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grodno) (314,800) and [Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest,_Belarus) (298,300).[^cite_note-264]

Like many other Eastern European countries, Belarus has a negative population growth rate and a negative natural growth rate. In 2007, Belarus's population declined by 0.41% and its [fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) was 1.22,[^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-3-265] well [below the replacement rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-replacement_fertility). However, its [net migration rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_migration_rate) is 0.38 per 1,000, indicating that Belarus experiences slightly more immigration than emigration, unlike most neighbouring countries which experience significant negative net migration. As of 2015, 69.9% of Belarus's population is aged 14 to 64; 15.5% is under 14, and 14.6% is 65 or older. Its population is also ageing; the median age of 30–34 is estimated to rise to between 60 and 64 in 2050.[^cite_note-266] There are about 0.87 males per female in Belarus.[^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-3-265] The average life expectancy is 72.15 (66.53 years for men and 78.1 years for women).[^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-3-265] Over 99% of Belarusians aged 15 and older are [literate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literate).[^cite_note-The_World_Factbook-3-265]&#x20;

### Languages

Main article: [Languages of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Belarus)



Bilingual Belarusian–Russian sign in Belarusian town [Rakaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakaw) in 2014

Belarus's two official languages are [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) and [Belarusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_language).[^cite_note-268] According to data published by the [National Statistical Committee of the Republic of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Statistical_Committee_of_the_Republic_of_Belarus), the [2019 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Belarus_census) recorded that 54.1% of the population described Belarusian as their "mother tongue" compared to 42.3% who described Russian in that way.[^cite_note-269] In addition, 71.4% described Russian and 26% described Belarusian as the "language normally spoken at home".[^cite_note-270] Minorities also speak [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language), [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) and [Eastern Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Yiddish).[^cite_note-271] Following the election of [Alexander Lukashenko](156589.md), plans to have most schools teach in Belarusian were scrapped and they continued mainly teaching in Russian.[^cite_note-272] The annual circulation of Belarusian-language literature also significantly decreased from 1990 to 2020.[^cite_note-273]

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Belarus)



[Saint Sophia Cathedral in Polotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sophia_Cathedral_in_Polotsk) is one of the oldest churches in Belarus. Its current style is an ideal example of [baroque architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_in_Poland) in the former [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth).

According to the census of November 2011, 58.9% of all Belarusians adhered to some kind of religion; out of those, [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy) made up about 82%: Eastern Orthodox in Belarus are mainly part of the [Belarusian Exarchate of the Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Exarchate_of_the_Russian_Orthodox_Church), though a small [Belarusian Autocephalous Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Autocephalous_Orthodox_Church) also exists.[^cite_note-Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_of_the_Republic_of_Belarus-2011-274] [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_Belarus) is practised mostly in the western regions, and there are also different denominations of Protestantism.[^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276] Minorities also practice [Greek Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Greek_Catholic_Church), [Judaism](15624.md), [Islam](6037917.md) and [neo-paganism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_paganism). Overall, 48.3% of the population is Orthodox Christian, 41.1% is not religious, 7.1% is Roman Catholic and 3.3% follows other religions.[^cite_note-Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_of_the_Republic_of_Belarus-2011-274]

Belarus's Catholic minority is concentrated in the western part of the country, especially around [Grodno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grodno), consisting of a mixture of Belarusians and the country's [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) and [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_people) minorities.[^cite_note-277] President Lukashenko has stated that Orthodox and Catholic believers are the "two main confessions in our country".[^cite_note-278]

Belarus was once a major centre of European Jews, with 10% of the population being [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Belarus). But since the mid-20th century, the number of Jews has been reduced by [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust), deportation, and emigration, so that today it is a very small minority of less than one per cent.[^cite_note-279] The [Lipka Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipka_Tatars), numbering over 15,000, are predominantly [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Belarus). According to Article 16 of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Belarus), Belarus has no official religion. While the [freedom of worship](46006.md) is granted in the same article, religious organisations deemed harmful to the government or social order can be prohibited.[^cite_note-Constitution_of_Belarus-2004a-171]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Belarus)

### Arts and literature



The [Opera and Ballet Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Opera_and_Ballet_of_Belarus) in Minsk

The Belarusian government sponsors annual cultural festivals such as the [Slavianski Bazaar in Vitebsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavianski_Bazaar_in_Vitebsk),[^cite_note-280] which showcases Belarusian performers, artists, writers, musicians, and actors. Several state holidays, such as [Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Belarus\)) and [Victory Day](337670.md), draw big crowds and often include displays such as fireworks and military parades, especially in Vitebsk and Minsk.[^cite_note-281] The government's Ministry of Culture finances events promoting Belarusian arts and culture both inside and outside the country.

Belarusian literature[^cite_note-282] began with 11th- to 13th-century religious scripture, such as the 12th-century poetry of [Cyril of Turaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_of_Turaw).[^cite_note-283]

By the 16th century, [Polotsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polotsk) resident [Francysk Skaryna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francysk_Skaryna) translated the Bible into Belarusian. It was published in [Prague](23844.md) and [Vilnius](32597.md) sometime between 1517 and 1525, making it the first book printed in Belarus or anywhere in Eastern Europe.[^cite_note-Belarus-284] The modern era of Belarusian literature began in the late 19th century; one prominent writer was [Yanka Kupala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanka_Kupala). Many Belarusian writers of the time, such as [Uładzimir Žyłka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U%C5%82adzimir_%C5%BDy%C5%82ka), Kazimir Svayak, [Yakub Kolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakub_Kolas), [Źmitrok Biadula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%B9mitrok_Biadula), and [Maksim Haretski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maksim_Haretski), wrote for *[Nasha Niva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasha_Niva)*, a Belarusian-language paper published that was previously published in Vilnius but now is published in Minsk.[^cite_note-285]

After Belarus was incorporated into the Soviet Union, the Soviet government took control of the Republic's cultural affairs. At first, a policy of "Belarusianization" was followed in the newly formed Byelorussian SSR. This policy was reversed in the 1930s, and the majority of prominent Belarusian intellectuals and nationalist advocates were either exiled or killed in Stalinist purges.[^cite_note-286] The free development of literature occurred only in Polish-held territory until Soviet occupation in 1939. Several poets and authors went into exile after the Nazi occupation of Belarus and would not return until the 1960s.[^cite_note-Belarus-284]



Poet and librettist [Vintsent Dunin-Martsinkyevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vintsent_Dunin-Martsinkyevich)

The last major revival of Belarusian literature occurred in the 1960s with novels published by [Vasil Bykaŭ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasil_Byka%C5%AD) and [Uladzimir Karatkievich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uladzimir_Karatkievich). An influential author who examined the catastrophes the country has suffered was [Ales Adamovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ales_Adamovich). He was named by [Svetlana Alexievich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svetlana_Alexievich), the Belarusian winner of the [Nobel Prize in Literature](23385442.md) 2015, as "her main teacher, who helped her to find a path of her own".[^cite_note-287]

[Music in Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Belarus) largely comprises a rich tradition of folk and religious music. The country's folk music traditions can be traced back to the times of the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania). In the 19th century, Polish composer [Stanisław Moniuszko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanis%C5%82aw_Moniuszko) composed operas and chamber music pieces while living in Minsk. During his stay, he worked with Belarusian poet [Vintsent Dunin-Martsinkyevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vintsent_Dunin-Martsinkyevich) and created the opera *Sialanka* (*Peasant Woman*). At the end of the 19th century, major Belarusian cities formed their own opera and ballet companies. The ballet *[Nightingale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightingale_\(ballet\))* by M. Kroshner was composed during the Soviet era and became the first Belarusian ballet showcased at the National Academic Vialiki Ballet Theatre in Minsk.[^cite_note-288]

After the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), music focused on the hardships of the Belarusian people or on those who took up arms in defence of the homeland. During this period, [Anatoly Bogatyrev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Bogatyrev), creator of the opera *In Polesye Virgin Forest*, served as the "tutor" of Belarusian composers.[^cite_note-Belarusguide.com-289] The National Academic Theatre of Ballet in Minsk was awarded the [Benois de la Dance Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prix_Benois_de_la_Danse) in 1996 as the top ballet company in the world.[^cite_note-Belarusguide.com-289] Rock music has become increasingly popular in recent years, though the Belarusian government has attempted to limit the amount of foreign music aired on the radio in favour of traditional Belarusian music. Since 2004, Belarus has been sending artists to the [Eurovision Song Contest](9954.md).[^cite_note-290][^cite_note-291]

[Marc Chagall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Chagall) was born in Liozna (near [Vitebsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitebsk)) in 1887. He spent the [World War I](4764461.md) years in Soviet Belarus, becoming one of the country's most distinguished artists and a member of the modernist [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde) and was a founder of the Vitebsk Arts College.[^cite_note-292][^cite_note-293]

### Dress

The traditional Belarusian dress originates from the [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') period. Due to the cool climate, clothes were designed to conserve body heat and were usually made from [flax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flax) or [wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool). They were decorated with ornate patterns influenced by the neighbouring cultures: Poles, Lithuanians, Latvians, Russians, and other European nations. Each region of Belarus has developed specific design patterns.[^cite_note-294] One ornamental pattern common in early dresses currently decorates the hoist of the [Belarusian national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Belarus), adopted in a [disputed referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Belarusian_referendum) in 1995.[^cite_note-295]

### Cuisine



[Draniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato_pancake), the national dish

[Belarusian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_cuisine) consists mainly of vegetables, meat (particularly pork), and bread. Foods are usually either slowly cooked or [stewed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewed). Typically, Belarusians eat a light breakfast and two hearty meals later in the day. [Wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) and [rye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye) bread are consumed in Belarus, but rye is more plentiful because conditions are too harsh for growing wheat. To show hospitality, a host traditionally presents an offering of [bread and salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread_and_salt) when greeting a guest or visitor.[^cite_note-296]

### Sport

See also: [Belarus at the Olympics](1927547.md)

Belarus has competed in the Olympic Games since the [1994 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Winter_Olympics) as an independent nation. Receiving heavy sponsorship from the government, [ice hockey](14790.md) is the nation's second most popular sport after [football](10568.md). The [national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus_national_football_team) has never qualified for a major tournament; however, [BATE Borisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_BATE_Borisov) has played in the [Champions League](44220.md). The [national hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus_national_men's_ice_hockey_team) finished fourth at the [2002 Salt Lake City Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2002_Winter_Olympics) following an upset win over Sweden in the quarterfinals and regularly competes in the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Hockey_World_Championships), often making the quarterfinals. Belarusian players are present in the [Kontinental Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kontinental_Hockey_League) in Eurasia, particularly for Belarusian club [HC Dinamo Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HC_Dinamo_Minsk), and several have played in the [National Hockey League](21809.md) in North America. The [2014 IIHF World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_IIHF_World_Championship) was hosted in Belarus and the [2021 IIHF World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_IIHF_World_Championship) was supposed to be co-hosted in Latvia and Belarus, but was cancelled due to widespread protests and security concerns. The [2021 UEC European Track Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEC_European_Track_Championships) in cycling was also cancelled because Belarus was not considered a safe host.



[Victoria Azarenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka), professional [tennis](29773.md) player and a former [world No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_players) in singles

[Darya Domracheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darya_Domracheva) is a leading [biathlete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biathlon) whose honours include three gold medals at the [2014 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Winter_Olympics).[^cite_note-297] [Tennis](29773.md) player [Victoria Azarenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka) became the first Belarusian to win a [Grand Slam](197638.md) singles title at the [Australian Open](97851.md) in 2012.[^cite_note-298] She won the gold medal in mixed doubles at the [2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics) with [Max Mirnyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Mirnyi), who holds ten Grand Slam titles in [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singles_tennis).

Belarus, either by itself or via the Soviet programme, has produced a number of highly-accomplished [gymnasts](231482.md) such as triple Olympic champion (1972) [Olga Korbut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Korbut), double Olympic champion (1988) and 1989 World All-Around Champion [Svetlana Boguinskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svetlana_Boguinskaya), two-time World All-Around Champion (1994, 1997) [Ivan Ivankov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Ivankov), and most notably, 6-time Olympic Champion (1992) [Vitaly Scherbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaly_Scherbo) who holds a very special place in gymnastics history. As of at least as recently as after the 2008 Beijing Summer Olympics, Scherbo is the only male gymnast in the 100+ year history of the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artistic_Gymnastics_World_Championships) to be a World Champion on all 8 events.[^cite_note-299]

Other notable Belarusian sportspeople include cyclist [Vasil Kiryienka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasil_Kiryienka), who won the [2015 Road World Time Trial Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_UCI_Road_World_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_time_trial), and middle-distance runner [Maryna Arzamasava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryna_Arzamasava), who won the gold medal in the [800m at the 2015 World Championships in Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Women's_800_metres). [Andrei Arlovski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Arlovski), who was born in [Babruysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babruysk), [Byelorussian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byelorussian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), is a current [UFC](169660.md) fighter and the former [UFC heavyweight champion of the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_UFC_champions). [Aryna Sabalenka](50222577.md) is a noted women's tennis player, currently (Nov 2025) world No. 1.

Belarus is known for its strong rhythmic gymnasts. Gymnasts include [Inna Zhukova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inna_Zhukova), who earned silver at the [2008 Beijing Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Beijing_Olympics), [Liubov Charkashyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liubov_Charkashyna), who earned bronze at the 2012 London Olympics, and [Melitina Staniouta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melitina_Staniouta), Bronze All-Around Medalist of the 2015 World Championships. The Belarusian senior group earned bronze at the [2012 London Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_London_Olympics).

### World Heritage Sites

Belarus has four [UNESCO](21786641.md)-designated [World Heritage Sites](44940.md): the [Mir Castle Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_Castle_Complex), the [Nesvizh Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nesvizh_Castle), the [Belovezhskaya Pushcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bia%C5%82owie%C5%BCa_Forest) (shared with [Poland](22936.md)), and the [Struve Geodetic Arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Struve_Geodetic_Arc) (shared with nine other countries).[^cite_note-300]
