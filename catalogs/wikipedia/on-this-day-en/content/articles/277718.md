**Jeddah**[^cite_note-5] is the largest city in [Mecca Province](468731.md), the [second-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Saudi_Arabia) in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) after [Riyadh](51554.md), and the country’s main commercial center. It is in the [Hejaz region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz) on the [Red Sea](26191.md) coast. The city’s metropolitan area is coterminous with [Jeddah Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeddah_Governorate?action=edit\&redlink=1). Its prominence grew in 647 CE when the [Caliph Uthman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uthman) of the [Rashidun Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate) designated it as the main port serving travelers to [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

Jeddah is the largest city in [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz) and the [ninth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_metropolitan_areas_in_the_Middle_East) in the [Middle East](19323.md). It also serves as the administrative center of the [OIC](47725.md). [Jeddah Islamic Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeddah_Islamic_Port), on the [Red Sea](26191.md), is the thirty-sixth largest seaport in the world[^cite_note-8] and the second-largest and second-busiest seaport in the [Middle East](19323.md).

Jeddah is the principal gateway to [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca), the [holiest city in Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiest_sites_in_Islam), 65 kilometers (40 mi) to the east, while [Medina](36636.md), the second-holiest city, is 360 kilometers (220 mi) to the north. Economically, Jeddah is focusing on further developing capital investment in scientific and engineering leadership within Saudi Arabia, and the Middle East.[^cite_note-9] Jeddah was ranked fourth in the [Africa](5334607.md), MiddIe East, and [-stan countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/-stan#Countries) region in the Innovation Cities Index in 2009.[^cite_note-10]

## Etymology and spelling

There are at least two [etymologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology) of *Jeddah*, according to Jeddah Ibn Al-Qudaa'iy, the chief of the Quda'a clan. The more common account has it that the name is derived from جدة *Jaddah*, the [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) word for "grandmother". According to folk belief, the [Tomb of Eve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Eve), who is considered the grandmother of humanity, is located in Jeddah.[^cite_note-11]

The [Maghrebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghrebis) traveler [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta) visited Jeddah during his world trip around 1330. He wrote the name of the city into his diary as "Jiddah".[^cite_note-12]

The British [Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office](59653.md) and other branches of the British government formerly used the older spelling of "Jedda", contrary to other English-speaking usages, but in 2007, it changed to the spelling "Jeddah".[^cite_note-13]

[T. E. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._E._Lawrence) felt that any transcription of Arabic names into English was arbitrary. In his book *Revolt in the Desert*, Jeddah is spelled in three different ways on the first page alone.[^cite_note-14]

On official Saudi maps and documents, the city name is transcribed "Jeddah", which is now the prevailing usage.

## History

### Pre-Islam

Traces of early activity in the area are attested by [Thamudic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thamudic) inscriptions excavated in Wadi Briman[^cite_note-15] (وادي بريمان), east of the city, and [Wadi Boweb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Boweb?action=edit\&redlink=1) (وادي بويب), northwest of the city. The oldest [mashrabiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashrabiya) found in Jeddah dates back to the [pre-Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Islamic_Arabia) era.[^cite_note-16]

Some believe that Jeddah was inhabited before [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), who led a naval expedition to the Red Sea, by fishermen who considered it a center from which they sailed, as well as a place for rest and well-being.[^cite_note-17] According to the Ministry of Hajj, Jeddah has been settled for more than 2,500 years.[^cite_note-18]

Excavations in the [old city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Balad,_Jeddah) have been interpreted to give the fact that Jeddah was founded as a fishing hamlet by the Yemeni [Quda'a tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quda'a) (Arabic: بني قضاعة), who left to settle in [Makkah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) (Mecca) after the collapse of [Marib Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marib_Dam) in Yemen in 115 BC.[^cite_note-19]

### Under the Caliphates

Jeddah first achieved prominence around A.D. 647, when the third Muslim [Caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph), [Uthman Ibn Affan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uthman_Ibn_Affan), turned it into a port making it the port of Makkah instead of Al Shoaib port, which was southwest of Makkah.[^cite_note-20]

The [Umayyads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) inherited the entire [Rashidun Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate) including [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz) and ruled from 661 to 750. In 702, Jeddah was briefly occupied by pirates from the [Kingdom of Axum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Axum).[^cite_note-21] However, Jeddah remained a key civilian harbor, serving fishermen and pilgrims travelling by sea for the [Hajj](23487999.md). It is also believed that the [Sharifdom of Makkah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_of_Mecca), an honorary Viceroy to the holy land, was first started in this period of the Islamic Caliphate.[^cite_note-22] Jeddah has been established as the main city of the historic Hijaz province and a historic port for pilgrims arriving by sea to perform their Hajj pilgrimage in Mecca.[^cite_note-23]

In 750 in the [Abbasid Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Revolution), the [Abbasids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate) successfully took control of almost the whole Umayyad Empire, excluding [Morocco (Maghrib)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barghawata) and [Spain (Al-Andalus)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_C%C3%B3rdoba).[^cite_note-24] From 876, Jeddah and the surrounding area became the object of wars between the Abbasids and the [Tulunids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulunids) of Egypt, who at one point gained control of the emirates of [Egypt](8087628.md), [Syria](7515849.md), [Jordan](7515964.md) and [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz). The power struggle between the Tulunid Governors and the Abbasids over Hejaz lasted for nearly twenty-five years, until the Tulunids finally withdrew from [Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia) in 900.[^cite_note-25]

In 930 AD, the main Hejazi cities of [Medina](36636.md), [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) and [Taif](201969.md) were heavily sacked by the [Qarmatians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qarmatians). It is probable, though not historically confirmed, that Jeddah itself was attacked.

Soon after, in early 935, the [Ikhshidids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikhshidid_dynasty), the new power in Egypt, took control of the Hejaz region. There are no historical records that detail the Ikhshidid rule of Hejaz. At this point in time, Jeddah was still unfortified and without walls.

### The Fatimids, Ayyubids, and Mamluks



*[Tabula Rogeriana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabula_Rogeriana)*, a reproduction of one of the most advanced [early world maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_world_maps), by [Muhammad al-Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_al-Idrisi) produced in 1154 shows the location of Jeddah. This is a [south-up map orientation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-up_map_orientation).

In 969 AD, the [Fatimids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimids) from Algeria took control in Egypt from the [Ikhshidid Governors of Abbasids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikhshidid_dynasty) and expanded their empire to the surrounding regions, including The Hijaz and Jeddah. The Fatimids developed an extensive trade network in both the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) and the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) through the [Red Sea](26191.md). Their trade and diplomatic ties extended all the way to China and its [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty), which eventually determined the economic course of [Tihamah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tihamah) during the [High Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Middle_Ages).[^cite_note-EB-2024-26][^cite_note-27]

After [Saladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saladin)'s conquest of Jerusalem in 1171, he proclaimed himself sultan of Egypt, after dissolving the [Fatimid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_Caliphate) upon the death of [al-Adid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Adid), thus establishing the [Ayyubid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty). Ayyubid conquests in Hejaz included Jeddah, which joined the Ayyubid dynasty in 1177 during the leadership of [Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif) Ibn Abul-Hashim Al-Thalab (1094–1201). During their relatively short-lived tenure, the Ayyubids ushered in an era of economic prosperity in the lands they ruled and the facilities and patronage provided by the Ayyubids led to a resurgence in intellectual activity in the [Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world). This period was also marked by an Ayyubid process of vigorously strengthening [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim) dominance in the region by constructing numerous *[madrasas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasas)* (Islamic schools) in their major cities. Jeddah attracted Muslim sailors and merchants from [Sindh](28972.md), [Southeast Asia](28741.md) and [East Africa](260987.md), and other distant regions.[^cite_note-EB-2024-26]

In the year 1258, after the fall of Baghdad, the capital of the Abbasid Empire, to the Mongols, [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz) became a part of the [Mamluk Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk).[^cite_note-28]

The Portuguese explorer [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama), having found his way around the [Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope) and obtaining pilots from the coast of [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar) in AD 1497, pushed his way across the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) to the shores of [Malabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamorin) and [Calicut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Kozhikode), attacked fleets that carried freight and Muslim pilgrims from India to the [Red Sea](26191.md), and struck terror into the surrounding potentates. The Princes of [Gujarat](53707.md) and [Yemen](350939.md) turned for help to [Egypt](8087628.md). Sultan [Al-Ashraf Qansuh al-Ghawri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ashraf_Qansuh_al-Ghawri) accordingly fitted out a fleet of 50 vessels under the Governor of Jeddah, [Hussein the Kurd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Husain_Al-Kurdi) (aka. Mirocem). Jeddah was soon fortified with a wall, using forced labor, as a harbor of refuge from the [Portuguese](23033.md), allowing [Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia) and the Red Sea to be protected.

### Ottoman Empire

Main articles: [Hejaz Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz_Vilayet) and [Attack on Jeddah (1541)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Jeddah_\(1541\))



[Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) attack in 1517 by Gaspar Correia (c. 1496–1563) - originally from *Lendas da India* by [Gaspar Correia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspar_Correia).

In 1517, the [Ottoman Turks](22278.md) conquered the Mamluk Sultanate in Egypt and Syria during the reign of [Selim I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selim_I).[^cite_note-History_of_Arabia-29]

The Ottomans rebuilt the weak walls of Jeddah in 1525 following the defense of the city against the [Lopo Soares de Albergaria's Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lopo_Soares_de_Albergaria) at the [Siege of Jeddah (1517)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jeddah_\(1517\)). The new stone wall included six [watchtowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchtower) and six [city gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_gate). They were constructed to defend against the Portuguese attack. Of the six gates, the Gate of Mecca was the eastern gate and the Gate of [Al-Magharibah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb), facing the port, was the western gate. The Gate of Sharif faces south. The other gates were the Gate of Al-Bunt, Gate of Al-Sham (also called Gate of Al-Sharaf), and Gate of [Medina](36636.md), facing north.[^cite_note-30] The Turks also built [The Qishla of Jeddah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Qishla_of_Jeddah), a small castle for the city's soldiers. In the 19th century, these seven gates were minimized into four giant gates with four towers. These giant gates were the Gate of Sham to the north, the Gate of Mecca to the east, the Gate of Sharif to the south, and the Gate of Al-Magharibah on the seaside.

Jeddah became a direct Ottoman [Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyalet), while the remaining [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz) under Sharif Barakat II became a vassal state to the Ottoman Empire eight years after the Siege of Jeddah in 1517. The Portuguese attempted to attack the port again in 1541, but were [repelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Jeddah_\(1541\)).

Parts of the city wall still survive today in the [old city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Balad,_Jeddah). Even though the Portuguese were successfully repelled from the city, fleets in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) were at their mercy. This was evidenced by the [Battle of Diu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Diu_\(1531\)). The Portuguese soldiers' cemetery can still be found within the old city today and is referred to as the site of the [Christian Graves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non%E2%80%93Muslim_Cemetery).[^cite_note-31]

[Ahmed Al-Jazzar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Al-Jazzar), the Ottoman military man mainly known for his role in the [Siege of Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Acre_\(1799\)), spent the earlier part of his career at Jeddah. In Jeddah in 1750, he killed some seventy rioting nomads in retaliation for the killing of his commander, Abdullah Beg, earning him the nickname "Jezzar" (butcher).

On 15 June 1858, rioting in the city, believed to have been instigated by a former police chief in reaction to British policy in the [Red Sea](26191.md), led to the [massacre of 25 Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeddah_massacre_of_1858), including the British and French consuls, members of their families, and wealthy Greek merchants.[^cite_note-32] The British frigate [HMS *Cyclops*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Cyclops_\(1839\)), anchored at the port, bombarded the city for two days in retaliation.[^cite_note-33]

### First Saudi State and Ottoman–Saudi War

Main article: [Ottoman-Saudi War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman-Saudi_War)

In 1802, [Nejdi forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Saudi_State) conquered both Mecca and Jeddah from the Ottomans. When Sharif [Ghalib Efendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghalib_Efendi) informed [Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan) [Mahmud II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_II) of this, the Sultan ordered his [Egyptian](8087628.md) viceroy [Muhammad Ali Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_of_Egypt) to retake the city. Muhammad Ali successfully regained the city in the [Battle of Jeddah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jeddah_\(1813\)) in 1813.

### World War I and the Hashemite Kingdom

Main articles: [Arab Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Revolt) and [Kingdom of Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hejaz)



Mohammed Abu Zenada, one of the Chiefs of Jeddah and the advisor to the [Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_of_Hejaz) during the surrender to [King Abdulaziz Ibn Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Saud_of_Saudi_Arabia) in 1925.

During [World War I](4764461.md), Sharif [Hussein bin Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_bin_Ali,_Sharif_of_Mecca) declared a revolt against the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), seeking independence from the Ottoman Turks and the creation of a single unified [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) state spanning from [Aleppo](159244.md) in [Syria](7515849.md) to [Aden](38050.md) in [Yemen](350939.md).

King Hussein declared the [Kingdom of Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hejaz). Later, Hussein was involved in a war with [Ibn Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Saud_of_Saudi_Arabia), who was the Sultan of [Nejd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nejd). Hussein abdicated following the fall of [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca), in December 1924, and his son [Ali bin Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_of_Hejaz) became the new king.

### Kingdom of Saudi Arabia



[King Abdulaziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Saud) sitting with Abdullah Ali Reda on the day he entered Jeddah in 1925

A few months later, [Ibn Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Saud_of_Saudi_Arabia), whose clan originated in the central [Nejd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nejd) province, conquered [Medina](36636.md) and Jeddah via an agreement with Jeddans following the [Second Battle of Jeddah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jeddah_\(1925\)). He deposed [Ali bin Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_of_Hejaz), who fled to [Baghdad](4492.md), eventually settling in [Amman](45007.md), [Jordan](7515964.md), where his descendants became part of its [Hashemite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashemite) royalty.

As a result, Jeddah came under the sway of the [Al-Saud dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Saud) in December 1925. In 1926, Ibn Saud added the title King of Hejaz to his position of Sultan of Nejd.[^cite_note-34] Today, Jeddah has lost its historical role in peninsular politics after it fell within the new province of [Makkah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makkah_Province), whose provincial capital is the city of [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca).

From 1928 to 1932, the new Khuzam Palace was built as the new residence of King Abdul Aziz in Jeddah. The palace lies south of the old walled city and was constructed under the supervision of the engineer [Mohammed bin Awad bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_bin_Awad_bin_Laden). After 1963, the palace was used as a royal guesthouse; since 1995, it has housed the Regional Museum of Archaeology and Ethnography.[^cite_note-35]

The remaining walls and gates of the old city were demolished in 1947. In 1939, a [military airstrip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiddah_International_Airport_\(1981\)) was built in Jeddah, serving as the first airport in Jeddah. It was upgraded into the headquarters of the RSAF by the 1950s. On 14 October 1952, Jeddah Airport was officially inaugurated and opened under the patronage of His Royal Highness Prince Saud Bin Abdulaziz. A fire in 1982 destroyed some ancient buildings in the old town center, called [Al-Balad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Balad,_Jeddah), but much is still preserved. A house-by-house survey of the old districts was made in 1979, showing that some 1000 traditional buildings still existed, though the number of structures with great historic value was far less. In 1990, a Jeddah Historical Area Preservation Department was founded.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

The modern city has expanded wildly beyond its old boundaries. The built-up area expanded mainly to the north along the Red Sea coastline, reaching the new airport during the 1990s and since edging its way around it toward the [Obhur Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obhur_Creek), some 27 km (17 mi) from the old city center.[^cite_note-38]

Main article: [Demolitions in Jeddah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolitions_in_Jeddah)

In October 2021, Saudi authorities, led by [Mohammad bin Salman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_bin_Salman), initiated a [large-scale demolition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolitions_in_Jeddah) and eviction plan in neighborhoods in the southern part of Jeddah to make way for the [Jeddah Central Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeddah_Central), a revitalization project under [Saudi Vision 2030](50299103.md).[^cite_note-39] The demolitions affected 558,000 people in more than 60 neighborhoods. [Amnesty International](18947898.md) confirmed through official documents that some of the residents were notified about evictions only 24 hours before, while others were between 1–6 weeks. In some cases "evacuate" was written on the buildings, while the state media and billboards informed others about the demolitions to others.[^cite_note-AI-22-06-2022-40] Saudi state media claimed the majority of affected neighborhoods were "rife with diseases, crime, drugs and theft" and home to predominantly [undocumented immigrants](7124412.md).[^cite_note-41] In January 2022, Saudi authorities announced a compensation scheme that accounted for 47% of those evicted.[^cite_note-42]

## Geography



Map of Jeddah from OpenStreetMap

Jeddah is located in Saudi Arabia's Red Sea [coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_plain) (called [Tihamah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tihamah)). Jeddah lies in the Hijazi Tihama (تهامة الحجاز) region which is in the lower [Hijaz mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hijaz_mountains). Historically, politically and culturally, Jeddah was a major city of [Hejaz Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz_Vilayet), the [Kingdom of Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hejaz) and other regional political entities according to Hijazi history books. It is the 100th largest city in the world by land area.

### Climate

Jeddah features an [arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid_climate) (*BWh*) under [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), with a tropical temperature range. Unlike other Saudi Arabian cities, Jeddah retains its warm temperatures in winter, which can range from 15 °C (59 °F) at dawn to 28 °C (82 °F) in the afternoon. Summer temperatures are extremely hot, often breaking the 40 °C (104 °F) mark in the afternoon. Summers are also quite steamy, with dew points often exceeding 27 °C (80 °F), particularly in September.

Rainfall in Jeddah is generally sparse, and usually occurs in small amounts in November and December. Heavy thunderstorms are common in winter. The thunderstorm of December 2008 was the largest in recent memory, with rain reaching around 80 mm (3 in). The lowest temperature ever recorded in Jeddah was 9.8 °C (49.6 °F) on 10 February 1993.[^cite_note-NOAA-43] The highest temperature ever recorded in Jeddah was 52.0 °C (125.6 °F) on 22 June 2010,[^cite_note-NOAA-43] which is the highest temperature to have ever been recorded in Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-wu-44][^cite_note-45]

[Dust storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_storm) happen in summer and sometimes in winter, coming from the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md)'s deserts or from [North Africa](21714.md).[^cite_note-46] Occasionally, the dust storms accompany thunderstorms.

## Economy



Mall of Arabia is the largest shopping mall in Jeddah.

Jeddah has long been a port city. Even before being designated the port city for Mecca, Jeddah was a trading hub for the region. In the 19th century, goods such as mother-of-pearl, tortoise shells, frankincense, and spices were routinely exported from the city. Apart from this, many imports into the city were destined for further transit to the Suez, Africa, or Europe. Many goods passing through Jeddah could not be normally found in the city or even in Arabia.[^cite_note-52]

All of the capitals of the [Middle East](19323.md) and North Africa are within two hours flying distance of Jeddah, making it the second commercial center of the Middle East after Dubai.[^cite_note-53]

Also, Jeddah's industrial district is the fourth largest in Saudi Arabia after [Riyadh](51554.md), [Jubail](632008.md) and [Yanbu](629638.md).

### King Abdullah Street



King Abdullah Street

King Abdullah Street is one of the most important streets in Jeddah and runs from King Fahd Road by the waterfront in the west of Jeddah to the eastern end of the city. It is famous for hosting numerous corporate offices and commercial developments. It will be near the HSR Entrance in Jeddah central train station which connects Jeddah with Makkah, Al-Madinah, and King Abdullah Economic City (KAEC). It also has the second tallest flagpole in the world at a height of 170 m (558 ft). This road faced a catastrophe in 2011 when it was submerged in rainwater.[^cite_note-54]

### Tahliyah Street

Main article: [Tahliya Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahliah_Street_\(Jeddah\))



Galleria, a hotel on Tahliyah Street

Tahliyah Street (EXT. Prince Mohamed Bin Abdulaziz St) is an important fashion and shopping street in central Jeddah. It contains many upscale department and high fashion brands stores as well as boutiques. It has been renamed "Prince Mohammad bin Abdul Aziz Road" by the government, but this official name is not widely used. It also has many [fine dining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_dining) options.

### Madinah Road

[Madinah](36636.md) Road is a historically significant street in Jeddah. It links the Southern districts with the North and contains the main offices of several companies and showrooms. The northern end of the road links to the [King Abdul Aziz Int'l Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdulaziz_International_Airport), which is a contributing factor to heavy traffic on this road at most times during the day.

## Culture

### Religious significance



A woman from Jeddah in traditional clothing, 1873

Most citizens are [Sunni Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam). The government, courts, and civil and criminal laws enforce a moral code established by Shari'ah. A very small minority of Saudi citizens are [Shia Muslims](26961.md), and there is also a large foreign workforce, mainly from countries like the Philippines and Pakistan.

The city has over 1,300 [mosques](19894.md).[^cite_note-55] The law does not allow other religions' buildings, books, icons, and expressions of faith. However, private religious observance not involving Muslims nor offending public order and morality is tolerated.

Since the 7th century, Jeddah has hosted millions of Muslim pilgrims from all over the world on their way to the [Hajj](23487999.md).[^cite_note-56] This merge with pilgrims has a major impact on the society, religion, and economy of Jeddah.

### Cuisine



Hejazi [Saleeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saleeg)

Jeddah's multi-ethnic citizenry has influenced Jeddah's traditional cuisine.

Some dishes are native to the Hejaz, like [Saleeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saleeg). Mabshūr (مَبْشُور) is a white-rice dish, cooked in broth, often made with chicken instead of lamb meat. Jeddah cuisine is popular as well, with dishes like Foul, Shorabah Hareira (Hareira soup), Mugalgal, Madhbi (chicken grilled on stone), Madfun (literally meaning "buried"), Magloobah, Kibdah, Manzalah (usually eaten at [Eid ul-Fitr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_ul-Fitr)), Magliya (a local version of [falafel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falafel)) and Saiyadyia able to be acquired in many traditional restaurants around the city.

Some dishes popular in the city were imported from other Saudi regions, like [Kabsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabsa) from Najd and Arīka (عَريكة) and Ma'sūb (مَعْصُوب) from the southern Saudi region. Other dishes were imported from other cultures through Saudis of different origins, like [Mantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manti_\(food\)), Yaghmush (يَغْمُش) and [Ruz Bukhāri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilaf) (رُز بُخاري) from Central Asia, [Burēk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B6rek), [Šurēk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsoureki), and Kabab Almīru (كباب الميرو) from Turkey and the Balkans, [Mandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandi_\(food\)) from Yemen, [Mutabbag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtabak) (مُطَبَّق) from Yemen-Malaysia, [Biryāni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biry%C4%81ni) and Kābli (كابلي) rice dishes from [South Asia](21566765.md).

The most popular local fast-food chain, begun in 1974, is [Al Baik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albaik), with branches in Jeddah and the neighboring cities of Makkah, Madinah and Yanbu. Their main dish is broasted (broiled and roasted) chicken, commonly known by Jeddawis as "Broast", and a variety of [seafood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seafood). The popular fast food chain recently started opening branches internationally including Bahrain, and Dubai in the UAE.[^cite_note-57]

### Open-air art



The bicycle roundabout is one of the most iconic open-air art installations in Jeddah.



Copper statue *Hope for the Right Path* from Belgian artist [Hubert Minnebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Minnebo) at the Sword Roundabout ([Tahlia Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahlia_Street))

During the oil boom in the late 1970s and 1980s, there was a focused civic effort led by Mohamed Said Farsi, who was then the city's mayor,[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] to bring art to Jeddah's [public areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_space). As a result, Jeddah contains a large number of modern open-air sculptures and works of art, typically situated in [roundabouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundabout). Sculptures include works by [Jean/Hans Arp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean/Hans_Arp), [César Baldaccini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Baldaccini), [Alexander Calder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Calder), [Henry Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Moore), [Joan Miró](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Mir%C3%B3), [Hubert Minnebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Minnebo) and [Victor Vasarely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Vasarely). They often depict traditional Saudi items such as coffee pots, incense burners, palm trees, etc. Islamic tradition prohibits the depiction of living creatures, especially the human form, and this has affected the art. Other statues include a giant geometry set, a giant bicycle, a huge block of concrete with several cars protruding from it at odd angles, and a monumental sculpture by [Aref Rayess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aref_Rayess) called "Swords of God (Soyuf Allah)". At the interchange between Al-Madinah Road, King Abdulaziz Road, and Prince Abullah Al-Faisal Road, there are large sculptures of camels that are the center of a roundabout. The roundabout is commonly known as 'The Camel Roundabout'.[^cite_note-60]

### Museums and collections

There are about a dozen museums or collections in Jeddah, with varied educational aims and professionalism.[^cite_note-61] These include the [Jeddah Regional Museum of Archaeology and Ethnography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeddah_Regional_Museum_of_Archaeology_and_Ethnography) run by the Deputy Ministry of Antiquities and Museums, the Jeddah Municipal Museum, the [Nasseef House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasseef_House), the [Humane Heritage Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humane_Heritage_Museum), the private Abdul Rauf Hasan Khalil Museum and the private Arts Heritage Museum.

#### Jeddah Season

Jeddah Season is a part of the Saudi government's Saudi Seasons initiative that aims at launching high-level tourism activities in Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-65] The first version of the season was held in June–July 2019.[^cite_note-Fareed-2019-66] Around 150 activities and events were organized in five destinations in Jeddah.[^cite_note-Fareed-2019-66] Saudi Seasons 2019 aimed at shedding light on the diverse Saudi culture and heritage.[^cite_note-67] Jeddah was chosen because it is one of the most culturally-rich Saudi cities, with a history that spans over 3,000 years.[^cite_note-Fareed-2019-66] Most of Jeddah Season's events and activities were held at [King Abdullah Sports City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdullah_Sports_City), Jeddah's historical area, Al-Hamra Corniche, and the Jeddah Waterfront.[^cite_note-68] Jeddah Season aims to make Jeddah the most preferred tourist destination in the world and the best season of all the Saudi Seasons.[^cite_note-Seasons-2019-69]

### Media

Jeddah is served by four major Arabic-language newspapers, *[Asharq Al-Awsat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asharq_Al-Awsat)*, *[Al Madina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Madina_Press)*, *[Okaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okaz)*, and *[Al Bilad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bilad_\(Saudi_newspaper\))*, as well as two major English-language newspapers, the *[Saudi Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Gazette)* and *[Arab News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_News)* (which also covers other Middle Eastern events). *Okaz* and *Al-Madina* are the primary newspapers of Jeddah and some other Saudi cities, with over a million readers; their focus is mainly local.

Internet blogs specifically informative of the locality are abundant in Jeddah, catering mostly to the widespread expatriate population. Of these are constituted websites that have garnered international acclaim for informativeness, such as Jeddah Blog, the recipient of the Bronze Expat Blog Award in 2012 and the Gold Award in 2013 and among Feedspot's Top 100 Middle East blogs.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Other amateur websites relating to specific topics in the region exist as well.

Jeddah represents the largest radio and television market in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md). Television stations serving the city area include Saudi TV1, [Saudi TV2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_T.V._Channel_II), [Saudi TV Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_TV_Sports), [Al Ekhbariya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Ekhbariya), the [ART channels network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Radio_and_Television_Network) and hundreds of cable, satellite, and other specialty television providers.

The [Jeddah TV Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeddah_TV_Tower) is a 250 m (820 ft) high television tower with an observation deck.

### Old Jeddah

Main article: [Al-Balad, Jeddah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Balad,_Jeddah)



The architecture of buildings in Jeddah's historic Al-Balad area

The Old City known as [Al-Balad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Balad,_Jeddah), with its traditional multistory buildings and merchant houses that often still belong to the families that inhabited them before the oil-era, has lost ground to more modern developments.[^cite_note-73] Nonetheless, the Old City contributes to the cultural identity of Jeddah and the Hijaz more generally. Since it was granted UNESCO World Heritage status, in 2014, several traditional buildings have been restored and made open to the public. In 2019, the Saudi crown prince, [Muhammad bin Salman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_bin_Salman), issued a royal decree ordering the [Ministry of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_\(Saudi_Arabia\)) to restore 50 historical buildings in Jeddah.[^cite_note-74] Several historic mosques from different eras are located in al-Balad, as well as one of the oldest museums in the city, called Bayt Naseef (the House of Naseef), which displays local furniture and interior design from the past 150 years, approximately.

## Historical Jeddah

Main article: [Al-Balad, Jeddah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Balad,_Jeddah)



The historic center



Saloom's house in historical Jeddah



Old Dutch Christian church ruins in Jeddah

Historical Jeddah is situated on the eastern shore of the [Red Sea](26191.md). From the 7th century AD, it was established as a major port for Indian Ocean trade routes, channeling goods to Mecca. It was also the gateway for Muslim pilgrims to Mecca who arrived by sea. These twin roles saw the city develop into a thriving multicultural center, characterized by a distinctive architectural tradition, including tower houses built in the late 19th century by the city's mercantile elites, and combining Red Sea coastal coral building traditions with influences and crafts from along the trade routes.[^cite_note-75]

Within a defensive wall that was built during Ottoman rule, the old city of Jeddah, Al-Balad, was divided into districts, or *Haras*, where business and trade centered around traditional *souks*, or market places, and *khans*, covered markets that were generally connected to shops.[^cite_note-76]

### Harrat Al-Mathloum (District of the Wronged)

Located in the northeast of Jeddah, this district was named after Abdulkarim Al-Barzangi, a Hijazi rebel who was crucified by the Ottomans. Some of its landmarks are:

- Dar Al-Qabil

- Dar Al-Ba'ashin

- Dar Al-Sheikh

- The Al-Shafi'i mosque

The oldest mosque in town, its [minaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minaret) was built in the 13th century, and its pillars date back to Ottoman rule.

- Mosque of Uthman bin Affan

Also called the Ebony Mosque because of its two ebony pillars, it was mentioned in the writings of [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta) and [Ibn Jubayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Jubayr).

- Al-Mia'mar Mosque

An old mosque built in the 17th century.

- Souq Al-Jama

One of the oldest markets in town.

### Harrat Al-Sham (The Levantine District)

Located in the north and named after its orientation, some of its landmarks are:

- Dar Al-Sadat

- Dar Al-Serti

- Dar Al-Zahid

- Dar Al-Banajah

- Al-Basha Mosque

Built by Bakr Basha, the governor of Jeddah in 1735.

Bait Bajanaid

- Dar Al-Jamjoom

### Abdul Raouf Khalil Museum

Founded by Sheikh Abdul Raouf Khalil in 1996, this museum not only presents the rich Islamic cultural heritage of the city, but also its pre-Islamic history that goes back 2500 years; it traces the various civilizations that inhabited the region. Located in the downtown district, it boasts a large collection of items and artifacts that belonged to the Ottoman Turks and the fishermen tribes who were the first inhabitants of the region.[^cite_note-77]

### King Fahd's Fountain

Main article: [King Fahd's Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Fahd's_Fountain)



[King Fahd's Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Fahd's_Fountain) in Jeddah

[King Fahd's Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Fahd's_Fountain) was built in the 1980s and can be seen from a great distance. At 312 meters (1,024 ft), the Fahd Fountain is the highest water jet in the world, according to the [Guinness World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records).[^cite_note-King_Fahd_Fountain_-_treklens.com-78] The fountain was donated to the City of Jeddah by the late [King Fahd bin Abdul Aziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahd_of_Saudi_Arabia), after whom it was named.

### Al-Rahmah Mosque



Al-Rahmah Mosque

Main article: [Al-Rahmah Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Rahmah_Mosque)

Sometimes referred to as the floating mosque because of its location above water, this mix of old and new architecture was built in 1985. It is a popular spot among tourists and natives looking to lounge by the seaside.

### King Saud Mosque

Main article: [King Saud Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Saud_Mosque)

Built in 1987 by Egyptian architect Abdel Wahed El Wakil, King Saud Mosque is the largest mosque in the city, displaying beautiful Islamic architecture.[^cite_note-79]

### King Abdullah Sports City

Main article: [King Abdullah Sports City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdullah_Sports_City)

King Abdullah Sports City is a multi-use stadium used mostly for association football. It opened in 2014 north of Jeddah and has a full capacity of 62,241 spectators. It is the largest stadium in Jeddah, and the second-largest in Saudi Arabia.

This proposed tower, formerly known as the [Kingdom Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Tower), is being built in Jeddah by Prince [Al-Waleed bin Talal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Waleed_bin_Talal) and will stand 1-kilometer (0.62 mi) tall. Upon its completion, it will be the tallest skyscraper in the world. Construction began in April 2013 and there was steady progress, but in January 2018, building owner JEC halted structural concrete work with the tower about one-third completed due to labor issues with a contractor following the [2017–19 Saudi Arabian purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9319_Saudi_Arabian_purge). JEC had said they planned to restart construction in 2020.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] In September 2023 [MEED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MEED) reported that the construction of the tower had restarted.[^cite_note-84] Completion is expected in 2029.[^cite_note-85]

### Entrance of Mecca

Bab Makkah, also known as Makkah Gate, is a limestone coral gateway that leads into the historic Al-Balad district of Jeddah.

&#x20;The [Makkah Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca_Gate), named the "Quran Gate", is located 60 km outside Jeddah on the [Makkah Mukkarram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) road of the [Jeddah–Makkah Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_40_\(Saudi_Arabia\)). It is the entrance to Mecca and the birthplace of [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad). The gate was designed in 1979 by Egyptian architect [Samir Elabd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samir_Elabd?action=edit\&redlink=1) for the architectural firm IDEA Center. The structure is that of a book, representing the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran), sitting on a *[rehal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehal_\(book_rest\))*, or book stand.[^cite_note-Makkah_Gate_Design_-_elabdarchitecture.com-87]

### Libraries

The central library at King Abdulaziz University (main branch) is a five-story building that has a large collection of Arabic and English language books, rare books, and documents as well as access to several online databases. It is open for public access and allows the borrowing of books after requesting a library card. Saturdays are dedicated to female visitors.[^cite_note-98]

King Abdul Aziz Public Library is a philanthropic institution that was founded and supported by the Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques King Abdullah Bin Abdulaziz, chairman of its board of directors. Established in 1985, the library was officially opened by the King on 27 February 1987. It emphasizes Islamic and Arabic heritage and history of the Kingdom. The library is divided into three branches (men's, women's, and children's).[^cite_note-99]

The limited number of libraries is criticized by the public. As a result, King Abdullah, Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques, has approved the King Abdullah Project for the Development of Public Libraries, and approximately SAR150 million is budgeted to be spent.[^cite_note-100]

In April 2014, Prince Mishaal Ibn Abdullah Abdulaziz opened a new public library in Jeddah by Makkah Governor, under the name of King Fahd Public Library.[^cite_note-kau.edu.sa-101]

King Fahd Public Library was built over an area of 17,000 square meters (180,000 sq ft) within the main Campus of [King Abdulaziz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdulaziz_University) (KAU) in Jeddah. It includes a diverse collection of books and reference material classified into three sections to meet the needs and wants of a wide range of readership. Spaces have been set apart for youths, children and women.[^cite_note-kau.edu.sa-101]

## Issues and challenges

The city is challenged by pollution, weak [sewage systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage), and a weak storm drain system that has led to massive flooding, heavy traffic, epidemics, and water shortages.

### Pollution and environment

[Air pollution](10934212.md) is a problem for Jeddah, particularly on hot summer days. The city has experienced bush fires, [landfill](211285.md) fires, and pollution from the two industrial zones in the north and the south of the metropolitan area. A water treatment factory and the [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeddah_Seaport) also contribute to water pollution. Much of the seafront, however, is considered to be safe and clean. [Ramboll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramboll) has acted as Environmental Consultant on the Jeddah [Environmental Impact Assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Impact_Assessment) as well as the Jeddah Environmental Social Masterplan.[^cite_note-109]

### Terrorism

At midday on 6 December 2004, militants loyal to [the regional affiliate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Arabian_Peninsula) of the terrorist group [Al-Qaeda](1921.md) launched an attack on the U.S. Consulate, killing five employees. The group was led by Fayez ibn Awwad Al-Jeheni, a former member of the [religious police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_for_the_Promotion_of_Virtue_and_the_Prevention_of_Vice_\(Saudi_Arabia\)). Two of the other assailants were subsequently identified by Saudi authorities as residents of Jeddah's Al-Jamia suburb and other slum areas on Saudi Arabia's increasingly urbanized west coast. Buildings were attacked, hostages were taken and used as human shields, targeting both Americans and non-Americans alike. Despite a protracted siege, the chancery/consular section of the building was never penetrated.[^cite_note-briefing_underscores-110] Closed-circuit video feeds documented that Saudi security personnel assigned to protect the facility fled when the vehicle carrying the terrorists pulled up to the front gate and passed the Delta barrier.[^cite_note-111] Inside the compound, however, an armed Saudi security guard employed by the embassy shot and killed one of the five terrorists before being fatally shot himself.

The attackers spread and ignited a flammable liquid on the front of the chancery building, and opened fire on the front doors, both of which actions did not have any penetrating effect. The Consulate's U.S. Marines released tear gas in front of the chancery building, but the terrorists had already left that location. More than an hour later, Saudi special forces made it through traffic and, along with others from their unit who arrived in a helicopter, fought to retake the compound. In the final shootout, two of the terrorists were killed, with another dying later in hospital, and one militant was captured alive. Four Saudi special forces and a further ten hostages were wounded in the crossfire.[^cite_note-briefing_underscores-110][^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

The five U.S. Foreign Service employees killed in the terrorist attack were Ali Yaslem Bin Talib, Imad e-Deen Musa Ali, Romeo de la Rosa, Mohammed Baheer Uddin, and Jaufar Sadik, nationals of Yemen, Sudan, Philippines, India and Sri Lanka, respectively.[^cite_note-114]

The attack underscored the ongoing vulnerabilities of Westerners to threats, terrorist actions, and the environs. In a communiqué posted in online publications such as *[Sawt al-Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_of_Jihad)* (Voice of Jihad) and *Mu'askar al-Battar* (Al-Battar Training Camp), Al-Qaeda hinted at the symbolic nature of the U.S. Consulate attack, stating: "Know that the Mujahideen are determined to continue on their path, and they will not be weakened by what has happened to them."[^cite_note-briefing_underscores-110]

Terrorist activities have persisted from 2004 to the present day. In 2004, there was an unsuccessful shooting attack on a U.S. Marine visiting the Saudi American Bank and an attempt to simultaneously explode car bombs at Saudi American Bank and Saudi British Bank branches in Jeddah on the anniversary of the 2001 "9-11" terrorist attacks on the U.S.[^cite_note-115] On 26 August 2012, a spokesman for the [Interior Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_home_affairs) announced that terrorists were arrested in Jeddah who had been preparing explosives for attacks within the kingdom.[^cite_note-116]

In 2022, the [Houthis](17240332.md) launched a missile attack against an Aramco facility near the [Jeddah Corniche Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeddah_Corniche_Circuit), where the [Formula One](10854.md) [Saudi Arabian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Saudi_Arabian_Grand_Prix) was held.

### Traffic



Traffic on Medina Road in Jeddah

Roads and highways within and exiting the city are frequently clogged with traffic. Mass transit is rare and planning is nascent; most Jeddawi adults have at least one car. Days immediately preceding and following the holy days are particularly noisome and cost hundreds of thousands of person-hours because of traffic jams.

The *Saudi Gazette* reports that there is a plan in the works to tackle the traffic issue. A reported three billion Saudi Riyals will be put into constructing flyovers and underpasses in an effort to expedite traffic. The plan is scheduled to take about five years from its start to finish.[^cite_note-117]

### Sewage

See also: [Al-Musk Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Musk_Lake)



[Al-Musk Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Musk_Lake), located east of the city, is a sewage lake serving Jeddah.

Prior to the construction of a waste treatment plant, Jeddah's wastewater was disposed of by either discharge into the sea or via absorption into deep underground pits. However, even with the ever-increasing population, the original sewer system has hardly been expanded. The original plant cannot cope with the amount of waste inundating it daily. As a result, some untreated sewage is discharged directly into the sea and the entire northern part of the city remains completely unconnected to the sewage system, instead relying on septic tanks. This has been responsible for a large number of sewage tankers.

In late 2011, a storm drainage system was built in the south Jeddah area (similar to that of the Los Angeles storm drain) to reduce the risk of floods.[^cite_note-118]

### Floods



A tunnel in King Abdullah St. was filled with water during the 2009 floods.

On [25 November 2009, heavy floods affected the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Jeddah_floods) and other areas of [Makkah Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makkah_Province).[^cite_note-AFP-119][^cite_note-BBC-120] The floods were described by civil defence officials as the worst in 27 years.[^cite_note-Gazette1126-121] As of 26 November 2009, 77 people were reported to have been killed,[^cite_note-SGaid-122] and more than 350 were missing.[^cite_note-AFP-119] Some roads were under a meter (three feet) of water on 26 November, and many of the victims were believed to have drowned in their cars. At least 3,000 vehicles were swept away or damaged.[^cite_note-AFP-119][^cite_note-SGaid-122][^cite_note-SGdamage-123] The death toll was expected to rise as [flood](50482.md) waters receded, allowing rescuers to reach stranded vehicles.[^cite_note-ANdeaths-124]

On 26 January 2011, again, heavy floods affected the city and other areas of [Makkah Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makkah_Province). The cumulative rainfall exceeded the 90 mm (3.5 in) recorded in four hours during the 25 November 2009 flash floods. Streets including Palestine Street, Madinah Road, and Wali Al-Ahad Street were either flooded or jammed with traffic. Cars were seen floating in some places. Meanwhile, eyewitnesses told local newspaper *Arab News* that East Jeddah was swamped and floodwater was rushing west towards the Red Sea, turning streets into rivers once again.



A tunnel in King Abdullah St. was filled with water during the 2011 floods.

On 17 November 2015, heavy floods affected the city. Streets affected by the flood include Palestine Street, Madinah Road, and many others. Cars were seen burning, and many trees fell as a result of the violent flood.[^cite_note-125] Three deaths were also reported. Two of the fatalities (including a child) were hit by [lightning](61344.md) while crossing a street.

On 21 November 2017, heavy floods affected the city once more and Jeddah Islamic Port stopped operations for about three hours. Jeddah police received 11,000 phone calls on 911 from people enquiring about alternative roads and weather conditions.[^cite_note-126] There were 250 reports of electrocution. Five people were electrocuted, two died.

On [24 November 2022, heavy floods affected Jeddah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Jeddah_floods). Jeddah was heavily damaged from the flood and caused more than thousands of cars to be damaged by the flood and caused power outages to most of the city and the flood was approximately 1-2 meters high. As a result, flights were delayed, schools were closed, and two people were killed.
