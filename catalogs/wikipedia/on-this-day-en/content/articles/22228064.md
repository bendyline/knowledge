**Parkinson's disease** (**PD**), or simply **Parkinson's**, is a [neurodegenerative disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurodegenerative_disease) primarily of the [central nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_nervous_system), affecting both [motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_system) and non-motor systems. The motor symptoms, collectively called [parkinsonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinsonism), include [tremors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremor), slowness in initiating movement ([bradykinesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradykinesia)), [rigidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spasticity), and difficulty maintaining balance ([postural instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postural_instability)). Non-motor symptoms such as [autonomic nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomic_nervous_system) failures ([dysautonomia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysautonomia)), [sleep abnormalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_abnormalities), decreased ability to smell ([anosmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anosmia)), and [behavioral changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavior_change_\(individual\)) or [neuropsychiatric problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_disorder), such as [cognitive impairment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_impairment), [psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychosis), and [anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety), may appear at any stage of the disease. Symptoms typically develop gradually, and non-motor issues become more prevalent as the disease progresses.

Parkinson's disease has no single cause; rather, genetic and environmental factors interact to affect critical cellular processes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen_ShaulFrenkelGurevich2024-4][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeheshti2025-5] Parkinson's disease involves the gradual decay and loss of [dopamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine)-producing [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron) in a brain region called the [substantia nigra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substantia_nigra) and other related cell groups in the [brainstem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brainstem).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERameshArachchige2023200–201,_203-6] [Misfolded proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_folding), such as [alpha-synuclein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-synuclein), [aggregate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_aggregation) to form clumps called [Lewy bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewy_body), if not cleared from cells by [cellular degradation systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autophagy). Protein accumulation stimulates the release of pro-inflammatory molecules by the [microglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microglia), a protective response that can cause [neuroinflammation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroinflammation), further neuronal damage, and disruption of metabolic systems, if remaining chronic.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERameshArachchige2023200–201,_203-6]

Diagnosis is primarily based on [signs and symptoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signs_and_symptoms), typically motor-related, identified through [neurological examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurological_examination). [Medical imaging techniques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging) such as [PET scanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positron_emission_tomography) can support the diagnosis. PD typically manifests in individuals over 60, with about one percent affected. In those younger than 50, the condition is termed "early-onset PD".

No cure for PD is known, and treatment focuses on alleviating symptoms. Initial treatment typically includes [levodopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levodopa), [MAO-B inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAO-B_inhibitor), or [dopamine agonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine_agonist). As the disease progresses, these medications become less effective and may cause [involuntary muscle movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyskinesia). Diet and rehabilitation therapies can help improve symptoms. [Deep brain stimulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_brain_stimulation) is used to manage severe motor symptoms when drugs are ineffective. Little evidence exists for treatments addressing non-motor symptoms, such as sleep disturbances and mood instability. Life expectancy for those with PD is near-normal, but is decreased for early-onset.

## Classification and terminology

See also: [Parkinsonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinsonism) and [Parkinson-plus syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson-plus_syndrome)

Parkinson's disease is a [neurodegenerative disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurodegenerative_disease) affecting both the [central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_nervous_system) and [peripheral nervous systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripheral_nervous_systems), characterized by the [loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_death) of [dopamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine)-producing [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurons) in the [substantia nigra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substantia_nigra) region of the brain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERameshArachchige2023200–201,_203-6] It is classified as a [synucleinopathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synucleinopathy) due to the abnormal accumulation of the protein [alpha-synuclein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-synuclein), which aggregates into [Lewy bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewy_bodies) within affected neurons.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalabresiMechelliNataleVolpicelli-Daley20231,_5-7]

The loss of dopamine-producing neurons in the substantia nigra causes movement abnormalities, leading to the categorization of Parkinson's as a [movement disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_disorder).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENational_Institute_of_Neurological_Disorders_and_Stroke-1] In 30% of those with PD, disease progression leads to cognitive decline, resulting in [Parkinson's disease dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson's_disease_dementia) (PDD).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWallaceSegerstromvan_HorneSchmitt2022149-8] Alongside [dementia with Lewy bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dementia_with_Lewy_bodies), PDD is one of the two subtypes of [Lewy body dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewy_body_dementia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHansenLingLashleyHolton2019635-9]

The four cardinal motor symptoms of Parkinson's—slowed movements ([bradykinesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradykinesia)), [postural instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postural_instability), [rigidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spasticity), and [tremor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremor)—are called [parkinsonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinsonism).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBhattacharyya20177-10][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStanford_University_School_Medicine-11] Parkinsonism is not exclusive to Parkinson's disease and can occur in other conditions,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBolognaTruongJankovic20221–6-12] including [HIV infection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV_infection) and [recreational drug use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MPTP#Discovery_in_users_of_illicit_drugs).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELetaUrsoBatzuLau20221122-13][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELangston2017S11-14] Neurodegenerative diseases that feature parkinsonism, but have distinct differences are grouped under the umbrella of [Parkinson-plus syndromes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson-plus_syndrome), or alternatively, atypical parkinsonian disorders.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPrajjwalKolanuReddyAhmed20241–3-15][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOlfatiaShoeibiaLitvanb2019101-16]

## Signs and symptoms

Main article: [Signs and symptoms of Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signs_and_symptoms_of_Parkinson's_disease)

### Motor

See also: [Parkinsonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinsonism)

The stooped posture of a patient with Parkinson's

The small, jagged handwriting of a PD patient

Motor symptoms include a stooping posture, the [Parkinsonian gait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinsonian_gait), and [micrographia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micrographia_\(handwriting\))—jagged, diminutive handwriting.

The cardinal motor features are tremor, bradykinesia, rigidity, and postural instability, collectively termed [parkinsonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinsonism).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAbusrairElsekailyBohlega20222-17] Appearing in 70–75% of those with PD,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAbusrairElsekailyBohlega20222-17][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoustafaChakravarthyPhillipsGupta2016730-18] tremor is often the predominant motor symptom.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAbusrairElsekailyBohlega20222-17] Resting tremor is the most common, but kinetic tremors—occurring during voluntary movements—and postural tremor—preventing upright, stable posture—also occur.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoustafaChakravarthyPhillipsGupta2016730-18] Tremor largely affects the hands and feet:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoustafaChakravarthyPhillipsGupta2016730-18] a classic parkinsonian tremor is "[pill-rolling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pill-rolling)", a resting tremor in which the thumb and index finger make contact in a circular motion at 4–6 Hz frequency.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAbusrairElsekailyBohlega20224-19][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESveinbjornsdottir2016319-20]

Bradykinesia describes difficulties in [motor planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_planning), beginning, and executing, resulting in overall slowed movement with reduced amplitude that affects sequential and simultaneous tasks.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBolognaPaparellaFasanoHallett2019727–729-21] Bradykinesia can also lead to [hypomimia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypomimia), reduced facial expressions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESveinbjornsdottir2016319-20] [Rigidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spasticity), also called rigor, refers to a feeling of stiffness and resistance to passive stretching of muscles.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoustafaChakravarthyPhillipsGupta2016728-22] [Postural instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postural_instability) typically appears in later stages, leading to [impaired balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_disorder) and [falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falls_in_older_adults).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPalakurthiBurugupally20191–2-23] Postural instability also leads to a forward stooping posture.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPalakurthiBurugupally20191,_4-24]

Beyond the cardinal four, other motor deficits, termed secondary motor symptoms, commonly occur.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoustafaChakravarthyPhillipsGupta2016727–728-25] Notably, gait disturbances result in the [parkinsonian gait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinsonian_gait), which includes shuffling and [paroxysmal deficits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paroxysmal_dyskinesia), where a normal gait is interrupted by rapid footsteps—known as festination—or sudden stops, impairing balance and causing falls.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoustafaChakravarthyPhillipsGupta2016731-26][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMirelmanBonatoCamicioliEllis20191-27] Most people with PD experience speech problems, including [stuttering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttering), [hypophonic, "soft" speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypophonia), [slurring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysarthria), and festinating speech (rapid and poorly intelligible).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoustafaChakravarthyPhillipsGupta2016734-28] Handwriting is commonly altered in PD, decreasing in size—known as [micrographia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micrographia_\(handwriting\))—and becoming jagged and sharply fluctuating.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoustafaChakravarthyPhillipsGupta2016732-29] Grip and dexterity are also impaired.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoustafaChakravarthyPhillipsGupta2016733-30]

### Neuropsychiatric and cognitive

[Neuropsychiatric symptoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuropsychiatric_symptoms) such as [anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety), [apathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apathy), [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_\(mood\)), [hallucination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucinations), and [impulse control disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impulse_control_disorders) occur in up to 60% of those with PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAarslandaKrambergera2015659–660-31] They often precede motor symptoms, vary with disease progression,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAarslandaKrambergera2015659–660-31] and may appear at any stage of the disease including the [prodromal phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prodrome).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBatzuPodlewskaGibsonChaudhuri2024sec._"Abstract"-32] Fluctuating symptoms including [dysphoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysphoria) (the opposite of euphoria), [fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatigue), and slowness of thought, are also common.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeintraubMamikonyan2019sec._"Non-motor_fluctuations"-33] Some neuropsychiatric symptoms are not directly caused by neurodegeneration, but rather by its pharmacological management.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAarslandaKrambergera2015660-34]

Cognitive impairments rank among the most prevalent and debilitating non-motor symptoms.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBiundoWeisAntonini20161-36] These deficits may emerge in the early stages or before diagnosis,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBiundoWeisAntonini20161-36][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGonzalez-LatapiBayramLitvanMarras202174-37] and their prevalence and severity tend to increase with disease progression. Ranging from [mild cognitive impairment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mild_cognitive_impairment) to severe [Parkinson's disease dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson's_disease_dementia), these impairments include [executive dysfunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_dysfunction), [slowed cognitive processing speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradyphrenia), and disruptions in time perception and estimation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGonzalez-LatapiBayramLitvanMarras202174-37]

### Autonomic

[Autonomic nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomic_nervous_system) failures, known as [dysautonomia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysautonomia), can appear at any stage of PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPalmaKaufmann2018372–373-38][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPfeiffer20201464-39] They are among the most debilitating symptoms and greatly reduce quality of life.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPalmaKaufmann2018373-40] Although almost all individuals with PD have cardiovascular autonomic dysfunction, only some are symptomatic.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPalmaKaufmann2018373-40] Chiefly, [orthostatic hypotension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthostatic_hypotension)—a sustained [blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_pressure) drop of at least 20 mmHg [systolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systole) or 10 mmHg [diastolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diastolic) after standing—occurs in 30–50% of cases. This can result in [lightheadedness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightheadedness) or [fainting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncope_\(medicine\)); subsequent falls are associated with higher morbidity and mortality.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPalmaKaufmann2018373-40][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPalmaKaufmann2020sec._"Synopsis",_"Epidemiology",_"Approach_to_the_patient_with_orthostatic_hypotension"-41]

Other autonomic failures include [gastrointestinal issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrointestinal_issues) such as chronic constipation, [impaired stomach emptying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastroparesis) and subsequent [nausea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nausea), [excessive salivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersalivation), and [dysphagia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysphagia) (difficulty swallowing); all greatly reduce quality of life.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPfeiffer20201465–1467-42] Dysphagia, for instance, can prevent pill swallowing and lead to [aspiration pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspiration_pneumonia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPfeiffer20201468-43] [Urinary incontinence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urinary_incontinence), [sexual dysfunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_dysfunction), and [thermoregulatory dysfunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermoregulation)—including heat and cold intolerance and excessive sweating—also frequently occur.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPfeiffer20201471–1473-44]

### Other

Sensory deficits appear in up to 90% of people with PD and are usually present at early stages.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZhuLiYeJiang2016685-45] [Nociceptive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nociceptive_pain) and [neuropathic pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuropathic_pain) are common,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZhuLiYeJiang2016685-45] with [peripheral neuropathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripheral_neuropathy) affecting up to 55% of individuals.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECorràVila-ChãSardoeiraHansen2023225–226-46] [Visual impairments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_impairments) are also frequently observed, including deficits in [visual acuity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_acuity), [color vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_vision), [eye coordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_coordination), and [visual hallucinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_hallucinations).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZhuLiYeJiang2016688-47] An [impaired sense of smell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anosmia) is also prevalent.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZhuLiYeJiang2016687-48] Individuals often struggle with spatial awareness, recognizing faces and emotions, and may experience challenges with reading and double vision.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeilSchragWarrenCrutch20162828,_2831–2832-49]

[Sleep disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_disorder) are highly prevalent in PD, affecting up to 98%.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStefaniHögl2020121-50] These disorders include [insomnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insomnia), [excessive daytime sleepiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excessive_daytime_sleepiness), [restless legs syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restless_legs_syndrome), [REM sleep behavior disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/REM_sleep_behavior_disorder) (RBD), and [sleep-disordered breathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep-disordered_breathing), many of which can be worsened by medication. RBD may begin years before the initial motor symptoms. Individual presentation of symptoms varies, although most people affected by PD show an altered [circadian rhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circadian_rhythm) at some point of disease progression.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDodetHouotLeu-SemenescuCorvol20241-51][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBolluSahota2017381–382-52]

PD is also associated with a variety of [skin disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_disorder) that include [melanoma](716631.md), [seborrheic dermatitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seborrheic_dermatitis), [bullous pemphigoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullous_pemphigoid), and [rosacea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosacea).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENiemannBillnitzerJankovic202161-53] Seborrheic dermatitis is recognized as a premotor feature that indicates dysautonomia and demonstrates that PD can be detected not only by changes of [nervous tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervous_tissue), but also tissue abnormalities outside the nervous system.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlmikhlafi20247-54]

## Causes

Main article: [Causes of Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causes_of_Parkinson's_disease)

Parkinson's disease has no single cause: rather, genetic and environmental factors interact and affect critical cellular processes in a complex interplay.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen_ShaulFrenkelGurevich2024-4][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeheshti2025-5] Parkinson's disease (PD) involves the gradual degeneration of [dopamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine) producing [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron) in a brain region called the [substantia nigra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substantia_nigra) and other related cell groups in the [brainstem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brainstem).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERameshArachchige2023200–201,_203-6] This is accompanied by the accumulation of [misfolded proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_folding) such as [alpha-synuclein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-synuclein). Alpha-synuclein is normally found in the [presynaptic terminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_synapse) of neurons. If alpha-synuclein is mis-folded and not cleared from cells by [cellular degradation systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autophagy), it can build up to form clumps of proteins called [Lewy bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewy_body) and Lewy neurites. Accumulation stimulates the release of pro-inflammatory molecules by the [microglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microglia), a protective response that can cause inflammation and neuronal damage if it becomes chronic.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERameshArachchige2023200–201,_203-6] [Neuroinflammation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroinflammation) causes the [blood-brain barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood%E2%80%93brain_barrier) (BBB) to become more permeable, allowing dangerous substances and inflammatory cells to enter the brain and interfere with metabolic functions. Dysfunction in [mitochondria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrion), which are central to [cellular energy production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_respiration), increases [oxidative stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidative_stress) and [cell death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_death).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGeorgoulaNtavaroukasCampbellPapoutsopoulou2024-55][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELauKotzurRichter2024-56][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERameshArachchige2023-57] [Protein aggregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_aggregation), inflammation and metabolic dysregulation in [lysosomal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysosome), [endosomal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endosome), and mitochondrial systems are interconnected mechanisms which create a cycle of inflammation, cellular stress and damage in Parkinson's disease.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELiuLiWuQiao2025-58][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeheshti2025-5][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorrisSpillantiniSueWilliams-Gray2024-59]

The cumulative effects of many different environmental exposures over a lifetime interact with underlying genetic factors to influence PD development and progression.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakowskiKoubekChenGoutman2024-60] Genetically, about 5–10% of cases are familial[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEToffoliVieiraSchapira20201-61] while others are classed as [idiopathic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiopathic_disease) with no clearly identifiable cause.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENational_Institute_of_Neurological_Disorders_and_Stroke-1] Variations in specific genes have been identified as risk factors and linked to contributing neural mechanisms.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorrisSpillantiniSueWilliams-Gray2024-59][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBeheshti2025-5] Environmentally, exposures to [pesticides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesticides), metals, [solvents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvent), other [toxicants](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/toxicant) and [air pollution](10934212.md) are increasingly seen as major risk factors in PD development.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorseyBloem2024453–454-62][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDe_MirandaGoldmanMillerGreenamyre202246-63] The brain is particularly vulnerable to chemicals that are able to cross the [blood-brain barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood%E2%80%93brain_barrier).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESakowskiKoubekChenGoutman2024-60] Body-first and brain-first models of Parkinson's disease have been proposed to explain the connection of PD mechanisms with exposure to known environmental risk factors via the gut and the [olfactory system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfactory_system).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorseyDe_MirandaHorsagerBorghammer2024363-64][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorseyBloem2024453–454-62] [Traumatic brain injury](1057414.md)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDelicBeckPangCitron20201–2-65] and [Type 2 diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_2_diabetes) are additional risk factors.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBantounouShoaibMazzoleniModalavalasa2024-66] Exercise,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELangeskov-ChristensenFranzénGrøndahl_HvidDalgas2024-67] coffee consumption,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENemzerAl-TaherAbshiruMejia2025-68] and diets rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and fish are protective factors associated with lower risk of PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnwarAhmadImtiazAhmad2024-69]

### Genetic

A ribbon diagram of the protein parkin

[Ribbon diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribbon_diagram) of [parkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkin_\(protein\))

Parkinson's can be narrowly defined as a genetic disease, as rare inherited gene variants have been firmly linked to monogenic PD, and most cases carry variants that increase PD risk.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorrisSpillantiniSueWilliams-Gray2024-59][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEToffoliVieiraSchapira20202-70][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESallesTirapeguiChaná-Cuevas20242-71] PD [heritability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritability) is estimated to range from 22 to 40%.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorrisSpillantiniSueWilliams-Gray2024-59] Around 15% of diagnosed individuals have a [family history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_history_\(medicine\)), of which 5–10% can be attributed to a causative risk gene [mutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation). Carrying one of these mutations may not lead to disease. Rates of familial PD vary by ethnicity; monogenic PD occurs in up to 40% of [Arab-Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab-Berber) and 20% of [Ashkenazi Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jewish) people with PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESallesTirapeguiChaná-Cuevas20242-71]

Notable genetic risk variants include *[SNCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-synuclein)* (which encodes alpha-synuclein), *[LRRK2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LRRK2)*, and *[VPS35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VPS35)* for [autosomal dominant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autosomal_dominant) inheritance, and *[PRKN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkin_\(protein\)) (parkin)*, *[PINK1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PINK1)*, and *[DJ1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ-1)* for [autosomal recessive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autosomal_recessive) inheritance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorrisSpillantiniSueWilliams-Gray2024-59][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBandres-CigaDiez-FairenKimSingleton20202-72] *LRRK2* is the most common autosomal dominant variant, responsible for 1–2% of all PD cases and 40% of familial cases.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETannerOstrem2024-73][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEToffoliVieiraSchapira20201-61] Parkin variants are associated with nearly half of recessive, early-onset monogenic PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEToffoliVieiraSchapira20201–2-74] Mutations in the *[GBA1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GBA1)* gene are associated with cognitive decline.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETannerOstrem2024-73]

### Environmental

See also: [Environmental health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_health) and [Exposome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposome)

The limited heritability of Parkinson's suggests environmental factors are involved, though identifying these risk factors and establishing [causality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causality) is challenging due to PD's decade-long prodromal (early signs) period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDe_MirandaGoldmanMillerGreenamyre202246-63]

Dorsey and Bloem (2024) argue that environmental toxicants such as pesticides, air pollution and [trichloroethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trichloroethylene) are linked to PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorseyBloem2024453–454-62] Certain pesticides and herbicides—such as [paraquat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraquat), [glyphosate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyphosate), and [rotenone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotenone)—are the most established environmental toxicants for PD and are likely causal.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorseyBloem2024454-75][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERietdijkPerez-PardoGarssenvan_Wezel20171-76] PD prevalence is associated with local pesticide use, and many pesticides are mitochondrial toxins.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorseyBloem2024453–455-77] Paraquat, for instance, structurally resembles metabolized [MPTP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MPTP),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorseyBloem2024454-75] which selectively kills dopaminergic neurons by inhibiting [mitochondrial complex 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrial_complex_1) and is widely used to [model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_models) PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELangston2017S14-78][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorseyBloem2024454-75] Pesticide exposure after diagnosis may also accelerate disease progression.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorseyBloem2024454-75]

### Hypotheses

#### Prionic

The hallmark of PD is the formation of [protein aggregates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_aggregation), beginning with [alpha-synuclein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-synuclein) fibrils and followed by [Lewy bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewy_body) and Lewy neurites.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWuSchekman20241-79] The [prion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prion) hypothesis suggests that alpha-synuclein aggregates are pathogenic and can spread to neighboring, healthy neurons and seed new aggregates. Some propose that the heterogeneity of PD may stem from different "strains" of alpha-synuclein aggregates and varying anatomical sites of origin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrundinMelki20179809-80][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVázquez-VélezZoghbi202196-81] Alpha-synuclein propagation has been demonstrated in cell and animal models and is the most popular explanation for the progressive spread through specific neuronal systems.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDickson2018S31-82] However, therapeutic efforts to clear alpha-synuclein have failed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWuSchekman20241–2-83] Additionally, postmortem brain tissue analysis shows that alpha-synuclein pathology does not clearly progress through the nearest neural connections.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrundinMelki20179812-84]

#### Braak's

Main article: [Parkinson's disease and gut-brain axis § Braak's hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson's_disease_and_gut-brain_axis#Braak's_hypothesis)

In 2002, [Heiko Braak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiko_Braak) and colleagues proposed that PD begins outside the brain and is triggered by a "neuroinvasion" of some unknown pathogen.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorseyDe_MirandaHorsagerBorghammer2024363-64][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERietdijkPerez-PardoGarssenvan_Wezel20172-85] The pathogen enters through the nasal cavity and is swallowed into the digestive tract, initiating Lewy pathology in both areas.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERietdijkPerez-PardoGarssenvan_Wezel20171-76][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorseyDe_MirandaHorsagerBorghammer2024363-64] This alpha-synuclein pathology may then travel from the gut to the central nervous system through the [vagus nerve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vagus_nerve).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERietdijkPerez-PardoGarssenvan_Wezel20173-86] This hypothesis could explain the presence of Lewy pathology in both the enteric nervous system and olfactory tract neurons, as well as clinical symptoms such as loss of smell and gastrointestinal problems.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERietdijkPerez-PardoGarssenvan_Wezel20172-85] Environmental toxicants ingested in a similar manner might also trigger PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorseyDe_MirandaHorsagerBorghammer2024363–364,_371–372-87]

### Risk factors

As 90% of PD cases are idiopathic, of an unknown cause, the identification of the risk factors that may influence disease progression or severity is critical.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDe_MirandaGoldmanMillerGreenamyre202246-63] The most significant risk factor in developing PD is age, with a prevalence of 1% in those over 65 and around 4.3% in those over 85.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEColemanMartin20222321–2322-88] [Traumatic brain injury](1057414.md) significantly increases PD risk.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDelicBeckPangCitron20201–2-65] Consumption of milk and dairy products but not fermented milk and cheese is associated with a higher PD risk. Explanations such as environmental contamination of milk have been proposed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETumasAurelianoRiederSchuh2025-89] Individuals with PD have a higher risk of developing [melanoma](716631.md), with shared risk factors and some common clinical features.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYeWenAl-KuwariChen2020-90] Also, an association was found between [methamphetamine](11038318.md) use and increased PD risk.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELappin2025-91][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVincentShukla2025-92]

### Protective factors

Although no compounds or activities have been mechanistically established as [neuroprotective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroprotection) for PD,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrottySchwarzschild20201-93][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFabbriRascolFoltynieCarroll20242-94] several factors have been found to be associated with a decreased risk.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrottySchwarzschild20201-93] Exercise[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELangeskov-ChristensenFranzénGrøndahl_HvidDalgas2024-67] and diets rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and fish are protective factors associated with lower risk of PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnwarAhmadImtiazAhmad2024-69] Consumption of [caffeine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caffeine) as an ingredient of [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) or [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea) is strongly associated with neuroprotection.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrotewoldaAlbina20243-95] [Tobacco use](30942.md) and [smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_smoking) are strongly associated with decreased risk, but it is unclear whether this results from a neuroprotective effect or [reverse causation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_causation).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrotewoldaAlbina20241–2-96][^cite_note-FOOTNOTETumasAurelianoRiederSchuh2025-89] Various tobacco and smoke components have been hypothesized to be neuroprotective, including [nicotine](38272.md), [carbon monoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_monoxide), and [monoamine oxidase B inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoamine_oxidase_inhibitor).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrotewoldaAlbina20242-97][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoseSchwarzschildGomperts2024268–269-98]

Although findings have varied, usage of [nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonsteroidal_anti-inflammatory_drugs) (NSAIDs) such as [ibuprofen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibuprofen) may be neuroprotective.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESinghTripathiSingh202110-99] [Calcium channel blockers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_channel_blockers) may also have a protective effect, with a 22% risk reduction reported.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELinPangLiOu20241-100] Higher blood concentrations of [urate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urate)—a potent [antioxidant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioxidant)—have been proposed to be neuroprotective.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrotewoldaAlbina20242-97] [Alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage) consumption has been suggested to be associated with PD risk, but there is no clear relationship between alcohol consumption and PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETumasAurelianoRiederSchuh2025-89]

## Mechanisms

Main article: [Pathophysiology of Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathophysiology_of_Parkinson's_disease)



Parkinson's results from the death of [dopamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine)-releasing [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron) in the [substantia nigra pars compacta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substantia_nigra_pars_compacta), seen by the loss of dark [neuromelanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuromelanin) in the lower inset.

Parkinson's disease has two hallmark pathophysiological processes: the abnormal aggregation of alpha-synuclein that leads to Lewy pathology, and the degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the [substantia nigra pars compacta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substantia_nigra_pars_compacta).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPardo-MorenoGarcía-MoralesSuleiman-MartosRivas-Domínguez20233-101][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVázquez-VélezZoghbi202188-102] The death of these neurons reduces available dopamine in the [striatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striatum), which in turn affects circuits controlling movement in the [basal ganglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_ganglia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVázquez-VélezZoghbi202188-102] By the time motor symptoms appear, 50–80% of all dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra have degenerated.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVázquez-VélezZoghbi202188-102]

Cell death and Lewy pathology, though, are not limited to the substantia nigra.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDickson2018S32-103] The [six-stage Braak system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braak_staging) holds that alpha-synuclein pathology begins in the [olfactory bulb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfactory_bulb) or outside the central nervous system in the [enteric nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enteric_nervous_system) before ascending the brain stem.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYeRobakYuCykowski202398-104] In the third Braak stage, Lewy body pathology appears in the substantia nigra,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYeRobakYuCykowski202398-104] and by the sixth step, Lewy pathology has spread to the limbic and neocortical regions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVázquez-VélezZoghbi202193-105] Although Braak staging offers a strong basis for PD progression, around 50% of individuals do not follow the predicted model.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendersonTrojanowskiLee20192-106] Lewy pathology is highly variable and may be entirely absent in some persons with PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDickson2018S32-103][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYeRobakYuCykowski202396-107]

### Alpha-synuclein pathology

Further information: [Protein aggregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_aggregation) and [Lewy body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewy_body)



[Lewy bodies and Lewy neurites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewy_bodies) stained brown in PD brain tissue

Alpha-synuclein is an intracellular protein typically localized to [presynaptic terminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presynaptic_terminals) and involved in [synaptic vesicle trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synaptic_vesicle_trafficking), [intracellular transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracellular_transport), and [neurotransmitter release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotransmitter_release).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHendersonTrojanowskiLee20192-106][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChenGuWang2022-108] When [misfolded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misfolded), it can aggregate into [oligomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oligomer) and [protofibrils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protofibril?action=edit\&redlink=1) that in turn lead to Lewy body formation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChenGuWang2022-108][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMenšíkováMatějColosimoRosales20228-109][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorghammer20185-110] Due to their lower [molecular weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molar_mass), oligomers and protofibrils may disseminate and be transmitted to other cells more rapidly.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorghammer20185-110]

Lewy bodies consist of a fibrillar exterior and a granular core. Although alpha-synuclein is the dominant [proteinaceous](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/proteinaceous) component, the core contains mitochondrial and autophagosomal membrane components, suggesting a link with organelle dysfunction.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVázquez-VélezZoghbi202195-111][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVázquez-VélezZoghbi202189-112] Whether Lewy bodies themselves contribute to or are simply the result of PD pathogenesis is unclear; alpha-synuclein oligomers can independently mediate cell damage, and neurodegeneration can precede Lewy body formation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMenšíkováMatějColosimoRosales20226-113] It is of note that although the alpha-synuclein seeding activity (the propensity to form aggregates) significantly differs between the CSF of PD patients and healthy controls,[^cite_note-114] no statistically significant associations between baseline CSF alpha-synuclein seeding activity and PD progression have yet been found.[^cite_note-115]

### Pathways involved in neurodegeneration

See also: [Neurodegeneration § Mechanisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurodegeneration#Mechanisms)

Three major pathways—[vesicular trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesicular_trafficking), lysosomal degradation, and mitochondrial maintenance—are known to be affected by and contribute to PD pathogenesis, with all three linked to alpha-synuclein.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVázquez-VélezZoghbi202196–99-116] High-risk gene variants also impair all three of these processes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVázquez-VélezZoghbi202196–99-116] All steps of vesicular trafficking are impaired by alpha-synuclein. It blocks [endoplasmic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmic_reticulum) (ER) vesicles from reaching the [Golgi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golgi_apparatus)—leading to [ER stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XBP1#endoplasmic_reticulum_stress_response)—and Golgi vesicles from reaching the [lysosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysosome), preventing alpha-synuclein degradation and leading to its build-up.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVázquez-VélezZoghbi202196–97-117] Risky gene variants, chiefly *GBA*, further compromise lysosomal function.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVázquez-VélezZoghbi202198–99-118] Although the mechanism is not well established, alpha-synuclein can impair mitochondrial function and cause subsequent [oxidative stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidative_stress). Mitochondrial dysfunction can, in turn, lead to further alpha-synuclein accumulation in a [positive feedback loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_feedback_loop).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVázquez-VélezZoghbi202199-119] Microglial activation, possibly caused by alpha-synuclein, is also strongly indicated.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVázquez-VélezZoghbi2021100-120][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYeRobakYuCykowski2023112-121] Additionally, oxidative stress and other bioenergetic challenges have also been implicated in PD progression independent of alpha-synuclein pathology.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123]

#### Mitochondrial dysfunction

See also: [Mitochondrial disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrial_disease)

Mitochondrial dysfunction is well-established in PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorschePereiraKleinGrünewald202145-124] Increased [oxidative stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidative_stress) and reduced [calcium buffering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_buffering) may contribute to neurodegeneration.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorschePereiraKleinGrünewald202146,_51–53-125] The finding that [MPP<sup>+</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MPP+)—a [respiratory complex I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_complex_I) inhibitor and MPTP metabolite—caused parkinsonian symptoms strongly implied that mitochondria contributed to PD pathogenesis.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorschePereiraKleinGrünewald202145-124] Additionally, faulty gene variants involved in familial PD—including *PINK1* and *Parkin*—prevent the elimination of dysfunctional mitochondria through [mitophagy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitophagy).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBorschePereiraKleinGrünewald202147–49-126]

#### Neuroinflammation

Some hypothesize that neurodegeneration arises from a chronic [neuroinflammatory state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroimmune_system) created by local activated [microglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microglia) and infiltrating immune cells.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorrisSpillantiniSueWilliams-Gray2024-59] Mitochondrial dysfunction may also drive immune activation, particularly in monogenic PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorrisSpillantiniSueWilliams-Gray2024-59] Some [autoimmune disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoimmune_disorders) increase the risk of developing PD, supporting an autoimmune contribution.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETanChaoWestChan2020303-127] Additionally, [influenza](19572217.md) and [herpes simplex virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herpes_simplex_virus) infections increase the risk of PD, possibly due to a [viral protein resembling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_mimicry) alpha-synuclein.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETanChaoWestChan2020304-128] PD risk is also decreased with [immunosuppressants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunosuppressants).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMorrisSpillantiniSueWilliams-Gray2024-59]

## Diagnosis

Diagnosis of PD is largely clinical, relying on [medical history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_history) and examination of symptoms, with an emphasis on symptoms that appear in later stages.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEArmstrongOkun2020548-129][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERizzoCopettiArcutiMartino20161-130] Although early-stage diagnosis is not reliable,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERizzoCopettiArcutiMartino20161-130][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUgrumov2020997-131] prodromal diagnosis may consider previous family history of PD and possible early symptoms such as [rapid eye movement sleep behavior disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_eye_movement_sleep_behavior_disorder) (RBD), reduced [sense of smell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfaction), and gastrointestinal issues.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEArmstrongOkun2020551-132] Isolated RBD is a particularly significant sign, as 90% of those affected will develop some form of neurodegenerative parkinsonism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETolosaGarridoScholzPoewe2021391-133] Diagnosis in later stages requires parkinsonism, specifically bradykinesia and rigidity or tremor. Further support includes other motor and non-motor symptoms and genetic profiling.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEArmstrongOkun2020551–552-134]

A PD diagnosis is typically confirmed by any two of these criteria: responsiveness to levodopa, resting tremor, levodopa-induced dyskinesia, or a [dopamine transporter scan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DaT_scan) confirmation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEArmstrongOkun2020551–552-134] If these criteria are not met, atypical parkinsonism is considered.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEArmstrongOkun2020551-132] Definitive diagnoses can only be made [post mortem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopsy) through pathological analysis.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERizzoCopettiArcutiMartino20161-130] Misdiagnosis is common, with a reported error rate near 25%, and diagnoses often change during follow-ups.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERizzoCopettiArcutiMartino20161-130][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeimKrismerDe_MarziSeppi2017916-135] Diagnosis can be further complicated by multiple overlapping conditions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERizzoCopettiArcutiMartino20161-130]

### Imaging



Reduced [radioisotopic](37245.md) [F-DOPA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorodopa) uptake in the [striatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striatum) of a PD patient, captured through [PET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positron_emission_tomography)

Diagnosis can be aided by molecular imaging techniques such as [magnetic resonance imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging) (MRI), [positron emission tomography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positron_emission_tomography) (PET), and [single-photon emission computed tomography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-photon_emission_computed_tomography) (SPECT).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBidesiAndersenWindhorstShalgunov2021660-136][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoonstraMichielseTemelHoogland2020175-137] As both conventional MRI and [computed tomography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan) (CT) scans are usually normal in early PD, they can be used to exclude other pathologies that cause parkinsonism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeimKrismerDe_MarziSeppi2017916-135][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrooks2010597-138] [Diffusion MRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffusion-weighted_magnetic_resonance_imaging) can differentiate PD from [multiple system atrophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_system_atrophy).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETolosaGarridoScholzPoewe2021392-139] Emerging MRI techniques of at least 3.0 T [field strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging#Construction_and_physics)—including [neuromelanin-MRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRI_sequence#Neuromelanin_imaging), [1H-MRSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_spectroscopic_imaging), and [resting state fMRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resting_state_fMRI)—may detect abnormalities in the substantia nigra, nigrostriatal pathway, and elsewhere.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeimKrismerDe_MarziSeppi2017916-135]

Unlike MRI, PET and SPECT use [radioisotopes](37245.md) for imaging.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBidesiAndersenWindhorstShalgunov2021665-140] Both techniques can aid diagnosis by characterizing PD-associated alterations in the metabolism and [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine_transporter) of dopamine in the basal ganglia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuwijnvan_Boheemende_HaanTissingh2015-141][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBidesiAndersenWindhorstShalgunov2021664–672-142] Largely used outside the United States, iodine-123-meta-iodobenzylguanidine [myocardial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardium) [scintigraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scintigraphy) can assess heart muscle denervation to support a PD diagnosis.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEArmstrongOkun2020552-143]

### Differential diagnosis

See also: [Parkinson-plus syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson-plus_syndrome)

[Differential diagnosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differential_diagnosis) of Parkinson's is among the most difficult in [neurology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurology).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeimKrismerDe_MarziSeppi2017915-144] Differentiating early PD from [atypical parkinsonian disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson-plus_syndrome) is a major difficulty. In their initial stages, PD can be difficult to distinguish from the atypical neurodegenerative parkinsonisms, including [multiple system atrophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_system_atrophy), [dementia with Lewy bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dementia_with_Lewy_bodies), and the [tauopathies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauopathies) [progressive supranuclear palsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_supranuclear_palsy) and [corticobasal degeneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corticobasal_degeneration).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETolosaGarridoScholzPoewe2021389-145][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECaproniColosimo202021-146] Other conditions that may present similarly to PD include vascular Parkinsonism, [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease), and [frontotemporal dementia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontotemporal_dementia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETolosaGarridoScholzPoewe2021390-147][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECaproniColosimo202015,_21-148]

The International Parkinson and Movement Disorder Society has proposed a set of criteria that, unlike the standard Queen's Square Brain Bank Criteria, includes non-exclusionary "red-flag" clinical features that may not suggest PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETolosaGarridoScholzPoewe2021390–391-149] A large number of "red flags" has been proposed and adopted for various conditions that might mimic the symptoms of PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECaproniColosimo202014-150] Diagnostic tests, including gene sequencing, molecular imaging techniques, and assessment of smell may also distinguish PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETolosaGarridoScholzPoewe2021392-139] MRI is particularly powerful due to several unique features for atypical parkinsonisms.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETolosaGarridoScholzPoewe2021392-139]

## Management

Main article: [Management of Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_of_Parkinson's_disease)

As of 2025, no disease-modifying therapies exist that reverse or slow neurodegeneration.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrottySchwarzschild20201-93][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFabbriRascolFoltynieCarroll20242-94] Management typically combines lifestyle modifications with [physical therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_therapy).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConnollyLang2014-153] Current pharmacological interventions purely target symptoms, by either increasing endogenous [dopamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine) levels or directly mimicking dopamine's effect on the patient's brain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEde_BieClarkeEspayFox20203-154][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConnollyLang2014-153] These include dopamine agonists, MAO-B inhibitors, and levodopa—the most widely used and effective drug.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEde_BieClarkeEspayFox20201,_3-155][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEConnollyLang2014-153] The optimal time to initiate pharmacological treatment is debated,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKobylecki2020395-156] but initial dopamine agonist and MAO-B inhibitor treatment and later levodopa therapy are common.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEde_BieClarkeEspayFox20204-157] Invasive procedures such as [deep brain stimulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_brain_stimulation) may be used when medications are ineffective.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELimousinFoltynie2019234-158][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBronsteinTagliatiAltermanLozano2011169-159]

### Medications

#### Levodopa



[LCE (levodopa/carbidopa/entacapone)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbidopa/levodopa/entacapone) pills contain a cocktail of the dopamine precursor levodopa and [COMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catechol-O-methyltransferase) and [AAAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromatic_L-amino_acid_decarboxylase) inhibitors.

[Levodopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levodopa) is the most widely used and most effective therapy—the [gold standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_standard_\(test\))—for PD treatment.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEde_BieClarkeEspayFox20201,_3-155] The compound occurs naturally and is the immediate precursor for dopamine synthesis in the dopaminergic neurons of the substantia nigra.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETambascoRomoliCalabresi20181239-160] Levodopa administration reduces the dopamine deficiency in parkinsonism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeWittFahn2016S5-S6-161][^cite_note-FOOTNOTETambascoRomoliCalabresi20181239–1240-162]

Despite its efficacy, levodopa poses several challenges. Its administration has been called the "pharmacologist's nightmare".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETambascoRomoliCalabresi20181240-163][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELetaKlingelhoeferLongardnerCampagnolo20231466-164] Its metabolism outside the brain by [aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromatic_L-amino_acid_decarboxylase)  and [catechol-O-methyltransferase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catechol-O-methyltransferase)  can cause nausea and vomiting; inhibitors such as [carbidopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbidopa), [entacapone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entacapone), and [benserazide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benserazide) are usually taken with levodopa to mitigate these effects.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELetaKlingelhoeferLongardnerCampagnolo20231466–1468-165][^cite_note-FOOTNOTETambascoRomoliCalabresi20181241-166][^cite_note-168] Long-term levodopa use may also [induce dyskinesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levodopa-induced_dyskinesia) and motor fluctuations.[^cite_note-169] Although this often causes levodopa use to be delayed to later stages, earlier administration leads to improved motor function and quality of life.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEde_BieClarkeEspayFox20201,_3–4-170]

#### Dopamine agonists

[Dopamine agonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine_agonists) are an alternative or complement to levodopa therapy. They activate dopamine receptors in the striatum, with reduced risk of motor fluctuations and dyskinesia, and are efficacious in both early- and late-stage PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJingYangTaximaimaitiWang20231226-171] The agonist [apomorphine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apomorphine) is often used for drug-resistant OFF time in later-stage PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJingYangTaximaimaitiWang20231226-171][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKobylecki2020396-172] After five years of use, impulse-control disorders may occur in over 40% of those taking dopamine agonists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKobylecki2020395-156] A problematic, narcotic-like withdrawal effect may occur when agonist use is reduced or stopped.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKobylecki2020395-156][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEde_BieClarkeEspayFox20201-173] Compared to levodopa, dopamine agonists are more likely to cause fatigue, daytime sleepiness, and hallucinations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEde_BieClarkeEspayFox20201-173]

#### MAO-B inhibitors

MAO-B inhibitors—such as [safinamide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safinamide), [selegiline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selegiline) and [rasagiline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasagiline)—increase the amount of dopamine in the basal ganglia by inhibiting the activity of [monoamine oxidase B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoamine_oxidase_B), an enzyme that breaks down dopamine.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobakisFahn2015433–434-174] These compounds mildly alleviate motor symptoms when used as monotherapy, but can also be used with levodopa and at any disease stage.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobakisFahn2015433-175] Common side effects are nausea, dizziness, insomnia, sleepiness, and orthostatic hypotension.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEArmstrongOkun2020-176] MAO-Bs are known to increase serotonin and cause a potentially dangerous condition known as [serotonin syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin_syndrome).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobakisFahn2015435-177]

#### Other drugs

Treatments for non-motor symptoms of PD have not been well studied, and many medications are used [off-label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-label).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETannerOstrem2024-73] A diverse range of symptoms beyond those related to motor function can be treated pharmaceutically.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEThe_National_Collaborating_Centre_for_Chronic_Conditions-178] Examples include [cholinesterase inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetylcholinesterase_inhibitor) for cognitive impairment and [modafinil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modafinil) for [excessive daytime sleepiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excessive_daytime_sleepiness).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeppiRay_ChaudhuriCoelhoFox2019183,_185,_188-179] [Fludrocortisone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fludrocortisone), [midodrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midodrine), and [droxidopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droxidopa) are commonly used off-label for orthostatic hypotension related to autonomic dysfunction. Sublingual [atropine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atropine) or [botulinum toxin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botulinum_toxin) injections may be used off-label for drooling. [SSRIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSRIs) and [SNRIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNRIs) are often used for depression related to PD, but a risk exists of [serotonin syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin_syndrome) with the SSRI or SNRI antidepressants.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETannerOstrem2024-73] [Doxepin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doxepin) and rasagiline may reduce physical fatigue in PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEElbersVerhoefvan_WegenBerendse2015-180]

### Invasive interventions



Placement of an electrode into the brain for [deep brain stimulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_brain_stimulation)

Surgery for PD first appeared in the 19th century, and by the 1960s, had evolved into [ablative brain surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ablative_brain_surgery) that lesioned the [basal ganglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basal_ganglia), [thalamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalamus), or [globus pallidus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globus_pallidus) (a [pallidotomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallidotomy)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELozanoTamLozano20181–2-181] The discovery of levodopa for PD treatment caused ablative therapies to largely disappear.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELozanoTamLozano20182-182][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBronsteinTagliatiAltermanLozano2011165-183] Ablative surgeries experienced a resurgence in the 1990s, but were quickly superseded by newly developed [deep brain stimulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_brain_stimulation) (DBS).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBronsteinTagliatiAltermanLozano2011165-183] Although [gamma knife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma_knife) and [high-intensity focused ultrasound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-intensity_focused_ultrasound) surgeries have been developed for pallidotomies and [thalamotomies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalamotomies), their use is rare as of 2025.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELozanoTamLozano20186-184][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoosaMartínez-FernándezEliasdel_Alamo20191244–1249-185]

DBS involves the implantation of [electrodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrodes) called [neurostimulators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurostimulation), which send electrical impulses to specific parts of the brain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELimousinFoltynie2019234-158] DBS for the [subthalamic nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subthalamic_nucleus) and [globus pallidus interna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globus_pallidus_interna) has high efficacy for up to 2 years, but long-term efficacy is unclear and likely decreases with time.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELimousinFoltynie2019234-158] DBS typically targets rigidity and tremor,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBronsteinTagliatiAltermanLozano2011168-186] and is recommended for PD patients who are intolerant or do not respond to medication.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBronsteinTagliatiAltermanLozano2011169-159] Cognitive impairment is the most common exclusion criteria.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBronsteinTagliatiAltermanLozano2011166-187]

### Rehabilitation

Further information: [Management of Parkinson's disease § Rehabilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_of_Parkinson's_disease#Rehabilitation)

Although pharmacological therapies can improve symptoms, autonomy, and the ability to perform everyday tasks is still reduced by PD. Rehabilitation is often useful, but the scientific support for any single rehabilitation treatment is limited.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETofaniRanieriFabbriniBerardi2020891-188]

Exercise programs are often recommended, with preliminary evidence of efficacy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELangeskov-ChristensenFranzénGrøndahl_HvidDalgas2024-67][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEErnstFolkertsGollanLieker2023-189][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrottySchwarzschild20201–2-190][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAhlskog2011292-191] Regular [physical exercise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_exercise) with or without physical therapy can be beneficial to maintain and improve mobility, flexibility, strength, gait speed, and quality of life.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEErnstFolkertsGollanLieker2023-189] Aerobic, mind-body, and resistance training may be beneficial in alleviating PD-associated depression and anxiety.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAhlskog2011292-191][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECostaPratide_OliveiraBrito2024-192] [Strength training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strength_training) may increase [manual dexterity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manual_dexterity) and strength, facilitating daily tasks that require grasping objects.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERamazzinaBernazzoliCostantino2017620–623-193] Aerobic exercise, resistance training, and balance- and task-specific training have been found to improve strength, [VO<sub>2</sub> max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VO2_max) and balance. While flexibility training is commonly used, it has a lower strength of recommendation compared to aerobic and resistance training.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOsborneBotkinColon-SemenzaDeAngelis20223,_7–10,_14-194]

A Parkinson's patient on a tricycle

Exercise, like the [tricycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricycle) ride of this PD patient, is often recommended.



Participants at a Power for Parkinson's exercise class do exercises specifically targeting Parkinson's symptoms.

In improving flexibility and range of motion for people experiencing rigidity, generalized relaxation techniques such as gentle rocking have been found to decrease excessive muscle tension. Other effective techniques to promote relaxation include slow rotational movements of the extremities and trunk, rhythmic initiation, [diaphragmatic breathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaphragmatic_breathing), and [meditation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meditation).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEO'SullivanSchmitz2007873,_876-195] Deep diaphragmatic breathing may also improve chest-wall mobility and [vital capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vital_capacity) decreased by the stooped posture and respiratory dysfunctions of advanced Parkinson's.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEO'SullivanSchmitz2007880-196] Rehabilitation techniques targeting gait and the challenges posed by bradykinesia, shuffling, and decreased arm swing include [pole walking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_walking), [treadmill walking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treadmill), and [marching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marching) exercises.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEO'SullivanSchmitz2007879-197] Long-term physiotherapy (greater than six months) reduces the need for antiparkinsonian medication; multidisciplinary rehabilitation programs combined with physiotherapy can result in reduction in the levodopa-equivalent dose.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOkadaOhtsukaKamataYamamoto20211619–1620,_1624–1625-198]

[Speech therapies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_therapies) such as the [Lee Silverman voice treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Silverman_voice_treatment) may reduce the effect of speech disorders associated with PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcDonnellRischbiethSchammerSeaforth2018607–609-199] [Occupational therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_therapy) as a rehabilitation strategy can improve quality of life by enabling people with PD to find engaging activities and communal roles, adapt to their living environment, and improve domestic and work abilities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETofaniRanieriFabbriniBerardi2020891,_900-200]

### Diet

PD poses digestive problems such as constipation and [prolonged emptying of stomach contents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastroparesis), and a balanced diet with periodical nutritional assessments is recommended to avoid weight loss or gain and minimize the consequences of gastrointestinal dysfunction. In particular, a Mediterranean diet is advised and may slow disease progression.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELister202099–100-201][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarichellaCeredaPezzoli20091888-202] As it can compete for uptake with [amino acids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acids) derived from protein, levodopa should be taken 30 minutes before meals to minimize such competition. Low-protein diets may also be needed by later stages.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarichellaCeredaPezzoli20091888-202] As the disease advances, swallowing difficulties often arise. Using [thickening agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thickening_agent) for liquid intake and an upright posture when eating may be useful; both measures reduce the risk of choking. [Gastrostomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrostomy) can be used to deliver food directly into the stomach.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarichellaCeredaPezzoli20091887-203][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPasrichaGuerrero-LopezKuo2024212-204] Increased water and fiber intakes are used to treat constipation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPasrichaGuerrero-LopezKuo2024216-205]

### Palliative care

As PD is incurable, palliative care aims to improve the quality of life for both the patient and family by alleviating the symptoms and stress associated with illness.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhoche2012S2–S3-206][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilcox201026-207][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFerrellConnorCordesDahlin2007741-208] Early integration of palliative care into the disease course is recommended, rather than delaying until later stages.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhoche2012S2–S3-206] Palliative-care specialists can help with physical symptoms, emotional factors such as loss of function and jobs, depression, and fear, as well as existential concerns.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhoche2012S3-209] Palliative-care team members also help guide difficult decisions caused by disease progression, such as wishes for a [feeding tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feeding_tube), [noninvasive ventilator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-invasive_ventilation) or [tracheostomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracheotomy), use of [cardiopulmonary resuscitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiopulmonary_resuscitation), and entering [hospice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospice) care.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECasey201320–22-210][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBernatBeresford2013135,_137,_138-211]

## Prognosis

| --- | --- | --- |

| Mild-motor predominant | 14.3 | 20.2 |
| Intermediate | 8.2 | 13.1 |
| Diffuse malignant | 3.5 | 8.1 |

Because PD has multiple [causes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cause_\(medicine\)) and can affect people in different ways, it is hard to predict how it will progress, and prognoses can be highly variable.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECorcoranKluger2023956-212][^cite_note-FOOTNOTETolosaGarridoScholzPoewe2021385-215] On average, life expectancy is reduced in those with PD, with younger age of onset resulting in greater life expectancy decreases.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDommershuijsenDarweeshBen-ShlomoKluger20232–3-216] Although PD subtype categorization is controversial, the 2017 Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative study identified three broad scorable subtypes of increasing severity and more rapid progression - mild-motor predominant, intermediate, and diffuse malignant. Mean years of survival after diagnosis were 20.2, 13.1, and 8.1.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECorcoranKluger2023956-212]

Around 30% of individuals with PD develop dementia, which is 12 times more likely to occur in the elderly with severe PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurueta-GoyenaMuiñoGómez-Esteban201726-217] Dementia is less likely to arise in tremor-dominant PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurueta-GoyenaMuiñoGómez-Esteban201727-218] Parkinson's disease dementia is associated with a reduced [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life) in people with PD and their [caregivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caregiver), increased mortality, and a higher probability of needing [nursing home care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursing_home_care).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECaballolMartíTolosa2007S358-219]

The incidence rate of falls is around 45–68%, three times that of healthy individuals, and half of such falls result in serious injuries. Falls increase [mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortality_rate).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurueta-GoyenaMuiñoGómez-Esteban2024395-220] Around 90% of those with PD develop [hypokinetic dysarthria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypokinetic_dysarthria), which worsens with disease progression and can hinder communication.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAtalarOguzGenc2023163-221] Over 80% develop [difficulty swallowing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysphagia); consequent inhalation of gastric and oropharyngeal secretions can lead to [aspiration pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspiration_pneumonia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChuaWangChanChan20241-222]

## Epidemiology

As of 2024, PD is the second-most common neurodegenerative disease and the fastest growing in total cases.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-ShlomoDarweeshLlibre-GuerraMarras2024283-223][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVardenWalkerO'Callaghan20241-224] As of 2023, global [prevalence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prevalence) was estimated to be 1.51 per 1000.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZhuCuiZhangYan2024e464-225] Men are reportedly 1.5 times more likely to have PD than women, but this may vary with different populations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESiddiqiOrtizSimardLi2025-226] Age plays the biggest role in the risk of getting PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDelizTannerGonzalez-Latapi2024166-227] As [global life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) has increased, PD prevalence has also risen, with an estimated increase in cases by 74% from 1990 to 2016. However, increased life expectancy does not fully explain this change: when age was accounted for, PD prevalence rates still increased by 21.7% in this time period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-ShlomoDarweeshLlibre-GuerraMarras2024284-228] The number is predicted to rise to over 12 million by 2040.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDorseyShererOkunBloem2018S4-229]

This increase may be due to a number of global factors, including prolonged life expectancy, increased industrialisation, and [decreased smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_control).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-ShlomoDarweeshLlibre-GuerraMarras2024284-228] Although genetics is the sole factor in a minority of cases, most cases of PD are likely a result of [gene-environment interactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene-environment_interactions); [concordance studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordance_\(genetics\)) with [twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twins) have found PD [heritability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritability) to be just 30%.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-ShlomoDarweeshLlibre-GuerraMarras2024286-230] The influence of multiple genetic and environmental factors complicates epidemiological efforts.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDelizTannerGonzalez-Latapi2024165-231]

Relative to Europe and North America, disease prevalence is lower in Africa, but similar in Latin America.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-ShlomoDarweeshLlibre-GuerraMarras2024285-232] Although China is predicted to have nearly half of the global PD population by 2030,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELiMaCuiHe20191-233] estimates of prevalence in Asia vary.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-ShlomoDarweeshLlibre-GuerraMarras2024285-232] Potential explanations for these geographic differences include genetic variation, environmental factors, [health care access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_access), and life expectancy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-ShlomoDarweeshLlibre-GuerraMarras2024285-232] Although PD incidence and prevalence may vary by race and ethnicity, significant disparities in care, diagnosis, and study participation limit [generalizability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalizability) and lead to conflicting results.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBen-ShlomoDarweeshLlibre-GuerraMarras2024285-232][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDelizTannerGonzalez-Latapi2024165-231] Within the United States, high rates of PD have been identified in the [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States), the [South](179553.md), and agricultural regions of other states, collectively termed the "PD belt".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDelizTannerGonzalez-Latapi2024164–165-234] The association between rural residence and PD has been hypothesized to be caused by environmental factors including herbicides, pesticides, and industrial waste.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDelizTannerGonzalez-Latapi2024164–165-234]

Patient Pierre D. (right) served as the model for [William Gowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gowers_\(neurologist\))' illustration of the disease.

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELewisPlun-FavreauRowleySpillane2020389-235]

In 1817, English physician [James Parkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Parkinson) published the first full medical description of the disease as a neurological syndrome in his monograph *An Essay on the Shaking Palsy*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoetz20111–2-236][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELees2007S327-237] He presented six clinical cases, including three he had observed on the streets near [Hoxton Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoxton_Square) in [London](17867.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoetz20112-238] Parkinson described three cardinal symptoms: tremor, postural instability, and "paralysis" (undistinguished from rigidity or bradykinesia), and speculated that the disease was caused by trauma to the [spinal cord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_cord).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELouis19971069-239][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELees2007S328-240]

Little discussion of or investigation into the "shaking palsy" occurred until 1861, when Frenchman [Jean-Martin Charcot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Martin_Charcot)—regarded as the father of [neurology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurology)—began expanding Parkinson's description, adding bradykinesia as one of the four cardinal symptoms.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELouis19971069-239][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoetz20112-238][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELees2007S328-240] In 1877, Charcot renamed the disease after Parkinson, as the tremor suggested by "shaking palsy" is not present in all.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoetz20112-238][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELees2007S328-240] Subsequent neurologists who made early advances to the understanding of PD include [Armand Trousseau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armand_Trousseau), [William Gowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gowers_\(neurologist\)), [Samuel Kinnier Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Alexander_Kinnier_Wilson), and [Wilhelm Erb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Heinrich_Erb).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELees2007S329-241]

Although Parkinson is typically credited with the first detailed description of PD, many previous texts reference some of the disease's clinical signs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBereczki2010290-242] In his essay, Parkinson himself acknowledged partial descriptions by [Galen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galen), [William Cullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Cullen), [Johann Juncker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Juncker), and others.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELees2007S328-240] Possible earlier but incomplete descriptions include a [Nineteenth Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteenth_Dynasty_of_Egypt) Egyptian [papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papyrus), the [ayurvedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayurvedic) text *[Charaka Samhita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charaka_Samhita)*, [Ecclesiastes 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastes_12)\:3, and a discussion of tremors by [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELees2007S328-240][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlonder20183–4-243] Multiple [traditional Chinese medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_medicine) texts may include references to PD, including a discussion in the [Yellow Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Emperor)'s Internal Classic (c. 425–221 BC) of a disease with symptoms of tremor, stiffness, staring, and stooped posture.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlonder20183–4-243] In 2009, a systematic description of PD was found in the Hungarian medical text *Pax corporis* written by Ferenc Pápai Páriz in 1690, some 120 years before Parkinson. Although Páriz correctly described all four cardinal signs, it was only published in Hungarian and was not widely distributed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBereczki2010290–293-244][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBlonder20183-245]

In 1912, [Frederic Lewy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederic_Lewy) described microscopic particles in affected brains, later named Lewy bodies.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESousa-SantosPozzobonTeixeira20241–2-246] In 1919, [Konstantin Tretiakoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Tretiakoff) reported that the substantia nigra was the main brain structure affected, corroborated by [Rolf Hassler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolf_Hassler) in 1938.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELees2007S331-247] The underlying changes in dopamine signaling were identified in the 1950s, largely by [Arvid Carlsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvid_Carlsson) and [Oleh Hornykiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleh_Hornykiewicz).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFahn2008S500–S501,_S504–S505-248] In 1997, [Polymeropoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihael_Polymeropoulos) and colleagues at the [NIH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health) discovered the first gene for PD,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPolymeropoulosLavedanLeroyIde1997-249] *SNCA*, which encodes alpha-synuclein. Alpha-synuclein was, in turn, found to be the main component of Lewy bodies by [Spillantini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Grazia_Spillantini), [Trojanowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Q._Trojanowski), [Goedert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Goedert), and others.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchulz-Schaeffer2010131-250] Anticholinergics and surgery were the only treatments until the use of levodopa,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELanska2010507-251][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuridiLozano19971180–1183-252] which, although first synthesized by [Casimir Funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_Funk) in 1911,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFahn2008S497-253] did not enter clinical use until 1967.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFahn2008S501-254] By the late 1980s, deep brain stimulation introduced by [Alim Louis Benabid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alim_Louis_Benabid) and colleagues at [Grenoble](50972.md), France, emerged as an additional treatment.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECoffey2009209–210-255]

## Society and culture

### Social consequences

A sketch showing hypomimia, a blank, expressionless face

The [reduced ability to facially express emotions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypomimia)—as depicted here by French anatomist [Paul Richer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Richer) in 1888—can harm social well-being.

For some people with PD, masked facial expressions and difficulty moderating facial expressions of emotion or recognizing other people's facial expressions can affect social well-being.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPrengerMadrayVan_HedgerAnello20202-256] As the condition progresses, tremor, other motor symptoms, difficulty communicating, or mobility issues may interfere with social engagement, causing individuals with PD to feel isolated.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrooksCarterWilsonWynne20232,7-257] Public perception and awareness of PD symptoms such as shaking, hallucinating, slurring speech, and being off balance is lacking in some countries and can lead to stigma.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrooksCarterWilsonWynne20232-258]

### Cost

The economic cost of Parkinson's to both individuals and society is high.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchiessCataldiOkunFothergill-Misbah2022931-259] In many low- and middle-income countries, public health systems may not fully cover Parkinson's disease therapies, leading to disparities in access to treatment. In contrast, high-income countries with universal healthcare typically cover standard treatments such as levodopa and specialist care. [^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchiessCataldiOkunFothergill-Misbah2022931-259] Indirect costs include lifetime earnings losses due to premature death, productivity losses, and caregiver burdens.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYangHamiltonKopilBeck20201-260] The duration and progressive nature of PD can place a heavy burden on caregivers;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchiessCataldiOkunFothergill-Misbah2022933-261] family members, such as spouses, dedicate around 22 hours per week to care.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYangHamiltonKopilBeck20201-260]

In 2010, the total economic burden of PD across Europe, including indirect and direct medical costs, was estimated to be €13.9 billion (US\$14.9 billion).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchiessCataldiOkunFothergill-Misbah2022929-262] The total burden in the United States was estimated to be \$51.9 billion in 2017, and is projected to surpass \$79 billion by 2037.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYangHamiltonKopilBeck20201-260] As of 2022, no rigorous economic surveys had been performed for low- or middle-income nations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchiessCataldiOkunFothergill-Misbah2022930-263] Preventive care has been identified as crucial to slow the rapidly increasing incidence of Parkinson's from overwhelming national health systems.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchiessCataldiOkunFothergill-Misbah2022933-261]

### Advocacy

The birthday of James Parkinson, 11 April, has been designated as World Parkinson's Day.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELees2007S327–S334-264] A red tulip was chosen by international organizations as the symbol of the disease in 2005; it represents the 'James Parkinson' tulip [cultivar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultivar), registered in 1981 by a Dutch horticulturalist.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlaxoSmithKline-265]

Advocacy organizations include the [National Parkinson Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Parkinson_Foundation), which has provided more than \$180 million in care, research, and support services since 1982,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENational_Parkinson_Foundation-266] [Parkinson's Disease Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson's_Disease_Foundation), which has distributed more than \$115 million for research and nearly \$50 million for education and advocacy programs since its founding in 1957 by William Black;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETime_1960-267][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParkinson's_Disease_Foundation-268] the [American Parkinson Disease Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Parkinson_Disease_Association), founded in 1961;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAmerican_Parkinson_Disease_Association-269] and [Parkinson's Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkinson's_Europe?action=edit\&redlink=1), founded in 1992.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParkinson's_Europe-270]

### Notable cases

Michael J. Fox and Muhammad Ali are seen speaking to reporters, with the marble walls of the U.S. Senate behind them

Actor [Michael J. Fox](68904.md) and boxer [Muhammad Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali) (center) are pictured in 2002 speaking before the [US Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Senate) to urge increased funding for Parkinson's research.

In the 21st century, the diagnosis of PD among notable figures has increased the public's understanding of the disorder.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParkinson's_Foundation-271] Actor [Michael J. Fox](68904.md) was diagnosed with PD at 29 years old,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEThe_Michael_J._Fox_Foundation_for_Parkinson's_Research-272] and has used his diagnosis to increase awareness of the disease.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavis2007-273] To illustrate the effects of the disease, Fox has appeared without medication in television roles and before the [United States Congress](31756.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrockes2009-274] [The Michael J. Fox Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Michael_J._Fox_Foundation), which he founded in 2000, has raised over \$2 billion for Parkinson's research.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBurlesonBreen2023-275]

Boxer [Muhammad Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali) showed signs of PD when he was 38, but was undiagnosed until he was 42; he has been called the "world's most famous Parkinson's patient". [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrey2006-276] Whether he had PD or [parkinsonism related to boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dementia_pugilistica) is unresolved.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMatthews200610–23-277] Cyclist and Olympic medalist [Davis Phinney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_Phinney), diagnosed with PD at 40, started the [Davis Phinney Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_Phinney_Foundation) in 2004 to support PD research.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMacur2008-278][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavis_Phinney_Foundation-279]

Musician [Ozzy Osbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozzy_Osbourne) was diagnosed with PD; he performed a 5 July 2025, farewell reunion concert with the band he co-founded, [Black Sabbath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sabbath), weeks before his death. The event raised over \$190 million, some of which went to Parkinson's disease research.[^cite_note-280]

[Adolf Hitler](2731583.md) is believed to have had PD, and the condition may have partially influenced his decision-making.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuptaKimAgarwalLieber20151447–1452-281][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoettcherBonneySmithermanSughrue2015E8-282][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELieberman199695-283]

## Clinical research

Main article: [Research in Parkinson's disease](25200778.md)

A European astronaut is seen in zero gravity conducting research with a micropipette

Astronaut [Alexander Gerst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Gerst) conducted PD research aboard the [International Space Station](15043.md) in 2018.

As of 2024, no disease-modifying therapies exist that reverse or slow the progression of PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrottySchwarzschild20201-93][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFabbriRascolFoltynieCarroll20242-94] Active research directions include the search for new [animal models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_model) of the disease and development and trial of [gene therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_therapy), [stem cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stem_cell) transplants, and [neuroprotective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroprotective) agents.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPoeweSeppiTannerHalliday2017-284] Improved treatments will likely combine therapeutic strategies to manage symptoms and enhance outcomes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPardo-MorenoGarcía-MoralesSuleiman-MartosRivas-Domínguez20231-285] Reliable [biomarkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomarker_\(medicine\)) are needed for early diagnosis, and research criteria for their identification have been established.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELiLe2020183-286][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeinzelBergGasserChen2019-287]

### Neuroprotective treatments

See also: [Anti-α-synuclein drug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-%CE%B1-synuclein_drug)

[Drugs that prevent alpha-synuclein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-alpha-synuclein_drugs) oligomerization and aggregation or promote their clearance are under active investigation, and potential therapeutic strategies include [small molecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_molecules) and [immunotherapies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunotherapies) including [vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccines) and [monoclonal antibodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoclonal_antibodies).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPardo-MorenoGarcía-MoralesSuleiman-MartosRivas-Domínguez202312–13-288][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlfaidiBarkerKuan20241-289][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJasutkarOhMouradian2022208-290] While immunotherapies show promise, their efficacy is often inconsistent.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlfaidiBarkerKuan20241-289] Anti-inflammatory drugs that target [NLRP3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NLRP3) and the [JAK-STAT signaling pathway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JAK-STAT_signaling_pathway) offer another potential therapeutic approach.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPardo-MorenoGarcía-MoralesSuleiman-MartosRivas-Domínguez202310–11-291]

As the [gut microbiome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gut_microbiome) in PD is often disrupted and produces toxic compounds, [fecal microbiota transplants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fecal_microbiota_transplants) might restore a healthy microbiome and alleviate various motor and non-motor symptoms.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPardo-MorenoGarcía-MoralesSuleiman-MartosRivas-Domínguez202312–13-288] [Neurotrophic factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotrophic_factors)—[peptides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptides) that enhance the growth, maturation, and survival of neurons—show modest results, but require invasive surgical administration. [Viral vectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_vectors) may represent a more feasible delivery platform.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPardo-MorenoGarcía-MoralesSuleiman-MartosRivas-Domínguez202313-292] Calcium channel blockers may restore the calcium imbalance present in PD, and are being investigated as a neuroprotective treatment.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPardo-MorenoGarcía-MoralesSuleiman-MartosRivas-Domínguez202310-293] Other therapies, such as [deferiprone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deferiprone), may reduce the abnormal accumulation of iron in PD.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPardo-MorenoGarcía-MoralesSuleiman-MartosRivas-Domínguez202310-293]

### Cell-based therapies

Main article: [Cell-based therapies for Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell-based_therapies_for_Parkinson's_disease)

Researchers at Argonne National Laboratory examining a culture of induced pluripotent stem cells

Researchers at [Argonne National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonne_National_Laboratory) examine [induced pluripotent stem cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_pluripotent_stem_cells) (iPSCs) for use in Parkinson's and other diseases: the [action potentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_potentials) of one such iPSC differentiated into a [dopaminergic neuron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopaminergic_neuron) are visible at right.

In contrast to other neurodegenerative disorders, many PD symptoms can be attributed to the loss of a single cell type. Consequently, dopaminergic neuron regeneration is a promising therapeutic approach.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParmarGrealishHenchcliffe2020103-294] Although most initial research sought to generate dopaminergic neuron precursor cells from fetal brain tissue,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParmarGrealishHenchcliffe2020103–104-295] [pluripotent stem cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluripotency)—particularly [induced pluripotent stem cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_pluripotent_stem_cells) (iPSC)—have become an increasingly popular tissue source.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParmarGrealishHenchcliffe2020106-296][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHenchcliffeParmar2018134-297]

Both fetal and iPSC-derived DA neurons have been transplanted into patients in clinical trials.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParmarGrealishHenchcliffe2020106,_108-298][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchweitzerSongHerringtonPark20201926-299] Although some individuals have seen improvement, the results are highly variable. Adverse effects, such as [dyskinesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tardive_dyskinesia) arising from excess dopamine release by the transplanted tissues, have also been observed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParmarGrealishHenchcliffe2020105,_109-300][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHenchcliffeParmar2018132-301]

### Gene therapy

Main article: [Gene therapy in Parkinson's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_therapy_in_Parkinson's_disease)

[Gene therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_therapy) for PD seeks to restore the healthy function of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra by delivering genetic material—typically through a viral vector—to these diseased cells.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVan_LaarVan_LaarSan_SebastianMerola2021S174-302][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHittiYangGonzalez-AlegreBaltuch201916-303] This material may deliver a functional, [wild-type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_type) version of a gene, or [knock down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_knockdown) a pathological variant.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHittiYangGonzalez-AlegreBaltuch201916–17-304] Experimental gene therapies for PD have aimed to increase the expression of [growth factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_factors) or enzymes involved in dopamine synthesis, such as [tyrosine hydroxylase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrosine_hydroxylase).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVan_LaarVan_LaarSan_SebastianMerola2021S174,_S176-305] The one-time delivery of genes circumvents the recurrent invasive administration required to administer some peptides and proteins to the brain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHittiYangGonzalez-AlegreBaltuch201921-306] MicroRNAs are an emerging PD gene therapy platform that may serve as an alternative to viral vectors.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShaheenShaheenOsamaNashwan20245–6-307]
