The **Saab JAS 39 Gripen** (Swedish: [\[ˈɡrǐːpɛn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Swedish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/90/Sv-JAS_39_Gripen.ogg/Sv-JAS_39_Gripen.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/90/Sv-JAS_39_Gripen.ogg/Sv-JAS_39_Gripen.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; lit. '[Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin)')[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-FOOTNOTENilsson2012-4] is a [light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_fighter) single-engine supersonic [multirole fighter aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multirole_combat_aircraft) manufactured by the Swedish [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace) and defence company [Saab AB](26968.md). The Gripen has a [delta wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_wing) and [canard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canard_\(aeronautics\)) configuration with [relaxed stability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relaxed_stability) design and [fly-by-wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fly-by-wire) flight controls. Later aircraft are fully [NATO](21133.md) interoperable. As of 2025, more than 280 Gripens of all models, A–F, have been delivered.[^cite_note-6]

In 1979, the [Swedish government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Sweden) began development studies for "an aircraft for fighter, attack, and [reconnaissance](20338498.md)" ([Swedish](26689.md): *ett jakt-, attack- och spaningsflygplan*, hence "JAS") to replace the [Saab 35 Draken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_35_Draken) and [37 Viggen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_37_Viggen) in the [Swedish Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Air_Force). A new design from Saab was selected and developed as the JAS 39. The first flight took place in 1988, with delivery of the first serial-production airplane in 1993.[^cite_note-Milestones-2] It entered service with the Swedish Air Force in 1996.[^cite_note-Milestones-2] Upgraded variants, featuring more advanced avionics and adaptations for longer mission times, began entering service in 2003.

To market the aircraft internationally, Saab formed partnerships and collaborative efforts with overseas aerospace companies. On the export market, early models of the Gripen achieved moderate success, with sales to nations in Central Europe, South Africa, and Southeast Asia. Bribery was suspected in some of these procurements, but Swedish authorities closed the investigation in 2009.[^cite_note-Kwast-7]

A major redesign of the Gripen series, previously referred to as *Gripen NG* (Next Generation) or *Super JAS*, now designated *JAS 39E/F Gripen*[^cite_note-\:1-8] began deliveries to the Swedish Air Force and [Brazilian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Air_Force) in 2019. Changes from the JAS C to JAS E include a larger fuselage, a more powerful engine, increased weapons payload capability, and new cockpit, avionics architecture, electronic warfare system, a new AESA radar and other improvements.

## Development

### Origins

In the late 1970s, Sweden sought to replace its aging [Saab 35 Draken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_35_Draken) and [Saab 37 Viggen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_37_Viggen).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrawley2002147-9] The [Swedish Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Air_Force) required an affordable Mach 2 aircraft with good [short-field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-field_landing) performance for a defensive dispersed basing plan in the event of invasion; the plan included 800 m long by 17 m wide rudimentary runways that were part of the [Bas 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas_90) system.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAltaya2011''Características_especiais''-12] One goal was for the aircraft to be smaller than the Viggen while equalling or improving on its payload-range characteristics.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpick2000426–27-13] Early proposals included the [Saab 38](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_38), also called B3LA, intended as an attack aircraft and trainer,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBjöreman2009139–49-14] and the [A 20](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab%20A%2020), a development of the Viggen that would have capabilities as a fighter, attack and sea reconnaissance aircraft.[^cite_note-15] Several foreign designs were also studied, including the [General Dynamics F-16 Fighting Falcon](11642.md), the [McDonnell Douglas F/A-18 Hornet](45998.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200372-16] the [Northrop F-20 Tigershark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_F-20_Tigershark) and the [Dassault Mirage 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGreenSwanborough1987225-17] Ultimately, the Swedish government opted for a new fighter to be developed by [Saab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_Group).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200372-16]

In 1979, the government began a study calling for a versatile platform capable of "JAS", standing for *Jakt* ([air-to-air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_warfare)), *Attack* ([air-to-surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_attack)), and *Spaning* (reconnaissance), indicating a [multirole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multirole_combat_aircraft), or swingrole, fighter aircraft that could fulfill multiple roles during the same mission.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200372-16] Several Saab designs were reviewed, the most promising being "Project 2105" (redesignated "Project 2108" and, later, "Project 2110"), recommended to the government by the [Defence Materiel Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Defence_Materiel_Administration) (*Försvarets Materielverk*, or FMV).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200372-16] In 1980, *[Industrigruppen JAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrigruppen_JAS)* (*IG JAS*, "JAS Industry Group") was established as a joint venture by [Saab-Scania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_Group#History), [LM Ericsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LM_Ericsson), [Svenska Radioaktiebolaget](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svenska%20Radioaktiebolaget), [Volvo Flygmotor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Flygmotor) and [Försvarets Fabriksverk](https://sv.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%B6rsvarets%20Fabriksverk), the industrial arm of the Swedish armed forces.[^cite_note-18]

The preferred aircraft was a single-engine, lightweight single-seater, embracing [fly-by-wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fly-by-wire) technology, canards, and an aerodynamically unstable design.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200373-19] The powerplant selected was the [Volvo-Flygmotor RM12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_RM12), a licence-built derivative of the [General Electric F404](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_F404)−400; engine development priorities were weight reduction and lowering component count.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200373-19][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECross198627-20] On 30 June 1982, with approval from the [Riksdag](158651.md),[^cite_note-grip_so_far-21] the FMV issued contracts worth [SEK](16709.md) 25.7 billion to Saab, covering five prototypes and an initial batch of 30 production aircraft.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200373,_88-22][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeijsper200312-23] By January 1983, a Viggen was converted to a flying test aircraft for the JAS 39's intended avionics, such as the fly-by-wire controls.[^cite_note-24] The JAS 39 received the name *Gripen* (griffin) via a public competition,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENilsson2012-4] which is the [heraldry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraldry) on Saab's logo.[^cite_note-27]

### Testing, production, and improvements

| --- |

Saab rolled out the first Gripen on 26 April 1987, marking the company's 50th anniversary.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWinchester2004216-28] Originally planned to fly in 1987,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECross198627-20] the first flight was delayed by 18 months due to issues with the flight control system. On 9 December 1988, the first prototype (serial number 39-1) took its 51-minute maiden flight with pilot Stig Holmström at the controls.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200373-19][^cite_note-29] During the test programme, concern surfaced about the aircraft's avionics, specifically the fly-by-wire [flight control system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_flight_control_system) (FCS), and the relaxed stability design. On 2 February 1989, this issue led to the crash of the prototype during an attempted landing at [Linköping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link%C3%B6ping); the test pilot [Lars Rådeström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_R%C3%A5destr%C3%B6m) walked away with a broken elbow. The cause of the crash was identified as [pilot-induced oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot-induced_oscillation), caused by problems with the FCS's pitch-control routine.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200373-19][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

In response to the crash, Saab and US firm Calspan introduced software modifications to the aircraft. A modified [Lockheed NT-33A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_T-33_Shooting_Star) was used to test these improvements, which allowed flight testing to resume 15 months after the accident. On 8 August 1993, production aircraft 39102 was destroyed in an accident at an aerial display for the [Stockholm Water Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_Water_Festival). Test pilot Lars Rådeström lost control of the aircraft during a roll at low altitude when the aircraft stalled, forcing him to eject. Saab later found the problem was high amplification of the pilot's quick and significant stick command inputs. The ensuing investigation and flaw correction further delayed test flying by several months, resuming in December 1993.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200373-19]

The first order included an option for another 110, which was exercised in June 1992. Batch II consisted of 96 one-seat JAS 39As and 14 two-seat JAS 39Bs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeijsper200312,_21-32][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMatláryØ̈sterud2007150-33] The JAS 39B variant is 66 cm (26 in) longer than the JAS 39A to accommodate a second seat, which also necessitated the deletion of the cannon and a reduced internal fuel capacity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200388-34] By April 1994, five prototypes and two series-production Gripens had been completed; but a [beyond-visual-range missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond-visual-range_missile) (BVR) had not yet been selected.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEForsberg1994223-35] A third batch was ordered in June 1997, composed of 50 upgraded single-seat JAS 39Cs and 14 JAS 39D two-seaters,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200388-34] known as 'Turbo Gripen', with NATO compatibility for exports.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAltaya2011''Diversas_versões''_1a-36] Batch III aircraft, delivered between 2002 and 2008, possess more powerful and updated avionics, [in-flight refuelling](237949.md) capability via retractable probes on the aircraft's [starboard side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starboard_side), and an on-board oxygen-generating system (OBOGS) for longer-duration missions.[^cite_note-last_swaf-37] In-flight refuelling was tested via a specially equipped prototype (39‐4) used in successful trials with a [VC10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_VC10) from the [Royal Air Force](25679.md) in 1998.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200388-34]

### Teaming agreements

Head on view of fighter jet banking right while releasing flares against a background of green woodland

Czech Air Force Gripen C deploying defensive flares, 2011

During the 1995 [Paris Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Air_Show), Saab Military Aircraft and [British Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace) (BAe, now [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems)) announced the formation of the joint-venture company Saab-BAe Gripen AB with the goal of adapting, manufacturing, marketing and supporting Gripen worldwide.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200388-34][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELorell2002147-38] The deal involved the conversion of the A and B series aircraft to the "export" C and D series, which developed the Gripen for compatibility with [NATO](21133.md) standards.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEliasson2010256-39] This co-operation was extended in 2001 with the formation of Gripen International to promote export sales.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELorell2002147–48-40] In December 2004, Saab and BAE Systems announced that BAE was to sell a large portion of its stake in Saab, and that Saab would take full responsibility for marketing and export orders of the Gripen.[^cite_note-41] In June 2011, Saab announced that an internal investigation revealed evidence of acts of corruption by BAE Systems, including [money laundering](19390.md) in South Africa, one of the Gripen's customers.[^cite_note-42]

On 26 April 2007, Norway signed a [NOK](64149.md)150 million joint-development agreement with Saab to co-operate in the development programme of the Gripen, including the integration of Norwegian industries in the development of future versions of the aircraft.[^cite_note-nla-43] In June of the same year, Saab also entered an agreement with [Thales Norway A/S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Group) concerning the development of communications systems for the Gripen fighter. This order was the first awarded under the provisions of the Letter of Agreement signed by the [Norwegian Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Ministry_of_Defence) and Gripen International in April 2007.[^cite_note-nla-43] As a result of the [United States diplomatic cables leak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_diplomatic_cables_leak) in 2010, it was revealed that US diplomats had become concerned with co-operation between Norway and Sweden on the topic of the Gripen, and had sought to exert pressure against a Norwegian purchase of the aircraft.[^cite_note-Norway_gripen_local-44]

In December 2007, as part of Gripen International's marketing efforts in Denmark, a deal was signed with Danish technology supplier [Terma A/S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terma_A/S) that let them participate in an Industrial Co-operation programme over the next 10–15 years. The total value of the programme was estimated at over [DKK](236323.md)10 billion, and was partly dependent on a procurement of the Gripen by Denmark.[^cite_note-dla-45] Subsequently, Denmark elected to procure the F-35 Joint Strike Fighter.

### Controversies and scandals

Developing an advanced multi-role fighter was a major undertaking for Sweden. The predecessor 37 Viggen had been criticized for occupying too much of Sweden's military budget and was branded "a cuckoo in the military nest" by critics as early as 1971. At the 1972 party congress of the [Socialdemokraterna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Sweden\)), the dominant party in Swedish politics since the 1950s, a motion was passed to stop any future projects to develop advanced military aircraft.[^cite_note-P3Dok-46] In 1982, the Gripen project passed in the Riksdag by a margin of 176 for and 167 against, with the entire Social Democratic party voting against the proposal due to demands for more studies. A new bill was introduced in 1983[^cite_note-47] and a final approval was given in April 1983 with the condition that the project was to have a predetermined fixed-price contract,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBjöreman2009137–49-48] a decision that would later be criticized as unrealistic due to later cost overruns.[^cite_note-P3Dok-46]

According to Annika Brändström, in the aftermath of the 1989 and 1993 crashes, the Gripen risked a loss of credibility and the weakening of its public image. There was public speculation that failures to address technical problems exposed in the first crash had directly contributed to the second crash, which thus had been avoidable.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrändström200361,_72-49] Brändström observed that media elements had called for greater public accountability and explanation of the project; ill-informed media analysis had also distorted public knowledge of the Gripen.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrändström200357–62-50] The sitting Conservative government quickly endorsed and supported the Gripen – Minister of Defense [Anders Björck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Bj%C3%B6rck) issued a public reassurance that the project was very positive for Sweden.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrändström200358–62,_72–73-51] In connection to the Gripen's marketing efforts to multiple countries, including South Africa, Austria, the Czech Republic and Hungary, there were reports of widespread bribery and corruption by BAE Systems and Saab.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-saab_clean-53] In 2007, Swedish journalists reported that BAE had paid bribes equivalent to millions of dollars.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] Following criminal investigations in eight countries, only one individual in Austria, [Alfons Mensdorf-Pouilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfons_Mensdorf-Pouilly?action=edit\&redlink=1), was prosecuted for bribery. The scandal tarnished the international reputation of the Gripen, BAE Systems, Saab, and Sweden.[^cite_note-saab_clean-53]

### Operational costs

The Gripen's cost has been subject to frequent attention and speculation. Price of purchase is what receives most attention during the procurement process, but over the lifetime of an aircraft system the operating costs will take the larger part of the total budget. It is not uncommon that pilots and aircraft remain on the ground because the allocated funds for training have run out. Without sufficient practice, pilots cannot use the abilities of the aircraft to their full potential, which means a state of the art aircraft with poorly trained pilots can be expected to perform worse than trained pilots in slightly less capable aircraft.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-Flygrevyn-58]

In 2008, Saab announced reduced earnings for that year, partly attributing this to increased marketing costs for the aircraft.[^cite_note-59] In 2008, after Norway said its evaluation found the F-35A would cost them less than the Gripen NG, Saab disputed their cost calculations as overestimates and in excess of real world performance with existing operators.[^cite_note-Norway_calculations-60] A 2007 report by the European Union Institute for Security Studies stated the total research and development costs of Gripen were €1.84 billion.[^cite_note-61] According to a study by [Jane's Information Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane's_Information_Group) in 2012, the Gripen's operational cost was the lowest among several modern fighters; it was estimated at \$4700 per flight hour.[^cite_note-SP.com_operational_cost-62] The Swedish Ministry of Defense estimated the cost of the full system, comprising 60 Gripen E/F, at SEK 90 billion distributed over the period 2013–42. The Swedish Armed Forces estimated that maintaining 100 C/D-model aircraft until 2042 would cost SEK 60 billion (€6.6 billion in 2013), while buying aircraft from a foreign supplier would cost SEK 110 billion (€12.1 billion).[^cite_note-63] In 2023 Aviation Week on Behalf of SAAB conducted a study that found the Gripen C/D costs \$20643 (\$10815 maintenance and \$9828 operations) per hour and the E/F \$22174 per hour (\$12200 maintenance and \$9974 operations).[^cite_note-\:0-64]

### JAS 39E/F and other developments&#x20;



Swedish Air Force Gripen C undergoing in-flight refuelling

A two-seat aircraft, designated "Gripen Demo", was ordered in 2007 as a testbed for various upgrades.[^cite_note-\:3-67][^cite_note-FI_Saab_reveals_Demo-68] It was powered by the [General Electric F414G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_F414#Variants), a development of the [Boeing F/A-18E/F Super Hornet](845012.md)'s engine.[^cite_note-NG_capabilities-69] The Gripen NG's maximum takeoff weight was increased from 14000 to 16000 kg (30900–35300 lb), internal fuel capacity was increased by 40 per cent by relocating the undercarriage, which also allowed for two additional [hardpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardpoint) be added on the fuselage underside. Its combat radius was 1,300 kilometres (810 mi) when carrying six [air-to-air missiles](320568.md) (4 RR + 2 IR) and external tanks.[^cite_note-FI_Saab_reveals_Demo-68][^cite_note-gripenNGforRNLAF2008Aug-70] The [PS-05/A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PS-05/A) radar is replaced by the new Raven ES-05[^cite_note-71] [active electronically scanned array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_electronically_scanned_array) (AESA) radar, which is based on the Vixen AESA radar family from [Selex ES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selex_ES) (since 2016 Finmeccanica, then [Leonardo S.p.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_S.p.A.)).[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-flight_radar-73] The Gripen Demo's [maiden flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_flight) was conducted on 27 May 2008.[^cite_note-gripen_demo-74] On 21 January 2009, the Gripen Demo flew at Mach 1.2+ at 28000 ft (8540 m) without reheat to test its [supercruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercruise) capability.[^cite_note-75] The Gripen Demo served as a basis for the Gripen E/F, also referred to as the Gripen NG (Next Generation) and MS (Mission System) 21.[^cite_note-AW_Sweetman1-76][^cite_note-77]

Saab studied a variant of the Gripen capable of operating from aircraft carriers in the 1990s. In 2009, it launched the Sea Gripen project in response to India's [request for information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Request_for_information) on a [carrier-based aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier-based_aircraft). Brazil may also require new carrier aircraft.[^cite_note-naval_Gripen-78][^cite_note-DID_BRAZIL-79] Following a meeting with [Ministry of Defence](203793.md) (MoD) officials in May 2011, Saab agreed to establish a development center in the UK to expand on the Sea Gripen concept.[^cite_note-sea_gripen_1411-80] In 2013, Saab's Lennart Sindahl stated that development of an optionally manned Gripen E capable of flying unmanned operations was being explored by the firm; further development of optionally manned and carrier versions would require customer commitment.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-UAV-82] On 6 November 2014, the Brazilian Navy expressed interest in a carrier-based Gripen.[^cite_note-83]

In 2010, Sweden awarded Saab a four-year contract to improve the Gripen's radar and other equipment, integrate new weapons, and lower its operating costs.[^cite_note-84] In June 2010, Saab stated that Sweden planned to order the Gripen NG, designated JAS 39E/F, and was to enter service in 2017 or earlier dependent on export orders.[^cite_note-AW_Sweetman1-76] On 25 August 2012, following Switzerland's intention to buy 22 of the E/F variants, Sweden announced it planned to buy 40–60 Gripen E/Fs.[^cite_note-DID-85] On 17 January 2013, the Swedish government decided to purchase 60 Gripen Es.[^cite_note-e_order-86][^cite_note-87] Subsequent to a national referendum in 2014 Switzerland decided not to procure replacement fighters and postponed their procurement process.

In July 2013, assembly began on the first pre-production Gripen E.[^cite_note-88] Originally 60 JAS 39Cs were to be retrofitted as JAS 39Es by 2023,[^cite_note-89] but this was revised to Gripen Es having new-built airframes and some reused parts from JAS 39Cs.[^cite_note-90] In March 2014, Saab revealed the detailed design and indicated plans to receive military type certification in 2018.[^cite_note-Hoyle_2014‐03‐13-91] The first Gripen E was rolled out on 18 May 2016.[^cite_note-92] Saab delayed the first flight from 2016 to 2017 to focus on civilian-grade software certification;[^cite_note-93] high speed taxi tests began in December 2016.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] In September 2015, Saab Aeronautics head Lennart Sindahl stated that an [Electronic Warfare](82272.md) version of the [Gripen F](#Variants) two-seater could be developed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMader2015-96] On 15 June 2017, Saab completed the Gripen E's first flight.[^cite_note-97] By May 2018, the Gripen E had attained supersonic flight and was to commence load tests.[^cite_note-98] On 24 November 2021, Saab announced that the first six Gripen Es were ready to be delivered to the Swedish and Brazilian air forces. The flight test programme with pre-production Gripen Es continued even after initial deliveries to both the Swedish and Brazilian Air Forces.[^cite_note-gripen_e_serial_delivery-99]

## Design

### Overview

Jet aircraft performing rolls while climbing

Swedish Air Force Gripen C underside in flight, 2012

The Gripen is a multirole fighter aircraft, intended as a light-weight and agile aerial platform with advanced, highly adaptable avionics. It has [canard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canard_\(aeronautics\)) control surfaces that contribute a positive lift force at all speeds, while the generous lift from the [delta wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_wing) compensates for the rear stabiliser producing negative lift at high speeds, increasing [induced drag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_drag).[^cite_note-100] Being [intentionally unstable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inherently_unstable) and employing digital fly-by-wire flight controls to maintain stability removes many flight restrictions, improves [manoeuvrability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermaneuverability), and reduces drag.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELindqvistWidfeldt200350–60,_133–37-101][^cite_note-saab_tech_specs-102] The Gripen also has good short takeoff performance, being able to maintain a high sink rate and strengthened to withstand the stresses of short landings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGreenSwanborough1987227–28-103] The aircraft is fitted with retractable tricycle landing gear, with a double-wheeled nose gear and single main wheels.[^cite_note-104] A pair of [air brakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_brake_\(aircraft\)) are located on the sides of the rear fuselage; the canards also angle downward to act as air brakes and decrease landing distance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200381–82-105] It is capable of flying at a 70–80 degrees angle of attack.[^cite_note-106]

To enable the Gripen to have a long service life, roughly 50 years, Saab designed it to have low maintenance requirements.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEliasson2010125,_130-107] Major systems such as the RM12 engine and PS-05/A radar are [modular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modularity) to reduce operating cost and increase reliability.[^cite_note-saab_tech_specs-102] The Gripen was designed to be flexible, so that newly developed sensors, computers, and armaments could be integrated as technology advances.[^cite_note-108] The aircraft was estimated to be roughly 67% sourced from Swedish or European suppliers and 33% from the US.[^cite_note-dn-109]

One key aspect of the Gripen programme that Saab have been keen to emphasize has been technology-transfer agreements and industrial partnerships with export customers.[^cite_note-110] The Gripen is typically customized to customer requirements, enabling the routine inclusion of local suppliers in the manufacturing and support processes. A number of South African firms provide components and systems – including the communications suite and electronic warfare systems – for the Gripens operated by the South African Air Force.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHenk200673-111] Operators also have access to the Gripen's source code and technical documentation, allowing for upgrades and new equipment to be independently integrated.[^cite_note-112] Some export customers intend to domestically assemble the Gripen; it has been proposed that Brazilian aerospace manufacturer [Embraer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer) may produce Gripens for other export customers as well.[^cite_note-Braz_build-113][^cite_note-Grip_e_value-114]

### Avionics and sensors

All of the Gripen's avionics are fully integrated using five [MIL-STD-1553](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIL-STD-1553)B digital [data buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_\(computing\)), in what is described as "[sensor fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensor_fusion)".[^cite_note-saab_tech_specs-102] The total integration of the avionics makes the Gripen a "programmable" aircraft, allowing software updates to be introduced over time to increase performance and allow for additional operational roles and equipment.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrédriksen2001280-115] The [Ada programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_programming_language) was adopted for the Gripen, and is used for the primary flight controls on the final prototypes from 1996 onwards and all subsequent production aircraft.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrisberg1998288-116] The Gripen's software is [continuously being improved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_delivery) to add new capabilities, as compared to the preceding Viggen, which was updated only in an 18-month schedule.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEliasson2010125-117]

Much of the data generated from the onboard sensors and by cockpit activity is digitally recorded throughout the length of an entire mission. This information can be replayed in the cockpit or easily extracted for detailed post-mission analysis using a data transfer unit that can also be used to insert mission data to the aircraft.[^cite_note-gripen_mission-118][^cite_note-saab_cockpit-119] The Gripen, like the Viggen, was designed to operate as one component of a networked national defence system, which allows for automatic exchange of information in [real-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real-time_data) between Gripen aircraft and ground facilities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEliasson2010126-120] According to Saab, the Gripen features "the world's most highly developed data link".[^cite_note-saab_tech_specs-102] The Gripen's Ternav tactical navigation system combines information from multiple onboard systems such as the [air data computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_data_computer), [radar altimeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_altimeter), and [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPS) to continuously calculate the Gripen's location.[^cite_note-121]

The Gripen entered service using the PS-05/A [pulse-Doppler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse-Doppler_radar) multi-mode [X band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_band) radar, developed by [Ericsson](9424.md) and [GEC-Marconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GEC-Marconi), which is based on the latter's advanced [Blue Vixen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Vixen) radar for the [Sea Harrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Harrier) that also served as the basis for the Eurofighter's [CAPTOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euroradar_CAPTOR) radar.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELake20082-122][^cite_note-saab_tech_specs-102] The all-weather radar is capable of locating and identifying targets 120 km (74 mi) away,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200374-123] and automatically tracking multiple targets in the upper and lower spheres, on the ground and sea or in the air. It can guide several [beyond visual range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_visual_range) air-to-air missiles to multiple targets simultaneously.[^cite_note-124] Saab stated the PS-05/A is able to handle all types of air defence, air-to-surface, and reconnaissance missions,[^cite_note-saab_tech_specs-102] and is developing a Mark 4 upgrade to it.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126] The Mark 4 version has a 150% increase in high-altitude air-to-air detection ranges, detection and tracking of smaller targets at current ranges, 140% improvement in air-to-air mode at low altitude, and full integration of modern weapons such as the [AIM-120C-7 AMRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-120C), [AIM-9X Sidewinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9X), and [MBDA Meteor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBDA_Meteor) missiles.[^cite_note-127]



Swedish Air Force Gripen E with IRST sensor in front of the canopy

The future Gripen E/F will use a new AESA radar, Raven ES-05, based on the Vixen AESA radar family from Selex ES.[^cite_note-flight_radar-73] Among other improvements, the new radar is to be capable of scanning over a greatly increased field of view and improved range.[^cite_note-stratpost-128] In addition, the new Gripen integrates the Skyward-G [Infra-red search and track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infra-red_search_and_track) (IRST) sensor, which is capable of passively detecting thermal emissions from air and ground targets in the aircraft's vicinity.[^cite_note-other_sensors-129] The sensors of the Gripen E are claimed to be able to detect low [radar cross-section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_cross-section) (RCS) targets at beyond visual range.[^cite_note-130] Targets are tracked by a "best sensor dominates" system, either by onboard sensors or through the Transmitter Auxiliary Unit (TAU) data link function of the radar.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

### Cockpit



Gripen C cockpit

The primary flight controls are compatible with the Hands On Throttle-And-Stick ([HOTAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HOTAS)) control principle – the centrally mounted stick, in addition to flying the aircraft, also controls the cockpit displays and weapon systems. A triplex, digital fly-by-wire system is employed on the Gripen's flight controls,[^cite_note-saab_tech_specs-102] with a mechanical backup for the throttle.[^cite_note-Reaktionsmotor_12_idg.se-133] Additional functions, such as communications, navigational and decision support data, can be accessed via the Up Front Control Panel, directly above the central cockpit display.[^cite_note-annotated_cockpit-134] The Gripen includes the EP-17 cockpit display system, developed by Saab to provide pilots with a high level of situational awareness and reduces pilot workload through intelligent information management. The Gripen features a sensor fusion capability, information from onboard sensors and databases is combined, automatically analysed, and useful data is presented to the pilot via a wide field-of-view [Head-Up Display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head-Up_Display), three large multi-function colour displays, and optionally a [Helmet Mounted Display System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmet_Mounted_Display_System) (HMDS).[^cite_note-saab_cockpit-119]



Brazilian Air Force Gripen NG cockpit with Wide Area Display

Of the three multi-function displays (MFD), the central display is for navigational and mission data, the display to the left of the center shows aircraft status and electronic warfare information, and the display to the right of the center has sensory and fire control information.[^cite_note-annotated_cockpit-134] In two-seat variants, the rear seat's displays can be operated independently of the pilot's own display arrangement in the forward seat. Saab has promoted this capability as being useful during electronic warfare and [reconnaissance](146911.md) missions, and while carrying out command and control activities.[^cite_note-saab_cockpit-119] In May 2010, Sweden began equipping their Gripens with additional onboard computer systems and new displays.[^cite_note-135] The MFDs are interchangeable and designed for redundancy in the event of failure, flight information can be presented on any of the displays.[^cite_note-Reaktionsmotor_12_idg.se-133]

Saab and BAE developed the Cobra HMDS for use in the Gripen, based on the Striker HMDS used on the Eurofighter.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELarssonEkrot20101-136] By 2008, the Cobra HMDS was fully integrated on operational aircraft, and is available as an option for export customers; it has been retrofitted into older Swedish and South African Gripens.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELarssonEkrot20101-136] The HMDS provides control and information on target cueing, sensor data, and flight parameters, and is optionally equipped for night time operations and with chemical/biological filtration.[^cite_note-gripen_mission-118] All connections between the HMDS and the cockpit were designed for rapid detachment, for safe use of the ejection system.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELarssonEkrot20102,_4-137]

### Engine

&#x20;Headphone-wearing technician in green uniform, inspecting the engine nozzle of a single-engine fighter jet

A technician inspecting a Gripen's [RM12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_RM12) engine in-situ

Further information: [Volvo RM12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_RM12)

All in-service Gripens as of January 2014 are powered by a Volvo RM12 [turbofan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbofan) engine (now [GKN](424537.md) Aerospace Engine Systems), a licence-manufactured derivative of General Electric F404, fed by a [Y-duct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y-duct?action=edit\&redlink=1) with [splitter plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splitter_plate_\(aeronautics\)); changes include increased performance and improved reliability to meet single engine use safety criteria, as well as a greater resistance to [bird strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_strike) incidents.[^cite_note-Reaktionsmotor_12_idg.se-133][^cite_note-138] Several subsystems and components were also redesigned to reduce maintenance demands.[^cite_note-Volvo_10000-139] By November 2010, the Gripen had accumulated over 143000 flight hours without a single engine-related failure or incident; Rune Hyrefeldt, head of Military Program management at Volvo Aero, stated: "I think this must be a hard record to beat for a single-engine application".[^cite_note-Volvo_10000-139]

Like the Gripen, test models of the Dassault Rafale used F404 engines until replaced by the [Snecma M88](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snecma_M88)-2 engine on production aircraft.[^cite_note-140] A JAS 39C variant powered by a new 80–93 kN (8,200–9,500 kgf; 18,000–21,000 lbf) thrust SNECMA M88-3 engine was proposed.[^cite_note-FI19910410-141][^cite_note-FI19940921-142] The M88-3 variant would have a new low pressure compressor (LPC) with a new variable [stator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stator_\(compressor\)) vane stage and an increased mass flow of 73.4 kg/s (9,710 lb/min).[^cite_note-FI19990609-143]

The JAS 39E and F variants under development are to adopt the F414G powerplant, a variant of the General Electric F414. The F414G can produce 20% greater thrust than the current RM12 engine, enabling the Gripen to supercruise at a speed of Mach 1.1 while carrying an air-to-air combat payload.[^cite_note-NG_capabilities-69] In 2010, Volvo Aero stated it was capable of further developing its RM12 engine to better match the performance of the F414G, and claimed that developing the RM12 would be a less expensive option.[^cite_note-karl_19-144] Prior to Saab's selection of the F414G, the [Eurojet EJ200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurojet_EJ200) had also been under consideration for the Gripen; proposed implementations included the use of [thrust vectoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust_vectoring).[^cite_note-145]

### Equipment and armaments

The Gripen is compatible with a number of different armaments, beyond the aircraft's single 27 mm [Mauser BK-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauser_BK-27) cannon (omitted on the two-seat variants),[^cite_note-146] including air-to-air missiles such as the [AIM-9 Sidewinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIM-9_Sidewinder), air-to-ground missiles such as the [AGM-65 Maverick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-65_Maverick), and [anti-ship missiles](140725.md) such as the [RBS-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RBS-15).[^cite_note-147] In 2010, the Swedish Air Force's Gripen fleet completed the MS19 upgrade process, enabling compatibility with a range of weapons, including the long-range MBDA Meteor missile, the short-range [IRIS-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS-T) missile and the GBU-49 laser-guided bomb.[^cite_note-View_from_Sweden-148] Speaking on the Gripen's selection of armaments, Saab's campaign director for India, Edvard de la Motte stated that: "If you buy Gripen, select where you want your weapons from: Israel, Sweden, Europe, US ... South America. It's up to the customer".[^cite_note-stratpost-128]

In flight, the Gripen is typically capable of carrying up to 6,500 kg (14,330 lb) of assorted armaments and equipment.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrédriksen2001280-115] Equipment includes external sensor pods for reconnaissance and target designation, such as [Rafael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Advanced_Defense_Systems)'s [LITENING](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LITENING) targeting pod, Saab's Modular Reconnaissance Pod System, or Thales' Digital Joint Reconnaissance Pod.[^cite_note-149] The Gripen has an advanced and integrated electronic warfare suite, capable of operating in an undetectable passive mode or to actively jam hostile radar; a missile approach warning system passively detects and tracks incoming missiles.[^cite_note-other_sensors-129][^cite_note-150] In November 2013, it was announced that Saab will be the first to offer the [BriteCloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BriteCloud) expendable Active jammer developed by Selex ES.[^cite_note-151] In June 2014, the Enhanced Survivability Technology Modular Self Protection Pod, a defensive missile countermeasure pod, performed its first flight on the Gripen.[^cite_note-152]

Saab describes the Gripen as a "swing-role aircraft", stating that it is capable of "instantly switching between roles at the push of a button". The human/machine interface changes when switching between roles, being optimized by the computer in response to new situations and threats.[^cite_note-gripen_mission-118] The Gripen is also equipped to use a number of different communications standards and systems, including SATURN secure radio, [Link 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_16), ROVER, and satellite uplinks.[^cite_note-153] Equipment for performing long range missions, such as an [air-to-air refuelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-to-air_refuelling) probe and Onboard Oxygen Generation System (OBOGS), was integrated on the Gripen C/D.[^cite_note-154]

### Usability and maintenance



Swedish Air Force Gripen taking off from a [road runway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_strip) that was part of the [Bas 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas_90) system

During the [Cold War](325329.md), the [Swedish Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Armed_Forces) were to be ready to defend against a possible invasion. This scenario required defensive [force dispersal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_dispersal) of combat aircraft in the Bas 90 system to maintain an air defence capacity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpick2000426-155] Thus, a key design goal during the Gripen's development was the ability to operate from snow-covered landing strips of only 500 metres (1,600 ft);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELindqvistWidfeldt2003-156] furthermore, a short-turnaround time of just ten minutes, during which a team composed of a technician and five conscripts would be able to re-arm, refuel, and perform routine inspections and servicing inside that time window before returning to flight for air-to-air missions. For air-to-ground missions this turnaround time using the same resources is slightly longer at twenty minutes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200382-157][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChantTaylor199948-158]

During the design process, great priority was placed on facilitating and minimizing aircraft maintenance; in addition to a maintenance-friendly layout, many subsystems and components require little or no maintenance at all.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEliasson2010130-159] Aircraft are fitted with a Health and Usage Monitoring System (HUMS) that monitors the performance of various systems, and provides information to technicians to assist in servicing it.[^cite_note-160] Saab operates a continuous improvement programme; information from the HUMS and other systems can be submitted for analysis.[^cite_note-161] According to Saab, the Gripen provides "50% lower operating costs than its best competitor".[^cite_note-saab_tech_specs-102]

A 2012 *[Jane's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane's) Aerospace and Defense Consulting* study compared the operational costs of a number of modern combat aircraft, concluding that Gripen had the lowest *cost per flight hour* (CPFH) when fuel used, pre-flight preparation and repair, and scheduled airfield-level maintenance together with associated personnel costs were combined. The Gripen had an estimated CPFH of US\$4700 whereas the next lowest, the F-16 Block 40/50, had a 49% higher CPFH at \$7000.[^cite_note-SP.com_operational_cost-62][^cite_note-162]

In 2024, a study was begun to see whether a Gripen could launch a small satellite into low earth orbit, building on previous research in this area. It is more common for commercial airliners to be used for this purpose but the [Eurofighter Typhoon](167667.md) has also been studied for this task[^cite_note-163] (see also *[ASM-135 ASAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASM-135_ASAT)*).

## Operational history

### Current users

#### Sweden



Gripen NG technology demonstrator in flight

The [Swedish Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Air_Force) placed a total order for 204 Gripens[^cite_note-164] in three batches. The first delivery occurred on 8 June 1993, when 39102 was handed over to the Flygvapnet during a ceremony at Linköping;[^cite_note-165] the last of the first batch was handed over on 13 December 1996.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMatláryØ̈sterud2007150-33] The Air Force received its first Batch II example on 19 December 1996.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeijsper200330-166] Instead of the fixed-price agreement of Batch I, Batch II aircraft were paid as a "target price" concept: any cost underruns or overruns would be split between FMV and Saab.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200388-34]

The JAS 39 entered service with the [Skaraborg Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skaraborg_Wing) (F 7) on 1 November 1997.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEden2004390-167][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpick2000431-168] The final Batch III aircraft was delivered to FMV on 26 November 2008.[^cite_note-last_swaf-37] This was accomplished at 10% less than the agreed-upon price for the batch, putting the JAS 39C [flyaway cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flyaway_cost) at under US\$30 million.[^cite_note-last_swaf-37] This batch of Gripens was equipped for in-flight refuelling from specially equipped [TP84s](7697.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliams200388-34] In 2007, a programme was started to upgrade 31 of the air force's JAS 39A/B fighters to JAS 39C/Ds.[^cite_note-169] The SwAF had a combined 134 JAS 39s in service in January 2013.[^cite_note-2013_Aerospace-170] In March 2015, the Swedish Air Force received its final JAS 39C.[^cite_note-171]

On 29 March 2011, the Swedish parliament approved the Swedish Air Force for a 3-month deployment to support the UN-mandated no-fly zone over Libya. Deployment of eight Gripens, ten pilots, and other personnel began on 2 April.[^cite_note-172] On 8 June 2011, the Swedish government announced an agreement to extend the deployment for five of the Gripens.[^cite_note-173] By October 2011, Gripens had flown more than 650 combat missions, almost 2000 flight hours, and delivered approximately 2000 reconnaissance reports to NATO.[^cite_note-174] Journalist Tim Hepher suggested that the Libyan operations might stimulate sales of the Gripen and other aircraft.[^cite_note-175]



Swedish Air Force Gripen C during Arctic Challenge exercise, 2013

In November 2012, Lieutenant Colonel Lars Helmrich of the Swedish Air Force testified to the Riksdag regarding the Gripen E. He stated that the then-current version of the Gripen would be outdated in air-to-air combat by 2020.[^cite_note-176] With 60 Gripens having been judged to be the minimum required to defend Swedish Airspace, the Swedish Air Force wanted to have 60–80 Gripens upgraded to the E/F standard by 2020.[^cite_note-Riksdagen-177]

On 25 August 2012, the Swedish government announced that 40–60 JAS 39E/F Gripens were expected to be procured and in service by 2023.[^cite_note-DID-85][^cite_note-178] On 11 December 2012, the Riksdag approved the purchase of 40 to 60 JAS 39E/Fs with an option to cancel if at least 20 aircraft are not ordered by other customers.[^cite_note-en.europeonline-magazine.eu-179] on 17 January 2013, the government approved the deal for 60 JAS 39Es to be delivered between 2018 and 2027.[^cite_note-e_order-86] On 3 March 2014, the Swedish defence minister stated that another 10 JAS 39Es might be ordered; this was later confirmed by the government.[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181]

There are also plans to keep some of the Gripen C/D active after 2025. This was recommended by the Swedish defence advisory committee in 2019.[^cite_note-182]

In 2006, Swedish Gripen aircraft participated in [Red Flag – Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Flag_%E2%80%93_Alaska), a multinational air combat exercise hosted by the United States Air Force. Gripens flew simulated combat sorties against F-16 Block 50, Eurofighter Typhoon and F-15C and scored ten kills, including a Eurofighter Typhoon and five F-16 Block 50s on day one of the exercises with no losses.[^cite_note-theaviationgeekclub-183] Three Swedish Gripen C also participated in a war game against five Royal Norwegian Air Force F-16 Block 50 fighters in Sweden. Swedish Gripen Cs and Norwegian F-16s flew three combat sorties; Gripen Cs scored five kills in each sortie against Norwegian F-16s, and on the last sortie an F-16 scored a kill against a Gripen.[^cite_note-theaviationgeekclub-183]

In December 2022 Sweden ordered an upgrade package from Saab for a number of their Gripen C/D fighters. The upgrade includes new engines, a new radar and a new electronic warfare system. The order value is approximately SEK 3.5 billion and the contract period is 2023–29.[^cite_note-184] In September 2023 Swedish FMV and Saab signed additional orders for the Gripen E and C/D versions. The new orders enables the C/D version to continue serving after 2030 parallel with the introduction of the E.[^cite_note-185]

The first Gripen E was officially delivered to the Swedish Air force on 20 October 2025.[^cite_note-186]

2000C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000#Mirage_2000C) aircraft.[^cite_note-Flightglobal_Brazil-193] In February 2009, Saab submitted a tender for 36 Gripen NGs.[^cite_note-194] In early 2010, the Brazilian Air Force's final evaluation report reportedly placed the Gripen ahead, a decisive factor being lower unit cost and operational costs.[^cite_note-195] After delays due to Brazilian Air Force F-39E Gripen flies over [Brasília](4752.md) in October 2020

On 24 October 2014, Brazil and Sweden signed a 39.3 billion SEK (US\$5.44 bn, R\$13 bn) contract for 28 Gripen E (single-seat version) and eight Gripen F (dual-seat version) fighters for delivery from 2019 to 2024 and maintained until 2050;[^cite_note-SaabBR-204][^cite_note-Thisdell-2014-205] the Swedish government will provide a subsidized 25-year, 2.19% interest rate loan for the buy.[^cite_note-206] At least 15 aircraft are to be assembled in Brazil, Brazilian companies shall be involved in its production; Gripen Fs are to be delivered later. An almost US\$1 billion price increase since selection is due to developments requested by Brazil,[^cite_note-207] such as the "Wide Area Display" (WAD), a panoramic 19 by 8 inches touchscreen display.[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209] The compensation package is set at US\$9 billion, or 1.7 times the order value.[^cite_note-210] The [Brazilian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Navy) is interested in the [Gripen Maritime](#Variants) to replace its [Douglas A-4KU Skyhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_A-4_Skyhawk#Brazil) carrier-based fighters.[^cite_note-DID_BRAZIL-79][^cite_note-Jennings-2013-211] In 2015, Brazil and Sweden finalised the deal to develop the Gripen F, designated F-39 by Brazil.[^cite_note-212][^cite_note-FG_F-39_designation-213]

The first Brazilian F-39E Gripen flight took place on 26 August 2019, from Saab's facility in Linköping, Sweden.[^cite_note-214] It was handed over to the Brazilian Air Force on 10 September 2019 for flight testing.[^cite_note-presents-215] The fighter arrived in Brazil on 20 September 2020,[^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217] and then was transported by land to [Navegantes International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navegantes_Airport). On 24 September, it took off to the [Embraer unit in Gavião Peixoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_Unidade_Gavi%C3%A3o_Peixoto_Airport), in [São Paulo](229422.md) state, for the test program for flight control systems, weapon integration, communication systems and others. The fighters will be part of the [1st Air Defense Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Brazilian_Air_Force_units) (1º GDA), based at the [Anápolis Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An%C3%A1polis_Air_Force_Base).[^cite_note-aeroflap-218] The deliveries of operational fighters will begin in 2021.[^cite_note-219] According to Saab executive Eddy De La Motte, the first F-39F will be delivered in 2023.[^cite_note-220] In 2021, Brazil started F-39E supersonic flight tests at high altitude above 16000 feet. According to Saab executive Mikael Franzén, Brazil will start receiving production aircraft with IRST from November 2021.[^cite_note-221] The service has a requirement for 108 Gripens, to be delivered in three batches.[^cite_note-FlightGlobal-2014-222]



Brazilian Air Force F-39E Gripen front view during an exercise, October 2022

On 1 February 2022, the Brazilian Air Force commander [Carlos de Almeida Baptista Júnior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_de_Almeida_Baptista_J%C3%BAnior) told newspaper *[Folha de S. Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folha_de_S._Paulo)* that Brazil is in initial planning phase for negotiations with Saab for a new batch of 30 Gripen E/Fs,[^cite_note-2nd_Brazilian_batch-223] "our capacity planning takes us today, by our employment assumptions, to 66 Gripens in operation"; this planning phase is expected to be finished by mid-2022.[^cite_note-2nd_Brazilian_batch-223] The negotiation and Brazilian intention was confirmed by the Saab's chief executive Håkan Buskhe in February 2019.[^cite_note-224] The confirmation comes after media rumors that the service saw the Lockheed Martin F-35 as an ideal candidate to continue the modernization process in the coming years, after Gripen's recent failed bids in Finland and Switzerland, rumors that Baptista denied.[^cite_note-2nd_Brazilian_batch-223][^cite_note-225] On 1 April 2022, Brazil received the first two series produced F-39E.[^cite_note-226] On 22 April 2022, the Brazilian Air Force announced the purchase of four more Gripens E/F for the first batch, totaling 40 aircraft, and the ongoing studies for a second batch.[^cite_note-4_more_aircraft_2022-227] On 23 May 2022, Commander Baptista Júnior announced at a press conference that the second batch will consist of 26 Gripens, priced around US\$85 million per unit (US\$2.2 billion); these new units plus the four ordered in April 2022 will be assembled at the Embraer factory in Gavião Peixoto.[^cite_note-2nd_batch_26_units-228][^cite_note-2nd_batch_26_units_2-229][^cite_note-2nd_batch_26_units_3-230] On 1 August 2022, the Saab's chief executive Micael Johansson, confirmed that Brazil has initiated formal negotiations for 26 more Gripen fighters.[^cite_note-231]



Brazilian Air Force F-39E Gripen flies over [Gavião Peixoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavi%C3%A3o_Peixoto), March 2026

Saab and Embraer inaugurated the production line for Gripen E in Brazil on 9 May 2023.[^cite_note-232] On 23 November 2023, the newspaper *[Folha de S. Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folha_de_S._Paulo)* reported ongoing negotiations between the Brazilian and Swedish governments, for a deal involving 14 more Gripens for the Brazilian Air Force, in exchange of four Embraer [C-390 Millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-390_Millennium) transport aircraft for the Swedish Air Force, according to sources, the deal will be finalized in 2024.[^cite_note-233] On 9 November 2024, the Brazilian Minister of Defence [José Múcio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_M%C3%BAcio) signed a letter of intent, to increase the contract with Saab by 25%, increasing to 45 Gripens for the FAB. In exchange, the Swedish Air Force announced the selection and the beginning of negotiations to procure the Embraer C-390 Millenium airlifter.[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237] On 16 September 2025, the FAB commander [Marcelo Kanitz Damasceno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Kanitz_Damasceno) and the Swedish Minister of Defence [Pål Jonson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A5l_Jonson) announced negotiations for six more Gripen Es and a batch of 12 second-hand Gripen C/Ds from the Swedish Air Force inventory.[^cite_note-238]

The 12th and first domestically produced unit for Brazil was officially presented on 25 March 2026.[^cite_note-auto-239] In June 2026, the Defence minister of Brazil, signed a MoU for a further batch of 20 Gripen Es intended to be built in Brazil.[^cite_note-240] The new jets will be stationed at [Santa Maria AFB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_Air_Force_Base) in the southern region of Brazil.[^cite_note-241]

#### Czech Republic



Czech Air Force Gripen C with Čáslav AFB in the background

When the [Czech Republic](5321.md) became a NATO member in 1999, it needed to replace its existing Soviet-built [MiG-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-21) fleet with aircraft compatible with NATO standards. In 2000, the Czech Republic began evaluating a number of aircraft, including the F-16, F/A-18, Mirage 2000, Eurofighter Typhoon and the Gripen. One major procurement condition was the industrial [offset agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offset_agreement), set at 150% of the expected purchase value.[^cite_note-242] In December 2001, having reportedly been swayed by Gripen International's generous financing and offset programme, the Czech government announced that the Gripen had been selected.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpreen200777-243] In 2002, the deal was delayed until after parliamentary elections had taken place; alternative means of air defence were also studied, including leasing the aircraft.[^cite_note-244]

&#x20;Three-quarter bottom view of two jet aircraft inn flight against a blue sky

Two Czech Air Force Gripen C during a [Baltic Air Policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Air_Policing) mission in [Lithuania](17675.md)

On 14 June 2004, it was announced that the Czech Republic was to lease 14 Gripens, modified to comply with NATO standards.[^cite_note-245] The agreement also included the training of Czech pilots and technicians in Sweden. The first six were delivered on 18 April 2005.[^cite_note-246] The lease was for an agreed period of 10 years at a cost of €780 million; the 14 ex-Swedish Air Force aircraft included 12 single-seaters and two JAS 39D two-seat trainers.[^cite_note-247][^cite_note-Gripen_Family-248] In September 2013, the Defence and Security Export Agency announced that a follow-up agreement with the Czech Republic had been completed to extend the lease by 14 years, until 2029; leased aircraft shall also undergo extensive modernization, including the adoption of new datalinks.[^cite_note-249] The lease also has an option of eventually acquiring the fighters outright.[^cite_note-Gripen_Family-248] In 2014, the lease was extended to 2027 and the Saab service contract was extended to 2026.[^cite_note-fg2014-05-250][^cite_note-fg2015-05-251]

In November 2014, Czech Air Force commander General Libor Štefánik proposed leasing a further six Gripens due to Russia's deteriorating relationship with the West;[^cite_note-252] a Ministry of Defence spokesperson stated that the notion was the commander's personal vision and fleet expansion was not on the agenda for years to come.[^cite_note-253] In 2015, the service decided to upgrade its fleet to the MS20 configuration.[^cite_note-254] The MS20 upgrade was completed in 2018.[^cite_note-255]

#### Hungary

&#x20;Jet aircraft with centerline external fuel tank during invert flight against blue sky

Hungarian Air Force Gripen C during inverted flight, 2007

Following Hungary's membership of NATO in 1999, there were several proposals to achieve a NATO-compatible fighter force. Considerable attention went into studying second-hand aircraft options as well as modifying the nation's existing MiG-29 fleet. In 2001, Hungary received several offers of new and used aircraft from various nations, including Sweden, Belgium, Israel, Turkey, and the US.[^cite_note-256] Although the Hungarian government initially intended to procure the F-16, in November 2001 it was in the process of negotiating a 10-year lease contract for 12 Gripen aircraft, with an option to purchase the aircraft at the end of the lease period.[^cite_note-257][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELarrabee200324-258]

As part of the procurement arrangements, Saab had offered an offset deal valued at 110 per cent of the cost of the 14 fighters.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStohlGrillot200946-259] Initially, Hungary had planned to lease several Batch II aircraft; however, the inability to conduct [aerial refuelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_refuelling) and weapons compatibility limitations had generated Hungarian misgivings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELarrabee200325-260] The contract was renegotiated and was signed on 2 February 2003 for a total of 14 Gripens, which had originally been A/B standard and had undergone an extensive upgrade process to the NATO-compatible C/D 'Export Gripen' standard.[^cite_note-JAS_39C-261] The last aircraft deliveries took place in December 2007.[^cite_note-262]

While the [Hungarian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Air_Force) operates a total of 14 Gripen aircraft under lease,[^cite_note-Gripen_Family-248] in 2011, the country reportedly intended to purchase these aircraft outright.[^cite_note-treddje-263] However, in January 2012, the Hungarian and Swedish governments agreed to extend the lease period for a further 10 years; according to Hungarian Defense Minister [Csaba Hende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csaba_Hende), the agreement represented considerable cost savings.[^cite_note-264]

Two Gripens were lost in crashes in May and June 2015,[^cite_note-crash201506A-265][^cite_note-crash201506B-266] leaving 12 Gripens in operation.[^cite_note-267] From 2017, Hungary returned to operating 14 fighters.[^cite_note-Fleet_complete-268]

In August 2021, a contract was signed with Saab to modernise the Gripen fleet of the Hungarian Air Force. The radar will be upgraded to PS-05/Mk4 and the software will be upgraded to MS 20 Block 2 level. New weapons would be added to the arsenal of the Hungarian Gripens.[^cite_note-269] The IRIS-T missiles were ordered in December 2021.[^cite_note-270]

In February 2024, it was announced that a contract was signed to buy four additional Gripen C aircraft.[^cite_note-271]

By June 2026 the four newly ordered MS20 Block 2 Gripens were delivered to Kecskemét, activating a lease-to-ownership clause that schedules transfer of Hungary's 14 original C/D airframes to full ownership by the end of 2026.[^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273]

On 24 August 2026, one Hungarian Gripen crashed near the town of [Szolnok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szolnok). The pilot ejected safely.[^cite_note-274]

#### South Africa



[South African Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Air_Force) Gripen C in flight

In 1999, South Africa signed a contract with BAe/Saab for the procurement of 26 Gripens (C/D standard) with minor modifications to meet its requirements.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEliasson2010\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidISgwc1BGN40CpgPA256_256\]-275] Deliveries to the [South African Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Air_Force) commenced in April 2008.[^cite_note-FltGlob_South_Africa-276] By April 2011, 18 aircraft (nine two-seater aircraft and nine single-seaters) had been delivered.[^cite_note-277] While the establishment of a Gripen Fighter Weapon School at [Overberg Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overberg_Air_Force_Base) in South Africa had been under consideration, in July 2013 Saab ruled out the option due to a lack of local support for the initiative; Thailand is an alternative location being considered,[^cite_note-278] as well as the Čáslav Czech air base.[^cite_note-279]

Between April 2013 and December 2013, South African contractors held prime responsibility for maintenance work on the Gripen fleet as support contracts with [Saab](26968.md) had expired; this arrangement led to fears that extended operations may not be possible due to a lack of proper maintenance and spares availability.[^cite_note-280] In December 2013, [Armscor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armscor_\(South_Africa\)) awarded Saab a long-term support contract for the company to perform engineering, maintenance, and support services on all 26 Gripens through 2016.[^cite_note-281] On 13 March 2013, South African Defense Minister [Nosiviwe Mapisa-Nqakula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nosiviwe_Mapisa-Nqakula) stated that "almost half of the SAAF Gripens" have been stored because of an insufficient budget to keep them flying.[^cite_note-282] In September 2013, the SAAF decided not to place a number of its Gripens in long-term storage; instead all 26 aircraft would be rotated between flying cycles and short-term storage.[^cite_note-283] Speaking in September 2013, Brigadier-General John Bayne testified that the Gripen met the SAAF's minimum requirements, as the country faced no military threats.[^cite_note-284]

#### Thailand

Three-quarter hind bottom view of jet aircraft in flight generating wingtip vortices, against a blue cloudy sky

[Royal Thai Air Force](1778758.md) Gripen C in flight generating wingtip vortices

In 2007, Thailand's Parliament authorised the [Royal Thai Air Force](1778758.md) (RTAF) to spend up to 34 billion baht (US\$1.1 billion) as part of an effort to replace Thailand's existing [Northrop F-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_F-5) fleet.[^cite_note-thai_grip_dip-285] In February 2008, the RTAF ordered six Gripens (two single-seat C-models and four two-seat D-models) from Saab; deliveries began in 2011.[^cite_note-286] Thailand ordered six more Gripen Cs in November 2010; deliveries began in 2013.[^cite_note-287] Thailand may eventually order as many as 40 Gripens.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAcharya2009163-288] In 2010, [Surat Thani Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat_Thani_Airport) was selected as the RTAF's main Gripen operating base.[^cite_note-289]

The first of the six aircraft were delivered on 22 February 2011.[^cite_note-FI_Thai_first_6-290] Saab delivered three Gripens in April 2013, and three more in September 2013.[^cite_note-Thai2-291] In September 2013, RTAF Marshal Prajin Jantong stated that Thailand was interested in purchasing six more aircraft, although no second order had been placed.[^cite_note-thai_grip_dip-285][^cite_note-Th_vill-292] Thai Supreme Commander General Thanasak Patimapragorn has stated that the RTAF intends the Gripen's information systems to be integrated with army and navy systems. The armed forces were to officially inaugurate the Gripen Integrated Air Defence System during 2014.[^cite_note-thai_grip_dip-285]

In August 2024, the Royal Thai Air Force announced having selected the Gripen E/F over the F-16 Block 70/72 to replace its 12 existing [F-16s](11642.md).[^cite_note-293]

On 26 July 2025, reports emerged that RTAF Gripens were deployed in combat for the first time to strike Cambodian positions during the [2025 Cambodia–Thailand border conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Cambodia%E2%80%93Thailand_border_conflict).[^cite_note-294][^cite_note-295][^cite_note-296]

On 25 August 2025 a 5.3 billion SEK contract for the purchase of 3 Gripen E and 1 Gripen F was signed with delivery to be completed by 2030.[^cite_note-297]

In 2021, Canada's Future Fighter Capability Project was launched to find an aircraft suitable for replacing the country's aging fleet of [CF-18s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-18). After [Dassault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault) and [Airbus](26220236.md) exited the competition, and Boeing's Super Hornet was disqualified, only the [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin)'s [F-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35) and Saab's Gripen remained to compete. The FFCP evaluated both aircraft as meeting the mandatory requirements of the [Royal Canadian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Air_Force), however the F-35 reportedly significantly outperformed the Gripen in every category.[^cite_note-313] This led the [Trudeau Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/29th_Canadian_Ministry) to ultimately select the F-35 in 2023, despite campaigning in 2015 on a promise to not purchase the F-35. However, in 2025 following repeated threats by President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) to turn Canada into the "51st State" and the onset of the [US-initiated trade war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_United_States_trade_war_with_Canada_and_Mexico), the new Canadian government under Prime Minister [Mark Carney](13571049.md) opted to re-evaluate and review the F-35 purchase.[^cite_note-314] In November 2025, Saab offered to create 10000 new jobs in Canada and offered full technology transfer and domestic production of the Gripen E if Canada agreed to purchase the aircraft.[^cite_note-315] In the wake of Trump's threats to [annex Greenland by force](82044223.md) and consistent rhetoric about [making Canada the 51st State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_expansionism_under_Donald_Trump), government sources in [Ottawa](22219.md) indicated that the government was now strongly considering the Gripen for at least half of its fleet due to the potential job creation, and as part of Canada's broader efforts to diversify its trade and reduce defense dependency with the United States.[^cite_note-316]

As of November 2025, Saab is in talks with the Canadian aeronautical industry to localise the production of the Gripen if Canada decides to purchase the Gripen for its air force.[^cite_note-317] Canada originally planned to purchase 88 F-35A, but as of February 2026, the only firm order is for 16 F-35s along with long-lead items for another 14 aircraft.[^cite_note-318] If Canada selects the Gripen, some of the Gripens for Ukraine could be manufactured in Canada.[^cite_note-canukr-319]

#### Portugal

Following Trump's radical shift in foreign policy for the United States, Portugal is considering abandoning plans to procure the F-35 and turning attention to European alternatives.[^cite_note-323] As part of its bid to Portugal, on 25 September 2025 Swedish defence minister Pål Jonson visited and a memorandum was signed between Saab and Portuguese companies OGMA and Critical Software, which would allow for production of Gripen parts and for maintenance to take place in Portugal if it were to be procured.[^cite_note-324]

### Upgrades

Gripen aircraft are continually upgraded with hardware and software. Configurations are known as MS## or Materialsystem ##. Configurations are further subdivided in blocks.[^cite_note-337]

Examples are:

Gripen C/D

- MS20 Block 2.1: Introduced 2024

- MS20 Block 3: To be introduced 2025

- MS20 Block 4: To be introduced 2028 (system refresh, AIM-120C-8, Britecloud, [Taurus KEPD 350](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurus_KEPD_350), enhanced performance RM12, upgraded data link)

Gripen E

- MS21 Initial air-to-air version to be introduced 2025

- MS22 Air-to-ground version

- MS23 Enhanced version (Link 16 upgrade, AIM-120C-8, [Taurus KEPD 350](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurus_KEPD_350), GPS M-Code)

## Accidents and incidents

Main article: [Accidents and incidents involving the JAS 39 Gripen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accidents_and_incidents_involving_the_JAS_39_Gripen)

As of January 2017, Gripen aircraft have been involved in at least 10 incidents, including nine [hull loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_loss) accidents, with one loss of life.[^cite_note-365]

The first two crashes, in 1989 and 1993 respectively, occurred during public displays of the Gripen and resulted in considerable negative media reports. The first crash was filmed by a [Sveriges Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sveriges_Television) news crew and led to critics calling for development to be cancelled.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrändström200317–18,_23–24-366] The second crash occurred in an empty area on the island of [Långholmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A5ngholmen) during the 1993 Stockholm Water Festival with tens of thousands of spectators present. The decision to display the Gripen over large crowds was publicly criticized, and was compared to the 1989 crash.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrändström20035–6-367][^cite_note-368] Both the 1989 and 1993 crashes were related to flight control software issues[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEden2004389-369] and pilot-induced oscillation; the flight control system was corrected by 1995.[^cite_note-370] The first and only fatal crash occurred on 14 January 2017 at [Hat Yai International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hat_Yai_International_Airport), Thailand, during an airshow for Thai Children's Day; the pilot did not survive.[^cite_note-371] The next crash occurred on 21 August 2018 at [Kallinge Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kallinge) near the southern Swedish town of [Ronneby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronneby); the pilot was able to successfully eject from the aircraft.[^cite_note-372][^cite_note-373] The following investigation by the [Swedish Accident Investigation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Accident_Investigation_Authority) led to the conclusion by DNA analysis of the engine that it collided with [Great cormorant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_cormorant) birds at a speed of 304 kn (563 km/h) and height 1,400 ft (430 m).[^cite_note-374][^cite_note-375] On 24 August 2026, a Hungarian Gripen crashed near [Szolnok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szolnok), Hungary. The pilot ejected safely, but suffered spinal injuries.[^cite_note-376][^cite_note-377]
