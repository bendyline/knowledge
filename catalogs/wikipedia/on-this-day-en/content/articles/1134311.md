**Semarang** ([Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_script): ꦏꦸꦛꦯꦼꦩꦫꦁ, *Kutha Semarang*) is the [capital](181337.md) and largest city of [Central Java](453584.md) province in [Indonesia](14579.md). It was a major port during the [Dutch](21148.md) [colonial era](23476997.md), and is still an important regional center and port today. The city has been named as the cleanest tourist destination in Southeast Asia by the ASEAN Clean [Tourist City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_city) Standard (ACTCS) for 2020–2022.[^cite_note-5]

It has an area of 373.78 km<sup>2</sup> (144.32 sq mi) and had a population of 1,555,984 at the 2010 census[^cite_note-Biro_Pusat_Statistik_2011-6] and 1,653,524 at the 2020 census,[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2021-7] making it Indonesia's ninth most populous city[^cite_note-8] after [Jakarta](16275.md), [Surabaya](212635.md), [Bekasi](1915107.md), [Bandung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung), [Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan), [Depok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depok), [Tangerang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangerang) and [Palembang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palembang); the official population estimate as at mid 2025 was 1,722,421 (comprising 851,637 males and 870,784 females).[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2026-1] The built-up [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) had 3,183,516 inhabitants at the 2010 census spread over two cities and 26 districts.[^cite_note-9] The [Semarang metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semarang_metropolitan_area) (a.k.a. *Kedungsepur*) has a population of over 6 million in 2020 (*see Greater Semarang section*). The population of the city is predominantly [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people) with significant [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians) presence.

The history of Semarang goes back to the 9th century, when it was known as Bergota. At the end of the 15th century, the Demak Sultanate appointed an Islamic scholar named [Ki Ageng Pandan Arang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ki_Ageng_Pandan_Arang?action=edit\&redlink=1) to build an Islamic school in this fishing village. On 2 May 1547, [Sultan Hadiwijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joko_Tingkir) of [Pajang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Pajang) declared Pandan Arang as the first bupati (mayor) of Semarang, thus creating Semarang administratively and politically.

In 1678, Sunan [Amangkurat II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amangkurat_II) promised to give control of Semarang to the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) (VOC) as a part of a debt payment.[^cite_note-EVNI_1919_740-4-10][^cite_note-Cribb_2000_86-95-11] In 1682, the Semarang state was founded by the Dutch colonial power. On 5 October 1705 after years of occupations, Semarang officially became a VOC city when Susuhunan [Pakubuwono I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakubuwono_I) made a deal to give extensive trade rights to the VOC in exchange of wiping out [Mataram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mataram_Sultanate)'s debt. The VOC, and later, the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md) government, established [tobacco](30942.md) plantations in the region and built roads and railroads, making Semarang an important colonial trading centre.&#x20;

The historic presence of a large [Indo (Eurasian)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo_people) community in the area of Semarang is also reflected by the fact a creole mix language called [Javindo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javindo) existed there.[^cite_note-12]

### Classical Indische Town (1678–1870)



The early VOC settlement of Semarang with its prominent pentagonal fortress.

Semarang was handed by the [Sultan of Mataram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mataram_Sultanate) to the Dutch East Indies in 1678.[^cite_note-EVNI_1919_740-4-10] The city was pictured as a small settlement with a pious Muslim area called *Kauman*, a Chinese quarter, and a Dutch fortress. The fortress has a pentagonal form with only one gate in the south and five monitoring towers to protect the Dutch settlement from rebellion actions, segregating the spaces between Dutch settlement and other areas.[^cite_note-13] In fact, only the city of Semarang was referred to the Dutch quarter while the other ethnic settlements were considered as villages outside the city boundary. The city, known as de Europeesche Buurt, was built in classical European style with a church located in the centre, wide boulevards, streets and villas.[^cite_note-Pratiwo._2005-14] According to Purwanto (2005),[^cite_note-15] the urban and architectural form of this settlement is very similar to the design principles applied in many Dutch cities.

Due to the long and costly Java War, there was not much funding from the Dutch East Indies government, and this affected Semarang's development. Most land in the area was used for rice cultivation and the only small improvement was the development of a surrounding fortress. Although less developed, Semarang is a fairly well organized city, in which urban activities were concentrated along the river and the settlement is linked to a market where different ethnic groups met to trade. The existence of the market, in the later years, become a primary element and a generator of urban economic growth.[^cite_note-Nas,_P._J._M._2002-16]

After the [fall of Fort Cornelis in late August 1811](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Java_\(1811\)), Governor-General [Jan Willem Janssens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Willem_Janssens) withdrew eastward toward Central Java, making Semarang region a key corridor for his retreat and attempted regrouping. British forces under Colonel [R. R. Gillespie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Rollo_Gillespie) advanced through Semarang and its hinterland toward Salatiga, engaging Dutch troops along the route. The campaign ended with Janssens’s surrender to Lieutenant-General [Sir Samuel Auchmuty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Samuel_Auchmuty) at Tuntang on 18 September 1811, which marked the transfer of Java from Dutch to British rule.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoehkardi202117–18-17]

After the departure of [Herman Willem Daendels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Willem_Daendels), Napoleonic governor of Java, the Dutch reorganized Java into [Residencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residency_\(administrative_division\)), and Semarang became the seat of the new [Semarang Residency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semarang_Residency) in 1817. An important influence on urban growth was the Great Mail Road project in the 1847, which connected all the cities in the northern coast of Central and East Java and positioned Semarang as the trade centre of agricultural production.[^cite_note-Colombijn,_F._2002-18] The project was soon followed by the development of the [Staatsspoorwegen Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatsspoorwegen) and the connecting roads into the inner city of Semarang at the end of the 19th century.[^cite_note-Nas,_P._J._M._2002-16] Colombijn (2002)[^cite_note-Colombijn,_F._2002-18] marked the development as the shift of urban functions, from the former river orientation to all services facing the roads.

### Modern city (1870–1922)



Coat of Arms of Semarang during Dutch colonial era, granted in 1827.

The [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md)' mail and railway projects improved communication and transportation, bringing an economic boom to the city in the 1870s. Hospitals, churches, hotels, and mansions were built along the new main roads of Mataram Street, Bojongscheweg, and Pontjolscheweg.[^cite_note-Pratiwo._2005-14] The Javanese quarters of town known as [kampongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampong) grew increasingly densely populated, reaching as many as 1000 inhabitants per [hectare](24919513.md) and degrading living conditions.[^cite_note-Cobban,_J._L._1993-19] Mortality remained high into the early 20th century, with newcomers, overcrowding, and poor hygiene triggering [cholera](7591.md) and [tuberculous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculous) outbreaks.[^cite_note-20] [Dysentery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysentery), [typhoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoid), and [malaria](20423.md) were also rife.[^cite_note-schnee-21] The city doctor [Willem T. de Vogel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_T._de_Vogel) advocated strenuously for reducing overcrowding and improving living conditions by extending Semarang into the less malarial hill country to its south;[^cite_note-schneye-22] his fellow councilman [Hendrik Tillema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Freerk_Tillema) had campaigned on a platform of combatting malaria and joined De Vogel's scheme, broadening it into a "village improvement" ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *kampongverbetering*) movement.[^cite_note-Cobban,_J._L._1993-19] Purchasing land in the heights with their own money, the two men and some friends passed it on to the city with an initial zoning plan by [KPC de Bazel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_Petrus_Cornelis_de_Bazel) in 1907 but could never convince a majority of the council to support its development.[^cite_note-schnee-21][^cite_note-schneye-22] Changing tack, Tillema then worked to improve the existing kampongs in the city's malarial districts by improving drainage and providing more sanitary public toilets and public housing.[^cite_note-Cobban,_J._L._1993-19]

A decade later, the town approved [Thomas Karsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Karsten)'s revised plan for the area, using it to build larger villas for the Dutch and wealthy Chinese and Javanese rather than allowing its use by the poor.[^cite_note-schnee-21][^cite_note-schneye-22][^cite_note-schnappi-23] This area became known as Candi Baru ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Nieuw Tjandi*) and forms the core of the present-day Candisari District. Although it remained highly stratified by class,[^cite_note-Pratiwo._2005-14] Candi Baru had less ethnic segregation than the older area of town and incorporated public squares, athletic facilities, and places for public bathing and washing that could be used communally.[^cite_note-Cote,_J._2004-24] With most work remaining in the lower city and transportation slow or expensive, few of the lower classes were interested in moving to the district[^cite_note-schneye-22] but it set a pattern that was followed with three more successful housing plans between 1916 and 1919. The population grew by 55%, adding 45,000 Javanese, 8500 Chinese, and 7000 Europeans. Karsten's approach to town planning emphasized its aesthetic, practical, and social requirements articulated in economic terms rather than purely racial ones.[^cite_note-Cote,_J._2004-24]

Driven by economic growth and spatial city planning, the city had doubled in size and expanded to the south by the 1920s, creating a nucleus of a metropolis where multi-ethnic groups lived and traded in the city. The villages in the suburbs such as Jomblang and Jatingaleh steadily became the satellite towns of Semarang, more populated with a bigger market area. Before the invasion of Japan in 1942, Semarang had already become the capital of [Central Java](453584.md) province, as the result of trade and industrial success and spatial planning.[^cite_note-Pratiwo._2005-14]

In 1918 the Indonesian People's Hall (Gedung Rakyat Indonesia) was established in Semarang by [Sarekat Rakyat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarekat_Rakyat?action=edit\&redlink=1). The building functioned as a center for political and social activities, hosting meetings, congresses, and educational courses for activists. In 1927, fearing the impact activities there, the Dutch colonial government ordered its closure.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoehkardi202127-25]

### Japanese occupation and early independence

The [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) military occupied the city, along with the rest of Java, in 1942, during the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War) of [World War II](32927.md). During that time, Semarang was headed by a military governor called a Shiko, and two vice governors known as Fuku Shiko. One of the vice governors was appointed from [Japan](15573.md), and the other was "chosen" from the local population.&#x20;

After [Indonesian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_independence) in 1945, Semarang became the capital of [Central Java](453584.md) on 18 August 1945,[^cite_note-26] headed by Mr. Moch.Ichsan.[^cite_note-27]

On the afternoon of 17 August 1945, news of the [Proclamation of Indonesian Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_Indonesian_Independence) reached Semarang. At 12\:30 p.m. the following day, activists, youth, politicians, indigenous government officials, and other community leaders—including Mr. KRT [Wongsonegoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wongsonegoro), [Subandrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subandrio), and [Ibnu Parna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibnu_Parna)—gathered in Purusara (Pusat Rumah Sakit Rakyat, People's Central Hospital) (present day [Kariadi Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kariadi_Hospital)) to discuss their response to the proclamation. The meeting resolved to establish a local government under the authority of the Republic of Indonesia, with Wongsonegoro as its head. The outcome of this assembly was published in the *Sinar Baru* newspaper on 19 August 1945.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoehkardi202142–43-28]

### Military history

It also became the site of a [battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Semarang) (Five days battle, *Pertempuran lima hari*) between the new Indonesian military and Japanese soldiers in October 1945.[^cite_note-29] Shortly after that, what had been the Indonesia-conducted [Ambarawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambarawa), [Magelang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magelang), and [Ungaran offensives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ambarawa) reached Semarang city on 15 December 1945, causing an 87-day battle that swept away the [British](31717.md) and [Dutch military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_military) completely.

## Administration

On 1 March 1906, based on *Staatsblad* of 1906 no. 120, Semarang was officially designated as a city (*stadsgemeente* or *kota madya*), covering an area of 9,940 ha.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoehkardi20218-30]

Semarang city administration is headed by mayor, with a legislative assembly. Both mayor and the 50 members of [legislative assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semarang_City_Regional_House_of_Representatives) are elected by direct vote. The government of Semarang City had implemented the smart city concept since 2013.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

Juridically, Semarang City is a municipality (second level area) consisting of 16 [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Indonesia) (*kecamatan*), which are again divided into 177 [urban villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villages_of_Indonesia) (*kelurahan*). ## Geography



The view of downtown Semarang.

Semarang is located on the northern [coast](5236.md) of Java. The municipality lies between 6°50′ and 7°10′ south latitude and 109°35′ and 110°50′ east longitude. It is bounded by [Kendal Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendal_Regency) to the west, [Demak Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demak_Regency) to the east, [Semarang Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semarang_Regency) to the south, and the [Java Sea](467143.md) to the north. Its coastline is 13.6 km long and its area is 373.78 km². The municipality's elevation ranges from 0.75 to 348 metres above sea level.[^cite_note-BPSSemarang2025-33]

Lowland areas in Semarang City are very narrow. The lowland area in western Semarang only has a width of 4 km (2.5 miles) from the coastline, while in the eastern Semarang, the low-lying area has a width of 11 km (6.8 miles) from the coastline. This lowland area is a flood plain from the large rivers that flow in Semarang City, such as Kali Garang (West Flood Canal), Pengkol River, and Bringin River. This low-lying area stretches on the northern side of Semarang and covers almost 40% of the total area of Semarang. This lowland area is known as the lower town (Semarang Ngisor), as well as the center of the city's economic activity. Under these conditions, the lower city area is often hit by annual flooding and its peak during the rainy season. In a number of regions, especially North Semarang, floods are sometimes also caused by overflowing sea tides (tidal floods). The hilly area in Semarang stretches on the south side. These hills are part of a series of northern Java mountain ranges that stretch from Banten to East Java. The hilly area in the city of Semarang is known as the upper city (Semarang Dhuwur). This hilly region is also the upstream area of the big rivers that flow in the city of Semarang. The upper city area is also near [Mount Ungaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ungaran).[^cite_note-SemarangProfile-34]

### Climate

Semarang features a [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Am*). The city celebrates Christmas and New Year in summer (due to the location in Southern Hemisphere); however, summer months (December to February) are the rainiest, coldest and wettest months. The city features distinctly wetter and drier months, with winter (June through August) being the driest months and hotter than summer. Spring and autumn are the transitions between wet and dry seasons and hotter than both summer and winter. Semarang on average sees approximately 2,300 mm (91 in) of rain annually. Average temperatures in the city are relatively consistent, hovering around 28 °C (82 °F). [Diurnal temperature variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnal_temperature_variation) slightly increases in the dry season.

### Semarang River and flood control

Like Singapore River, Semarang is constructing Semarang River at Banjir Kanal Barat (Garang River) near Karangayu Bridge. In the middle of July 2011, gardens in river banks and some traditional boats are available to use. The project will be finished in 2013 with river gardens, trotoars, garden lighting, water activities, art sites, sport sites and balconies and stairs for sightseeing.[^cite_note-38] In August 2011, a 421 m (1,380 ft) tunnel dodger at Kreo river has been finished and Jatibarang Dam construction can begin, with completion targeted for July 2013. The dam is planned to release 230 m<sup>3</sup>/s (8,100 cu ft/s) of flood water and will generate 1.5 MW of electricity, provide a drinking water resource and a boost to tourism.[^cite_note-39]

The largest ethnic group in Semarang is the [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people), followed by minorities of Chinese, [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Indonesians), [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Indonesians), and others (including local ethnicities such as [Sundanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundanese_people), [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak), [Madura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurese_people), [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Indonesians), [Balinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balinese_people) etc.). The dominant religion is [Islam](6037917.md) with a significant [Christian](5211.md) minority. Semarang has a large Chinese community. As in other regions of Java, especially in Central Java, they have mingled closely with the local population and use Javanese in communication for hundreds of years. About 3.5% of the city's population is ethnic Chinese, many residing in a [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown) in the vicinity of Gang Pinggir. The Chinatown is called "Kampong Pecinan Semawis" and expresses many aspects of traditional Chinese culture including foods, rituals, and houses of worship.

## Economy

In 1939, according to Moehkardi citing *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, exports from [Port of Semarang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Tanjung_Emas) reached 38,522,000 gulden (accounting for approximately 5% of total exports from the Dutch East Indies) while the imports through the port reached 48,774,000 gulden, representing about 10% of the total imports of the colony.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMoehkardi202113-43]

In 2025, Semarang's [gross regional domestic product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_regional_domestic_product) (GRDP) at current prices was 288.05 trillion rupiah, compared with 267.62 trillion rupiah in 2024 and 204.87 trillion rupiah in 2021. At constant 2010 prices, real GRDP growth was 6.49% in 2025, following positive growth in each year from 2021 to 2025. Gross fixed capital formation accounted for 60.92% of GRDP and household final consumption for 40.88%; Semarang contributed 14.83% of the economy of [Central Java](453584.md) in 2025.[^cite_note-BPSSemarangGRDP2026-44]

## Transportation

### Road

Semarang is on the [Indonesian National Route 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Route_1) road, which connects it to [Merak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merak,_Banten) and Ketapang ([Banyuwangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banyuwangi)). [Indonesian National Route 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Route_14) toward [Bawen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bawen?action=edit\&redlink=1) starts here. Semarang has a toll road, the [Semarang Toll Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semarang_Toll_Road).[^cite_note-45] The city is connected to [Solo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surakarta) by [Semarang–Solo Toll Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semarang%E2%80%93Solo_Toll_Road).[^cite_note-46]

Semarang's largest bus terminals are Mangkang and Terboyo.[^cite_note-47] The primary means of public transportation is by minibus, called "*bis*". *Ojek* ([motorcycle taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcycle_taxi)), *Angkot* ([share-taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_taxi)) micro-buses, taxi-cabs plays vital role in public transportation of the city. [Go-Jek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go-Jek) and [Grab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grab_\(company\)) have online taxi and *Ojek* services.

Semarang is served by [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) called *Trans Semarang*, which operates in six routes.[^cite_note-48] [Perum DAMRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perum_DAMRI) also serves in six designated routes in the city.

### Rail



Semarang Old Town seen from [Semarang Tawang railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semarang_Tawang_railway_station).

Semarang was connected to [Surakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surakarta) (Solo) by a rail line in 1870.[^cite_note-49] At present there are two large train stations in Semarang: [Semarang Poncol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semarang_Poncol_railway_station) and [Semarang Tawang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semarang_Tawang_railway_station). Semarang is connected to [Bandung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung), [Jakarta](16275.md), and [Surabaya](212635.md) by inter-city train services. *[Kedungsepur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kedungsepur_\(train\))* commuter rail connects Semarang Poncol Station eastward to [Ngrombo Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngrombo_railway_station?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Grobogan Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grobogan_Regency).

[Sam Poo Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Poo_Kong), the oldest Chinese temple in the city.

- [Semarang old town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_architecture_in_Semarang) (Kota Lama), which is sometimes referred to by locals as "Little Netherlands." It was established in the 18th century when Indonesia was a Dutch colony. There are more than 50 well-maintained colonial buildings in Kota Lama, displaying 18th, 19th, and 20th century European-influenced architecture.

- China Town: [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown) in Semarang has a night market known as 'Pasar Semawis', which is known for its cuisine and Chinese new year celebration.

  [^cite_note-53]

- [Tugu Muda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tugu_Muda) (Youth Monument) is a monument built to commemorate the services of the heroes who have fallen in the [Battle of Five Days in Semarang](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pertempuran%20Lima%20Hari). The height of Tugu Muda is 53 meters. Tugu Muda is located in front of [Lawang Sewu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawang_Sewu) at Pemuda street. It depicts the Tugu Muda fighting spirit and patriotism of Semarang residents, especially the youth who are persistent, self-sacrificing in high spirits maintaining the independence of Indonesia.

  [^cite_note-54]

- [Lawang Sewu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawang_Sewu) ([Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language) for "A Thousand Doors") was built as the headquarters of the [Dutch East Indies Railway Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_Indies_Railway_Company). The colonial era building is reputedly a haunted house.

- Blenduk Church (Nederlandsch Indische Kerk) was built in 1753, the church is one of the oldest building in Kota Lama.

- The [Sam Poo Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Poo_Kong) temple is the oldest Chinese temple in the city.

  [^cite_note-55]

  &#x20;[Tay Kak Sie Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tay_Kak_Sie_Temple) was established in 1746, it is dedicated to [Guanyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanyin) Bodhisattva and various Taoist Deities.

- [Central Java Grand Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mosque_of_Central_Java): This mosque has a Muslim museum, located at Jl. Gajah Raya. The architecture of the mosque is inspired by the mosques in Mecca and Medina.

There are several sport centres in Semarang. ### Cuisine



[Lumpia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumpia) Semarang.

Semarang is widely known for its [bandeng presto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandeng_presto) (pressure-cooked milkfish), [Lumpia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumpia), [Wingko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wingko), [Tahu Gimbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahu_Gimbal?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Ganjel Rel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti_gambang). Semarang has also been called 'The city of Jamu' because it is an important centre for the production of *jamu* which are a range of Indonesian herbal medicines that are popular across Indonesia[^cite_note-62] Semawis Market, also known as Pecinan Semarang (Semarang's Chinatown), hosts a plethora of street food vendors, offering a wide varieties of dishes.[^cite_note-63]

### Festivals

[Dugderan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dugderan) is an annual festival in Semarang designated to welcome the Ramadan month (a fasting month for Moslems). The word "dug" describes the sound of bedug (traditional Indonesian musical instrument). The word "der" describes the sound of fireworks.

The icon of the festival is a special puppet dragon-like animal called Warak Ngendog. The word "warak" stands for "holy" and the word "ngendog" expresses a reward for Muslims. Warak Ngendog's feet are chained, representing people's desire that should be postponed during this holy month. As Dugderan is a festival unique for Semarang, it represents an important attraction for both local people and visitors.[^cite_note-64]

## Greater Semarang

Greater Semarang (known as *Kedungsepur*) was initially defined by the government as Semarang city, [Semarang Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semarang_Regency), [Salatiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salatiga) city, [Kendal Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendal_Regency), and [Demak Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demak_Regency).[^cite_note-67] It was later extended to include the western part (12 districts only) of [Grobogan Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grobogan_Regency). Despite the definition, rural and urban cores remain distinct and have not amalgamated into a continuous urban sprawl as is the case in [Greater Jakarta](1340014.md).
