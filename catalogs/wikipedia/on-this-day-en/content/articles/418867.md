The **Fifth Fleet** is a [numbered fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_of_the_United_States_Navy#Numbered_fleets) of the [United States Navy](20518076.md). Its area of responsibility encompasses approximately 2.5 million mi<sup>2</sup> (6.5 million km<sup>2</sup>) and includes the [Persian Gulf](24761.md), [Red Sea](26191.md), [Arabian Sea](2747.md), and parts of the [Indian Ocean](14580.md). It shares a commander and headquarters with [U.S. Naval Forces Central Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Naval_Forces_Central_Command) (NAVCENT) in [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Support_Activity_Bahrain). Fifth Fleet/NAVCENT is a component command of, and reports to, [United States Central Command](338482.md) (CENTCOM).

Established during [World War II](32927.md) in 1944, the Fifth Fleet conducted extensive operations that led to the defeat of [Japanese forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy_in_World_War_II) in the [Central Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean_Areas_\(command\)), including battles for the [Mariana Islands campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_and_Palau_Islands_campaign), [Iwo Jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Iwo_Jima), and [Okinawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_campaign). In 1947, two years after the Allied victory, it was stood down. After remaining inactive for 48 years, the Fifth Fleet was reactivated in 1995. It contributed significant forces in the [Global War on Terrorism](13425800.md) and continues to project US naval power in the Middle East.

## World War II

The Fifth Fleet was initially established during [World War II](32927.md) on 26 April 1944 from the [Central Pacific Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Pacific_Force?action=edit\&redlink=1) under the command of [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_\(United_States\)) [Raymond Spruance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Spruance). Central Pacific Force was itself part of [Pacific Ocean Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean_Areas_\(command\)). The ships of the Fifth Fleet also formed the basis of the [Third Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Third_Fleet), which was the designation of the "Big Blue Fleet" when under the command of Admiral [William F. Halsey, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_F._Halsey,_Jr.)[^cite_note-2] Spruance and Halsey would alternate command of the fleet for major operations, allowing the other admiral and his staff time to prepare for the subsequent one. A secondary benefit was confusing the Japanese into thinking that they were actually two separate fleets as the fleet designation flipped back and forth. Under Admiral Spruance, the Fifth Fleet was, by June 1944, the largest combat fleet in the world, with 535 warships.[^cite_note-Beevor609-3]

While operating under Spruance's command as the Fifth Fleet, the fleet took part in the [Mariana Islands campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_and_Palau_Islands_campaign) of June–August 1944, the [Iwo Jima campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Iwo_Jima) of February–March 1945, and the [Okinawa campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_campaign) of April–June 1945. During the course of these operations, it conducted [Operation Hailstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Hailstone) (a major raid against the Japanese naval base at [Truk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuuk_Atoll)) in February 1944, defeated the [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy) in the [Battle of the Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Philippine_Sea) in June 1944, and blunted the Japanese [Operation Ten-Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ten-Go) – sinking the Japanese [battleship](4054.md) [*Yamato*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_battleship_Yamato) in the process – in April 1945.

The [British Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Pacific_Fleet) operated as part of the Fifth Fleet from March to May 1945 under the designation [Task Force 57](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_Force_57).[^cite_note-4] Halsey then relieved Spruance of command and the British ships, like the rest of the Fifth Fleet, were resubordinated to the Third Fleet.

The Fifth Fleet's next major combat operation would have been [Operation Olympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Downfall#Olympic), the invasion of [Kyushu](16914.md) in the [Japanese Home Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_archipelago), scheduled to begin on 1 November 1945. The size of the operation resulted in plans for the Fifth Fleet to function as a separate command, operating simultaneously with the Third Fleet for the first time. The [atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki) and subsequent cessation of hostilities in August 1945 made this operation unnecessary, and the Fifth Fleet did not return to combat again during the war.

The commanders of the Fifth Fleet during this era were Admirals Spruance (26 April 1944 – 8 November 1945), [John Henry Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Henry_Towers) (8 November 1945 – 18 January 1946), [Frederick C. Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_C._Sherman) (18 January 1946 – 3 September 1946), and [Alfred E. Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_E._Montgomery) (5 September 1946 – 1 January 1947).[^cite_note-5] The Fifth Fleet was deactivated in January 1947. The position of Commander, Fifth Fleet, became Commander, [First Task Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_First_Fleet). Montgomery became Commander, First Task Fleet, upon the deactivation of the Fifth Fleet.

## In the Middle East after 1995

Prior to the first [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) in 1990–1991, U.S. naval operations in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) region were directed by the Commander, Middle Eastern Force (COMMIDEASTFOR). Since this organization was considered insufficient to manage large-scale combat operations during the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), the [Seventh Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Seventh_Fleet) — primarily responsible for the Western Pacific and Indian Ocean and normally based in Japan – was given the temporary task of managing the force during the period. However, no numbered fleet existed permanently within the USCENTCOM area of responsibility. In 1995, [John Scott Redd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Scott_Redd) proposed and founded the only new U.S. Navy Fleet in half a century, serving as the first Commander, Fifth Fleet (COMFIFTHFLT) since World War II.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] After a 48-year hiatus, the [Fifth Fleet](418867.md) was reactivated, replacing COMMIDEASTFOR, and it now directs operations in the Persian Gulf, Red Sea, and Arabian Sea. Its headquarters are at [NSA Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Support_Activity_Bahrain) located in [Manama](20481.md), [Bahrain](18933277.md).



[U.S. Navy](20518076.md), [Royal Navy](26061.md), and [Royal Australian Navy](26327.md) [destroyers](8779.md) and [frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frigates) on joint operations in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md).

For the early years of its existence, its forces normally consisted of an Aircraft [Carrier Battle Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Battle_Group) (CVBG), an [Amphibious Ready Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_Ready_Group) (ARG), [surface combatants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_combatant), [submarines](28825.md), maritime patrol and reconnaissance aircraft, and logistics ships. After the [September 11 terrorist attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_terrorist_attack) and the beginning of [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom), the naval strategy of the U.S. changed. Consequently, the policy of always maintaining a certain number of ships in various parts of the world also changed.

However, its usual configuration now includes a [Carrier Strike Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Strike_Group) (CSG), Amphibious Ready Group or [Expeditionary Strike Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditionary_Strike_Group) (ESG), and other ships and aircraft with almost 15,000 people serving afloat and 1,000 support personnel ashore.[^cite_note-8]

[Carrier Strike Group Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Strike_Group_Three) formed the core of the naval power during the initial phase of [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom) in 2001. Commander, Carrier Group Three, Rear Admiral [Thomas E. Zelibor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_E._Zelibor?action=edit\&redlink=1), arrived in the Arabian Sea on 12 September 2001 and was subsequently designated Commander Task Force 50 (CTF 50), commanding multiple carrier strike groups and coalition forces. The Task Force conducted strikes against [Al-Qaeda](1921.md) and [Taliban](30635.md) forces in [Afghanistan](737.md). Task Force 50 comprised over 59 ships from six nations including six aircraft carriers, stretching over 800 nautical miles.[^cite_note-9]

Naval operations in the Middle East were the subject of DOD Exercise [Millennium Challenge 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Challenge_2002), during which unanticipated maneuvers by opposing forces director Lieutenant General [Paul Van Riper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Van_Riper) USMC (retd.) led to heavy losses to the 'imaginary' exercise U.S. fleet.[^cite_note-10]

In August 2002, Marines from the [22nd Marine Expeditionary Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_Marine_Expeditionary_Unit) (Special Operations Capable (MEU) (SOC)) carried out a long-range deployment exercise from the amphibious assault ship [USS *Wasp* (LHD 1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Wasp_\(LHD-1\)) into Djibouti. During the deployment the MEU also participated in Operation Sea Eagle in the Gulf of Aden and Operation Infinite Anvil in the Horn of Africa.[^cite_note-11]

Fifth Fleet forces peaked in early 2003, when five USN aircraft carriers (CV and CVNs), six amphibious assault ships ([LHAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LHA_\(hull_classification_symbol\)) and [LHDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_Helicopter_Dock)) and their embarked Marine Corps air ground combat elements, their escorting and supply vessels, and over 30 [Royal Navy](26061.md) vessels were under its command.

In the [Persian Gulf](24761.md), [United States Coast Guard](32223.md) surface ships attached to the Fifth Fleet were under Commander, [Destroyer Squadron 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destroyer_Squadron_50) (CDS-50) commanded by [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_\(United_States_O-6\)) John W. Peterson of the Navy.[^cite_note-12] Coast Guard cutters *Boutwell*, *Walnut*, and the four patrol boats were part of this group. The shore detachments, MCSD and [Patrol Forces Southwest Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrol_Forces_Southwest_Asia) also operated under the command of CDS-50. For actual operations, the Coast Guard forces were part of two different task forces. The surface units were part of [Task Force 55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_Force_55?action=edit\&redlink=1) (CTF-55). Command of CTF-55 actually shifted during OIF. Initially, Rear Admiral [Barry M. Costello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_M._Costello), Commander of the Constellation Battle Group, commanded CTF-55. The surface forces were designated Task Group 55.1 (TG-55.1) with Commander Destroyer Squadron 50 (CDS-50) as the task group commander. In mid-April, the Constellation Battle Group left the NAG and the Destroyer Squadron 50 staff commanded TF-55 for the remainder of OIF major combat operations. In the aftermath of the fall of [Baghdad](4492.md) in April 2003, the very large force of ships was quickly drawn down.

On 6 May 2005, a party of Marines reportedly landed in [Somaliland](51425.md), the autonomous and self-declared state in northern Somalia. The landings were purportedly conducted to carry out searches, as well as to question locals regarding the whereabouts of terrorist suspects. Three ships, including a helicopter carrier, were reported in a nearby anchorage, likely a MEU/ARG. United States military officials denied the allegations and said operations were not being conducted in Somaliland.[^cite_note-13]

On 3 January 2012, following the end of the ten-day Velayat 90 naval maneuvers by the [Iranian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Navy) in the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md), the [Iranian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Army) chief of staff, General [Ataollah Salehi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ataollah_Salehi), was quoted by the state news agency [IRNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRNA) as warning the United States to not deploy [*John C. Stennis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_John_C._Stennis) back to the Persian Gulf.[^cite_note-Hafezi-14][^cite_note-15] On 4 January 2011, [Fars News Agency](5000099.md) reported that a bill was being prepared for the [Iranian Parliament](383276.md) to bar foreign naval vessels from entering the Persian Gulf unless they receive permission from the Iranian navy, with Iranian lawmaker Nader Qazipour noting: "If the military vessels and warships of any country want to pass via the Strait of Hormuz without coordination and permission of Iran's navy forces, they should be stopped by the Iranian armed forces."[^cite_note-Erdbrink-16] Also, Iranian Defense Minister [Ahmad Vahidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Vahidi) reiterated that "transnational forces" have no place in the Persian Gulf region.[^cite_note-Erdbrink-16] On 6 January 2012, armed Iranian speedboats reportedly harassed two U.S. naval vessels, the amphibious transport dock [*New Orleans*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_New_Orleans_\(LPD-18\)) and the [Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Coast_Guard) [cutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_class_patrol_boat) [*Adak*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Adak), as they transited the Strait of Hormuz into the Persian Gulf.[^cite_note-17]

On 9 January 2012, [Carrier Strike Group One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Strike_Group_One), led by the carrier [*Carl Vinson*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Carl_Vinson), joined Carrier Strike Group Three in the North Arabian Sea, with [Carrier Strike Group Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Strike_Group_Nine), led by the carrier [*Abraham Lincoln*](199106.md), en route to the Arabian Sea amid rising tension between the United States and the [Islamic Republic of Iran](14653.md) over U.S. naval access to the Strait of Hormuz.[^cite_note-18] On 19 January 2012, Carrier Strike Group Nine entered the Fifth Fleet's area of responsibility (AOR) and relieved Carrier Strike Group Three.[^cite_note-19] That same day during an interview on the [*Charlie Rose*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Rose_Show) program, [Mohammad Khazaee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Khazaee), Iran's [ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador_of_Iran_to_the_United_Nations) to the [United Nations](31769.md), stated that Iran would consider closing the Strait of Hormuz if Iran's security was endangered.[^cite_note-20]



Arleigh Burke-class destroyer [USS *Truxtun* (DDG-103)](765604.md) transits the Persian Gulf during maritime security operations, October 2014

For December 2012 and January 2013, Carrier Strike Group Three was the only carrier strike group operating with the U.S. Fifth Fleet until relieved by the [Carrier Strike Group Ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Strike_Group_Ten). This is because of the temporary two-month rotation of the [Carrier Strike Group Eight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Strike_Group_Eight) back to the United States in order to resurface the flight deck of that group's flagship, the carrier [*Dwight D. Eisenhower*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Dwight_D._Eisenhower).[^cite_note-21] *Dwight D. Eisenhower*, [Carrier Air Wing Seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_Air_Wing_Seven), and the guided-missile cruiser [*Hue City*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Hu%C3%A9_City) returned to base on 19 December 2012, and the guided missile destroyers [*Jason Dunham*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Jason_Dunham), [*Farragut*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Farragut_\(DDG-99\)), and [*Winston S. Churchill*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Winston_S._Churchill) were scheduled to return to base in March 2013.[^cite_note-22]

In September 2016, [Commander Amphibious Task Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_Amphibious_Task_Group), [Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(Royal_Navy\)) [Andrew Burns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Burns_\(Royal_Navy_officer\)), set off from the UK with [*Ocean*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Ocean_\(L12\)), along with helicopters from 845 Naval Air Squadron, No. 662 Squadron AAC and No. 27 Squadron RAF, [*Bulwark*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDM_Oiapoque), and element of [3 Commando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3_Commando) Brigade HQ [Royal Marines](93985.md), [RFA *Mounts Bay*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RFA_Mounts_Bay) and [MV *Eddystone Point*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point-class_sealift_ship) under his flag. This deployment was known as the [Joint Expeditionary Force (Maritime)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Expeditionary_Force_\(Maritime\)) 2016. The Amphibious Task Group was planned to sail to the Red Sea and Persian Gulf, where Burns was to assume command of the United States Fifth Fleet [Task Force 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_Force_50?action=edit\&redlink=1) until March 2017.[^cite_note-MaltaRoyalSendOff-23][^cite_note-24]

On 1 December 2018, Commander, Fifth Fleet, [Vice Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_admiral_\(United_States\)) [Scott A. Stearney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Stearney) was found dead in his residence in Bahrain. No foul play was suspected. Deputy commander Rear Admiral [Paul J. Schlise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_J._Schlise) assumed command in his place.[^cite_note-USNI20181201a-25][^cite_note-NBCN20181201a-26] Vice Admiral [Jim Malloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_J._Malloy) flew to Bahrain to provide support.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-JJM-28] Malloy was formally nominated to succeed Stearney on 4 December[^cite_note-JJM-28][^cite_note-USC-29][^cite_note-30] and quickly confirmed by voice vote of the full [United States Senate](24909346.md) on 6 December.[^cite_note-USC-29][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] Vice Adm. Malloy assumed command on 7 December.[^cite_note-33]

### 2026 war damage

On 28 February 2026, as part of a broader retaliatory wave following joint [U.S.–Israeli strikes on Iran](82533582.md), Iranian missiles and drones struck the Naval Support Activity Bahrain, the headquarters of the U.S. Fifth Fleet in Manama.[^cite_note-34] Satellite imagery later confirmed that two AN/GSC-52B satellite communications (SATCOM) terminals were destroyed, along with damage to several warehouses and at least one base service facility.[^cite_note-35] The acting Navy Secretary noted Iran "blew the hell out of" USN infrastructure in Bahrain, and some reports described the base as destroyed. ### Coalition Forces Maritime Component Command

Together with Naval Forces Central Command, Fifth Fleet oversees four naval task forces monitoring maritime activity:

- [Combined Task Force 150](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Task_Force_150) that patrols from Hormuz, halfway across the Arabia Sea, South as far as the Seychelles, through the Gulf of Aden, up through the strait between Djibouti and Yemen known as the Bab Al Mandeb and into the Red Sea and, finally, around the [Horn of Africa](62142.md);

- Combined Task Force 152 patrols the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) from the northern end where area of responsibility of CTF 158 ends and down to the Strait of Hormuz between Oman and Iran where the area of responsibility for CTF 150 begins;

- [Combined Task Force 151](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Task_Force_151) patrols mostly the same area as CTF 150 but is primarily focused on deterring and disrupting [Somali piracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_piracy) attack on commercial shipping and leisure craft;

- CTF 52 (as above) patrols the same area as CTF 152 but is focused on countermining/demining activity.

  [^cite_note-53]
