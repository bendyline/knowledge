# List of Grand Slam men's singles champions

&#x20;  Many changes in the [Grand Slam tennis tournaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_\(Tennis\)) have affected the number of titles won by various players during its history. These changes have included the opening of the French national championships to international players in 1925, the elimination of the challenge round in 1922, and the admission of professional players in 1968 (the start of the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_tennis#Open_Era)).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

All of these tournaments have been listed based on the modern definition of a tennis major, rather than when they were officially recognized by the [ILTF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ILTF). The Grand Slam tournaments are the annual four major tennis events played in the Open Era, which began in 1968, superseding the Amateur Era. The [Australian](97851.md#History) and [U.S.](82729.md#History) tournaments were officially recognized by the ILTF in 1924, and the [French Championships](147724.md#History) followed a year later in 1925 when it became open to all international players. The [United States Lawn Tennis Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Lawn_Tennis_Association) (USLTA) had several grievances with the ILTF and refused to join when it was formed in 1913.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

From 1913 to 1923, there were three official championships recognized by the ILTF:

- [World Grass Court Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Grass_Court_Championships) – Wimbledon.

- [World Hard Court Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Hard_Court_Championships), held in Paris on clay courts.

- [World Covered Court Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Covered_Court_Championships), held in Europe on an indoor wood surface.

During that same time period the USLTA recognized the U.S. National Championships

- [U.S. National Championships](82729.md), held in New York on grass.



[Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) holds the all-time record of 24 major singles titles, including a record 10 [Australian Open](97851.md) titles. He is the only singles player to hold all four majors simultaneously on [three different surfaces](#Non-calendar_year_Grand_Slam) (2016), and the only one to complete a triple [Career Grand Slam](#Career_Grand_Slam) (2023).



[Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) holds an all-time record of 14 [French Open](147724.md) titles, the most at any slam in tennis history. He is the second man in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_tennis#Open_Era) to complete a double Career Grand Slam (2022).



[Roger Federer](262376.md) holds an all-time record of 8 [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Championships,_Wimbledon) titles. He also held the most major titles in singles between 2009 and 2022.



[Bill Tilden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Tilden) won 10 major singles titles in the 1920s, including an all-time record of seven [U.S. Championships](82729.md) titles.



[Don Budge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Budge) is the only man to have won six consecutive major singles titles, from [Wimbledon 1937](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) to [U.S. Championships 1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_U.S._National_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles), and the first player ever to complete a [Grand Slam](#Grand_Slam) (1938).



[Ken Rosewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Rosewall) holds a record 15 [Pro Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_tennis_tournaments_before_the_Open_Era#Professional_majors) titles, and [23 major titles overall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_male_players_statistics#Most_major_singles_titles), counting both amateur and professional circuits.



[Roy Emerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Emerson) was the first man in history to win each major title twice (1967), and the only man to have completed a Career Grand Slam in both [singles](#Career_Grand_Slam) and [doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Grand_Slam_men's_doubles_champions#Individual_2).



[Rod Laver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Laver) is the only man to complete a Grand Slam more than once in his career, in 1962 as an amateur and in 1969 as a professional.



[Pete Sampras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Sampras) won 14 major singles titles in his career, including seven titles at Wimbledon.



[Andre Agassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Agassi) was the first man to complete a Career Grand Slam across three different surfaces (1999), and the first to win a [Career Golden Slam](#Career_Golden_Slam) (winning the four majors and an [Olympic singles gold medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Olympic_medalists_in_tennis#Men's_singles)).

## Grand Slam achievements

Main article: [Grand Slam (tennis)](197638.md)

These are players who achieved some form of a tennis [Grand Slam](197638.md). They include a Grand Slam, non-calendar year Grand Slam, Career Grand Slam, Career Golden Slam, and Career Super Slam. No male player has won a single season [Golden Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Grand_Slam_women's_singles_champions#Golden_Slam). The tennis [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_tennis#Open_Era) began in 1968, after the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Australian_Open) and before the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_French_Open).
