General elections were held in [Malta](19137.md) on 30 May 2026, with 356,832 registered voters entitled to vote to elect all members of the [House of Representatives](37044813.md).[^cite_note-1] The ruling [Labour Party](404834.md) led by [Robert Abela](62535882.md) won the election, securing 36 seats with 52% of the first preference vote. The party became the first to win four consecutive elections, in spite of its vote share declining by three percentage points from the [2022 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Maltese_general_election).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] The [Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_Party_\(Malta\)), led by [Alex Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Borg_\(politician\)), won 29 seats with 45% of the vote, narrowing the gap between the two parties from 39,474 votes in the 2022 election to 21,721.[^cite_note-Grech-4][^cite_note-Farrugia-5] The Nationalist Party received 2 additional seats through the Proportionality Corrective Mechanism, for a total of 31 seats.[^cite_note-6] Both parties received 6 additional seats each through the Gender Corrective Mechanism, resulting in a Labour Party total of 42 seats, and a Nationalist Party total of 37 seats.[^cite_note-7]

## Background

The [previous elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Maltese_general_election) in March 2022 saw the [Labour Party](404834.md), which has governed the country since 2013, receive 55% of the popular vote and win 44 out of 79 seats in the [House of Representatives](37044813.md).[^cite_note-8] [Robert Abela](62535882.md), the [prime minister](464388.md) and leader of the Labour Party since 2020, and his [new cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_Government_2022%E2%80%932027) were sworn in on 30 March 2022.[^cite_note-9] [Bernard Grech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Grech), the leader of the [Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_Party_\(Malta\)), was re-elected unopposed in May 2022.[^cite_note-10] The current Leader of the Opposition is [Alex Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Borg_\(politician\)), leader of the Nationalist Party, since 10 September 2025.[^cite_note-11] The election was announced following a deal to transfer [Manoel Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manoel_Island) back to the Maltese government, and in the context of the ongoing [war in Iran](82533582.md).[^cite_note-Borg-12]

Despite media speculation for months that Abela was considering calling a [snap election](462915.md), Abela repeatedly denied the rumours. On 22 March, Abela unveiled a focus on a "stronger Malta" for Labour's forthcoming manifesto in a speech to supporters.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Following hints during his Freedom Day speech of an impending election, the [Labour Party](404834.md) held a Congress from 12 to 17 April in which the election manifesto guidelines were approved by the party's members.[^cite_note-15]

In an address on national television, Abela announced a snap election for 30 May 2026, surprisingly citing geopolitical instability as requiring a new mandate from the Maltese people.[^cite_note-16]

## Electoral system

MPs are elected from 13 five-seat [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Malta#Electoral_districts) by [single transferable vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_transferable_vote) using [optional preferential voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optional_preferential_voting).[^cite_note-\:0-20] Candidates who pass the [Droop quota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droop_quota) in the first round are elected, and any surplus votes transferred to the remaining candidates, who will be elected if this enables them to pass the quota.[^cite_note-\:0-20] The lowest ranked candidates are then eliminated one-by-one with their preferences transferred to other candidates, who are elected as they pass the quotient, until all five seats are filled.[^cite_note-\:1-21] If only two parties obtain seats, if the party which wins a majority of first preference votes fails to achieve a parliamentary majority, it is awarded additional seats to ensure a one-seat majority.[^cite_note-\:1-21]

In 2018, the [Parliament of Malta](37044813.md) lowered the national voting age to 16.[^cite_note-22] During the 2017–2022 legislature a gender-corrective mechanism was introduced, with Article 52(A) of the Constitution stating that provides for up to 12 additional seats for unelected candidates from "the under-represented sex" in case one of both makes up less than 40% of the elected MPs.[^cite_note-maltaind-20220328-23] Candidates are allowed to run in two different constituencies, and if elected in both, they must vacate one of the seats. Their replacement is chosen by a "casual election", in which the votes for the vacating candidate are redistributed.[^cite_note-x147-24]

Despite conducting elections under a [proportional](23298.md) [ranked preferential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranked_voting) system, Malta has a stable [two-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-party_system), with only the Labour Party and Nationalist Party having a proven realistic chance of forming a government.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Prior to the [2017 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Maltese_general_election), when the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Malta\)) won two seats while running in a joint list with the Nationalist Party, the last time a party/parties other than the Labour Party or the Nationalist Party won seats were the pro-Church parties in alliance with the Nationalist Party in [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Maltese_general_election).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

## Campaign

### Labour Party

The [Labour Party](404834.md) began its campaign by publishing their slogan *Int Malta* (*You Are Malta*) and multiple billboards with the slogan.[^cite_note-49] On 28 April, only a day after the election announcement, Labour held it first mass rally.[^cite_note-50] The party also published a series of proposals such as the increase of [maternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternity_leave) and various other leaves. It also included multiple [tax reductions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_break) and financial support largely aimed at young people, first-time buyers and seniors. On 29 April, the Labour Party announced it would be making [Gozo Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gozo_Channel) trips free for people, provided they do not bring their car with them, alongside other Gozo-related proposals, including a 45 million euro interconnector, the modernisation of all their schools and road reparations.[^cite_note-51] One of the most important rallies was held on 1 May, which is also [Workers' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Day) where the party announced major promises to the people.[^cite_note-52]

### Nationalist Party

The [Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_Party_\(Malta\)) started its campaign by announcing their slogan *Nifs Ġdid* (*A Breath of Fresh Air*) while also installing billboards and banners with the slogan. On 28 April the party hosted its first Mass Meeting in [Pietà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet%C3%A0,_Malta), outside of the Party HQ. During the mass meeting the party announced multiple proposals related to healthcare such as a new hospital in the North, a new hospital in [Gozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gozo), an expansion of [Mater Dei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mater_Dei_Hospital), free cancer treatment, and others.[^cite_note-\:3-30] Without any media presence, [Alex Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Borg_\(snooker_player\)) announced a new set of proposals which ensured that students in secondary education will be offered maritime related courses. He also suggested the reform of [Transport Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_Malta).[^cite_note-53] During a rally in [Mellieħa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellie%C4%A7a), Borg promised minimum-wage stipends for healthcare students, an overall 25% increase in stipends, and heavy tax incentives for returning healthcare expats.[^cite_note-maltatoday.com.mt-35] On 2 May, the party announced it would make Gozo a region.[^cite_note-54]

### AD+PD

Before the election was announced [AD+PD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AD+PD) and [Momentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momentum_\(political_party\)) announced an electoral agreement.[^cite_note-55] On 28 April, the AD+PD announced its slogan, *Ilkoll* (*All Of Us*).[^cite_note-56] The next day, the party announced its candidates for the election which include: Mark Zerafa in the 1st and 9th districts, Mario Mallia in the 2nd and 8th district, Brain Decelis in the 3rd and 4th district, Melissa Bagley in the 5th district, Sandra Gauci in the 6th and 12th district, Carmel Cacopardo in the 7th and 11th district, Marcus Lauri in the 10th district and Luke Caruana in the 12th and 13th districts.[^cite_note-57]

### Momentum

On 28 April, on the same day as the AD+PD, Momentum announced its slogan, *Bidla Ta' Veru* (*Real Change*). Apart from [Arnold Cassola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Cassola), leading [University of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Malta) researcher, Professor Pierre Schembri-Wismayer will run on the Momentum ticket. On 30 April, Momentum unveiled its candidates for all[13 districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Malta).[^cite_note-58]

### Aħwa Maltin

On 29 April [Aħwa Maltin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C4%A7wa_Maltin) announced they would contest all constituencies under the slogan *Malta għall-Maltin* (*Malta for Maltese*). Their campaign has focused on seven key points: protection and support for the family, strict immigration controls, affordable housing, support for small businesses and workers, safeguarding cultural heritage and [animal welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_welfare), preservation of the Maltese identity, and tackling the cost of living.[^cite_note-q248-59]

### Imperium Europa

On 28 April [Imperium Europa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperium_Europa) announced that party leader Eman Cross would contest the 1st and 9th districts. This is the first general election the party has run in since 2008, as it had since then focused solely on [European Parliament](9581.md) elections.[^cite_note-\:3-30] On the same day, Imperium Europa's [Instagram](31591547.md) account was banned from the platform.[^cite_note-\:7-31]

### Independents

[Perennial candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perennial_candidate#Malta) Nazzareno Bonnici, better known as *Żaren tal-Ajkla* (Żaren of the Eagle), announced that he would be campaigning in the third and sixth districts.[^cite_note-zaren-ajkla-60] Noel Apap also contested as an independent candidate.[^cite_note-61]

### Involvement of A.I. in Campaigning

The election is regarded as the first election [Malta](19137.md) had in the age of [A.I.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.I.) as edits and A.I. photos and videos filled [social media](5897742.md). Less than a day after the announcement of the election, videos and photos aimed at mocking the two parties flooded social media. Most of these photos were centered around the slogans, *Nifs Ġdid* and *Int Malta*. Examples of these jokes were 'Int India' or 'You are India' because of the large number of [Indian](14533.md) immigrants in the country. On the other hand, photos began circulating of [Alex Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Borg_\(politician\)) smoking [cannabis](1481886.md).[^cite_note-79] A.I. involvement was not reserved to the two main parties, but smaller parties most notably [Aħwa Maltin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C4%A7wa_Maltin) made a short edit showing their leader Paul Salamone being smarter and more competent than [Robert Abela](62535882.md).[^cite_note-80]

## Opinion polls

Main article: [Opinion polling for the 2026 Maltese general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_for_the_2026_Maltese_general_election)



[Local regression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_regression) trend line of poll results, with each line corresponding to a political party.

[Opinion polling conducted in 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_for_the_2026_Maltese_general_election) has generally shown the Labour Party maintaining a lead over the Nationalist Party, although the margin varies across surveys. A MaltaToday survey conducted in March 2026 placed the [Labour Party](404834.md) at approximately 48.2% and the [Nationalist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_Party_\(Malta\)) at 45.6%, indicating a narrow lead.[^cite_note-\:2-81] Earlier surveys in January 2026 reported similar results, with Labour polling at around 48.9% and the Nationalist Party at 45.7%.[^cite_note-82] A separate survey conducted by Vincent Marmarà in February 2026 suggested a wider gap when accounting for undecided voters, projecting Labour support at 52.8% compared to around 42.6% for the Nationalist Party.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

Cost of living, purchasing power, and the economy are considered significant electoral issues, yet a KPMG report indicates that wages have effectively remained stagnant since 2018.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86]

## Results

The Labour Party secured a fourth consecutive election victory on 31 May, winning 51.8% of the vote and a majority of 21,721 votes. The Nationalist Party received the support of 44.7% of the electorate, while third parties collectively obtained 3.5%.[^cite_note-Farrugia-5] The Labour Party mainly dominated in the south of the [island of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_\(island\)), while the Nationalist Party dominated in the north of the island of Malta and narrowly flipped [Gozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gozo).[^cite_note-Grech-4][^cite_note-Farrugia-5]

## Aftermath

The [Labour Party](404834.md) declared victory at approximately 11\:00 am.[^cite_note-mto_liveblog-91] The initial projected vote gap between the two major parties was 18,000, a reduction of over half from the 2022 general elections, however the final tally released by the Electoral Commission indicates a gap of 21,721 votes, still a decrease of 3.34% for Labour. [Alex Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Borg_\(politician\)) conceded defeat and responded to his loss with a sense of positivity on social media, stating that his work will continue and that he will work to further reduce the gap.[^cite_note-mto_liveblog-91]
