The **1954 FIFA World Cup** was the fifth edition of the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), the quadrennial international [football](10568.md) tournament for senior men's national teams of the nations affiliated to [FIFA](11049.md). It was held in [Switzerland](26748.md) from 16 June to 4 July. Switzerland was selected as the [host country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_hosts#1950_and_1954_FIFA_World_Cups) in July 1946.[^cite_note-hosts-1] At the tournament, several all-time records for goalscoring were set, including the highest average number of goals scored per game. The tournament was won by [West Germany](250204.md), who defeated tournament favourites [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) 3–2 in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) for their first World Cup title. [Uruguay](679782.md), the defending champions, were eliminated by Hungary in the semi-finals and would lose to [Austria](695226.md) in the match for third place.

The [highest scoring match of a men’s World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria_v_Switzerland_\(1954_FIFA_World_Cup\)) happened in the quarter-finals of this tournament, when [Austria](695226.md) defeated hosts [Switzerland](681862.md) 7–5.

## Host selection

Switzerland was awarded the tournament unopposed at a meeting in [Luxembourg City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_City) on 22 July 1946, the same day Brazil was selected to host the [1950 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-hosts-1]

## Qualification

Main article: [1954 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification)

The hosts (Switzerland) and the defending champions (Uruguay) qualified automatically. Of the remaining 14 places, 11 were allocated to Europe (including Egypt, Turkey, and Israel), two to the Americas, and one to Asia.

Scotland, Turkey, and South Korea made their World Cup debuts at this tournament (Turkey and Scotland had qualified for the [1950 competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup) but both withdrew). South Korea became the first independent Asian country to participate in a World Cup tournament. Austria appeared following a hiatus from [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup). Several teams, such as Hungary and Czechoslovakia were back into the tournament after missing out the [1950 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup).

German teams as well as [Japan](993546.md) were allowed to qualify again, after having been banned from the [1950 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup). West Germany qualified against fellow Germans from the [Saarland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland_national_football_team) (which then was a French protectorate), while East Germany did not enter, having cancelled international football matches after the [East German uprising of 1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uprising_of_1953_in_East_Germany). [Argentina](454699.md) declined to participate for the third successive World Cup.

## Summary

### Format

#### Group stage

The 1954 tournament used a unique format. The sixteen qualified teams were divided into four groups of four teams each. Each group contained two seeded teams and two unseeded teams. Only four matches were scheduled for each group, each pitting a seeded team against an unseeded team. This contrasts with the usual [round-robin](939742.md) in which every team plays every other team: six matches in each group. Another oddity was that [extra time](633242.md#Association_football), which in most tournaments is not employed at the group stage, was played in the group games if the score was level after 90 minutes, with the result being a draw if the scores were still level after 120 minutes.[^cite_note-jura-2]

Two points were awarded for a win and one for a draw. The two teams with the most points from each group progressed to the knockout stage. In the case of a tie between two teams for second place, the two tied teams competed in a play-off to decide which team would progress to the next stage, with extra time and drawing of lots if necessary.[^cite_note-jura-2][^cite_note-3] Had all four teams in a group been tied on points, there would have been two further play-offs – one play-off between the two seeded teams, and the other between the two unseeded teams, again with extra time and drawing of lots if necessary – with the winner of each play-off progressing to the quarter-finals.[^cite_note-jura-2]



Qualified countries

Two of the four groups ended up requiring play-offs – one between Switzerland and Italy, and the other between Turkey and West Germany. In each match, the unseeded team (Switzerland and West Germany) repeated an earlier victory against the seeded team (Italy and Turkey) to progress. The fact that two group matches were played twice, while other group opponents never faced each other at all, attracted criticism; newly elected FIFA President [Rodolphe Seeldrayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolphe_Seeldrayers) declared that this group format would be abandoned in future world cups.[^cite_note-4]

#### Quarter-finals

For each of the first two quarter-finals, one team progressing from group 1 was drawn against one team progressing from group 2. For the remaining two quarter-finals, this procedure was repeated for groups 3 and 4.[^cite_note-jura-2] Before the tournament, it was stated that in the event of a quarter-final being tied after 90 minutes, 30 minutes of extra time would be played, followed by drawing of lots if necessary.[^cite_note-jura-2] Later, it was stated that a quarter-final could be replayed in this situation.[^cite_note-nzz-5] The draw was scheduled to be held on Sunday 20 June, though in fact it was delayed into the early morning of Monday 21 June.[^cite_note-6]

#### Semi-finals

For the semi-finals, a further draw was held, with each semi-final featuring one team from groups 1–2 against one team from groups 3–4.[^cite_note-jura-2] In the event of a semi-final being tied after extra time, it would be replayed once, followed by drawing of lots if necessary.[^cite_note-jura-2]

The draw for the semi-finals, held on Sunday 27 June, was delayed by a complaint from the Hungarian team concerning the manner in which their quarter-final against Brazil had been played.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-nzz-5]

#### Final

The final would be replayed if scores were level after extra-time. If the replay was also tied, the winner would be decided by the tournament organising committee,[^cite_note-jura-2] or by drawing of lots.[^cite_note-8]

### Seeding

Before qualification was complete, the eight seeded teams were determined by FIFA. They were [Austria](695226.md), [Brazil](149286.md), [England](9904.md), [France](250197.md), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team), [Italy](362466.md), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_men's_national_football_team), and [Uruguay](679782.md).

These seedings were thrown into disarray when, in an unexpected result, [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) eliminated Spain in qualification. FIFA resolved this situation by giving Turkey the seeding that had previously been allocated to Spain.[^cite_note-9]

### Notable results

The [Germany national football team](250204.md), then limited to the area of [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany) only, had been reinstated as full FIFA members only in late 1950, had [played only 18 games since](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany_national_football_team_results), and were unseeded. When meeting the seeded Turkish side at Wankdorf stadium in Berne, the Turks scored early, but the Germans convincingly won the encounter, which would turn out as the first of two within six days.

The South Koreans, the other unseeded team, lost 7–0 and 9–0 against the seeded sides of Hungary and Turkey. West Germany, being denied the chance to play such an easy opponent, had to face the seeded team of Hungary, a favourite to win the World Cup. [Sepp Herberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepp_Herberger), the West German coach, did not want his A-squad to suffer a possible defeat against a strong opponent while trying to qualify for a rather meaningless first place, and gambled by going the easier route into the play-offs and sending in a reserve side which lost 8–3 in Basel. A benefit was that the Hungarians did not get to know the best German players in case both teams would meet again - which they did, in the final, along with referee [William Ling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ling_\(referee\)). Now two teams were tied for second place on 2 points, and with no tie breaking procedures in effect, even though West Germany had beaten Turkey head-to-head, they had to play-off against each other, a match-up that West Germany easily won for the second time within six days, this time in Zürich.

Hungary's team captain [Ferenc Puskás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferenc_Pusk%C3%A1s), considered by many as the best player in the world in that time, was injured by West German defender [Werner Liebrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Liebrich), and had to miss Hungary's next two matches. Puskás played for Hungary in the final, despite still being in a questionable condition.[^cite_note-10]

In the quarter-finals, the favourites Hungary beat Brazil 4–2 in one of the most violent matches in football history, which became infamous as the [Battle of Berne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Berne_\(1954_FIFA_World_Cup\)). Meanwhile, the World Cup holders Uruguay sent England out of the tournament, also by 4–2. West Germany dispatched [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) 2–0, and Austria beat the host nation Switzerland in [the game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria_v_Switzerland_\(1954\)) with the most goals in any World Cup match, 7–5.

In the first semi-final, West Germany beat Austria 6–1.

In the other semi-final, one of the most exciting games of the tournament, Hungary led Uruguay 1–0 at halftime, but the game went to extra time with a 2–2 score after 90 minutes. The deadlock was broken by [Sándor Kocsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A1ndor_Kocsis) with two late goals to take Hungary through to the final, with Uruguay finally losing their unbeaten record in World Cup Final matches. Uruguay then went on to be beaten for a second time as Austria secured third place.

### Final: "The Miracle of Bern"

Main article: [1954 FIFA World Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup_Final)

At the [Wankdorf Stadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wankdorf_Stadion) in Berne, 60,000 people watched the final between West Germany and Hungary, a rematch of a first-round game, which Hungary had won 8–3 against the reserves of the German team. The [Golden Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Team) of the Hungarians were favourites, as they were unbeaten for a record of 32 consecutive matches, but they had had two tough knockout matches. It started raining on match day–in Germany this was dubbed *Fritz-Walter-Wetter* ("Fritz Walter's weather") because the West German team captain [Fritz Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Walter) was said to play his best in the rain, a result of having contracted [malaria](20423.md) during the war. [Adi Dassler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Dassler) of *Adidas* provided the West German team with a new kind of shoes, with exchangeable studs that were replaced during halftime.&#x20;



Card autographed by coach [Sepp Herberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepp_Herberger) and the 11 German players that appeared in the final

Hungary's Ferenc Puskás played again in the final, even though he was not fully fit. Despite this he put his team ahead after only six minutes and with [Zoltán Czibor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zolt%C3%A1n_Czibor) adding another two minutes later it seemed that the pre-tournament favourites would take the title. However, with a quick goal from [Max Morlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Morlock) in the 10th minute and an equaliser by [Helmut Rahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Rahn) in the 19th, the tide began to turn.

The second half featured telling misses by the Hungarian team. Barely six minutes before the end of the match, the popular German radio reporter [Herbert Zimmermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Zimmermann_\(football_commentator\)) delivered the most famous line in the German commentary, recommending that "Rahn should shoot from deep", which he did. The second goal from Rahn gave West Germany a 3–2 lead while the Hungarian reporter [György Szepesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gy%C3%B6rgy_Szepesi) burst into tears. Later, Zimmermann called Puskás offside before he kicked the ball into [Toni Turek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Turek)'s net with 2 minutes left. While referee [William Ling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ling_\(referee\)) pointed to the centre spot, linesman [Griffiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Mervyn_Griffiths) signalled offside. After a one-minute consultation, Ling disallowed the claimed equaliser.

The West Germans were handed the [Jules Rimet Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Rimet_Trophy) as World Cup winners, while the crowd sang along to the tune of the [national anthem of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_anthem_of_Germany) – there was disquiet in the stadium as the Germans fans decided to sing the anthem's first stanza *Deutschland über alles*,[^cite_note-11] instead of the uncontroversial third *Unity and Justice and Freedom* which was supposed to be sung at official events according to a 1952 decree. In Germany the success is known as the "Miracle of Bern" (*Das Wunder Von Bern*), and was memorialised in a [2003 film of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Miracle_of_Bern). For the Hungarians, the defeat was a disaster, and remains controversial for apparent refereeing errors and claims of [doping](860860.md).

One controversy concerns the 2–2 equaliser. Hungarian goalie [Gyula Grosics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyula_Grosics) jumped to catch [Fritz Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Walter)'s corner shot, but [Hans Schäfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Sch%C3%A4fer) obstructed him (in plain sight of TV cameras), allowing the ball to reach Rahn, who then scored. Another controversy concerns allegations of doping. Though teammates steadfastly denied this rumour, German historian [Guido Knopp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Knopp) claimed in a 2004 documentary for German public channel [ZDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZDF)[^cite_note-12] that the players were injected with shots of [vitamin C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_C) at half-time, using a needle earlier taken from a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) sports doctor. This resulted in both the better condition of the West German team in the second half and the wave of [jaundice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaundice) among their players following the tournament. A [Leipzig University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_University) study in 2010 posited that the West German players had been injected with the banned substance [methamphetamine](11038318.md).[^cite_note-13]

Most controversial was the offside ruling for Puskás's intended 87th-minute equaliser. The camera filming the official footage was in a bad position to judge the situation, but eyewitnesses claimed that the referee was wrong, including West German substitute player [Alfred Pfaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Pfaff).[^cite_note-14] In 2004, North German regional public channel [NDR](2917610.md) aired unofficial footage appearing to show that Puskás was onside.[^cite_note-15]

The venues in Bern, Zurich and Lausanne each hosted 5 matches, the venue in Geneva hosted 4 matches, and the venue in Lugano only hosted 1 match.

## Squads

Main article: [1954 FIFA World Cup squads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup_squads)

The 16 finalists named squads of 22 for the finals, though South Korea only named 20 players in their squad. Unlike recent tournaments, there were no requirements for teams to name three goalkeepers; most teams did, but 6 did not. Some teams also chose to leave some of their named squad at home, only bringing them to Switzerland if necessary.

## In film

The final scene of [Rainer Werner Fassbinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainer_Werner_Fassbinder)'s 1979 film *[The Marriage of Maria Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Marriage_of_Maria_Braun)* takes place during the finals of the 1954 World Cup; in the scene's background, the sports announcer is celebrating West Germany's victory and shouting *"Deutschland ist wieder was!"* (Germany is something again); the film uses this as the symbol of Germany's recovery from the ravages of the Second World War.

[Sönke Wortmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B6nke_Wortmann)'s 2003 German box-office hit *[The Miracle of Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Miracle_of_Bern)* (in German: *Das Wunder von Bern*) re-tells the story of the German team's route to victory through the eyes of a young boy who admires the key player of the final, [Helmut Rahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Rahn).
