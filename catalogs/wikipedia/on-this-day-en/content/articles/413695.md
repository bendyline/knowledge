**Meerut** is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_India) that serves as the administrative headquarters of the [Meerut district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_district) in the [Indian state](375986.md) of Uttar Pradesh. The city lies in [western Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Uttar_Pradesh) and is part of the Indian [National Capital Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Capital_Region_\(India\)) (NCR). Meerut is located 80 km (50 mi) northeast of the national capital, [New Delhi](51585.md), and 480 km (300 mi) northwest of the state capital, [Lucknow](392095.md).[^cite_note-CDPcityprofile2-11] As of 2025, Meerut's urban (metro) population is estimated at ≈ 1.875 million. The population of [Meerut Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Municipal_Corporation) is projected to be about 1.907 million, while the wider Meerut metropolitan region is estimated at approximately 2.077 million. Meerut is the second or third-most populous city in NCR, after [Delhi](37756.md)[^cite_note-12] and [Ghaziabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaziabad).[^cite_note-13]

In 1803, Meerut was as a significant location during the [British colonial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_India), serving as [the site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Cantonment) of one of [India's largest cantonments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Cantonments_in_India).[^cite_note-14] Meerut was one of the [first locations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_mutiny) where the [1857 rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857) against British rule began. It has been called the "Sports City of India" due to its sports goods industry.[^cite_note-15] Meerut produces large number of [musical instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_instrument) in India. It is also one of [Asia](689.md)'s largest gold markets. The city is an education hub in western Uttar Pradesh and has the third-highest per capita income after [Ghaziabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaziabad_district) and [Gautam Buddha Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautam_Buddha_Nagar_district) ([Noida](986046.md) and [Greater Noida](4942600.md)).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

India's first [Regional Rapid Transit System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Rapid_Transit_System), [Delhi Meerut RRTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Meerut_Regional_Rapid_Transit_System), is operational in Meerut and serves as an inter-city high-speed metro corridor.[^cite_note-18] Meerut is connected by three [expressways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled-access_highway), including the [Delhi-Meerut Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Meerut_Expressway), the [Ganga Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga_Expressway), and the Meerut-Kanpur Expressway.[^cite_note-19]

## Origin of the name

The name Meerut ([Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi_language): [मेरठ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%A0) *Meraṭh*) is believed to have evolved from *Mayarashtra* (मयराष्ट्र), the capital city of the mythological kingdom ruled by [Mayasura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayasura), a legendary architect and the father of [Mandodari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandodari), wife of [Ravana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravana). Over the centuries, the name underwent several transformations—such as *Mairashtra*, *Mai-dant-ka-khera*, and *Mairaath*—before settling into its current form, Meerut.[^cite_note-20]

Another tradition attributes the city's origin to King [Yudhishthira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yudhishthira), who is said to have granted the land to Mayasura in recognition of his architectural prowess. According to this account, Mayasura named the region Maharashtra (not to be confused with the modern day Indian state of [Maharashtra](20629.md)), which eventually morphed into Meerut due to linguistic and historical evolution.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

An alternative version suggests, that Meerut was part of the dominions of [King Mahipala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahipala), the ruler of [Indraprastha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indraprastha), and that the city's name is derived from his association with the area.[^cite_note-Homepage2-23][^cite_note-dg2-24][^cite_note-pundir2-25]

## History

### Ancient era

Archaeological excavations conducted between 1950 and 1952 at Vidura-ka-Tila, a collection of several mounds named after [Vidura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidura) 37 km (23 miles) northeast of Meerut, concluded that Meerut contains the remains of the ancient city of [Hastinapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastinapur), the capital of the [Kauravas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kauravas) and [Pandavas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandavas) of *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata)*, which was washed away by floods from the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges).[^cite_note-off-26][^cite_note-imp2542-27][^cite_note-mdat-28]

Meerut also contained a [Harappan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harappan_Civilisation) settlement known as [Alamgirpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamgirpur), the easternmost settlement of the [Indus Valley civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_civilisation). Meerut was a centre of [Buddhism](3267529.md) during the reign of [Mauryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan) emperor [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka) (r. 273 BC to 232 BC.), and remains of [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) structures were found near the [congregational mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregational_mosque) in the present day city.[^cite_note-brit2-29] The [Ashoka Pillar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillars_of_Ashoka), at [Delhi Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Ridge), was carried to Delhi from Meerut by [Firuz Shah Tughluq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firuz_Shah_Tughluq) (r. 1351–1388).[^cite_note-imp2542-27][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] It was later damaged in a 1713 explosion and restored in 1867.[^cite_note-32]

In [Hindu mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_mythology), Meerut has a strong connection to the *Mahabharata*. One of the main characters in the epic, [Draupadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draupadi), is believed by the locals to have been born here.[^cite_note-auto-33]

### Muslim conquests

Main article: [Subah of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subah_of_Delhi)

In the eleventh century AD, the region to the south-west of the city was ruled by Har Dat, the *Dor [Raja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja)* of [Bulandshahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulandshahr). He built a fort, which was long known for its strength and was mentioned in the *[Ain-i-Akbari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ain-i-Akbari)*.[^cite_note-imp264-34] He was later defeated by [Mahmud of Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni) in 1018 and surrendered along with his forces to Mahmud.[^cite_note-35] The Jama Masjid (Congregational mosque), a prominent local landmark, was said to be built by Mahmud's [vizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizier) in this period. Shortly after its capture, the city was regained by the local Hindu raja and part of his fortifications, built for the city's defence, survived into modern times.[^cite_note-36] [Muhammad of Ghor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_of_Ghor)'s [mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk) general [Qutb-ud-din Aybak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutb-ud-din_Aybak) attacked and captured Meerut in 1193.[^cite_note-37]

[Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur) attacked and sacked Meerut in 1399. The city was held by Ilyas Afghan and his son Maula Muhammad Thaneswari, who were assisted by non-Muslims led by Safi. Timur tried to negotiate a surrender, but the inhabitants of the fort replied saying that [Tarmashirin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarmashirin) had tried to capture the city in the past, but failed. Incensed, Timur attacked the city with 10,000 cavalry. The forces scaled the walls and Safi was killed in the battle. The inhabitants were killed and their wives and children were enslaved. The fortifications and houses were razed to the ground, and the prisoners were ordered to be [flayed alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flaying).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

The city then came under the rule of the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) and saw a period of relative tranquility.[^cite_note-imp2552-40] During the rule of [Mughal Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Emperor) [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar) (r. 1556–1605), there was a mint producing copper coins in the city.[^cite_note-brit2-29] During the reign of Akbar, Meerut was listed in the *Ain-i-Akbari* as a [pargana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pargana) under [Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subah_of_Delhi) [Sarkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarkar_\(administrative_division\)), producing a revenue of 4,391,996 [dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dam_\(Indian_coin\)) for the imperial treasury and supplying a force of 300 infantry and 100 cavalry.[^cite_note-Ain-i-Akbari2-41]

A major part of Meerut was controlled by [Sayyid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid) [jagirdars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagirdar) of [Abdullapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullapur,_Meerut), from 16th to late 18th century. Sayyed Mir Abdulla Naqvi Al Bukhari built Kot Fort in Abdullapur in 16th century, and it became his main residence.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] The descendants of Syed Sadarudin Shah Kabir Naqvi Al Kannauji Bukhari are still present in Meerut; Sadarudin was a chief advisor of [Sikandar Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikandar_Lodi) and the father of saint [Shah Jewna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Jewna).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] The famous Pakistani writer Syed Qudrat Naqvi Al Bukhari was born in Meerut.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

Nawab Mansab Ali Khan was one of the most influential personalities of Meerut; he built the famous Karbala and Masjid, known as Mansabiya, in 1882.[^cite_note-jagran.com2-52]

The city saw [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Confederacy) and [Maratha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) invasions in the 18th century, with interruptions by Taga Brahman and [Rohillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohilla_dynasty). [Walter Reinhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Reinhardt_Sombre), a European soldier, established himself at [Sardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardhana), and some parts of the district came under his rule. Upon his death, these areas came into the hands of [Begum Samru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begum_Samru). During this time, the southern part of the district had remained under Maratha rule.[^cite_note-imp2-53]

### Colonial era

Main articles: [Ceded and Conquered Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceded_and_Conquered_Provinces), [North-Western Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-Western_Provinces), [United Provinces of Agra and Oudh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_Agra_and_Oudh), [Meerut mutiny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_mutiny), and [Meerut Conspiracy Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Conspiracy_Case)

In 1803, with the [fall of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Delhi_\(1803\)), [Daulat Rao Scindia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daulat_Rao_Sindhia) of the [Marathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathas) ceded the territory to the British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) (EIC). The cantonment of Meerut was established in 1806 due to its proximity to Delhi and its location in the fertile [Ganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga)–[Yamuna](354286.md) [Doab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doab) region. The city was made headquarters of the [eponymous district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_district) in 1818.[^cite_note-imp2552-40][^cite_note-kinship-54][^cite_note-imp2562-55]



[1857 Mutineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_mutiny)' Mosque

Meerut is often associated with the [1857 rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857) against [Company rule in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_India).[^cite_note-56] The slogan "*Dilli Chalo*" ("Let's march to Delhi!") was first spoken in the city, and the [Meerut cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Cantonment) was the place where the rebellion started. Another name for Meerut is "Kranti Dhara," or "the land of revolution," denoting the place where India's independence movement began.

The revolt, which catapulted Meerut into international prominence, began in March 1857 in [Barrackpore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrackpore), [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency). Indian sepoy [Mangal Pandey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangal_Pandey) shot at two of his commanding officers, missed, then [attempted suicide](26809587.md). He was later executed. By April, Pandey's uprising had spread across North India and reached Meerut, which was then the second-largest [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) garrison. Here, Europeans and native [sepoys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepoy) were evenly balanced, with a little more than 2,000 on each side. The European cantonment was separated from the Indian one. [Sadar Bazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddar_Bazaar) and Lal Kurti Bazar were close by, the latter named after the [red uniforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_coat_\(military_uniform\)) worn by the [Presidency armies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_armies). On 24 April 1857, Meerut's commander, Colonel Carmichael Smyth, paraded 90 Indian sepoys of the [Bengal Cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Cavalry), most of whom had come from Uttar Pradesh and [Bihar](442482.md). He ordered the soldiers to fire the new [Enfield cartridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern_1853_Enfield) they had been supplied with. The cartridges were covered with paper that had to be torn off, and Muslim soldiers believed the paper was greased with [pig fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lard) ([pigs in religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigs_in_religion)) and Hindus thought it was greased with [cow fat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallow) ([cows in Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cows_in_Hinduism)). Thus, they refused to touch them when ordered to.[^cite_note-57]

All 85 soldiers were stripped of their uniforms, and [court-martialed](58625.md); they were all sentenced to a decade in prison. The prisoners, who were [upper-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_class) members of a [cavalry regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalry), were shocked at the harsh sentences handed down to them. On 10 May 1857, [Kotwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotwal) [Dhan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhan_Singh) opened the gates of the prison. These soldiers, along with the other imprisoned soldiers, escaped prison and declared themselves free. They proceeded to mutiny, attack, and kill several Company officials in the city to bring it under their control. This marked the beginning of a widespread revolt across northern India, as the soldiers marched towards Delhi. 10 May is celebrated as a local holiday in Meerut.[^cite_note-58]



The [United Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_British_India) in 1903.

Meerut was also the venue of the [Meerut Conspiracy Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Conspiracy_Case) in March 1929, in which several [trade unionists](17626.md), including three [Englishmen](18803164.md), were arrested by the colonial authorities for organising a railway [strike action](155134.md). The case quickly became the subject of attention in England, inspiring a 1932 play titled *Meerut Prisoners* by left-wing [Manchester](20206.md) street theatre group the *Red Megaphones*, which highlighted the detrimental effects of [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism) and [industrialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialisation).[^cite_note-59] Electricity was first introduced to Meerut in 1931.[^cite_note-kinship-54] In the 1940s, during the height of the [Indian independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement), cinema-goers in Meerut had an unofficial policy of refusing to stand up when *[God Save the King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_Save_the_King)* played before the film was shown.[^cite_note-victoria's_secret2-60] The last session of the [Indian National Congress](149333.md) (INC) before the [independence of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Independence_Act_1947) in 1947, was held at Victoria Park in Meerut on 26 November 1946. It was in this session that the [Constitution-making committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_Assembly_of_India) was constituted.[^cite_note-victoria's_secret2-60]

### Post-independence era

Main articles: [1987 Meerut riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Meerut_riots) and [2006 Meerut fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Meerut_fire)

The city and district also suffered from communal [Hindu-Sikh riots in 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_anti-Sikh_riots)[^cite_note-61] and [Hindu-Muslim riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu-Muslim_riots) in 1982.[^cite_note-62] In May 1987, the [Hashimpura massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashimpura_massacre) occurred when personnel of the [Provincial Armed Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Armed_Constabulary) (PAC) shot and killed 42 Muslims; the trial of the case is still pending.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] In 2006, a [fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Meerut_fire) at a consumer electronics "[Brand India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_India)" fair in Victoria Park Stadium killed at least 100 people, with authorities confirming 45 fatalities, although a specific figure on a toll was difficult to determine and was believed to be much higher.[^cite_note-65]

Meerut has a [monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon_in_India) influenced [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical) climate characterised by hot summers and cooler winters. Summers last from early April to late June and are extremely hot, with temperatures reaching up to 49 °C (120 °F).[^cite_note-ch32-67]

The monsoon arrives in late June and continues till the middle of September. Temperatures drop slightly, with increased cloud cover and higher humidity. Temperatures rise again in October and the city then has a mild, dry winter season from November to the middle of March.[^cite_note-ch32-67] The lowest temperature ever recorded is −0.4 °C (31.3 °F), on 6 January 2013.[^cite_note-68] The rainfall averages about 845 millimetres (33 in) per annum, which is suitable for crop cultivation. Most of the rainfall is received during the monsoon. The humidity varies from 30 to 100%.[^cite_note-ch32-67]

&#x20;Meerut has been ranked 25th best "National Clean Air City" (under Category 1 >10 [lakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakh) population cities) in India according to 'Swachh Vayu Survekshan 2024 Results.[^cite_note-69]

## Administration

### General administration

Main articles: [Meerut district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_district) and [Meerut division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_division)

The [Meerut division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_division) consists of [six districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Uttar_Pradesh), and is headed by the [Divisional Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisional_Commissioner_\(India\)) of Meerut, who is a senior [IAS officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service). The Commissioner is the head of local government institutions (including Municipal Corporations) in the division, and is in charge of infrastructural development in their division.[^cite_note-\:222-75][^cite_note-\:4222-76][^cite_note-\:522-77][^cite_note-\:622-78][^cite_note-79] The [District Magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_collector) of Meerut reports to the [Divisional Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisional_Commissioner_\(India\)). The current Commissioner is Mr. Surendra Singh, IAS .[^cite_note-\:1-80][^cite_note-81]

The [Meerut district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_district) administration is headed by the [District Magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Collector) of Meerut, who is an IAS officer. The [DM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Collector) is in charge of property records and revenue collection for the central government and oversees the [elections](1435365.md) held in the city.[^cite_note-\:222-75][^cite_note-\:423-82][^cite_note-\:632-83][^cite_note-\:532-84][^cite_note-85] The district is subdivided into three [tehsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil), namely Meerut, [Mawana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawana) and [Sardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardhana), each headed by a [Sub-Divisional Magistrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-Divisional_Magistrate). The tehsils are further divided into 12 blocks.[^cite_note-msme2-86] The current District Magistrate of Meerut is Dr. Vijay Kumar Singh.[^cite_note-87]

### Police administration

Main article: [Uttar Pradesh Police](14570210.md)

The Meerut district comes under the Meerut police zone and the Meerut police range of [Uttar Pradesh Police](14570210.md). The Meerut zone is headed by an [IPS officer](451884.md) of the rank of [Additional Director General of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additional_director_general_of_police) (ADG), whereas the Meerut range is headed by an [IPS officer](451884.md) of the rank of [Inspector General of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector-general_of_police) (IG). Currently, Bhanu Bhaskar is the ADG of the Meerut Zone and Praveen Kumar is the current IG of the Meerut range.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

The District Police of Meerut is headed by the [Senior Superintendent of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintendent_of_Police_\(India\)) (SSP) who is an [IPS](451884.md) officer. He is assisted by four [Superintendents of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintendent_of_Police_\(India\)) (SP)/[Additional Superintendent of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additional_superintendent_of_police) (Addl. SP) (City, Rural Area, Traffic and Crime). The Meerut district is divided into numerous police circles, each headed by a [Circle Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_Officer) of the rank of [Deputy Superintendent of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Superintendent_of_Police). SP (Traffic) and SP (Crime) are assisted by one Circle Officer of the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police.[^cite_note-\:43-90] The current SSP is Avinash Pandey.[^cite_note-\:43-90]

### Infrastructure and civic administration

Main article: [Meerut Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Municipal_Corporation)

The development of infrastructure in the city is overseen by the Meerut Development Authority (MDA),[^cite_note-91] which comes under the Housing Department of [Uttar Pradesh government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Uttar_Pradesh). The Divisional Commissioner of Meerut acts as the *[ex-officio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_officio_member)* Chairman of MDA, whereas a vice-chairman, a government-appointed IAS officer, looks after the daily matters of the authority. The current vice-chairman of Meerut Development Authority is Sanjay Kumar Meena.[^cite_note-92]

The city is administered by [Meerut Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Municipal_Corporation), which is responsible for performing civic administrative functions administered by the [Municipal Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_commissioner_\(India\)) ([PCS Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Civil_Service_\(Uttar_Pradesh\))) whereas the Mayor is the ceremonial head of the corporation. The current Municipal Commissioner of Meerut Municipal Corporation is Saurabh Gangwar.

### Municipal finance

According to financial data published on the CityFinance Portal of the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs,[^cite_note-93] the Meerut Municipal Corporation reported total revenue receipts of ₹430 crore (US\$52 million) and total expenditure of ₹353 crore (US\$42 million) in 2022–23. Tax revenue accounted for about 10.5% of the total revenue, while the corporation received ₹382 crore in grants during the financial year.

### Central government offices

The office of the [Chief Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Commissioner_of_Income_Tax), Customs and Central Excise, Meerut Zone, has jurisdiction over 13 districts of [Uttarakhand](1429154.md) and 14 districts of [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md). This jurisdiction was carved out of the Lucknow Zone. It comprises the erstwhile Customs and [Central Excise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Excise) Commissioners of Meerut and [Noida](986046.md). The Meerut Commissionerate was bifurcated into two Commissionerates, namely, 'Meerut-I and Ghaziabad' and the Noida Commissionerate was bifurcated into 'Noida and Meerut-II'. In addition, the jurisdiction of Central Excise Division Bareilly was included in the jurisdiction of Meerut-II Commissionerate.[^cite_note-94]

The CGHS department of Meerut provides comprehensive health care facilities for central government employees and pensioners and their dependents residing in the city.

### Demand for High Court bench in Meerut

Main article: [Allahabad High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahabad_High_Court)

Almost 54% of all cases reaching the [Allahabad High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahabad_High_Court) of Uttar Pradesh originate from the 22 districts of western Uttar Pradesh, but western Uttar Pradesh does not have a [High Court bench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_Bench). People must travel 700km away to [Allahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahabad) for hearings. Six high courts ([Shimla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himachal_Pradesh_High_Court), [Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_High_Court), [Jaipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthan_High_Court), [Chandigarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_and_Haryana_High_Court), [Nainital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himachal_Pradesh_High_Court), [Jammu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Jammu_&_Kashmir_and_Ladakh)) from other states are closer than Allahabad from western Uttar Pradesh.[^cite_note-95] Western Uttar Pradesh has been advocating to have a high court bench in Meerut so that Western Uttar Pradesh can get access to judicial services. The bench in the western part of the state was first proposed by the government in 1955.

## Meerut Cantonment

Main articles: [Meerut Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Cantonment) and [7th Indian Cavalry Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Indian_Cavalry_Brigade)



Mall Road in [Meerut Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Cantonment).

[Meerut Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Cantonment) was established by the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) in 1803 after the [Battle of Laswari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Laswari). It is one of the [largest cantonments of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Cantonments_in_India) both in land area 3,568.06 hectares (35.68 km<sup>2</sup>) and population (93,684 (civil + military) people) as per the 2011 census.[^cite_note-96] The [Revolt of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857#Meerut) began in "[Kali Paltan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali_Paltan_Mandir)" in Meerut Cantonment and Indian soldiers stationed here actively participated in the rebellion.[^cite_note-CDPcityprofile2-11][^cite_note-cbh2-97] The cantonment surrounds the old city from 3 sides – from Pallavpuram to Sainik Vihar to [Ganga Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga_Nagar,_Meerut).[^cite_note-98] It is well connected with the rest of country by roads as well as by rail. The [Delhi](37756.md)-[Niti Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niti_Pass) Road ([State Highway No. 45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_highways_in_Uttar_Pradesh)) passes through Meerut Cantonment.[^cite_note-cbh2-97] Meerut cantonment was the divisional headquarters of the [7th (Meerut) Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_\(Meerut\)_Division) of the [British Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army) from 1829 to 1920.[^cite_note-99]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Uttar_Pradesh)

### Development



[Delhi–Meerut Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Meerut_Expressway)



Atop an under-construction [overpass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overpass).

Meerut is the 63rd-fastest-growing urban area in the world.[^cite_note-100] It is the fastest developing city in Uttar Pradesh, after [Noida](986046.md) and [Ghaziabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaziabad).[^cite_note-101] According to a June 2011 report by US financial services firm [Morgan Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Stanley) gave Meerut the 5th spot on the "vibrancy" index, ahead of [Delhi](37756.md) and [Mumbai](19189.md).[^cite_note-mss2-102] The city ranked second on both the financial penetration index, which measures things like the presence of ATMs and bank branches, and on the consumption index, indicating the city's transformation into an urban town. Notably, [western Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Uttar_Pradesh) accounts for 51.71% of state GDP.[^cite_note-103]

While the city ranked in the bottom 10 in job creation, the report suggests that overall there are plenty of signs of "potential for urbanization" including future employment opportunities.[^cite_note-104] The infrastructure segment of Meerut is currently going through a boom phase with many new projects like Expressways, Metro, Freight corridors coming up in and around the city.[^cite_note-credai2-105][^cite_note-toi22-106] The Upper Ganga Canal Expressway development has also been completed. On the [India City Competitiveness Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_City_Competitiveness_Index), the city ranked 46th in 2017.[^cite_note-107] It is India's largest refined sugar producer.[^cite_note-108]

Meerut is a prospective [Logistic Hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics) in western Uttar Pradesh due to projects like [Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Dedicated_Freight_Corridor) and expressway projects like - [Delhi Meerut Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Meerut_Expressway), [Ganga Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga_Expressway), [Delhi Meerut RRTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Meerut_Regional_Rapid_Transit_System).

### Industry



The cover of the book *Sangeet [Puranmal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puran_Mal) Ka* (lit. The Music of Puranmal) by Ramlal. The book was published in 1879 from the city.

Meerut is one of the important industrial towns of western Uttar Pradesh with several traditional and modern industries.[^cite_note-ch32-67] It is traditionally known for [handloom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loom) works and [scissors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scissors) industry ([Meerut scissors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_scissors)).[^cite_note-CDPeconomicbase2-109] Meerut was one of the first cities in northern India where publishing was set up during the 19th century. It was a major center of [commercial publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publishing) during the 1860s and 1870s.[^cite_note-110]

Meerut is a rich agricultural area, being in proximity of Delhi, it is ideal for industry. As of 2011, it is home to 520 micro, small, and medium-scale industries.[^cite_note-did2-111] As of August 2006, Meerut has about 23,471 industrial units, including 15,510 small-scale units and 7,922 cottage industries.[^cite_note-112] [Sanspareils Greenlands (SG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanspareils_Greenlands), [Sareen Sports (SS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sareen_Sports_Industries) and BDM one of India's biggest sports goods manufacturers are based out of Meerut.

Existing industries in the city include tyres, textiles, transformers, sugar, distillery, chemical, engineering, paper, publishing, and sports goods manufacturing.[^cite_note-CDPeconomicbase2-109][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-did2-111] Prospective industries include [IT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology) and [ITES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITES), Logistics.[^cite_note-114]

Meerut is one of two centres of sports goods manufacturing in India – the other being [Jalandhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalandhar).[^cite_note-115] There are numerous sports companies in the city especially for cricket namely [SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sareen_Sports_Industries), SF, [SG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanspareils_Greenlands), RM Sports,[^cite_note-116] BDM, GEM etc. Players like [MS Dhoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS_Dhoni), [Virender Sehwag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virender_Sehwag), [Yuvraj Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuvraj_Singh), [Kieron Pollard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kieron_Pollard), [Virat Kohli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virat_Kohli), [Kumar Sangakkara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumar_Sangakkara) and many others have used bats made in Meerut.[^cite_note-117] The 40,000-capacity [Kailash Prakash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kailash_Prakash) Stadium is located in Meerut.

[Uttar Pradesh State Industrial Development Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UP_State_Industrial_Development_Corporation) (UPSIDC) has two industrial estates in the city, namely [Partapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partapur,_Uttar_Pradesh) and Udyog Puram.[^cite_note-upsidc12-118][^cite_note-upsidc22-119]

## Transport

### Road



Delhi-Meerut expressway



Delhi–Meerut Expressway



Delhi–Meerut Expressway

Meerut is well-connected by road to major cities like Delhi, [Noida](986046.md), [Faridabad](1180794.md), [Ghaziabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaziabad,_India), [Haridwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haridwar), [Bulandshahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulandshahr), etc. A large number of people commute to Delhi, Noida, [Greater Noida](4942600.md), and Ghaziabad every day for work.[^cite_note-120]

Three national highways ([NH-58](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_58_\(India\)), [NH-119](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_119_\(India\)) & [NH-235](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_235_\(India\))) and two expressways pass through Meerut. The [Delhi–Meerut Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Meerut_Expressway) - a 90 km long [controlled-access expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled-access_highway) connects Meerut with Delhi via [Dasna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasna) in [Ghaziabad district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaziabad_district). [Prime Minister](24452.md) [Narendra Modi](444222.md) laid the [foundation stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornerstone) for the expressway on 31 December 2015, and the expressway was completed and opened for public on 1 April 2021, also an under construction [Ganga Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga_Expressway).[^cite_note-121]

There are two main [bus terminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_station), namely Bhainsali bus terminal and Sohrab Gate bus terminal from where [Uttar Pradesh State Road Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh_State_Road_Transport_Corporation) (UPSRTC) buses travel to cities all over the state and all nearby cities. A [JNNURM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JNNURM) scheme was put in place.[^cite_note-CDPintroduction2-122] [Low Floor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-floor_bus) city buses (under JNNURM),[^cite_note-123] normal city buses, [auto rickshaws](593765.md), and [rickshaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickshaw) are convenient public transport options to commute within the city.[^cite_note-124]

Many new transport infrastructure projects like the inner ring road, outer ring road and construction of new flyovers are proposed and being made as well.[^cite_note-125] The under-construction [Ganga Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga_Expressway) will start from Meerut till [Allahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allahabad), and in the future, it will be extended from Meerut to [Haridwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haridwar). Other expressways that will pass nearby Meerut are the [Upper Ganga Canal Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Ganga_Canal_Expressway), [Delhi–Saharanpur–Dehradun Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Saharanpur%E2%80%93Dehradun_Expressway) and Gorakhpur–Shamli Expressway.

#### Expressways

Main article: [Delhi–Meerut Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Meerut_Expressway)

Since Meerut has a very favourable location for industries and is suitable for being a logistics hub, it has many expressways like [Delhi Meerut Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Meerut_Expressway), [Ganga Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga_Expressway). It also is nearby (within 30 miles) of other expressways like [Delhi Mumbai Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Mumbai_Expressway), [Yamuna Expressway](19787093.md), [KMP Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Peripheral_Expressway), and [Eastern Peripheral Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Peripheral_Expressway).

### Railways

Main articles: [Meerut City Junction railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_City_Junction_railway_station), [Meerut Cantt railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Cantt_railway_station), and [Delhi–Meerut–Saharanpur line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Meerut%E2%80%93Saharanpur_line)

Meerut lies on the [Delhi–Meerut–Saharanpur line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Meerut%E2%80%93Saharanpur_line),[^cite_note-126] and has four railway stations: [Meerut City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_City_railway_station), [Meerut Cantt.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Cantt_railway_station), [Partapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partapur,_Uttar_Pradesh) and Pabli Khas. Meerut City railway station is the busiest. The railway line between [Delhi](37756.md) and Meerut was constructed in 1864,[^cite_note-kinship-54] and the Meerut Cantt station, which serves as a secondary railway station, was founded in 1865.

About 20,000 passengers travel daily to Delhi and back.[^cite_note-127] Around 27 pairs of trains run between Meerut and Delhi, and four between Meerut and [Khurja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khurja_Junction_railway_station). Two trains are available for [Lucknow](392095.md) daily, namely [Nauchandi Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauchandi_Express) and [Rajya Rani Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajya_Rani_Express). A weekly train goes to [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai) and [Kochuveli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochuveli). Daily trains connect Meerut to [Mumbai](19189.md), [Ahmedabad](206337.md), [Jaipur](166537.md), [Rajkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajkot) and many cities in other states.

#### Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor

Main article: [Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Dedicated_Freight_Corridor)

[Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Dedicated_Freight_Corridor) or Eastern DFC is a [broad gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_ft_6_in_gauge_railway) [freight corridor in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedicated_freight_corridors_in_India). The railway will run between [Ludhiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludhiana) in [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India) and [Dankuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dankuni) (near [Kolkata](47905.md)) in [West Bengal](34040.md) via Meerut and [Khurja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khurja) in Uttar Pradesh.

Apart from this, [Delhi Meerut RRTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Meerut_Regional_Rapid_Transit_System) ([RapidX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RapidX)) will also be used for inter-city cargo movement. Vinay Kumar Singh, managing director of [NCRTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Capital_Region_Transport_Corporation) said, "During non-peak hours, the ridership would be low, so we can use the time to move cargo, including perishable goods."[^cite_note-128]

[Uttar Pradesh government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Uttar_Pradesh) has allocated land for building a large logistic hub in Modipuram region of Uttar Pradesh.[^cite_note-129]

#### Metro project

Main article: [Meerut Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Metro)

On 30 December 2014, the [Uttar Pradesh Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Uttar_Pradesh) approved the proposed metro rail project in Meerut, to boost the urban mass transport infrastructure in the city. The state government nominated [RITES Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RITES) and [Uttar Pradesh Metro Rail Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uttar_Pradesh_Metro_Rail_Corporation) (UPMRC) to prepare the respective [detailed project report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detailed_Project_Report) (DPR) and as a coordinator, respectively. The development authorities are nodal agencies for the DPR.[^cite_note-130]

The metro project got approval from the [divisional commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisional_Commissioner_\(India\)). It was decided in the meeting that the project would be along two corridors, by dividing the project into two phases – Phase I from Partapur to Pallavpuram, and Phase II from [Rajban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajban) Market to Gokalpur village. The main stations on the first corridor in the first phase will be [Partapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partapur,_Uttar_Pradesh), Panchwati Enclave, Rithani, Rithani West, [Shatabdi Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatabdi_Nagar_RRTS_station), Devlok, Madhavpuram, [Meerut Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_City_Junction_railway_station) Road, Lajpat Bazaar, Begampul, Gandhi Bagh, Lekha Nagar, Pallavpuram Dorli, Ansal City and Pallavpuram. While in the second phase, the corridor in the Partapur-Pallavpuram route will cover 20 km and will have a total number of 18 stations in between, the 10 km-long route from Rajban Market to Gokalpur village will have nine stations.[^cite_note-131]

#### Regional Rapid Transit System (RRTS)

Main article: [Delhi–Meerut Regional Rapid Transit System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Meerut_Regional_Rapid_Transit_System)



[Meerut Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Metro) [trainset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trainset)

The NCR Transport Plan 2021 proposed a rail-based mass transit system, called the Regional Rapid Transit System (RRTS) between Delhi to Meerut, with the [Shahdara](8233057.md)-[Ghaziabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaziabad) section scheduled for construction during 2001-11, and the Ghaziabad-Meerut section scheduled for 2011–21.[^cite_note-ncr212-132]

In September 2010, the RRTS was reported to be proposed between [Anand Vihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anand_Vihar) and Meerut with the project in its initial stages. The cost was projected to be around ₹1,000 crore (US\$100 million) with the expected time of the journey being 45 minutes.[^cite_note-min2-133] In November 2010, the train speed was proposed to be between 130 and 160 kmph, with stations at [Anand Vihar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anand_Vihar_RRTS_station), [Sahibabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahibabad_RRTS_station), [Mohan Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohan_Nagar_metro_station), [Ghaziabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaziabad_RRTS_station), [Guldhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guldhar_RRTS_station), [Duhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duhai_RRTS_station), [Moradnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murad_Nagar_RRTS_station), [Modinagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modinagar_North_RRTS_station), [Meerut South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_South_RRTS_station), [Shatabdi Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatabdi_Nagar_RRTS_station), Meerut Centre, Begumpul, Meerut North, Pallavpuram being the stops.[^cite_note-134]

On 14 December 2010, the NCR Planning Board, Meerut Development Authority (MDA), and [Meerut Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Municipal_Corporation) approved the project.[^cite_note-HT2-135] In August 2011, it was reported that the project tender had been awarded to [Delhi Integrated Multi-Modal Transit System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Integrated_Multi-Modal_Transit_System) (DIMTS). The proposed system was to have dedicated trains between Anand Vihar and Meerut, with no stops in between, and trains that will stop at stations will be constructed after a gap of 4–5 km. The reported stations were Anand Vihar, [Vaishali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishali_metro_station), Mohan Nagar, Meerut Road (Airtel Cut), Morta, Duhai, Muradnagar, Gang Nahar, Modinagar, [Mohiuddinpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohiuddinpur), Meerut Bypass Cut and Pallavpuram, with completion expected in 2017. The track between Anand Vihar to Dabur was proposed to be underground with the rest of the track overhead.[^cite_note-naredco2-136]

On 11 July 2013, the [Union Cabinet of India](553883.md) approved the formation of the [National Capital Region Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Capital_Region_Transport_Corporation) Limited (NCRTCL), with a seed capital of ₹100 crore (US\$10 million). The corporation will take up the construction of the 90 km-long Delhi-Ghaziabad-Meerut corridor on a priority basis (along with two other corridors) with planned completion in 2016.[^cite_note-137] It was reported that the Detailed Project Reports (DPRs) for the three corridors were under the process of finalisation.[^cite_note-138]

In December 2013, problems were reported in the proposed alignment of the Delhi-Meerut corridor.[^cite_note-139] In January 2014, it was reported that the proposed alignment had to be changed due to objections by [NHAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highways_Authority_of_India) and the feasibility report had to be prepared again. The new proposed alignment increased the length from 90 km to 106 km.[^cite_note-140]

In March 2018, the project's construction was started after the [foundation stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornerstone) was laid by [Prime Minister](24452.md), [Narendra Modi](444222.md).[^cite_note-141] As of 2023, the alignment is 82 km (51 mi) long, and the 17 km first stretch, as the priority corridor, from [Sahibabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahibabad) to Duhai is almost completed, and will be opened by March 2023, while the next stretch till [Meerut South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_South_RRTS_station) will be completed by the first quarter of 2024. The entire corridor will be completed by March 2025.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] As of February 2026, the Meerut Metro, integrated with the Delhi–Meerut Regional Rapid Transit System (RRTS), is operational.[^cite_note-145] The system is part of India's first regional rapid transit network, also known as Namo Bharat.

### Air

Main article: [Dr. Bhimrao Ambedkar Airstrip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Bhimrao_Ambedkar_Airstrip)

The nearest airport is [Hindon Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindon_Airport) at Ghaziabad, located 64 km (40 mi) away, while the major airport, [Indira Gandhi International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi_International_Airport) at Delhi is about 100 km (62 mi) away.

The [Dr. Bhim Rao Ambedkar Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_Airport) is located at [Partapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partapur,_Uttar_Pradesh). It was proposed by the state government that the airstrip be converted to an international airport to reduce pressure on Delhi Airport.[^cite_note-air2-146] However, plans to expand the Domestic airport were called off after protests against land acquisition started in other parts of the state.[^cite_note-147] Following an accident in May 2012, the city administration barred private flights from using the airstrip.[^cite_note-148]

The city has long demanded an airport. However, the government has stalled the project multiple times due to land acquisition reasons and project costs. Even [Ajit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajit_Singh_\(politician,_born_1939\)), the minister of civil aviation announced the project, but the project was not completed. BJP MP [Rajendra Agarwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_Agrawal) met Civil Aviation Minister [Jyotiraditya Scindia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyotiraditya_Scindia) ministerwho promised that the [Ministry of Civil Aviation](10257026.md) will begin running the airport as soon as the Uttar Pradesh government satisfied the demand for land. As of October 2023, the project is awaiting approval for land acquisition from Uttar Pradesh Government.[^cite_note-149]

As of 2017, Meerut ranks 328 (based on population), 189 (based on population density), 648 (based on built-up area) among the world's urban areas.[^cite_note-156]

According to the 2001 census, the city ranked second in terms of population in NCR[^cite_note-157] and 25th in India.[^cite_note-158]

## Culture



[Mustafa Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Castle) was built in 1900.

Many [traditional Indian festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festivals_in_India), including [Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holi), [Dussehra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami), [Diwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diwali), [Eid](154944.md) are celebrated in Meerut. Notably, the [Nauchandi Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauchandi_Mela) (Nauchandi Fair), which began in 1672, is held annually two weeks after Holi.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-uptour22-176] The fair typically runs for approximately 15 days and attracts a large number of visitors. Events at the fair include poetry recitations in languages such as [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi_poetry), [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu_poetry) and [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_poetry).[^cite_note-177]

Meerut’s cultural identity is closely linked to its traditional crafts, including the production of scissors and brass musical instruments. The granting of GI tags to Meerut scissors and the Meerut bugle highlights the city’s long-standing artisanal heritage and its contribution to India’s industrial and ceremonial traditions. Meerut’s bugle-making heritage dates back to the late 19th century, when the instrument became integral to battlefield communication and regimental drills under British rule, and later evolved into a symbol of discipline and ceremonial music across the Indian Army, paramilitary units, and police forces. The GI tag is expected to protect the authentic Meerut bugle from imitation, enhance market value, and support the livelihoods of local artisans by promoting their craft both nationally and internationally.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179]

Meerut serves as the headquarters of the [Roman Catholic](606848.md) [Diocese of Meerut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Meerut), which encompasses the districts of Meerut, [Muzaffarnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarnagar_district), [Saharanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharanpur_district), [Dehradun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehradun_district), [Haridwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haridwar_district), [Moradabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moradabad_district), [Rampur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rampur_district), [Jyotiba Phule Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amroha_district), [Ghaziabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaziabad_district), [Baghpat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagpat_district) and the [Dhampur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhampur) [tehsil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) of [Bijnor district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijnor_district).[^cite_note-180]

The cuisine of Meerut reflects influences from [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_cuisine), [Awadhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awadhi_cuisine), and [Mughlai traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughlai_cuisine). Local dishes commonly associated with the city include much of the [vegetarian dishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetarian_cuisine) and [sweets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_sweets_and_desserts) as most of the population is vegetarian.[^cite_note-auto-33]

### Nauchandi Mela

Main article: [Nauchandi Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauchandi_Mela)



One of the entrance gates of [Nauchandi Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauchandi_Mela) ground at Meerut.

The Nauchandi Mela is an annual fair held at Nauchandi Ground in Meerut.[^cite_note-\:23-181][^cite_note-\:32-182] The fair stretches for about a month and is organised by the Municipal Corporation of Meerut. It generally starts from the second Sunday after [Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holi).[^cite_note-\:23-181] The main exhibits are the artistic and religious rituals followed in rustic Uttar Pradesh. The fair witnesses more than 50,000 visitors every year. The [Indian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Railways)' [Nauchandi Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauchandi_Express) train is named after this fair.

The fair has a prominent history dating back several hundreds of years. It started in the year 1672 AD as a one-day cattle trading fair and has been held every year after, excluding 1858, the year after [1857 revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857), which started from Meerut.[^cite_note-183]

Since then, cattle trading has been replaced by many other activities. The fair features shops for [Lucknow](392095.md)'s [chikan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chikan_\(embroidery\)) work, [Moradabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moradabad)'s [brassware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brassware), [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi)'s carpets, rugs and silk [sarees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banarasi_sari), [Agra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra)'s footwear, [Meerut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur)'s leather items, etc. Meerut's own products like sports goods, scissors, and confections like [gajaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gajak) and [nan-khatai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nankhatai) are also sold.[^cite_note-\:32-182] The fair features [rides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amusement_ride), [wheels](175541.md), circus performances and recreational activities remain a big attraction of the fair.[^cite_note-184]

### Film and television

Main article: [Hindi cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi_cinema)

Meerut is home to a film industry, which has a following in western Uttar Pradesh and [Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haryanvi_cinema). The films are usually [folk tales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore_of_India), comedies, or localised versions of [Bollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi_cinema) hits.[^cite_note-185] The films which have been shot here include *[Sonu Ke Titu Ki Sweety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonu_Ke_Titu_Ki_Sweety)*, [*Zero*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_\(2018_film\)), and *[Rajma Chawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajma_Chawal)*.

Notable people from Meerut in the film and television industry include [Bharat Bhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharat_Bhushan_Bhalla), [Aziz Mian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aziz_Mian), [Mandakini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandakini_\(actress\)),[^cite_note-186] [Achint Kaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achint_Kaur),[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-stars-188] [Kailash Kher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kailash_Kher),[^cite_note-189] [Chitrangada Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitrangada_Singh),[^cite_note-stars-188][^cite_note-midday-190] [Vishal Bhardwaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishal_Bhardwaj),[^cite_note-stars-188] [Deepti Bhatnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepti_Bhatnagar)[^cite_note-stars-188][^cite_note-midday-190][^cite_note-191] and [Pravesh Rana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pravesh_Rana).[^cite_note-192]

The city is home to [Chaudhary Charan Singh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaudhary_Charan_Singh_University) (formerly Meerut University), [Swami Vivekanand Subharti University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swami_Vivekanand_Subharti_University) and [IIMT University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIMT_University) (the oldest engineering institute in Meerut district, established in 1997). The city has one government-run engineering college, [Sir Chhotu Ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhotu_Ram) Institute of Engineering and Technology, a constituent college of [Chaudhary Charan Singh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaudhary_Charan_Singh_University). There are schools affiliated with recognised boards such as [ICSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Certificate_of_Secondary_Education), [CBSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Board_of_Secondary_Education), [IB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Baccalaureate), and the state board. St. Mary's Academy, Meerut (a [Patrician Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrician_Brothers) school) is a nationally top-ranked boys' school.&#x20;

[Chaudhary Charan Singh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaudhary_Charan_Singh_University) (CCSU) is a public and state university that has many degree colleges affiliated with it. They fall into two divisions: [Saharanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharanpur) and Meerut with nine districts including [Saharanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharanpur), Meerut, [Muzaffarnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muzaffarnagar), [Ghaziabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaziabad_district,_India), [Shamli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamli), [Gautam Budh Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautam_Budh_Nagar), [Bagpat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagpat), [Hapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hapur) and [Bulandshahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulandshahr) administered by a [vice-chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-chancellor) and a registrar ([PCS officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Civil_Service_\(Uttar_Pradesh\))). The city has three medical colleges: [Lala Lajpat Rai Memorial Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lala_Lajpat_Rai_Memorial_Medical_College), [Subharti Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subharti_Medical_College) and [Mulayam Singh Yadav Medical College & Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulayam_Singh_Yadav_Medical_College_&_Hospital).

Tourist destinations in and around Meerut include the following:

- [Digamber Jain Bada Mandir Hastinapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digamber_Jain_Bada_Mandir_Hastinapur) – Located on the banks of the old ravine of the [Ganges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganges), it is considered one of the holiest places on earth by Jains. It is believed to be the birthplace of three Jain Tirthankaras. There are many ancient [Jain temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_temple) in [Hastinapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastinapur) with the Digamber Jain Mandir, [Jambudweep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jambudweep), [Kailash Parvat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kailash) Rachna, and [Shwetambar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Avet%C4%81mbara) Jain Temple being the most well known. Apart from Jain temples, the Pandeshwar temple, the Historical Gurdwara, and the [Hastinapur Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastinapur_Sanctuary) are worth being seen.

  [^cite_note-195]

- Government Freedom Struggle Museum and Shaheed Smarak ([Martyr's Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_martyrs'_monuments_and_memorials)) - Government Freedom Struggle Museum, Meerut was established in 1997. It is located in the Shaheed Smarak compound on Delhi Road, about 6 km north-east of the [city railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerut_City_Junction_railway_station) and at a distance of about 200 meters from the Delhi Bus Station. Visitors can stay in various guesthouses, private lodges, and hotels. The museum's main aim is the collection, preservation, documentation, and exhibition of cultural property and to make it available for educational activities as well as for the creation of awareness of Meerut's history. Some postal stamps, pictures, postcards, memorial coins related to the events of 1857, and later coins are also in the collection of the museum. The museum is in the developing stages and efforts are being made to collect more specimens. The museum organises educational programs such as lectures, seminars, and competitions related to history, culture, philosophy, the freedom struggle, and religion. It also aims to coordinate with other cultural and educational organisations for disseminating Indian culture, particularly the events related to the long-drawn freedom struggle of India.

  [^cite_note-196]

- [Shahi Jama Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahi_Jama_Masjid) – The Jama Masjid was built by Hasan Mahdi, [Sultan Mahmud Ghaznavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni)'s [Wazir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizier) in 1019 AD (older than the [Qutb Minar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutb_Minar)).

  [^cite_note-off-26]

  [^cite_note-imp2652-197]

  &#x20;It is considered the first [masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masjid) in [North India](1552939.md).

  [^cite_note-198]

  &#x20;Although it was restored by [Humayun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humayun),

  [^cite_note-off-26]

  [^cite_note-imp2652-197]

  &#x20;it is one of the oldest mosques in India. Some believe that the first North Indian Mosque is [Quwwat/Qubbat ul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutb_Minar_complex) in Delhi and then [Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhai_Din_Ka_Jhonpra) in [Ajmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajmer).

- [St. John's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._John's_Church,_Meerut) – This church was established by Chaplain the Reverend Henry Fisher on behalf of the [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) in 1819 in the cantonment area and was completed in 1822.

  [^cite_note-off-26]

  [^cite_note-uptour22-176]

  &#x20;It is considered one of the oldest churches in North India. The Church was dedicated to the people by Bishop Wilson. It has a [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) of 10,000 people.

  [^cite_note-off-26]

  &#x20;During the [War of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857), this church was the scene of heavy fighting between Indian and British forces.

  [^cite_note-199]

- [Augarnath Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kali_Paltan_Mandir) – This temple (also known as Kalipaltan Mandir locally) is located at the site where the soldiers of the War of 1857 planned their operations. The temple also houses a memorial built to honour the martyrs of the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857). The old temple has been replaced by a modern version.

  [^cite_note-200]

  &#x20;Augharnath Temple is also one of [Shiva's oldest temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Shiva_temples_in_India).

Other places of interest include Mansa Devi Temple, Baleni, [Basilica of Our Lady of Graces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady_of_Graces), [Sardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardhana) and the Chandi Devi Temple which was built by [Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Holkar) queen [Devi Ahiliyabai Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahilyabai_Holkar).[^cite_note-off-26][^cite_note-mdat-28]
