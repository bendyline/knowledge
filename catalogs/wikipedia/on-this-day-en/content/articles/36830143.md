Accession of [Ukraine](31750.md) to the [European Union](9317.md) (EU) is on the agenda for [future enlargement of the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_enlargement_of_the_EU).

On 28 February 2022, four days after [it was invaded by Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Ukraine applied for membership of the European Union (EU). Ukrainian president [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) requested immediate admission under a "new special procedure",[^cite_note-Treisman-4] and the presidents of eight EU states called for an accelerated accession process.[^cite_note-5] [European Commission](9974.md) [president](24468.md) [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md) stated that she supports Ukrainian accession, but that the process would take time.[^cite_note-Treisman-4] On 10 March 2022, the [Council of the European Union](5866.md) asked the commission for its opinion on the application.[^cite_note-\:0-6] On 8 April 2022, von der Leyen presented Zelenskyy with a legislative questionnaire,[^cite_note-DW-7] which Ukraine responded to on 9 May.[^cite_note-\:9-8]

On 17 June 2022, the [European Commission](9974.md) recommended that the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) grant Ukraine [candidate status for accession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_enlargement_of_the_European_Union) to the EU.[^cite_note-\:2-9][^cite_note-\:3-10] On 23 June 2022, the [European Parliament](9581.md) adopted a resolution calling for the immediate granting of candidate status for EU membership to Ukraine.[^cite_note-\:6-11][^cite_note-\:7-12] On the same day, the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) granted Ukraine the status of a candidate for accession to the EU.[^cite_note-\:8-13] It is one of nine current EU candidate countries, together with [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Albania_to_the_European_Union), [Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_to_the_European_Union), [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Georgia_to_the_European_Union), [Moldova](54111451.md), [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Montenegro_to_the_European_Union), [North Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_North_Macedonia_to_the_European_Union), [Serbia](10038148.md), and [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Turkey_to_the_European_Union).

On 14 December 2023, the European Council decided to open accession negotiations with Ukraine.[^cite_note-eurointegration.com.ua-14] Accession negotiations officially opened on 25 June 2024, at the same time as those with [Moldova](54111451.md).[^cite_note-negopen-15][^cite_note-16] This signified another milestone in this country's ongoing efforts to align more closely with [Western nations](21208200.md) and diminish Russia's influence.[^cite_note-apnews.com-17] If Ukraine becomes a member of the EU, it will be another [former Soviet country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states) after [Estonia](28222445.md), [Latvia](17514.md) and [Lithuania](17675.md), which all joined the EU in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_enlargement_of_the_European_Union).

In June 2025, Hungary blocked the opening of EU accession talks. Ex-Hungarian prime minister [Viktor Orbán](750582.md) says it would mean "integrating war" into the European Union.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] German chancellor [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md) cast doubt on the possibility of Ukraine joining the European Union by 2034.[^cite_note-20] Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) announced that Russia did not oppose Ukraine's membership in the EU, but opposed its membership in [NATO](21133.md).[^cite_note-21]

### 2002–2005

On 12 January 2002, the [European Parliament](9581.md) noted that Ukraine may enter the EU in the future.[^cite_note-41] In 2002, EU Enlargement Commissioner Guenther Verheugen said that "the European perspective for Ukraine does not necessarily mean membership in the next 10-20 years, although it is possible." To join the European Union, the applicant state must meet the political and economic conditions commonly known as the [Copenhagen Criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Criteria) (adopted at the Copenhagen Summit in 1993), namely a democratic government that recognizes the rule of law and relevant freedoms and institutions. According to the Maastricht Treaty, each current Member State, as well as the European Parliament, must agree on any enlargement.

Obtaining the status of a full member of the EU as a strategic goal of Ukraine was first declared by president of Ukraine Viktor Yushchenko immediately after his election in early 2005.[^cite_note-42] On 13 January 2005, the European Parliament almost unanimously (467 in favor, 19 against) adopted a resolution on the European Parliament's intentions to converge with Ukraine on membership. The European Commission notes that, although a certain preparatory period has yet to pass, the admission of new members is not ruled out, to which President Yushchenko responded with his intention to apply for membership "in the near future."

Several influential EU leaders at the time expressed support for improving ties with Ukraine. In particular, Polish Foreign Minister Adam Rotfeld stated on 21 March 2005 that Poland would support Ukraine's European integration aspirations under any circumstances. In particular, he said: "At this stage, we should focus on concrete steps of cooperation instead of empty talks about pan-European cooperation." Three days later, a survey in the six largest EU countries showed the commitment of EU citizens to accept Ukraine as a full member in the future.

In October 2005, European Commission president Jose Manuel Barroso said that "Ukraine's future is in the EU." However, on 9 October 2005, the European Commission, in a new version of the Development Strategy Paper, stated that the implementation of enlargement plans (Croatia and the former Yugoslav Republics) could block the accession of Ukraine, Belarus and Moldova. Enlargement Commissioner Olli Rehn said the EU should avoid "too much enlargement", stressing that the current enlargement plan looks complete.[^cite_note-43]

Although Ukrainian officials and political scientists mentioned several specific dates for possible membership, only Ukraine's European Neighborhood Policy has been officially proposed to Ukraine by the EU so far. The presidential administration has been critical of the proposed status of neighborhood relations.

### 2007–2014

In March 2007, Ukraine was offered a Free Trade Agreement with the EU. Although this proposal provoked a much stronger reaction from the Ukrainian state, it did not contain specific plans for Ukraine's accession to the EU in the near future. Some Western European politicians have spoken of the temporary "fatigue of enlargement" of European institutions. Ukrainian observers identify the so-called "resistance group" of Ukraine's accession to the EU. In particular, when concluding the text of the Enhanced Agreement between Ukraine and the EU in March 2007, references to the prospect of membership were excluded from it. "Any mention of the prospect of Ukraine's accession to the European Union has been excluded from the draft enhanced Ukraine-EU agreement due to France's position." wrote the influential German newspaper Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung. Italy's position depends on the domestic political situation of this country. Thus, during the election campaign in this country, the government of [Silvio Berlusconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Berlusconi) gave diplomatic signals that he was ready to support Ukraine's European integration aspirations. His political opponent [Romano Prodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romano_Prodi), on the other hand, said that "Ukraine's prospects for joining the EU are the same as in New Zealand."

According to the Eastern Partnership policy, Ukraine can become a member of the European Union.[^cite_note-euobserver.com-44] On 27 February 2014, the European Parliament passed a resolution that recognized Ukraine's right to "apply to become a Member of the Union, provided that it adheres to the principles of democracy, respects fundamental freedoms and human and minority rights, and ensures the rule of law".[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] The European Parliament notes that in accordance with Article 49 of the Treaty with the EU, Georgia, Moldova and Ukraine, like any other European country, have a European perspective and can apply for EU membership in accordance with the principles of democracy, - said in a resolution of the European Parliament in Brussels, adopted at the last session before the elections to the European Parliament, which took place on 23–25 May 2014.[^cite_note-47] On 27 June 2014, President Jose Manuel Barroso of the European Commission stated that the Association Agreement is the beginning of Ukraine's accession to the EU.[^cite_note-48] On the same day, EU Enlargement Commissioner Stefan Fuele stated that he believed in Ukraine's future membership in the EU.[^cite_note-49]

### 2014–2025

In March 2016, [President of the European Commission](24468.md) [Jean-Claude Juncker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Juncker) stated that it would take at least 20–25 years for Ukraine to join the EU and NATO.[^cite_note-50] In June 2018, President of Ukraine [Petro Poroshenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petro_Poroshenko) said he expects [Ukraine](31750.md) will join the [European Union](9317.md) and the [North Atlantic Treaty Organisation](21133.md) by 2030.

On 21 February 2019, the [Constitution of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ukraine) was amended to enshrine the norms on the strategic course of Ukraine for membership in the European Union and [NATO](21133.md) in the preamble of the Basic Law, three articles and transitional provisions.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

At the X session of the Ukraine–Poland–Lithuania Interparliamentary Assembly, which ended on 8 June 2019 in Kyiv, the parties signed a final document containing an agreement on the strategy of 2025 and 2027 as a period for Ukraine's possible accession to the EU. In 2027, when Lithuania will hold the EU presidency for the second time, the issue of Ukraine will be the main issue on the agenda. If this opportunity is not used, the next "window" will open in 2039, when Poland will preside over the EU and Lithuania will preside only in 2041.[^cite_note-53]

On 23 July 2020, Poland, Lithuania and Ukraine created a tripartite platform for political, economic, cultural and social cooperation – the [Lublin Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lublin_Triangle), which aims to support Ukraine's integration into the EU and NATO.

&#x20;In February 2021, the leader of the presiding [Christian Democratic Union of Germany](60584.md), [Armin Laschet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armin_Laschet), who was considered a likely successor to Angela Merkel as Chancellor of Germany, supported the idea of EU enlargement and giving Ukraine a European perspective:[^cite_note-54]

> *"The question of Ukraine's accession to the EU does not arise at the moment, but it will inevitably arise in the future. We must support Ukraine on its difficult path and at the same time open a European perspective."*

Quite a few experts believe that in times of deteriorating relations between Russia and the EU,[^cite_note-55] Ukraine has a window of opportunity to join the European Union. [Pavlo Klimkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlo_Klimkin) noted that Ukraine still does not meet any criteria for joining the European Union, as it does not have an established democracy, rule of law and a full-fledged market economy. According to him, the first chance was lost in early 2005, when Yushchenko was persuaded not to apply, and in 2014, it was much more difficult to do so.[^cite_note-56]

On 11 February 2021, the European Parliament published a report on Ukraine's success in implementing the Association Agreement with the European Union. The document highlights both the main successes of Ukraine on this path, as well as failures or moments that hinder the reform process in the country. In general, the European Union is not yet ready to officially talk about the prospects of Ukraine's accession to the ranks of member states, but Ukraine's European perspective is recognized.[^cite_note-57] In 2021, Ukraine was preparing to formally apply for EU membership in 2024, in order to join the European Union in the 2030s.[^cite_note-\:1-58]

On 13 September 2023, during her [State of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_European_Union) address, [President of the European Commission](24468.md), [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md), stated that the future of Ukraine was "in our Union".[^cite_note-59]

On 14 December 2023, the European Council decided to open accession negotiations with Ukraine.[^cite_note-60] [Hungary](13275.md) long opposed talks starting accession negotiations, did not [veto](74661.md) the move. Prime Minister [Viktor Orban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Orban) left the room momentarily in what officials described as a pre-agreed and constructive manner, while the other 26 leaders went ahead with the vote.

On 21 June 2024, the European Union agreed to start membership negotiations with Ukraine.[^cite_note-negopen-15]

In 2025, [deputy prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Vice_Prime_Minister_of_Ukraine) for [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU-Ukraine_relations) and [Euro-Atlantic integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine%E2%80%93NATO_relations) [Olha Stefanishyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olha_Stefanishyna) announced a roadmap to [Ukraine](31750.md)'s accession.[^cite_note-61]

In July 2025, Ukrainian lawmakers voted to scuttle the independence of anti-corruption agencies in the country.[^cite_note-62] The European Commission warned Ukraine that this move will have consequences for EU accession.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] On 31 July, President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) restored the independence of Ukraine's two main anti-corruption agencies hours after lawmakers approved the bill 331 to 0, saying the legislation "guarantees the absence of any kind of outside influence (or) interference".[^cite_note-65]

## Application



Signing of the application for accession to the EU, 28 February 2022

Following the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in 2022, there were additional calls to start a formal accession process: Ukraine reiterated its desire to become a member of the union,[^cite_note-66] and European Commission president von der Leyen stated that Ukraine belongs in the European Union.[^cite_note-67] Slovak prime minister [Eduard Heger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Heger) expressed support for an accelerated accession process.[^cite_note-68]

On 26 February 2022, [Polish president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_president) [Andrzej Duda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Duda) called for Ukraine's accelerated accession to the EU. On 27 February, [Slovenian prime minister](27646969.md) [Janez Janša](1038986.md), together with [Polish prime minister](40543.md) [Mateusz Morawiecki](48505487.md), proposed a plan for Ukraine's rapid integration into the EU by 2030 in a letter to [European Council president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_European_Council) [Charles Michel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Michel). [Slovak prime minister](692138.md) [Eduard Heger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Heger) also proposed to the EU to create a new special procedure for Ukrainian accession, in order to help Ukraine get back on its feet and recover from the war in the future.

On 28 February, Ukraine officially submitted a letter of application for membership.[^cite_note-Treisman-4] Due to the ongoing crisis, President Zelenskyy requested immediate admission to the European Union under a special procedure.[^cite_note-Treisman-4][^cite_note-69] On the same day, eight EU states signed a letter supporting an accelerated accession process for Ukraine,[^cite_note-Presidents-70] and on 1 March, Hungarian foreign minister [Péter Szijjártó](26166814.md) stated his country would also support an accelerated process.[^cite_note-HU-71] On 1 March, the European Parliament, following a debate in which the president of Ukraine addressed and received applause, recommended that Ukraine be made an official candidate for EU membership.[^cite_note-72] The European Parliament voted to advance Ukraine's membership with 637 in favour, 13 against, and 26 abstained.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

On 1 March, the presidents of eight EU member states (Bulgaria, the Czech Republic, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Slovakia and Slovenia) signed an open letter calling on Ukraine to be given the prospect of EU membership and start the process of negotiations immediately.[^cite_note-75] On the same day, [Hungarian foreign minister](25144043.md) Péter Szijjártó called for Ukraine's accelerated accession to the European Union.

On 2 March, Spanish foreign minister [José Manuel Albares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Albares) stated that "belonging to the EU is not a capricious process or one that can be done by a mere political decision", reminding that the candidate country "must meet certain social, political and economic standards".[^cite_note-76]

On 7 March, the EU said it will formally assess Ukraine's application[^cite_note-77] and on 10 March, the [Council of the European Union](5866.md) asked the commission for its opinion on the application.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

On 9 March, the Polish Senate adopted a resolution calling on the countries of the European Union to support the accelerated process of Ukraine's accession to the EU with 93 votes in favor. "Ukrainian society has undoubtedly proved that it is ready to be part of a united Europe and ready to pay with blood for devotion to European values. Ukrainian soldiers, defending the borders of their country, protect the whole of Europe," the document reads.



Presentation of the EU membership questionnaire on 8 April 2022, by the [President of the European Commission](24468.md) [von der Leyen](2991932.md) and the [President of Ukraine](20688479.md) [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md).

On 8 April, Commission president von der Leyen, after visiting [Bucha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucha,_Kyiv_Oblast) in the aftermath of [a massacre there](70455343.md), visited Kyiv and met with President Zelenskyy. Von der Leyen presented Zelenskyy with the legislative questionnaire to begin Ukraine's application and offered to fast-track the process.[^cite_note-DW-7] Borrell announced that the EU delegation to Ukraine, headed by [Matti Maasikas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matti_Maasikas), will return to Kyiv after it was evacuated at the outbreak of war.[^cite_note-78] On 17 April, Ukraine responded to the first part of the legislative questionnaire,[^cite_note-EP-79] while it responded to the second and final part on 9 May.[^cite_note-\:9-8]

### Candidacy

According to [Olha Stefanishyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olha_Stefanishyna), the Ukrainian deputy prime minister for European and Euro-Atlantic integration, three EU countries opposed granting candidate status to Ukraine; according to Bloomberg, the Netherlands and Denmark were two of them. Germany offered to give Ukraine a conditional status, under promises to fulfill certain requirements.[^cite_note-80]

On 29 May, in order to support Ukraine's accession to the EU, in particular to obtain the status of a candidate for membership in the European Union,[^cite_note-81] the [Government of Ukraine](1372585.md) launched a communication campaign "Embrace Ukraine. Strengthen the Union" set for 25–26 June.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

After a meeting with Volodymyr Zelenskyy on 16 June in Kyiv, the leaders of [Germany](11867.md), [Italy](14532.md), [Romania](25445.md) and [France](5843419.md) called for immediate candidate status for EU membership for Ukraine.[^cite_note-84] [Federal Chancellor of Germany](20890626.md) [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz) noted that Ukraine's further movement into the EU, if all countries agree to grant it candidate status, depends on its own efforts.[^cite_note-85] Separately, [French president](24899.md) [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) explained that the visit to Ukraine of the leaders of the three largest EU economies - Germany, Italy and France, as well as the [president of Romania](273659.md) was aimed at creating unanimity in the European Union regarding Ukraine's first step towards accession, which is the granting of candidate status: it was accepted the decision to grant Ukraine candidate status without additional conditions, but then to impose conditions on reforms at all subsequent stages.[^cite_note-86]

On 17 June, [Foreign Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Denmark\)) [Jeppe Kofod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeppe_Kofod) said that the Danish government is ready to support granting Ukraine candidate status for EU accession if the European Commission provides a positive recommendation.[^cite_note-87] The Netherlands on the same day appealed to the European Commission with a call to carefully prescribe the conditions for Ukraine's further approach to EU membership. In particular, the European Commission is called upon to prescribe in its conclusion what reforms Ukraine will have to implement in the future. The Netherlands provided a detailed description of what they consider to be the essential criteria.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

Also on 17 June, the [European Commission](9974.md) recommended that the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) grant Ukraine the perspective to become a member of the European Union and candidate status for accession.[^cite_note-\:2-9][^cite_note-\:3-10][^cite_note-\:4-36] Simultaneously with the recommendation to approve the candidate status, the Commission listed seven required reforms to be implemented by Ukraine:[^cite_note-90]

1. Reform of the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Ukraine);

2. Continuation of judicial reform;

3. Anti-corruption, including the appointment of the head of the [SAPO](57303521.md);

4. Anti-money laundering;

5. Implementation of the anti-oligarchic law, including recommendations of the [Venice Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice_Commission);

6. Harmonization of audio-visual media legislation with that of the European Union;

7. Change in legislation on [national minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minorities_in_Ukraine).

On 23 June, the [European Parliament](9581.md) adopted a resolution calling for the immediate granting of candidate status for membership of the European Union to Ukraine and [Moldova](19260.md), as well as to support the European perspective for [Georgia](48768.md).[^cite_note-\:6-11][^cite_note-\:7-12] On the same day, the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) granted Ukraine the status of a candidate for accession to the European Union,[^cite_note-\:8-13] contingent on meeting the seven required reforms.[^cite_note-\:5-91]

## Negotiations

On 2 February 2023, the [European Commission](9974.md) published an analytical report on Ukraine's alignment with the EU acquis, "complet\[ing] the Commission's Opinion on Ukraine's application for membership of the EU adopted on 17 June 2022".[^cite_note-2022Report-101]

On 8 November 2023, the [European Commission](9974.md) recommended opening accession negotiations with Ukraine.[^cite_note-O'Carroll-37] On 14 December 2023, the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) decided to open accession negotiations with Ukraine.[^cite_note-eurointegration.com.ua-14] On 21 June 2024, the European Union agreed to start membership negotiations with Ukraine.[^cite_note-negopen-15][^cite_note-apnews.com-17] Accession negotiations began on 25 June 2024, at the same time as those with [Moldova](54111451.md).[^cite_note-negopen-15][^cite_note-June2024UkrMol-102]

On 13 November 2024, the Ukrainian Government announced the completion of the screening of the first cluster of negotiation chapters.[^cite_note-\:12-103]

On 16 January 2025, the [European Commission](9974.md) submitted its first screening report to the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council).[^cite_note-104] [European Pravda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Pravda) also reported that the screening process will last until autumn 2025, according to a [European Commission](9974.md) spokesperson.

On 13 March 2025, the Ukrainian Government announced the completion of the screening of the second cluster of negotiation chapters.[^cite_note-105]

On 30 September 2025, the European Commission announced that Ukraine completed bilateral screening in all chapters, however, further efforts to formally proceed with negotiations were being blocked by Hungary over concerns about minority language rights of ethnic Hungarians in Ukraine.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] Despite this veto, Ukraine received a Draft Common Position (DCP) on clusters 1, 2 and 6 during the informal meeting of EU Ministers for European Affairs with Ukraine on 11 December 2025,[^cite_note-108] allowing technical work on negotiations to begin. This marked the first time in EU history that an official negotiations position was revealed to a candidate country prior to the official opening of its respective cluster.&#x20;

At the [COREPER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COREPER) meeting on 12 June 2026 the 27 EU member ambassadors gave a positive assessement on the opening of the Fundamentals cluster with both Ukraine and Moldova. Formal opening of the 5 chapters inside the Fundamentals cluster took place during the Second Accession Conference with Ukraine on 15 June 2026.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-Fundamentals_Open-110]

During the Third Accession Conference with Ukraine on 14 July 2026, Cluster 6 (External Relations) was formally opened along with the two chapters inside of it: Chapter 30 (External Relations) and Chapter 31 (Foreign, Security & Defence Policy).[^cite_note-111]

## Public opinion

### In Ukraine

Main article: [Ukraine–European Union relations § Popular support of Ukraine's integration with the European Union](5612265.md#Popular_support_of_Ukraine's_integration_with_the_European_Union)



The [flag of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Europe) flying on the [tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnytsia_water_tower) in [Vinnytsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnytsia)

#### 2010s

Traditionally, [Western Ukraine](3258511.md) is found to be generally more enthusiastic about EU membership than [Eastern Ukraine](13065940.md). In July 2012 and in May 2014, residents of [West Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ukraine) (74% in July 2012 and 81% in May 2014), [Central Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Ukraine) (59% and 64%) and [North Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Ukraine) (56% and 71%) were the biggest supporters for EU membership. A June 2013 poll, on behalf of *[Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle)*, found that 52% of [Eastern Ukraine](13065940.md) was in favor of joining the EU.[^cite_note-DWpoll613-150] But in a poll by [ComRes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ComRes) (for [CNN](62028.md)) in May 2014 only 19% of Eastern Ukraine considered Ukraine joining the European Union "Good". The May 2014 ComRes poll found out that in the three easternmost [oblasts of Ukraine](994124.md), [Kharkiv Oblast](494901.md), [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) and [Luhansk Oblast](494904.md), 37% favored an alliance with [Russia](25391.md), 14% backed an alliance with the European Union and 49% stated Ukraine would be better off if it did not ally with either.

Citizens aged between 20 and 39 appeared to be the strongest supporters of joining the EU in May 2010 and December 2011 (in December 2011 the opinion of the age group 18–29 did not vary from one region to another).[^cite_note-poll510-151][^cite_note-KDIFEUUKRDec11-152] In the May 2014 ComRes poll, people aged between 36 and 55 were the strongest supporters of joining the EU. Ukraine's EU ambassador, [Kostiantyn Yelisieiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostiantyn_Yelisieiev), stated in July 2011 that [business tycoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_oligarchs) and politicians from [Ukraine's Russian speaking east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Ukraine) were as much pro-EU as the [Ukrainian speaking west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ukraine) of the country: "If any politician today in Ukraine declared himself to be against European integration, he would be politically dead."[^cite_note-Yeliseyev-153]

#### 2020s

91% of Ukrainians support joining the European Union during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine),[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155] according to a poll conducted by the [Rating Sociological Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rating_\(sociological_group\)) on 30–31 March 2022,[^cite_note-156] up from 66.4% in February 2015.[^cite_note-157]

According to the 2025 annual survey of opinion in Ukraine, 82% of Ukrainians say their country has good relations with the European Union (21 points up since 2016), and 70% would [vote to join the EU in a referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Referendums_related_to_the_European_Union), with 9% opposed. The latest poll found that trust in the EU now stands at 76% – the highest since 2017 (+18 points). Half of those asked (49%) have a positive view of the European Union, compared to just 7% with a negative view. Over three-quarters (76%) believe EU membership would bring more advantages than disadvantages for Ukraine.[^cite_note-158]

### In the EU

#### 2020s

According to a survey conducted by Ifop commissioned by the Yalta European Strategy and the [Fondation Jean-Jaurès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fondation_Jean-Jaur%C3%A8s) from 3–7 March 2022, Ukraine's accession to the EU is supported by 92% of respondents in [Poland](22936.md), 71% in [Italy](14532.md), 68% in [Germany](11867.md), and 62% in [France](5843419.md).[^cite_note-159]

The Flash Eurobarometer survey conducted in April in all EU countries shows the greatest support for Ukraine's accession to the EU in [Portugal](23033.md), where 87% of respondents supported it. This is followed by [Estonia](28222445.md) (83%), [Lithuania](17675.md) (82%), Poland (81%) and [Ireland](147575.md) (79%). [Hungarians](17106358.md) are the most skeptical about Ukraine's accession, with only 48% of respondents supporting the idea and 37% against it. At the same time, [Hungary](13275.md) has the highest share of the population who is undecided on this issue - 16% (the same in France and [Belgium](3343.md)).[^cite_note-160]

According to a poll commissioned by the New Europe Center, which has *[European Pravda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Pravda)*, among those who have decided on their position, 68% of [Germans](152735.md), 65% of [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people), and 65% of [Dutch people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) were in favor of granting Ukraine a candidacy. At the same time, 32% of Germans and 35% of the French and the Dutch each oppose such a decision. In general, 46% of German residents support granting Ukraine the status of a candidate for EU membership, 22% are against it, another 25% found it difficult to answer, and 7% found it difficult to answer the question. Among French residents, 42% support granting Ukraine candidate status, 24% do not support it, 26% do not have a clear position, another 9% found it difficult to answer. In the [Netherlands](21148.md), 45% of respondents support Ukraine's candidacy, 24% are opposed, 21% found it difficult to choose an answer, and 10% do not know.

In June 2025, Hungary held a public consultation in which, of the 2 million people who participated, 95% voted against Ukraine joining the EU, while only 5% supported the bid.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162] On the other hand, in a major poll conducted in March-April 2025 by Hungary's main opposition party Tisza which included a question on Ukraine's accession, 58.2% (about 662,000 out of 1,137,266 voters) supported Ukraine's future EU membership.[^cite_note-163]

A June 2025, a poll in Poland found that support for Ukraine's accession to the EU was at 35% while 42% opposed.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166]
