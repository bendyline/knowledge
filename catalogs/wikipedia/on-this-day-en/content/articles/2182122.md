**Ernesto Guillermo Ruffo Appel**[^cite_note-1] (born 25 June 1952) is an American and Mexican politician who served as the 10th [governor of Baja California](3303570.md) from 1989 to 1995. As a member of the [National Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Action_Party_\(Mexico\)) (PAN), Ruffo was the first [state governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mexican_state_governors) not affiliated with the ruling [Institutional Revolutionary Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutional_Revolutionary_Party) (PRI) and its forerunners since 1929. He has served as a member of the Advisory Council of the [We Are Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Are_Mexico) party since February 2026.

## Life and career

Born to Mexican parents in [San Diego](28504.md), California, U.S., on 25 June 1952,[^cite_note-2] Ruffo attended elementary school in the coastal town of [Ensenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensenada,_Baja_California) in the state of [Baja California](199793.md). He attended the [Monterrey Institute of Technology and Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterrey_Institute_of_Technology_and_Higher_Education) (ITESM), where he graduated with a bachelor's degree in business. He became a member of the PAN in 1985 and was elected [municipal president](2907414.md) of the [municipality of Ensenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensenada_\(municipality\)) in 1986. In 1989 he was elected [governor of the state of Baja California](3303570.md) in a major [upset](1169845.md) in which he defeated the PRI's [Margarita Ortega Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margarita_Ortega_Villa) with 52.3% of the votes cast.[^cite_note-cultura-3][^cite_note-sil63-4]

[In 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Mexican_general_election), he supported the presidential candidacy of [Vicente Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicente_Fox) and, during Fox's administration, was the Coordinator of Migration Affairs for the Northern Border from 2000 to 2003.[^cite_note-5] He was an alternate [senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Republic_\(Mexico\)) in the [60th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LX_Legislature_of_the_Mexican_Congress) and [61st Congresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LXI_Legislature_of_the_Mexican_Congress) (2006 to 2012), and he later served as a senator for Baja California in the [62nd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LXII_Legislature_of_the_Mexican_Congress) and [63rd Congresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LXIII_Legislature_of_the_Mexican_Congress) (2012 to 2018).[^cite_note-sil63-4] In the [2018 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Mexican_general_election), he was elected to a [plurinominal seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_regions_of_Mexico) in the [Chamber of Deputies](411807.md) for the duration of the [64th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LXIV_Legislature_of_the_Mexican_Congress).[^cite_note-6]

Ruffo resigned from the PAN's National Executive Committee on 26 October 2018 over the direction the party was taking.[^cite_note-7]

In February 2026, he joined the Advisory Council of [We Are Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Are_Mexico), which later secured its registration as a [national political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_parties_in_Mexico) in June 2026; he did not, however, resign his membership in the PAN.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-univ-9]

On 16 July 2026, he was arrested in Ensenada on charges related to [organized crime](5182370.md) and [fuel smuggling](82108276.md).[^cite_note-10] The We Are Mexico party demanded his immediate release, accusing the federal government of orchestrating a "smokescreen" to detract public attention from scandals implicating current governor of Baja California [Marina del Pilar Ávila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_del_Pilar_%C3%81vila_Olmeda).[^cite_note-univ-9]
