The **KAIROS rocket** (カイロスロケット), or **Kii-based Advanced & Instant Rocket System**,**[^cite_note-1]** is a Japanese [solid-fuel rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-fuel_rocket) designed to launch [small satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_satellite) of mass up to 250 kg to [low Earth orbit](47568.md) and up to 150 kg to [sun-synchronous orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun-synchronous_orbit) by the [private spaceflight](752732.md) company [Space One](76117343.md).[^cite_note-2] It consists of three solid fuel powered stages and a liquid propellant upper stage.

## Namesake

The namesake of the KAIROS rocket is the Greek word *[Kairos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kairos)*, which means the subjective 'right time' as contrasted with *[Chronos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronos)* which is the objective clock time. Kairos is also an alternate spelling of the name of [Caerus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caerus), the Greek deity of luck and opportunity.[^cite_note-\:02-3]

## Manufacture

[IHI Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHI_Aerospace) manufactures the KAIROS rocket at Tomioka Plant in the city of [Tomioka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomioka,_Gunma), [Gunma Prefecture](212632.md).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-\:2-5]

## Launches

### Launch site

Launches are planned from [Spaceport Kii](76449414.md) in [Kushimoto, Wakayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushimoto,_Wakayama), Japan, a dedicated launch site built by [Shimizu Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimizu_Corporation).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-\:3-7] This spaceport is accessible from [Tokyo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haneda_Airport) (Haneda Airport) via [Nanki–Shirahama Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanki%E2%80%93Shirahama_Airport) in approximately 2 hours and 20 minutes, and from [Kansai International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_International_Airport) in approximately 2 hours and 30 minutes.[^cite_note-\:3-7]

### First launch

The first launch was planned on 9 March 2024[^cite_note-8] but was postponed because a ship was spotted in the "maritime warning area" set up in waters near the launch pad.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-\:0-10][^cite_note-\:1-11] The launch eventually took place on 13 March 2024, but the vehicle exploded five seconds after liftoff.[^cite_note-12] The remains of the rocket and payload fell close to the launch pad, but no substantial damage was found.[^cite_note-13] Space One announced several hours later that the [autonomous flight termination system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_termination_system) could have activated and ended the mission.[^cite_note-Nikkei20240313-14] On 25 August 2024, the company's director confirmed that a destruct command was issued on the rocket. The AFTS detected a speed and level of thrust on the first stage that was lower than was modeled. Because this was the first rocket launch in Japan that used a flight safety system capable of intervening in a launch without human involvement, the destruct criteria were set to be particularly conservative.[^cite_note-\:5-15]

### Second flight

The second flight took place on 18 December 2024, which was canceled because of a failed rocket shortly after launch.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] The mission was planned to carry five satellites, including four [CubeSats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CubeSats) and one [microsatellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_satellite).[^cite_note-18] These satellites include payloads developed by [Taiwan Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Space_Agency), Space Cubics LLC, Terra Space Inc. and [Lagrapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrapo), as well as an additional satellite owned by an unnamed customer.[^cite_note-19] The rocket began tumbling during first stage burn. The flight was terminated some time after.[^cite_note-\:4-20]

### Third flight

The third flight of KAIROS took place on March 4, 2026, lifting off from Spaceport Kii at 9\:10pm but was also unsuccessful with flight termination measures being implemented at around 70 seconds after liftoff.[^cite_note-\:6-21] With this third failure the commercial viability of Space One was thrown into doubt as Japan seeks to increase its domestic launch industry.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]
