**Bucheon** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 부천; Korean pronunciation: [\[pu.tɕʰʌn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Korean)) is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_South_Korea) in [Gyeonggi Province](323103.md), South Korea. Bucheon is located 25 kilometers (16 mi) away from [Seoul](19159283.md), of which it is a [satellite city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_town).[^cite_note-2] It is located between [Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon) and [Seoul](19159283.md).

Bucheon is the second most densely populated city in South Korea after Seoul,[^cite_note-auto-3] and as a result, administrative districts were abolished in July 2016 in favor of providing greater public service in community centers.[^cite_note-auto-3]

Major manufacturing operations are located in the northern areas of the city, while the areas in the south where [Seoul Subway Line 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_7) and [Seoul Subway Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_1) pass are dense commercial and residential areas.

## History

In 1914, the outer areas of [Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon) (including Gwangyo-dong, old Incheon's city center) and [Bupyeong County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bupyeong_District#History_of_%22old_Bupyeong%22) were joined under the name *Bucheon*. In 1931, Gyenam township (*myeon*, 계남면) was renamed Sosa township (*myeon*, 소사면). In 1936, the westernmost part of Bucheon, then part of old Incheon, was incorporated in Incheon and in 1940 some other part of old Incheon belonging to Bucheon Country was incorporated in Incheon again while part of old Bupyeong was annexed to Incheon at the same time. In 1941, Sosa township promoted to *eup* (town) status.[^cite_note-4]

On January 1, 1963, when the great expansion of Seoul was implemented, several districts were combined to [Yeongdeungpo District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongdeungpo_District) of [Seoul](19159283.md) as below.[^cite_note-5]

In 1988, two districts were established. Bucheon was divided into Nam-gu ("south", 남구) and Jung-gu ("central", 중구) along the Seoul-Incheon trainline. Present day Sosa-gu was formerly called Nam-gu.

In 1993, Bucheon's Jung-gu was separated into two further districts, forming [Wonmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonmi-gu) (원미구) and [Ojeong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojeong-gu) (오정구) Districts.

The three districts were abolished in July 2016 as Bucheon decided to become a unified city without any administrative districts.[^cite_note-6] However, on January 1, 2024, the Bucheon municipal government decided to return the three districts, along with the creation of 37 new administrative districts (or [dongs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_\(administrative_division\))).[^cite_note-7]

## Arts and education

Bucheon promotes itself as the cultural centre of the Seoul Metropolitan Area. The [Bucheon Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucheon_Philharmonic_Orchestra) is located there, an annual international film festival is held in July called the [Bucheon International Fantastic Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucheon_International_Fantastic_Film_Festival) or **BiFan**, and an annual international [bboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bboy) competition called Bucheon Bboy International Championship (BBIC) held by [Jinjo Crew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinjo_Crew) since 2016.

Bucheon's institutions of higher education include: [Bucheon College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucheon_College), [Yuhan College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuhan_College), [Catholic University of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_Korea) Bucheon campus, and [Seoul Theological University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Theological_University).

The city was designated "City of Literature" and has been part of the [UNESCO Creative Cities Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network) since 2017.[^cite_note-8]

## Transportation



Map of [Seoul Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Metropolitan_Subway) lines passing through Bucheon over its municipal districts before 2018

Bucheon has an extensive bus network of local and area buses that connect the city to Seoul, Incheon and other surrounding cities. Bucheon Bus Terminal has buses connecting to different cities and provinces throughout the country. The [Seoul Subway System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Metropolitan_Subway) [Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_1) and [Line 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_7) runs through Bucheon. There are currently five stations in Seoul Subway Line Line 1 within Bucheon, including [Bucheon station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucheon_station) and [Songnae station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songnae_station), and seven stations in the Line 7, including [Kkachiul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kkachiul_station), [Bucheon Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucheon_Stadium_station), [Chunui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chunui_station), [Sinjung-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinjung-dong_station), [Bucheon City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucheon_City_Hall_station), and [Sang-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sang-dong_station), which continues through Incheon ending at [Seongnam station (Incheon)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seongnam_station_\(Incheon\)).

Two more subway lines were recently added. The [Sosa-Wonsi Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosa-Wonsi_Line) opened in 2018, connecting Bucheon's [Sosa station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosa_station) of the Line 1 with [Ansan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansan)'s [Choji station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choji_station) of [Seoul Subway Line 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_4). The [Daegok-Sosa Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daegok-Sosa_Line) opened in 2022, connecting with the Sosa-Wonsi Line as a single line. It will provide subway service in the northern area of Bucheon and connect the city to [Gimpo International Airport station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimpo_International_Airport_station) where transfers to [Seoul Subway Line 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_5), [Seoul Subway Line 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_9), [AREX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AREX) and [Gimpo Goldline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimpo_Goldline) will be available. The line terminates at [Daegok station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daegok_station_\(Goyang\)) in [Goyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goyang), which offers a transfer to [Seoul Subway Line 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Subway_Line_3) and [Gyeongui-Jungang Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeongui-Jungang_Line).

In addition, the city is getting a completely new subway line in the future which will connect Bucheon's northern Wonjeong area to [Hongik University station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongik_University_station).[^cite_note-11]

## Religion

There are a number of churches in Bucheon, including a Full Gospel Church near Lotte Department Store and the English-language "Bucheon [Onnuri English Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onnuri_Community_Church)" (BOEM)[^cite_note-12] in Sang Dong. There is also a Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints located a short ways north of Bucheon station. Evangelism and mission is a key expression of Christianity in the Bucheon churches. There is also Seogwangsa Temple, beside Weonmisan Mountain.
