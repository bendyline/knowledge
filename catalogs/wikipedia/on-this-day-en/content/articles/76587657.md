General elections were held in [Malawi](19088.md) on 16 September 2025 to elect the [president](516080.md), the 229 members of the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Malawi\)) and 509 local government councillors.[^cite_note-1]&#x20;

The presidential election resulted in a victory for former president [Peter Mutharika](29471682.md) of the [Democratic Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Progressive_Party_\(Malawi\)), who received 57% of the vote. Incumbent president [Lazarus Chakwera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazarus_Chakwera) of the [Malawi Congress Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_Congress_Party) finished second with 33% of the vote.&#x20;

## Background

The election was the second conducted under the [two-round](25932.md) constitutional threshold introduced after the annulment of the [2019 Malawian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Malawian_general_election) and the subsequent [fresh presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Malawian_presidential_election) the following year that brought [Lazarus Chakwera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazarus_Chakwera) to power. In July 2024, the [United Transformation Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Transformation_Movement) (UTM) withdrew from the governing [Tonse Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonse_Alliance_\(Malawi\)), leaving the [Malawi Congress Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_Congress_Party) (MCP) to contest the elections on its own.[^cite_note-auto2-2]&#x20;

## Electoral system

The president of Malawi is elected using a [two-round system](25932.md). Should no candidate secure an absolute majority in the first round, a run-off is held within 30 days. Members of the National Assembly are elected in single-member constituencies by [first-past-the-post voting](261709.md).[^cite_note-3]&#x20;

According to the [Malawi Electoral Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_Electoral_Commission), 7.2 million voters were enrolled after the three registration phases, with women constituting 57% of the electorate.[^cite_note-4] At 65%, the number of eligible voters who enrolled was less from the 80% recorded in 2019.[^cite_note-5]&#x20;

## Campaign

The official campaign period began on 14 July 2025 at the [Bingu International Conference Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingu_International_Conference_Centre) (BICC) in Lilongwe, with activities continuing through 14 September, two days before polling on 16 September 2025, according to the MEC election calendar.[^cite_note-18]&#x20;

### Presidential debates

The Presidential Debates Taskforce, chaired by MISA Malawi, organised debates ahead of the 2025 general election with support from the National Democratic Institute. The first debate was held on 21 August 2025 at the BICC and broadcast nationally.[^cite_note-19] A second debate was rescheduled to 9 September 2025. President [Lazarus Chakwera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazarus_Chakwera) and former president [Peter Mutharika](29471682.md) did not participate.[^cite_note-20]&#x20;

### Peace initiative by the Public Affairs Committee

On 8 September 2025, the [Public Affairs Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Affairs_Committee_\(Malawi\)) (PAC) held a National Day of Prayers and Peace Declaration signing at the BICC in Lilongwe. The event brought together several presidential candidates, including President Lazarus Chakwera, Joyce Banda, Atupele Muluzi, and Dalitso Kabambe, who pledged to promote peaceful elections.[^cite_note-21]&#x20;

The gathering was marked by gestures of unity. Peter Mutharika and Michael Usi's absence drew criticism.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] PAC chairperson Patrick Thawale stressed that the declaration would help manage disputes during and after the elections.[^cite_note-24]&#x20;

## Conduct

Civil society and media advocates such as MISA Malawi have called for unrestricted press freedom and safety for journalists, citing prior instances of intimidation during electoral periods.[^cite_note-29] In June 2025, a rally calling for the resignation of top officials of the [Malawi Electoral Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_Electoral_Commission) (MEC) was attacked by masked, machete-wielding men.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] MEC engaged six international observer missions, undertook gender‑sensitive and risk‑management training, and facilitated party briefings on voter roll verification to reinforce preparedness and credibility.[^cite_note-32]&#x20;

On 29 August 2025, MEC completed the accreditation of election observers for the 16 September general election, approving 19 international observers and 40 local observers.[^cite_note-33]&#x20;

On 27 August 2025, MEC conducted a dry-run of its Results Management System (RMS) ahead of the general election. The exercise, held at the national tally centre in [Lilongwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilongwe) and in selected constituencies, aimed to test the speed, accuracy, and security of results transmission.[^cite_note-34] The test revealed some challenges, including network glitches in [Blantyre](642604.md) and Lilongwe that delayed the transmission of results. MEC acknowledged the issues but stated that the system's integrity remained intact and pledged to resolve the problems before polling day.[^cite_note-35]&#x20;

### Results management and observer concerns

In their preliminary report, international and regional observer missions, including the [European Union](9317.md) and [Southern African Development Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Development_Community), urged MEC to ensure transparency in the management of election results. The missions, along with several civil society groups, expressed concern about delays in publishing partial results and called for safeguarding election data and materials against tampering or loss.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]&#x20;

On 18 September 2025, some political party representatives raised concerns after tally kits from districts including [Nkhata Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nkhata_Bay_District), [Luchenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luchenza), and [Kasungu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasungu_District) arrived at the national tally centre in [Lilongwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilongwe) without proper security seals. The handover was briefly halted, with MEC pledging to investigate; it later resumed after parties received printouts from the devices for verification.[^cite_note-38]&#x20;

On 21 September, MEC chairperson [Annabel Mtalimanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annabel_Mtalimanja) cautioned political parties against making unverified claims of victory during the tallying process, saying that premature declarations can erode public trust and threaten national peace.[^cite_note-39]&#x20;

### Irregularities and investigations

On 19 September, the MCP said it had found evidence of irregularities in the vote count in 13 of Malawi's 28 districts. Separately, police arrested eight election data entry clerks in the Lilongwe area on suspicion of "manipulating data".[^cite_note-40] An investigation was also launched into the alleged suicide attempt of an election returning officer who had offered bribes in exchange for manipulating the election results. Four domestic broadcasters also stopped live dashboards of their unofficial voting tallies without explanation that same day.[^cite_note-41]&#x20;

On 20 September, [United Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Democratic_Front_\(Malawi\)) (UDF) president and presidential candidate [Atupele Muluzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atupele_Muluzi) claimed there was vote‐rigging in the party's stronghold districts, citing [Mangochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangochi_District) and [Balaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaka_Township,_Malawi) as some of the areas where serious irregularities occurred. He also alleged that many UDF monitors were directed to leave polling centers and told to return only after results had already been tallied in order to sign the official record sheets. He called for investigations into the matter and urged that all votes cast be properly accounted for.[^cite_note-42]&#x20;

Civil society organisations, through the Civil Society Elections Integrity Forum (CSEIF), on 20 September called for a speedy and transparent investigation into the recent arrests of electoral officials and clerks in Lilongwe Nkhoma Constituency. Separate inquiries were also reported in Mangochi, [Chikwawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chikwawa_District), [Blantyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blantyre_District), [Machinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinga_District), and [Rumphi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumphi_District), highlighting the broader scope of concern. In a related incident, another presiding officer in Chikwawa allegedly attempted suicide after failing to reconcile manual and electronic tallies. Observers warned that unresolved issues across these cases could undermine public trust in the electoral process.[^cite_note-43]&#x20;

On 21 September, MCP presidential runningmate [Vitumbiko Mumba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitumbiko_Mumba) claimed during a press briefing that three men at Jenda roadblock had been following him for several days from the [Mzuzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mzuzu) area with the intention of attacking him. However, Inspector General of Police Merlyn Yolamu stated that the police were not aware of the alleged incident.[^cite_note-44] In the same briefing, MCP spokesperson [Jessie Kabwila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessie_Kabwila) also alleged that during vote counting in some polling centres, the party's monitors were told to leave, with some reportedly being chased away and others injured.[^cite_note-45]&#x20;

On 24 September, before announcing the winner, the commission stated that it had addressed all complaints received from political parties and stakeholders during and after the voting process, including those related to vote counting, data entry, and monitor access at polling centres. MEC chairperson [Annabel Mtalimanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annabel_Mtalimanja) confirmed that investigations and corrective actions were carried out where necessary, and the official presidential results were gazetted on the same day to formalize the outcome of the election.[^cite_note-46]&#x20;

## Results

### President

Initial results suggested that former president [Peter Mutharika](29471682.md) of the [Democratic Progressive Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Progressive_Party_\(Malawi\)) was leading in several areas, including the lakeshore districts of [Nkhata Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nkhata_Bay_District) and [Mangochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangochi_District), in the presidential election.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] Official tallies were still awaited from the [Malawi Electoral Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_Electoral_Commission). Despite this, both the MCP and the DPP claimed victory on 17 September.[^cite_note-49] From 19 September, the commission began releasing official results, starting with four councils, among them [Luchenza municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luchenza) and [Likoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likoma_District).[^cite_note-50]&#x20;

On 18 September 2025 independent candidate Phunziro Mvula conceded defeat, stating that he accepted the outcome and pledged to run again in 2030.[^cite_note-51] The following day, Smart Swira also conceded defeat and urged Malawians to accept the results.[^cite_note-52] On 20 September, [Kondwani Nankhumwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kondwani_Nankhumwa) of the People's Development Party (PDP) and independent candidate Adil James Chilungo likewise conceded defeat, with Nankhumwa acknowledging that the preliminary results did not favour his party.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] On 21 September, [UTM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Transformation_Movement) presidential candidate [Dalitso Kabambe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalitso_Kabambe) conceded defeat and congratulated Peter Mutharika and the DPP, saying the unofficial results reflected the will of Malawians and urged a peaceful transfer of power.[^cite_note-55] These concessions were made before the commission had officially announced the winner of the presidential election.&#x20;

On 23 September, partial results from 24 of Malawi's 36 electoral councils showed Mutharika leading the presidential vote with about 66% of valid ballots counted, with Chakwera trailing significantly.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] Former president [Bakili Muluzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakili_Muluzi) publicly asked Chakwera to accept the likely outcome.[^cite_note-58] The Malawi Council of Churches also appealed to Chakwera to concede defeat gracefully, noting that the electoral "table cannot change" in his favour.[^cite_note-59]&#x20;

President Chakwera conceded defeat in a televised address on 24 September 2025, several hours before the commission had announced final results. At that point, only 24 of the 36 electoral councils were declared, yet Chakwera acknowledged that his opponent had achieved an insurmountable lead and urged Malawians to support a peaceful transition.[^cite_note-60]&#x20;

Some analysts interpreted Mutharika's strong showing in the presidential contest as partly the product of a [protest vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protest_vote) against the incumbent, citing public dissatisfaction over economic mismanagement, scarcity of goods, and weak decision-making by Chakwera's government. Experts also noted that votes that might otherwise have gone to UTM were diverted to Mutharika, reflecting strategic voting dynamics.[^cite_note-61]&#x20;

### National Assembly

The commission began releasing official parliamentary results on 29 September, starting with 13 of the 36 electoral councils. Three constituencies, [Mzimba South East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mzimba_South_East_Constituency) and Nkhata Bay Central – were withheld due to the need to examine [voided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoilt_vote) votes, while in Nkhotakota Liwaladzi the parliamentary election was postponed following a [court order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_order). On the same day, the commission also announced that it had nullified the parliamentary results for the Lilongwe Mtandire – Mtsiliza constituency over irregularities.[^cite_note-63]&#x20;

On 30 September, the commission announced results for 224 constituencies, with the DPP winning the largest share of seats.[^cite_note-64] It also pledged to conduct parliamentary [by-elections](155434.md) in four constituencies where voting had been nullified or not completed.[^cite_note-65] In Lilongwe Chilobwe constituency, the commission had earlier declared MCP's Lawrence Chakakala Chaziya elected unopposed after no other candidate was nominated,[^cite_note-66] bringing the total constituencies to 229 as required.&#x20;

The number of women in parliament increased from 40 to 48.[^cite_note-67]&#x20;

## Aftermath

### Voter turnout and electoral integrity

The 2025 general election saw a notable increase in voter turnout, with 5,502,982 individuals casting their ballots, representing 76.4% of the 7.2 million registered voters. This marks a significant rise from the 64.8% turnout in the [2020 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Malawian_presidential_election). Analysts attribute this improvement to widespread public dissatisfaction with the prevailing economic conditions and renewed confidence in the electoral process, bolstered by reforms and increased transparency by MEC.[^cite_note-auto20-69]&#x20;

However, the election also experienced a rise in [null and void](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoilt_vote) votes. Approximately 155,225 votes were invalidated, accounting for 2.8% of the total votes cast. This figure more than doubles the 1.28% recorded in the 2020 election. Experts suggest that this increase may be due to insufficient voter education and the complexity introduced by having 17 presidential candidates on the ballot, which reduced the size of the voting boxes and potentially led to voter errors.[^cite_note-auto20-69]&#x20;

### Inauguration and transition of power

[Peter Mutharika](29471682.md) was sworn in as [president of Malawi](516080.md) on 4 October 2025 at [Kamuzu Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamuzu_Stadium) in [Blantyre](642604.md), in a ceremony attended by government officials, supporters, and several regional leaders, including Zimbabwean president [Emmerson Mnangagwa](6390734.md), Mozambican president [Daniel Chapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Chapo), and Botswanan president [Duma Boko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duma_Boko).[^cite_note-70] Outgoing President [Lazarus Chakwera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazarus_Chakwera) did not attend the ceremony; however, the [MCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_Congress_Party) issued a goodwill message wishing Mutharika success.[^cite_note-71]&#x20;

In his inaugural address, Mutharika acknowledged that he was inheriting a country facing economic challenges such as high inflation, shortages of fuel and food, and debt constraints, and pledged a “tough and painful” return to work rather than promises of “milk and honey".[^cite_note-72]&#x20;

### Government formation and appointments

Within days of taking office, President Mutharika named [Joseph Mwanamveka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Mwanamveka) as his [finance minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_and_Economic_Affairs_\(Malawi\)) - a role Mwanamveka had previously held between 2019 and 2020.[^cite_note-73] In addition, he appointed [Enoch Chihana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enoch_Chihana) as [second vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vice-President_of_Malawi), [George Chaponda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Chaponda) as [foreign affairs minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Malawi\)) and [Alfred Gangata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Gangata) as minister of state.[^cite_note-74]&#x20;

### Debate over leader of the Opposition

With independents having emerged as the single largest bloc in the early unofficial results, some observers and commentators argued that the [leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition) in the National Assembly should have come from among the independent MPs rather than from MCP. This was seen as a possible departure from precedent, given MCP’s traditional role as opposition leader, and was noted as a factor that could influence negotiations over parliamentary leadership roles.[^cite_note-75]&#x20;

### Reversal of headquarters relocations

On 10 October, President Mutharika issued an [executive order](210541.md) reversing the earlier government directive that had moved several parastatal head offices from [Blantyre](642604.md) and [Zomba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zomba,_Malawi) to [Lilongwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilongwe). Under the new order, agencies including the [Malawi Communications Regulatory Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_Communications_Regulatory_Authority), [Malawi Electoral Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_Electoral_Commission), and [Malawi Housing Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Malawi_Housing_Corporation\&action=edit\&redlink=1) were instructed to return to their original locations in Blantyre within three months, while the [Malawi Prison Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_Prison_Service) was directed to relocate back to Zomba.[^cite_note-76]&#x20;

### Domestic reaction, political significance, and commentary

The transition of power in Malawi has been widely noted as a positive example of democratic turnover in a region often dominated by long-standing incumbents.[^cite_note-chatham-77] Analysts have drawn attention to how the 2025 election was influenced heavily by economic discontent: with inflation persistently high, shortages of essential goods, and donor aid cuts, many voters appeared to use the election as a means to express dissatisfaction with the Chakwera government.[^cite_note-78] Some commentators observed that the result suggests that party support in Malawi is not immutable, and that political comebacks are possible when incumbents mismanage economic or governance challenges.[^cite_note-chatham-77]&#x20;

## Court rulings

On 11 September, five days before the polls, the High Court in Blantyre, presided over by Justice Chimbizgani Kacheche (Judicial Review Cause No. 19 of 2025), dismissed an opposition request for an independent audit of MEC’s election management system. The court upheld MEC’s decision to deny the audit but ruled that results could not be announced solely on the basis of electronic transmission and should await the arrival of manual tallies as well.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]&#x20;

On 22 September, the High Court in Lilongwe dismissed an application by [Madalitso Kazombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madalitso_Kazombo) (former MCP MP and former Deputy Speaker in the last parliament) seeking permission for a judicial review of the [Malawi Electoral Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_Electoral_Commission)'s conduct in relation to the elections. The case (Judicial Review Cause No. 61 of 2025), heard by Justice Violet Palikena-Chipao, was dismissed on the grounds of procedural irregularities in the documents submitted, such as typographical errors, incorrect legal citations, unclear arguments and inconsistent facts. The court granted Kazombo liberty to refile with properly prepared documents.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82]&#x20;

On 23 September, President Chakwera and the governing MCP sought an [injunction](39343.md) to restrain MEC from announcing the winner, but the High Court, presided over by Judge Howard Pemba, dismissed the application. However, the court allowed the claimants to pursue a judicial review of the matter,[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] although Chakwera later withdrew the case after conceding defeat.[^cite_note-85]&#x20;

In the days following the announcement of parliamentary results, at least 83 election petitions were filed by losing candidates in constituencies across the country. These challenges spanned multiple districts, including [Machinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinga_District), [Chikwawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chikwawa_District), [Dowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dowa_District), and [Kasungu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasungu_District), and contested outcomes in several parliamentary seats. The High Court heard the cases, marking one of the most extensive waves of post-election litigation in Malawi’s recent history.[^cite_note-86]&#x20;
