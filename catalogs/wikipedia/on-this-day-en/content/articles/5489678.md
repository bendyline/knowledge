**Thongloun Sisoulith** ([Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_language): ທອງລຸນ ສີສຸລິດ; born 10 November 1945)[^cite_note-\:0-2] is a Laotian politician and historian who serves as the [general secretary](30663327.md) of the [Lao People's Revolutionary Party](203651.md) and the seventh [president of Laos](2135738.md) since 2021.[^cite_note-auto-3]

Thongloun was born and educated in [Houaphanh province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houaphanh_province), before receiving education in [Leningrad](24320051.md) and [Moscow](19004.md) in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). During the [Laotian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laotian_Civil_War), he supported the [Pathet Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathet_Lao) as a teacher. He then became the Deputy Minister of Foreign Affairs in 1987 which lasted to 1992, where he then served in other posts in government. In 2001, he became the [deputy prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Laos) before becoming the [minister of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Laos\)) in 2006. Described as a 'moderate' by the U.S embassy in [Vientiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vientiane), Thongloun helped improve [Laos–United States relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laos%E2%80%93United_States_relations), which was welcomed in 2009 by Senator [Jim Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Webb) and in 2010 by [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton); while improving Laos' relationship with China and Vietnam. In 2016, he became the prime minister where he undertook anti-corruption campaigns. He then became general secretary of the LPRP, and president in 2021.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

## Early life and education

Thongloun Sisoulith was born into a [Tai Deng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai_Daeng_people) family in the [Houaphan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houaphan) province of Laos on 10 November 1945. He went on to study at Pedagogical College of Neo Lao Hak Sat in Houaphan from 1962 to 1969.[^cite_note-\:1-4] He was further educated in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [Vietnam](202354.md). After finishing graduating, he received a doctor of philosophy in history and a master of arts.[^cite_note-\:2-5] From 1973 to 1978, Thongloun studied a Master of Linguistics and Literature at the Herzen Pedagogical Institute in Leningrad, Soviet Union. Then from 1981 to 1984, he studied a PhD in History of International Relations at the Academy of Social Sciences in Moscow.[^cite_note-\:1-4][^cite_note-\:2-5]

## Teaching career

During the Laotian Civil War, Thongloun served with the Communist Pathet Lao as a teacher. From 1967 to 1969, Thoungloun served in Educational Department of the Neo Lao Hak Sat as a senior member, before in 1969 he became a member of the Office of the Representative of the Neo Lao Hak Sat in [Hanoi](56667.md), [North Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vietnam). He then returned to and from 1978 to 1979, where he served as a lecturer at the [National University of Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Laos) following the Communist takeover of Vientiane in 1975.[^cite_note-\:1-4] He headed the Russian language programme at the university.[^cite_note-\:3-6]

## Political career

Throughout his years in government, he has a list of appointments. He was Deputy Minister of Foreign Affairs from 1987 to 1992, Minister of Labour and Social Welfare from 1993 to 1997, and a member of the [National Assembly](4501980.md) from 1998 to 2000. He became [deputy prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Laos) and President of the State Planning Committee on 27 March 2001,[^cite_note-7] and he was additionally appointed Foreign Minister on 8 June 2006, replacing [Somsavat Lengsavad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somsavat_Lengsavad). He was chosen to become [Prime Minister of Laos](3971499.md) at the [10th Party Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_National_Congress_of_the_Lao_People's_Revolutionary_Party) on 23 January 2016.[^cite_note-8]

### As Prime Minister (2016–2021)

After Thongloun became the Prime Minister of Laos, he began anti-corruption campaigns in the Lao government. From April 2016 to February 2017, the State Inspection Authority made 25 arrests related to corruption.[^cite_note-\:4-9] Under Thongloun, the government auctioned off [BMW 7 Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_7_Series) and [Mercedes-Benz](18564164.md) cars issued by the government and replaced them with [Toyota Camrys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Camry).[^cite_note-10] In 2017, there were disputes on the [Cambodia–Laos border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia%E2%80%93Laos_border), and Cambodian Prime Minister [Hun Sen](393779.md) warned: "I appeal to Laos Prime Minister Thongloun Sisoulith to withdraw troops from Cambodian territory without any conditions."[^cite_note-11]

On 23 July 2018, a saddle dam part of the Xe Pian-Xe Namnoy hydropower project on the [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong) river in [Champasak province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champasak_province) collapsed, killing 71 people whilst displacing 14,440. The dam was being worked on by Laos, [Thailand](30128.md) and [South Korea](27019.md), with there being warnings of the dam's unsafety in preceding days.[^cite_note-12] As Prime Minister, Thongloun postponed meetings and travelled to the affected area in [Sanamxay district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanamxay_district) to oversee relief efforts.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

On 4 January 2020, Thongloun represented Laos in Hanoi at the 42nd meeting of the Vietnam – Laos Inter-Governmental Committee with the Vietnamese Prime Minister. During the meetings, he praised Vietnam's economy, with both countries agreeing to strengthen ties and cooperation between each other.[^cite_note-15] During the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Laos), Laos cooperated with Vietnam and [Cambodia](334751.md), with Thongloun holding phone calls with his Cambodian and Vietnamese counterparts [Hun Sen](393779.md) and [Nguyễn Xuân Phúc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguy%E1%BB%85n_Xu%C3%A2n_Ph%C3%BAc).[^cite_note-16] At the 36th ASEAN summit on 26 June 2020, he represented Laos where he spoke on the success of [ASEAN](106539.md) during the COVID-19 pandemic.[^cite_note-17] On 10 June, he praised the National Taskforce Committee for its handling of the COVID-19 pandemic in Laos following 59 days of no new cases of COVID-19, and support from other countries such as China, United States and Vietnam.[^cite_note-18]

In August 2020, he urged the media to continue "defeating the fake, deceptive, and harmful news" on social media sites. This was interpreted by international media as a way to tell the media in Laos to not report negatively on the government.[^cite_note-19] On 13 December 2020, Thongloun spoke to the [United Nations](31769.md) on how Laos continues to follow the [Paris Agreement](30242372.md), and other policies which aim to reduce carbon emissions. He asked for support from other nations to assist Laos reach these targets.[^cite_note-20]

At the [11th National Congress of the Lao People's Revolutionary Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_National_Congress_of_the_Lao_People's_Revolutionary_Party) on 15 January 2021, he was elected the party's general secretary, and hence the *de facto* leader of Laos,[^cite_note-auto-3] becoming the first civilian with no military background to be general secretary.[^cite_note-21] Thongloun was sworn into office on 22 March 2021, replacing previous leader Bounnhang Vorachith after the latter's retirement. Phankham Viphavanh replaced Thongloun as Prime-minister.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

## Leadership

Following his swearing in as general secretary and president, Thongloun vowed to create economic growth and reduce poverty in Laos by working with the Lao People's Revolutionary Party (LPRP).[^cite_note-\:1-4] In March 2021, Laos began using the Russian [Sputnik V COVID-19 vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputnik_V_COVID-19_vaccine) as the main vaccine during its vaccination programmes.[^cite_note-\:3-6]

On 3 December 2021, he and [General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) opened the [Boten–Vientiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boten%E2%80%93Vientiane_railway) section of the Lao-China railway.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] Laos faces rising debt to China, with half of Laos' external debt being owed to China. At the 27th Future of Asia conference in Tokyo on 27 May 2022, Thongloun downplayed concerns over a Chinese debt-trap.[^cite_note-24]

Thongloun was re-elected as general secretary on 8 January 2026 at the 1st plenary session of the [12th Central Committee](82068549.md).[^cite_note-25]

### Foreign policy



Thongloun with Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) before the [Moscow Victory Day Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Moscow_Victory_Day_Parade) in Moscow, Russia, 9 May 2024

Under Thongloun, Laos finds itself balancing its relationships between China and Vietnam.[^cite_note-26] On 26 May 2023, following the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) and [tensions between China and Taiwan](2596399.md), Thongloun warned that these conflicts may escalate into larger scale wars.[^cite_note-27]

In October 2024, Thongloun attended the [16th BRICS summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_BRICS_summit) in [Kazan](202295.md), Russia, where he met with [General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) [Xi Jinping](2017814.md) and other leaders.[^cite_note-28]

## Personal life

He is married to Naly Sisoulith (born 1947). The couple has three children: two sons and one daughter. Thongloun's daughter Moukdavanh is a deputy director general of the International Organization Department within the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Laos\))[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30], Thongloun's son Thongly is Secretary of [Lao People's Revolutionary Youth Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_People's_Revolutionary_Youth_Union).[^cite_note-31]

In addition to [Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_language), he speaks [Vietnamese](32511.md), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language).[^cite_note-\:0-2]
