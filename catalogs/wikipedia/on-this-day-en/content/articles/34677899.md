**Iliana Malinova Iotova**[^cite_note-3] ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) **Todorova**; born 24 October 1964) is a Bulgarian politician and journalist who has served as [president of Bulgaria](335070.md) since 23 January 2026. She is the [first woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_elected_and_appointed_female_state_leaders) to hold the office. A member of the [Bulgarian Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Socialist_Party) (BSP), Iotova previously served as [vice president](4684983.md) under [Rumen Radev](51575780.md) from 2017 to 2026, when she became the first Bulgarian vice president to assume the presidency intra-term due to Radev's resignation.

## Early life

Iliana Malinova Iotova was born in [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia), [People's Republic of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Bulgaria) on 24 October 1964.[^cite_note-auto2-4][^cite_note-bta1-5] She studied in the [Lycée Français de Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyc%C3%A9e_Fran%C3%A7ais_de_Sofia).[^cite_note-auto4-6][^cite_note-bta1-5] She received a degree in Bulgarian and French [philology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology) from the [University of Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sofia) and later specialized at the [École nationale d'administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_nationale_d'administration) (ENA, National School of Administration) in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg), France, and the Center for European Studies (CEES) of the [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg).[^cite_note-bta1-5]

In the 1980s, Malinova worked as a nurse at the First Hospital of Sofia St John the Baptist.[^cite_note-vesti-7] From 1990 to 1997, she worked at [Bulgarian National Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_National_Television) as a reporter, editor, director, presenter of news and current affairs programmes and also became head of the News and Current Affairs Directorate.[^cite_note-auto4-6][^cite_note-bnr-8][^cite_note-bta1-5] She then worked as the director of the press service of the [Bulgarian Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Socialist_Party) in 1997.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-bnr-8][^cite_note-bta1-5]

## Political career



Iotova during a debate on the [Schengen area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_area), 19 April 2016

In the [2005 national elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Bulgarian_parliamentary_election), Iotova was elected to the [National Assembly](2122384.md), an office she held until 2007.[^cite_note-bnr-8][^cite_note-bta1-5] During her term, she headed the Bulgarian delegation to the [Assemblée parlementaire de la Francophonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembl%C3%A9e_parlementaire_de_la_Francophonie) and member of the delegation to the [Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Council_of_Europe).[^cite_note-bta1-5]

### Member of the European Parliament (2007–2017)

Iotova became a [Member of the European Parliament](258909.md) in 2007 and was re-elected in 2014.[^cite_note-auto1-10] There she was part of the [Progressive Alliance of Socialists and Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Alliance_of_Socialists_and_Democrats) group.[^cite_note-auto1-10][^cite_note-bta1-5]

In parliament, Iotova served on the [Committee on the Internal Market and Consumer Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_on_the_Internal_Market_and_Consumer_Protection) (2007–09), the [Committee on Fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_on_Fisheries) (2009–14), and the [Committee on Petitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_on_Petitions) (2009–14).[^cite_note-auto2-4] From 2012 until 2013, she was also a member of the Special Committee on Organised Crime, Corruption and Money Laundering. From the [2014 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_European_Parliament_election), she served as vice-chairwoman of the [Committee on Civil Liberties, Justice and Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_on_Civil_Liberties,_Justice_and_Home_Affairs), under the leadership of chairman [Claude Moraes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Moraes).[^cite_note-auto4-6][^cite_note-auto2-4] In this capacity, she was her parliamentary group's [rapporteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapporteur) on a 2015 report calling for the equitable distribution of 40,000 refugees across the European Union.[^cite_note-11]

In addition to her committee assignments, Iotova served as chairwoman of the parliament's delegation to the EU-Montenegro Stabilisation and Association Parliamentary Committee and as member of the delegation to the [Parliamentary Assembly of the Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Mediterranean).[^cite_note-auto3-12] She was also a member of the European Parliament Intergroup on Integrity (Transparency, Anti-Corruption and Organized Crime).[^cite_note-13]

### Vice President of Bulgaria (2017–2026)



Iotova between President Radev and [European Commission President](24468.md) [Jean-Claude Juncker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Juncker) in Sofia, 12 January 2018

Ahead of Bulgaria's [2016 presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Bulgarian_presidential_election), Iotova was officially nominated as the Bulgarian Socialist Party's running mate for presidential candidate [Rumen Radev](51575780.md). After her election to the Vice Presidency, she resigned her MEP seat.[^cite_note-14] As Vice President, Iotova focused on relations with Bulgarian communities abroad and on promoting Bulgarian education and language.[^cite_note-bnr-8] They both were sworn in on 19 January 2017 and began their office on 22 January.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-Officially-16]

On 1 February 2021, President Radev officially announced that he and Iliana Iotova would run for a second term in the [2021 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Bulgarian_general_election).[^cite_note-17] In the second round, held on 21 November, Rumen Radev defeated Gerdjikov with 66% of the vote, thus securing a second term in office for Iotova as well.[^cite_note-18] She was sworn in on 19 January 2022 and her term officially began on 22 January.[^cite_note-bta1-5]

On 21 March 2024, Iotova participated in an educational forum in Lisbon, which she sponsored, and in which she called for the need to create a Bulgarian cultural institution abroad to promote Bulgarian culture and language internationally.[^cite_note-19]

### President of Bulgaria (2026–present)

During an address to the nation on 19 January 2026, the then-president [Rumen Radev](51575780.md) announced his intention to resign.[^cite_note-20] The following day, Radev submitted his resignation as President to the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Bulgaria).[^cite_note-21] Three days later on 23 January, the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Bulgaria) formally accepted Radev's resignation, making Iotova the country's first female president.[^cite_note-22] Shortly after the ruling, Radev left the "Dondukov 2" palace together with Iotova, the new President, and addressed supporters, promising a unified struggle at the [upcoming legislative elections](76476389.md).[^cite_note-23] Iotova will complete the remainder of Radev's term until the [2026 Bulgarian presidential election](78095655.md).[^cite_note-bta1-5][^cite_note-Officially-16]

On 11 February, Iotova nominated [Andrey Gyurov](81825567.md), the deputy governor of the [Bulgarian National Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_National_Bank), as acting prime minister.[^cite_note-24]

## Personal life

Aside from her native [Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language), she is fluent in [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and speaks [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language).[^cite_note-bta1-5] Iotova married Andrey Iotov, a doctor she met at the First Hospital of Sofia St. John the Baptist in 1985. They have one child.[^cite_note-vesti-7][^cite_note-bta1-5]
