**Samaritan's Purse** is an [evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) [Christian humanitarian aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_humanitarian_aid) organization that provides aid to people in physical need as a key part of its [Christian missionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_mission) work. The organization's president is [Franklin Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Graham), son of Christian evangelist [Billy Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Graham). The name of the organization is derived from the [New Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Testament) [Parable of the Good Samaritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parable_of_the_Good_Samaritan). With international headquarters in [Boone, United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boone,_North_Carolina), the organization also maintains warehouse and aviation facilities in nearby [North Wilkesboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Wilkesboro,_North_Carolina) and [Greensboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensboro,_North_Carolina), North Carolina.

## History

Samaritan's Purse was founded in 1970 by [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist) pastor [Robert Pierce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pierce), the founder of [World Vision International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Vision_International), in Hollywood, California, and was relocated to [Boone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boone,_North_Carolina), North Carolina by [Franklin Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Graham) in 1980.[^cite_note-2]<sup>: 139 </sup>[^cite_note-3]<sup>: 2015 </sup>[^cite_note-4]

Franklin Graham met Pierce in 1973, and they made several trips together to visit relief projects and missionary partners in Asia and elsewhere. Graham became president of Samaritan's Purse in 1979, following Pierce's death the prior year.[^cite_note-5]<sup>: 136 </sup>

By 2022, Samaritan's Purse had offices in the United States, Australia, Canada, Germany, the United Kingdom and South Korea. Beyond the offices in those six countries, Samaritan's Purse also has field offices in 15 countries as of August 2025. The organization provides assistance in more than 100 countries.[^cite_note-6] It operates worldwide as Samaritan's Purse, Ippan Shadan Houjin in Japan and as the Emmanuel Group, a wholly owned aircraft title holding corporation formed in 2004.[^cite_note-\:3-1]

### Operation Christmas Child

Operation Christmas Child was created in 1990 by [Dave Cooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_J_Cooke) and his wife Gill for children in Romania.[^cite_note-10]<sup>: 332 </sup>[^cite_note-11]<sup>: 149 </sup>[^cite_note-12]<sup>: 254 </sup> Each November thousands of churches, schools, groups, and individual donors prepare and collect shoeboxes filled with toys, school supplies, personal items, and other small gifts. A booklet of Bible stories is often distributed alongside the shoebox gifts which are given to children based on need alone, regardless of their faith. These boxes are then distributed overseas by volunteers. As of 2024, over 232 million shoeboxes have been delivered.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

In 1994, a pioneering figure of the program, [Mary Damron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Damron), collected 1,256 shoeboxes from family and neighbors in her impoverished community of [Wyoming County, West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming_County,_West_Virginia).[^cite_note-15] Damron was one of the first major collectors of shoeboxes for Operation Christmas Child, and soon became an ambassador and missionary for the program. She traveled to over 40 countries during her time as a missionary with Samaritan's Purse. Damron died in December 2024, aged 70.[^cite_note-16]

In November 2022, the organization reached a milestone by distributing its 200 millionth Christmas shoebox.[^cite_note-17] Elizabeth Groff, who was born in Ukraine and adopted by an American family in 2007, delivered the 200 millionth shoebox gift to a child in Ukraine.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

The program uses "follow-up" evangelism[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] with pamphlets of Bible stories that are given to families that receive the boxes.[^cite_note-24]

The follow-up evangelism program of Operation Christmas Child is called "The Greatest Journey". It is a 12-week discipleship program for children who receive shoebox gifts.[^cite_note-25]

### U.S. disaster relief

Samaritan's Purse launched a large civilian airlift operation to relieve those affected by [Hurricane Helene](77945659.md) (late September 2024). The organization completed 358 missions (helicopter and fixed wing). 35,000 volunteers participated in cleaning the affected areas from mud, trees, and debris.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] In November 2024, Samaritan's Purse announced the launch of its new Hurricane Helene Rebuild Program.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] In May 2025, [Franklin Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Graham) started welcoming the first families into their new homes, which were provided free-of-charge and fully furnished.[^cite_note-30]

### Emergency aid



&#x20;A Samaritan's Purse [Douglas DC-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-8) used for the emergency transport of basic necessities and aid workers

The organization's medical mission in [Liberia](17791.md), West Africa, was one of only two medical NGOs active in Liberia during the beginning of the [2014 Ebola outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola_virus_epidemic_in_West_Africa). Samaritan's Purse and SIM USA both have been actively engaged in treating the outbreak of [Ebola](40817590.md) hemorrhagic fever in Liberia. On August 1, 2014, the organization announced that it was evacuating 60 nonessential personnel from Liberia. [Dr. Kent Brantly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_Brantly), a Texas-based doctor working for the organization, was the first U.S. citizen to contract the Ebola virus in Liberia while treating the disease. He arrived in the United States on Saturday, August 2, and was treated and subsequently released after nearly three weeks in a special isolation unit of [Emory University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University_Hospital) in [Atlanta](3138.md), Georgia.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] The organization recorded their mission in Liberia in the documentary *Facing Darkness*. Samaritan's Purse has a fleet of 24 aircraft (22 planes and 2 helicopters) for the emergency transport of basic necessities and aid workers.[^cite_note-35]

The organization launched hygiene and public awareness campaigns about prevention and diagnosis and airlifted nearly 200 tons of relief supplies to the country. Samaritan's Purse also responded to the large Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo in 2018. During that crisis, the organization set up a 56-bed emergency [field hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hospital) in Komanda where teams of Samaritan's Purse doctors and nurses treated nearly 600 patients who were in desperate need of care.[^cite_note-36]

### Ukraine aid

According to the Ukrainian government, Samaritan's Purse has been operating in Ukraine through Operation Christmas Child since 1996.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] Samaritan's Purse began its full response in Ukraine in February 2022 when [Russia's full-scale invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) began. Since the start of the war, the organization, both directly and through local partners, has been delivering food, water, warm clothing, shelter, winter heating solutions and medical assistance. More than 10 million Ukrainians have benefited from this aid. The organization has established cooperation with 2,000 Ukrainian partners.[^cite_note-auto2-39][^cite_note-auto-40][^cite_note-41]

The organization stepped efforts to aid Ukraine in the midst of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in 2022. The first mission staff arrived in Ukraine in February 2022. During the initial phase of the war, a DC-8 aircraft transported up to 84,000 pounds of aid, which was then delivered to Ukraine through Poland. The organization's warehouse in North Wilkesboro, North Carolina, was used for the aid distribution.[^cite_note-auto3-42] By December 2022, the organization had distributed 100 million pounds (45,000 metric tons) of food to those affected by the war in Ukraine.[^cite_note-43] The organization's aircraft was also used for evacuating Ukrainian refugees. In May 2022, 28 Ukrainians were flown to Canada.[^cite_note-44]

The organization donated various supplies especially for the youth in schools such as medication, food, supplies, blankets, tarps, and bags. The donations were made through various methods of transportation such as semi trucks being loaded with roughly 80,000 donation goods, being transferred at Piedmont Triad International Airport on the DC-8 airplane, and then being air lifted to Poland where the goods are transported across the border to Ukraine. The organization accomplished its 30th airlift donation in the month of September having started in February, equating to roughly 4 airlifts per month. It is estimated that 5.5 million Ukrainians were aided due to the efforts with food, water, and supplies. Furthermore, the organization provided roughly 30 emergency field support hospitals in Lviv with the result of aiding nearly 18,000 patients.[^cite_note-45]

The deployment of the emergency field hospital in Ukraine began on March 3, 2022.[^cite_note-auto-40] The hospital was located in an underground parking lot of a shopping centre in Lviv.[^cite_note-46] In September 2022, it was reported that the organization supported 30 medical facilities across Ukraine.[^cite_note-auto3-42] According to Ukraine's Ministry of Health, more than 33,700 outpatient consultations were provided and over 260 surgeries were performed in the field hospitals set up with Samaritan's Purse support. Additionally, 253 metric tons of medical supplies were delivered to 164 medical facilities in Ukraine.[^cite_note-auto2-39] In December 2022, generators were provided for hospitals in the Kherson region, as well as for the so-called points of invincibility[^cite_note-47] across the region.[^cite_note-48] Samaritan's Purse also trained 18,000 local Ukrainian medical professionals.[^cite_note-49]

To further support Ukraine, in March 2023, Samaritan's Purse and its Ukrainian subsidiary, the Samaritan's Purse Ukraine Charitable Foundation, signed a five-party [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) and cooperation with Ukrainian government structures, including the Ministry of Social Policy, the Ministry of Health, and the Ministry for Reintegration of Temporarily Occupied Territories.[^cite_note-auto2-39][^cite_note-auto-40]

### Israel and Gaza aid

The organization provided support to those affected by the [Gaza war](74998519.md). In November 2023, the President of Samaritan's Purse, Franklin Graham, visited Israel to assess the damage from the 7 October Hamas-led attack on Israel and met with Israeli Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) in Tel Aviv.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-auto1-51] The organization provided hygiene kits, hot meals, food boxes, and vouchers for internally displaced people in Israel. Also, rescue teams were provided with medical equipment, including defibrillators and 1,000 trauma kits for rapid emergency care. Hot meals and food were delivered in partnership with 50 local churches.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-auto4-53] During his visit, Franklin Graham suggested that [Magen David Adom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magen_David_Adom), Israel's emergency services system, equivalent to the Red Cross first responders, needed a small fleet of armored ambulances.[^cite_note-auto1-51] As of September 2024, Samaritan's Purse is donating 42 ambulances to Magen David Adom.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-auto4-53] The organization was also working to support two Christian communities in Gaza by coordinating evacuation routes for them.[^cite_note-auto1-51] In July 2025, Samaritan's Purse began airlifting food to Gaza, sending 65 tons of food for starving Palestinians on three air missions. [^cite_note-55]

## Financials

The organization's 2021 financial statement listed \$758 million in cash donations and another \$245 million in donated goods and services. 85% of its \$676 million in expenses went to ministry expenses with the largest share (42%) going to their Operation Christmas Child project, 17% to emergency relief, and 7% to its medical missions. Most expenses come from direct costs in delivering in emergencies, medical relief, and Operation Christmas Child (57%) along with staff salaries and other employment expenses (20%).[^cite_note-\:3-1] In 2024, Samaritan's Purse had a total revenue of \$1.2 billion, and reported that 84.66% of its expenses went to ministry work, 6.88% went toward general and administrative costs, and 8.6% went toward fundraising.[^cite_note-70] [Financial Accountability](https://www.samaritanspurse.org/our-ministry/financial-accountability/)[^cite_note-71]

The organization has received a 4 star rating (out of 4 stars) from the monitoring organization [Charity Navigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charity_Navigator).[^cite_note-72]

Samaritan's Purse has received \$350 million in US federal grants since 2016, though as of 2025 that funding accounts for less than 5% of its revenue.[^cite_note-AP,_Christian_aid_groups,_Feb_12,_2025-73] In 2020, it declined federal funding from the [Trump administration](52231341.md) originally withheld from the [World Health Organization](33583.md).[^cite_note-74] In 2025, the Trump administration made significant cuts to [USAID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAID), halting a range of disaster relief, global health, and other foreign humanitarian assistance. Samaritan's Purse receives such assistance, including money spent on medicine and hunger relief in Africa. Franklin Graham said he believed his organization would be exempted from the cuts and supported efforts to "assess and reexamine" aid programs.[^cite_note-AP,_Christian_aid_groups,_Feb_12,_2025-73][^cite_note-WSOC,_Samaritan's_Purse_continues,_Feb_10,_2025-75]

## Controversy

The organization has been criticized for requiring staff to sign a controversial Statement of Faith which rejects [homosexuality](5488304.md) and [same-sex marriage](92656.md).[^cite_note-\:2-78] This criticism arose when Samaritan's Purse set up an Emergency Field Hospital in New York City during COVID-19. The organization's board of directors, which includes Franklin Graham's son, Edward Bell Graham, has also been criticized for the \$622,000 yearly salary paid to Franklin Graham (as of 2015), which was 40–50% more than the salary paid to the heads of similar non-profit organizations.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] In 2024, The Charlotte Observer published a report comparing the 15 largest nonprofits in North Carolina. Samaritan's Purse had one of the highest revenues in North Carolina and Franklin Graham brought in the lowest salary out of all of the highest earners.[^cite_note-93]

In March 2001, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that Samaritan's Purse had "blurred the line between church and state" in the way it had distributed publicly funded aid to victims of the [January 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2001_El_Salvador_earthquake) and [February 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2001_El_Salvador_earthquake) El Salvador earthquakes. Residents from several villages stated they first had to sit through a half-hour prayer meeting before receiving assistance.[^cite_note-94] In a statement, [USAID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAID) said Samaritan's Purse had not violated federal guidelines, but emphasized the need for the organization to "maintain adequate and sufficient separation" between prayer sessions and publicly funded activities.[^cite_note-95] Franklin Graham has said that Samaritan's Purse does not force religion on others, even in privately funded ministry work where government grants are not involved. In 2020, he told The New York Times that the organization's staff "would not force themselves on somebody and try to force our religion on somebody or what we believe. We don't do that." In 2025, Graham told [Christianity Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_Today), "Like the biblical story of the Good Samaritan, we help people in the ditches of life regardless of their beliefs or background—without asking for anything in return."[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

In 2003, Islamic leaders criticized Samaritan's Purse within the United Kingdom after its president, Franklin Graham, called [Islam](6037917.md) a "very evil and wicked religion",[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-guardian-99] leading to opposition campaigns by the Islamic leaders.[^cite_note-100] Samaritan's Purse responded to accusations of being anti-Islamic by highlighting their long history of non-denominational co-operation and charity work in throughout the Muslim world without attempting to preach or proselytize.[^cite_note-101] Franklin Graham told the [Baptist Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist_Press), "As the leader of an international Christian relief organization, my calling is to provide food, clothing, housing, supplies and medical care to hurting people everywhere regardless of faith. Samaritan's Purse has provided more relief and aid to Muslim people than to any other people in the world."[^cite_note-102]

The Operation Christmas Child project has been criticized in several countries, most notably in the UK,[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] but also in Ireland,[^cite_note-105] India[^cite_note-106] and Canada.[^cite_note-107] In the United States, Ibrahim Hooper of the Council on American-Islamic Relations has stated that such religion-and-relief groups are "using their position of power to try to persuade people to leave their faith."[^cite_note-108] In 2003, the British supermarket chain [Co-op](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Co-operative_Group) and [South Wales Fire Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Wales_Fire_and_Rescue_Service) both suspended their support for the project after numerous complaints about its religious connections.[^cite_note-guardian-99][^cite_note-bbcshoe-109] Samaritan's Purse responded by stating that Christian literature was only handed out where its staff "deemed it appropriate".[^cite_note-bbcshoe-109]

Franklin Graham drew scrutiny in 2009 for getting a full-time salary from Samaritan's Purse, while at the same time receiving a full-time salary from [Billy Graham Evangelistic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Graham_Evangelistic_Association) (BGEA). This was called into question after his 2008 compensation from both organizations totaled \$1.2 million (most of this was the result of a new IRS rule that required him to re-report deferred retirement contributions that had already been reported over the previous three years).[^cite_note-110] Some experts on non-profits have questioned whether one person can perform two full-time jobs leading organizations that employ hundreds and spend hundreds of millions around the world.[^cite_note-111] In response to the questions about his compensation, Graham decided to give up his salary from BGEA, stating his calling to the ministry "was never based on compensation." He also had contributions to his retirement plans suspended until the economy bounced back.[^cite_note-112] However, Graham was again criticized in 2015 when it was revealed he had again taken up his salary from BGEA, and that his annual compensation was significantly higher than that of the CEO's of similar but much larger non-profit organisations.[^cite_note-113]

In 2010, an American woman and two Sudanese men were kidnapped while working for Samaritan's Purse in [Sudan](27421.md). The two men were released promptly, but the woman was held for three months. Upon her return to the US, she sued Samaritan's Purse and their security contractor, Clayton Consultants, a hostage negotiation consultancy owned by [Triple Canopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Canopy), accusing the organization "of failing to train its security personnel adequately and of willfully ignoring warning signs that abductions were a threat to foreigners." The organization settled out of court in March 2012.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

In May 2013, Franklin Graham wrote a letter to President Obama stating his concern that the [IRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRS) targeted Samaritan's Purse prior to the [2012 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_presidential_election) with a partisan audit.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

In August 2013, [Thankyou Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thankyou_\(company\)) announced that it would no longer support Samaritan's Purse because it is not a signatory to the code of conduct run by the [Australian Council for International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Council_for_International_Development), which bans aid as a vehicle for promoting religion or political groups.[^cite_note-tsmh-118]

## Response to the COVID-19 outbreak

### Italy

On March 17, 2020, Samaritan's Purse dispatched over 105 disaster response specialists, as well as two airlifts carrying 38 tons of medical equipment and a field hospital to [Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremona), [Italy](14532.md) which started operations on March 20, 2020. The organization treated more than 280 patients, and the city council later made Samaritan's Purse honorary citizens.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121]

### Alaska

Samaritans' Purse airlifted 8 tons of medical supplies to [Alaska](624.md) on April 7, 2020, to help provide supplies to remote communities.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

### New York

In cooperation with [New York City](645042.md)'s [Mount Sinai Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Sinai_Hospital_\(Manhattan\)), Samaritan's Purse constructed a 14 tent, 68-bed [field hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hospital) in [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park) on March 29, 2020, to increase Mount Sinai's surge capacity.[^cite_note-USATODAY1-125][^cite_note-BUSINESSINSIDER1-126][^cite_note-127] Through April, over 315 people were treated there. By early May, all patients had been discharged, and there were plans to dismantle the tents.[^cite_note-\:1-128][^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130]

#### Criticism

Before the field hospital opened, journalists, politicians and LGBTQ activists raised concerns that it was only recruiting Christian medical staff and that it would provide inadequate and discriminatory care.[^cite_note-Independent1-131][^cite_note-Independent2-132][^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135][^cite_note-NYDailyNews1-136] Volunteers are required to adhere to a [statement of faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed), which includes, among other points, a definition of marriage as "exclusively the union of one genetic male and one genetic female" A separate part of the statement affirms that "God will banish the unrighteous to everlasting punishment in hell."[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-\:0-138][^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] Both of these reflect long-standing evangelical Christian beliefs, though critics argued that their inclusion raised concerns about potential discrimination in practice. New York Mayor [Bill de Blasio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_de_Blasio) stated that the presence of Samaritan's Purse was "very troubling," while New York State Senator [Brad Hoylman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Hoylman) told *NBC News* that he considered it "a shame that the federal government has left us in the position of having to accept charity from such bigots".[^cite_note-Independent2-132][^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142][^cite_note-\:0-138] Franklin Graham later responded to Hoylman's request for public reassurance by stating: "We do not make distinctions about an individual's religion, race, sexual orientation, or economic status. We certainly do not discriminate, and we have a decades-long track record that confirms just that."[^cite_note-USATODAY1-125][^cite_note-NYDailyNews1-136][^cite_note-143] Mount Sinai Health System also affirmed that Samaritan's Purse adhered to its own non-discrimination policies, with a spokesperson noting that both organizations were "unified in our mission to provide the same world-class care to anyone and everyone who needs it. No questions asked."[^cite_note-auto6-144][^cite_note-145] New York City's Commission on Human Rights subsequently closed an investigation into the hospital after finding no evidence it had discriminated against patients.[^cite_note-146] The group's departure was hailed as a victory by LGBTQ rights activists.[^cite_note-147]

[Hedge fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_fund) manager [Whitney Tilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Tilson), who volunteered his time and money to help build the field hospital, said that though he found the positions of the group on sexuality "abhorrent", in his view, helping the Covid response was more important than protesting the group's ideologies.[^cite_note-covid-148] He said: "I'll have the ideological discussions with them later. If they're here saving my fellow New Yorkers' lives, and not even asking to be paid for it, then I will absolutely help them do that."[^cite_note-covid-148]

Joint plans between Mount Sinai Hospital, Samaritan's Purse and the [Episcopal Diocese of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Diocese_of_New_York) to convert the [Cathedral of St. John the Divine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_St._John_the_Divine) into a 200-bed hospital were shelved on April 9, 2020. Although this decision was attributed at least in part to the assessment that virus-related hospitalizations had already plateaued,[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] Bishop [Andrew M.L. Dietsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_M._L._Dietsche) of the Episcopal Diocese of New York later said that Graham's "exclusionary" and "narrow" attitude about Christianity was central to the decision. Specifically, Samaritan's Purse requires its employees to oppose gay marriage, which, in Dietsche's words, was incompatible with the work the New York Diocese had done "around the full inclusion of gay and lesbian people."[^cite_note-\:1-128] In a story detailing Franklin Graham's response to the controversy, The New York Times cited Graham as saying that Samaritan's Purse has never discriminated against a patient. He also said that the organization had a legal right to hire staff who share their Christian beliefs because it is a religious charity.[^cite_note-auto6-144]
