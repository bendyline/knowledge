***Science*** is the [peer-reviewed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review) [academic journal](324572.md) of the [American Association for the Advancement of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Science)[^cite_note-2007_PR_Communication_and_Humanities_Award-2][^cite_note-3] (AAAS) and one of the world's top academic journals.[^cite_note-Lemonick-4] It was first published in 1880, is currently circulated weekly and has a subscriber base of around 130,000. Because institutional subscriptions and online access serve a larger audience, its estimated readership is over 400,000 people.[^cite_note-5]

*Science* is based in the [District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Columbia), United States, with a second office in [Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge), England, United Kingdom.

## Contents

The major focus of the journal is publishing important original scientific research and research reviews, but *Science* also publishes science-related news, opinions on [science policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_policy) and other matters of interest to scientists and others who are concerned with the wide implications of science and technology. Unlike most [scientific journals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_journals), which focus on a specific field, *Science* and its rival *[Nature](43427.md)* cover the full range of [scientific disciplines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_academic_disciplines#Natural_sciences). According to the *[Journal Citation Reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_Citation_Reports)*, *Science*'s 2024 [impact factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_factor) was 45.8.[^cite_note-WoS-6]

Studies of methodological quality and reliability have found that some high-prestige journals including *Science* "publish significantly substandard structures", and overall "reliability of published research works in several fields may be decreasing with increasing journal rank".[^cite_note-Brembs2018-7]

Although it is the journal of the AAAS, membership in the AAAS is not required to publish in *Science*. Papers are accepted from authors around the world. Competition to publish in *Science* is very intense, as an article published in such a highly cited journal can lead to attention and career advancement for the authors. Fewer than 7% of articles submitted are accepted for publication.[^cite_note-8]

## History

Title page of the first volume of the resurrected journal (February–June 1883)

Title page of the first volume of the resurrected journal (February–June 1883)

*Science* was founded by English-born New York journalist [John Michels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Michels_\(editor\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1880[^cite_note-DCP-9] with financial support from [Thomas Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Edison) and later from [Alexander Graham Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Graham_Bell).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] (Edison received favorable editorial treatment in return, without disclosure of the financial relationship, at a time when his reputation was suffering due to delays producing the promised commercially viable light bulb.)[^cite_note-12] However, the journal never gained enough subscribers to succeed and ended publication in March 1882. Alexander Graham Bell and Gardiner Greene Hubbard bought the magazine rights and hired young entomologist [Samuel H. Scudder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Hubbard_Scudder) to resurrect the journal one year later. They had some success while covering the meetings of prominent American scientific societies, including the AAAS.[^cite_note-AAAS_1848_1899-13] However, by 1894, *Science* was again in financial difficulty and was sold to psychologist [James McKeen Cattell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_McKeen_Cattell) for \$500 (equivalent to \$18,610 in 2025).

In an agreement worked out by Cattell and AAAS secretary [Leland O. Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leland_Ossian_Howard), *Science* became the journal of the American Association for the Advancement of Science in 1900.[^cite_note-AAAS_1900_1940-14] During the early part of the 20th century, important articles published in *Science* included papers on [fruit fly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drosophila_melanogaster) [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics) by [Thomas Hunt Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hunt_Morgan), [gravitational lensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_lens) by [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein), and [spiral nebulae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiral_nebulae) by [Edwin Hubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Hubble).[^cite_note-AAAS_1900_1940-14] After Cattell died in 1944, the ownership of the journal was transferred to the AAAS.[^cite_note-150_Years-15]

After Cattell's death in 1944, the journal lacked a consistent editorial presence until Graham DuShane became editor in 1956. In 1958, under DuShane's leadership, *Science* absorbed *[The Scientific Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scientific_Monthly)*, thus increasing the journal's circulation by over 62% from 38,000 to more than 61,000.[^cite_note-maturing3-16] Physicist [Philip Abelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Abelson), a co-discoverer of [neptunium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptunium), served as editor from 1962 to 1984. Under Abelson the efficiency of the review process was improved and the publication practices were brought up to date.[^cite_note-maturing3-16] During this time, papers on the [Apollo program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program) missions and some of the earliest reports on AIDS were published.[^cite_note-change3-17]

Biochemist [Daniel E. Koshland Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_E._Koshland_Jr.) served as editor from 1985 until 1995. From 1995 until 2000, neuroscientist [Floyd E. Bloom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floyd_E._Bloom) held that position.[^cite_note-change3-17] Biologist [Donald Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Kennedy) became the editor of *Science* in 2000. Biochemist [Bruce Alberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Alberts) took his place in March 2008.[^cite_note-18] Geophysicist [Marcia McNutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcia_McNutt) became editor-in-chief in June 2013.[^cite_note-19] During her tenure the family of journals expanded to include *Science Robotics* and *Science Immunology*,[^cite_note-20] and [open access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_access) publishing with *[Science Advances](45385909.md)*.[^cite_note-21] [Jeremy M. Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_M._Berg) became editor-in-chief on July 1, 2016.[^cite_note-22] Former [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis) Provost [Holden Thorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden_Thorp) was named editor-in-chief on Monday, August 19, 2019.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

In February 2001, draft results of the [human genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_genome) were simultaneously published by *Nature* and *Science* with *Science* publishing the [Celera Genomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celera_Corporation) paper and *Nature* publishing the publicly funded [Human Genome Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genome_Project). In 2007, *Science&#x20;*(together with [*Nature*](43427.md)) received the [Prince of Asturias Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Asturias_Awards) for Communications and Humanity.[^cite_note-25] In 2015, [Rush D. Holt Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush_Holt_Jr.), chief executive officer of the AAAS and executive publisher of *Science*, stated that the journal was becoming increasingly international: "\[I]nternationally co-authored papers are now the norm—they represent almost 60 percent of the papers. In 1992, it was slightly less than 20 percent."[^cite_note-Science_and_Diplomacy.-26]

## Availability

See also: [Nova ScienceNow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_ScienceNow)

The latest editions of the journal are available online, through the main journal website, only to subscribers, AAAS members, and for delivery to [IP addresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_address) at institutions that subscribe; students, K–12 teachers, and some others can subscribe at a reduced fee. However, research articles published after 1997 are available free (with online registration) one year after they are published i.e. [delayed open access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delayed_open_access).[^cite_note-delayed_oa-1] Significant public-health related articles are also available free, sometimes immediately after publication. AAAS members may also access the pre-1997 Science archives at the *Science* website, where it is called "Science Classic".

The journal also participates in initiatives that provide free or low-cost access to readers in developing countries, including [HINARI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HINARI), OARE, [AGORA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGORA), and [Scidev.net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scidev.net).

Other features of the *Science* website include the free "**ScienceNow**" section with "up to the minute news from science",[^cite_note-27] and "**ScienceCareers**", which provides free career resources for scientists and engineers. ***Science Express*** (**Sciencexpress**) provides advance electronic publication of selected *Science* papers.[^cite_note-Science_Express-28]
