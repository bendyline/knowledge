The **Palestinian Civil Police Force** (**PCP**; [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الشرطة المدنية الفلسطينية, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *_al-Shurtah al-Madaniyah al-Filistiniyah_*), commonly known as the **Palestinian Police**, is the [Civil Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police) organization tasked with traditional [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement) duties in the territory governed by the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md). The Civil Police is a part of the [Palestinian Security Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Security_Services).&#x20;

## History

The Civil Police was formally established with the May 1994 signing of the [Gaza–Jericho Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza%E2%80%93Jericho_Agreement), a chapter in the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords) process, under the umbrella of the [General Security Service](311752.md). Founded with over 10,000 officers, it was the largest substituent of the Palestinian National Security Forces.[^cite_note-globalsecurity.org-1] The agreement called for the civil police maintaining public order from 25 stations throughout the Palestinian-administered parts of the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) and the [West Bank](33209.md).[^cite_note-mfa-2]

Long-time Police head [Ghazi al-Jabali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazi_al-Jabali) was criticised for corruption and curbing press and civil rights freedoms. In 2004, he was kidnapped by the Jenin Martyrs Brigades, part of the [Popular Resistance Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Resistance_Committees), and only released after Palestinian President [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat) agreed to fire him. Al-Jabali was replaced with Arafat's cousin, Musa Arafat, a move which did little to restore public confidence in Police.[^cite_note-globalsecurity.org-1][^cite_note-3]

According to the [International Crisis Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Crisis_Group), Palestinian police and security forces succeeded in effectively "preventing, prosecuting and reducing crime" in the late 1990s.&#x20;

During the course of the [Second Intifada](71717.md) and its armed conflict, Palestinian police were unable to patrol armed or in uniform, lest they be engaged by [Israeli security forces](315904.md) as combatants. As the armed conflict petered out after three years, Palestinian police forces returned to regular operations in 2003–2004.[^cite_note-icg-4]

### Gaza Strip

Main article: [Palestinian Civil Police Force (Hamas)](81695807.md)

Since the [Hamas takeover of the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gaza_\(2007\)) the control of the police in [Gaza Strip](12047.md) came under the control of [Hamas](13913.md), while police forces in the West Bank remain under the Palestinian National Authority.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

As a result of the 2025 [Gaza peace plan](81215079.md), responsibility for policing in the [Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip_under_Resolution_2803) is to be assumed by a [Gaza civil police](81634311.md) force.

## Development

In order to aid the Palestinian government in "establishing sustainable and effective civil policing arrangements", the [European Union Co-ordinating Office for Palestinian Police Support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Police_Mission_for_the_Palestinian_Territories), EUPOLCOPPS, was established beginning 2006.

The Palestinian Civil Police development includes creation of a Jericho Police Training School,[^cite_note-7] and strengthening of investigative sectors like Crime Investigation Department, Anti-Narcotics General Administration, and Investigation Section.[^cite_note-8]

The EUCOPPS mission has facilitated the training of Civil Police personnel as well as the donation of equipment including vehicles from European donor-states.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

A Palestinian [Local Aid Coordination Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Aid_Coordination_Secretariat?action=edit\&redlink=1) strategic report on the Civil Police lists among the force's strengths its leadership's youth and academic credentials, high loyalty and commitment to regulations and motivation, while weaknesses included poorly or undefined legal frameworks and logistical shortages, especially communications equipment and transportation.[^cite_note-11]

Since 2007, the Palestinian Civil Police and the [Israel Police](745595.md) have increased cooperation as [confidence-building measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence-building_measures) and a part of [Palestinian institution-building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salam_Fayyad#Fayyadism:_Reform_plans). Since their jurisdictions are intertwined, cooperative tactics include having Israel police issue [traffic citations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_citation) to Palestinian drivers and repatriating the proceeds to the Palestinian Civil Police.[^cite_note-12]
