The ***Nimitz* class** is a [class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_class) of ten [nuclear-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_marine_propulsion) [aircraft carriers](2219.md) [in service with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_United_States_Navy_carrier_air_operations) the [United States Navy](20518076.md). The [lead ship](855210.md) of the class is named after [World War II](32927.md) [United States Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Pacific_Fleet) commander [Fleet Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_admiral_\(United_States\)) [Chester W. Nimitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_W._Nimitz), who was the last living U.S. Navy officer to hold the rank. With an overall length of 1,092 ft (333 m) and a full-load [displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_\(ship\)) of over 100,000 long tons (100,000 t),[^cite_note-Polmar-3] the *Nimitz*-class ships were the largest warships built and in service until [USS *Gerald R. Ford*](4050542.md) entered the fleet in 2017.[^cite_note-9]

Instead of the gas turbines or [diesel–electric systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel%E2%80%93electric_powertrain#Ships) used for propulsion on many modern warships, the carriers use two [A4W](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A4W_reactor) nuclear [pressurized water reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressurized_water_reactor). The reactors produce steam to drive steam turbines which drive four propeller shafts and can produce a maximum speed of over 30 [knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_\(unit\)) (56 km/h; 35 mph) and a maximum power of around 260,000 [shaft horsepower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsepower#Shaft_horsepower) (190 MW). As a result of nuclear power, the ships are capable of operating for over 20 years without refueling and are predicted to have a service life of over 50 years. They are categorized as nuclear-powered aircraft carriers and are numbered with consecutive [hull numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_number) from CVN-68 to CVN-77.[^cite_note-10]

All ten carriers were constructed by [Newport News Shipbuilding Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_Newport_News) in [Virginia](32432.md). [USS *Nimitz*](186973.md), the [lead ship](855210.md) of the class, was [commissioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_commissioning) on 3 May 1975, and [USS *George H. W. Bush*](156573.md), the tenth and last of the class, was commissioned on 10 January 2009. Since the 1970s, *Nimitz*-class carriers have participated in many conflicts and operations across the world, including [Operation Eagle Claw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Eagle_Claw) in Iran, the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), and more recently in [Iraq](5043324.md) and [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_\(2001%E2%80%93present\)).

The angled [flight decks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_deck) of the carriers use a [CATOBAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CATOBAR) arrangement to operate aircraft, with [steam catapults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_catapult) and [arrestor wires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arresting_gear) for launch and recovery. As well as speeding up flight deck operations, this allows for a much wider variety of aircraft than with the [STOVL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STOVL) arrangement used on smaller carriers. An embarked [carrier air wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_air_wing) comprising around 64 aircraft is normally deployed on board. The air wings' [strike fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strike_fighter) are primarily [F/A-18E and F/A-18F Super Hornets](845012.md). In addition to their aircraft, the vessels carry short-range defensive weaponry for [anti-aircraft warfare](146640.md) and missile defense.

The unit cost was about US\$8.5 billion in [FY](5752099.md#United_States) 2012 dollars,[^cite_note-Navy.mil_CVN-1] equal to US\$11.4 billion in 2024.[^cite_note-inflation-USGDP-2]

## Description

The *Nimitz*-class aircraft carriers have a length of 1,092 ft (333 m) overall and 1,040 ft (317 m) at the waterline, with a [beam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beam_\(nautical\)) of 252 ft (77 m) overall and 134 ft (41 m) at the waterline; the individual ships have slight variations in their dimensions.[^cite_note-Navy.mil_CVN-1][^cite_note-NVR_Nimitz-11] They were initially designed with a full-load displacement of 87,000 long tons (88,000 t; 97,000 short tons) and a draft of 37 ft (11 m), but the ships would be delivered several thousand tons heavier, particularly for later members of the class.[^cite_note-Navy.mil_CVN-1] As the vessels were overhauled and installed more equipment, loaded displacement would climb to exceed 100,000 long tons (100,000 t; 110,000 short tons). For example, [USS *Abraham Lincoln*](199106.md) currently displaces 104,112 long tons (105,783 t; 116,605 short tons) at full load.[^cite_note-12] The ships' nominal complement comprises: 3,000–3,200; 1,500 (air wing); and 500 (other).[^cite_note-Navy.mil_CVN-1]

### Design

The *Nimitz*-class aircraft carriers were ordered to supplement the aircraft carriers of the [*Midway*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midway-class_aircraft_carrier), [*Forrestal*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forrestal-class_aircraft_carrier), [*Kitty Hawk*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitty_Hawk-class_aircraft_carrier) and [*Enterprise* classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise-class_aircraft_carrier), maintaining the strength and capability of the U.S. Navy after the older carriers were decommissioned.[^cite_note-CVN_21-13] The ships were designed to be improvements on previous [U.S. aircraft carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_aircraft_carriers_of_the_United_States_Navy), particularly the *[Enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Enterprise_\(CVN-65\))* and [*Forrestal*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forrestal-class_aircraft_carrier) supercarriers, although the arrangement of the vessels is relatively similar to that of the *Kitty Hawk* class.[^cite_note-Polmar_113-14] Among other design improvements, the two reactors on *Nimitz*-class carriers take up less space than the eight reactors used on *Enterprise*. Along with a more generally improved design, *Nimitz*-class carriers can carry 90% more aviation fuel and 50% more ordnance when compared to the *Forrestal* class.[^cite_note-15]

The U.S. Navy has stated that the carriers could withstand three times the damage sustained by the [*Essex* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex-class_aircraft_carrier) inflicted by Japanese air attacks during [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-SE-8] The hangars on the ships are divided into three fire bays by thick steel doors that are designed to restrict the spread of fire. This addition has been present on U.S. aircraft carriers since World War II, after the fires caused by [kamikaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamikaze) attacks.[^cite_note-Fire_Protection-16]

The first ships were designed around the time of the [Vietnam War](32611.md), and certain aspects of the design were influenced by operations there. To a certain extent, the carrier operations in Vietnam demonstrated the need for increased capabilities of aircraft carriers over their survivability; they were used to send sorties into the war and were, therefore, less subject to attack. As a result of this experience, *Nimitz*-class carriers were designed with larger stores of aviation fuel and larger [magazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magazine_\(artillery\)#Naval_magazines) compared to previous carriers, although this was partly a result of increased space available by the new design of the ships' propulsion systems.[^cite_note-Friedman_p316-17]

A major purpose of the carriers was initially to support the U.S. military during the [Cold War](325329.md). They were designed with capabilities for that role, including using nuclear power instead of oil for greater endurance and the ability to adjust their weapons systems on the basis of new intelligence and technological developments.[^cite_note-18] They were initially categorized only as attack carriers, but ships have been constructed with [anti-submarine capabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) since [USS *Carl Vinson*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Carl_Vinson).[^cite_note-ASW-19][^cite_note-20] As a result, the ships and their aircraft can participate in a wide range of operations, including sea and air blockades; [mine](22102.md) laying; and missile strikes on land, air, and sea.[^cite_note-Cold_war_ship-21]

Because of a design flaw, ships of this class have inherent [lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_\(watercraft\)) to starboard when under combat loads that exceed the capability of their list control systems. The problem appears to be especially prevalent on some of the more modern vessels. This problem has been previously rectified by using damage control voids for ballast, but a solution using solid ballast that does not affect the ship's survivability has been proposed.[^cite_note-list-22][^cite_note-Navy.mil_CVN-1]

### Construction

All ten *Nimitz*-class carriers were constructed between 1968 and 2006 at Newport News Shipbuilding in Newport News, Virginia. The first three units of the class were erected in Dry Dock 11, the other seven ships were constructed in the largest [dry dock](213739.md) in the western hemisphere, Dry Dock 12, now 2,172 feet (662 m) long after a recent expansion.

Beginning with [USS *Theodore Roosevelt*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Theodore_Roosevelt_\(CVN-71\)), the aircraft carriers were manufactured with modular construction. This means that whole sections could be welded together with plumbing and electrical equipment already fitted, improving efficiency. The modules were lifted into the dry dock using [gantry cranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gantry_cranes) and welded. In the case of the bow sections, these can weigh over 1,500,000 pounds (680 t).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] This method was originally developed by [Ingalls Shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingalls_Shipbuilding) and increases the rate of work because much of the fitting out does not have to be carried out within the confines of the already-finished hull.[^cite_note-25]

The total cost of construction for each ship was around \$4.5 billion.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-CVN_21-13]

### Propulsion

See also: [Robert McNamara as Secretary of Defense § Naval aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_McNamara_as_Secretary_of_Defense#Naval_aviation)

Shipyard employees reattaching the bronze propeller of USS George Washington while in dry dock

One of the four propellers of *George Washington*

All ships of the class are powered by two [A4W nuclear reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A4W_reactor), housed in separate compartments. The reactors produce heat through [nuclear fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fission), which heats water to produce steam. This is then passed through four turbines, which are shared by the two reactors. A [gearbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_\(mechanics\)) transmits power to four propeller shafts, producing a maximum speed of over 30 knots (56 km/h) and maximum power of 260,000 [brake horsepower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsepower#Brake_horsepower) (194 MW).[^cite_note-SE-8]  The turbines power the four [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze) propellers, each with a diameter of 25 feet (7.6 m) and a weight of 66,000 pounds (30 t). Behind these are the two rudders, which are 29 feet (8.8 m) high and 22 feet (6.7 m) long, and each weighs 110,000 pounds (50 metric tons).[^cite_note-USN_Info-27]

The *Nimitz*-class ships constructed since [USS *Ronald Reagan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Ronald_Reagan) also have [bulbous bows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulbous_bow) to improve speed and [fuel efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_efficiency) by reducing [wave-making resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave-making_resistance).[^cite_note-28] As a result of nuclear power, the ships are capable of operating continuously for over 20 years without refueling and are predicted to have a service life of over 50 years.[^cite_note-USN_Info-27]

### Armament and protection

Firing of a missile from Theodore Roosevelt while at sea, seen from the flight deck. There is a Phalanx cannon on the left, with a white, domed upper section and a black cannon on the lower part.

The firing of a [Sea Sparrow missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-7_Sea_Sparrow) from *Theodore Roosevelt*. A [Phalanx CIWS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalanx_CIWS) is in the left of the image.



The counter-measure wash-down system of *Ronald Reagan* being tested. This is part of the ship's [defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBRN) against chemical, biological or radiological threats, and also against fires

In addition to the aircraft carried on board, the ships carry defensive equipment for use against missiles and hostile aircraft. These consist of either two or three [RIM-7 Sea Sparrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-7_Sea_Sparrow) or [RIM-162 Evolved SeaSparrow Missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-162_ESSM) Mk 29 missile launchers designed for defense against aircraft and [anti-ship missiles](140725.md), as well as either three or four 20 mm [Phalanx CIWS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalanx_CIWS).[^cite_note-29]

USS *Ronald Reagan* has none of these, having been built with the Mk 49 Guided Missile Launching Systems for [RIM-116 Rolling Airframe Missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIM-116_Rolling_Airframe_Missile), two of which have also been installed on [USS *Nimitz*](186973.md) and [USS *George Washington*](198834.md). These will be installed on the other ships as they return for [Refueling Complex Overhaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refueling_Complex_Overhaul) (RCOH).[^cite_note-SE-8][^cite_note-Navy.mil_CVN-1] Since USS *Theodore Roosevelt*, the carriers have been constructed with 2.5 in (64 mm) [Kevlar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevlar) armor over vital spaces, and earlier ships have been retrofitted with it: *Nimitz* in 1983–1984, *Dwight D. Eisenhower* from 1985 to 1987 and *Carl Vinson* in 1989.[^cite_note-Fontenoy-7][^cite_note-30]

The ships' other countermeasures are four Sippican [SRBOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SRBOC) (super rapid bloom off-board [chaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaff_\(countermeasure\))) six-barrel Mk 36 decoy launchers, which deploy infrared [flares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flare_\(countermeasure\)) and [chaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaff_\(countermeasure\)) to disrupt the sensors of incoming missiles; an SSTDS torpedo defense system; and an [AN/SLQ-25 Nixie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/SLQ-25_Nixie) torpedo countermeasures system. The carriers also use [AN/SLQ-32(V)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AN/SLQ-32_Electronic_Warfare_Suite) [jamming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_jamming_and_deception) systems to detect and disrupt hostile [radar](25676.md) signals in addition to the [electronic warfare](82272.md) capabilities of some of the aircraft on board.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

The presence of [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_nuclear_weapon) on board U.S. aircraft carriers since the end of the Cold War has neither been confirmed nor denied by the U.S. government. As a result, the presence of a U.S. aircraft carrier in a foreign port has occasionally provoked protest from local people, for example, when *Nimitz* visited [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai), India, in 2007. At that time, the Strike Group commander [Rear Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rear_admiral_\(United_States\)) John Terence Blake stated, "The U.S. policy \[...] is that we do not routinely deploy nuclear weapons on board *Nimitz*."[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

In May 2013, *George H. W. Bush* conducted the first carrier-borne end-to-end at-sea test of the Surface Ship Torpedo Defense System (SSTDS). The SSTDS combined the passive detection of the Torpedo Warning System (TWS) that finds, classifies, and tracks torpedoes with the hard-kill capability of a Countermeasure Anti-Torpedo (CAT), an encapsulated miniature torpedo designed to locate, home in on, and destroy hostile torpedoes.[^cite_note-35] This was to increase protection against wake-homing torpedoes like the [Type 53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_53_torpedo) that do not respond to acoustic decoys. The pieces of the SSTDS were engineered to locate and destroy incoming torpedoes in a matter of seconds; each system included one TWS and 8 CATs. Initial operational capability (IOC) was planned for 2019, and all aircraft carriers were to be outfitted by 2035.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] The Navy suspended work on the project in September 2018 due to poor reliability of the components; hardware, already installed on five carriers, is to be removed by 2023.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

### Carrier air wing

Main article: [Carrier air wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_air_wing)



An [F/A-18 Hornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18_Hornet) launches from the flight deck of *Harry S. Truman*. Other aircraft are stored on deck.

In order for a carrier to deploy, it must embark one of ten Carrier Air Wings (CVW).[^cite_note-41] The carriers can accommodate a maximum of 130 F/A-18 Hornets[^cite_note-42] or 85–90 aircraft of different types, but current numbers are typically 64 aircraft. Although the air wings are integrated with the operation of the carriers they are deployed to, they are regarded as separate entities. As well as the aircrew, the air wings are also made up of support personnel involved in roles including maintenance, aircraft and ordnance handling, and emergency procedures. Each person on the flight deck wears color-coded clothing to make their role easily identifiable.[^cite_note-43]

A typical carrier air wing includes 36-48 [F/A-18E or F Super Hornets](845012.md) as strike fighters, split between 3-4 squadrons; up to 12 [F-35Cs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35C), contributed by a squadron of Navy or Marine aviation; 4–8 [EA-18G Growlers](833647.md) for electronic warfare; 4–6 [E-2D Hawkeyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-2_Hawkeye) for [airborne early warning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_early_warning) (AEW), 1-3 [C-2 Greyhounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-2_Greyhound) used for logistics (to be replaced by [CMV-22 Ospreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV-22_Osprey)); and a Helicopter Anti-Submarine Squadron of 6–8 [MH-60R and MH-60S Seahawks and 'Knighthawks'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SH-60_Seahawk), respectively. Marine [F/A-18C or D Hornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18C/D) squadrons are also sometimes deployed in place of F/A-18E/F Super Hornet and/or F-35C squadrons. Future aircraft planned for operation from *Nimitz* and future classes of aircraft carrier include the [MQ-25 Stingray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MQ-25_Stingray), and two squadrons of F-35C, replacing a F/A-18E/F Super Hornet squadron. Aircraft previously operated from *Nimitz*-class carriers include [F-4 Phantoms](11759.md), [RA-5C Vigilantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-5_Vigilante), [RF-8G Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-8_Crusader), [F-14 Tomcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-14_Tomcat), [S-3 Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-3_Viking), [EA-3B Skywarriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA-3B_Skywarrior), [EA-6B Prowlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA-6B_Prowler), [A-7 Corsair II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-7_Corsair_II), and [A-6E Intruder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-6E_Intruder) aircraft.[^cite_note-44]

### Flight deck and aircraft facilities



The hangar of *George Washington* during a replenishment at sea, 2009

The [flight deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_deck) is angled at nine degrees, which allows for aircraft to be launched and recovered simultaneously. This angle of the flight deck was reduced slightly compared to previous carriers, as the current design improves the airflow around the carrier.[^cite_note-Friedman_p316-17] Four steam [catapults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_catapult) are used to launch fixed-wing aircraft, and four [arrestor wires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrestor_wire) are used for recovery. The two newest carriers, *Ronald Reagan* and *George H. W. Bush*, have only three arrestor wires each, as the fourth was used infrequently on earlier ships and was therefore deemed unnecessary.[^cite_note-45]

This [CATOBAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CATOBAR) arrangement allows for faster launching and recovery as well as a much wider range of aircraft that can be used on board compared with smaller aircraft carriers, most of which use a simpler [STOVL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STOVL) arrangement without catapults or arrestor wires. The ship's aircraft operations are controlled by the air boss from Primary Flight Control or Pri-Fly. Four large elevators transport aircraft between the flight deck and the hangars below. These hangars are divided into three bays by thick steel doors that are designed to restrict the spread of fire.[^cite_note-Fire_Protection-16]

### Strike groups

Main article: [Carrier strike group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier_strike_group)

Four warships sailing alongside George Washington

The *George Washington* carrier strike group in the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md), 2006

When an aircraft carrier deploys, it takes a Carrier Strike Group (CSG), made up of several other warships and supply vessels that allow the operation to be carried out. The armament of the *Nimitz* class is made up only of short-range defensive weapons, used as a last line of defense against enemy missiles and aircraft. As with all surface ships, an aircraft carrier is particularly vulnerable to attack from below, specifically from submarines. An aircraft carrier is a very expensive, hard to replace, and strategically valuable asset, and therefore it logically has immense value as a target.[^cite_note-Stevens,_p._24-46]

As a result of its target value and vulnerability, aircraft carriers are always escorted by at least one submarine for protection. The other vessels in the Strike Group provide additional capabilities, such as long-range [Tomahawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_\(missile_family\)) missiles or the [Aegis Combat System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegis_Combat_System), and protect the carrier from attack. A typical Strike Group may include, in addition to an aircraft carrier: up to six [surface combatants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_combatant), including [guided-missile cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided-missile_cruiser) and [guided-missile destroyers](232005.md), used primarily for [anti-aircraft warfare](146640.md) and anti-submarine warfare, and [frigates](11464.md)/[guided-missile frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided-missile_frigate), prior to their retirement from USN service. Guided missile frigates will again accompany CSGs when the Navy commissions the [USS Constellation (FFG-62)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Constellation_\(FFG-62\)), the lead ship of the [Constellation-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation-class_frigate). Also making up part of the group is one or two [attack submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_submarine) for seeking out and destroying hostile surface ships and submarines and an [ammunition, oiler, and supply ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_combat_support_ship) from [Military Sealift Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Sealift_Command) to provide logistical support.[^cite_note-Stevens,_p._24-46] The numbers and types of vessels that make up each strike group can vary from group to group, depending on deployments, mission, and availability.[^cite_note-47]

### Design differences within the class

While the designs of the last seven ships, beginning with *Theodore Roosevelt*, differ slightly from those of the earlier ships, the U.S. Navy considers all ten carriers a single class.[^cite_note-Navy.mil_CVN-1] When the older carriers come in for [Refueling and Complex Overhaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refueling_and_Complex_Overhaul) (RCOH), their nuclear power plants are refueled, and they are upgraded to the standards of the later carriers. Other modifications may be performed to update the ships' equipment.[^cite_note-Navy.mil_CVN-1][^cite_note-NorthropGrumman-Refueling_and_Complex_Overhaul-48]

The ships were initially classified only as attack carriers but have been constructed with anti-submarine capabilities since *Carl Vinson*. These improvements include more advanced radar systems and facilities enabling the ships to operate aircraft in a more effective anti-submarine warfare role, including fitting common undersea picture (CUP) technology, which uses [sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonar) to allow for better assessment of the threat from submarines.[^cite_note-ASW-19]

*Theodore Roosevelt* and later carriers have slight structural differences from the earlier *Nimitz* carriers, such as improved protection for ordnance stored in their [magazines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magazine_\(artillery\)). Other improvements include upgraded flight deck ballistic protection, first installed on *George Washington*, and the [high-strength low-alloy steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-strength_low-alloy_steel) (HSLA-100) used for constructing ships starting with *John C. Stennis*.[^cite_note-Polmar_113-14][^cite_note-49] More recently, older ships have had their flight decks upgraded with a new non-slip material fitted on new-build ships to improve safety for crew members and aircraft.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

The last carrier of the class, *George H. W. Bush*, was designed as a "transition ship" from the *Nimitz* class to the replacement [*Gerald R. Ford* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford-class_aircraft_carrier). *George H. W. Bush* incorporates new technologies, including improved propeller and bulbous bow designs, a reduced [radar cross-section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_cross-section), and electronic and environmental upgrades.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] The ship's cost was \$6.2 billion. The earlier *Nimitz*-class ships each cost around \$4.5 billion.[^cite_note-54] To lower costs, some new technologies and design features were also incorporated into USS *Ronald Reagan*, the previous carrier, including a redesigned [island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_deck#Full_length).[^cite_note-55]

## Service history

### 1975–1989

One of the first major operations in which the ships were involved was [Operation Eagle Claw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Eagle_Claw) launched by *Nimitz* in 1980 after she had deployed to the Indian Ocean in response to the [taking of hostages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_hostage_crisis) in the U.S. embassy in [Tehran](57654.md).[^cite_note-Cold_war_ship-21] Although initially part of the [U.S. Atlantic Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Atlantic_Fleet),[^cite_note-NVR_Dwight_D._Eisenhower-57] *Dwight D. Eisenhower* relieved *Nimitz* in this operation after her service in the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md).[^cite_note-DANFS_Nimitz-56] *Nimitz* conducted a [Freedom of Navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Navigation) exercise alongside the aircraft carrier [USS *Forrestal*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Forrestal) in August 1981 in the [Gulf of Sidra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Sidra), near [Libya](17633.md). During this exercise, two of the ship's F-14 Tomcats shot down two Libyan aircraft in what became known as the [Gulf of Sidra incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Sidra_incident_\(1981\)).[^cite_note-Love:_History-70] Both *Nimitz* and *Dwight D. Eisenhower* conducted contingency operations off Lebanon in support of the [Multinational Force in Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_Force_in_Lebanon).[^cite_note-71] *Dwight D. Eisenhower* operated off the coast of Libya during Operation Arid Farmer, the code-name for U.S. military assistance to [Sudan](27421.md), [Egypt](8087628.md) and the government of [Hissène Habré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiss%C3%A8ne_Habr%C3%A9) of [Chad](5488.md) during the [Chadian-Libyan conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chadian-Libyan_conflict).[^cite_note-72] *Nimitz* operated off the coast of Lebanon after the hijacking of [TWA Flight 847](512123.md).[^cite_note-73] In 1987, *Carl Vinson* participated in the first U.S. carrier deployment in the [Bering Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Sea),[^cite_note-74] and *Nimitz* provided security during the [1988 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics) in [Seoul](19159283.md).[^cite_note-75] Both *Nimitz* and *Carl Vinson* participated in [Operation Earnest Will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Earnest_Will), the escort of U.S. flagged [tankers](2506366.md) in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md).[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

### 1990–2000

Three helicopters in the hangar of Nimitz

USN [RH-53D Sea Stallion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_CH-53) helicopters aboard *Nimitz* in early 1980, prior to execution of [Operation Eagle Claw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Eagle_Claw)

The two most significant deployments the *Nimitz* class was involved in during the 1990s were the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) and its aftermath and [Operation Southern Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Southern_Watch) in southern Iraq. All active vessels were engaged in both of these to some extent, with Operation Southern Watch continuing until 2003.[^cite_note-78] Most carriers in operation in [Operation Desert Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Shield_\(Gulf_War\)) and [Operation Desert Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Storm) played supporting roles, with only *Theodore Roosevelt* playing an active part in combat operations.[^cite_note-Tucker-79]

Throughout the 1990s and more recently, *Nimitz*-class carriers have been deployed as part of humanitarian missions. While deployed in the Gulf War, *Abraham Lincoln* was diverted to the Pacific Ocean to participate alongside 22 other ships in [Operation Fiery Vigil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Fiery_Vigil), evacuating civilians following the eruption of [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo) on [Luzon Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon_Island) in the Philippines. In October 1993, *Abraham Lincoln* deployed to [Somalia](27358.md) to assist UN humanitarian operations there, spending four weeks flying patrols around [Mogadishu](60154.md) while supporting U.S. troops during [Operation Restore Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Restore_Hope). The same ship also participated in [Operation Vigilant Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Vigilant_Sentinel) in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) in 1995.[^cite_note-DANFS_Abraham_Lincoln-64]

*Dwight D. Eisenhower* participated in [Operation Uphold Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Uphold_Democracy), the US effort to restore the democratically elected government of [Haiti](13373.md), stationing the [10th Mountain Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Mountain_Division) on board.[^cite_note-80]

*Theodore Roosevelt* flew patrols in support of the [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurd) over northern Iraq as part of [Operation Provide Comfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Provide_Comfort) in 1991. In 1995, *Theodore Roosevelt* participated in [Operation Deliberate Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Deliberate_Force) participating alongside other [NATO](21133.md) air forces. In 1996, *George Washington* played a peacekeeping role in [Operation Decisive Endeavor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Decisive_Endeavor) in Bosnia and Herzegovina.[^cite_note-81] In 1998, *Carl Vinson* participated in [Operation Desert Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_bombing_of_Iraq). In 1999, *Theodore Roosevelt* was called to the [Ionian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionian_Sea) to support [Operation Allied Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Allied_Force) alongside other NATO militaries.[^cite_note-82]

### 2001–present



*Harry S. Truman* engaging in flight operations during Operation Iraqi Freedom

*Harry S. Truman*'s maiden deployment was in November 2000. The carrier's air wing flew 869 combat sorties in support of Operation Southern Watch, including a strike on Iraqi air defense sites on 16 February 2001, in response to Iraqi [surface-to-air missile](182664.md) fire against United Nations coalition forces.[^cite_note-83]

After the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md), *Carl Vinson* and *Theodore Roosevelt* were among the first warships to participate in [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom) in Afghanistan. *Carl Vinson* sailed towards the Persian Gulf intending to support Operation Southern Watch in July 2001. This changed in response to the attacks, and the ship changed course to travel towards the North Arabian Sea, where she launched the first airstrikes in support of the operation on 7 October 2001.[^cite_note-84]

Following the attacks, *John C. Stennis* and *George Washington* participated in [Operation Noble Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Noble_Eagle), carrying out homeland security operations off the [West Coast of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States). All active ships have been involved in Iraq and Afghanistan since that time. This included the [invasion](201936.md) in 2003, as well as providing subsequent support for [Operation Iraqi Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Iraqi_Freedom) since then.[^cite_note-85]

The carriers have also provided aid after natural disasters. In 2005, *Abraham Lincoln* supported [Operation Unified Assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Unified_Assistance) in Indonesia after the [December 2004 tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_Ocean_earthquake),[^cite_note-86] and *Harry S. Truman* provided aid after [Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina) later in 2005.[^cite_note-87]



Crew of *Abraham Lincoln* filling water containers while deployed to assist humanitarian efforts in the aftermath of 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami

The *Ronald Reagan* Carrier Strike Group performed humanitarian assistance and disaster relief operations in the Philippines in June 2008 after [Typhoon Fengshen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Fengshen_\(2008\)), which killed hundreds from the central island regions and the main island of [Luzon](69375.md).[^cite_note-88] In January 2010, *Carl Vinson* operated off [Haiti](13373.md), providing aid and drinking water to [earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Haiti_earthquake) survivors as part of the U.S.-led [Operation Unified Response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Unified_Response), alongside other major warships and [hospital ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospital_ship) [*Comfort*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USNS_Comfort).[^cite_note-89]

The carriers, *George H. W. Bush*, *Harry S. Truman*, *Dwight D. Eisenhower*, *Theodore Roosevelt*, *John C. Stennis* and *Abraham Lincoln* participated in [Operation Inherent Resolve](44120613.md).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]

### Refueling Complex Overhaul

Main article: [Refueling and overhaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refueling_and_overhaul)

Abraham Lincoln in drydock. Equipment and vehicles are on deck for the overhaul

*Abraham Lincoln* in dry dock, 1990

In order to refuel their nuclear power plants, the carriers each undergo a [Refueling and Overhaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refueling_and_Overhaul) (RCOH) once in their service lives. This is also the most substantial overhaul the ships undergo while in service and involves bringing the vessels' equipment up to the standards of the newest ships. The ship is placed in a dry dock, and essential maintenance is carried out, including painting the hull below the waterline and replacing electrical and mechanical components such as valves. Because of the large time periods between the ships' constructions, the armament and designs of the newer ships are more modern than those of the older ships. In RCOH, the older ships are refitted to the standards of the newer ships, which can include upgrades to the flight deck, aircraft catapults, combat systems, and radar systems; precise details can vary significantly between the ships. The improvements normally take around four years to complete. The RCOH for USS *Theodore Roosevelt* took four years to complete (2009–2013) and cost about \$2.6 billion.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] Planned Incremental Availability is a similar procedure, although it is less substantial and does not involve refueling the nuclear power plants.[^cite_note-100]

### Symbolic and diplomatic roles



*George Washington* underway with the [Royal Malaysian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Malaysian_Navy) [*Lekiu*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekiu-class_frigate) frigates KD *Jebat* and KD *Lekiu* during a transit of the [Andaman Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andaman_Sea)

Because of their status as the largest warships in the U.S. Navy, the deployment of an aircraft carrier can fulfill a symbolic role, not just as a deterrent to an enemy but often as a diplomatic tool in strengthening relations with allies and potential allies. The latter of these functions can occur either as a single visit to a country, in which senior naval officers are allowed to observe the operation of the carrier and interact with its senior officers,[^cite_note-101] or as part of an international task force. This can be in combat operations, such as the [NATO bombing of Yugoslavia](322473.md) in 1999,[^cite_note-102] or training deployments, such as [Exercise RIMPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_RIMPAC). In addition, carriers have participated in international [Maritime security operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_security_operations), combating [piracy](50715.md) in the Persian Gulf and off the coast of Somalia.[^cite_note-Navy.mil_CVN-1][^cite_note-103]

### Accidents and incidents

On 26 May 1981, an [EA-6B Prowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_EA-6B_Prowler) crashed on the flight deck of *Nimitz*, killing 14 crewmen and injuring 45 others.[^cite_note-Cold_war_ship-21][^cite_note-time-104] Forensic testing of the personnel involved showed that several tested positive for marijuana. While this was not found to have directly caused the crash, the investigation's findings prompted the introduction of mandatory drug testing of all service personnel.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106]

Pilots have been able to eject safely in several cases of ditched aircraft. However, fatal aircraft crashes have occurred; in 1994, Lieutenant [Kara Hultgreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara_Hultgreen), the first female F-14 Tomcat pilot, was killed while attempting to land on board *Abraham Lincoln* during a training exercise.[^cite_note-DANFS_Abraham_Lincoln-64]

Fires have also caused damage to the ships; in May 2008, while rotating through to her new homeport at [Yokosuka Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fleet_Activities_Yokosuka) in [Yokosuka, Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokosuka,_Japan), *George Washington* suffered a fire that cost \$70 million in repairs, injured 37 sailors and led to the ship undergoing three months of repairs at San Diego; this led to its having to miss the 2008 [RIMPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIMPAC) exercises and delayed the final withdrawal from service of [USS *Kitty Hawk*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Kitty_Hawk_\(CV-63\)). The fire was caused by unauthorized smoking near improperly stored flammable refrigerant compressor oil.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109]

## Future and planned replacement



Aerial view of [USS *Harry S. Truman*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Harry_S._Truman) (CVN-75) alongside [USS *Gerald R. Ford*](4050542.md) (CVN-78), the lead ship of the follow-on [*Gerald R. Ford* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford-class_aircraft_carrier)

*Nimitz*-class carriers were initially designed to have a 50-year service life.[^cite_note-Navy.mil_CVN-1] At the end of their service life, ships will be [decommissioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_decommissioning). This process will first take place on *Nimitz* and is estimated to cost from \$750 to \$900 million. This compares with an estimated \$53 million for a conventionally powered carrier. Most of the difference in cost is attributed to the [deactivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship-Submarine_Recycling_Program) of the nuclear power plants and the safe removal of radioactive material and other contaminated equipment.[^cite_note-110]

A new class of carriers, the [*Gerald R. Ford* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_R._Ford-class_aircraft_carrier), is being constructed to replace previous vessels after decommissioning. Ten of these are expected, and the first has entered service as of 22 July 2017 to replace [*Enterprise*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Enterprise_\(CVN-65\)). Most of the rest of these new carriers are to replace the oldest *Nimitz* ships as they reach the end of their service lives.[^cite_note-111] The new carriers will have a similar design to *George H. W. Bush* (using an almost identical hull shape) and technological and structural improvements.[^cite_note-112]

The Navy reported in early 2022 that it was conducting a study to determine if the *Nimitz*-class carrier lives could be extended to as long as 55 years.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]
