**Lithuania–United States relations** are the [bilateral](429154.md) [foreign relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations) between [Lithuania](17675.md) and the [United States](3434750.md). [Lithuania](17675.md) is one of the most pro-[United States](3434750.md) nations in the world, with 73% of Lithuanians viewing the U.S. positively in 2011.[^cite_note-1] According to the 2012 U.S. Global Leadership Report, 48% of [Lithuanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanians) approve of U.S. leadership, with 20% disapproving and 32% uncertain.[^cite_note-2]

## History



A Lithuanian poster from 1922 celebrating the recognition of Lithuania by the United States.

The United States established diplomatic relations with Lithuania on 28 July 1922.[^cite_note-3] During World War II, Lithuania was at various times occupied by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [Nazi Germany](21212.md). The [Soviet invasion and occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Baltic_states) forced the closure of the Legation to Lithuania on 5 September 1940. However, [Lithuanian Diplomatic Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Diplomatic_Service) in the United States [continued uninterrupted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Legations_\(1940%E2%80%931991\)). American prisoners of war were among [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) POWs held by the Germans in the [Stalag Luft VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_Luft_VI) [POW camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) in [German-occupied Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Lithuania_during_World_War_II).[^cite_note-4] The United States never recognized the forced incorporation of Lithuania into the USSR and views the present government of Lithuania as a [legal continuation of the interwar republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_continuity_of_the_Baltic_states). Following the [Dissolution of the USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_USSR), Lithuania and the United States re-established diplomatic relations on 6 September 1991.[^cite_note-5] In 2007, the United States and Lithuania celebrated 85 years of continuous diplomatic relations. Lithuania has enjoyed most-favored-nation treatment with the United States since December 1991. Since 1992, the United States has committed more than \$100 million in Lithuania to economic and political transformation and to [humanitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitarian) needs. The United States and Lithuania signed an agreement on bilateral trade and intellectual property protection in 1994 and a bilateral investment treaty in 1997. In 1998, the United States signed a "[Charter of Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_of_Partnership?action=edit\&redlink=1)" with Lithuania and the other Baltic countries establishing bilateral working groups focusing on improving regional [security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security), [defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military), and economic issues.[^cite_note-6]

Today, over 650,000 individuals who identify as [Lithuanian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_American) live in the United States. Lithuanian immigration began before the United States even became a country, with individuals like [Alexander Curtius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Curtius) settling in [New Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam) (what would later become [New York City](645042.md)) in 1659. Lithuania was part of the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) until 1795, when foreign powers [partitioned it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland) and Lithuania was largely incorporated into the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). Despite attempts by the [Tsarist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsarist_autocracy) government in [Moscow](19004.md) to prevent residents of the empire from emigrating, many Lithuanians came to the United States throughout the 19th and early 20th Centuries, settling primarily in the [Northeast](431669.md) (especially [Pennsylvania](23332.md)) and the [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest). Lithuanian immigration tapered off with the passage of [nativist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativism_\(politics\)) legislation like the [Emergency Quota Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_Quota_Act) of 1921 and the [Immigration Act of 1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_Act_of_1924) in [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_United_States). Smaller waves of Lithuanian migration to the United States occurred at the end of [World War II](32927.md) (thanks to the [Displaced Persons Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displaced_Persons_Act)) and when Lithuania regained its independence in 1990.

In November 2002, [George W. Bush](3414021.md) became the first US president to visit [Lithuania](17675.md).[^cite_note-7] It was a visit following the [NATO](21133.md) invitation to join the alliance.

> Anyone who would choose Lithuania as an enemy has also made an enemy of the United States of America.

— [George W. Bush](3414021.md), November 2002 in Vilnius

[^cite_note-8]

In July 2023, Lithuania hosted [2023 Vilnius summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Vilnius_summit), receiving the US President [Joe Biden](145422.md) along with around 40 other leaders. During the visit, President Biden [gave a speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Joe_Biden_speech_in_Vilnius) in [Vilnius University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_University).[^cite_note-9]
