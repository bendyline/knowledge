**Gauteng** ([/xaʊˈtɛŋ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*khow-TENG*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key),[^cite_note-6] Sotho: [\[xɑ́.úˈtʼèŋ̀\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA); [Sotho-Tswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotho-Tswana_languages) for 'place of gold'; [Zulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zulu_language): *eGoli* or *iGoli* [\[îːˈɡóːlì\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Nguni)) is one of the nine [provinces](53215.md) of [South Africa](17416221.md).

Situated on the [Highveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highveld), Gauteng is the smallest province by land area in South Africa.[^cite_note-7] Although Gauteng accounts for only 1.5% of the country's land area, it is the most populous province in South Africa, with more than a quarter (26%) of the national population;[^cite_note-StatsZaGp-8] the provincial population was approximately 16.1 million, according to mid-year 2022 estimates.[^cite_note-Stats01-9] Highly urbanised, the province's capital is also the country's largest city, [Johannesburg](58893.md). Gauteng is the wealthiest province in South Africa and is considered the financial hub of South Africa; the financial activity is mostly concentrated in Johannesburg. It also contains South Africa's administrative capital, [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria), and other large areas such as [Midrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midrand), [Vanderbijlpark](1413961.md), [Ekurhuleni](3876354.md) and the affluent [Sandton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandton). The largest township, [Soweto](28573.md), is also found in this province. Politically, it is the most closely contested province between the nationalist [African National Congress](2503.md) and the liberal [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(South_Africa\)) in South Africa.[^cite_note-News24-10]

## Etymology

The name *Gauteng* is derived from [Sotho-Tswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotho%E2%80%93Tswana_languages) *gauta*, meaning 'gold'.[^cite_note-GautengRenamed-11] There was a thriving gold industry in the province following the [1886 discovery of gold in Johannesburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witwatersrand_Gold_Rush).[^cite_note-GPG-about1-12] In [Sesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotho_language), [Setswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tswana_language) and [Sepedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Sotho_language) the name *Gauteng* was used for Johannesburg and surrounding areas long before it was adopted in 1994 as the official name of the province.

## History



A snippet of text showing the Sesotho word "Gaudeng" (modern *Gauteng*) in Jacottet's [*A practical method to learn Sesuto: with exercises and a short vocabulary*](https://archive.org/stream/practicalmethodt00jacorich#page/198/mode/2up), published in 1906

Gauteng was formed from part of the old [Transvaal province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_\(province\)) after South Africa's first multiracial elections on 27 April 1994. It was initially called [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria)–[Witwatersrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witwatersrand)–[Vereeniging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vereeniging) (PWV) and was renamed "Gauteng" on the 28th of June 1995, the same day two other provinces were renamed.[^cite_note-GautengRenamed-11] The term "PWV" describing the region existed long before the establishment of a province by that name,[^cite_note-ZAHistoryGP-13] with the "V" sometimes standing for "[Vaal Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaal_Triangle)" rather than Vereeniging.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

At the [Sterkfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterkfontein) caves, some of the oldest fossils of hominids have been discovered, such as [Mrs. Ples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrs._Ples) and [Little Foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Foot).[^cite_note-SouthAfrica.NetGP-16]

Events in this area were not written down until the 19th century;[^cite_note-SouthAfrica.NetGP-16] information from before that time is lost or difficult to confirm.[^cite_note-SouthAfrica.NetGP-16] The first records are from the early 19th century, when settlers originating from the [Cape Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Colony) defeated chief [Mzilikazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mzilikazi) and started establishing villages in the area.[^cite_note-SouthAfrica.NetGP-16]

The city of Pretoria, established in 1855 as the capital of the [South African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Republic), witnessed rapid growth until the discovery of gold in the Witswatersrand area in 1886, which led to the founding of Johannesburg.[^cite_note-ZAHistoryGP-13][^cite_note-SouthAfrica.NetGP-16] Despite slower development compared to Johannesburg, Pretoria maintained significance, notably due to its pivotal role in the [Second Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War).[^cite_note-SouthAfrica.NetGP-16] The nearby town of [Cullinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cullinan,_South_Africa) gained international acclaim in 1905 when the largest diamond ever discovered, the [Cullinan Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cullinan_Diamond), was mined there.[^cite_note-ZAHistoryGP-13]

Many events crucial to the anti-[apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) struggle happened in present-day Gauteng, such as the [Freedom Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Charter) of 1955, [Women's March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_March_\(South_Africa\)) of 1956, [Sharpeville massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharpeville_massacre) of 1960, the [Rivonia Trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivonia_Trial) of 1963 and 1964, the [little Rivonia Trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Rivonia_Trial) of 1964, the [Soweto uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soweto_uprising) of 1976, [Sharpeville Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharpeville_Six) of 1984 and [Boipatong massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boipatong_massacre) of 1992.[^cite_note-ZAHistoryGP-13] The [Apartheid Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid_Museum) documents this era.[^cite_note-ZAHistoryGP-13]

## Law and government

Main article: [Government of Gauteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Gauteng)



The Johannesburg City Hall, home of the Gauteng Provincial Legislature

Gauteng is governed by the [Gauteng Provincial Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauteng_Provincial_Legislature), a 73-person [unicameral](204304.md) legislature elected by [party-list proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party-list_proportional_representation). The legislature elects one of its members as [Premier of Gauteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_Gauteng) to lead the executive, and the Premier appoints an [Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Council_of_Gauteng) of up to 10 members of the legislature to serve as heads of the various government departments. The provincial government is responsible for the topics allocated to it in the [national constitution](441610.md), including such fields as basic education, health, housing, social services, agriculture and environmental protection.

The most recent election of the provincial legislature was [held on 8 May 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_general_election,_2019), and the [African National Congress](2503.md) (ANC) won 50.19% of the vote and a 37-seat majority in the legislature. The official opposition is the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(South_Africa\)), which won 27.45% of the vote and 20 seats. Other parties represented are the [Economic Freedom Fighters](40005536.md) with eleven seats and the [Freedom Front Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Front_Plus) with three seats. The [Inkatha Freedom Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inkatha_Freedom_Party) and [African Christian Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Christian_Democratic_Party) hold one seat each.[^cite_note-17] Premier [David Makhura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Makhura) of the ANC was re-elected as premier on 22 May 2019, at the first meeting of the legislature after the general election.[^cite_note-18] Makhura resigned from the position on 6 October 2022 and [Panyaza Lesufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panyaza_Lesufi) of the ANC was elected to replace him. In the [2024 South African general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_South_African_general_election), held on 29 May, the ANC received 34% of the vote, while the DA received 28%.[^cite_note-News24-10]

The [Gauteng Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauteng_Division) of the [High Court of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_South_Africa), which has seats in Pretoria and Johannesburg, is a [superior court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_court) with [general jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_jurisdiction) over the province. Johannesburg is also home to the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_South_Africa), South Africa's highest court, and to a branch of the [Labour Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Court_of_South_Africa) and [Labour Appeal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Appeal_Court_of_South_Africa).

## Geography



The undulating hills that form part of the rural areas in the province just north of Johannesburg. Although Gauteng is a heavily urbanised province, much of its area is extensively cultivated for agriculture.

Gauteng's southern border is the [Vaal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaal_River), which separates it from the [Free State](447006.md).[^cite_note-GautengRenamed-11] It also borders on [North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Province_\(South_Africa\))[^cite_note-GautengRenamed-11] to the west, [Limpopo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limpopo_Province)[^cite_note-GautengRenamed-11] to the north, and [Mpumalanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpumalanga_Province)[^cite_note-GautengRenamed-11] to the east. Gauteng is the only landlocked province of South Africa without a foreign border.[^cite_note-GautengRenamed-11] Most of Gauteng is on the [Highveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highveld), a high-altitude grassland (circa 1,500 m or 4,921 ft above sea level). Between [Johannesburg](58893.md) and [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria), there are low parallel ridges and undulating hills, some part of the [Magaliesberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magaliesberg) Mountains and the [Witwatersrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witwatersrand). The north of the province is more [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics), due to its lower altitude and is mostly dry [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna) habitat.

### Witwatersrand area

Further information: [Witwatersrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witwatersrand)

In the southern half of Gauteng, the Witwatersrand area is an older term describing a 120 km wide oblong-shaped conurbation from [Randfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randfontein) in the West to [Nigel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel,_Gauteng) in the East, named after the [Witwatersrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witwatersrand), a geologically and economically important series of low ridges and their associated plateau that greater Johannesburg developed on. This area is also often referred to simply as "Witwatersrand", "the Rand" or "the Reef" (archaic, after the gold reefs that precipitated the development of the area), and was the "W" in *PWV*, the initial name for Gauteng. It has traditionally been divided into the three areas of [East Rand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Rand) (governed by the [Ekurhuleni Metropolitan Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekurhuleni_Metropolitan_Municipality)), Central Rand (approximately today's [Johannesburg Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Johannesburg_Metropolitan_Municipality)) and [West Rand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Rand).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### Climate

The climate is mostly influenced by altitude. Even though the province is at a [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropics) latitude, the climate is comparatively cooler, especially in [Johannesburg](58893.md), at 1,700 m (5,577 ft) above sea level ([Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria) is at 1,330 m or 4,364 ft). Most precipitation occurs as brief afternoon thunderstorms; however, relative humidity never becomes uncomfortable. Winters are crisp and dry with frost occurring often in the southern areas. Snow is rare, but it has occurred on some occasions in the [Johannesburg](58893.md) metropolitan area.[^cite_note-News24Snow-21][^cite_note-IOLNewsSnow-22]

As of the [2022 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_South_African_census), Gauteng had a population of 15,099,422, an increase of 23.0% from the last census in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_South_African_census). Despite being the smallest province by area, it has the highest population of any South African province, with 24.3% of the total South African population.[^cite_note-2022_census-34]

As of 2022, there are 5,318,665 households in Gauteng. The population density is 831/km<sup>2</sup>. The density of households is 155.86/km<sup>2</sup>.

About 22.1% of all households are made up of individuals. The average household size fell slightly between the 2011 census and 2022 census, from 3.1 to 2.8 persons.[^cite_note-cib2022-35]

The province's age distribution was 23.6% under the age of 15, 19.6% from 15 to 24, 37.9% from 25 to 44, 15.0% from 45 to 64, and 4.0% who are 65 years of age or older. The median age is 27 years. For every 100 females there are 101.2 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there are 102.3 males.

### Life expectancy

Gauteng is the province with the second highest [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) in the country in 2019 with females having a life expectancy of 69 years and males having a life expectancy of 64 years.[^cite_note-Stats01-9]

### Urban conurbation

Historically described as the PWV complex,[^cite_note-37] the urban conurbation of Gauteng, referred to as the Gauteng City Region,[^cite_note-gcro.ac.za-38] contains the major urban populations of [Johannesburg](58893.md) (7,860,781 as of 2011), [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria) (1,763,336), [Vereeniging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vereeniging) (377,922), [Evaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaton) (605,504) and [Soshanguve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soshanguve) (728,063), coming to an urban population of over 11 million.[^cite_note-39] Thomas Brinkhoff lists a "Consolidated Urban Area" in Gauteng as having a population of 13.1 million as of January 2017.[^cite_note-40] The future governmental plans for the region indicate the gradual urbanisation and consolidation towards the creation of a megalopolis that connects these metros. The GCRO is a collaboration between the Universities of Johannesburg and Witwatersrand, the city of Johannesburg, Gauteng Provincial Government, and SALGA-Gauteng. The GCRO's purpose is to collect information and create a database on the Gauteng City Region to provide to government, lawmakers and civil society an informed understanding of the fastest urbanizing region in Southern Africa.[^cite_note-gcro.ac.za-38]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Gauteng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Gauteng)

Gauteng is considered the [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_system) hub of [South Africa](17416221.md) and contributes heavily in the financial, manufacturing, transport, technology, and [telecommunications](33094374.md) sectors, among others. It also plays host to a large number of overseas companies requiring a commercial base in and gateway to [Africa](5334607.md).

Gauteng is home to the [Johannesburg Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JSE_Securities_Exchange),[^cite_note-JSEinJozi-41] the largest stock exchange in Africa. Some of the largest companies in Africa and abroad are based in Gauteng, or have offices and branches there, such as [Vodacom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodacom),[^cite_note-VodacomHistory-42] [MTN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTN_Group),[^cite_note-MTNInfo-43] [Neotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neotel),[^cite_note-NeotelInfo-44] [Microsoft South Africa](19001.md)[^cite_note-MicrosoftZA-45] and the largest [Porsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche) Centre in the world.[^cite_note-PorscheZA-46]

Although Gauteng is the smallest of South Africa's nine provinces—it covers a mere 1.5% of the country's total land area,[^cite_note-StatsZaGp-8] the province is responsible for a third of South Africa's [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP).[^cite_note-47] Gauteng generates about 10% of the total GDP of [sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md) and about 7% of total African GDP.[^cite_note-48] Gauteng has the highest GDP and GDP per capita of all South Africa's provinces.[^cite_note-49] Gauteng is also the province with the most taxpayers and the highest average taxable income per taxpayer according to the [South African Revenue Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Revenue_Service).[^cite_note-50]

## Transport

[SANRAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Roads_Agency), a parastatal, is responsible for the maintenance, development and management of all [national road networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Roads_of_South_Africa) in South Africa.[^cite_note-SANRALInfo-51] SANRAL is responsible for instituting the Gauteng Freeway Improvement Project, which was met with a lot of opposition due to the tolling of Gauteng motorists.[^cite_note-PoliticsWebGFIP-52][^cite_note-GPGovTransport-53] Many important [national routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_routes_\(South_Africa\)) run through Gauteng such as the [N1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N1_road_\(South_Africa\)), [N3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N3_road_\(South_Africa\)), [N4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N4_road_\(South_Africa\)), [N12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N12_road_\(South_Africa\)), [N14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N14_road_\(South_Africa\)) and the [N17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N17_road_\(South_Africa\)). Johannesburg is quite dependent on [freeways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg_freeways) for transport in and around the city. The [R21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R21_\(Gauteng\)), [R24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R24_\(Gauteng\)), [R59](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R59_\(South_Africa\)), [M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_\(Johannesburg\)) and [M2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M2_\(Johannesburg\)) all run through Johannesburg while the [R80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R80_\(South_Africa\)) connects Pretoria Central to Soshanguve. The Gauteng Freeway Improvement Project led to a large decrease in traffic congestion when construction finished 2011–2012. [Cape Town](6653.md), for the first time in decades, is now the most congested city in South Africa.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

[PUTCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PUTCO), the largest commuter bus operator in South Africa,[^cite_note-PutcoNum1-56] services the Gauteng area extensively.[^cite_note-PutcoWebsite-57] The [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) system [Rea Vaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rea_Vaya) also serves to transport people from Johannesburg's southern neighbourhoods into and around the [CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district).[^cite_note-ReaVaya-58] In an interview, [Parks Tau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_Tau) stated that by 2040, Johannesburg will be dominated by pedestrians and public transport as opposed to the use of private transport or informal transport, such as minibus taxis.[^cite_note-ParksTauInterview-59]

[Gautrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautrain) and [Metrorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrorail_Gauteng) both service the province's public transport sector where trains are concerned[^cite_note-Gautrain-60][^cite_note-61] and Gautrain offers a bus service that transports commuters to and from various train stations and predetermined bus stops.[^cite_note-62] Metrorail trains are considered one of the most cost-effective methods of transportation in and around Gauteng.[^cite_note-TrainsRCheap-63]

The [O. R. Tambo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._R._Tambo_International_Airport), [Rand Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rand_Airport), [Lanseria International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanseria_International_Airport), [Wonderboom Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonderboom_Airport) and [Grand Central Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Airport) are located in Gauteng.[^cite_note-ORTambo-64][^cite_note-RandAirport-65][^cite_note-Lanseria-66]

There is a large informal transport sector in Gauteng, consisting of thousands of [minibus taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minibus_taxi), which many of the urban and rural population makes use of.[^cite_note-TrainsRCheap-63][^cite_note-TaxiJHBinfo-67] However, it is noted that taxis are often unsafe as their drivers ignore the rules of the road and the vehicles are often not [roadworthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roadworthiness).[^cite_note-TaxiJHBinfo-67] The City of Johannesburg stated that: "major initiatives are under way to completely reform the taxi industry and provide more comfort and safety to customers." In March 2017, it is reported that Gauteng alone has 4,7 million registered vehicles under the "GP" abbreviation via the eNatis system.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-TaxiJHBinfo-67]

## Conservation

Although Gauteng province is dominated by the urban areas of Johannesburg and Pretoria, it has several nature reserves. Gauteng is home to the [Cradle of Humankind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_Humankind) [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md) which includes the [Sterkfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterkfontein) caves and the [Wonder Cave Kromdraai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonder_Cave_Kromdraai). Johannesburg is home to the largest human-made [urban forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_forest) in the world.[^cite_note-JHBforest-72][^cite_note-JHBforest2-73][^cite_note-JHBforest3-74]

## Sport



[Loftus Versfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loftus_Versfeld) Stadium, one of Gauteng's various stadia and venue for the 2010 FIFA World Cup



[FNB Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FNB_Stadium) South Africa and Africa's largest stadium



[Wanderers Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanderers_Stadium) South Africas largest Cricket stadium



[Kyalami Grand Prix Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyalami_Grand_Prix_Circuit)



[Gold Reef City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Reef_City) amusement park

Gauteng's favourable weather conditions throughout the year make it an ideal hub for sports and other outdoor activities.[^cite_note-GPSportInfo-75]

The province is home to many stadiums and sporting grounds, notably [Soccer City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FNB_Stadium), [Ellis Park Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellis_Park_Stadium), [Odi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odi_Stadium), [Loftus Versfeld Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loftus_Versfeld_Stadium), [Lucas Moripe Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Moripe_Stadium), [Giant Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Stadium), [Orlando Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Stadium), [Johannesburg Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg_Stadium), the [Wanderers Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanderers_Stadium) and [SuperSport Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SuperSport_Park).

Several teams from Gauteng play in the country's top-level [soccer](10568.md) league, the [Premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Premiership), including [Mamelodi Sundowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamelodi_Sundowns_F.C.), [SuperSport United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SuperSport_United_F.C.), [Kaizer Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaizer_Chiefs_Football_Club) and [Orlando Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Pirates_Football_Club). The national squad [Bafana Bafana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bafana_Bafana) frequently play at Soccer City in Johannesburg. During the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md), the first world cup held by an African nation,[^cite_note-FIFAZA-76] Gauteng's stadia hosted many games. The first FIFA world cup match on African soil took place at Soccer City on 11 June 2010.[^cite_note-FIFA1stMatch-77][^cite_note-FIFASoccerCityMatches-78] Along with Soccer City, Loftus Versfeld Stadium and Ellis Park Stadium hosted matches in Gauteng.[^cite_note-FIFASoccerCityVenues-79]

[Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_football), or more accurately [rugby union](25405.md), is a popular sport in South Africa, and in Gauteng in particular. Two rugby teams from Gauteng participate in the [United Rugby Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Rugby_Championship): the [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria)-based [Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulls_\(rugby_franchise\)), and the [Johannesburg](58893.md)-based [Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lions_\(Super_Rugby\)). Three Gauteng-based teams play in the country's domestic competition, the [Currie Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currie_Cup): the [Blue Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Bulls) from Pretoria, the [Golden Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Lions) from Johannesburg and the [Falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcons_\(rugby_team\)) from the [East Rand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Rand,_Gauteng). In 1995, South Africa hosted the [1995 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Rugby_World_Cup) and proceeded to win the tournament at Ellis Park Stadium on 24 June 1995.[^cite_note-ESPNScrumRWC95-80] The events surrounding the world cup formed the basis of the story for the movie [Invictus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invictus_\(film\)).[^cite_note-InvictusInfo-81]

Many South African universities take part in the [Varsity Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varsity_Rugby) league. Of these, the Gauteng universities include the University of Pretoria, the University of Johannesburg and the University of the Witwatersrand.[^cite_note-VarsityRugby-82]

[Cricket](25675557.md) is also widely popular among all cultural groups. The [Highveld Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highveld_Lions_cricket_team) represent both Gauteng and [North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_\(South_African_province\)) in the country's three domestic competitions—the [first-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_cricket) [CSA 4-Day Domestic Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSA_4-Day_Domestic_Series), the [List A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_A_cricket) [one-day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_overs_cricket) [CSA One-Day Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSA_One-Day_Cup) and the [Twenty20](770690.md) [CSA T20 Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSA_T20_Challenge).

Many [marathons](49132.md) take place in Gauteng, such as the [Soweto Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soweto_Marathon), Gauteng Marathon, the Arwyp Medical Centre 15 km Nite Race and the Trisport Joburg City Triathlon.[^cite_note-GPMarathon-83][^cite_note-GautengMarathon-84][^cite_note-ArwypMarathon-85][^cite_note-JoziTriSport-86]

[Golf](19568112.md), horse racing and swimming are also popular. The [Vaal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaal_River) facilitates water sports in the forms of jet skiing, water skiing and motor boating.[^cite_note-GPSportInfo-75] Adventure sports are also quite popular in Gauteng, particularly skydiving, paragliding and hang-gliding.[^cite_note-GPSportInfo-75]

The [amusement park](137327.md) [Gold Reef City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Reef_City) is situated in Gauteng,[^cite_note-GRCAboutUs-87] as is the [Johannesburg Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg_Zoo)[^cite_note-JHBZoo-88] and the [Pretoria Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Zoological_Gardens_of_South_Africa).[^cite_note-PTAZoo-89] Botanical gardens in the province include the [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria_National_Botanical_Garden) and [Walter Sisulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Sisulu_National_Botanical_Garden) national botanical gardens maintained by the South African National Botanical Institute as well as the [Johannesburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannesburg_Botanical_Garden) and [Manie van der Schijff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manie_van_der_Schijff_Botanical_Garden) botanical gardens.[^cite_note-SANBI-Gardens-90][^cite_note-JhbCityParks-Overview-91]

The [Ticketpro Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticketpro_Dome) and the [Gallagher Convention Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallagher_Convention_Centre), which are both popular events and expos venues, are also located within Gauteng.[^cite_note-CocaColaDome-92][^cite_note-GallagherEstate-93] The province also has a [Formula One](10854.md) racetrack, the [Kyalami Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyalami). The most recent F1 race at the venue was in [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Formula_One_World_Championship).
