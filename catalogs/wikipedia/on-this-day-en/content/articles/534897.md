It serves as the administrative center of [Starobilsk Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starobilsk_Raion). The modern settlement was founded in 1686, and it was granted city status in 1938. The city has a population of 15,947 (2022 estimate).[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-1] After the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), it has been under [Russian](25391.md) occupation since 28 February 2022.[^cite_note-2]

## History

### Prehistory

Presumably, Starobilsk traces its heritage to the [settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloboda_\(settlement\)) of **Bielska Sloboda** which originally might have been named after [Okolnichy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okolnichy) [Bogdan Belsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogdan_Belsky) of [Litvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litvin) [Bielsky family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belsky_family_\(Gediminid\)) who at that time was a subject of the [Grand Duchy of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow).

Bielsky arrived at the banks of [Siversky Donets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets) to build a fortress at the southern borders *Tsare-Borisov* (after Muscovite [Tsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia) [Boris Godunov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Godunov)) which was erected not far away in 1598–1600. In 1602 Godunov became suspicious of Belsky and order him to be arrested, stripped of any estates, and exiled to [Siberia](29639.md). After the death of Godunov Belsky was granted amnesty in 1605 due to the fact that his sister being the wife of the deceased Boris Godunov, [Maria Skuratova-Belskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Skuratova-Belskaya), became a regent. Belsky was sent as a *voivode* to [Kazan](202295.md) where in 1611 was killed by a mob after refusing to pledge allegiance to [False Dmitry II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_Dmitry_II). Sloboda gradually became abandoned, while the fortress was destroyed in 1612 in one of the Tatar raids.

### Origin

In 1686 the settlement was repopulated by servicemen of the Ostrohozk Sloboda Cossack Regiment who originally came from Poltava and Chernihiv regions and named their settlement after a town of [Bilsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilsk,_Kotelva_Raion), [Cossack Hetmanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate) that might have belonged to another Litvin who sided with Muscovites, Theodore Bielsky.

Being runaway serfs, the Tsarist government allowed them to settle in the military frontier with the Crimean realm to carry out border guard functions. After the place became populated with serfs from the central regions of today's Russia, the Tsarist government took measures to find and return those fugitives. In 1701 the Ambassadorial [Prikaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prikaz) decided to conduct a population census in new settlements along [Aidar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aidar_River) and [Siversky Donets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siversky_Donets). Most population avoided the census. According to data of [stolnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolnik) M.Pushkin who in 1703 conducted a population census in 34 settlements, in **Bielsky** was registered only 41 residents although in reality there were much more.

Trying to meet the demands of Russian landlords who repeatedly turned to the Tsar with complaints and requests to return fugitives, on 6 July 1707 [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) issued an edict (ukase) about the search of "newly arrived from Rus all ranks of people". To the Don was sent a punitive detachment under the command of Colonel Prince Yuriy Dolgorukiy.[^cite_note-3] He was charged to search for fugitives and "take them to those landowners from whom they ran away". That action led to the well-known [Bulavin Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulavin_Rebellion). Struggling with the rebellion, Tsarist troops eventually burnt the settlement to the ground. In 1732 the settlement was repopulated again by peasants from around [Ostrogozhsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogozhsk) (Ostrohozk) turning it into a [sloboda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloboda_\(settlement\)) **Stara-Bila**. Among the first of its new residents were again servicemen of the Ostrohozk Regiment led by [sotnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotnik) I. Senelnykov. In 1782 Staro-Bila was assigned to the Derkul stud of Bilovodsk district ([Voronezh Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voronezh_Governorate)). On Tsarist edict (ukase) from 1 May 1797 sloboda Staro-Bila was renamed into **Starobelsk** and became the administrative center of [Starobelsk uyezd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starobelsk_uyezd) in [Kharkov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkov_Governorate) of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire).

### Modern era



Starobilsk in 1911

Founded on 12 October 1851, Starobilsk "Joy of All Who Sorrows" Convent (Свято-Скорботний жіночий монастир) became a spiritual center for the region. After the [Bolshevik revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution), the convent was restricted and, in April 1924, it was closed down. In 1992, the state returned it to the Orthodox Church. It was reconsecrated and opened in 1995.

During the [Ukrainian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence), the town was occupied by [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Army) troops during the [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers)' advance through Ukraine in the spring of 1918 but soon became a center of activity for the [Revolutionary Insurrectionary Army of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Insurrectionary_Army_of_Ukraine) or Anarchists. A photograph in the City Regional Museum (Старобільський краєзнавчий музей) shows Anarchist leader [Nestor Makhno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestor_Makhno) addressing the people of Starobilsk from a balcony on the main square in 1919. Afterwards, it was administratively part of the [Donets Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets_Governorate) of Ukraine.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

#### Prison camp for Polish POW officers



Memorial plaque to Polish POWs held by the Soviets in the local POW camp and then murdered in the [Katyn massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre)

During [World War II](32927.md), the old convent was the site of a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) prison camp for [Polish](22936.md) [prisoners of war](25008.md) (POWs), especially officers. Forty-eight of those who died in the camp were buried at the Starobilsk cemetery. In 1994, their bodies were moved to the Polish War Quarter built by the [Council for the Protection of Struggle and Martyrdom Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_for_the_Protection_of_Struggle_and_Martyrdom_Sites) at the [Chmyrivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chmyrivka_rural_hromada) cemetery. A plaque on the outside wall of the convent states that *more than 4,000 Polish prisoners of war were confined* inside the convent and ultimately executed in 1940. The Starobilsk prisoners were executed at the District Directorate of the NKVD in [Kharkiv](2788558.md) and later buried in the forest in [Pyatykhatky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piatykhatky,_Kharkiv). These executions, together with executions of Polish officers held in POW camps at [Kozelsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozelsk), [Ostashkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostashkov), and some smaller camps, became known as the [Katyn massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyn_massacre).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

The German [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) entered Starobilsk in late 1942, and evacuated nine months later, destroying much of the city but neglecting to dynamite the milk factory. The Germans operated a Nazi prison in the city.[^cite_note-12] The town was rebuilt around this factory, which in turn helped the region recover after the war.

### 21st century

During the first phase of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md) many places in [Luhansk Oblast](494904.md) were taken over by [pro-Russian separatists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_separatist_forces_in_Donbas); however, Starobilsk remained under Ukrainian control.[^cite_note-13] The flag of the [Luhansk People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_People's_Republic) was raised over the Hotel Aidar on 17 June 2014, but swiftly removed. The city was occupied by a military presence for two years thereafter, during which time the statue of Lenin in Starobilsk city park was toppled by a tank.

In 2016, Lenin Street was renamed Monastery Street as it had been before the Bolshevik revolution.

#### Russian occupation

Main article: [Eastern Ukraine offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ukraine_offensive)

On 24 February 2022, at the start of the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), Russian Forces began [an assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ukraine_campaign) on Starobilsk, consisting of an unspecified number of Tanks, BMPS, and infantry.

On 6 March 2022, hundreds of locals gathered and took down the flag of the [Luhansk People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_People's_Republic), burning it and singing the [Ukrainian national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Anthem_of_Ukraine). The pro-Russian forces dispersed the pro-Ukrainian meeting with shots in the air.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

On 11 August 2022, Askyar Laishev, a former law enforcement officer and collaborator, was killed in a car bombing on Shevchenko Street.[^cite_note-16]

In early September 2022, Ukraine launched a major [counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ukrainian_eastern_counteroffensive) in the region. On 13 September, Ukrainian [Governor of Luhansk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Luhansk_Oblast), [Serhiy Haidai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serhiy_Haidai), stated that Russian forces had fled Starobilsk adding that the city was "practically empty".[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] As for December 2022, the latter claim was proven to be false, as the Ukrainian counteroffensive has [stalled outside Svatove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Svatove%E2%80%93Kreminna_line), around 60 km from Starobilsk.[^cite_note-19]

On 1 April 2024 a car bomb in the centre of Starobilsk killed the pro-Russian Deputy Head of the Centre for Servicing Educational Organisations of the Luhansk People’s Republic, Valerii Chaika.[^cite_note-20]

On 22 May 2026, a [drone strike](83243266.md) hit a student dormitory and educational buildings of Starobilsk College of Luhansk Pedagogical University. Russian and Russian-installed officials attributed the attack to Ukraine. 21 people were killed while 42 others were injured.[^cite_note-21]
