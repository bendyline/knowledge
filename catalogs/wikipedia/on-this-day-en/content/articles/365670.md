**Indore** ([/ɪnˈdɔːr/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/64/Indore.ogg/Indore.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/64/Indore.ogg/Indore.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [ISO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_15919): *Indaura*, Hindi: [\[ɪn̪d̪ɔːr\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Hindi_and_Urdu)) is the largest city of the [Indian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_and_union_territory_capitals_in_India) of [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md) where it serves as the [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_and_union_territory_capitals_in_India) and the administrative headquarters of the eponymous [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_district) and [division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_division). Modern-day Indore was established on the banks of the [Kanh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanh_River) and [Saraswati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraswati_River_\(Madhya_Pradesh\)) rivers and traces its roots to its 16th-century founding as a trading hub between the [Deccan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau) and [Delhi](37756.md).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Following the conquest of [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa) by [Peshwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) [Baji Rao I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baji_Rao_I), the city came under the control of the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Confederacy) until its annexation by the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Prior to [Indian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement), [the Kingdom of Indore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_State) was a nineteen [Gun Salute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salute_state) [Princely State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_State) of the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) with the [Holkars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Holkar) as rulers.[^cite_note-15]

With an estimated population of 5.6 million in 2025 distributed over nearly 10 thousand square kilometres, the metropolitan region encompassing Indore, [Ujjain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjain), [Dewas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewas), [Pithampur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pithampur) and [Nagda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagda) serves as the financial capital of Madhya Pradesh. One of India's main industrial hubs, the metropolitan region is a major center for pharmaceutical and automotive manufacturing with, notably, [Asia’s longest test track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATRAX).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-\:6-20]

The largest education hub in central India, it is home to an [Indian Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Technology_Indore) and an [Indian Institute of Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institute_of_Management_Indore) and is one of the cities selected under the [central government's](553883.md) flagship [Smart Cities Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Cities_Mission) and [Swachh Survekshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swachh_Survekshan) programmes.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

Consistently ranked as India's [cleanest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cleanest_cities_in_India), Indore is regularly commended for its clean air and proper waste management.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

## Etymology

[Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) inscriptions name Indore as "Indrapura".[^cite_note-auto1-31] It is believed that the city is named after its Indreshwar Mahadev Temple, where [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva) is the presiding deity.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] It is believed that [Indra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indra) himself did *[Tapasya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapas_\(Indian_religions\))* (meditation) in this place and led sage Swami Indrapuri to establish the temple. Later, [Tukoji Rao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tukoji_Rao_Holkar) who belonged to the Holkar clan of the Marathas and was the feudatory of Indore, renovated the temple.[^cite_note-34] The older name of Indore is believed to be **Indur**, as referenced in historical records.[^cite_note-35] In 2017, a proposal was brought forward by the Indore Municipal Corporation to rename the city as "Indur".[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

## History

### Gupta Empire (Gupta era)

[Gupta Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) inscription mentions Indore as city/town of Indrapura in Gupta Indore Copper plate inscription dated 146 Gupta era or 465 CE.[^cite_note-38] These are also some of the earliest mentions of Indore where the city is mentioned as 'Indrapura'.[^cite_note-39] Indrapura (modern day Indore) was then known for its sun temple, where in 464–65 CE, Gupta king [Skandagupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skandagupta) had made an endowment for the permanent maintenance of the city's sun temple. The temple was constructed by two merchants of the city – Achalavarman and Bhṛikuṇṭhasiṁha.[^cite_note-auto1-31][^cite_note-40]

### Holkar era (Maratha Empire)

Main article: [Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holkar)

During the [Mughal era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire), the area under the modern Indore district was equally divided between the administrations (*[sarkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarkar_\(administrative_division\))*&#x73;) of [Ujjain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjain) and [Mandu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandu,_Madhya_Pradesh). Kampel was the headquarters of a *mahal* (administrative unit) under the Ujjain *sarkar* of [Malwa Subah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa_Subah) (province). The area of the modern Indore city was included in the [Kampel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampel,_Indore) [pargana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pargana) (administrative unit).[^cite_note-gazetteer_1971-41]

In 1715, the [Marathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) invaded this region (Mughal territory) and demanded [chauth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauth) (tax) from the Mughal *Amil* (administrator) of Kampel. The Amil fled to [Ujjain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjain), and the local [zamindars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamindar) agreed to pay Chauth to the Marathas. The chief zamindar, [Nandlal Chaudhary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rao_Nandlal_Chaudhary) (later known as Nandlal Mandloi), paid a chauth of around Rs. 25,000 to the Marathas. [Jai Singh II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jai_Singh_II), the Mughal Governor of Malwa, reached Kampel on 8 May 1715, and defeated the Marathas in a battle near the village. The Marathas came back in early 1716, and raided Kampel in 1717. In March 1718, the Marathas, led by [Santaji Bhonsale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhonsle_\(clan\)), invaded Malwa again, but were unsuccessful this time.

By 1720, the headquarters of the local pargana were transferred from Kampel to Indore, due to the increasing commercial activity in the city. In 1724, the Marathas under the new [Peshwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) [Baji Rao I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baji_Rao_I), launched a fresh attack on the Mughals in Malwa. Baji Rao I himself led the campaign, accompanied by his lieutenants [Udaji Rao Pawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhar_State), [Malhar Rao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malhar_Rao_Holkar) and [Ranoji Scindia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranoji_Scindia). The Mughal [Nizam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qamar-ud-din_Khan,_Asaf_Jah_I) met the [Peshwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) at [Nalchha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalchha) on 18 May 1724, and acceded to his demand of collecting chauth from the area. The Peshwa returned to the [Deccan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan), but left [Malhar Rao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malhar_Rao_Holkar) at Indore to oversee the chauth collection.

The Marathas maintained friendly relations with Nandlal Chaudhary, who held influence over the local [Sardars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar) (chiefs). In 1728, they defeated the Mughals decisively in the battle of [Amjhera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_involving_the_Maratha_Empire), and consolidated their authority in the area over the next few years. On 3 October 1730, [Malhar Rao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malhar_Rao_Holkar) was appointed as the Maratha chief of [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa). The local zamindars, who had the title of [Chaudhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chowdhury), came to be known as *Mandloi*s (after [mandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil), an administrative unit) during the [Maratha reign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire). The [Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Holkar) dynasty of the Marathas, which controlled the region, conferred the title of *Rao* upon the local zamindar family.[^cite_note-42] After Nandlal died, his son Tejkarana was accepted as the *Mandloi* of Kampel by the [Peshwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) [Baji Rao I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baji_Rao_I). The pargana was formally granted to [Malhar Rao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malhar_Rao_Holkar) by merging 28 and one-half Pargana by the Peshwa in 1733. The pargana headquarters were transferred back to Kampel during his reign. After his death, his daughter-in-law [Ahilyabai Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahilyabai_Holkar) moved the headquarters to Indore in 1766. The [tehsil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) of Kampel was converted into Indore tehsil by a change in the name.[^cite_note-gazetteer_1971-41] Ahilyabai Holkar moved the state's capital to [Maheshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maheshwar) in 1767, but Indore remained an important commercial and military centre.

### Princely State (Indore/Holkar State)

Main article: [Indore State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_State)

See also: [Dewas Junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewas_Junior), [Dewas Senior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewas_Senior), [Dhar State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhar_State), and [Gwalior State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior_State)



[Ahilyabai Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahilyabai_Holkar) on a 2025 stamp of India

[Ahalyabai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahilyabai_Holkar) Holkar is fondly remembered as a noble, saintly and courageous woman. She ruled the kingdom of Indore (which was then part of the wider [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire)) for several decades. Her rule is remembered as a golden age in Indore's history. From an agricultural background herself, she married Khande Rao, prince of Indore. Thereafter, she resided in the Royal Palace. Later, she was trained in statecraft and accompanied the army to war on many occasions. At that time the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) (which was founded by [Chhatrapati Shivaji Raje Bhonsale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivaji)) was at the apex of its power. There were frequent battles and skirmishes, both against foreigners as well as internal feuds. In one such battle in 1754, [Ahalyabai's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahilyabai_Holkar) husband was killed. Her aged father-in-law ([Malhar Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malhar_Rao_Holkar)) was shattered at the death of his son. He summoned Ahalyabai, who he loved deeply, and said: "You are now my son. I wish that you look after my kingdom".[Malhar Rao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malhar_Rao_Holkar) died in 1766, 12 years after the death of his son Khande Rao. Malhar Rao's grandson and Khande Rao's only son [Male Rao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Male_Rao_Holkar) became the ruler of Indore in 1766, under Ahilya Bai's regency, but he too died within few months in April 1767. Ahilya Bai became the ruler of Indore after the death of her son with Khande Rao. [Ahilya Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahilyabai_Holkar) was a great pioneer and builder of Hindu temples who constructed hundreds of temples and [Dharmashalas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharamshala_\(type_of_building\)) throughout India.

In 1818, the [Holkars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Holkar) were defeated by the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) during the [Third Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Anglo-Maratha_War), in the [Battle of Mahidpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mahidpur) by which the capital was again moved from Maheshwar to Indore. A residency with British residents was established at Indore, but Holkars continued to rule [Indore State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_State) as a [princely state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) mainly due to efforts of their [Dewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewan) Tatya Jog. During that time, Indore has established the headquarters of the British Central Agency. [Ujjain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjain) was originally the commercial centre of Malwa. But the British administrators such as [John Malcolm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Malcolm) decided to promote Indore as an alternative to Ujjain because the merchants of Ujjain had supported anti-British elements.[^cite_note-AF_1998-43]

In 1906 electric supply was started in the city, the fire brigade was established in 1909, and 1918, the first master-plan of the city was made by noted architect and town planner, [Patrick Geddes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Geddes).[^cite_note-Geddes-44] During the period of [Maharaja Tukoji Rao Holkar II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tukojirao_Holkar_II) (1852–86) efforts were made for the planned development and industrial development of Indore. With the introduction of Railways in 1875, the business in Indore flourished during the reigns of [Maharaja Shivaji Rao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivajirao_Holkar), [Maharaja Tukoji Rao Holkar III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tukojirao_Holkar_III) and [Maharaja Yeshwant Rao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshwantrao_Holkar_II).

### Post-independence

After India's independence in 1947, Holkar State, along with several neighbouring princely states, acceded to the Indian Union. On 1 November 1956, when [Madhya Bharat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Bharat) was merged into [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md), Indore became part of the newly formed state. Indore a city today of nearly 4.5 million (2018) residents, has been transformed from a traditional commercial urban centre into the modern dynamic commercial capital of the state. In December 2025, a [drinking water contamination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Indore_drinking_water_contamination) incident occurred in Bhagirathpura, a neighbourhood of Indore. The incident resulted in the deaths of 21 people, while approximately 1,400 residents experienced gastrointestinal symptoms consistent with a waterborne infection.[^cite_note-47]

## Geography

### Climate

Indore lies on a borderline between a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) (*Aw*) and a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cwa*). Because of its high elevation the climate is much more moderate compared to the *[North Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Indian)* plains, even during the hottest months the nights are relatively cool, which is known as *[Shab-e-Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shab-e-Malwa)*.[^cite_note-48] Three distinct seasons are observed: summer, monsoon and winter. The coldest temperature was 1.1 °C (34.0 °F) on 27 December 1936.[^cite_note-49]

Indore gets moderate rainfall of 700 to 800 mm (28 to 31 in) during June–September due to the [southwest monsoon](57630.md).

## Demographics

See also: [List of cities in Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Madhya_Pradesh)

Indore is the most populous city in Madhya Pradesh. It is also the largest [metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis) city in Central India. According to the 2011 census of India, the population of Indore city (the area under the municipal corporation and outgrowths) was 1,994,397.[^cite_note-CensusGov2011-7][^cite_note-census_city-61] The population of the Indore metropolis ([urban agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_agglomeration) that includes neighbouring areas) was 2,170,295.[^cite_note-census_metro-8] In 2011, the city had a population density of 25,170 people per square mile (9,718 per square km), rendering it the most densely populated of all the municipalities with population over 100,000 in [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md). As per the 2011 census, 1,502,775 were literates, which translates to 75.4% of the total population, with an effective literacy rate (of population 7 years and above) 85.5%, with male literacy of 89.8% and female literacy was 81.2%.[^cite_note-CensusGov2011-7] The [Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_and_Scheduled_Tribes) populations in 2011 were 309,229 and 54,075, respectively. The total number of households were 404711.[^cite_note-CensusGov2011-7]

According to the 2011 census of India, the population of Indore city (the area under the municipal corporation and outgrowths) was 1,994,397.[^cite_note-62] As per estimates, the population in 2025 is approximately 3,482,830.[^cite_note-63]

## Government and politics

### Civic administration

Main article: [Indore Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_Municipal_Corporation)

Indore has a city government, or municipality, with a mayor-council form of government. In 1870, the first municipality was constituted in Indore. Bakshi Khuman Singh (Commander In Chief) of Holkar State Army was appointed as chairman.[^cite_note-\:3-71] Indore municipality became the first city to have an elected municipal government responsible for the welfare and growth of the city.[^cite_note-\:3-71] In 1956, during the reorganisation of states, Indore was included in [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md), and later that same year, was declared a [municipal corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_corporation).[^cite_note-\:3-71]

[Indore Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_Municipal_Corporation) (IMC) is spread over an area of 269 square km.[^cite_note-villages-5][^cite_note-72] The Indore Municipal Corporation (IMC) is a unicameral body consisting of 69 Council members whose districts are divided into 12 zones and these zones had been further divided into 69 [wards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_\(electoral_subdivision\)) defined by geographic population boundaries.[^cite_note-73] In 2014, 29 villages were added into the fold of the municipality.[^cite_note-74] In 2015, 23 more villages were added. After these inclusions, the number of wards went up to 85, and zones 19.[^cite_note-villages-5][^cite_note-75]

The mayor and [councillors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Councillor) are elected to five-year terms. The municipal or local elections to Indore Municipal Corporation were last held in 2015.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] The next elections were to be held early in 2020, but as per a report from February 2020, these have not happened, although preparation of voter rolls and ward reservation happened later in July.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] The elected wing of the city government is headed by the Mayor and the incumbent Mayor is Pushya Mitra Bhargava. As per a report from the [Free Press Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Free_Press_Journal), Indore was notified in 2018 for not having constituted ward committees.[^cite_note-81] The executive wing is headed by Pratibha Pal, the [municipal commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Commissioner_\(India\)) of Indore, who is also the first female commissioner of the city.[^cite_note-82]

On 8 May 2020, the budget for of ₹ 4,763 crore for 2020–21 was approved for the civic body.[^cite_note-83] Major sources of revenue for the municipality include collection of property tax, water tax, rent.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

Indore municipality follows the guidelines as per the [Madhya Pradesh Municipal Corporations Act 1956](http://www.janaagraha.org/asics/report/Madhya-Pradesh-Municipal-Corporation-Act-1956.pdf) [Archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20180920174748/http://www.janaagraha.org/asics/report/Madhya-Pradesh-Municipal-Corporation-Act-1956.pdf) 20 September 2018 at the [Wayback Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayback_Machine), as well as the [Madhya Pradesh Municipalities Act, 1961](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/3583/1/Municipal%20Corporation%20ACT%201961.pdf).[^cite_note-86] The IMC is responsible for public education, correctional institutions, libraries, public safety, recreational facilities, sanitation, water supply, local planning, and welfare services.

### Other agencies

Main article: [Indore Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_Development_Authority)

Most of the regions surrounding the city are administered by the [Indore Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_Development_Authority) (IDA). IDA works as an apex body for planning and co-ordination of development activities in the Indore Metropolitan Region (IMR) comprising Indore and its agglomeration covering an area of 398.72 km<sup>2</sup> (153.95 sq mi). Primarily, IDA develops new residential areas. During the early stage of development of such areas, the IDA is responsible for developing basic infrastructure. Once a sizeable number of plots are sold, the area is formally transferred to the IMC, which is then responsible for the maintenance of the infrastructure in the area.[^cite_note-105]

The IDA consists of two appointed components; the [collector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_collector) of the [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_district), who has executive powers, and the IDA Board which includes a chairman appointed by [Government of Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Madhya_Pradesh), [Municipal Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Commissioner_\(India\)) of Indore and five members form Town and Country Planning Department, Forest Department, Public Health Engineering, Public Works Department and MP Electricity Board[^cite_note-106] who scrutinise the collector's decisions and can accept or reject his budget proposals each year. The role of IDA is to implement the master plan for Indore prepared by the Town and Country Office, Bhopal.[^cite_note-107] The headquarters of the IDA is at Race Course Road, Indore.[^cite_note-108]

## Civic utilities

Electricity in Indore is supplied by the Madhya Pradesh Paschim Kshetra Vidyut Vitaran Company Limited, the state's agency.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]

Major source of water in Indore are [Narmada River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narmada_River) and [overhead tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_tank).[^cite_note-\:4-111] In 2016, the central government sanctioned 70 crores under the [AMRUT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Mission_for_Rejuvenation_and_Urban_Transformation) scheme to overhaul the water facilities.[^cite_note-\:4-111] Indore used to receive 350 MLD of water from Jalud pumping station on Narmada river; while in 2019 the quantity reduced by 100 MLD, as of 2020 it has regained its capacity.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] The municipality supplies around 450 MLD water through piped connections to parts of the town on alternate days, other parts of the city have shortage of water.[^cite_note-114] The urban poor and unauthorised slum area face a massive shortage of water, and need to purchases private water in drums, or need to have to access private tubewells.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] A joint study done by [UN Habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Human_Settlements_Programme), IMC and [WaterAid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WaterAid) mapping poverty surveyed 176545 households (approximately 1/3rd of the households in the city), and found that 72% of the households did not have access to piped water supply.[^cite_note-117]

As of 2012, Indore generated nearly 240 MLD of sewage.[^cite_note-118] As per a report from August 2018, Indore achieved 100% treatment of the faecal matter generated in the city.[^cite_note-\:5-119] The city has three [sewage treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_treatment) plants (STPs), which includes a [sequencing batch reactor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequencing_batch_reactor) with a capacity of 245 MLD, the largest in the world, as well as a 78 MLD and 12 MLD [Upflow anaerobic sludge blanket digestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upflow_anaerobic_sludge_blanket_digestion) at Kabitkhedi and a third with a capacity of 122 MLD.[^cite_note-\:5-119]

As of 2019, Indore generates over 1,115 [metric tons](31185.md) (MT) of garbage a day, of which 650 MT is [wet waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_sorting) and 465 MT is [dry waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_sorting).[^cite_note-\:5-119] The municipality has 100% door to door collection and segregation.[^cite_note-\:5-119] The city has a Centralized Processing Unit situated at Devguradia, Nemawar Road over an area of 146 acres. Waste collected at the garbage transfer stations in city is weighed, compressed and moved to this site for final processing.[^cite_note-\:5-119] Indore set up a Plastic Collection Centre (PCC) to reuse and recycle the city's [plastic waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_pollution) and installed a plastic cleansing machine known as a 'Phatka Machine'.[^cite_note-\:5-119]

## Culture

### Food

Indore's culinary culture has a blend of Maharashtrian, Malwi, Rajasthani and Gujarati influence. The street food of the city is especially popular. Two of the most noteworthy street food places in Indore are [Chappan Dukan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chappan_Dukan) and [Sarafa Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarafa_Bazaar).[^cite_note-\:2-135]

As part of the Smart City project, the Chappan Dukan area has been developed as a smart food street. The cost of this project is ₹ 40 million and has been implemented in a 45-day period.[^cite_note-136]

Sarafa Bazaar, which is India's only night street-food market, attracts large crowds from the city and tourists from various places.[^cite_note-\:1-137]

Indore is also notable for its wide variety of *namkeen*s or savoury items and has many popular *namkeen* stores across the city. Most popular food items of Indore include poha, kachori, samosa, jalebi, gulab jamun, rabdi, gajak, imarti, bhel, pani puri, hot dog, egg banjo, moong bhajiya, moong daal halwa, dahi wada, [sabudana khichri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabudana_khichri), sabudana wada, *[dhokla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhokla)*, jeeravan, and sev.[^cite_note-\:1-137]

The city is debatably the most vegetarian city in India; nearly 49% of population is vegetarian at some level.[^cite_note-138]

### Festivals

Rang Panchami is one of the most vibrant festivals celebrated in Indore, marked by a grand procession known as the "Ger".[^cite_note-142] Unlike the Holi festival celebrated across India, Rang Panchami in Indore has a distinct cultural identity. Thousands gather in the old city area as traditional music, dancing, and colour-throwing fill the streets. Organised by local groups and supported by the municipal corporation, the event includes decorated trucks, DJs, and water cannons spraying coloured water. The celebration showcases Indore’s unique blend of tradition and modernity.

### Public transport

Main article: [Indore City Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_City_Bus)

Indore's City Bus transport system runs through 277 km (172 mi) of road with a daily ridership of over 140,000.[^cite_note-147] Atal Indore City Transport Services Ltd, a PPP scheme operates buses and radio taxis in the city. The buses designated as City Bus operate on 64 Routes[^cite_note-\:0-148] with a fleet of 487 buses, with 421 bus stops.[^cite_note-\:0-148] The buses are colour-coded into three colours: Blue, Magenta and Orange according to their route.[^cite_note-149]

Indore BRTS (iBUS) is a bus rapid transit system with 53 air-conditioned[^cite_note-150] buses equipped with services like GPS and IVR which are used to track the position of the bus with information displayed on LED displays installed at the bus stops.

Indore Auto Rickshaw is a service for small distance travel. Daily approx. 500,000 people travel within the city.

[Indore Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_Metro) is a [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system that has completed its successful first phase trail run.[^cite_note-151]

Indore Cable Car was announced in January 2021 as another public transport for the city to decongest traffic. It will also be the first cable car in India to run on crowded streets.[^cite_note-152]

Buses for other cities which are near Indore are operated by state transport and private transport operators. Cities like Ujjain, Dewas, Khandwa, Khargone are connected through daily road buses.

## Cityscape

### Rajwada Palace



Rajwada palace

[Rajwada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajwada) Palace is a historical palace in Indore city. It was built by the [Holkars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Holkar) of the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) about two centuries ago. This seven-storied structure is located near the [Chhatris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishnapura_Chhatri).

### Shiv Vilas Palace

[Shiv Vilas Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiv_Vilas_Palace) is a royal palace built during the reign of the Holkar dynasty. Also called the 'new palace', it was built after the Rajwada palace was damaged in an attack. It is on the right side of the Rajwada palace.

### Gomatgiri

Gomatgiri is a revered Jain pilgrimage site situated on a hill near Indore, Madhya Pradesh, India. Spread over an expanse of lush greenery, this serene location is home to a majestic 21-foot statue of Gomateshwar, also known as Bahubali, which mirrors the famous statue located in Shravanabelagola, Karnataka. Established in 1981, Gomatgiri offers a tranquil retreat for spiritual seekers and visitors alike, with its picturesque views and spiritually uplifting atmosphere. The complex houses 24 marble temples, each dedicated to one of the 24 [Tirthankaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirthankaras) of [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism), symbolising the religion's rich heritage and teachings. This place is not only a spiritual centre but also a popular tourist attraction, drawing visitors from all corners for its architectural beauty and the peace it offers away from the bustle of city life.

### Kanch Mandir

[Kanch Mandir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanch_Mandir), literally 'temple of glass', is a famous Jain temple in Indore, built by Sir Seth Hukumchand Jain. Its construction began sometime around 1903.

### Khajrana Ganesh Mandir

[Khajrana Ganesh Mandir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khajrana_Ganesh_Temple) is a pilgrimage centre in Indore, dedicated to [Lord Ganapati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesha). The current temple was built during the reign of [Holkar Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holkar_Dynasty). The main Idol of the temple was placed in a well to save it from the mughal ruler Aurangzeb. It was recovered by the Holkar ruler [Maharani Ahilya Bai Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahilyabai_Holkar). It is situated in the Khajrana area, a little distance away from Kalka Mata Temple.

### Lalbagh Palace



Lalbagh Palace

[Lalbagh Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalbagh_Palace) was built by the [Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Holkar) Dynasty between 1886 and 1921. The interior is beautified with striated Italian marble pillars, many chandeliers and classical columns, murals of Greek deities, a baroque-cum-rococo dining room, an English-library-style office with leather armchairs, a Renaissance sitting room, and a Palladian queen's bedroom which was the fashion among many of the late Raj-era Indian nobility, accompanied by a billiards room. There is imitation of the [Buckingham Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_Palace) gates creak at the entrance along with 28-hectare ground, where, near to the palace, stands the statue of [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria).[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176]

### Manik Bagh

[Manik Bagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manik_Bagh) palace was built in 1930 when Maharajah Yashwant Rao Holkar II commissioned the construction of the Manik Bagh ("Jewel Garden") palace. The architect was [Eckart Muthesius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eckart_Muthesius) (1904–1989) from Germany. The maharaja was at a young age at that time, as was Muthesius who was just a couple of years older. The work outside and inside was done in a late [art deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_deco) and the [international style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Style_\(architecture\)) of [modern architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_architecture).[^cite_note-177]

### Yeshwant Club



The [Yeshwant Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshwant_Club), established in 1934

The [Yeshwant Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshwant_Club) came into existence in 1934 at the behest of late [the Maharaja Tukoji Rao III Holkar of Indore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tukojirao_Holkar_III). The club was established for their son, [Yuvraj Yeshwant Rao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshwantrao_Holkar_II). Spread over 14 acres, it is a [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) legacy of the [Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Holkar) rulers of [Indore State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_State). Initially, the club was opened for [Maratha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha) royalty, nobility, aristocracy, and the officers (Natives and British) of the [Holkar State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indore_State). Later its doors were opened for the business elites. Post-Indian Independence, the admission criteria were revised according to the changing times. Maharani Usha Devi, the daughter of late [Maharaja Yeshwant Rao II Holkar of Indore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeshwantrao_Holkar_II) is the chief patron of the club, the Honorary Chief Minister of [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md) being the president of the club.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181]

### Sirpur Lake



Birds at Sirpur wetland

The [Sirpur Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirpur_Lake) is the most important bird-watching site in [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa). It is a [Ramsar site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ramsar_Sites_in_India) located on Dhar Road in Indore. It is known for more than 180 species of resident and migratory birds. It was restored to a viable bird habitat from a heavily encroached pool over four decades since 1980 by [Padmashri Bhalu Mondhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhalchandra_Dattatray_Mondhe) and his NGO The Nature Volunteers, and was declared an [Important Bird and Biodiversity Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Important_Bird_and_Biodiversity_Area) in 2015–16.

Indore received accreditation under the Ramsar Convention's Wetland City Accreditation Scheme alongside Udaipur, making it one of India's first two Ramsar Wetland Cities.[^cite_note-\:02-182]

The city's ecological planning heritage dates to 1912, when British planner [Patrick Geddes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Geddes) incorporated environmental considerations into his layout design. A key wetland associated with Indore's accreditation is [Sirpur Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirpur_Lake). Under the Ramsar framework, accredited cities must provide progress updates every three years and demonstrate cross-sectoral accountability in wetland management. The accreditation of Indore was facilitated by Wetlands International South Asia (WISA) as a knowledge partner. Dr. Ritesh Kumar of WISA noted that Colombo's experience, where flooding of the national parliament building, constructed in a wetland, forced a complete rethinking of [city planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning), illustrates the global lesson that integrating ecological resilience into urban development is not optional but a practical necessity.[^cite_note-\:02-182]

It is one of the most important bird habitats in [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa) region. Another Ramsar site near Indore is [Yashwant Sagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yashwant_Sagar).

### Pitra Parvat

Pitra Parvat is known for the statue of Lord Hanuman situated there. The sculpture is made with 8 elements in golden colour. It took 125 workers and seven years to complete the statue of the deity [Lord Hanuman.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman) Also present there, is a temple that has the small statue of [Lord Hanuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman) for the devotees to [worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worship). [Lord Hanuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman) is seen sitting in the lap of *Mother* *Anjani* in this temple.[^cite_note-183]

Pitreshwar Hanuman Temple, located at Pitru Parvat on Bijasan Road in Indore, is known for its 72-foot tall [Ashtadhatu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashtadhatu) statue of Lord Hanuman. Unlike typical depictions, this statue presents Hanuman in a meditative posture, making it a unique spiritual and cultural landmark. The site has become a popular tourist destination, combining religious significance with natural beauty and environmental awareness.

### Annapurna Temple

Annapurna Temple, located in Indore, Madhya Pradesh, is a prominent Hindu temple dedicated to Goddess Annapurna, the deity of nourishment. Inspired by the architecture of South Indian temples, the temple features a towering gopuram, intricate carvings, and vibrant sculptures. It is not only a place of worship but also a cultural and architectural attraction in the city. The temple complex also houses shrines dedicated to Lord Shiva, Lord Hanuman, and Kal Bhairav. It is a popular destination for devotees and tourists alike.
