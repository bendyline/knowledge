**Peter "Fulton" John Sheen** (May 8, 1895 – December 9, 1979) was an [American Catholic](410868.md) [prelate](185996.md) who served as the [Bishop of Rochester](2119305.md) from 1966 to 1969. He was known for his preaching, especially on television and radio.

Ordained a priest of the [Diocese of Peoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Peoria) in Illinois in 1919, Sheen quickly became a renowned theologian, earning the [Cardinal Mercier Prize for International Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Mercier_Prize) in 1923. He went on to teach theology and philosophy at the [Catholic University of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_America) in Washington, D.C. and served as a parish priest before he was appointed an auxiliary bishop for the [Archdiocese of New York](245234.md) in 1951. He held this position until 1966, when he was made Bishop of Rochester. He resigned in 1969 as his 75th birthday approached and was made archbishop of the [titular see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_see) of [Newport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Newport_and_Menevia) (a diocese in [Wales](69894.md)).

For 20 years as "Father Sheen," later [monsignor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsignor), he hosted the night-time radio program *[The Catholic Hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Catholic_Hour)* on [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Radio) (1930–1950) before he moved to television and presented *[Life Is Worth Living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_Is_Worth_Living)* (1952–1957). Sheen's final presenting role was on the syndicated *[The Fulton Sheen Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fulton_Sheen_Program)* (1961–1968) with a format that was very similar to that of the earlier *Life Is Worth Living* show. For that work, Sheen won an [Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award) for Most Outstanding Television Personality, and was featured on the [cover of *Time* magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_covers_of_Time_magazine_\(1950s\)).[^cite_note-CUA-bio-2] Starting in 2009, his shows were being re-broadcast on the [EWTN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EWTN) and the [Trinity Broadcasting Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Broadcasting_Network)'s [Church Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Channel) cable networks.[^cite_note-foundation-3] His contribution to televised preaching resulted in Sheen often being called one of the first [televangelists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Televangelists).[^cite_note-4]

The cause for his [canonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonization) was officially opened in 2002. Sheen was scheduled to be beatified in Peoria on December 21, 2019, but this was postponed after Bishop [Salvatore Matano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Ronald_Matano) of Rochester expressed concern that Sheen's alleged assignment of a priest who had been the subject of a 1963 [sexual misconduct](457212.md) case might be cited unfavorably in a forthcoming report from the [New York Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Attorney_General). The Diocese of Peoria countered that the priest had been assigned not by Sheen but by his successor, and that Sheen had been "exonerated" following thorough examination.[^cite_note-delay-5]

On September 24, 2026, Sheen was [beatified](233724.md) in [St. Louis](27687.md), by Cardinal [Luis Antonio Tagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Antonio_Tagle) on the mandate of [Pope Leo XIV](54520372.md).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-causesanti.va-7]

## Early life

Sheen was born on May 8, 1895, in [El Paso, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso,_Illinois), the oldest of four sons of Newton and Delia Sheen. His parents were of Irish descent, and their own parents were from [Croghan, County Roscommon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croghan,_County_Roscommon), [Connacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connacht). He was baptized as "Peter John" and called "P.J." as a boy. However, he came to prefer the name "Fulton", his mother's maiden name.[^cite_note-cause-bio-1][^cite_note-time19520414-8] As an infant, Sheen contracted [tuberculosis](30653.md).[^cite_note-9]

After the family had moved to nearby [Peoria, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoria,_Illinois), Sheen's first role in the Catholic Church was as an [altar boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar_boy) at [Cathedral of Saint Mary of the Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Mary_of_the_Immaculate_Conception_\(Peoria,_Illinois\)) in Peoria.[^cite_note-cause-bio-1][^cite_note-time19520414-8]

Sheen graduated in 1913 from high school at [Spalding Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Our_Lady/Spalding_Institute) in Peoria with valedictorian honors. In 1913 he entered [St. Viator College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Viator_College) in [Bourbonnais, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbonnais,_Illinois) and graduated in 1917.

Deciding to become a priest, he started his studies at [Saint Paul Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Paul_Seminary_School_of_Divinity) in St. Paul, Minnesota.

## Ordination and further education

Sheen was ordained a priest for the Diocese of Peoria at the Cathedral of Saint Mary on September 20, 1919, by Bishop [Edmund Dunne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Michael_Dunne).[^cite_note-cause-bio-1] After his 1919 ordination, Sheen continued his studies at the [Catholic University of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_America) in Washington, D.C.[^cite_note-time19520414-8][^cite_note-FJSweb-10] He celebrated his first Christmas Mass at St. Mark Parish in Peoria.[^cite_note-11] His youthful appearance was still evident on one occasion when a local priest, unaware of his identity, asked Sheen to assist as [altar boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar_boy) during the celebration of the Mass.[^cite_note-time19520414-8]

After finishing his studies at Catholic University of America, he entered the [Catholic University of Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_Leuven_\(1834%E2%80%931968\)) in [Belgium](3343.md), earning a [Doctor of Philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Philosophy) degree in 1923.[^cite_note-FJSweb-10][^cite_note-12] His doctoral thesis was titled "The Spirit of Contemporary Philosophy and the Finite God".[^cite_note-13] At [Leuven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuven), he became the first American to win the [Cardinal Mercier Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Mercier_Prize_for_International_Philosophy) for the best philosophical treatise.[^cite_note-time19520414-8] In 1924, Sheen went to Rome to attend the [Pontificium Collegium Internationale Angelicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_University_of_Saint_Thomas_Aquinas).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

## Priestly life

After Sheen returned to Peoria in 1926, Bishop Dunne assigned him as curate at St. Patrick's, a poor parish in Peoria. At that time, both [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in New York and [Oxford University](31797.md) in England wanted Sheen to teach philosophy. However, Sheen took the assignment at St. Patrick's without any complaints and later said that he enjoyed his time there. Nine months later, Dunne sent Sheen to [Catholic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_America) to teach.[^cite_note-\:0-16]

However, instead of Catholic University of America, Sheen chose to teach theology at [St. Edmund's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Edmund's_College,_Ware) in Ware, England, where he met the priest [Ronald Knox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Knox). He also assisted the pastor at [St. Patrick's Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Patrick's_Church,_Soho_Square) in the Soho section of [London](17867.md). In 1928, Sheen finally returned to Catholic University of America, where he would teach philosophy until 1950.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-time19520414-8]

In 1950, Sheen became the national director of the [Society for the Propagation of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_for_the_Propagation_of_the_Faith) in the United States. During his 16 years as director, Sheen raised millions of dollars for missionary efforts worldwide. He also donated \$10 million that he earned from his later television programs.[^cite_note-cause-bio-1]

### Auxiliary Bishop of New York



Portait of Sheen as a priest

On May 28, 1951, [Pope Pius XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII) appointed Sheen as an auxiliary bishop for New York. He was consecrated in Rome at the Basilica of [Santi Giovanni e Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santi_Giovanni_e_Paolo_al_Celio) on June 11, 1951.[^cite_note-ch-18] The principal [consecrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecrator) was Cardinal [Adeodato Giovanni Piazza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adeodato_Giovanni_Piazza). The co-consecrators were Archbishop [Leone Giovanni Battista Nigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leone_Giovanni_Battista_Nigris) and Archbishop [Martin John O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_John_O'Connor).[^cite_note-ch-18]

### Bishop of Rochester

On October 21, 1966, [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) named Sheen as bishop of Rochester, N.Y.[^cite_note-ch-18] While serving in Rochester, Sheen created the Sheen Ecumenical Housing Foundation. He also denounced American involvement in the [Vietnam War](32611.md) in late July 1967.[^cite_note-19] On [Ash Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_Wednesday) in 1967, Sheen decided to give St. Bridget's Parish building to the federal [Housing and Urban Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_and_Urban_Development) program. Sheen wanted to let the government use it for [black Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Americans). There was a protest since Sheen acted on his own accord. The pastor disagreed and the deal fell through.[^cite_note-20]

On October 6, 1969, Sheen resigned as bishop of Rochester.[^cite_note-ch-18] Paul VI then named him as archbishop of the [titular see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_see) of [Newport, Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Wales).

### Ecumenical efforts

In the 1950's and 1960's, Sheen made notable efforts to seek common ground with Christians from non-Catholic churches, both [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy) and Protestant. He occasionally celebrated the Byzantine [Divine Liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Liturgy), with papal permission awarding him certain [bi-ritual faculties](214903.md#Bi-ritual_faculties).[^cite_note-21] He often commended the Protestant devotion to Bible study and stated that he himself used Protestant commentaries when preparing sermons.[^cite_note-Treasure1993-22]<sup>: 79 </sup>

Sheen's autobiography summarized his ecumenical outlook when he stated that "every religion in the world has a segment of that truth."[^cite_note-Treasure1993-22]<sup>: 148 </sup>

## Broadcasting career

### Radio

In 1930, Sheen began a weekly NBC Sunday-night radio broadcast, *The Catholic Hour*.[^cite_note-FJSweb-10] During one broadcast, Sheen termed [World War II](32927.md) as not just a political struggle but also a "theological one". He referred to German Chancellor [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md) as an example of the "[Anti-Christ.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Christ)"[^cite_note-23]*[Time Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* referred to Sheen in 1946 as "the golden-voiced Msgr. Fulton J. Sheen, U.S. Catholicism's famed proselytizer," and reported that his radio broadcast received 3,000 to 6,000 letters weekly from listeners. By 1950, *The Catholic Hour* had a weekly audience of four million listeners.[^cite_note-24]

### Television



Bishop Sheen on *[Life Is Worth Living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_Is_Worth_Living)* (1958)

&#x20;At the Catholic University of America, Sheen in 1940 provided [voice-over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice-over) commentary for an [Easter Sunday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Sunday) Mass. Broadcast on the experimental station [W2XBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W2XBS), it was one of the first televised religious services in the United States. During the sermon, Sheen remarked,&#x20;

> "This is the first religious television in the history of the world. Let, therefore, its first message be a tribute of thanks to God for giving the minds of our day the inspiration to unravel the secrets of the universe."[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

On February 12, 1952, Sheen began a weekly television program on the [DuMont Television Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuMont_Television_Network) called *[Life Is Worth Living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_Is_Worth_Living)*.[^cite_note-27] Filmed at the [Adelphi Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelphi_Theatre_\(New_York\)) in New York City, the program consisted of Sheen speaking extemporaneously before a live audience. The show did not have a script or [cue cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cue_cards), although Sheen occasionally used a [chalkboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackboard). He was not paid for the program. *Life is Worth Living* was scheduled in a [prime time](24973.md) slot on Tuesday nights at 8\:00 p.m. Its competition was the *[Texaco Star Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texaco_Star_Theater)* on [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC), a variety show starring comedian [Milton Berle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Berle) and the [*Frank Sinatra Show*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frank_Sinatra_Show_\(1950_TV_series\)), another [variety program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_show) on [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS).[^cite_note-\:2-28]

Defying all expectations, *Life is Worth Living* became very popular. The *Frank Sinatra Show* was canceled in April 1952, leading to speculation that it was due to the success of Sheen's show.[^cite_note-\:2-28] Berle, known as "Uncle Miltie," frequently used old [vaudeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaudeville) material on his show. Joking about Sheen, he said. "He uses old material, too." Berle also observed, "If I'm going to be eased off the top by anyone, it's better that I lose to the One for whom Bishop Sheen is speaking."[^cite_note-time19520414-8] Sheen responded to Berle, saying that perhaps people should start calling him "Uncle Fultie."[^cite_note-TVQ-29]

*[Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_\(magazine\))* and *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* magazines ran feature stories on Sheen. The number of stations carrying *Life Is Worth Living* jumped from three to fifteen in less than two months. The show started receiving 8,500 letters per week. The show received four times as many requests for studio tickets as could be fulfilled. The sponsor [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_\(electrical_appliances\)), a manufacturer of TVs and appliances, paid the production costs in return for a one-minute commercial at the show's opening and another minute at its close.[^cite_note-TVQ-29]

In 1952, Sheen won an [Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award) for Most Outstanding personalit&#x79;*.*[^cite_note-Trib09-30][^cite_note-\:3-31] In accepting his Emmy, Sheen, "I feel it is time I pay tribute to my four writers – Matthew, Mark, Luke and John." At that time, Berle quipped, "We both work for 'Sky Chief'", a reference to God and Berle's sponsor, [Texaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texaco). *Time* called Sheen "the first 'televangelist'" and the Archdiocese of New York could not meet the demand for tickets.[^cite_note-time19520414-8] Sheen received a second Emmy nomination in 1953.[^cite_note-\:3-31]

One of Sheen's best-remembered presentations came in February 1953, when he forcefully denounced the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). Sheen gave a dramatic reading of the burial scene from Shakespeare's *[Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar_\(play\)).* He substituted the names of Soviet leaders (Stalin, [Lavrenty Beria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavrenty_Beria), Georgy Malenkov, and [Andrey Vyshinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Vyshinsky)) for those of characters in the play (Julius Caesar, [Gaius Cassius Longinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaius_Cassius_Longinus), Marc Antony and [Marcus Junius Brutus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Junius_Brutus)). Sheen concluded the presentation by saying, "Stalin must one day meet his judgment." Days later, Stalin suffered a stroke and died within the week.[^cite_note-snopes-32]

*Life Is Worth Living* ran until 1957, drawing as many as 30 million people weekly, mostly non-Catholics.[^cite_note-33] He received his third Emmy nomination in 1957.

Sheen returned to television in 1961 with a nationally syndicated series, *The Fulton Sheen Program.* Running until 1968, it was broadcast first in black-and-white and later in color. The format of the series was essentially the same as *Life Is Worth Living*.

### International cassette tape ministry

In September 1974, Auxiliary Bishop [Thomas William Lyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_William_Lyons) of Washington asked Sheen to speak at a [retreat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retreat_\(religious\)) for [diocesan priests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocesan_priests) at the Loyola Retreat House in [Faulkner, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faulkner,_Maryland). Sheen's talks were recorded on [reel-to-reel tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reel-to-reel_audio_tape_recording).[^cite_note-ReferenceA-34]

Sheen requested the recorded talks be produced for distribution. This was the first production of a worldwide [cassette tape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassette_tape) ministry called Ministr-O-Media, a nonprofit company that operated on the grounds of St. Joseph's Parish in [Pomfret, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomfret,_Maryland). The retreat album was *Renewal and Reconciliation* and included nine 60-minute audiotapes.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-34]

### Evangelization

According to a 1952 article in *Time Magazine*, Sheen was responsible for converting to Catholicism the writer [Heywood Broun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heywood_Broun), the politician [Clare Boothe Luce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Boothe_Luce), the automaker [Henry Ford II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ford_II), the writer [Louis F. Budenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_F._Budenz), the union organizer [Bella Dodd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bella_Dodd), the theatrical designer [Jo Mielziner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo_Mielziner) and the violinist and composer [Fritz Kreisler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Kreisler).[^cite_note-HaddenLuce1952-35] Each conversion process took an average of 25 hours of lessons, and reportedly more than 95% of his students in private instruction were baptized.[^cite_note-time19520414-8]

## Falling-out with Cardinal Spellman



Cardinal Spellman in 1946

&#x20;In the foreword of the 2008 edition of Sheen's autobiography, *Treasure in Clay: The Autobiography of Fulton J. Sheen*, the journalist [Raymond Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Arroyo) explains why Sheen ended *Life Is Worth Living.*[^cite_note-Treasure2008-36] Arroyo wrote that "It is widely believed that Cardinal [Francis Spellman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Spellman) drove Sheen off the air."[^cite_note-Treasure2008-36]

> "In the late 1950s, the government donated millions of dollars' worth of powdered milk to the New York Archdiocese. In turn, Cardinal Spellman handed that milk over to the Society for the Propagation of the Faith to distribute to the poor of the world. On at least one occasion, he demanded that the director of the Society, Bishop Sheen, *pay* the Archdiocese for the donated milk. He wanted millions of dollars. Despite Cardinal Spellman's considerable powers of persuasion and influence in Rome, Sheen refused."[^cite_note-Treasure2008-36]

Sheen pleaded his case about the funds directly to [Pope Pius XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII), who sided with him. Spellman later confronted Sheen and stated, "I will get even with you. It may take six months or ten years, but everyone will know what you are like."[^cite_note-Treasure2008-36] Besides being pressured to leave television, Sheen also "found himself unwelcome in the churches of New York City. Spellman canceled Sheen's annual Good Friday sermons at [St. Patrick's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Patrick's_Cathedral_\(Manhattan\)) and discouraged clergy from socializing with him."[^cite_note-Treasure2008-36] In 1966, Spellman allegedly persuaded Pope Paul VI to appoint Sheen as bishop of Rochester and terminate his leadership at the Society for the Propagation of the Faith.[^cite_note-Treasure2008-36] Sheen never talked about the quarrel with Spellman. In his autobiography, Sheen even went so far as to praise the cardinal.[^cite_note-Treasure2008-36]

## Later years

On October 15, 1969, one month after celebrating his 50th anniversary as a priest, Sheen resigned as bishop of Rochester. Pope Paul VI then appointed him as [archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop) of the [titular see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_see) of [Newport, Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Newport_and_Menevia). The ceremonial position allowed Sheen to devote more time to his extensive writing. During his lifetime, Sheen wrote 73 books and numerous articles and columns.[^cite_note-Trib09-30]

The actor Ramón Gerard Antonio Estévez adopted the stage name of [Martin Sheen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Sheen) partly in admiration of the bishop.[^cite_note-37]

On October 2, 1979, two months before Sheen's death, [Pope John Paul II](23805.md) visited St. Patrick's Cathedral during his [papal visit to the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_visits_to_the_United_States). During the ceremony, the pope embraced Sheen and said, "You have written and spoken well of the Lord Jesus Christ. You are a loyal son of the Church."[^cite_note-38]

## Death and legacy

Beginning in 1977, Sheen "underwent a series of surgeries that sapped his strength and even made preaching difficult."[^cite_note-Treasure2008-36] Throughout that time, he continued to work on his autobiography, parts of which "were recited from his sickbed as he clutched a crucifix."[^cite_note-Treasure2008-36] Soon after that, Sheen underwent [open-heart surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-heart_surgery) at [Lenox Hill Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenox_Hill_Hospital) in Manhattan.[^cite_note-Trib09-30]

Sheen died on December 9, 1979, in his private chapel while praying before the [Blessed Sacrament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blessed_Sacrament).[^cite_note-CUA-bio-2] He was interred in the crypt of [St. Patrick's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Patrick's_Cathedral,_New_York) near the deceased [archbishops of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishops_of_New_York). In 2019, after a long legal battle, the archdiocese transferred Sheen's remains to the Diocese of Peoria, which re-interred them in the Cathedral of St. Mary in Peoria.[^cite_note-transferral-39]

## Cause for canonization

The Archbishop Fulton J. Sheen Foundation was formed in 1998 by Gregory J. Ladd and Lawrence F. Hickey to spread information about Sheen's life. The foundation approached Cardinal [John O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_John_O'Connor) of the Archdiocese of New York for permission to commence the process of his cause, which was under the authority of the [Diocese of Peoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Peoria).[^cite_note-foundation-3]

In 2002, Sheen's [cause for canonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonization) was opened by Bishop [Daniel R. Jenky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_R._Jenky) of Peoria. From then on, Sheen was referred to as a "[Servant of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servant_of_God)". In February 2008, Sheen's archives were sealed at a ceremony during a special [Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_\(liturgy\)) at the Cathedral of Saint Mary of the Immaculate Conception in Peoria.[^cite_note-Trib09-30] In 2009, the diocesan phase of the investigation ended and the records were sent to the [Congregation for the Causes of Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints) at the Vatican.

On June 28, 2012, the Vatican recognized Sheen's life as one of [heroic virtue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heroic_virtue),[^cite_note-48] a major step towards eventual beatification.[^cite_note-\:1-47] From then on, Sheen was titled [Venerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venerable). According to the [Catholic News Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_News_Service) and *The Catholic Post* (the newspaper of the Peoria Diocese), the case of a newborn boy who had no discernible pulse for 61 minutes, who was about to be declared dead at [OSF Saint Francis Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OSF_Saint_Francis_Medical_Center) in Peoria, Illinois, as a stillborn infant, and yet lived to be healthy without physical or mental impairment, was in the preliminary stages of being investigated as the possible miracle needed for Sheen's potential beatification. If the miracle is approved at the diocesan level and then by the [Congregation for the Causes of Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Causes_of_Saints) at the Vatican, by being both medically unexplainable and directly attributable theologically to Sheen's intercession, according to expert panels in both subject areas, beatification may proceed. Another such miracle would be required for him to be considered for canonization.

On September 7, 2011, a tribunal of inquiry was sworn in to investigate the alleged healing. During a special Mass on December 11, 2011, at St. Mary's Cathedral in Peoria, the documentation gathered by the tribunal over nearly three months was boxed and sealed. It was then shipped to the Vatican for consideration by the Congregation for the Causes of Saints, concluding the diocesan tribunal's work.[^cite_note-49]

### Transfer of remains



Sheen's tomb at St. Mary's Cathedral in Peoria, Illinois

In September 2014, the canonization cause was suspended because of a disagreement with the Archdiocese of New York concerning the transfer of Sheen's remains to the Diocese of Peoria.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] In a press release on June 14, 2016, it was announced that Sheen's surviving family petitioned the [New York Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Supreme_Court) to allow the transfer of Sheen's remains to Peoria. The press release stated that "on several occasions, the Archdiocese \[of New York] has declared its desire to cooperate with the wishes of the family."[^cite_note-52]

In an action brought in [New York Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Supreme_Court) in November 2016, Justice Arlene P. Bluth ordered the Archdiocese of New York to grant permission to disinter Sheen's body. The court ruled that the archdiocese's objection that Sheen would not want the disinterment was without factual basis. Given that his elevation to sainthood was being blocked, the court found the family had sufficient justification for moving his body.[^cite_note-53]

&#x20;In February 2018, the [New York State Appellate Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Appellate_Division) overturned Bluth's decision and ordered an [evidentiary hearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidentiary_hearing) be held as to whether moving Sheen's body would be consistent with his wishes.[^cite_note-54] The court noted that,

> "it is unclear if Archbishop Sheen's direction in his will to be buried in '[Calvary Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvary_Cemetery_\(Queens\)), the official cemetery of the Archdiocese of New York' evinces an express intention to remain buried in the Archdiocese of New York, or was merely a descriptive term for Calvary Cemetery."&#x20;

In June 2018, after re-examining the case and holding the evidentiary hearing, Bluth affirmed her earlier ruling. The archdiocese allowed Peoria to begin the work on his cause for canonization, which eventually would have required at the least a collection of his relics. When the casket was opened, Sheen’s remains had fully skeletonized and his burial vestments had disintegrated.[^cite_note-55]

The archdiocese announced on June 9, 2019, that it was officially giving up the fight to keep Sheen's remains at St. Patrick's Cathedral.[^cite_note-56] On June 27, 2019, the remains were transferred to [St. Mary's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Mary_of_the_Immaculate_Conception_\(Peoria,_Illinois\)) in Peoria.[^cite_note-transferral-39]

### Beatification



The Mass for the Beatification of Blessed Fulton J. Sheen, 2026

On July 6, 2019, the [Congregation for the Causes of Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_the_Causes_of_Saints) promulgated the decree approving Sheen's miracle needed for beatification. The miracle involved the unexplained recovery of James Fulton Engstrom, a boy stillborn in September 2010 to Bonnie and Travis Engstrom of the town of [Goodfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodfield,_Illinois), near Peoria. Engstrom's parents prayed for the intercession of Sheen for their son's recovery. Pope Francis approved the miracle, and Sheen was scheduled for beatification on December 21, 2019, at the Cathedral of St. Mary in Peoria.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

On December 3, 2019, the Diocese of Peoria announced that the Vatican had postponed Sheen's beatification.[^cite_note-59] [Salvatore Matano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Matano), the bishop of Rochester, had expressed concern that Sheen's alleged posting of a priest who had been named in a 1963 sexual misconduct case might be cited unfavorably in a report from New York State Attorney General [Letitia James](1598829.md). The Diocese of Peoria countered that the priest had been assigned not by Sheen but by his successor, and that Sheen's involvement in the matter had already been "thoroughly examined" and that he had been "exonerated" and "never put children in harm's way."[^cite_note-delay-5]

In March 2026, it was announced that his [beatification](233724.md) would occur on September 24, 2026, in [St. Louis, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri), presided over by Cardinal [Luis Antonio Tagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Antonio_Tagle).[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-causesanti.va-7] James Fulton Engstrom, healed by Sheen's intercession, attended the ceremony with his family and presented a relic of Sheen's lower jaw and teeth.[^cite_note-61]



Cardinal Luis Tagle at the Beatification of Blessed Fulton J. Sheen, 2026

Sheen was beatified by Tagle, who represented [Pope Leo XIV](54520372.md), on September 24, 2026 at the [The Dome at America’s Center](452735.md) in St. Louis. That morning, [United States](3434750.md) President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) published a message in honor of the occasion.[^cite_note-62] A Byzantine [divine liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Liturgy) was celebrated to commemorate the event at Peoria's [Cathedral of Saint Mary of the Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Mary_of_the_Immaculate_Conception_\(Peoria,_Illinois\)).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]
