The Netherlands has been represented at the [Eurovision Song Contest](9954.md) 65 times since making its debut as one of the seven countries at the first contest in [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1956). The country has missed five contests, twice because the dates coincided with [Remembrance of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remembrance_of_the_Dead) (1985 and 1991), twice because of being relegated due to poor results the previous year (1995 and 2002), and once due to the inclusion of [Israel](3190362.md) in the context of the [Gaza war](74998519.md) (2026). It has missed the final despite qualifying once, in 2024, due to the alleged personal misconduct of its entrant which led to disqualification. The umbrella organisation of the [Dutch public broadcasting system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_public_broadcasting_system), [Nederlandse Publieke Omroep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Publieke_Omroep) (NPO), will perform the duties of the participating broadcaster starting in 2027. The Netherlands has hosted the contest five times: in [Hilversum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilversum) ([1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1958)), [Amsterdam](844.md) ([1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1970)), [The Hague](30269.md) ([1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1976) and [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1980)), and [Rotterdam](26049.md) ([2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2021)).

The Netherlands has won the contest five times, with "[Net als toen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_als_toen)" performed by [Corry Brokken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corry_Brokken) ([1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1957)), "[Een beetje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'n_Beetje)" by [Teddy Scholten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddy_Scholten) ([1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1959)), "[De troubadour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_troubadour)" by [Lenny Kuhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenny_Kuhr) in a four-way tie ([1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1969)), "[Ding-a-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ding-a-dong)" by [Teach-In](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teach-In_\(band\)) ([1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1975)), and "[Arcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_\(song\))" by [Duncan Laurence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Laurence) ([2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2019)). The country's other top five results are "[Als het om de liefde gaat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Als_het_om_de_liefde_gaat)" by [Sandra and Andres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_and_Andres) (fourth, [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1972)), "[I See a Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_See_a_Star)" by [Mouth and MacNeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouth_and_MacNeal) (third, [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1974)), "[Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_\(Maggie_MacNeal_song\))" by [Maggie MacNeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggie_MacNeal) (fifth, [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1980)), "[Rechtop in de wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rechtop_in_de_wind)" by [Marcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcha) (fifth, [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1987)), "[Hemel en aarde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemel_en_aarde)" by [Edsilia Rombley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edsilia_Rombley) (fourth, [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1998)), and "[Calm After the Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calm_After_the_Storm)" by [The Common Linnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Common_Linnets) (second, [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2014)). It finished last in [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1958), [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1962), [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1963), [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1968), and in the second semi-final in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2011).

After the introduction of semi-finals in 2004, the Netherlands failed to reach the final for eight years in a row from 2005 to 2012, but has since participated in nine of the last thirteen finals.

## History

### 1956–1969: Nederlandse Televisie Stichting

The Netherlands was one of seven countries to participate in the contest's inaugural edition in [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1956), represented by the public broadcaster [Nederlandse Televisie Stichting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Televisie_Stichting) (NTS). From the contest's inception, NTS established an annual national final, *[Nationaal Songfestival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationaal_Songfestival)*, to select its Eurovision entries. Success followed quickly: in [1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1957), [Corry Brokken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corry_Brokken) won the contest in [Frankfurt](10992.md) with "[Net als toen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_als_toen)", giving the Netherlands its first victory. As a result, NTS hosted the [1958 contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1958) in [Hilversum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilversum). The Netherlands secured a second victory just two years later at the [1959 contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1959) in [Cannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes), where [Teddy Scholten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddy_Scholten) performed "[Een beetje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'n_Beetje)", a song about being unfaithful in a relationship. Because NTS had only recently hosted the 1958 edition, it declined to host again; the [1960 contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1960) was therefore held in [London](17867.md).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]



[Teddy Scholten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddy_Scholten) arriving at [Schiphol Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schiphol_Airport) after winning the [1959 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1959)

This initial period of success was followed by a string of poor results, including consecutive *[nul-points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nul_points)* finishes in [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1962) and [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1963). Despite these competitive struggles, the Dutch entries set significant milestones for diversity and representation within the contest. In [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1964), the Netherlands was represented by [Anneke Grönloh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anneke_Gr%C3%B6nloh), the first performer of Asian origin to appear in the competition.[^cite_note-eurostory-2026-6] Two years later, in [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1966), Dutch representative [Milly Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milly_Scott) became the first Black artist to participate.[^cite_note-eurostory-2026-6] Following another last-place finish in [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1968) with [Ronnie Tober](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronnie_Tober)'s "Morgen", the Netherlands achieved a major turning point in [1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1969). At the international final in [Madrid](41188263.md), the Dutch entry, "[De troubadour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_troubadour)" by [Lenny Kuhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenny_Kuhr), finished in a four-way tie for first place alongside [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest), the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest), and [Spain](2996040.md). Because the contest lacked a tie-breaking mechanism at the time, all four countries were declared joint winners.[^cite_note-7]



[Milly Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milly_Scott) posing before the [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1966) scoreboard

### 1970–2009: Nederlandse Omroep Stichting

In May 1969, NTS merged into the [Nederlandse Omroep Stichting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Omroep_Stichting) (NOS), which assumed responsibility for the country's Eurovision participation beginning in 1970. Following the four-way tie of 1969, the Netherlands won a [drawing of lots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drawing_lots_\(decision_making\)) against France to secure the hosting rights for the [1970 contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1970).[^cite_note-ons_amsterdam-1970-8] The event was staged at the [RAI Convention Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAI_Amsterdam) in [Amsterdam](844.md) and hosted by [Willy Dobbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willy_Dobbe).[^cite_note-ons_amsterdam-1970-8] The Netherlands continued to achieve strong results throughout the early 1970s; [Sandra and Andres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_and_Andres) finished fourth in [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1972) with "Als het om de liefde gaat", followed by a third-place finish in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1974) with "[I See a Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_See_a_Star)" by [Mouth and MacNeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouth_and_MacNeal). In [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1975), Teach-In won the *Nationaal Songfestival* and went on to secure the country's fourth Eurovision victory in [Stockholm](26741.md). Their entry, "[Ding-a-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ding-a-dong)", received six 12-point scores and became the first song in Eurovision history to win the contest from the opening position of the running order.



[Teach-In](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teach-In_\(band\)) on stage for their [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1975) winner's reprise

As the reigning champions, the Netherlands hosted the [1976 contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1976) at the [Congresgebouw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Forum_\(The_Hague\)) in [The Hague](30269.md), with 1957 winner Corry Brokken serving as presenter. Returning artist [Sandra Reemer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Reemer) placed ninth with "The Party's Over". The Congresgebouw hosted the competition again in [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1980) after [Israel](3190362.md), having won two consecutive editions, declined hosting duties.[^cite_note-9] The Dutch entry that year, [Maggie MacNeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggie_MacNeal)'s "Amsterdam", achieved a fifth-place finish. In 1982, the [national selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1982) courted controversy when the song "Jij en ik" was chosen over "[Fantasy Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantasy_Island_\(The_Millionaires_song\))";[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] performed by [Bill van Dijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_van_Dijk), it ultimately finished third-to-last at the [international final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1982). The Netherlands withdrew from the contest for the first time in [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1985), because the event coincided with the national [Remembrance of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remembrance_of_the_Dead). Instead, the contest was aired with Dutch commentary as part of an experimental [Europa TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europa_TV) broadcast the following day.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

In [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1986), facing financial constraints, NOS collaborated with fellow public broadcaster [Veronica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronica_\(media\)), which agreed to broadcast the contest and cover the participation fees.[^cite_note-berkenbosch-1986-14][^cite_note-het_parool-1986-15] The following year, [Marcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcha) finished in a joint-fifth place with "Rechtop in de wind", marking the country's first top-five result since 1980. The Netherlands withdrew for a second time in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1991), as the final again fell on Remembrance Day. In 1993 and 1994, NOS internally selected an artist, and used *Nationaal Songfestival* to select the song. This method yielded a sixth-place finish for [Ruth Jacott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Jacott) and "Vrede" in [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1993), but proved less successful in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1994) when [Willeke Alberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willeke_Alberti)'s "Waar is de zon?" placed 23rd, resulting in the country's relegation from the [1995 contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1995).

*Nationaal Songfestival* returned in 1996 with a revamped format featuring five semi-finals. The eventual winners, [Maxine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxine_\(singer\)) and [Franklin Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Brown), placed seventh in [Oslo](22309.md) with "De eerste keer". Two years later, [Edsilia Rombley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edsilia_Rombley) secured fourth place with "Hemel en aarde", achieving the Netherlands' best Eurovision result since 1975. However, fortunes declined into the new millennium; Michelle's 18th-place finish with "Out on My Own" in [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2001) led to a forced relegation from the [2002 contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2002). The Netherlands returned in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2003) with [Esther Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Hart_\(singer\)), who placed 13th with "One More Night" and was honoured with the [Marcel Bezençon Artistic Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Bezen%C3%A7on_Awards#Artistic_Award). This brief recovery was cut short by the introduction of the semi-final format in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2004); following [Glennis Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glennis_Grace)'s failure to qualify with "My Impossible Dream" in [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2005), the Netherlands entered a lengthy non-qualification streak.[^cite_note-de_schatkamer-2026-16]

### 2010–2025: TROS and AVROTROS

In 2010, [TROS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TROS) succeeded NOS as participating broadcaster, after NOS decided that handling the country's Eurovision participation no longer aligned with its core public broadcasting tasks.[^cite_note-17] That year, [Sieneke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sieneke) was selected to perform the song "Ik ben verliefd (Sha-la-lie)", but failed to qualify for the final. [3JS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3JS) had the lowest score of all participants in the [2011 contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2011), and in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2012), [Joan Franka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Franka) also failed to qualify. The Netherlands missed out on the final eight years in a row, making it the country with the longest period of non-qualification in the contest.[^cite_note-18]



[Anouk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anouk_\(singer\)) at a press conference after qualifying for the [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2013) final

To reverse this trend, TROS shifted its strategy in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2013) by internally selecting rock singer [Anouk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anouk_\(singer\)). Granted full artistic freedom,[^cite_note-19] Anouk performed "[Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birds_\(Anouk_song\))", advancing to the final and securing the Netherlands' first top 10 finish since [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1999). Building on this success, TROS – which merged into [AVROTROS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AVROTROS) the next year – continued to select its entry internally. In [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2014), [the Common Linnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Common_Linnets) won their semi-final and finished second overall with "[Calm After the Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calm_After_the_Storm)". Aside from a [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2015) non-qualification by [Trijntje Oosterhuis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trijntje_Oosterhuis), the Netherlands achieved steady success over the next three years, qualifying with [Douwe Bob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douwe_Bob) (11th), [Ogene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogene_\(group\)) (11th), and [Waylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waylon_\(singer\)) (18th). The resurgence culminated in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2019), when [Duncan Laurence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Laurence) secured the country's fifth victory – and first in 44 years – with the song "[Arcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcade_\(song\))".[^cite_note-de_schatkamer-2026-16]



[Duncan Laurence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Laurence) with the [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2019) trophy

[Rotterdam](26049.md) was chosen to host the [2020 contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2020), which was ultimately cancelled due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and replaced by the non-competitive broadcast *[Eurovision: Europe Shine a Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision:_Europe_Shine_a_Light)* from [Hilversum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilversum).[^cite_note-20] [Rotterdam Ahoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Ahoy) staged the rescheduled event in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2021), hosted by [Chantal Janzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chantal_Janzen), [Jan Smit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Smit_\(singer\)), Edsilia Rombley, and [Nikkie de Jager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikkie_de_Jager). Automatic finalist [Jeangu Macrooy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeangu_Macrooy) placed 23rd with "[Birth of a New Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_of_a_New_Age)", notably introducing the [Sranan Tongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sranan_Tongo) language to the contest.[^cite_note-21] The country's momentum continued in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2022) when [S10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S10_\(singer\)) finished 11th with "[De diepte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_diepte)", the first Dutch-language entry since 2010.[^cite_note-22] However, the qualification streak was ended the following year, when [Mia Nicolai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Nicolai) and [Dion Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dion_Cooper) failed to advance from the semi-finals. In [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2024), [Joost Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joost_Klein) placed second in his semi-final with "[Europapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europapa)", but was disqualified prior to the final following an altercation with a production staff member.[^cite_note-23] The most recent Dutch participation in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2025) saw [Claude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_\(singer\)) finishing 12th with the song "[C'est la vie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C'est_la_vie_\(Claude_song\))".

On 4 December 2025, AVROTROS announced that it would not take part in the 2026 contest, citing the inclusion of [Israel](3190362.md) amidst the ongoing [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-reuters12-24] The contest was instead broadcast by NOS and [NTR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NTR_\(broadcaster\)).[^cite_note-nos-20252-25] AVROTROS will also not take part in [2027](80066921.md),[^cite_note-26] with the umbrella organisation [NPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Publieke_Omroep) taking over.[^cite_note-NPO2027-27]

## Non-participation

The Netherlands has missed only five contests in its Eurovision history. The country was absent in [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1985) and [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1991) due to the date of both contests coinciding with the Dutch [Remembrance of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remembrance_of_the_Dead),[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] in [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1995) and [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2002) due to relegation as a result of the country's poor results in the previous year, and in [2026](79992910.md) due to the inclusion of [Israel](3190362.md) in light of the [Gaza war](74998519.md). Despite this boycott of the contest, NPO took up participation responsibilities the following year.

The Netherlands did compete in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2000), but the broadcast of that year's final on [Nederland 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NPO_2) was halted an hour after it commenced due to the [Enschede fireworks disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enschede_fireworks_disaster), which had taken place a few hours before.[^cite_note-30] The points awarded by the Netherlands were taken from the back-up jury vote, as there was no televote after the program was cut short.

## Broadcasts and commentators

Over the years Dutch commentary has been provided by several experienced radio and television presenters, including [Willem Duys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Duys), [Ivo Niehe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivo_Niehe), [Pim Jacobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pim_Jacobs), Ati Dijckmeester, and [Paul de Leeuw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_de_Leeuw). [Willem van Beusekom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_van_Beusekom) provided NOS commentary every year from 1987 until 2005 (with the exceptions of 1991 and 1995).[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] He was replaced by his co-commentator [Cornald Maas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornald_Maas), who commentated on the contest from 2004 until 2010.

On 29 June 2010, Maas was sacked as commentator after posting insults on Twitter about [Sieneke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sieneke), [Joran van der Sloot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joran_van_der_Sloot) and the [Party for Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_for_Freedom) (PVV).[^cite_note-108] After this, DJ [Daniël Dekker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dani%C3%ABl_Dekker), who had been commentating next to Maas, took over together with [Jan Smit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Smit_\(singer\)). In 2014, Maas returned, now himself replacing Dekker, as commentator together with Smit. [Sander Lantinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sander_Lantinga) replaced Smit for 2021 due to Smit hosting the main contest.[^cite_note-109] In 2024, Smit stepped down as commentator and was replaced by [Jacqueline Govaert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Govaert).[^cite_note-van_dijk-2024-110]
