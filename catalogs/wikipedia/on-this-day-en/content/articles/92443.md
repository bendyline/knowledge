**Caen** ([/ˈkɒ̃/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) or [/ˈkɒn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); French: [\[kɑ̃\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/eb/Fr-Caen.ogg/Fr-Caen.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/eb/Fr-Caen.ogg/Fr-Caen.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_language): *Kaem*) is a city and [commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_France) 15 km (9.3 mi) inland from the northwestern coast of France. It is the [prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_in_France) of the [department](38523.md) of [Calvados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvados_\(department\)). The city proper has 105,512 inhabitants (as of 2018), while its [functional urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_area_\(France\)) has 470,000,[^cite_note-compar-3] making Caen the second largest urban area in [Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy_\(administrative_region\)) and the 19th largest in France.[^cite_note-4] It is also the third largest commune in all of Normandy after [Le Havre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Havre) and [Rouen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouen).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

Caen is located 200 km (120 mi) northwest of Paris, connected to the South of England by the Caen ([Ouistreham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouistreham)) to [Portsmouth](24207.md) ferry route through the [English Channel](9230.md). Situated a few kilometres from the coast, the landing beaches, the bustling resorts of [Deauville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deauville) and [Cabourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabourg), as well as [Norman Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Switzerland) and the [Pays d'Auge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pays_d'Auge), Caen is often considered the archetype of Normandy.

Caen is known for its historical buildings built during the reign of [William the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_I_of_England), who was buried there, and for the [Battle for Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_for_Caen), heavy fighting that took place in and around Caen during the [Battle of Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Normandy) in 1944, destroying much of the city. The city has now preserved the memory by erecting a memorial and a museum dedicated to peace, the [Mémorial de Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9morial_de_Caen).

## Etymology

The first references to the name of Caen are found in different acts of the dukes of [Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Normandy): *Cadon* 1021/1025,[^cite_note-7] *Cadumus* 1025,[^cite_note-8] *Cathim* 1026/1027.[^cite_note-9] Year 1070 of the Parker manuscript[^cite_note-10] of the *[Anglo-Saxon Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_Chronicle)* refers to Caen as *Kadum*,[^cite_note-11] and year 1086 of the Laud manuscript[^cite_note-12] gives the name as *Caþum*.[^cite_note-13] Despite a lack of sources as to the origin of the settlements, the name Caen would seem to be of [Gaulish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaulish) origin, from the words *catu-*, referring to military activities and *magos*, field, hence meaning "manoeuvre field" or "battlefield".[^cite_note-14] In [Layamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layamon)'s [*Brut*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layamon's_Brut), the poet asserts that [King Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Arthur) named the city in memory of [Sir Kay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Kay),[^cite_note-15] although the [historicity of King Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historicity_of_King_Arthur) is widely doubted.

Caen was known in Roman times as 'Catumagos', from the Gaulish roots *magos* meaning 'field' and *catu* meaning 'combat'. It remained a minor settlement throughout the Roman period.

In 912 Caen became the western capital of the [Duchy of Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Normandy)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaydn1910-16] and due to the patronage of the [Dukes of Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukes_of_Normandy) saw a major development of commence throughout the 10th century. Around 1060, [William the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Conqueror) began construction of the [Château de Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Caen), which became the centre of the ducal court. Duchess [Matilda of Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matilda_of_Flanders) also founded the [Benedictine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine) [Abbey of Sainte-Trinité, Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_of_Sainte-Trinit%C3%A9,_Caen) around the same time, eventually being buried in the abbey. Caen succeeded [Bayeux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayeux) as the capital of [Lower Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Normandy), complementing the second ducal capital of [Rouen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouen).

Caen fell to [Philip II of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_France) on 21 May 1204, and was incorporated along with the remainder of Normandy into the Kingdom of France.

### Hundred Years' War

Main article: [Battle of Caen (1346)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Caen_\(1346\))

In 1346, King [Edward III of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_III_of_England) led his army against the city, hoping to loot it. It was expected that a siege of perhaps several weeks would be required, but the army took the city in less than a day, on 26 July 1346, [storming and sacking it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Caen_\(1346\)), killing 3,000 of its citizens, and burning much of the merchants' quarter on the Île St-Jean. Only the castle of Caen held out, despite attempts to besiege it. A few days later, the English left, marching to the east and on to their victory at the [Battle of Crécy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cr%C3%A9cy). It was later [captured following a siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Caen_\(1417\)) by [Henry V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_V_of_England) in 1417 and treated harshly for being the first town to put up any resistance to his invasion. In 1450 towards the end of the war, French forces [recaptured Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Caen_\(1450\)).

### World War II

Main article: [Battle for Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_for_Caen)



A [Canadian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Army) bulldozer in Caen, 1944. The towers of the [Abbaye aux Hommes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-%C3%89tienne,_Caen) are behind.

During [World War II](32927.md), Caen was captured by [German](21212.md) forces during the [Battle of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_France) in 1940 and placed under [military occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_military_administration_in_occupied_France_during_World_War_II). In 1944, [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) forces launched [Operation Overlord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Overlord), invading German-occupied France and rapidly advancing through [Normandy](21724.md). From 6 June to 6 August 1944, the British [Second Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Army_\(United_Kingdom\)) fought the [battle of Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Caen) to dislodge German forces from the city. During the battle, Allied bombing raids heavily damaged the city and caused numerous French civilian casualties. After the battle, little of prewar Caen remained, and reconstruction efforts in the city continued until 1962.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

### Postwar

Postwar work included the reconstruction of complete districts of the city and the university campus. It took 14 years (1948–1962) and led to the current urbanization of Caen. Having lost many of its historic quarters and its university campus in the war, Caen does not have the atmosphere of a traditional Norman town such as [Honfleur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honfleur), [Rouen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouen), [Cabourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabourg), [Deauville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deauville) or [Bayeux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayeux).&#x20;

The Canadian Army Film and Photo Unit filmed the D-Day offensive and Orne breakout several weeks later. It returned several months later to document the city's recovery efforts. The resulting film, *You Can't Kill a City*, is preserved in the [National Archives of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Archives_of_Canada).

## Geography

Caen is in an area of high humidity. The river [Orne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orne_\(Normandy\)) flows through the city, as well as small rivers known as *les Odons*, most of which have been buried under the city to improve urban hygiene. Caen has a large flood zone, named "La prairie", located around the hippodrome, not far from the river Orne, which is regularly submerged.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

Caen is 10 km (6.2 mi) from the [Channel](9230.md). A canal ([Canal de Caen à la Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_de_Caen_%C3%A0_la_Mer)) parallel to the Orne was built during the reign of [Napoleon III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_III_of_France) to link the city to the sea at all times. The canal reaches the [English Channel](9230.md) at [Ouistreham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouistreham). A lock keeps the tide out of the canal and lets large ships navigate up the canal to Caen's freshwater harbours.

### Climate

Caen has an [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) that is somewhat ameliorated due to its slightly inland position. In spite of this, summers are still cool by French standards and the climate is typically maritime in terms of high precipitation, relatively modest sunshine hours and mild winters.

## Main sights

### Castle

The castle, the [Château de Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Caen), built c. 1060 by William the Conqueror, who successfully [conquered England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Conquest_of_England) in 1066, is one of the largest medieval fortresses of Western Europe. It remained an essential feature of [Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_dynasty) strategy and policy. At Christmas 1182, a [royal court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_court) celebration for Christmas in the [aula](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Special\:Search/aula) of Caen Castle brought together [Henry II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_II_of_England) and his sons, [Richard the Lionheart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_I_of_England) and [John Lackland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_England), receiving more than a thousand knights. Caen Castle, along with all of Normandy, was handed over to the French Crown in 1204. The castle saw several engagements during the [Hundred Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Years'_War) (1346, 1417, 1450) and was in use as a barracks as late as the Second World War. Bullet holes are visible on the walls of the castle where members of the [French Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Resistance) were shot during the Second World War. Today, the castle serves as a museum that houses the *Musée des Beaux-Arts de Caen* (Museum of [Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_art) of Caen) and *Musée de Normandie* (Museum of Normandy) along with many periodical exhibitions about arts and history. (See ["Timeline of Caen Castle"](https://web.archive.org/web/20060213224638/http://www.ville-caen.fr/mdn/RedecouvChateau/chronoGB.htm). ### Abbeys

In repentance for marrying his cousin [Mathilda of Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathilda_of_Flanders), William ordered two [abbeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey) to be built with the [Pope](23056.md)'s encouragement:

- [Église St.-Étienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_of_Saint-%C3%89tienne,_Caen), formerly the *Abbaye aux Hommes* (Men's Abbey). It was completed in 1063 and is dedicated to [St Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Stephen). The current [Hôtel de Ville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B4tel_de_Ville,_Caen) (City Hall) of Caen is built onto the South [Transept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transept) of the building.

  [^cite_note-28]

- [*Église de la Ste.-Trinité*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_of_Sainte-Trinit%C3%A9,_Caen), formerly the *Abbaye aux Dames* (Women's Abbey). It was completed in 1060 and is dedicated to the [Holy Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Trinity). The current seat of the regional council (*[conseil régional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conseil_r%C3%A9gional)*) of [Basse-Normandie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basse-Normandie) is nearby.

Aristide Olivier was re-elected mayor in the 2026 municipal elections.[^cite_note-mayor-1]

In 1952, the small commune of [Venoix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venoix) became part of Caen.[^cite_note-ehess-26]

In 1990, the agglomeration of Caen was organized into a district, transformed in 2002 into a *[Communauté d'agglomération](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communaut%C3%A9_d'agglom%C3%A9ration)* (*Grand Caen* (Greater Caen), renamed [Caen la Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communaut%C3%A9_d'agglom%C3%A9ration_Caen_la_Mer) in 2004), gathers 29 towns and villages, including [Villons-les-Buissons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villons-les-Buissons), [Lion-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion-sur-Mer), [Hermanville-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermanville-sur-Mer), which joined the Communauté d'agglomération in 2004. The population of the "communauté d'agglomération" is around 220,000 inhabitants.

In the former administrative organisation, Caen was a part of 9 [cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_in_France), of which it was the chief town. These cantons contained a total of 13 towns. Caen gave its name to a 10th canton, of which it was not part. Since the 2015 canton reorganization, Caen is part of the cantons of [Caen-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Caen-1), [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Caen-2), [3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Caen-3), [4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Caen-4) and [5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Caen-5).[^cite_note-decret-32]

### Public transport

#### Urban transport network



[The current Caen tramway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caen_tramway) opened in 2019.

Twisto is the urban transport network of Caen, including about sixty bus lines and 3 tramway lines.[^cite_note-33] The [present tram network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caen_tramway) officially opened on 27 July 2019 replacing the [Caen Guided Light Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caen_Guided_Light_Transit) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): ***TVR de Caen*** but known locally as the "tram"), a guided [trolleybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) network which operated from 2002 to 2017, which was closed due to reliability issues. The city previously had [a tramway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Caen) which operated from 1860 to 1937.

#### Rail



[Caen railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caen_station)

Caen also had several main and [branch railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branch_line) lines linking [Caen railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caen_station) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): ***Gare de Caen***) to all parts of Normandy with lines to [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gare_St-Lazare), [Vire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vire), [Flers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flers,_Orne), [Cabourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabourg), [Houlgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houlgate), [Deauville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deauville), [Saint-Lô](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-L%C3%B4), [Bayeux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayeux) and [Cherbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherbourg-Octeville).

Now the SNCF operates the Paris-Caen-Cherbourg, Caen-Rouen, Caen-[Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Mans)-Tours, Caen-[Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rennes) services and some others small lines,[^cite_note-34] while Railcoop will soon open new lines such as Lille-Amiens-Rouen-Caen-Rennes-Nantes and Paris-Caen-Brest making Caen railway station its north-western hub.[^cite_note-35]

Caen station is the second busiest in Normandy, after Rouen station.

### Air transport

[Caen - Carpiquet Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caen_-_Carpiquet_Airport) is the biggest airport in Normandy considering the number of passengers and flights that it serves every year. Most flights are operated by [HOP!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HOP!), Volotea and the French [national airline](465816.md) [Air France](14998012.md) operates flights to the French cities of [Lyon](8638634.md), Nice, Toulouse, Montpellier, Marseille, Biarritz, Ajaccio, Figari, Bastia and Calvi.[^cite_note-36]

### Water transport

Caen is served by the large port of [Ouistreham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouistreham), lying at the mouth of the [Caen Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caen_Canal) where it meets the [English Channel](9230.md). A cruise/ferry service operates between [Portsmouth](24207.md), England, and Caen/Ouistreham running both standard roll-on-roll-off car ferries and supercat fast ferries, with the latter making crossing from March to November. The ferry terminal is 15 km (9.3 mi) from Caen with a daytime shuttle bus service for foot passengers. There is also a cyclist road from Caen to Ouistreham.[^cite_note-37]

### Road transport

Caen is connected to the rest of France by [motorways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoroutes_of_France) to Paris ([A13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A13_autoroute)), [Brittany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany) and Southern France ([A84](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A84_autoroute)) and to [Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Mans) and central France ([A88](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A88_autoroute)–[A28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A28_autoroute)). The A13 and A88 are [toll roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_road) while the A84 is a toll-free motorway. The city is encircled by the [N814 ring-road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A9riph%C3%A9rique_\(Caen\)) (Boulevard Périphérique) that was completed in the late 1990s. The [N13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_nationale_13) connects Caen to [Cherbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherbourg-Octeville) and to Paris. A section of the former N13 (Caen-Paris) is now D613 (in Calvados) following road renumbering. The Boulevard Périphérique includes a [viaduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viaduct) called the [Viaduc de Calix](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viaduc%20de%20Calix) that goes over the canal and the river [Orne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orne_\(Normandy\)). The canal links the city to the sea to permit [cargo ships](786056.md) and ferries to dock in the [port of Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Caen). Ferries which have docked include the *[Quiberon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Quiberon)* and the *[Duc de Normandie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Duc_de_Normandie)*.

## Education

- The [University of Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Caen) has around 34,000 students in five different campuses and Caen is ranked 18th biggest student city of France. The University has a good reputation as it is ranked 16th in France.

  [^cite_note-38]

- The University is divided into 11 colleges, called *UFR* (*Unité fondamentale de Recherche*), six institutes, one Engineering School, two IUP and five local campuses. The University is one of the oldest in France, having been founded by [John of Lancaster, Duke of Bedford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_Lancaster,_Duke_of_Bedford), and [Henry VI of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VI_of_England), in 1432.

- A campus of the business school, the [École de management de Normandie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_de_management_de_Normandie), is also located in the city.

## Economy

## Music and theatre

The [Théâtre de Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%A9%C3%A2tre_de_Caen) (1963) is the home of the [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_music) [musical ensemble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_ensemble) [Les Arts Florissants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Arts_Florissants_\(ensemble\)). The organization was founded by conductor [William Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Christie_\(musician\)) in 1979 and derives its name from the [1685 opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_arts_florissants_\(opera\)) by [Marc-Antoine Charpentier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc-Antoine_Charpentier).

## Sport



[Stade Michel d'Ornano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Michel_d'Ornano)

From 1947 to 2006, Caen was a stage of the [Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France) a total of 15 times.[^cite_note-63] Further, Caen was one of the hosts of the [EuroBasket 1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_1983). The largest football team in the city is, [SM Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Malherbe_Caen), who as of 2024 are owned by superstar footballer [Kylian Mbappé](48711857.md).[^cite_note-64] There are three smaller football clubs in the area, [AG Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AG_Caen), [MOS Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOS_Caen) and [ASPTT Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASPTT_Caen).[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] The [Drakkars de Caen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drakkars_de_Caen) play ice hockey in the [FFHG Division 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FFHG_Division_1). In 2014, Caen was the location of the [2014 FEI World Equestrian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FEI_World_Equestrian_Games).

Caen has a racecourse called the [Hippodrome de la Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippodrome_de_la_Prairie?action=edit\&redlink=1); it has been used since 1837.[^cite_note-68] The course is designed specifically for [harness racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harness_racing).[^cite_note-69]

### Motto

Today, Caen has no motto, but it used to have one, which did not survive the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution). As a result, its spelling is archaic and has not been updated:[^cite_note-71]

> *Un Dieu, un Roy, une Foy, une Loy.*

(One God, one King, one Faith, one Law.)

This motto is reflected in a notable old [Chant royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chant_royal).[^cite_note-72]
