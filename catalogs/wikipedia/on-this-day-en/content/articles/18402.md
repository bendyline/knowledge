[8°50′18″S 13°14′04″E  /  8.83833°S 13.23444°E  / -8.83833; 13.23444](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Luanda\&params=8_50_18_S_13_14_04_E_region\:AO_type\:city) **Luanda** is the [capital](181337.md) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Angola) of [Angola](701.md). It is Angola's primary [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port), and its major industrial, cultural and urban centre. Located on Angola's northern Atlantic coast, Luanda is Angola's [administrative centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_centre), its chief seaport, and also the capital of the [Luanda Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luanda_Province). Luanda and its [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) is the most populous Portuguese-speaking [national capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_capital) in the world and the most populous [Lusophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusophone) city outside [Brazil](3383.md). In 2024 the population reached more than 8.8 million inhabitants (a third of Angola's population).

Among the oldest [colonial cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Africa) of Africa, Luanda was founded in January 1576 as *São Paulo da Assunção de Loanda* by Portuguese explorer [Paulo Dias de Novais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Dias_de_Novais), being occasionally called "Leonda" or "St Paul de Leonda" by non-Portuguese sources. The city served as the centre of the [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Angola) to Brazil before the institution was prohibited.

At the start of the [Angolan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Civil_War) in 1975, most of the white Portuguese left as refugees,[^cite_note-\:0-4] principally migrating to Portugal. Luanda's population increased greatly from internal refugees fleeing the war, but its infrastructure was inadequate to handle the increase. This also caused the exacerbation of slums, or [musseques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musseques), around Luanda.

In the 21st century, the city has been undergoing a major reconstruction.[^cite_note-5] Many new large developments are taking place that will alter its cityscape significantly.

Industries present in the city include the processing of agricultural products, beverage production, textile, cement, new car assembly plants, construction materials, plastics, metallurgy, cigarettes and shoes. The city is also notable as an economic centre for oil,[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] and a refinery is located in the city.

Luanda has been ranked as one of the most expensive cities in the world for expatriates.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] The inhabitants of Luanda are mostly members of the ethnic [Ambundu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambundu) people. In recent decades of the 21st century, the number of ethnic [Bakongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakongo) and [Ovimbundu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovimbundu) have also increased. Ethnic Europeans are mainly Portuguese.

Luanda was the main host city for the matches of the [2010 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).

### Portuguese colonization

See also: [Portuguese Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Angola) and [Colonial history of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_history_of_Angola)



[São Miguel Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_of_S%C3%A3o_Miguel), founded in 1576 by [Paulo Dias de Novais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Dias_de_Novais), today hosts the [Armed Forces Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_the_Armed_Forces_\(Angola\)).



Depiction of *São Paulo da Assunção de Luanda*, 1755.

[Portuguese](23033.md) explorer [Paulo Dias de Novais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Dias_de_Novais) founded Luanda on 25 January 1576[^cite_note-10] as "São Paulo da Assumpção de Loanda". He had brought one hundred families of settlers and four hundred soldiers. Most of the Portuguese community lived within the fort.[^cite_note-\:3-11] Several sources from as early as the 17th century called the city "St. Paul de Leonda".[^cite_note-IT-12][^cite_note-NZ-13][^cite_note-UK-14]

In 1618, the Portuguese built the fortress called *Fortaleza São Pedro da Barra*, and they subsequently built two more: [Fortaleza de São Miguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortaleza_de_S%C3%A3o_Miguel) (1634) and [Forte de São Francisco do Penedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_of_Sao_Francisco_do_Penedo) (1765–66). Of these, the Fortaleza de São Miguel is the best preserved.[^cite_note-15]

Luanda was Portugal's bridgehead from 1627, except during the [Dutch rule of Luanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Loango-Angola), from 1640 to 1648, as Fort Aardenburgh. The city served as the centre of [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Angola) to [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Brazil) from c. 1550 to 1836.[^cite_note-16] The slave trade was conducted mostly with the Portuguese colony of Brazil; Brazilian ships were the most numerous in the port of Luanda. This slave trade also involved local merchants and warriors who profited from the trade.[^cite_note-17] During this period, no large scale territorial conquest was intended by the Portuguese; only a few minor settlements were established in the immediate hinterland of Luanda, some on the last stretch of the [Kwanza River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwanza_River).

In the 17th century, the [Imbangala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imbangala) became the main rivals of the [Mbundu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mbundu_people) in supplying slaves to the Luanda market. In the 1751, between 5,000 and 10,000 slaves were annually sold.[^cite_note-a-18] By this time, Angola, a Portuguese colony, was in fact like a colony of Brazil, paradoxically another Portuguese colony. A strong degree of Brazilian influence was noted in Luanda until the [independence of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Brazil) in 1822.

In the 19th century, still under Portuguese rule, Luanda experienced a major economic revolution. The [slave trade](49005.md) was abolished in 1836, and in 1844, Angola's ports were opened to foreign shipping. By 1850, Luanda was one of the greatest and most developed Portuguese cities in the vast [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) outside [continental Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Portugal), full of trading companies, exporting (together with [Benguela](1126618.md)) [palm](57561.md) and [peanut oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut_oil), wax, [copal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copal), timber, ivory, cotton, coffee, and [cocoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean), among many other products. Maize, tobacco, [dried meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerky), and [cassava flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava) are also produced locally. The Angolan bourgeoisie was born by this time.[^cite_note-19]

In 1889, Governor [Brito Capelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermenegildo_Capelo) opened the gates of an aqueduct which supplied the city with water, a formerly scarce resource, laying the foundation for major growth.

### Estado Novo



[Portuguese Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Armed_Forces) marching in Luanda during the [Portuguese Colonial Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Colonial_War) (1961–74).

Main article: [Estado Novo (Portugal)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_Novo_\(Portugal\))

Throughout Portugal's dictatorship, known as the [Estado Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_Novo_\(Portugal\)), Luanda grew from a town of 61,208 with 14.6% of those inhabitants being white in 1940, to a wealthy cosmopolitan major city of 475,328 in 1970 with 124,814 Europeans (26.3%) and around 50,000 mixed race inhabitants (10.5%).[^cite_note-20]

Like most of [Portuguese Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Angola), the [cosmopolitan](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Cosmopolitan)[^cite_note-sister-21] city of Luanda was not affected by the [Portuguese Colonial War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Colonial_War) (1961–1974); economic growth and development in the entire region reached record highs during this period. In 1982, a report called Luanda the "Paris of Africa".[^cite_note-\:4-22]

### Independence

Main articles: [Angolan War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_War_of_Independence), [Portuguese Colonial War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Colonial_War), and [Angolan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Civil_War)



President [José Eduardo dos Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Eduardo_dos_Santos) with President of Brazil [Dilma Rousseff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilma_Rousseff) at the Presidential Palace in 2011.

By the time of [Angolan independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_War_of_Independence) in 1975, Luanda was a modern city with the majority of its population being African, but also dominated by a strong minority of white Portuguese origin.[^cite_note-23]

After the [Carnation Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnation_Revolution) in Lisbon on April 25, 1974, with the advent of independence and the start of the [Angolan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Civil_War) (1975–2002), most of the white Portuguese Luandans left as refugees,[^cite_note-\:0-4] principally for Portugal; however, many travelled over land to [South Africa](17416221.md).



Luanda is experiencing widespread urban renewal and redevelopment in the 21st century, backed largely by profits from oil and diamond industries.

The large numbers of skilled technicians among the force of [Cuban](5042481.md) soldiers sent in to support the Popular Movement for the Liberation of Angola ([MPLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MPLA)) government in the Angolan Civil War were able to make a valuable contribution to restoring and maintaining basic services in the city.

In the following years, however, slums called *[musseques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musseques)*—which had existed for decades—began to grow out of proportion and stretched several kilometres beyond Luanda's former city limits as a result of the decades-long civil war, and because of the rise of deep social inequalities due to large-scale migration of civil war refugees from other Angolan regions. For decades, Luanda's facilities were not adequately expanded to handle this huge increase in the city's population.

### 21st century



The [National Assembly of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Angola)

In 2001 the provisional Angolan governments cleared the Boavista slum in [Luanda Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luanda_Bay) so that a luxury housing redevelopment was possible.[^cite_note-24] By the early 2020s Luanda rose to become one of the world's most expensive cities for [ex pats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriate) to reside.[^cite_note-ft-25]

Following the [Luanda Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luanda_Agreement) in 2002, with the end of the [Angolan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Civil_War) and high economic growth rates fuelled by the wealth provided by the increasing oil and diamond production, major reconstruction started.[^cite_note-26] Luanda has been of major concern because its population had multiplied and had far outgrown the capacity of the city, especially because much of its infrastructure including water, electricity, and roads had become obsolete and degraded.

Luanda has been undergoing major road reconstruction in the 21st century, and new highways are planned to improve connections to [Cacuaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacuaco), [Viana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viana,_Angola), [Samba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samba,_Luanda), and the new airport.[^cite_note-otal.com-27] Major [social housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_housing) is also being constructed to house those who reside in slums, which dominate the landscape of Luanda. A large Chinese firm has been given a contract to construct the majority of replacement housing in Luanda.[^cite_note-28] The Angolan minister of health recently stated poverty in Angola will be overcome by an increase in jobs and the housing of every citizen.[^cite_note-29]

### Human geography

Luanda is divided into two parts, the *Baixa de Luanda* (lower Luanda, the old city) and the *Cidade Alta* (upper city or the new part). The *Baixa de Luanda* is situated next to the port, and has narrow streets and old colonial buildings.[^cite_note-30] However, new constructions have by now covered large areas beyond these traditional limits, and a number of previously independent nuclei — like Viana — were incorporated into the city.

### Metropolitan Luanda

Until 2011, the former Luanda Province comprised five [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_of_Angola). In 2011 the province was enlarged by the addition of two additional municipalities transferred from [Bengo Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengo_Province), namely Icolo e Bengo, and Quiçama. Excluding these additions, the five municipalities composed Greater Luanda. Two new municipalities have been created within Greater Luanda since 2017: [Talatona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talatona) and [Kilamba-Kiaxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilamba-Kiaxi). Since 2024, the Luanda Province has 17 municipalities and Luanda municipality has been dissolved. [Icolo e Bengo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icolo_e_Bengo_Province) became a separate province from Luanda.

#### Districts



Miradouro da Lua in Samba district

The city of Luanda is divided in six urban districts: [Ingombota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingombota), Angola Quiluanje, Maianga, Rangel, Samba and Sambizanga.

In Samba and Sambizanga, more high-rise developments are to be built. The capital Luanda is growing constantly - and in addition, increasingly beyond the official city limits and even provincial boundaries.

Luanda is the seat of a [Roman Catholic](606848.md) archbishop.[^cite_note-32] It is also the location of most of Angola's educational institutions, including the private [Catholic University of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_University_of_Angola) and the public [University of Agostinho Neto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Agostinho_Neto). It is also the home of the colonial Governor's Palace and the [Estádio da Cidadela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_da_Cidadela) (the "Citadel Stadium"), Angola's main stadium, with a total seating capacity of 60,000.[^cite_note-\:2-33]

### Climate

Luanda has a [hot semi-desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BSh*), bordering upon a [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate#Hot_desert_climates) (*BWh*). The climate is warm to hot but surprisingly dry, owing to the cool [Benguela Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguela_Current), which prevents moisture from easily condensing into rain. Frequent fog prevents temperatures from falling at night even during the completely dry months from May to October. Luanda has an annual rainfall of 405 millimetres (15.9 in), but the variability is among the highest in the world, with a co-efficient of variation above 40 percent.[^cite_note-34] The climate is largely influenced by the offshore Benguela current. The current gives the city a surprisingly low humidity despite its tropical latitude, which makes the hotter months considerably more bearable than similar cities in Western/Central Africa.[^cite_note-35] Observed records since 1858 range from 55 millimetres (2.2 in) in 1958 to 851 millimetres (33.5 in) in 1916. The short rainy season in March and April depends on a northerly counter current bringing moisture to the city: it has been shown clearly that weakness in the Benguela Current can increase rainfall about sixfold compared with years when that current is strong.[^cite_note-36]

#### Climate change

A 2019 paper published in [PLOS One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLOS_One) estimated that under [Representative Concentration Pathway 4.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_Concentration_Pathway#4.5), a "moderate" scenario of [climate change](5042951.md) where global warming reaches \~2.5–3 °C (4.5–5.4 °F) by 2100, the climate of Luanda in the year 2050 would most closely resemble the current climate of [Guatemala City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala_City). The annual temperature would increase by 0.7 °C (1.3 °F), the temperature of the coldest month by 0.4 °C (0.72 °F), and the temperature of the warmest month by 0.1 °C (0.18 °F).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] According to [Climate Action Tracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Action_Tracker), the current warming trajectory appears consistent with 2.7 °C (4.9 °F), which closely matches RCP 4.5.[^cite_note-42]

Moreover, according to the 2022 [IPCC Sixth Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPCC_Sixth_Assessment_Report), Luanda is one of 12 major African cities ([Abidjan](21371767.md), [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), [Algiers](1644.md), [Cape Town](6653.md), [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca), [Dakar](44251.md), [Dar es Salaam](8500.md), [Durban](313650.md), [Lagos](85232.md), [Lomé](57067.md), Luanda and [Maputo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maputo)) which would be the most severely affected by the future [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise). It estimates that they would collectively sustain cumulative damages of US\$65 billion under RCP 4.5 and US\$86.5 billion for the high-emission scenario RCP 8.5 by the year 2050. Additionally, RCP 8.5 combined with the hypothetical impact from [marine ice sheet instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_ice_sheet_instability) at high levels of warming would involve up to US\$137.5 billion in damages, while the additional accounting for the "low-probability, high-damage events" may increase aggregate risks to US\$187 billion for the "moderate" RCP4.5, US\$206 billion for RCP8.5 and US\$397 billion under the high-end ice sheet instability scenario.[^cite_note-43] Since sea level rise would continue for about 10,000 years under every scenario of climate change, future costs of sea level rise would only increase, especially without adaptation measures.[^cite_note-44]

[Luanda Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Our_Lady_of_Remedies) was built in 1628

The inhabitants of Luanda are primarily members of African ethnic groups, mainly [Ambundu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Mbundu_people), [Ovimbundu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovimbundu), and [Bakongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakongo). The official and the most widely used language is [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language), although several [Bantu languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages) are also used, chiefly [Kimbundu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimbundu), [Umbundu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbundu), and [Kikongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikongo).[^cite_note-\:1-46]

The population of Luanda has grown dramatically in recent years, due in large part to war-time migration to the city, which is safe compared to the rest of the country.[^cite_note-47] In 2006, however, Luanda saw an increase in violent crime, particularly in the shanty towns that surround the colonial urban core.[^cite_note-48]

There is a sizable minority population of [European](9239.md) origin, especially [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) (about 260,000), as well as [Brazilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_people). In recent years, mainly since the mid-2000s, immigration from Portugal has increased due to greater opportunities present in Angola's booming economy.[^cite_note-spiegel-49][^cite_note-50] There is a sprinkling of immigrants from other African countries as well, including a small expatriate [South African](17416221.md) community. A small number of people of Luanda are of mixed race — European/Portuguese and native African. Over the last decades, a significant [Chinese](372983.md) community has formed,[^cite_note-51] as has a much smaller [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people) community.[^cite_note-52]

## Economy

See also: [Economy of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Angola)



*Cidade Financeira de Luanda*



High rises in downtown Luanda

Around one-third of Angolans live in Luanda, 53% of whom live in poverty. Living conditions in Luanda are poor for most of the people, with essential services such as safe drinking water and electricity still in short supply, and severe shortcomings in traffic conditions.[^cite_note-54]

Luanda is one of the world's [most expensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_cities_for_expatriate_employees) cities for resident foreigners.[^cite_note-55] In Mercer's cost of living index, Luanda was ranked as top of the list due to the extremely high costs of goods and security. Luanda sits above [Seoul](19159283.md), [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva) and [Shanghai](27643.md) in the rankings. These costs have fueled rampant inequality in the city. Skyscrapers are left barren as the price of oil drops.[^cite_note-56]



*Marginal* promenade along the bay



Tower on *Rua Kwame Nkrumah*

New import tariffs imposed in March 2014 made Luanda even more expensive. As an example, a half-litre tub of vanilla ice cream at the supermarket was reported to cost US\$31. The higher import tariffs applied to hundreds of items, from garlic to cars. The stated aim was to try to diversify the heavily oil-dependent economy and nurture farming and industry, sectors that have remained weak. These tariffs have caused much hardship in a country where the average salary was US\$260 per month in 2010, the latest year for which data was available. However, the average salary in the booming oil industry was over 20 times higher at US\$5,400 per month.\[^cite_note-[www.independent.co.uk-57](http://www.independent.co.uk-57/)]

Manufacturing includes [processed foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convenience_food), [beverages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drink), [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile), [cement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cement) and other building materials, [plastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic) products, metalware, [cigarettes](38327.md), and shoes/clothes. [Petroleum](23195.md) (found in nearby off-shore deposits) is refined in the city, although this facility was repeatedly damaged during the [Angolan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Civil_War) of 1975–2002. Luanda has an excellent natural harbour; the chief exports are [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), [sugar](27712.md), [diamonds](8082.md), [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron), and [salt](1605200.md).[^cite_note-58]

The city also has a thriving building industry, an effect of the nationwide economic boom experienced since 2002, when political stability returned with the end of the civil war. Economic growth is largely supported by [oil extraction](8082027.md) activities, although great [diversification](63252200.md) is taking place. Large [investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_\(macroeconomics\)) (domestic and international), along with strong economic growth, has dramatically increased construction of all economic sectors in the city of Luanda.[^cite_note-economist.com-59] In 2007, the first modern shopping mall in Angola was established in the city at [Belas Shopping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belas_Shopping) mall.[^cite_note-angonot-60]

## Transport



The [Port of Luanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Luanda) administration

### Railway

Luanda is the starting point of the [Luanda railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luanda_railway) that goes due east to Malanje. The [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Civil_War) left the railway non-functional, but the railway has been restored up to [Dondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dondo,_Angola) and [Malanje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malanje).[^cite_note-61]

### Airports



Dr. António Agostinho Neto International Airport, Luanda

The main airport of Luanda was [Quatro de Fevereiro Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quatro_de_Fevereiro_Airport). A new [international airport](1519705.md), [Dr. António Agostinho Neto International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Ant%C3%B3nio_Agostinho_Neto_International_Airport) was constructed southeast of the city, a few kilometres from [Viana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viana,_Angola), and was expected to be opened in 2011.[^cite_note-62] However, as the Angolan government did not continue to make the payments due to the Chinese enterprise in charge of the construction, the firm suspended its work in 2010. The airport finally opened in November 2023. The new airport will gradually replace the old airport.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

### Port

The [Port of Luanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Luanda) serves as the largest [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) of [Angola](701.md) and is one of the busiest ports in Africa.[^cite_note-africanbusinesscentral-65] Major expansion of this port is also taking place.[^cite_note-66] In 2014, a new port is being developed at [Dande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dande), about 30 km to the north.



The [Port of Luanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Luanda) is one of the busiest ports in Africa.

[^cite_note-africanbusinesscentral-65]

### Road transport

Luanda's roads are in a poor state of repair, but are undergoing an extensive reconstruction process by the government in order to relieve [traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic) congestion in the city. Major road repairs can be found taking place in nearly every neighbourhood, including a major 6-lane [highway](48519.md) connected Luanda to [Viana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viana,_Angola).[^cite_note-67]

### Public transport

Public transit is provided by the suburban services of the [Luanda Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luanda_Railway), by the public company [TCUL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transporte_Colectivo_Urbano_de_Luanda), and by a large fleet of privately owned collective taxis as white-blue painted minibuses called *Candongueiro*. Candongueiros are usually [Toyota Hiace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Hiace) vans, that are built to carry 12 people, although the candongueiros usually carry at least 15 people. They charge from 100 to 200 kwanzas per trip. They are known to disobey traffic rules, for example not stopping at signs and driving over pavements and aisles.

In 2019, the [Luanda Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luanda_Light_Rail) network with an estimated cost of [US \$](18717338.md)3 billion was announced to begin construction in 2020.[^cite_note-68]
