The **ACM A. M. Turing Award** is an annual prize given by the [Association for Computing Machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_Computing_Machinery) (ACM) for contributions of lasting and major technical importance to [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science). It is generally recognized as the highest distinction in the field of computer science and is often referred to as the "[Nobel Prize of Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_considered_the_highest_in_a_field)".[^cite_note-ACM-2][^cite_note-BartoSutton-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] As of 2026, 81 people have been awarded the prize, with the most recent recipients being [Charles H. Bennett](1624482.md) and [Gilles Brassard](1627181.md), who won in 2025.

The award is named after [Alan Turing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Turing), also referred as "Father of Computer Science", who was a British mathematician and [reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader_\(academic_rank\)) in mathematics at the [University of Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Manchester). Turing is often credited as being the founder of [theoretical computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoretical_computer_science) and [artificial intelligence](1164.md),[^cite_note-7] and a key contributor to the Allied [cryptanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptanalysis) of the [Enigma cipher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enigma_cipher) during [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-bbc-copeland-8] From 2007 to 2013, the award was accompanied by a prize of US\$250,000, with financial support provided by [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel) and [Google](1092923.md).[^cite_note-ACM-2][^cite_note-9] Since 2014, the award has been accompanied by a prize of US\$1 million, with financial support provided by Google.[^cite_note-million-1][^cite_note-10]

Photo of The Turing Award on display at Nokia Bell Labs, August 2025.

The Turing Award of 1983, given to [Dennis Ritchie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Ritchie) and [Ken Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Thompson), on display at [Nokia Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia_Bell_Labs).

The first recipient, in 1966, was [Alan Perlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Perlis). The youngest recipient was [Donald Knuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Knuth), who won in 1974 at the age of 36,[^cite_note-11] while the oldest recipient was [Alfred Aho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Aho), who won in 2020 at the age of 79.[^cite_note-12] Three women have been awarded the prize: [Frances Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Allen) (in 2006),[^cite_note-Allen-13] [Barbara Liskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Liskov) (in 2008),[^cite_note-liskov-14] and [Shafi Goldwasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi_Goldwasser) (in 2012).[^cite_note-goldwasser-15]
