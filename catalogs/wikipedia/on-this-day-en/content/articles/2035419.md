There are two main holidays in [Islam](6037917.md) that are celebrated by [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) worldwide: [Eid al-Fitr](154944.md) and [Eid al Adha](358226.md). The timing of both holidays are set by the lunar [Islamic calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar), which is based upon the cycle of the moon, and so is different from the more common, European, solar-based [Gregorian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar). Every year, the Gregorian dates of the Islamic holidays change.

Both Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha follow a period of 9 holy days or nights: the last 9 nights of [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan) for Eid al-Fitr, and the first 9 days of [Dhu al-Hijjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhu_al-Hijjah) for Eid al-Adha. The [Night of Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_of_Power) (Arabic: لیلة القدر, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): Laylat al-Qadr), one of the last 10 nights of Ramadan, is the holiest night of the year.[^cite_note-1] Conversely, the [Day of Arafah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_Arafah), the day before Eid al-Adha, is the holiest day of the Islamic year.

There are a number of other days of note as well as festivals, some common to all Muslims, others specific to [Shia Islam](26961.md) or branches thereof.

Additionally, Friday is considered the holiest day of the week, and, in Islamic tradition, is considered a celebration in itself. [Friday prayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_prayer) (Juma) are congregational prayers held in [mosques](19894.md), and Muslims are encouraged to wear clean and refined clothes, perfume, and bathe. It is customary to eat special meals with family on this day.

## Holidays

Main articles: [Eid al-Fitr](154944.md) and [Eid al-Adha](358226.md)

Eid al-Fitr is celebrated at the end of [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan) (a month of fasting during daylight hours), and Muslims may perform acts of [zakat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakat) (charity) on the occasion, which begins after the new moon is sighted for the beginning of the month of [Shawwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawwal). Celebration begins with prayers on the morning of 1 Shawwal, followed by breakfast, and often celebratory meals throughout the day.

Eid al-Adha is celebrated on the tenth day of [Dhu al-Hijjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhu_al-Hijjah), when the [Hajj](23487999.md) pilgrimage takes place which lasts for four days. Muslims may perform an act of zakat and friendship by slaughtering a sheep or cow and distributing the meat to family, friends, and the poor. Muslims are also encouraged to be especially friendly and reach out to one another during this period.[^cite_note-2]

## Religious practices

### Fasting



The [Fanoos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanoos), a lantern used in homes, mosques and streets during Ramadan

Main article: [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan)

Muslims celebrate when they believe the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) was first revealed to Muhammed by fasting from dawn to sunset during [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan_\(calendar_month\)), the ninth month of the [Islamic calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar).[^cite_note-3] Fasting is considered a purifying experience so that Muslims can gain compassion and deepen their faith in [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Islam).[^cite_note-4] Those with certain health conditions such as diabetes, and children are exempt from fasting.[^cite_note-5] Travelers, and women who are menstruating or nursing a baby, are exempt from fasting but are required to fast later.[^cite_note-6]

## Dates of holidays and other days of note

The Islamic calendar is based on the [synodic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_period) of the [Moon's revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_phase) around the Earth, approximately 291⁄2 days. The Islamic calendar alternates months of 29 and 30 days (which begin with the new moon). Twelve of these months make up an Islamic year, which is 11 days shorter than the Gregorian year. Some Gregorian dates may vary slightly from those given, and may also vary by country. See [Islamic calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar#Astronomical_considerations).[^cite_note-7]
