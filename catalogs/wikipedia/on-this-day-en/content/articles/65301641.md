**Belinda Balluku** (born 9 October 1973) is an [Albanian](738.md) politician who has served as [Deputy Prime Minister of Albania](11930399.md) from 2022 until 2026, as well as serving as [Minister of Infrastructure and Energy](54109553.md) of Albania from 2019 until 2026.[^cite_note-albania_govt_sworn_in_ibna_2019-1][^cite_note-albania_govt_endorses_reshuffle_seenews_2019-2] She has also been a Member of the [Parliament of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Albania) representing the [District of Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Tirana) since 2021.

On 20 November 2025, the [Special Court Against Corruption and Organized Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Structure_against_Corruption_and_Organized_Crime#First_Instance) ordered the suspension from her governmental duties and a ban on leaving the country following her being formally charged by the [Special Prosecution Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Structure_against_Corruption_and_Organized_Crime) in connection with the [Llogara Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llogara_Tunnel) affair, including alleged violations of equality in public procurement related to the tunnel project and the fourth lot of [Tirana](31075.md)’s Outer Ring Road.[^cite_note-syri.net-3][^cite_note-4]

## Early and personal life

Balluku was born on 9 October 1973 in [Tirana](31075.md).[^cite_note-news1-5] She stated in an interview that her family was persecuted during the Communist era of Albania and that her dad, Çlirim Balluku, worked in [bitumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitumen) mines in [Selenicë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selenic%C3%AB).[^cite_note-6] Her grandfather was [Beqir Balluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beqir_Balluku), an early member of the communist movement in Albania and the [Minister of People's Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Albania\)), who was executed in November 1975 for organizing a [coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coup) against the government.[^cite_note-7]

She graduated from the Athens Institute of Technology with degrees in law and business administration, and later on obtained an honorary [master's degree](170346.md) in management from the [International Air Transport Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association)'s facilities in [Miami](53846.md).[^cite_note-8] She was previously director of the companies of [Albcontrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albcontrol), the National Air Traffic Agency, and was a senior member of the state-owned mobile telephone company [Telekom Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Albania) as the Director of Customer Care and later of marketing.[^cite_note-news1-5] She is the ex-wife of journalist [Sokol Balla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokol_Balla).[^cite_note-9] She has two children: Colin and Belen.[^cite_note-news1-5]

## Political career

In 2004, Mrs. Balluku joined the staff of the [Municipality of Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_of_Tirana), initially as a Public Relations Advisor and later as Director of the Mayor's Cabinet.[^cite_note-gov-10] During this period, she led the development of communication strategies with the public and the media.[^cite_note-gov-10] In 2006 she formally joined the [Socialist Party of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Albania).[^cite_note-vox-11] In 2019 she became [Minister of Infrastructure and Energy](54109553.md) in the cabinet of [Edi Rama](277805.md), and later in the [2021 Albanian parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Albanian_parliamentary_election) was elected to the [Parliament of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Albania) for the [District of Tirana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Tirana).[^cite_note-vox-11] In 2022 she announced that the ministry would attempt to implement a plan where citizens received scheduled energy in the case of emergencies.[^cite_note-vox-11] She is the political leader of the Socialist Party in the [Fier municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fier_municipality).[^cite_note-12]

## 2025 corruption investigation

In November 2025, Albania’s Special Anti-Corruption Structure ([SPAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Structure_against_Corruption_and_Organized_Crime)) announced criminal charges against Balluku and several officials from the Ministry of Infrastructure and Energy in connection with the public tender for the Llogara Tunnel.[^cite_note-\:0-13] According to the prosecutors’ filing, [Signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_\(software\)) messages recovered from the phone of Evis Berberi, then director of the Albanian Road Authority, allegedly showed Balluku discussing tender deadlines and bidder details.[^cite_note-\:0-13] SPAK accuses the group of “creating unjust advantages” for the [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) consortium Intekar Yapi & ASL Insaat in violation of Albania’s procurement laws (Law 162/2020 and Decision 285/2021). Balluku has denied any wrongdoing.

On 20 November 2025, the [Special Court Against Corruption and Organized Crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Structure_against_Corruption_and_Organized_Crime#First_Instance) ordered two security measures against Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Infrastructure and Energy Belinda Balluku: suspension from her governmental duties and a ban on leaving the country. Balluku had previously stated in [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Parliament) that she would not comment on ongoing investigations and had reaffirmed her support for the justice reform.[^cite_note-syri.net-3]

On February 26, [Edi Rama](277805.md), Prime Minister of Albania and leader of [Socialist Party of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Albania), dismissed Belinda Balluku from the government as the investigation intensified.[^cite_note-14]

### Parliamentary vote on immunity and arrest authorization

Earlier measures imposed by [SPAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Structure_against_Corruption_and_Organized_Crime) include suspension from public office and a ban on leaving Albania, with her passport blocked while the investigation proceeds.[^cite_note-15] Despite these existing restrictions, [SPAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Structure_against_Corruption_and_Organized_Crime) requested a stricter preventive measure (arrest or house arrest), arguing that there was risk of interference with the investigation, including alleged intimidation of witnesses and potential manipulation or destruction of evidence.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Because Balluku is a sitting member of parliament, SPAK needed the Assembly to lift her parliamentary immunity before any arrest measure could be executed.[^cite_note-19]

On 12 March 2026, the Albanian parliament rejected SPAK’s request to lift Balluku’s immunity, meaning she remains under investigation with existing restrictions such as the travel ban but cannot currently be arrested.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]
