A cattle raid during the [Swabian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_War), 1499

**Cattle raiding** is the act of [stealing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theft) live cattle, often several or many at once. In [Australia](4689264.md), such stealing is often referred to as **duffing** and the perpetrator as a **duffer**.[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-2] In parts of the country, however, especially in [Queensland](59051.md), the practice is known as **poddy-dodging** and the perpetrator as a **poddy-dodger**.[^cite_note-3] In [North America](21139.md), especially in the [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States) [cowboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboy) culture, cattle theft is dubbed **rustling** and one who engages in it is a **rustler**.[^cite_note-4]

## Historical cattle raiding

The act of cattle-raiding is quite ancient, first attested over seven thousand years ago,[^cite_note-5] and is one of the oldest-known aspects of [Proto-Indo-European culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_culture), being seen in inscriptions on artifacts such as the Norse [Golden Horns of Gallehus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horns_of_Gallehus)[^cite_note-6] and in works such as the [Old Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Irish) *[Táin Bó Cúailnge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1in_B%C3%B3_C%C3%BAailnge)* ("Cattle Raid of [Cooley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooley_Peninsula)"), the [*paṇis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ancient_Indo-Aryan_peoples_and_tribes#Vedic_tribes) of the *[Rigveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda),* the *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata)* cattle raids and cattle rescues.[^cite_note-7]

### Cattle Raiding in Ancient Greek Mythology

Cattle raiding played a major part in Ancient [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology).[^cite_note-8] [Hermes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermes), for example, raided [Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo)'s cattle when he was newly born.[^cite_note-9] An early story of [Oedipus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oedipus) from [Hesiod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesiod)'s [Works and Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Works_and_Days) had him die in a cattle raid and not blind without a kingdom like in the plays of [Sophocles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophocles) and [Euripides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euripides).[^cite_note-10] [Heracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heracles) raided the cattle of [Geryon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geryon);[^cite_note-11] which in [Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato)'s [Gorgias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorgias_\(dialogue\)) [Callicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callicles) used as an example to say the 'might makes right'.[^cite_note-12] In the [Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odyssey), [Odysseus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odysseus)' men chose to ignore a warning not to rob the cattle of the god [Helios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helios) because of hunger and were cursed to die.[^cite_note-13] Cattle raiding may have been part of initiation rites for ancient Greek youths.[^cite_note-14] These examples point to a culture of cattle raiding in the pre-Classical Hellenic world.[^cite_note-15]

### Central Asia

In his childhood, the [Turco-Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turco-Mongol_tradition) conqueror [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur) and a small band of followers raided travelers for goods, especially animals such as sheep, horses, and cattle. Around 1363, it is believed that Timur tried to steal a sheep from a shepherd but was shot by two arrows, one in his right leg and another in his right hand, where he lost two fingers. Both injuries disabled him for life. Timur's injuries have given him the names of Timur the Lame and Tamerlane by Europeans.[^cite_note-16]

### Ireland and Britain



Depiction of cattle raid in Ireland c. 1580 in [The Image of Irelande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Image_of_Irelande,_with_a_Discoverie_of_Woodkarne) by John Derricke.

In [Gaelic Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaelic_Ireland), cattle raiding, whether in retaliation for an insult under the code of conduct or to keep the whole clan fed during a difficult winter, was a common part of warfare between [Irish clans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_clans), as is often depicted in stories from [Irish mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_mythology), such as the *[Táin Bó Cúailnge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1in_B%C3%B3_C%C3%BAailnge)* and the *[Táin Bó Flidhais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1in_B%C3%B3_Flidhais)*. Cattle raiding and selling [protection against theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protection_racket) continued by [Irish clan chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Name) and [rapparees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapparee), particularly against the estates of [Anglo-Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Irish) landlords, well into the 18th century in Ireland.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

Warfare between [Scottish clans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_clans) was often for very similar reasons and, during the 17th and 18th centuries, many [Scottish clan chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_clan_chief) would similarly operate an extralegal [Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protection_racket) over the cattle herds of the [Lowland gentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_peerage) in return for [protection money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protection_money), which Highland Chiefs similarly used to feed their tenants and clansmen. Any cattle that were stolen from herds under the Chiefs' Watch were either retrieved, or he paid for them in full.[^cite_note-19]

Cattle-raiding by the [Border reivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_reivers) was a serious problem for many centuries on both sides of the [Anglo-Scottish border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Scottish_border).

### American Old West



The [Beefsteak Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beefsteak_Raid) (1864) during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War).

In the [American frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_frontier), rustling was considered a serious offense and in some cases resulted in [vigilantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigilante) hanging or shooting the thieves.[^cite_note-20]

[Mexican](652127.md) rustlers were a major issue during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) (1861–1865); the Mexican government was accused of supporting the habit. American rustlers also stole Mexican cattle from across the border. Failure to [brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livestock_branding) [new calves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maverick_\(animal\)) facilitated theft.

Conflict over alleged rustling was a major issue in the [Johnson County War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_County_War) of 1892 in [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming).

The transition from [open range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_range) to fenced grazing gradually reduced the practice of rustling in North America. In the 20th century, so called "suburban rustling" became more common, with rustlers anesthetizing cattle and taking them directly to auction. This often takes place at night, posing problems for law enforcement, because on very large ranches it can take several days for the loss of cattle to be noticed and reported. Convictions are extremely rare.

### Chile and Argentina

See also: [Malón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mal%C3%B3n) and [Banditry in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banditry_in_Chile)



*El Malón*, [Johann Moritz Rugendas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Moritz_Rugendas) (1802–1858)



*La vuelta del [malón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mal%C3%B3n)* (The Return of the Raiders) by [Ángel Della Valle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngel_Della_Valle) (1892).

Cattle raiding became a major issue at the end of the 19th century in Argentina, where cattle stolen during *[malones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mal%C3%B3n)* were taken through *[Camino de los chilenos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camino_de_los_chilenos)* across the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) to [Chile](5489.md), where they were exchanged for alcoholic beverages and [firearms](11966.md). Several indigenous groups and outlaws, such as the [Boroano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroano_people) and [Ranquel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranquel) peoples, and the [Pincheira brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pincheira_brothers), ravaged the southern frontier of Argentina in search of cattle. To prevent the cattle raiding, the Argentine government built a system of trenches called [Zanja de Alsina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanja_de_Alsina) in the 1870s. Most cattle raids ended after the military campaigns of the [Conquest of the Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_the_Desert) in the 1870s, and the following partition of [Patagonia](65433.md) established by the [Boundary Treaty of 1881 between Chile and Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Treaty_of_1881_between_Chile_and_Argentina).

In a letter to Chilean President [Manuel Montt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Montt) Mapuche chief [Mañil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma%C3%B1il) denounced the plunder of graves in search of [Mapuche silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapuche_silverwork), arson of [Mapuche houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruka_\(Mapuche\)) and other abuses against Mapuches that were happening in the newly created province. Mañil further accused intendant Villalón con Salbo of becoming rich by cattle theft.[^cite_note-21]

The return of Chilean veterans from the [War of the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Pacific) coincided with the Chilean Army's crushing of [Mapuche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapuche) resistance in the [Occupation of Araucanía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Araucan%C3%ADa) (1861–1883). This led to opportunities for bandits and veterans-turned-bandits to immigrate to the newly opened Araucanía territory,[^cite_note-salinas60-22][^cite_note-MembandidajeCRONO-23] leading to sudden rise in violence and in a region that was recovering from Chilean-Mapuche warfare.[^cite_note-cadiz-24] Bandits that immigrated to Araucanía allied with displaced Mapuche and made cattle theft their chief business.[^cite_note-salinas60-22] Stolen cattle was sold in marketplaces through the region.[^cite_note-salinas60-22]

### Southern Africa

In southern Africa, cattle were an essential part of the economies of its agropastoral societies, including [Nguni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguni_peoples) and [Sotho-Tswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotho-Tswana_peoples) speaking peoples. Cattle served as a store of wealth and were exchanged through dowries, tribal tributes, indemnities, and political alliances. Chiefs accumulated cattle through tributes, fines, forfeits, and raids, and redistributed them through loans for regular members to tend to, in exchange receiving some of the milk.[^cite_note-25] Cattle raids were regularly conducted during warfare.[^cite_note-King2017-26]

Raiding intensified during early nineteenth century amid environmental stress, changing trade networks, European settler encroachment, and state formation in a period known as the [Mfecane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mfecane) or *Difaqane,* as smaller chiefdoms were incorporated into larger polities. Captured cattle helped chiefs supply followers and reward military service, centralizing their power, while the loss of herds made smaller tribes dependent on more powerful chiefs.[^cite_note-Wright2009-27][^cite_note-Eldredge1992-28]

[Mzilikazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mzilikazi_KaMashobane), the founder of the [Ndebele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ndebele_people) nation, raided widely on the highveld, incorporating some conquered Sotho-Tswana groups while displacing others.[^cite_note-Wright2009-27] [Moshoeshoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshoeshoe_I), the founder of the [Basotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Lesotho) nation, raided nearby [Thembu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thembu) and [Xhosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_people) groups for cattle, and loaned them among his followers to consolidate his power.[^cite_note-King2017-26][^cite_note-BritannicaMoshoeshoe-29]

## Contemporary cattle raiding (1990–present)

### East Africa

See also: [Cattle raiding in Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle_raiding_in_Kenya)

The [Turkana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkana_people), [Pokot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokot_people), [Tugen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tugen_people), [Keiyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keiyo_people), [Marakwet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marakwet_people), and [Samburu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samburu_people) [Nilotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilotic_peoples) populations in northwestern [Kenya](188171.md) often raid each other for cattle.[^cite_note-30] Violent cattle rustling has caused massive loss of lives such as the Monday 12 March 2001 raid among the [Marakwet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marakwet_people) in Murkutwo Location, Elgeyo Marakwet County, suspected to have been caused by the [Pokot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokot_people).[^cite_note-31]

### South Sudan

Main article: [Sudanese nomadic conflicts](23013592.md)

[Conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinka-Nuer_conflict) over pastures and cattle raids has been happening between [Dinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinka_people) and [Nuer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuer_people) as they battle for grazing their animals.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

Cattle rustling is a major problem in rural areas of [South Sudan](32350676.md). In the state of [Jonglei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonglei), cattle raids in August 2011 left around 600 people dead. Once again in January 2012, ethnic clashes related to cattle theft killed between 2,000 and 3,000 people and displaced as many as 34,500 in the area around [Pibor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pibor).[^cite_note-34]
