The **Academy Award for Best Original Score** is an award presented annually by the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences) (AMPAS) to the best substantial body of music in the form of [dramatic underscoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_score) written specifically for the film by the submitting composer.[^cite_note-1] Some pre-existing music is allowed, though, but a contending film must include a minimum of original music. This minimum since [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/94th_Academy_Awards) is established as 35% of the music, which is raised to 80% for sequels and [franchise films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_series).[^cite_note-2] Fifteen scores are shortlisted before nominations are announced.

## History

The Academy began awarding movies for their scores in [1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Academy_Awards). The category was originally called **Best Scoring**. At the time, winners and nominees were a mix of original scores and adaptations of pre-existing material. Following the controversial win of [Charles Previn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Previn) for *[One Hundred Men and a Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Hundred_Men_and_a_Girl)* in [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_Academy_Awards), a film without a credited composer that featured pre-existing classical music, the Academy added a **Best Original Score** category in [1939](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Academy_Awards).[^cite_note-3] In [1942](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Academy_Awards), the distinction between the two Scoring categories changed slightly as they were renamed to **Best Music Score of a Dramatic Picture** and **Best Scoring of a Musical Picture**.[^cite_note-4] This marked the first time the category was split into separate genres. From [1942](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Academy_Awards) to [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/57th_Academy_Awards), musical scores had their own category, with the exception of [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30th_Academy_Awards), [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53rd_Academy_Awards), and [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/54th_Academy_Awards). Following the wins of four [Walt Disney Feature Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Animation_Studios) films in six years from [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/62nd_Academy_Awards) to [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/67th_Academy_Awards) (*[The Little Mermaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Mermaid_\(1989_film\))*, *[Beauty and the Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty_and_the_Beast_\(1991_film\))*, *[Aladdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aladdin_\(1992_Disney_film\))*, and *[The Lion King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lion_King)*) during a period called the [Disney Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_Renaissance), it was decided to once again split the Best Original Score category by genres, this time by combining comedies and musicals together. As [Alan Bergman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_and_Marilyn_Bergman), the chairman of the Academy's music branch said, "People were voting for the songs, not the underscores. We felt that Academy members outside the music branch didn't distinguish between the two. So when a score like *The Lion King* is competing against a drama like *[Forrest Gump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forrest_Gump),* it's apples and oranges – not in the quality of the score, but in the way it functions in the movie. There's a big difference."[^cite_note-auto-5] The category was therefore split into **Best Original Dramatic Score** and **Best Original Musical or Comedy Score** in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/68th_Academy_Awards). This change proved unpopular in the other branches of the Academy as [Charles Bernstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Bernstein_\(composer\)), chairman of the Academy's rules committee, noted that "no other Oscar category depended on a film's genre" and "the job of composing an underscore for a romantic comedy is not substantially different from working on a heavy drama."[^cite_note-auto-5] This split was reverted in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/72nd_Academy_Awards).

In [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/93rd_Academy_Awards), rules were changed to require that a film's score include a minimum of 60% original music. Franchise films and sequels must include a minimum of 80% new music.[^cite_note-Hammond-6] In [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/94th_Academy_Awards), the rules were changed again, lowering the minimum percentage of original music from 60% to 35% of the total music in the film.[^cite_note-7]

## Academy Award for Best Original Musical

The **Academy Award for Best Original Musical** is a category that was re-established in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/72nd_Academy_Awards) after the Best Original Musical or Comedy Score category was retired. It has never been awarded in its present form due to a prolonged drought of films meeting the sufficient eligibility requirements. The Music Branch Executive Committee of the Academy decides whether there are enough quality submissions to justify its activation.[^cite_note-oscars.org-8]

&#x20;According to the rules, the Best Original Musical is defined as follows:&#x20;

> An original musical consists of not fewer than five original songs by the same writer or team of writers, either used as voiceovers or visually performed. Each of these songs must be substantively rendered, clearly audible, intelligible, and must further the storyline of the motion picture. An arbitrary group of songs unessential to the storyline will not be considered eligible.[^cite_note-oscars.org-8]

## Winners and nominees

The following is the list of nominated composers organized by year, and listing both films and composers. The years shown in the following list of winners are the production years, thus a reference to 1967 means the [Oscars](324.md) presented in 1968 for films released in 1967.



[Louis Silvers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Silvers) was the inaugural winner of this category, winning for *[One Night of Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Night_of_Love)* (1934).

[Alfred Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Newman) has won the most awards in this category with 9 wins among 43 nominations. He won for *[Alexander's Ragtime Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander's_Ragtime_Band_\(film\))* (1938), *[Tin Pan Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin_Pan_Alley_\(film\))* (1940), *[The Song of Bernadette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Song_of_Bernadette_\(film\))* (1940), *[Mother Wore Tights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Wore_Tights)* (1947), *[With a Song in My Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/With_a_Song_in_My_Heart_\(film\))* (1952), *[Call Me Madam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_Me_Madam_\(film\))* (1953), *[The King and I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_King_and_I_\(1956_film\))* (1956), and *[Camelot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camelot_\(film\))* (1967).

Only one composer has won two Scoring Oscars the same year: in 1973, [Marvin Hamlisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Hamlisch) won Original Dramatic Score for *[The Way We Were](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Way_We_Were)* and Best Adaptation Score, for *[The Sting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sting)*. Hamlisch also won Best Song that year for *[The Way We Were](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Way_We_Were_\(song\))*, making him the only composer to win three music Oscars in the same year.

Only one composer has won Oscars three years in a row: [Roger Edens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Edens) won for *[Easter Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Parade_\(film\))* (1948), *[On the Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Town_\(film\))* (1949) and *[Annie Get Your Gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Get_Your_Gun_\(film\))* (1950).

Eight composers have won Oscars two years in a row:

1. [Ray Heindorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Heindorf) won for *[Yankee Doodle Dandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Doodle_Dandy)* (1942) and *[This Is the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_the_Army)* (1943).

2. [Franz Waxman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Waxman) won for *[Sunset Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset_Boulevard_\(film\))* (1950) and *[A Place in the Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Place_in_the_Sun_\(1951_film\))* (1951).

3. [Alfred Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Newman) won for *[With a Song in My Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/With_a_Song_in_My_Heart_\(film\))* (1952) and *[Call Me Madam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_Me_Madam_\(film\))* (1953). He won again two years in a row for *[Love Is a Many-Splendored Thing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Is_a_Many-Splendored_Thing_\(film\))* (1955) and *[The King and I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_King_and_I_\(1956_film\))* (1956).

4. [Adolph Deutsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolph_Deutsch) won for *[Seven Brides for Seven Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Brides_for_Seven_Brothers)* (1954) and *[Oklahoma!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma!_\(film\))* (1955).

5. [André Previn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Previn) won for *[Gigi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigi_\(1958_film\))* (1958) and *[Porgy and Bess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porgy_and_Bess_\(film\))* (1959). He won again two years in a row for *[Irma la Douce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irma_la_Douce)* (1963) and *[My Fair Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Fair_Lady_\(film\))* (1964).

6. [Leonard Rosenman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Rosenman) won for *[Barry Lyndon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Lyndon)* (1975) and *[Bound for Glory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bound_for_Glory_\(1976_film\))* (1976).

7. [Alan Menken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Menken) won for *[Beauty and The Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauty_and_the_Beast_\(1991_film\))* (1991) and *[Aladdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aladdin_\(1992_Disney_film\))* (1992).

8. [Gustavo Santaolalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavo_Santaolalla) won for *[Brokeback Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokeback_Mountain)* (2005) and *[Babel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babel_\(2006_film\))* (2006).

### Female nominees

As of 2025, only 11 women have been nominated in music score categories: [Ann Ronell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Ronell), Tylwyth Kymry, [Angela Morley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Morley), [Marilyn Bergman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_and_Marilyn_Bergman), [Rachel Portman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Portman), [Anne Dudley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Dudley), [Lynn Ahrens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn_Ahrens), [Hildur Guðnadóttir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildur_Gu%C3%B0nad%C3%B3ttir), [Germaine Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germaine_Franco), [Laura Karpman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Karpman), and [Camille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_\(French_singer\)). Kymry, Bergman, and Ahrens were nominated for their contribution as [lyricists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyricist).

Four women have won in the scoring categories. Three are composers: [Rachel Portman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Portman), who won for *[Emma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_\(1996_theatrical_film\))* (1996); [Anne Dudley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Dudley), who won for *[The Full Monty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Full_Monty)* (1997); and [Hildur Guðnadóttir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildur_Gu%C3%B0nad%C3%B3ttir), who won for *[Joker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joker_\(2019_film\))* (2019). The fourth is lyricist [Marilyn Bergman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_and_Marilyn_Bergman), who won for *[Yentl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yentl_\(film\))* (1983) in the Original Song Score category, sharing the award with co-lyricist [Alan Bergman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_and_Marilyn_Bergman) (her husband) and composer [Michel Legrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Legrand). Hildur is the only woman to win the award under no qualifications; Bergman won for Best Song Score while Portman and Dudley won for Best Musical or Comedy Score.

The female composers nominated for multiple Scoring Oscars are [Rachel Portman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Portman), who was nominated for *[Emma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_\(1996_theatrical_film\))* (1996) (for which she won for Best Musical or Comedy Score), *[The Cider House Rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cider_House_Rules)* (1999), and *[Chocolat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chocolat_\(2000_film\))* (2000); and [Angela Morley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Morley), who was nominated twice in the Original Song Score or Adaptation Score category for *[The Little Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Prince_\(1974_film\))* (1974) and *[The Slipper and the Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Slipper_and_the_Rose)* (1976).

### Notable nominees

[Dmitri Shostakovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Shostakovich) and [Duke Ellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Ellington) were both nominated the same year but lost to the arrangers of *[West Side Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Side_Story_\(1961_film\))*.

The scores of *[Midnight Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_Express_\(film\))* by [Giorgio Moroder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Moroder) in 1979, *[Slumdog Millionaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slumdog_Millionaire)* by [A.R. Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._R._Rahman) in 2009, and *[The Social Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Social_Network)* by [Trent Reznor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent_Reznor) and [Atticus Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atticus_Ross) in 2011 are the only scores with electronic-based music ever to win the award. In addition, the electronic-based scores of *[Witness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witness_\(1985_film\))* by [Maurice Jarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Jarre) in 1986, *[Rain Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_Man)* by [Hans Zimmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Zimmer) in 1989, and *[Her](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_\(2013_film\))* by [William Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Butler_\(musician\)), and [Owen Pallett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Pallett) in 2014 have also been nominated.

Noted nominated composers known for their music mostly outside the film world include: [Aaron Copland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Copland), [Kurt Weill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Weill), [Gian Carlo Menotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Carlo_Menotti), [Philip Glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Glass), [John Corigliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Corigliano), [Peter Maxwell Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Maxwell_Davies), [Randy Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Newman), [Richard Rodney Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Rodney_Bennett), [Stephen Schwartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Schwartz), [Andrew Lloyd Webber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Lloyd_Webber), [Artie Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artie_Shaw), [Trent Reznor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent_Reznor), [Quincy Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quincy_Jones), [Herbie Hancock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock), [Jon Batiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Batiste), and [Jonny Greenwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonny_Greenwood).
