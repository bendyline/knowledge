# Killed in action

For the symbol used to represent losses, see [Dagger (mark)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagger_\(mark\)).

"K.I.A." redirects here. For other uses of this initialism, see [KIA (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KIA_\(disambiguation\)).



The [Normandy American Cemetery and Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy_American_Cemetery_and_Memorial), near [Colleville-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleville-sur-Mer), [Normandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normandy_\(administrative_region\)), [France](5843419.md)

**&#x20;Killed in action** (**KIA**) is a [casualty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casualty_\(person\)) classification generally used by [militaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militaries) to describe the deaths of their personnel at the hands of enemy or hostile forces at the moment of action.[^cite_note-1] The [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md), for example, says that those declared KIA did not need to have fired their weapons, but only to have been killed due to a hostile attack. KIAs include those killed by [friendly fire](202484.md) during combat, but not from incidents such as accidental vehicle crashes, murder, or other non-hostile events or [terrorism](30636.md).[^cite_note-2] KIA can be applied both to front-line combat troops and naval, air, and support forces.

## Died of wounds

Furthermore, the term&#x20;**&#x20;died of wounds** (**DOW**) is used to denote personnel who reached a medical treatment facility before dying.[^cite_note-3] The category *died of wounds received in action* (*DWRIA*) is also used for combat-related casualties which occur after medical evacuation.[^cite_note-4]

## Missing, presumed dead

**PKIA** means **presumed killed in action**. This term is used when personnel are lost in battle, initially listed [missing in action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missing_in_action) (MIA), but after not being found, are later presumed to have not survived.[^cite_note-5] This is typical of naval battles or engagements in other hostile environments where recovering bodies is difficult. A vast number of soldiers killed in action went unidentified in [World War I](4764461.md), like [John Kipling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kipling), the son of British poet [Rudyard Kipling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudyard_Kipling), prompting the formation of the [Commonwealth War Graves Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_War_Graves_Commission).[^cite_note-John-6]

## NATO definition

[NATO](21133.md) defines killed in action or a battle casualty as a combatant who is killed outright or who dies as a result of wounds or other injuries before reaching a medical treatment facility or help from comrades.[^cite_note-7]
