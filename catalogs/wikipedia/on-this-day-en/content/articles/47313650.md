**Christopher David Bell** (born December 16, 1994) is an American professional [stock car racing](28349.md) driver. He competes full-time in the [NASCAR Cup Series](56571945.md), driving the No. 20 [Toyota Camry XSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Camry_\(XV80\)) for [Joe Gibbs Racing](2410190.md), part-time in the [NASCAR O'Reilly Auto Parts Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_O'Reilly_Auto_Parts_Series), driving the No. 19 [Toyota GR Supra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_GR_Supra) for JGR, and part-time in the [NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series), driving the No. 62 [Toyota Tundra TRD Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Tundra#Third_generation_\(XK70;_2021\)) for [Halmar Friesen Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halmar_Friesen_Racing). He is the [2017 NASCAR Camping World Truck Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_NASCAR_Camping_World_Truck_Series) Champion.

## Racing career

### Early career and dirt track racing



Bell's USAC midget at [Angell Park Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angell_Park_Speedway) in 2013

Bell began his racing career in micro sprints at I-44 Riverside Speedway in the early 2000s. He earned the Lucas Oil ASCS2 Championship in 2010 before winning the 66 Mike Phillips Memorial micro-[sprint car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprint_car_racing) race in 2011. The following year, he finished second in the Short Track Nationals at I-30 Speedway, a race sanctioned by the American Sprint Car Series.[^cite_note-1] In 2013, he joined Keith Kunz Motorsports in [USAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Auto_Club) racing, replacing [Kyle Larson](36163081.md).[^cite_note-2] On October 31, 2013, he joined CH Motorsports' [sprint car racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprint_car_racing) program.[^cite_note-3] Bell concluded 2013 as the USAC National Midget Champion. In 2014, he began racing asphalt Super Late Models for [Kyle Busch Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Busch_Motorsports),[^cite_note-Truck_debut-4] including competing in the [World of Outlaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_of_Outlaws) Sprint Car Series. On May 7, 2014, he won his first career WoO Sprint Car Series race at [Jacksonville Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville_Speedway).[^cite_note-5] As a Super Late Model driver, he won races at [New Smyrna Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Smyrna_Speedway), South Alabama Speedway and [Southern National Motorsports Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_National_Motorsports_Park).[^cite_note-Truck_debut-4] During the year, he won 24 [USAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Auto_Club) Midget races[^cite_note-6] and 26 total dirt races, including the [Turkey Night Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_Night_Grand_Prix).[^cite_note-Truck_Series_debut-7]



Bell inside his [Gateway Dirt Nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateway_Dirt_Nationals?action=edit\&redlink=1) car in 2018

In 2017, Bell won the [Chili Bowl Nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_Bowl_\(race\)), becoming the first Oklahoman to win the event since Andy Hillenburg in 1994.[^cite_note-8] Bell followed the 2017 Chili Bowl win with his third consecutive Turnpike Challenge victory, the most recent win coming at his hometown track I-44 Riverside Speedway in Oklahoma City. He finished the 2017 season with a win in the Tulsa Shootout's Winged Outlaw Division.[^cite_note-9]

Bell won the Chili Bowl again in 2018 and 2019 to become only the third three-time winner of the race.

During 2023 and most of 2024, Bell was reportedly restricted from dirt racing by Joe Gibbs Racing due to concerns about injury risk, with the team enforcing a ban that kept him sidelined from marquee events such as the Chili Bowl and other national midget and sprint car races.[^cite_note-ban-10]

Bell made his return to dirt competition in late 2024 after JGR officially lifted the ban that had kept him sidelined for nearly two years. He marked his comeback with a win at the Tulsa Shootout in the Non-Wing Outlaw division, his first since 2018, and followed that with a victory during the [World of Outlaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_of_Outlaws)' season-opening DIRTcar Nationals at [Volusia Speedway Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volusia_Speedway_Park).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

### NASCAR

#### 2015–2017: K\&N Pro Series West and Truck Series

In 2015, Bell made two [NASCAR K\&N Pro Series West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARCA_Menards_Series_West) starts at [Irwindale Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irwindale_Speedway) and [Iowa Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Speedway), driving the No. 54 for [Bill McAnally Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_McAnally_Racing); he finished 15th and second, respectively.[^cite_note-13] In June, Bell returned to Iowa to make his [Camping World Truck Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series) debut for KBM in the [American Ethanol 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Ethanol_200).[^cite_note-Truck_debut-4] Bell finished second to KBM teammate [Erik Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Jones) in the race's practice session, and finished fifth in the race.[^cite_note-Truck_Series_debut-7] On July 8, 2015, Bell was announced as [Justin Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Boston)'s replacement in the No. 54 at [Kentucky Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Speedway).[^cite_note-14] Bell won his first career Truck race in the [Mudsummer Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudsummer_Classic) at [Eldora Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eldora_Speedway) after holding off [Bobby Pierce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Pierce_\(racing_driver\)) on the [green–white–checker finish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green%E2%80%93white%E2%80%93checker_finish).[^cite_note-Motorsport-CBellTRDOnlyBeginning-2015-15][^cite_note-16]

On October 29, 2015, KBM announced that Bell would compete full-time in the Truck Series in 2016.[^cite_note-17] His season started on a terrifying note at Daytona, when on the final lap, [Brandon Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Brown_\(racing_driver\)) pushed [Timothy Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Peters) into him causing his truck to spin. His truck gripped the track, causing it to go on two wheels before it launched into the air and barrel-rolled multiple times.[^cite_note-18] The following week at [Atlanta Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_Motor_Speedway), Bell was leading the race on his way to win, when he blew a tire and crashed into the wall, ending his day. He would have one lone win in 2016 at Gateway. He would finish third in the final standings.[^cite_note-19]



Bell's No. 18 Xfinity car at [Road America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_America) in 2017



Bell's No. 4 truck at [Martinsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinsville_Speedway) in April 2017

In 2017, Bell won the second race of the season at Atlanta after winning the first two stages and passing [Matt Crafton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Crafton) for the lead with sixteen laps remaining.[^cite_note-20] In May, Bell made his [Pinty's Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Pinty's_Series) and [Xfinity Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Xfinity_Series) debuts; in the former, he drove the No. 22 for 22 Racing at the season-opening race at [Canadian Tire Motorsport Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Tire_Motorsport_Park).[^cite_note-21] In the latter, he joined the No. 18 of [Joe Gibbs Racing](2410190.md) at [Charlotte Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Motor_Speedway) as part of a seven-race schedule. He also drove the No. 20 at Iowa and [Richmond International Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_International_Raceway) before returning to the No. 18 to drive at [Kansas Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Speedway), [Texas Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Motor_Speedway), [Phoenix International Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_International_Raceway) and [Homestead-Miami Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead-Miami_Speedway).[^cite_note-22] At Mosport, he was the lone American in the field as he finished 23rd after suffering a brake failure.[^cite_note-23]

In his Xfinity debut, Bell got an impressive fourth-place finish despite being spun out early in the race by [Ryan Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Reed). During his second start at Iowa, Bell won the pole and looked on track for his first career win. Unfortunately, he was caught up in a multi-car crash late when he got turned while leading by the lap car of [Ross Chastain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Chastain) following a crash between the lapped cars of [Ryan Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Reed) and [Brennan Poole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brennan_Poole), which collected Bell and Chastain. The previous night, in the Truck race, he was also on track for a win but dropped back late and finished fifth. At Kentucky, Bell recovered from an early spin and outraced [John Hunter Nemechek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hunter_Nemechek) and [Brandon Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Jones_\(racing_driver\)) for his third truck win of the season and fifth of his career. Bell won his first Xfinity race at his fifth start in Kansas after catching and passing [Erik Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Jones) in the final laps. On November 17, 2017, Bell won the 2017 Truck Series championship by finishing second to his friend [Chase Briscoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Briscoe) and being the best finisher among the Championship 4 of Bell, Crafton, [Johnny Sauter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Sauter), and [Austin Cindric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Cindric). Bell ended his Truck Series season with five wins, fifteen top-fives, and 21 top-tens to win the championship.

#### 2018–2019: Xfinity Series



Bell during driver intros at Road America in 2018



Bell doing burnouts after winning the Xfinity race at [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover_International_Speedway) in October 2018

In 2018, Bell moved to the Xfinity Series full-time to drive the No. 20 car for Joe Gibbs Racing. Bell won his first race of the season at Richmond in April, holding off teammate [Noah Gragson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Gragson).[^cite_note-24] In July, Bell won three consecutive races at Kentucky, New Hampshire, and Iowa, becoming the first series regular since [Dale Earnhardt Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Earnhardt_Jr.) in 1999 to win three consecutive races.[^cite_note-25] Bell entered the Xfinity Series playoffs by winning at Richmond in September for the season sweep of the track. With this win, Bell would advance to the next round of the playoffs.[^cite_note-26] Bell won his sixth race of the season at Dover in October, setting a rookie win record in the Xfinity Series.[^cite_note-27] Bell won at the [ISM Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISM_Raceway) on November 10, 2018, advancing to the Championship 4 after being in a must-win situation after poor finishes in the two prior races at Kansas and Texas. Bell finished eleventh at Homestead and fourth in points.

Bell returned for his second full-time season in Xfinity competition in 2019. He started with a sixth at Daytona, and the following week won at Atlanta in dominating fashion. The win at Atlanta was the first win for the Toyota Supra since the Camry was the model used when Toyota joined both the Xfinity and Cup Series in 2007. Bell won his second race of the season at Bristol in April, where he also won the \$100,000 [Dash 4 Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dash_4_Cash) bonus. Bell won once again at Dover, winning his second Dash 4 Cash bonus. Bell won his fourth race of the season at Iowa in June.



Bell's No. 95 Toyota at [Daytona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytona_International_Speedway) in 2020

On June 28, 2019, Bell signed a contract extension with JGR through 2020.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] In the following day's [Camping World 300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Camping_World_300) at Chicagoland, Bell finished third but was disqualified after failing post-race inspection, relegating him to 38th.[^cite_note-30] Bell won races at New Hampshire in July and Road America in August, the latter of which was his first road course win in the Xfinity Series. In September, Bell won the first race of the playoffs at Richmond, advancing to the next round. In November, Bell would win at [Texas Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Motor_Speedway) and would punch his ticket into the championship round at [Homestead-Miami Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead-Miami_Speedway) for the second year in a row.[^cite_note-31] He finished the 2019 season third in points after finishing fifth at [Homestead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Ford_EcoBoost_300).[^cite_note-32]

#### 2020: Cup Series debut

On September 24, 2019, [Leavine Family Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leavine_Family_Racing) announced that Bell would drive the No. 95 Toyota in the Cup Series starting in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_NASCAR_Cup_Series). In addition, his Xfinity crew chief [Jason Ratcliff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Ratcliff) joined him in the team.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] In LFR's final season, Bell scored two top-fives and seven top-tens. He finished twentieth in points and earned a drive in the No. 20 at Joe Gibbs Racing for 2021.[^cite_note-35]

#### 2021: Joe Gibbs Racing



Bell's No. 20 Toyota at [Sonoma Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonoma_Raceway) in 2021

In his second race in JGR's No. 20, the [2021 O'Reilly Auto Parts 253](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_O'Reilly_Auto_Parts_253) on Daytona's road course, he passed [Joey Logano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_Logano) with two laps remaining to score his first career Cup victory. He became the first driver from [Oklahoma](22489.md) to win a Cup Series race, the 35th driver to win a NASCAR race in all three main series, and the one-hundredth winner in NASCAR's modern era.[^cite_note-36] In July, he returned to the Xfinity Series at New Hampshire, where he led 151 of 200 laps, including the final 146 to win.[^cite_note-37] During the playoffs, Bell made it to the Round of 12, but struggled with a poor finish at [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_Point_400), yet he rebounded with a fifth-place finish at [Talladega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_YellaWood_500). Following the [Charlotte Roval race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Bank_of_America_Roval_400), he was eliminated from the Round of 12.[^cite_note-2021Roundof8-38] He finished the season twelfth in the points standings.[^cite_note-2021_Cup-39]

#### 2022–2023: Championship Four



Bell's No. 20 car at [Sonoma Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonoma_Raceway) in 2022

Bell started the [2022 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NASCAR_Cup_Series) with a 34th-place finish at the [Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Daytona_500). At [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Folds_of_Honor_QuikTrip_500), he accidentally struck Jackman, David O'Dell, during his pit stop. Bell crossed the finish line in second place, but he was penalized to a 23rd-place finish for passing below the line on the final lap.[^cite_note-40] Bell scored a win at [New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ambetter_301) to become the 14th different winner of the season.[^cite_note-41] During the playoffs, Bell won at the [Charlotte Roval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Bank_of_America_Roval_400) to advance to the Round of 8 after being in a must-win situation.[^cite_note-Bell-42] At [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_South_Point_400), Bell got caught between a wreck involving [Kyle Larson](36163081.md) and [Bubba Wallace](35761776.md), resulting in a 34th-place finish and being well below the playoff cutline.[^cite_note-43] Bell won at [Martinsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Xfinity_500) to make the Championship 4 after being in another must-win situation.[^cite_note-44] He finished tenth at the [Phoenix finale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NASCAR_Cup_Series_Championship_Race) and a career-best third in the points standings.[^cite_note-45]

Bell began the [2023 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NASCAR_Cup_Series) with a third-place finish at the [2023 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Daytona_500). He scored his first win of the season at the [Bristol dirt race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Food_City_Dirt_Race).[^cite_note-46] During the playoffs, Bell won at [Homestead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_4EVER_400) to make the Championship 4.[^cite_note-47] At [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NASCAR_Cup_Series_Championship_Race), Bell suffered a 36th place DNF and was relegated to a fourth place finish in the points standings after his right-front brake exploded, sending him to the third turn wall.[^cite_note-48]

#### 2024: Late-season controversy



Bell’s No. 20 car at [Richmond Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_Raceway) in 2024

Bell started the [2024 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NASCAR_Cup_Series) with another third-place finish at the [2024 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Daytona_500). During the season, he won at [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Shriners_Children's_500), [Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Coca-Cola_600), and [New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_USA_Today_301).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] At [Martinsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Xfinity_500) during the playoffs, Bell ran against the outside wall in a move similar to [Ross Chastain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Chastain)'s "[Hail Melon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Xfinity_500)" to finish eighteenth, but NASCAR, which had banned the move in 2023, penalized him to 22nd place, the last car one lap down. Instead of being tied with [William Byron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Byron_\(racing_driver\)) in playoff points because of his placement, it resulted in him being eliminated from the playoffs.[^cite_note-52]

During the [Xfinity season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NASCAR_Xfinity_Series), Bell drove the JGR No. 20 to wins at [New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_SciAps_200) and [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Sport_Clips_Haircuts_VFW_200).[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

#### 2025: Early dominance and All-Star win



Bell's No. 20 car at [Las Vegas Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Motor_Speedway) in 2025

Cup Series

Bell started the [2025 season](77079684.md) with a 31st-place DNF at the [2025 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Daytona_500), but rebounded with three consecutive victories. A week later, he won at [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Ambetter_Health_400) in a close, controversial finish. Bell was ahead of [Carson Hocevar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson_Hocevar) and [Kyle Larson](36163081.md) at the time of caution coming to the checkered flag during an overtime restart.[^cite_note-55] The next week at [Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_EchoPark_Automotive_Grand_Prix), Bell won back-to-back Cup Series races for the first time in his career, using a slight tire advantage to pass [Kyle Busch](1467805.md) and then hold off [William Byron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Byron_\(racing_driver\)) to win the race.[^cite_note-56] A week later at [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Shriners_Children's_500), Bell became the first driver to win 3 races in a row in the [Next-Gen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Gen_\(NASCAR\)) era, leading 105 laps en-route to claiming the checkered flag during an overtime finish ahead of teammate [Denny Hamlin](2148196.md).[^cite_note-57] In May at [North Wilkesboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NASCAR_All-Star_Race), Bell started the [All-Star Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_All-Star_Race) from 2nd place after winning his heat race Saturday evening. Bell took on two right-side sticker tires during the promoter's caution late in the race, and used his tire advantage to pass [Joey Logano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_Logano) for the win.[^cite_note-58] Just days after his All-Star win, it was announced that Bell and longtime spotter [Stevie Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevie_Reeves) would part ways in a surprising move. Bell stated it was entirely Reeves' decision to quit the role and admitted he was caught off guard by the change. Reeves was replaced by Matt Philpott from [Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Coca-Cola_600) through [Pocono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_The_Great_American_Getaway_400).[^cite_note-59] From the Summer [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Quaker_State_400) race onward, Bell's spotter duties were taken over by Tab Boyd, who had previously worked with [Ricky Stenhouse Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Stenhouse_Jr.). Boyd became available after being released by [Hyak Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyak_Motorsports) in the wake of comments he made during the [Mexico City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Viva_M%C3%A9xico_250) race weekend.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

During the playoffs, Bell won [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Bass_Pro_Shops_Night_Race), passing [Carson Hocevar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson_Hocevar) and [Zane Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zane_Smith) on a late restart, before ultimately holding off a last-lap "bump and run" attempt from [Brad Keselowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Keselowski) to secure the win.[^cite_note-62] Bell entered the [Martinsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Xfinity_500) cutoff race third in the playoff standings, 37 points above the cutoff line, but only one point ahead of [Kyle Larson](36163081.md), who sat fourth in the standings. With [William Byron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Byron_\(racing_driver\)) winning the race from below the cutoff line and Larson earning eight more points than Bell on the day, he was eliminated during the Round of 8.[^cite_note-63] Bell ended the season 5th in the standings for the second consecutive year, setting career highs in wins and average finish, while marking his fourth straight season finishing in the top-five of the championship standings.

Xfinity and Truck Series

During the [Xfinity season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NASCAR_Xfinity_Series), Bell qualified the JGR No. 19 on pole for the [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Sport_Clips_Haircuts_VFW_200) race. He ran inside the top-ten for most of the day but finished 25th after sustaining damage in a restart incident with [Ross Chastain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Chastain) during Stage 3.[^cite_note-64] In [Mexico City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_The_Chilango_150), Bell drove the No. 24 for fellow Toyota team [Sam Hunt Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Hunt_Racing). He qualified third but suffered an engine failure while holding that position late in Stage 2, resulting in a 39th-place finish.[^cite_note-65]

Bell had not been scheduled to compete in the [2025 NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series); however, following injuries sustained by fellow dirt racer [Stewart Friesen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Friesen), he was tapped by [Halmar Friesen Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halmar_Friesen_Racing) to drive the No. 52 truck at [Watkins Glen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Mission_176_at_The_Glen). Bell led 30 laps and finished fourth.[^cite_note-66]

#### 2026



Bell's No. 20 car at [Las Vegas Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Motor_Speedway) in 2026

Cup Series

Bell started the [2026 season](79747972.md) with a 35th-place DNF at the [2026 Daytona 500](81427567.md). The rest of his regular season was defined by exceptional speed undermined by persistent misfortune. At [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Straight_Talk_Wireless_500) in March, Bell led a race-high 176 of 312 laps and held a lead of over three seconds before a late caution with 25 laps to go triggered a final pit cycle that dropped him outside the top five for the restart; he recovered to finish second to [Ryan Blaney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Blaney).[^cite_note-67] A similar scenario unfolded at [Nashville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Cracker_Barrel_400) in May, where a late caution with 13 laps remaining set up a four-lap dash that allowed teammate [Denny Hamlin](2148196.md) to pass him for the win; Bell finished second by 0.115 seconds.[^cite_note-68] Bell was also collected in accidents not of his making on four occasions while running inside the top five, at [Atlanta I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Autotrader_400) in February,[^cite_note-69] [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_AdventHealth_400) in April,[^cite_note-70] [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_W%C3%BCrth_400) in May,[^cite_note-71] and [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FireKeepers_Casino_400) in June.[^cite_note-ABC-72]

The Michigan incident was the most consequential, with Bell sustaining the hardest ever impact seen in the [Gen-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Gen_\(NASCAR\)) era.[^cite_note-73] While running second on Lap 148, [Chase Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_Elliott) got loose underneath Bell entering Turn 3 and made contact, sending the No. 20 into the outside wall at nearly 200 mph at a near 45-degree angle. The impact deformed the [SAFER barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAFER_barrier) and forced a 20-minute red flag for repairs.[^cite_note-ABC-72] Though Bell was evaluated and released from the infield care center, a couple of days later it was confirmed that Bell fractured his left wrist but was cleared to race at the next race in [Pocono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_The_Great_American_Getaway_400).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] Despite still being cleared to race the following week at the inaugural race at the [Coronado Street Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Anduril_250), the tight, wall-lined nature of the street course led him and crew chief [Adam Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Stevens) to plan an early exit; Bell handed the No. 20 to [O'Reilly Auto Parts Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Reilly_Auto_Parts_Series) driver [Brent Crews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brent_Crews) at the first caution, with Crews driving the remainder in relief. While wearing an arm brace, he finished fifth at [Sonoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Toyota/Save_Mart_350) and second four times. After earning seven runner-up finishes, Bell finally won the [Southern 500](83307953.md).

O'Reilly and Truck Series

During the [O'Reilly Auto Parts Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_NASCAR_O'Reilly_Auto_Parts_Series), Bell made [a start at Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Sport_Clips_Haircuts_VFW_200), where he finished third.

In the [Truck Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series), Bell continued his collaboration with [Halmar Friesen Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halmar_Friesen_Racing) from the previous year, running five events in the team's second full-time No. 62 truck: [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Buckle_Up_South_Carolina_200), [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Tennessee_Army_National_Guard_250), [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Ecosave_200), [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_DQS_Solutions_&_Staffing_250) and [North Wilkesboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FaithFest_250). Bell won the race at Bristol, his first win in the series since 2017.[^cite_note-76] Bell was supposed to run the [race at Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Black's_Tire_250) but would be replaced by [Leland Honeyman Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leland_Honeyman_Jr.)[^cite_note-77]

## CB Industries

On September 19, 2019, Bell in conjunction with fellow racer [Chad Boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_Boat) announced the formation of [CB Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CB_Industries?action=edit\&redlink=1), a [World of Outlaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_of_Outlaws) sprint car team fielding the No. 21 [Mobil 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobil_1) [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota) for Christopher Bell.[^cite_note-78] In the team's first-ever race on September 21, 2019, they won the [Jim Ford Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Ford_Classic?action=edit\&redlink=1).

On August 13, 2019, the team won with Bell in their first-ever start in the [World of Outlaws NOS Energy Drink Sprint Car Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_of_Outlaws_NOS_Energy_Drink_Sprint_Car_Series) at [Tri-State Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tri-State_Speedway_\(Indiana\)).
