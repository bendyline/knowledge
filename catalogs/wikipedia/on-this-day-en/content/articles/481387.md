# Resignation

For other uses, see [Resignation (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resignation_\(disambiguation\)).

**Resignation** is the formal act of relinquishing or vacating one's office or position. A resignation can occur when a person holding a position gained by [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election) or appointment steps down, but leaving a position upon the expiration of a term, or choosing not to seek an additional term, is not considered resignation.

When an employee chooses to leave a position, it is considered a resignation, as opposed to [involuntary termination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termination_of_employment). Whether an employee resigned or was terminated is sometimes a topic of dispute, because in many situations, a terminated employee is eligible for [severance pay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severance_package) and/or [unemployment benefits](378430.md), whereas one who voluntarily resigns may not be eligible. [Abdication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdication) is the equivalent of resignation for a reigning [monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch), [pope](23056.md), or holder of another similar position.

## Political examples



President Nixon's [last farewell gesture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V_sign) after [his resignation in 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon's_resignation_speech).

A resignation is a personal decision to exit a position, though outside pressure exists in many cases. For example, [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) resigned from the office of [President of the United States](24113.md) in August 1974 following the [Watergate scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watergate_scandal), when he was almost certain to have been [impeached](15328.md) by the [United States Congress](31756.md).[^cite_note-1]

### Deliberate manoeuvre

Resignation can be used as a political manoeuvre, as in the [Philippines](23440.md) in July 2005, when ten [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_\(government\)) officials resigned en masse to pressure [President](271126.md) [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo) to follow suit over [allegations of electoral fraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hello_Garci_scandal). Arroyo's predecessor, [Joseph Estrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Estrada), was successfully forced out of office during the [EDSA Revolution of 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_Revolution_of_2001) as he faced the first [impeachment](15328.md) trial held in the country's history.

In 1995, the [British Prime Minister](24150.md), [John Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Major), resigned as Leader of the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) in order to contest [a leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Conservative_Party_leadership_election) with the aim of silencing his critics within the party and reasserting his authority. Having resigned, he stood again and was re-elected. He continued to serve as prime minister until he was defeated in the [1997 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_United_Kingdom_general_election). Similarly, and also in the UK, [Nigel Farage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Farage) announced on 7 July 2026 that he was resigning as an MP to trigger a [by-election in Clacton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Clacton_by-election), [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex), and that he intended to stand for re-election.[^cite_note-2]

However, ascertaining whether an employee had an intent to resign depends on all the circumstances. As noted by the [Ontario Superior Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Superior_Court_of_Justice), an employee's storming off may not legally be a resignation.[^cite_note-3]

### When criticised

Although government officials may tender their resignations, they are not always accepted. This could be a gesture of confidence in the official, as with [US President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_President) [George W. Bush](3414021.md)'s refusal of his [Secretary of Defense](43998.md) [Donald Rumsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Rumsfeld)'s twice-offered resignation during the [Abu Ghraib prison abuse scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Ghraib_torture_and_prisoner_abuse).[^cite_note-4]

However, refusing a resignation can be a method of severe censure if it is followed by dismissal; [Alberto Fujimori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Fujimori) attempted to resign as [President of Peru](239109.md), but his resignation was refused so that [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Peru) could impeach him.[^cite_note-5]

## Delivery

For many public figures, primarily departing politicians, resignation is an opportunity to deliver a valedictory [resignation speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resignation_speech) in which they can elucidate the circumstances of their exit from office and in many cases deliver a powerful speech which often commands much attention. This can be used to great political effect, particularly as, subsequent to resigning, government ministers are no longer bound by [collective responsibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_responsibility) and can speak with greater freedom about current issues.

"Spending more time with family" is a common reason cited during public resignations,[^cite_note-Daly1996-6][^cite_note-FriedmanGreenhaus2000-7] especially as a euphemism when receding from scandal.[^cite_note-Smith2014-8]

## Other organisations

In [academia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_institution), a university president or the editor of a scientific journal may also resign, particularly in cases where an idea which runs counter to the mainstream is being promoted. In 2006, Harvard president [Larry Summers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Summers) resigned after making the provocative suggestion that the underrepresentation of female academics in math and science[^cite_note-9] could be due to factors other than sheer discrimination, such as personal inclination or innate ability.

In a club, society, or other [voluntary association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voluntary_association), a member may resign from an officer position in that organization or even from the organization itself. In [Robert's Rules of Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert's_Rules_of_Order), this is called a [request to be excused from a duty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Request_to_be_excused_from_a_duty).[^cite_note-10] A resignation may also be withdrawn.[^cite_note-11]

The [Catholic Church](606848.md) introduced provision at the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council) (1963-1965) for its bishops to resign if they "become less capable of fulfilling their duties properly because of the increasing burden of age or some other serious reason".[^cite_note-12]
