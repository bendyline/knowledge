**Newry** ([/ˈnjʊəri/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-Dictionary-4] from [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language) *An Iúraigh*[^cite_note-Dunbar-5]) is a [cathedral city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_the_United_Kingdom)[^cite_note-6] in [Northern Ireland](21265.md), standing on the [Clanrye River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newry_River) in counties [Armagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Armagh) and [Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Down). It is near [the border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland%E2%80%93United_Kingdom_border) with the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md), on the main routes between [Belfast](5046.md) (34 miles/55 km away) and [Dublin](8504.md) (67 miles/108 km away). The population was 27,913 in 2021.[^cite_note-Census_2021-3]

Newry was founded in 1144 as a [settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastic_settlement) around a [Cistercian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistercian) abbey.[^cite_note-Brit-7][^cite_note-8] In the 16th century the English dissolved the [abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey) and built [Bagenal's Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagenal's_Castle) on the site. Newry grew as a [market town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_town) and a [garrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison), and became a port in 1742 when the [Newry Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newry_Canal) was opened, the first [summit-level canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summit-level_canal) in Ireland. It is the episcopal seat of the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Dromore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Dromore). In 2002, as part of the [Golden Jubilee of Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Jubilee_of_Elizabeth_II), Newry was granted [city status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_the_United_Kingdom#Northern_Ireland) along with [Lisburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisburn).[^cite_note-9]

## Name

The name Newry is an anglicization of *An Iúraigh*, an oblique form of *An Iúrach*, which means "the grove of [yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxaceae) trees".[^cite_note-pni-10][^cite_note-11]

The modern Irish name for Newry is *An tIúr* (pronounced [\[ənʲ ˈtʲuːɾˠ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Irish)), which means "the yew tree". *An tIúr* is a shortening of *Iúr Cinn Trá* (Irish: [\[ˌuːɾˠ ˌcɪn̠ʲ ˈt̪ˠɾˠaː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Irish), "yew tree at the head of the strand"), which was formerly the most common Irish name for Newry.[^cite_note-pni-10] This relates to an [apocryphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocryphal) story that [Saint Patrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Patrick) planted a yew tree there in the 5th century.

The Irish name *Cathair an Iúir* (City of Newry) appears on some bilingual signs around the city.[^cite_note-12]

## History

There is evidence of continual human habitation in the area from early times. During the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age), the Newry area had a community that made very detailed jewellery for garments in abundance. Three of these Newry Clasps can be found in the [Ulster Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Museum), and a massive arm clasp from the same period was also found in Newry.[^cite_note-13] Three miles south of Newry is [Clontygorra Court Cairn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_cairn) which has large portal stones at its entrance. Excavations revealed pot sherds, hollow scrapers, a polished axe and the cremated remains of one person. Nearby is another, smaller court cairn.[^cite_note-14]

In AD 820, [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings) landed in the Newry area, "from whence they proceeded to [Armagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armagh), taking it by storm, and plundering and desolating the country around".[^cite_note-15]

### Early history

A [Cistercian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistercian) abbey was founded at Newry in 1144; in 1157 it was granted a charter by [Muirchertach Mac Lochlainn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muirchertach_Mac_Lochlainn), king of [Tír Eoghain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%ADr_Eoghain) and [High King of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_King_of_Ireland).[^cite_note-Brit-7][^cite_note-Dawkes-16] It might have been a [Benedictine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine) monastery before this.[^cite_note-Dawkes-16] Newry Abbey (now the area around Newry Museum) would have been a sprawling complex of buildings and the heart of a [monastic settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastic_settlement).[^cite_note-Dawkes-16] It existed for four centuries. The [abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey) was [dissolved by the English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_monasteries) in 1548, when it was recorded to consist of a church, steeple, college, chapter house, dormitory, a hall, a graveyard, two orchards, and one garden.[^cite_note-Dawkes-16] Modern archaeologists unearthed thirty-three burials from part of the former graveyard, and further bones were found in charnel pits. They included remains of men, women, and several youths, and some of the individuals suffered violent deaths.[^cite_note-Dawkes-16] It is believed this was a graveyard for the [lay community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laity) from when the abbey was still in existence.[^cite_note-Dawkes-16]

In April 1552, [Nicholas Bagenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Bagenal), Marshal of the English army in Ireland,[^cite_note-17] was granted ownership of the former abbey lands.[^cite_note-Dawkes-16] He built a [fortified house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortified_houses_in_Ireland) known as [Bagenal's Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagenal's_Castle) on the site of the abbey and its graveyard, re-using some of the abbey buildings.[^cite_note-Dawkes-16] Bagenal also had an earthen rampart built around his Castle and the small town of Newry.[^cite_note-Dawkes-16]

During the [Irish Rebellion of 1641](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Rebellion_of_1641), Newry was captured by Irish Catholic rebels led by the [Magennises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magennis) and [McCartans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCartan).[^cite_note-18] In May 1642, a Scottish [Covenanter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenanter) army landed in Ulster and seized Newry from the rebels. [James Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Turner_\(soldier\)), one of the Scottish officers, recounted that Catholic rebels and civilians were taken to the bridge over the Newry River and "butchered to death ... some by shooting, some by hanging ... without any legal process".[^cite_note-19] The Scottish general, [Robert Monro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Monro), said that sixty townsmen and two priests were summarily executed.[^cite_note-20] Turner also said that Scottish soldiers drowned and shot about a dozen Irishwomen before he stopped them from killing more.[^cite_note-21]

During the 1689 [Raid on Newry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raid_on_Newry), [Williamite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamite) forces under [Toby Purcell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toby_Purcell) repulsed an attack by the [Jacobites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobitism) under the [Marquis de Boisseleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_de_Rainier_de_Droue,_Marquis_de_Boisseleau). During the period of the [Battle of the Boyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Boyne), the Duke of Berwick set fire to the parts of the town which he had restructured to defend it.

### Modern era

Further information: [The Troubles in Newry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles_in_Newry)

The [1841 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1841_census_of_Ireland) gives the combined population of the two halves of the city as 11,972 inhabitants.[^cite_note-22] By 1881 the population of Newry had reached 15,590.[^cite_note-planningni.gov.uk-23]

During the [Irish War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_War_of_Independence) there were several assassinations and ambushes in Newry. On 12 December 1920, British reinforcements travelling from Newry to Camlough were ambushed by the [Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Republican_Army_\(1919%E2%80%931922\)) (IRA), who opened fire and threw grenades from [MacNeill's Egyptian Arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacNeill's_Egyptian_Arch). Three IRA members were fatally wounded in the exchange of fire.[^cite_note-24]

When [Ireland was partitioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Ireland) in 1921, Newry became part of Northern Ireland. From the 1920s to the 1960s, Newry [Urban District Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_district_\(Ireland\)) was one of the few councils in Northern Ireland which had a majority of councillors from the Catholic/Irish nationalist community. The reason, according to [Michael Farrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Farrell_\(activist\)), was that this community formed such a large majority in the town, around 80% of the population, making it impossible to [gerrymander](12987.md). Also, an oddity was that for a time it was controlled by the [Irish Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Ireland\)), after the left wing of the [Northern Ireland Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Labour_Party) defected to them in the 1940s.[^cite_note-25]

Newry saw several [violent incidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles_in_Newry) during the conflict known as [the Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles), including [a triple killing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Newry_killings) in 1971, [a bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newry_customs_bombing) in 1972, and a [mortar attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Newry_mortar_attack) in 1985. These continued into the late 1990s and even [in 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Newry_car_bombing) – such as [bomb scares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb_scare) and [car bombs](155282.md).

See also: [The Troubles in Killeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles_in_Killeen), for information on incidents at the border and customs post at Newry on the border with the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md) and close to Newry. In 2003, the [British Army](4887.md)'s hilltop watchtowers overlooking Newry were taken down. The British Army withdrew from the area on 25 June 2007 when they closed their final base at [Bessbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessbrook).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

## Geography

Newry lies in the most southeastern part of both [Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster) and [Northern Ireland](21265.md). About half of the city (the west) lies in [County Armagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Armagh) and the other half (the east) in [County Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Down). The [Clanrye River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newry_River), which runs through the city, from Carnmeen (north of the Carnbane Industrial Estate) until Middlebank (start of Warrenpoint Road), partly forms the historic border between County Armagh and County Down.

The city sits in a [valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gap_of_the_North), between the [Mourne Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mourne_Mountains) to the east and the [Ring of Gullion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_of_Gullion) to the south-west, both of which are designated [Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_of_Outstanding_Natural_Beauty). The [Cooley Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooley_Mountains) lie to the southeast. The Clanrye River runs through the centre of town, parallel to the [Newry Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newry_Canal). The city also lies at the northernmost end of [Carlingford Lough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlingford_Lough), where the canal enters the sea at Victoria Locks.

## Economy

Newry has traditionally been considered a merchant's town,[^cite_note-quinn20081218-46] and has maintained a reputation as one of the best provincial shopping-towns in [Northern Ireland](21265.md), with the Buttercrane Centre and [The Quays Newry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Quays_Newry) attracting large numbers of shoppers from as far away as [Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cork_\(city\)).[^cite_note-47]

In 2006 Newry house prices grew the most across the whole United Kingdom over the previous decade, as prices in the city had increased by 371% since 1996.[^cite_note-48] The city itself has become markedly more prosperous in recent years. Unemployment has reduced from over 26% in 1991 to scarcely 2% in 2008.[^cite_note-49]

Since the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis), residents of the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md) have increasingly been [cross-border shopping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-border_shopping) to Newry to buy cheaper goods due to the difference in currency. The harsh budget in the Republic of Ireland in October 2008, and the growing strength of the [euro](9472.md) against the [pound sterling](270673.md) and [VAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_added_tax) reductions in the United Kingdom, compared with increases in the Republic of Ireland, are among the reasons. This remarkable increase in cross-border trade has become so widespread that it has lent its name to a general phenomenon known as **the Newry effect**. In December 2008, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* described Newry as "the hottest shopping spot within the European Union's open borders, a place where consumers armed with euros enjoy a currency discount averaging 30 percent or more".[^cite_note-quinn20081218-46]

However the increased flow of trade has led to resultant tailbacks, sometimes several miles long, on approach roads from the south. This has created huge traffic and parking problems in Newry and the surrounding area. It has also become a political issue, with some politicians in the Republic of Ireland claiming that such cross-border shopping is "unpatriotic".[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-quinn20081218-46]

Newry is the global [HQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headquarters) of First Derivatives Plc.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

## Transport

- The [Newry Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newry_Canal) opened in 1742. It is the oldest Canal in Ireland or Britain and when functioning as an inland transport waterway, it ran for 18 miles (29 kilometres) to [Lough Neagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lough_Neagh). In 1777, Newry was ranked the fourth largest port in Ireland.

  [^cite_note-55]

  &#x20;Some surviving 18th and 19th century warehouses still line the canal, and now many houses, shops and restaurants.

- In 1885 an [electric tramway was opened between Newry and Bessbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessbrook_and_Newry_Tramway).

- [MacNeill's Egyptian Arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacNeill's_Egyptian_Arch) is a railway bridge located near Newry. It was selected for the design of the [British one pound coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_one_pound_coin) to represent Northern Ireland for 2006.

- Newry is served by an [Ulsterbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsterbus) bus station in the city centre. It is situated along The Mall, suspended over the [Clanrye River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clanrye_River). Services in Newry include local, regional and cross-border transport, with a free shuttle bus service to the local train station and services to local schools around Newry and Mourne.

- [Newry railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newry_railway_station), rebuilt in 2009, offers cross-border services on the [Dublin-Belfast line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin-Belfast_railway_line) as well as some regional services around areas of County Armagh and County Down. Transport to other places generally requires a change in either Belfast or Dublin.

- Newry is on the main M1/A1 route from [Dublin](8504.md) to [Belfast](5046.md). Originally the route passed through the town centre, but in the 60s was bypassed by the Abbey Link. This remained the sole relief road until 1996

  [^cite_note-56]

  &#x20;when it was superseded by a single carriageway bypass round the western side of the town. By 2008 the road on either side of the town had been upgraded to motorway/high quality dual carriageway standard (southwards from Cloghogue) and low quality dual carriageway (northwards from Beechill). In July 2010 a new high quality dual carriageway with motorway characteristics was opened to bridge the gap, thus connecting Dublin with Belfast by motorway/dual carriageway for the first time. The opening of this section of road meant that motorists could travel from [Clogh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clogh,_County_Antrim) in County Antrim to [Midleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midleton), County Cork by dual carriageway/motorway. Part of this older bypass is still in use between the Camlough Road ([A25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A25_road_\(Ireland\))) and the Belfast Road ([A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_road_\(Northern_Ireland\))). Newry suffers from very [heavy traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion) with shoppers coming from across the border.

  [^cite_note-57]

  &#x20;Newry is connected with [Warrenpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrenpoint) by a lower quality dual carriageway, some seven miles (eleven kilometres) to the south.

- Newry is linked to Belfast via [National Cycle Route 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cycle_Route_9), via [Portadown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portadown), [Lisburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisburn) and [Craigavon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craigavon,_County_Armagh).

  [^cite_note-58]

  &#x20;The route is planned to eventually extend to Dublin.

## Notable buildings



[Saint Patrick's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Patrick's,_Newry) was built in 1578 on the instructions of Nicholas Bagenal, who was granted the monastery lands by Edward VI, and is considered to be the first Protestant church in Ireland.



Catholic Cathedral of SS. Patrick and Colman, Newry

The Cathedral of SS Patrick and Colman on Hill Street was built in 1829 at a cost of £8,000. The structure, which consists of local granite, was designed and built by [Thomas Duff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Duff_\(architect\)), arguably Newry's greatest architect to date.[^cite_note-59]

Incidentally, Thomas Duff also was the architect for the Cathedral in Dundalk, a town just over the border in [County Louth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Louth), and it is said that he mixed up the plans for both cathedrals and sent [Dundalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dundalk) Cathedral to the builders in Newry, and Newry Cathedral to the builders in Dundalk.

[Newry Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newry_Town_Hall) is notable for being built over the [River Clanrye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Clanrye) which is the historic boundary between the counties of [Armagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Armagh) and [Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Down).[^cite_note-60]

The [Craigmore Viaduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craigmore_Viaduct?action=edit\&redlink=1) lies just north of the city on the [Northern Ireland Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Railways) Belfast-Dublin mainline. The bridge was designed by [Sir John MacNeill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Benjamin_Macneill) with construction beginning in 1849. The bridge was formally opened in 1852. The viaduct consists of eighteen arches the highest being 126 feet, the highest viaduct in Ireland. It is around one-quarter mile (400 metres) long and was constructed from local granite. The [Enterprise train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_train?action=edit\&redlink=1) link from Belfast to [Dublin](8504.md) crosses the bridge.

### Rugby Union

[Newry RFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newry_RFC) (also known as Newry Rugby Club, Newry RFU or Newry) is an Irish amateur rugby union club, founded in 1925. The club is a member of the Irish Rugby Football Union's Ulster branch. The club currently fields three senior teams and several junior teams ranging from under-12 to under-18 and a women's team for the first time in 2010–2011 season. The club's home ground is known as Telford Park. The team currently has two playing fields located at this ground along with the clubhouse on the outskirts of Newry.
