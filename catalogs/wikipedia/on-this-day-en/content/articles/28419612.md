The **19th century** began on 1 January 1801 and ended on 31 December 1900. It was the 9th century of the 2nd millennium. It was characterized by vast social upheaval. [Slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery) was [abolished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism) in much of [Europe](9239.md) and the [Americas](29833.md). The [First Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Industrial_Revolution), though it began in the late [18th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_century), expanded beyond its British homeland for the first time during the 19th century, particularly remaking the economies and societies of the [Low Countries](51402.md), [France](5843419.md), the [Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland), [Northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy), and the [Northeastern United States](431669.md). A few decades later, the [Second Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Industrial_Revolution) led to ever more massive [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization) and much higher levels of productivity, profit, and prosperity, a pattern that continued into the [20th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_century). The [Catholic Church](606848.md), in response to the growing influence and power of modernism, secularism and materialism, formed the [First Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Vatican_Council) in the late 19th century to deal with such problems and confirm certain Catholic doctrines as dogma. Religious missionaries were sent from the Americas and Europe to Asia, Africa and the Middle East.

In the [Middle East](19323.md), it was an era of change and reform. The [Islamic gunpowder empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_gunpowder_empires) fell into decline and European [imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialism) brought much of [South Asia](21566765.md), [Southeast Asia](28741.md), and almost all of [Africa](5334607.md) under [colonial rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_rule). Reformers were opposed at every turn by conservatives who strove to maintain the centuries-old Islamic laws and social order.[^cite_note-1] The 19th century also saw the collapse of the large [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire) and [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) empires, which paved the way for the growing influence of the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_colonial_empire), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Empire), and [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) empires along with the [United States](3434750.md).

The defeat of [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) in the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars) marked the end of France’s status as the world [superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower). Britain now claimed this status, and the British and Russian empires expanded considerably, becoming two of the world's leading powers. Russia expanded its territory to the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) and [Central Asia](6742.md). The [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) underwent a period of [Westernization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westernization) and reform known as the [Tanzimat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzimat), vastly increasing its control over core territories in the Middle East. However, it remained in decline and became known as the [sick man of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sick_man_of_Europe), losing territory in the [Balkans](4829.md) and [North Africa](21714.md).

The remaining powers in the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent), such as the [Maratha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) and [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire) empires, suffered a massive decline, and their dissatisfaction with the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company)'s rule led to the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857) and the company's dissolution. India was later ruled directly by the [British Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Crown) through the establishment of the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj). During the post-Napoleonic era (after 1815), Britain enforced what became known as the [Pax Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Britannica), which ushered in unprecedented [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization) on a massive scale. Britain's overseas possessions grew rapidly in the first half of the century, especially with the expansion of vast territories in Canada, Australia, India, and in the last two decades of the century in Africa. By the end of the 19th century, the British controlled a fifth of the world's land and a quarter of the world's population. After the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), France sought to rebuild its [colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_empire), having lost most of its major overseas possessions to the British in the 17th and 18th centuries, including [territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France) in [North America](21139.md) (modern-day [United States](3434750.md) and [Canada](5042916.md)), [French India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_India), and much of the [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Indies). French expansion resumed in the 19th century, beginning with the conquest of Algeria in 1830 and the establishment of influence in Africa. [French overseas possessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_French_possessions_and_colonies) expanded gradually in the second half of the century, and France significantly enlarged its colonial holdings, establishing French Indochina in Southeast Asia, French Polynesia, New Caledonia and the New Hebrides in Oceania and controlling over 40% of Africa. By the end of the 19th century, France possessed the [second largest colonial empire in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire) after the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire). It controlled over 13.5 million km<sup>2</sup> or 1/10 of the world’s land area, and exerted significant influence in East Asia through spheres of influence in China.

By the end of the century, Britain and France had colonized almost all of [South Asia](21566765.md) ([British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj)), [Southeast Asia](28741.md) ([French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina)), 80% of [Africa](5334607.md) and [Oceania](22621.md) ([Australia](4689264.md), [New Zealand](4913064.md), [French Polynesia](10737.md), [New Caledonia](21342.md), [New Hebrides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hebrides)). In [East Asia](19605700.md), China under the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) endured its [century of humiliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_of_humiliation) by foreign powers with Britain and France carving up spheres of influences in China that lasted until the first half of the 20th century. The last surviving man and woman, respectively, verified to have been born in the 19th century were [Jiroemon Kimura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiroemon_Kimura) (1897–2013) and [Nabi Tajima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabi_Tajima) (1900–2018), both Japanese.

## Overview



Official portrait of [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria), 1859

The first [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics) appeared in the 19th century, with the introduction of the [electric relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relay#History) in 1835, the [telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegraph) and its [Morse code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_code) protocol in 1837, the first telephone call in 1876,[^cite_note-2] and the first functional [light bulb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_bulb) in 1878.[^cite_note-3]

The 19th century was an era of rapidly accelerating [scientific discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_\(observation\)#In_science) and [invention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invention), with significant developments in the fields of mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, electricity, and metallurgy that laid the groundwork for the technological advances of the 20th century.[^cite_note-4] The [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) began in Great Britain and spread to continental Europe, North America, and Japan.[^cite_note-5] The [Victorian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era) was notorious for the employment of young children in factories and mines, as well as strict [social norms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_norm) regarding modesty and gender roles.[^cite_note-6] Japan embarked on a program of rapid modernization following the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration), before defeating China, under the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty), in the [First Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Sino-Japanese_War). [Advances in medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_medicine#19th_century:_rise_of_modern_medicine) and the understanding of human anatomy and disease prevention took place in the 19th century, and were partly responsible for rapidly accelerating [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) in the [Western world](21208200.md). Europe's population doubled during the 19th century, from approximately 200 million to more than 400 million.[^cite_note-7] The introduction of [railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport) provided the first major advancement in land transportation for centuries, changing the way people lived and obtained goods, and fuelling major [urbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization) movements in countries across the globe. Numerous cities worldwide surpassed populations of a million or more during this century. London became the world's [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_cities_throughout_history) and capital of the British Empire. Its population increased from 1 million in 1800 to 6.7 million a century later. The last remaining undiscovered landmasses of Earth, including vast expanses of interior [Africa](5334607.md) and [Asia](689.md), were [explored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration) during this century, and with the exception of the extreme zones of the Arctic and Antarctic, accurate and detailed maps of the globe were available by the 1890s. [Liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) became the pre-eminent [reform movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_movement) in Europe.[^cite_note-8]



[Arab slave traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_slave_trade) and their captives along the [Ruvuma River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruvuma_River), 19th century



Illustration of the [Battle of Konya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Konya) of the [Egyptian–Ottoman War (1831–1833)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian%E2%80%93Ottoman_War_\(1831%E2%80%931833\))

[Slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery) was greatly reduced around the world. Following a successful [slave revolt in Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Revolution), [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) and France stepped up the battle against the [Barbary pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_pirates) and succeeded in stopping their enslavement of Europeans. The UK's [Slavery Abolition Act 1833](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_Abolition_Act_1833) charged the British [Royal Navy](26061.md) with ending the global [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery).[^cite_note-9] The first colonial empire in the century to abolish slavery was the British, who did so in 1834. America's [Thirteenth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) following their [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) abolished slavery there in 1865, and in [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lei_%C3%81urea) slavery was abolished in 1888 (see [abolitionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism)). Similarly, [serfdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serfdom) was abolished in [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_reform_of_1861_in_Russia) in 1861.

The 19th century was remarkable in the widespread formation of new [settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settler) foundations which were particularly prevalent across North America and Australia, with a significant proportion of the two continents' largest cities being founded at some point in the century. [Chicago](6886.md) in the [United States](3434750.md) and [Melbourne](17306237.md) in Australia were non-existent in the earliest decades but grew to become the 2nd largest cities in the United States and British Empire respectively by the end of the century. In the 19th century, approximately 70 million people left Europe, with most migrating to the United States.[^cite_note-10]

The 19th century also saw the rapid creation, development, and codification of many sports, particularly in Britain and the United States. [Association football](10568.md), [rugby union](25405.md), [baseball](3850.md), and many other sports were developed during the 19th century, while the British Empire facilitated the rapid spread of sports such as [cricket](25675557.md) to many different parts of the world. Also, [women's fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1890s_in_Western_fashion) was a very sensitive topic during this time, as women showing their ankles was viewed to be scandalous.



The boundaries set by the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna), 1815

It also marks the fall of the [Ottoman rule](22278.md) of the [Balkans](4829.md) which led to the creation of [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia), [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bulgaria), [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Montenegro), and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) as a result of the [second Russo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1877%E2%80%931878\)), which in itself followed the great [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War).

## Wars

### Napoleonic Wars

Main article: [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of the Napoleonic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Napoleonic_era).



[Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon)'s retreat from Russia in 1812. The war is turning decisively against the French Empire.

The Napoleonic Wars were a series of major conflicts from 1803 to 1815 pitting the [French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) and its allies, led by [Napoleon I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_I), against a fluctuating array of [European powers formed into various coalitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_forces_of_the_Napoleonic_Wars), financed and usually led by the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland). The wars stemmed from the unresolved disputes associated with the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) and its [resultant conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars).

In the aftermath of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte) gained power in France in 1799. In 1804, he crowned himself [Emperor of the French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_of_the_French).

In 1805, the French victory over an Austrian-Russian army at the [Battle of Austerlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Austerlitz) ended the [War of the Third Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Third_Coalition). As a result of the [Treaty of Pressburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Pressburg_\(1805\)), the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) was dissolved.

&#x20;Later efforts were less successful. In the [Peninsular War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_War), France unsuccessfully attempted to establish [Joseph Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bonaparte) as King of Spain. In 1812, the [French invasion of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_invasion_of_Russia) had massive French casualties, and was a turning point in the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars).



[Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon), Emperor of the [First French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire)

In 1814, after defeat in the [War of the Sixth Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Sixth_Coalition), Napoleon abdicated and was exiled to [Elba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elba). Later that year, he escaped exile and began the [Hundred Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Days) before finally being defeated at the [Battle of Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Waterloo) and exiled to [Saint Helena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Helena), an island in the [South Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_Ocean).

After Napoleon's defeat, the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) was held to determine new national borders. The [Concert of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_of_Europe) attempted to preserve this settlement was established to preserve these borders, with limited impact.

### Latin American independence

Main article: [Spanish American wars of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence)

Portrait of the Chilean declaration of independence

The [Chilean Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_Declaration_of_Independence), 18 February 1818

[Mexico](3966054.md) and the majority of the countries in [Central America](6121.md) and [South America](26769.md) obtained independence from [colonial](7299.md) overlords during the 19th century. In 1804, [Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitian_Revolution) gained independence from France. In [Mexico](3966054.md), the [Mexican War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_War_of_Independence) was a decade-long conflict that ended in Mexican independence in 1821.

Due to the Napoleonic Wars, the royal family of Portugal [relocated to Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_the_Portuguese_Court_to_Brazil) from 1808 to 1821, leading to Brazil having a separate monarchy from Portugal.

The [Federal Republic of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Central_America) gained independence from Spain in 1821 and from Mexico in 1823. After several rebellions, by 1841 the federation had dissolved into the independent countries of [Guatemala](17238567.md), [El Salvador](9356.md), [Honduras](13394.md), [Nicaragua](21362.md), and [Costa Rica](5551.md).[^cite_note-11]

In 1830, the post-colonial nation of [Gran Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Colombia) dissolved and the nations of [Colombia](5222.md) (including modern-day Panama), [Ecuador](9334.md), and [Venezuela](32374.md) took its place.

### Revolutions of 1848

Main article: [Revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848)



Liberal and nationalist pressure led to the [European revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848).

The [Revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848) were a series of [political upheavals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_upheaval) throughout [Europe](9239.md) in 1848. The revolutions were essentially [democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) and liberal in nature, with the aim of removing the old [monarchical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy) structures and creating independent nation states.

The first revolution began in [January in Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848_in_the_Italian_states). Revolutions then spread across Europe after a separate revolution began in [France in February](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution_of_1848). Over 50 countries were affected, but with no coordination or cooperation among their respective revolutionaries.

According to Evans and von Strandmann (2000), some of the major contributing factors were widespread dissatisfaction with political leadership, demands for more participation in government and democracy, demands for freedom of the press, other demands made by the working class, the upsurge of nationalism, and the regrouping of established government forces.[^cite_note-12]

### Abolition and the American Civil War

Main articles: [Abolitionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism) and [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War)



Politician and philanthropist [William Wilberforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Wilberforce) (1759–1833) was a leader of the movement to [abolish the slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_Kingdom).

The [abolitionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism) movement achieved success in the 19th century. The [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md) was abolished in the United States in 1808, and by the end of the century, almost every government had banned slavery. The [Slavery Abolition Act 1833](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_Abolition_Act_1833) banned slavery throughout the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), and the [Lei Áurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lei_%C3%81urea) abolished slavery in Brazil in 1888.

[Abolitionism in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_States) continued until the end of the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War). [Frederick Douglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Douglass) and [Harriet Tubman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Tubman) were two of many American abolitionists who helped win the fight against slavery. Douglass was an articulate orator and incisive antislavery writer, while Tubman worked with a network of antislavery activists and safe houses known as the [Underground Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_Railroad).

The American Civil War took place from 1861 to 1865. Eleven [southern states](179553.md) seceded from the [United States](3434750.md), largely over concerns related to slavery. In 1863, President [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) issued the [Emancipation Proclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_Proclamation). Lincoln issued a preliminary[^cite_note-13] on September 22, 1862, warning that in all states still in rebellion ([Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America)) on January 1, 1863, he would declare their slaves "then, thenceforward, and forever free."[^cite_note-14] He did so.[^cite_note-15] The [Thirteenth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) to the Constitution,[^cite_note-16] ratified in 1865, officially abolished slavery in the entire country.

Five days after [Robert E. Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_E._Lee) surrendered at [Appomattox Courthouse, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appomattox_Courthouse,_Virginia), [Lincoln was assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Abraham_Lincoln) by actor and [Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) sympathizer [John Wilkes Booth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wilkes_Booth).

### Decline of the Ottoman Empire

Main article: [Decline and modernization of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decline_and_modernization_of_the_Ottoman_Empire)

Ibrahim Pasha During his Final Years

[Ibrahim Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Pasha_of_Egypt) of [Egypt](8087628.md), leader of the [Egyptian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Army) in the [Egyptian–Ottoman War (1831–1833)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian%E2%80%93Ottoman_War_\(1831%E2%80%931833\))

[^cite_note-17]

In 1817, the [Principality of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Serbia) became [suzerain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerainty) from the Ottoman Empire, and in 1867, it passed a constitution that defined its independence from the Ottoman Empire. In 1830, [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Greece) became the first country to break away from the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) after the [Greek War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_War_of_Independence). In 1831, the [Bosnian Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_uprising_\(1831%E2%80%931832\)) against Ottoman rule occurred. In 1831, The [First Egyptian–Ottoman War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian%E2%80%93Ottoman_War_\(1831%E2%80%931833\)) (1831–1833) occurred, between the Ottoman Empire and Egypt brought about by [Muhammad Ali Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_of_Egypt)'s demand to the Sublime Porte for control of Greater Syria, as reward for aiding the Sultan during the Greek War of Independence. As a result, Egyptian forces temporarily gained control of Syria, advancing as far north as [Kütahya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BCtahya). In 1876, [Bulgarians](151876.md) instigated the [April Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_Uprising_of_1876) against Ottoman rule. Following the [Russo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1877%E2%80%931878\)), the [Treaty of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Berlin_\(1878\)) recognized the formal independence of Serbia, [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Montenegro), and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania). [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Bulgaria) became autonomous.

### China: Taiping Rebellion

Main article: [Taiping Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiping_Rebellion)



A scene of the [Taiping Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiping_Rebellion)

The [Taiping Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiping_Rebellion) was the bloodiest conflict of the 19th century, leading to the deaths of around 20–30 million people. Its leader, [Hong Xiuquan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Xiuquan), declared himself the younger brother of [Jesus Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_Christ) and developed a new Chinese religion known as the [God Worshipping Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_Worshipping_Society). After proclaiming the establishment of the [Taiping Heavenly Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiping_Heavenly_Kingdom) in 1851, the Taiping army conquered a large part of China, capturing [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) in 1853. In 1864, after the death of Hong Xiuquan, [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) forces recaptured Nanjing and ended the rebellion.[^cite_note-18]

### Japan: Meiji Restoration

Main article: [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration)

During the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Japan) largely pursued an [isolationist foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakoku). In 1853, United States Navy Commodore [Matthew C. Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_C._Perry) threatened the Japanese capital [Edo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo) with gunships, demanding that they agree to open trade. This led to [the opening of trade relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakumatsu) between Japan and foreign countries, with the policy of [Sakoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakoku) formally ended in 1854.

By 1872, the Japanese government under [Emperor Meiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Meiji) had [eliminated the *daimyō* system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_the_han_system) and established a strong central government. Further reforms included the abolition of the [samurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samurai) class, rapid industrialization and modernization of government, closely following European models.[^cite_note-19]

#### Africa



Comparison of Africa in the years 1880 and 1913

In Africa, European exploration and technology led to the colonization of almost the entire continent by 1898. New medicines such as [quinine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinine) and more advanced [firearms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firearms) allowed European nations to conquer native populations.[^cite_note-KerrAfrica-20]

Motivations for the [Scramble for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa) included national pride, desire for raw materials, and Christian missionary activity. Britain seized control of Egypt to ensure control of the [Suez Canal](29323.md), but [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Empire) defeated Italy in the [First Italo–Ethiopian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Italo%E2%80%93Ethiopian_War) at the [Battle of Adwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Adwa). France, Belgium, Portugal, and Germany also had substantial colonies. The [Berlin Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Conference) of 1884–1885 attempted to reach agreement on colonial borders in Africa, but disputes continued, both amongst European powers and in resistance by the native populations.[^cite_note-KerrAfrica-20]

In 1867, [diamonds](8082.md) were discovered in the [Kimberley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberley,_Northern_Cape) region of South Africa. In 1886, gold was discovered in [Transvaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Republic). This led to colonization in Southern Africa by the British and business interests, led by [Cecil Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_Rhodes).[^cite_note-KerrAfrica-20]

Main article: [19th century in science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_century_in_science)

The 19th century saw the birth of science as a profession; the term **scientist** was coined in 1833 by [William Whewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Whewell),[^cite_note-24] which soon replaced the older term of **natural philosopher**. Among the most influential ideas of the 19th century were those of [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin) (alongside the independent researches of [Alfred Russel Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Russel_Wallace)), who in 1859 published the book *[The Origin of Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Origin_of_Species)*, which introduced the idea of [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) by [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection). Another important landmark in medicine and biology were the successful efforts to prove the [germ theory of disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_theory_of_disease). Following this, [Louis Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Pasteur) made the first [vaccine](32653.md) against [rabies](16946852.md), and also made many discoveries in the field of chemistry, including the [asymmetry of crystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomer). In chemistry, [Dmitri Mendeleev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Mendeleev), following the [atomic theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_theory) of [John Dalton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dalton), created the first [periodic table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodic_table) of [elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_element). In physics, the experiments, theories and discoveries of [Michael Faraday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Faraday), [André-Marie Ampère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9-Marie_Amp%C3%A8re), [James Clerk Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clerk_Maxwell), and their contemporaries led to the creation of [electromagnetism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetism) as a new branch of science. [Thermodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermodynamics) led to an understanding of heat and the notion of energy was defined. Other highlights include the discoveries unveiling the nature of atomic structure and matter, simultaneously with chemistry – and of new kinds of radiation. In astronomy, the planet Neptune was discovered. In mathematics, the notion of complex numbers finally matured and led to a subsequent analytical theory; they also began the use of [hypercomplex numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercomplex_number). [Karl Weierstrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Weierstrass) and others carried out the [arithmetization of analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetization_of_analysis) for functions of [real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Function_of_a_real_variable) and [complex variables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_variable). It also saw rise to [new progress in geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Euclidean_geometry) beyond those classical theories of Euclid, after a period of nearly two thousand years. The mathematical science of logic likewise had revolutionary breakthroughs after a similarly long period of stagnation. But the most important step in science at this time were the ideas formulated by the creators of electrical science. Their work changed the face of physics and made possible for new technology to come about including a rapid spread in the use of electric illumination and power in the last two decades of the century and radio wave communication at the end of the 1890s.

### Literature

Main articles: [Romantic poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_poetry) and [19th century in literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_century_in_literature)



Russian writer [Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy), author of *[War and Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_and_Peace)* and *[Anna Karenina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Karenina)*

On the literary front the new century opens with [romanticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism), a movement that spread throughout Europe in reaction to 18th-century rationalism, and it develops more or less along the lines of the Industrial Revolution, with a design to react against the dramatic changes wrought on nature by the steam engine and the railway. [William Wordsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Wordsworth) and [Samuel Taylor Coleridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Taylor_Coleridge) are considered the initiators of the new school in England, while in the continent the German *[Sturm und Drang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturm_und_Drang)* spreads its influence as far as Italy and Spain. French arts had been hampered by the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars) but subsequently developed rapidly. [Modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism) began.[^cite_note-29]

The Goncourts and [Émile Zola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89mile_Zola) in France and [Giovanni Verga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Verga) in Italy produce some of the finest [naturalist novels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalism_\(literature\)). Italian naturalist novels are especially important in that they give a social map of the new unified Italy to a people that until then had been scarcely aware of its ethnic and cultural diversity. There was a huge literary output during the 19th century. Some of the most famous writers included the Russians [Alexander Pushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pushkin), [Nikolai Gogol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Gogol), [Leo Tolstoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Tolstoy), [Anton Chekhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Chekhov) and [Fyodor Dostoyevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Dostoyevsky); the English [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens), [John Keats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Keats), [Alfred, Lord Tennyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred,_Lord_Tennyson) and [Jane Austen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Austen); the Scottish [Sir Walter Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Walter_Scott), [Thomas Carlyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Carlyle) and [Arthur Conan Doyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Conan_Doyle) (creator of the character [Sherlock Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes)); the Irish [Oscar Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Wilde); the Americans [Edgar Allan Poe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe), [Ralph Waldo Emerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Waldo_Emerson), and [Mark Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain); and the French [Victor Hugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Hugo), [Honoré de Balzac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honor%C3%A9_de_Balzac), [Jules Verne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Verne), [Alexandre Dumas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Dumas) and [Charles Baudelaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Baudelaire).[^cite_note-30]

Some American literary writers, poets and novelists were: [Walt Whitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Whitman), [Mark Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain), [Harriet Ann Jacobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Ann_Jacobs), [Nathaniel Hawthorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_Hawthorne), [Ralph Waldo Emerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Waldo_Emerson), [Herman Melville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Melville), [Frederick Douglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Douglass), [Harriet Beecher Stowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Beecher_Stowe), [Joel Chandler Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Chandler_Harris), and [Emily Dickinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Dickinson) to name a few.

- [1896](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1896): [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution) ends declaring Philippines free from Spanish rule.

- [1898](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1898): The United States gains control of [Cuba](5042481.md), [Puerto Rico](23041.md), and the [Philippines](23440.md) after the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War).

- [1898](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1898): [Empress Dowager Cixi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Dowager_Cixi) of [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) engineers a coup d'état, marking the end of the [Hundred Days' Reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Days'_Reform); the [Guangxu Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangxu_Emperor) is arrested.

- [1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900): [Exposition Universelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposition_Universelle_\(1900\)) held in Paris, prominently featuring the growing art trend [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau).

- [1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900)–[1901](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1901): [Eight nations invade China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-Nation_Alliance) at the same time and ransack [Forbidden City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbidden_City).

### Last survivors

Born on 19 April 1897, Japanese [Jiroemon Kimura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiroemon_Kimura) died on 12 June 2013, marking the death of the last man verified to have been born in the century.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] Kimura remains to date the [oldest verified man in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_verified_oldest_men).[^cite_note-bloomberg-37] Subsequently, on 21 April 2018, Japanese [Nabi Tajima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabi_Tajima) (born 4 August 1900) died as the last person to verifiably have been born in the century.[^cite_note-slate2-38]
