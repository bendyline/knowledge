**Gao** ([Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_language): ߜߊߏ ߘߌߣߋߖߊ tr. Gao Dineja) is a region in northeastern [Mali](19127.md). The capital city is [Gao](1255721.md).

## Geography

The region is bordered to the north by [Kidal Region](2214311.md), to the west by [Tombouctou Region](1164276.md) and [Taoudénit Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoud%C3%A9nit_Region), to the east by the [Ménaka Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9naka_Region), and most of the south by [Niger](21373.md) ([Tahoua Region](2000318.md) and [Tillabéri Region](2050923.md)), sharing a portion of the southern border with [Burkina Faso](3470.md) ([Sahel Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel_Region)). The towns include Gao, [Bourem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourem), and [Bamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamba,_Mali).

## Etymology

The name of the Gao Region comes from its capital of the same name. Older writings refer to the city variously as Kaukau, Kounkou, Kookou, and Kawkaw. [Al-Bakrī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bakri) stated that the name of the city originated as an [onomatopoeia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onomatopoeia) for the distinctive [sound of local tom-tom drums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_drum#Sound), which were played when the [king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gao_Empire#Arrival_of_Islam) sat down to eat.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-\:0-4]

In his French translation of *[Tarikh al-Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarikh_al-Sudan)*, Houdas proposed an alternative explanation for the city's name. He argued that it comes from the [Songhay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhay_languages) *Kokoy Korya–*&#x6D;eaning the "king's town" in reference to its status as the capital–suggesting this phrase was mistaken for a [toponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placename).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-\:0-4]

## History

Gao Region formerly comprised the entire eastern portion of the country, east of [Tombouctou Region](1164276.md). In 1991, the northern half of Gao Region became [Kidal Region](2214311.md).

The Gao Region is part of northern Mali that was separated and declared independent by the [National Movement for the Liberation of Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_Azawad) (MNLA) during the [Tuareg rebellion of 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_rebellion_\(2012\)). <sup>\[needs rephrasing for better context]</sup> After the [1st Battle of Gao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gao), the MNLA lost control to [Islamist militias](566821.md). Several other battles took place during the war, particularly in Gao.

## Demographics

The Gao Region is the eleventh most populated region in Mali, with a population of 727,517 in 2022.[^cite_note-2022census-7] With a total fertility rate at 5 births per woman, Gao has a lower TFR than the Malian national average of 6.1 births per woman.[^cite_note-censustfr-8]

Gao is the only [Songhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_people)-majority region in Mali, with the ethnic group constituting 54.10% of the regional population. The [Tuareg](103054.md) are the largest minority, representing roughly a third of Gao's population. Smaller minorities in the region include the [Arabs](2185.md) ([*Maure*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azawagh_Arabs)), [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_people), [Fula](102383.md), and [Bozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozo_people).[^cite_note-censusculture-9]

### Religion

The 2022 census found that 99.51% of the population in Gao was [Muslim](19541.md), 0.47% was [Christian](18337522.md), and 0.01% identified with [traditional faiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_traditional_religions), and 0.01% practised other religions. [Islam](6037917.md) is the dominant religion among all of Gao's main ethnic groups, with the Songhai, Tuareg, Arab, Fula, and Bozo peoples being almost entirely Muslim.[^cite_note-censusculture-9]
