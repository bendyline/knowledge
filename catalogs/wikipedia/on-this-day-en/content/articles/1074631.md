The **Common Security and Defence Policy** (**CSDP**) is the [European Union](9317.md)'s (EU) course of action in the fields of defence and [crisis management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_diplomacy), and a main component of the EU's [Common Foreign and Security Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Foreign_and_Security_Policy) (CFSP).

The CSDP involves the deployment of [military or civilian missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_military_and_civilian_missions_of_the_European_Union) to preserve peace, prevent conflict and strengthen international security in accordance with the principles of the [United Nations Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Charter). Military missions are carried out by [EU forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_forces_of_the_European_Union) established with secondments from the [member states](10890716.md)' armed forces. The CSDP also entails [collective self-defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance) amongst member states[^cite_note-Csdp-3] as well as a [Permanent Structured Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Structured_Cooperation) (PESCO) in which 26 of the 27 national armed forces pursue structural [integration](683704.md) (the exception being Malta). The [CSDP structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_of_the_Common_Security_and_Defence_Policy) – headed by the Union's [High Representative](750942.md) (HR/VP), [Kaja Kallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaja_Kallas), and sometimes referred to as the **European Defence Union** (**EDU**) in relation to its prospective development as the EU's defence arm[^cite_note-auto-4][^cite_note-European_Commission_-_PRESS_RELEASE-5][^cite_note-Archived_copy-6][^cite_note-7] – comprises:

- the [European Commission](9974.md)'s [Defence Industry Directorate-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorate-General_for_Defence_Industry_and_Space)

- the [External Action Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_External_Action_Service)'s (EEAS) [Crisis Management and Planning Directorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_Management_and_Planning_Directorate) (CMPD) and permanent Operation Headquarters (OHQs) for [command and control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_and_control_structure_of_the_European_Union) (C2) at the military/civilian strategic level, i.e. the [MPCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Planning_and_Conduct_Capability) and [CPCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_Planning_and_Conduct_Capability).

- a number of [Foreign Affairs Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_Council) (FAC) preparatory bodies – such as the [Military Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Military_Committee) (EUMC)

- four [agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agencies_of_the_European_Union), including the [Defence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Defence_Agency) (EDA).

The EU command and control structures are much smaller than the [North Atlantic Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Treaty_Organization)'s (NATO) Command Structure (NCS), which has been established for territorial defence.[^cite_note-8] It has been agreed that NATO's [Allied Command Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Command_Operations) (ACO) may be used for the conduct of the EU's missions. The MPCC, established in 2017 and to be strengthened in 2020, is the EU's first permanent military OHQ. In parallel, the [European Defence Fund](56119915.md) (EDF, established in 2017) marks the first time the [EU budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_of_the_European_Union) is used to finance multinational defence projects.

Decisions relating to the CSDP are proposed by the High Representative, adopted by the Foreign Affairs Council, generally requiring unanimity, to be then implemented by the High Representative.

Main article: [History of the Common Security and Defence Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Common_Security_and_Defence_Policy)



The commands of [Western Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Union_\(alliance\)) service branches were situated in the [Palace of Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Fontainebleau) from 1948 until they were transformed into NATO's [Supreme Headquarters Allied Powers Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Headquarters_Allied_Powers_Europe) in 1951.



Organizational chart for the [European Defence Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Defence_Community) (EDC), which – if ratified – would create a unified European defence force. This force would represent an autonomous European pillar within NATO, under the authority of the [Supreme Commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Allied_Commander_Europe).

The [post-war period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_World_War_II) saw several short-lived or ill-fated initiatives for European defence [integration](683704.md) intended to protect against potential Soviet or German aggression: The [Western Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Union_\(alliance\)) (WU, also referred to as the [Brussels Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brussels) Organisation, BTO) and the proposed [European Defence Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_establishing_the_European_Defence_Community) (EDC) were respectively cannibalised by the [North Atlantic Treaty Organisation](21133.md) (NATO) and rejected by the French Parliament. The [Western European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European_Union) (WEU) succeeded the WU in 1955,[^cite_note-10] but was largely overshadowed by NATO.[^cite_note-11]

In 1970 the [European Political Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Political_Cooperation) (EPC) brought about the [European Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Communities)' (EC) initial foreign policy coordination. Opposition to the addition of security and defence matters to the EPC led to the reactivation of the WEU in 1984 by its member states, which were also EC member states.

European defence integration gained momentum soon after the end of the Cold War, partly as a result of the [EC's failure to prevent the Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_and_the_European_Economic_Community#1988%E2%80%931992). In 1992, the WEU was given [new tasks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petersberg_Declaration), and the following year the [Treaty of Maastricht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Maastricht) founded the EU and replaced the EPC with the [Common Foreign and Security Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Foreign_and_Security_Policy) (CFSP) [pillar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_pillars_of_the_European_Union). In 1996 NATO agreed to let the WEU develop a so-called **European Security and Defence Identity** (**ESDI**).[^cite_note-12] The 1998 [St. Malo declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Malo_declaration) signalled that the traditionally hesitant [United Kingdom](31717.md) was prepared to provide the EU with autonomous defence structures.[^cite_note-13] This facilitated the transformation of the ESDI into the **European Security and Defence Policy** (**ESDP**) in 1999, when it was transferred to the EU. In 2003 the EU deployed its first CSDP missions, and adopted the [European Security Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Security_Strategy) identifying common threats and objectives. In 2009, the [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon) introduced the present name, CSDP, while establishing the EEAS, the mutual defence clause and enabling a subset of member states to pursue defence integration within PESCO. In 2011 the WEU, whose tasks had been transferred to the EU, was dissolved. In 2016 [a new security strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Global_Strategy) was introduced, which along with the [Russian annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), the [British withdrawal](41688778.md) from the EU and the [election of Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election) as US president have given the CSDP a new impetus.[^cite_note-14]

### Deployments

Main article: [List of military and civilian missions of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_military_and_civilian_missions_of_the_European_Union)



Since 2002, the European Union has intervened abroad thirty-five times in three different continents.

The first deployment of European troops under the ESDP, following the 1999 declaration of intent, was in March 2003 in the Republic of Macedonia (now [North Macedonia](23564616.md)). [Operation Concordia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Military_Operation_in_the_Republic_of_Macedonia) used NATO assets and was considered a success and replaced by a smaller police mission, [EUPOL Proxima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUPOL_Proxima?action=edit\&redlink=1), later that year. Since then, there have been other small police, justice and monitoring missions. As well as in the Republic of Macedonia, the EU has maintained its deployment of peacekeepers in [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), as part of [Operation Althea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Military_Operation_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina).[^cite_note-15]

Between May and September 2003 EU troops were deployed to the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md) (DRC) during "[Operation Artemis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Artemis)" under a mandate given by [UN Security Council Resolution 1484](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council_Resolution_1484) which aimed to prevent further atrocities and violence in the [Ituri Conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ituri_Conflict) and put the DRC's peace process back on track. This laid out the "framework nation" system to be used in future deployments. The EU returned to the DRC during July–November 2006 with [EUFOR RD Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUFOR_RD_Congo), which supported the [UN mission there](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Organisation_Mission_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) during the country's elections.

Geographically, EU missions outside the Balkans and the DRC have taken place in [Georgia](48768.md), [Indonesia](14579.md), [Sudan](27421.md), [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)), and [Ukraine](31750.md)–[Moldova](19260.md). There is also a judicial mission in [Iraq](7515928.md) ([EUJUST Lex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUJUST_Lex)). On 28 January 2008, the EU deployed its largest and most multi-national mission to Africa, [EUFOR Tchad/RCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUFOR_Tchad/RCA).[^cite_note-16] The UN-mandated mission involves troops from 25 EU states (19 in the field) deployed in areas of eastern [Chad](5488.md) and the north-eastern [Central African Republic](5478.md) in order to improve security in those regions. [EUFOR Tchad/RCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUFOR_Tchad/RCA) reached full operation capability in mid-September 2008, and handed over security duties to the UN (MINURCAT mission) in mid-March 2009.[^cite_note-17]

The EU launched its first maritime CSDP operation on 12 December 2008 ([Operation Atalanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Atalanta)). The concept of the European Union Naval Force (EU NAVFOR) was created on the back of this operation, which is still successfully combatting [piracy off the coast of Somalia](17015903.md) almost a decade later. A second such intervention was launched in 2015 to tackle migration problems in the southern Mediterranean ([EUNAVFOR Med](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUNAVFOR_Med)), working under the name [Operation SOPHIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sophia).

Most of the CSDP missions deployed so far are mandated to support [security sector reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_sector_governance_and_reform) (SSR) in host-states. One of the core principles of CSDP support to SSR is local ownership. The EU Council defines ownership as "the appropriation by the local authorities of the commonly agreed objectives and principles".[^cite_note-18] Despite EU's strong rhetorical attachment to the local ownership principle, research shows that CSDP missions continue to be an externally driven, top-down and supply-driven endeavour, resulting often in the low degree of local participation.[^cite_note-19]

## Structure

Main article: [Structure of the Common Security and Defence Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_of_the_Common_Security_and_Defence_Policy)

The CSDP involves [military or civilian missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_military_and_civilian_missions_of_the_European_Union) being deployed to preserve peace, prevent conflict and strengthen international security in accordance with the principles of the [United Nations Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Charter). Military missions are carried out by [EU forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_forces_of_the_European_Union) established with contributions from the [member states](10890716.md)' armed forces. The CSDP also entails [collective self-defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance) amongst member states[^cite_note-Csdp-3] as well as a [Permanent Structured Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Structured_Cooperation) (PESCO) in which 26 of the 27 national armed forces pursue structural [integration](683704.md). The CSDP structure, headed by the Union's [High Representative](750942.md) (HR/VP), Kaja Kallas, comprises:

- the [Defence Industry Directorate-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorate-General_for_Defence_Industry_and_Space) of the [European Commission](9974.md)

- relevant sections of the [External Action Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_External_Action_Service) (EEAS) — including the [Military Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Military_Staff) (EUMS) with its so-called [Military Planning and Conduct Capability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Planning_and_Conduct_Capability) (MPCC)

- a number of [Foreign Affairs Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Affairs_Council) (FAC) preparatory bodies – such as the [Military Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Military_Committee) (EUMC)

- four [agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agencies_of_the_European_Union), including the European [Defence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Defence_Agency) (EDA).

While the EU has a [command and control (C2) structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_and_control_structure_of_the_European_Union), it has no standing permanent military structure along the lines of the [North Atlantic Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Treaty_Organization)'s (NATO) [Allied Command Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Command_Operations) (ACO), although it has been agreed that ACO resources may be used for the conduct of the EU's CSDP missions. The MPCC, established in 2017 and to be strengthened in 2020, does however represent the EU's first step in developing a permanent military headquarters. In parallel, the newly established [European Defence Fund](56119915.md) (EDF) marks the first time the [EU budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_of_the_European_Union) is used to finance multinational defence projects. The CSDP structure is sometimes referred to as the **European Defence Union** (**EDU**), especially in relation to its prospective development as the EU's defence arm.[^cite_note-auto-4][^cite_note-European_Commission_-_PRESS_RELEASE-5][^cite_note-Archived_copy-6][^cite_note-20]

Decisions relating to the CSDP are proposed by the HR/VP, adopted by the FAC, generally requiring unanimity, and then implemented by the HR/VP.  The [EU command and control (C2) structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_and_control_structure_of_the_European_Union) is directed by political bodies composed of [member states](10890716.md)' representatives, and generally requires unanimous decisions. ## Strategy

Main article: [European Union Global Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Global_Strategy)

The European Union Global Strategy (EUGS) is the updated doctrine of the EU to improve the effectiveness of the CSDP, including the defence and security of the members states, the protection of civilians, cooperation between the member states' armed forces, management of immigration, crises etc. Adopted on 28 June 2016,[^cite_note-24] it replaces the [European Security Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Security_Strategy) of 2003. The EUGS is complemented by a document titled "Implementation Plan on Security and Defense" (IPSD).[^cite_note-25] [Deterrence theory](735661.md) is applied to deter aggressors as one of the core mandates of Common Security and Defence Policy, yet lacks credibility due to insufficient resources.[^cite_note-26]

### National

National armed forces' personnel combined (2016)[^cite_note-28]\
&#x20;View [source data](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Data\:National%20armed%20forces'%20personnel%20combined%20in%20the%20EU.tab).

The CSDP is implemented using civilian and military contributions from [member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_European_Union)' armed forces, which also are obliged to [collective self-defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance) based on [Treaty on European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_European_Union) (TEU).

Five EU states host nuclear weapons: France has its own nuclear programmes, while Belgium, Germany, Italy and the Netherlands host US nuclear weapons as part of NATO's [nuclear sharing policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_sharing#NATO). Combined, the EU possesses 300 warheads, and hosts between 90 and 130 US warheads. Italy hosts 70-90 [B61 nuclear bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B61_nuclear_bomb), while Germany, Belgium, and the Netherlands 10-20 each one.[^cite_note-29] The EU has the third largest arsenal of nuclear weapons, after the United States and Russia.

#### Naval forces

|  | This section needs to be **updated**. Please help update this article to reflect recent events or newly available information. *(November 2018)* |
| --- | --- |



The [French aircraft carrier *Charles de Gaulle*](639127.md) is one of the largest commissioned warships in the European Union.

The combined component strength of the naval forces of member states is some 514 commissioned warships. Of those in service, 4 are [fleet carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_carrier). The EU also has 4 [amphibious assault ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship) and 20 amphibious support ships in service. Of the EU's 49 submarines, 10 are [nuclear-powered submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_submarine) while 39 are conventional attack submarines.

[Operation Atalanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Atalanta) (formally European Union Naval Force Somalia) is the first ever (and still ongoing) naval operation of the European Union. It is part of a larger global action by the EU in the [Horn of Africa](62142.md) to deal with the [Somali](27358.md) crisis. As of January 2011, twenty-three EU nations participate in the operation.

France and Italy have [blue-water navies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-water_navy).[^cite_note-33]

#### Air forces

|  | This section needs to be **updated**. Please help update this article to reflect recent events or newly available information. *(November 2018)* |
| --- | --- |

The air forces of EU member states operate a wide range of military systems and hardware. This is primarily due to the independent requirements of each member state and also the national defence industries of some member states. However such programmes like the [Eurofighter Typhoon](167667.md) and [Eurocopter Tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter_Tiger) have seen many European nations design, build and operate a single weapons platform. 60% of overall combat fleet was developed and manufactured by member states, 32% are US-origin, but some of these were assembled in Europe, the remaining 8% being Soviet-made aircraft. As of 2014, it is estimated that the European Union had around 2,000 serviceable combat aircraft ([fighter aircraft](10929.md) and [ground-attack aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground-attack_aircraft)).[^cite_note-Flightglobal-65]

The EU's airlift capabilities evolved with the introduction of the [Airbus A400M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A400M) (another example of EU defence cooperation). The A400M is a [tactical airlifter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_airlift) with [strategic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_airlift) capabilities.[^cite_note-66] Around 140 were initially expected to be operated by 5 member states (Luxembourg, France, Germany, Spain and Belgium).

### Multinational

#### Established at Union level



[Irish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Army) personnel from the [Nordic Battle Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Battle_Group) at an exercise in 2010

The Helsinki Headline Goal Catalogue is a listing of [rapid reaction forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_reaction_force) composed of 60,000 troops managed by the European Union, but under control of the countries who deliver troops for it.

Forces introduced at Union level include:

- The **[battle groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_Battlegroup)** (**BG**) adhere to the CSDP, and are based on contributions from a coalition of member states. Each of the eighteen Battlegroups consists of a [battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion)-sized force (1,500 troops) reinforced with combat support elements.

  [^cite_note-69]

  [^cite_note-BBC_150307-70]

  &#x20;The groups rotate actively, so that two are ready for deployment at all times. The forces are under the direct control of the [Council of the European Union](5866.md). The Battlegroups reached full operational capacity on 1 January 2007, although, as of January 2013 they are yet to see any military action.

  [^cite_note-71]

  &#x20;They are based on existing *ad hoc* missions that the [European Union](9317.md) (EU) has undertaken and has been described by some as a new "[standing army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_army)" for Europe.

  [^cite_note-BBC_150307-70]

  &#x20;The troops and equipment are drawn from the [EU member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_States_of_the_European_Union) under a "lead nation". In 2004, [United Nations](31769.md) Secretary-General [Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan) welcomed the plans and emphasised the value and importance of the Battlegroups in helping the UN deal with troublespots.

  [^cite_note-72]

- The **[Medical Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Medical_Command)** (**EMC**) is a planned medical command centre in support of [EU missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_operations_of_the_European_Union), formed as part of the [Permanent Structured Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Structured_Cooperation) (PESCO).

  [^cite_note-73]

  &#x20;The EMC will provide the EU with a permanent medical capability to support operations abroad, including medical resources and a rapidly deployable medical task force. The EMC will also provide medical evacuation facilities, triage and resuscitation, treatment and holding of patients until they can be returned to duty, and emergency dental treatment. It will also contribute to harmonising medical standards, certification and legal (civil) framework conditions.

  [^cite_note-europa.eu-74]

- The **[Force Crisis Response Operation Core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EUFOR_Crisis_Response_Operation_Core)** (**EUFOR CROC**) is a flagship defence project under development as part of [Permanent Structured Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Structured_Cooperation) (PESCO). EURFOR CROC will contribute to the creation of a "full spectrum force package" to speed up provision of military forces and the EU's crisis management capabilities.

  [^cite_note-75]

  &#x20;Rather than creating a standing force, the project involves creating a concrete catalogue of military force elements that would speed up the establishment of a force when the EU decides to launch an operation. It is land-focused and aims to generate a force of 60,000 troops from the contributing states alone. While it does not establish any form of "[European army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_army)", it foresees an deployable, interoperable force under a single command.

  [^cite_note-76]

  &#x20;Germany is the lead country for the project, but the French are heavily involved and it is tied to President [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md)'s proposal to create a [standing intervention force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Intervention_Initiative). The French see it as an example of what PESCO is about.

  [^cite_note-77]

#### Provided through Article 42.3 TEU



Personnel of the European Corps in Strasbourg, France, during a change of command ceremony in 2013

This section presents an incomplete list of forces and bodies established intergovernmentally amongst a subset of [member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_European_Union). These organisations will deploy forces based on the collective agreement of their member states. They are typically technically listed as being able to be deployed under the auspices of NATO, the [United Nations](31769.md), the [European Union](9317.md) (EU) through Article 42.3 of TEU, the [Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe](22796.md), or any other international entity.

However, with the exception of the Eurocorps, very few have actually been deployed for any real military operation, and none under the CSDP at any point in its history.

Land Forces:

- The **[Eurocorps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocorps)** is an army corps of approximately 1,000 soldiers stationed in [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg), France. Based in the French city of [Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg), the corps is the nucleus of the [Franco-German Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-German_Brigade).

  [^cite_note-korphistory-78]

- The **[I. German/Dutch Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._German/Dutch_Corps)** is a multinational formation consisting of units from the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Army) and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army) armies. Due to its role as a NATO High Readiness Forces Headquarters, soldiers from other NATO member states, the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_United_States), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Denmark), [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Defence_Force), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Armed_Forces), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Italy), the [United Kingdom](31730.md) amongst others, are also stationed at Münster.

- The **[Multinational Corps Northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_Corps_Northeast)**, a Danish-German-Polish multinational corps

- The **[European Gendarmerie Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Gendarmerie_Force)**, an intervention force with militarised police functions which specializes in crisis management.

  [^cite_note-79]

Aerial:

- The **[European Air Transport Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Air_Transport_Command)** exercises operational control of the majority of the [aerial refueling](237949.md) capabilities and [military transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_transport_aircraft) fleets of its participating nations. Located at [Eindhoven Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_Airport) in the Netherlands, the command also bears a limited responsibility for exercises, aircrew training and the harmonisation of relevant national air transport regulations.

  [^cite_note-80]

  [^cite_note-81]

  &#x20;The command was established in 2010 to provide a more efficient management of the participating nations' assets and resources in this field.

Naval:

- The **[European Maritime Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Maritime_Force)** (**EUROMARFOR** or **EMF**) is a non-[standing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_army),

  [^cite_note-whatis-82]

  &#x20;military force

  [^cite_note-83]

  &#x20;that may carry out [naval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_warfare), [air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_warfare) and [amphibious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare) operations, with an activation time of 5 days after an order is received.

  [^cite_note-retro-84]

  &#x20;The force was formed in 1995 to fulfill missions defined in the [Petersberg Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petersberg_tasks), such as [sea control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_of_the_sea), [humanitarian](434679.md) missions, [peacekeeping](84617.md) operations, crisis response operations, and [peace enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_enforcement).

## Participation, relationship with NATO

Further information: [European Union–NATO relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union%E2%80%93NATO_relations)

The memberships of the EU and NATO are distinct, and some EU member states are traditionally neutral on defence issues. Out of the [27 EU member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_member_state), 23 are also members of NATO. Four non-NATO member states are members of the EU: Austria, Cyprus, Ireland, and Malta. Another four NATO members are EU applicants—Albania, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Turkey. Two others—Iceland and Norway—have opted to remain outside of the EU, however participate in the [EU's single market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Single_Market). Several EU member states were formerly members of the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact). Denmark had an [opt-out from the CSDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opt-outs_in_the_European_Union),[^cite_note-eda.europa1-1] however voted in a [referendum in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Danish_European_Union_opt-out_referendum) to begin to participate in the policy area.

## EUCAP Somalia

The EUCAP Somalia[^cite_note-87] is an example of an unarmed, non-executive, civilian Common Security and Defence Policy (CSDP) Mission. aimed at strengthening maritime security capacities, police sector, as well as promoting the [Rule of Law](25166191.md) in [Somalia](27358.md).[^cite_note-88] It provides strategic level advice, mentoring and training, on issues ranging from coast guard and police functions to police-prosecution cooperation and the drafting of laws. Initially launched as EUCAP Nestor[^cite_note-89] in 2012, the mission was reconfigured to focus on Somalia, thus renamed by the Council of the European Union, EUCAP Somalia in 2016.

In December 2024, the Council of European Union extended the [Mission’s mandate](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2024/12/16/horn-of-africa-and-somalia-operation-atalanta-eutm-and-eucap-somalia-s-mandates-extended-for-two-years/) to February 2027.

The most prominent goals of the Mission are to help Somalia to generate well trained police forces in line with the [Somali Transition Plan](https://press.un.org/en/2018/sc13341.doc.htm), contribute to secure one of the EU’s vital maritime trade routes, and assist to draft and implement sound legislative frameworks, including accountability systems.&#x20;

### Background

The mission was established in response to the persistent challenges posed by maritime insecurity in the Western Indian Ocean, particularly piracy, illegal fishing, human trafficking, and arms smuggling. These issues severely impacted both the security and economic stability of Somalia and the surrounding region. Somalia’s extensive coastline (the longest in mainland Africa) was unprotected for years due to the collapse of the government in the early 1990s.[^cite_note-90] Piracy flourished in the absence of effective maritime governance, leading to international efforts to assist the country in reclaiming control over its waters. EUCAP Somalia emerged as part of these efforts, complementing other international missions such as [Operation Atalanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Atalanta) and [EUTM Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Training_Mission_in_Somalia).

### Mandates

#### From 2012 to 2016

EUCAP Nestor's mandate is divided into two objectives: strengthening the maritime capacities of the beneficiary countries (excluding Somalia) and training a coastal police force and judges in Somalia, the primary aim being to get the countries in the region to work together to strengthen action at sea. EUCAP Nestor operates in five countries: [Djibouti](17207794.md), [Kenya](188171.md), [Seychelles](27288.md), Somalia ([Puntland](233569.md)/[Somaliland](51425.md)) and [Tanzania](30118.md). This is why it is relatively large in terms of staff numbers, with almost 200 people spread across the different countries. These experts provide legal, strategic and operational advice on maritime safety.[^cite_note-91]

#### From 2016 to 2021

When the name was changed in December 2016, so too was the mandate. EUCAP Somalia will drop its regional ambitions and focus solely on Somalia, and more specifically on the Coast Guard, with a view to strengthening ‘Somali maritime law enforcement capabilities’. The mandate is not limited to piracy, but covers any criminal activity or offence committed on the coast or in the sea.[^cite_note-92]

#### From 2021 to 2024

In December 2020, the Council of the European Union extended EUCAP's mandate until 31 December 2024. From 2021 onwards, the mission will also be helping Somalia to strengthen its police capabilities, in particular by developing the Federal Darwish police force and reinforcing the INTERPOL National Central Bureau in [Mogadishu](60154.md).[^cite_note-93] The EU's budget for the period 2023-2024 allocated is 81 million €.[^cite_note-94]

**Current mandate from 2025 to 2027**

In December 2024, the Council of European Union extended the [Mission’s mandate](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2024/12/16/horn-of-africa-and-somalia-operation-atalanta-eutm-and-eucap-somalia-s-mandates-extended-for-two-years/) to February 2027. EUCAP Somalia is tasked to advise, train, and equip the Somali Police Force and to support the development of a legal framework for Somalia’s internal security architecture. The mission’s work also includes providing monitoring support to regional maritime police forces, including the Somaliland and [Puntland Maritime Police Forces](34324730.md), to bolster maritime law enforcement and security. The Mission’s budget for its current mandate is €110 million and total staff of 169 [members](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eucap-som/about-eucap-somalia_en?s=332).

### [Three pillars of EUCAP Somalia](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eucap-som/about-eucap-somalia_en?s=332)

1. **Support the development of Somali Police Forces** by enhancing their capabilities through advising, training and equipping Somali Police Forces in line with the Somali Security Sector Development Plan, ensuring the police are better prepared to maintain security and stability across the country.

2. **Strengthen Maritime Police Capacities** in and around the three main Somali ports (Mogadishu, Berbera and Bossaso) by providing capacity building on coast guard functions.

3. **Promote the rule of law** by working with Somali authorities to enhance accountability within the police force and across the criminal justice chain. This includes supporting the development of legal frameworks necessary for the effective functioning of law enforcement, and security actors in both land and maritime domains, fostering governance and accountability.

### Achievements

Since its reconfiguration in 2016, EUCAP Somalia has made significant strides in enhancing Somalia’s maritime security capabilities. Recent achievements include improving the Somali Police Force's (SPF) command, control, and communication capabilities, as well as enhancing their ability to manage security incidents in Mogadishu. EUCAP has deployed a specialized multinational team to support the SPF's Maritime Police Unit and has played a key role in the development of Somaliland’s Coast Guard, conducting joint exercises with [EUNAVFOR Operation ATALANTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Atalanta). The mission also trained and equipped the Puntland Maritime Police Force, enhancing their capacity to enforce maritime law. Additionally, EUCAP has supported the Somali Attorney General’s Maritime Crimes Unit by providing training in internal procedures and investigations and launching an internship program for young lawyers. Despite the progress, Somalia continues to face challenges in maritime governance due to political instability and resource limitations, but EUCAP remains committed to addressing these issues with Somali authorities and international partners.[^cite_note-95]

[**Mission's achievements during mandate 2022 - 24**](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/2025/EUCAP%20infosheet%20Feb%202025.pdf)

- Increased the Somali Police Force command, control and communication capability.

- Trained Somali Police Force trainers for co-training activities.

- Enabling the establishment of the Maritime Rescue and Coordination Center in Mogadishu to enhance maritime security coordination along the Somali coastline and in regional waters.

- Supported the development of Somaliland Coast Guard, including through joint exercises with EUNAVFOR Operation ATALANTA and the provision of maritime equipment.

- Trained mobile training units for Somaliland Coast Guard and Somaliland Police.

- Trained and equipped the Puntland Maritime Police Force - Maritime Police Unit, enhancing their law enforcement capabilities.

- Supporting the maritime crimes unit of the Somali Attorney General's Office in internal procedures and maritime crimes investigations as well as an internship program for young lawyers in Mogadishu and Puntland.
