**Peter Brian Hegseth** (born June 6, 1980) is an American government official, veteran, and former television personality who has been serving as the 29th [United States secretary of defense](43998.md) since 2025.

Hegseth studied politics at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where he was the publisher of *[The Princeton Tory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princeton_Tory)*, a conservative student newspaper. In 2003, he was commissioned as an infantry officer in the [Minnesota Army National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Army_National_Guard), serving at [Guantanamo Bay Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_Naval_Base) and deployed to [Iraq](7515928.md) and [Afghanistan](737.md). Hegseth worked for several organizations after leaving Iraq, including as an executive director at [Vets For Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vets_For_Freedom) and Concerned Veterans for America. He became a contributor to [Fox News](11121.md) in 2014. Hegseth served as an advisor to President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) after supporting [his campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_2016_presidential_campaign) in 2016. From 2017 to 2024, Hegseth co-hosted *[Fox & Friends Weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_&_Friends_Weekend)*. He has written several books, including *[American Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Crusade)* (2020) and *[The War on Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_War_on_Warriors)* (2024).

In November 2024, then-President-elect Trump named Hegseth as his nominee for secretary of defense. In a [Senate Committee on Armed Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Committee_on_Armed_Services) hearing days before [Trump's second inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump's_second_inauguration), Hegseth faced allegations of sexual misconduct, financial mismanagement, and excessive drinking. Hegseth was confirmed by the [Senate](24909346.md) that month, with the vice president, [JD Vance](53396477.md), casting a tie-breaking vote. It was only the second time in U.S. history that a [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States) nominee's confirmation was decided by a vice president, following [Betsy DeVos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betsy_DeVos) during the [first Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Trump_administration) in 2017. Hegseth is the second-youngest secretary of defense after [Donald Rumsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Rumsfeld), who first occupied the position in the [Ford administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Gerald_Ford).

Hegseth has drawn criticism during [his tenure as secretary of defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Hegseth_as_Secretary_of_Defense) for a variety of actions such as the fallout from [a leaked government group chat on Signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_government_group_chat_leaks); alleged [war crimes](48626.md) related to [U.S. military strikes on alleged drug traffickers](80956668.md) in the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md); promoting Christianity inside the military; using unmarked military planes for boat strikes; the use of force and rules of engagement in the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md); an unprecedented purge of admirals and generals; and a military mandate for testosterone screening.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-\:0-7][^cite_note-\:9-8][^cite_note-Jaffe_07112025-9][^cite_note-\:10-10][^cite_note-11] Conversely, Hegseth has received praise from [Republican](32070.md) politicians, [right-wing](56522.md) political commentators, and [Christian nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_nationalist) organizations for his role as defense secretary.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

## Early life and education

A Renaissance brick building with green shrubbery and a large white spire

[Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University), where Hegseth studied (pictured in 2019)

Peter Brian Hegseth was born on June 6, 1980, in [Minneapolis](6097240.md), Minnesota.[^cite_note-15] He is of [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegians) descent, as his great-grandparents migrated from [Norway](21241.md) to Minnesota before they had children.[^cite_note-16] He was the first of three children of Brian Hegseth and Penelope Haugen.[^cite_note-Crowley-17][^cite_note-Strand-18][^cite_note-20] Hegseth's father was a basketball coach for high schools across Minnesota before retiring in 2019.[^cite_note-Strand-18] Hegseth's mother is an executive business coach[^cite_note-LaFraniere-21] who has taught with the Minnesota Excellence in Public Service (MEPS) Series, a fellowship and leadership program for Republican and [center-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center-right) women.[^cite_note-Crowley-17] Hegseth was raised in [Forest Lake, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Lake,_Minnesota),[^cite_note-Bolduc-22] and attended [Forest Lake Area High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Lake_Area_High_School).[^cite_note-23] He graduated on June 10, 1999 as [valedictorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valedictorian). Hegseth played on his high school's football team and was a [point guard](583875.md) on the basketball team, where he set school records for career three-point shots, single-season three-point shots, and single-season three-point shooting percentage. He was named all-conference twice and earned all-state honors as a senior.[^cite_note-Sweeney-24]

After high school, Hegseth enrolled at [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University),[^cite_note-Chang-25] where he graduated with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in politics in 2003.[^cite_note-Chang-25][^cite_note-26] According to *Reserve & National Guard Magazine*, he chose Princeton over an offer from the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy) to play for the school's [basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_Tigers_men's_basketball).[^cite_note-Manfre-27] Months before the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md), Hegseth joined the [Reserve Officers' Training Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Officers'_Training_Corps).[^cite_note-Philipps-Rosenberg-28] During his years at Princeton, Hegseth[^cite_note-Sweeney-24] was the publisher and editor-in-chief of *[The Princeton Tory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princeton_Tory)*, the school's conservative student newspaper.[^cite_note-Mayer-29]

In April 2002, Hegseth declared that, as publisher of *The Princeton Tory,* he would "defend the pillars of Western civilization against the distractions of diversity".[^cite_note-30] The editors of *The Princeton Tory* criticized [Halle Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_Berry) for accepting the [Academy Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress) for her performance in *[Monster's Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster's_Ball)* (2001) "on behalf of an entire race", and *[The New York Times](30680.md)* for announcing that it would print [gay marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_marriage) announcements, arguing that it would justify publishing marriage announcements for [incestuous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incest), [zoophilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoophilia), and [pedophilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedophilic) relationships.[^cite_note-31] In October, *The Princeton Tory* published an editorial calling [homosexuality](5488304.md) immoral. In response, the president of Princeton's student government, Nina Langsam, wrote a strongly worded email to Hegseth and *The Princeton Tory*'s publisher, Brad Simmons. Her email was published in the following issue.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

## Career

### Military service (2003–2006, 2010–2014, 2019–2021)



Hegseth meeting with Mahmood Kalaf Ahmed, the mayor of [Samarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarra), in 2005

After graduating from Princeton in 2003, Hegseth was commissioned as a second lieutenant in the [United States Army](32087.md) through the university's [Reserve Officers' Training Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_Officers'_Training_Corps) program.[^cite_note-Bolduc-22][^cite_note-Craven-34] He briefly worked as an equity-markets analyst at [Bear Stearns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear_Stearns).[^cite_note-Mayer-29] Hegseth completed his basic training at [Fort Benning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Benning) in [Columbus, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Georgia), in 2004,[^cite_note-Manfre-27] and for 11 months he was a [Minnesota Army National Guardsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Army_National_Guard) at [Guantanamo Bay detention camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_detention_camp).[^cite_note-Craven-34] There, he led a platoon of soldiers from the [New Jersey Army National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Army_National_Guard)[^cite_note-Philipps-Rosenberg-28] guarding detainees.[^cite_note-Mayer-29] By July 2005, he had returned to Bear Stearns;[^cite_note-Craven-34] shortly thereafter, he volunteered in the [Iraq War](5043324.md) as an infantry officer,[^cite_note-Manfre-27] where he received a [Bronze Star Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Star_Medal).[^cite_note-Mayer-29] Hegseth served in the [3rd Battalion, 187th Infantry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Battalion,_187th_Infantry_Regiment)[^cite_note-Philipps-Rosenberg-28] in the [101st Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Airborne_Division), led by Colonel [Michael D. Steele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_D._Steele).[^cite_note-Steinhauer-35] He began his tour in [Baghdad](4492.md) before moving to [Samarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samarra),[^cite_note-Bolduc-22] where he served as a [civil affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_affairs#United_States_Army) officer,[^cite_note-36] working with the city council and forming an alliance with council member Asaad Ali Yaseen.[^cite_note-Bolduc-22] Hegseth has described a near-death experience in Iraq in which a [rocket-propelled grenade](26286.md) hit his vehicle but failed to detonate.[^cite_note-37]

In 2010, Hegseth deployed with the Minnesota Army National Guard as a counterinsurgency instructor.[^cite_note-Bolduc-22] He volunteered to teach at the Counterinsurgency Training Center in [Kabul](16826.md), Afghanistan, for eight months,[^cite_note-Bolduc-22] during the [withdrawal of United States troops from Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_United_States_troops_from_Afghanistan_\(2011%E2%80%932016\));[^cite_note-Steinhauer-35] he taught one of the final classes at the school.[^cite_note-Philipps-Rosenberg-28] After completing his tour in 2014, he was promoted to [major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_\(United_States\))[^cite_note-Mayer-29] and assigned to the [Individual Ready Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_Ready_Reserve).[^cite_note-Manfre-27] Through the reserve, he joined the [District of Columbia Army National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Columbia_Army_National_Guard) in June 2019 as a traditional drilling service member, remaining in duty until March 2021.[^cite_note-38] He was barred from serving on duty at the [inauguration of Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inauguration_of_Joe_Biden) after a guardsman flagged Hegseth as an "insider threat", noting [a tattoo](#Tattoos) on his [biceps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biceps) of the words *[Deus vult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deus_vult)*.[^cite_note-39] He left the Individual Ready Reserve in January 2024, writing in his book *[The War on Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_War_on_Warriors)* (2024) that he resigned over the incident.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHegseth202460-40]

### Political activism (2006–2016)



Hegseth speaking at the Defend Freedom Tour in 2013

By August 2006,[^cite_note-Philipps-Rosenberg-28] Hegseth moved to [Manhattan](45470.md) and began working at the [Manhattan Institute for Policy Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Institute_for_Policy_Research),[^cite_note-Bolduc-22] where he met a [marine](17349325.md) who was working for [Vets for Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vets_for_Freedom), a political advocacy organization.[^cite_note-Mayer-29] He began working for Vets for Freedom in 2006 as an unpaid director;[^cite_note-LaFraniere-etal-41] by 2007, he was working full-time as an executive director,[^cite_note-42] and by 2008, he became the organization's president.[^cite_note-LaFraniere-etal-41] In May 2007, Hegseth appeared at a [presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain_2008_presidential_campaign) fundraiser for [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain).[^cite_note-43] In the months leading up to the [2008 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election), Vets for Freedom began supporting McCain.[^cite_note-44] As the group's chairman, he criticized Democratic nominee [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) for supporting "a dangerous policy of irreversible withdrawal."[^cite_note-45] By January 2009, Vets for Freedom had accrued hundreds of thousands of dollars in unpaid bills, leading to an internal campaign to oust Hegseth. The group merged with Military Families United, and he was removed from leadership by 2011.[^cite_note-Mayer-29]

After returning to Minnesota in February 2012,[^cite_note-Bolduc-22] Hegseth decided to enter the [Republican](32070.md) primary for the [United States Senate election in Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_Senate_election_in_Minnesota) and had selected a campaign manager, [Anne Neu Brindley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Neu_Brindley).[^cite_note-46] By April, his campaign had raised US\$160,000.[^cite_note-47] Hegseth lost to [Kurt Bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Bills) in the Republican convention in May,[^cite_note-48] and ended his candidacy days later.[^cite_note-49] He founded MN PAC to support similar candidates, though a third of the organization's funds went to parties for personal friends and family.[^cite_note-Mayer-29] Hegseth began working as president of Concerned Veterans of America, a group [funded by the Koch brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koch_network),[^cite_note-50] that year.[^cite_note-51] The group criticized President Obama for the [2014 Veterans Health Administration controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Veterans_Health_Administration_controversy).[^cite_note-52] Hegseth enrolled in the [Harvard Kennedy School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Kennedy_School) in 2009, but completed just one semester;[^cite_note-Bolduc-22] he graduated in 2013 with a degree in public policy.[^cite_note-Sweeney-24] In 2022,[^cite_note-Hill-53] to protest the offering of classes in [critical race theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_race_theory) at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), he reportedly wrote "Return to sender" on his degree[^cite_note-54] and sent it back to the university.[^cite_note-55]

Hegseth left Concerned Veterans for America in January 2016[^cite_note-Philipps-56] after allegations of financial mismanagement and alcoholism. A [whistleblower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistleblower) report accused Hegseth of fostering a [sexist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexist) and hostile workplace at Concerned Veterans for America; one whistleblower claimed that Hegseth had used organization funds as a personal expense account. Another former employee claimed he had witnessed Hegseth drunkenly chanting "Kill all Muslims!"[^cite_note-Mayer-29] In December, President-elect [Donald Trump](4848272.md) considered Hegseth for [secretary of veterans affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_veterans_affairs), but he faced opposition from veterans groups who viewed Hegseth's support for allowing all veterans to choose private doctors as untenable; [Paul Rieckhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Rieckhoff), the executive director of [Iraq and Afghanistan Veterans of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_and_Afghanistan_Veterans_of_America), said that selecting Hegseth would "be war" and "a radical departure" for the department.[^cite_note-Philipps-56] Trump later chose [David Shulkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Shulkin), with *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* noting Hegseth's lack of experience in operating a large organization.[^cite_note-57] Hegseth told podcaster [Shawn Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Ryan_\(United_States_Navy\)) that Trump found him too young to assume the position.[^cite_note-58] After Shulkin fell out of favor with the [Trump administration](52231341.md) in March 2018, Hegseth positioned himself as a potential candidate,[^cite_note-59] but Trump selected [Robert Wilkie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wilkie) after consulting Hegseth and financier [Isaac Perlmutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Perlmutter).[^cite_note-60]

### Fox News (2014–2024)



Hegseth interviewing Secretary of State [Mike Pompeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pompeo) on *[Fox & Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_&_Friends)* in March 2020

By June 2014, Hegseth was given a position as a regular contributor to [Fox News](11121.md)[^cite_note-61] by the network's executive, [Roger Ailes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ailes).[^cite_note-Samuels-Mastrangelo-62] In 2016, he was briefly a host on [TheBlaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheBlaze)[^cite_note-63] before regularly hosting *[Fox & Friends Weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_&_Friends_Weekend)* that year after Ailes's resignation, becoming an official co-host in January 2017.[^cite_note-Samuels-Mastrangelo-62] According to a Fox News executive in *[Hoax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoax_\(book\))* (2020), Jennifer Rauchet, a producer of *Fox & Friends Weekend* who later married him, "was favoring Pete with airtime" and "kept putting Pete on TV."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStelter202298-64] Hegseth served as a temporary host for [Laura Ingraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Ingraham) on *[The Ingraham Angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ingraham_Angle)* in an effort by the network to promote other staffers; the change occurred during the [boycott of *The Ingraham Angle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boycott_of_The_Ingraham_Angle) after comments Ingraham made about [David Hogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hogg), an activist and survivor of the [Parkland high school shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parkland_high_school_shooting).[^cite_note-65] He hosted *All-American New Year* (2018) with commentator [Lisa Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Kennedy).[^cite_note-66]

Hegseth's opinions expressed on *Fox & Friends* influenced Trump's policymaking in [his first term](52231341.md). In October 2018, as [a migrant caravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_migrant_caravans#Late_2018_caravan) began traveling to the United States, Trump claimed that "unknown Middle Easterners" had infiltrated the caravan. Trump apparently cited a comment that Hegseth had made on *Fox & Friends*, though Hegseth said he had not verified his statement's accuracy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBakerGlasser2022344-67] Hegseth had apparently based his claim on a statement Guatemalan president [Jimmy Morales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Morales) made after capturing 100 [ISIS](9087364.md) fighters in the country.[^cite_note-68] In negotiations to avert a [federal government shutdown](2150997.md), [Democrats](5043544.md) neared a deal until Hegseth urged Trump not to support a deal that did not include US\$5 billion for [his border wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trump_wall).[^cite_note-69] Trump repeated claims Hegseth had made correlating video games with mass shootings after two mass shootings [in El Paso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_El_Paso_Walmart_shooting) and [in Dayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Dayton_shooting) in August 2019.[^cite_note-70] Hegseth said he had spoken to Trump about pardoning war criminal [Clint Lorance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Lorance) and accused murderer [Mathew L. Golsteyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathew_L._Golsteyn), as well as reversing the demotion of [Eddie Gallagher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Gallagher_\(Navy_SEAL\)).[^cite_note-71]

At Fox News, Hegseth was the subject of multiple lawsuits. In 2015, he [threw an axe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axe_throwing) during a [Flag Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_Day) event in [New York City](645042.md), accidentally hitting a drummer from the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy). Video of the incident circulated widely online. The drummer, Jeff Prosperie, alleged that he had suffered "severe and serious personal injuries to his mind and body" and "permanent effects of pain, disability, disfigurement and loss of body function." Prosperie sued Hegseth three years later in 2018,[^cite_note-72] and the suit was resolved in 2019.[^cite_note-73] In *[Dominion Voting Systems v. Fox News Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_Voting_Systems_v._Fox_News_Network)* (2023), [Dominion Voting Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_Voting_Systems) included a segment of *Fox & Friends Weekend* featuring Hegseth with co-hosts [Will Cain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Cain) and [Rachel Campos-Duffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Campos-Duffy), in which they did not reject claims by [Rudy Giuliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudy_Giuliani) that the company's voting machines [facilitated voting fraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_or_misleading_statements_by_Donald_Trump_\(between_terms\)) in the [2020 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._presidential_election,_2020).[^cite_note-74]

Hegseth was chosen among Fox News's hosts to be featured on [Fox Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Nation), the network's streaming service.[^cite_note-75] To promote the service, he co-hosted a one-hour special, *Fox Nation First Look*, with [Jesse Watters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Watters), [Tomi Lahren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomi_Lahren), [Britt McHenry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Britt_McHenry), and [Tyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrus_\(wrestler\)).[^cite_note-76] On Fox Nation, Hegseth hosted *The Miseducation of America* (2022–2023), a television program criticizing "the Left's educational agenda".[^cite_note-77] He also hosted the series *Battle in the Holy Land* (2019–2023),[^cite_note-78] *The Life of Jesus* (2022–2023), and the special *Battle in Bethlehem* (2019), on the service.[^cite_note-Craven-34]

### Nomination and confirmation

Hegseth testifying at his confirmation hearing before the [Senate Committee on Armed Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Committee_on_Armed_Services) on January 14, 2025

On November 12, 2024, President-elect [Donald Trump](4848272.md) named Hegseth as his nominee for [secretary of defense](43998.md),[^cite_note-79] after [Arkansas](1930.md) senator [Tom Cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cotton) announced he would not serve as secretary.[^cite_note-80] Hegseth subsequently ended his contract with [Fox News](11121.md).[^cite_note-Hill-53] The selection of Hegseth was seen as a sign that Trump sought to appoint a loyalist to lead the [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense),[^cite_note-85] and his relative lack of experience surprised officials in the department.[^cite_note-88] According to *[Vanity Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanity_Fair_\(magazine\))*, [Trump's transition team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_presidential_transition_of_Donald_Trump) became aware the following day of [a sexual assault allegation](#Abuse_and_sexual_assault_allegations) involving Hegseth that occurred in [Monterey, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterey,_California), seven years prior;[^cite_note-89] *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* reported that senior officials on the team were surprised by the allegation and reconsidered his nomination.[^cite_note-90] Despite the allegation, Trump defended Hegseth[^cite_note-91] and several Republican senators indicated ### Initial actions

Hegseth being sworn in as secretary of defense by vice president [JD Vance](53396477.md) on January 25, 2025

Hegseth was sworn in as the secretary of defense by vice president [JD Vance](53396477.md) on January 25.[^cite_note-110] Hegseth identified several priorities for the Department of Defense, including to "revive the warrior ethos", restore trust in the military, redevelop the nation's industrial base, ease the department's process to purchase weaponry, defend the US domestically, engage with [Indo-Pacific](330456.md) to deter [China](5405.md), and support Trump's effort to "end wars responsibly"—including the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md) and the [Middle Eastern crisis](75895273.md).[^cite_note-111] In a call to [Israeli prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_prime_minister) [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) a day after being sworn in, Hegseth said that the United States was "fully committed" to the security of [Israel](9282173.md).[^cite_note-112] Hegseth revoked the security clearance and detail of [Mark Milley](38091005.md), the former [chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](364458.md) and [chief of staff of the Army](485507.md) who later became a critic of Trump, and ordered an inspector general inquiry into Milley's tenure as chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff; the [inspector general of the Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense_Office_of_Inspector_General), [Robert Storch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Storch), was removed from his position when Trump [dismissed several inspectors general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_dismissals_of_inspectors_general).[^cite_note-113] According to *The Washington Post*, the [Department of Defense Education Activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense_Education_Activity) began removing certain books on immigration and sexuality.[^cite_note-114]

Hegseth visited the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border) with [Tom Homan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Homan), Trump's border czar, in [El Paso, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso,_Texas), in February, where he stated that the federal government intended to gain complete "operational control of the southern border".[^cite_note-115] He renamed Fort Liberty to [Fort Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Bragg), its [original name honoring the Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._Army_installations_named_for_Confederate_soldiers) general [Braxton Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braxton_Bragg). The military base was now ostensibly renamed for [Roland L. Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_L._Bragg), a previously obscure soldier who served in World War II.[^cite_note-116] In a meeting before the [Ukraine Defense Contact Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_Defense_Contact_Group) at [NATO headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_headquarters), he opposed [NATO](21133.md) membership for [Ukraine](31750.md) and said that returning Ukraine's borders prior to the [annexation of Crimea by Russia](42228673.md) was "unrealistic".[^cite_note-117] The Department of Defense invited [Jack Posobiec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Posobiec), an [alt-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alt-right) political activist to accompany Hegseth, according to *[The Washington Post](102226.md)*.[^cite_note-118] Hegseth moderated his comments the following day, stating that it would be possible for Ukraine to [join NATO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine%E2%80%93NATO_relations) given Trump's discretion.[^cite_note-119]

In February 2025, Hegseth ordered officials within the Department of Defense to reduce funding on most initiatives[^cite_note-120] and began a purge from within the department,[^cite_note-121] firing three top [judge advocate generals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judge_Advocate_General's_Corps) and [Lisa Franchetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Franchetti), the chief of naval operations.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123] Hegseth stated that "we want lawyers who give sound constitutional advice" rather than "roadblocks to anything".[^cite_note-124] In March, he ordered [US Cyber Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Cyber_Command) to halt offensive operations against [Russia](25391.md), in an apparent effort to encourage Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) to [negotiate an end](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_negotiations_in_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) to the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md).[^cite_note-125] Also that month, the Defense Department canceled 91 of its research studies, including those on climate change impacts and social trends, while Hegseth later stated that the Defense Department "does not do climate change crap."[^cite_note-126] Separately, the [Trump administration](78298108.md) instructed Hegseth to "immediately" present "credible military options to ensure fair and unfettered US military and commercial access to the [Panama Canal](24849.md)".[^cite_note-127]

### Information disclosures and use of Signal

Further information: [United States government group chat leaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_government_group_chat_leaks)





Screenshots from the [leaked Signal chat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_government_group_chat_leaks) showing Pete Hegseth discussing plans for the [March–May 2025 United States attacks in Yemen](79465918.md)

In March 2025, [Jeffrey Goldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Goldberg), the editor-in-chief of *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic)*, [reported that](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_government_group_chat_leaks) he had been accidentally included by [Mike Waltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Waltz) in a [Signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_\(software\)) group chat where Hegseth shared information about [attacks in Yemen](79465918.md) hours before they occurred.[^cite_note-128] The discussions involved US officials, including Vance and secretary of state [Marco Rubio](5502549.md). According to *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, several officials with the Department of Defense expressed shock at the incident, while various former national security officials noted the potential for espionage amid [ongoing efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_telecommunications_hack) by [China](5405.md) to obtain telecommunications records. A spokesperson for the [National Security Council](44026.md) confirmed Goldberg's report and the authenticity of the messages. Hegseth rejected that war plans were shared and called Goldberg "deceitful" and a "discredited so-called journalist".[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130] Director of National Intelligence [Tulsi Gabbard](26328774.md) said no classified information was shared and CIA director [John Ratcliffe](42699041.md) said Signal was authorized for the group chat.[^cite_note-131]

The incident distressed Hegseth, who threatened to use a [polygraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygraph) on [Christopher W. Grady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_W._Grady), the acting [chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](364458.md).[^cite_note-Youssef-132] Goldberg later published most of the Signal chat.[^cite_note-133] The chat showed that Hegseth posted information including the launch times of [F-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-18) aircraft, [MQ-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MQ-9) drones and [Tomahawk missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_missiles), as well as the time when the F-18 aircraft would reach their targets, and the time when the bombs would land.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] Hegseth commented on the chat, writing that there were: "No names. No targets. No locations. No units. No routes. No sources. No methods. And no classified information".[^cite_note-136] The incident led to criticism from both Republicans and Democrats.[^cite_note-137] After the Signal leak, media outlet *[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel)* searched the Internet using a commercial information provider and password leaks, which revealed Hegseth's personal mobile number, personal email address and its password, and [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp) account.[^cite_note-138]

*[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* reported that Hegseth had brought his wife to two meetings with foreign defense officials in which sensitive information was discussed, one meeting in February, at [Brussels](3708.md), with NATO officials, and the other in March, at the Pentagon, with British Defense Secretary [John Healey](434625.md).[^cite_note-Long-139] Meanwhile, the [Associated Press](18935732.md) reported in March that Hegseth's brother, Phil, was listed by the Trump administration as a senior adviser to Hegseth, accompanying Hegseth to meetings, including in Congress, and on official foreign trips.[^cite_note-Copp-19] Phil, who previously worked in podcasting and media relations, was confirmed by Hegseth's office to be working in the Pentagon as a [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) liaison to the Department of Defense.[^cite_note-Copp-19] That month, *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* detailed a memorandum written by Hegseth orienting the department towards deterring a potential invasion of [Taiwan](25734.md) and supporting homeland defense by "assuming risk" in Europe. The document contained passages that were identical to those present in [Project 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_2025).[^cite_note-140]

According to *The Washington Post*, Hegseth had Signal installed on his computer to circumvent cellular communication issues and to more easily communicate with other Trump officials.[^cite_note-141] [CNN](62028.md) later reported that Ricky Buria, a former aide to secretary of defense [Lloyd Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Austin), had set up Signal on Hegseth's computer.[^cite_note-142] According to the Associated Press, the computer was on an unsecured [internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) line that was not using one of the Department of Defense's [IP addresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_address).[^cite_note-143] In April, the [Department of Defense Office of Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense_Office_of_Inspector_General) announced an inquiry into Hegseth's disclosure of classified information in the Signal chat.[^cite_note-144] That month, *The New York Times* reported that Hegseth had shared details on the attack in a second Signal chat with his wife, brother, and personal lawyer.[^cite_note-145] At the White House Easter Egg Roll, Hegseth suggested that the revelations were a coordinated smear campaign.[^cite_note-146] John Ullyot, the former spokesman for the Department of Defense, wrote in a *[Politico Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politico_Magazine)* opinion piece hours later that the department was in a "full-blown meltdown" and warned that Hegseth was at risk of losing his position.[^cite_note-147]

According to [NPR](29697232.md), the White House began looking for a secretary of defense to replace Hegseth the following day.[^cite_note-148] Nonetheless, he retained support from Trump,[^cite_note-149] who privately did not seek to relitigate a grueling Senate confirmation, enjoyed Hegseth's presence and appearance on television, and believed that firing him would lead to questions over Waltz's retention, while he publicly associated the controversy to "disgruntled employees" and boasted of Hegseth's work.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151] [Karoline Leavitt](71739774.md), the [White House press secretary](284101.md), stated that "the entire Pentagon is working against" Hegseth.[^cite_note-152] Representative [Don Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Bacon) became the first Republican House member to urge that Hegseth be fired.[^cite_note-153] At least five political appointees within the Department of Defense resigned by April 24.[^cite_note-Youssef-132] That day, Joe Kesper, Hegseth's chief of staff, resigned, but stated that he would remain at the department as a [special government employee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_government_employee).[^cite_note-154] The firings and resignations led to a crisis within the Department of Defense that was described as a "free-for-all" by one employee who spoke to *[Politico](9172097.md)*.[^cite_note-155]

In September 2025, Hegseth called over 800 generals and admirals from across the world for a rare and urgent meeting at [Marine Corps Base Quantico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Base_Quantico). *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* reported the meeting came on short notice and without a stated reason, sparking "confusion and alarm".[^cite_note-156]

On September 30, Hegseth addressed gathered leadership with a campaign-style rally, complaining of "woke" policies about diversity or investigations into hazing and restrictive rules of engagement. [ABC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_News_\(United_States\)) described the speech as "extraordinarily blunt", with Hegseth assailing "gender illusions" and promoting a "warrior ethos". Hegseth announced raising physical fitness and grooming standards to a "male level" and removing "fat generals" and "beardos"; announced an overhaul to the Office of Inspector General, saying it had been weaponized to put "complainers, ideologues and poor performers in the driver's seat"; and falsely stated that there had been a mandate for "racial quotas". Hegseth encouraged those gathered to resign if they disagreed with his changes. Following Hegseth, President Trump gave a speech praising his accomplishments and stated the military should [engage in domestic deployments](80838047.md) as "training grounds".[^cite_note-157] *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that officers remained "stone-faced" to maintain neutrality and that many privately said they found the speech insulting.[^cite_note-Jaffe_30092026-158]

### Caribbean boat strikes

Main article: [2025 United States military strikes on alleged drug traffickers](80956668.md)

Unclassified footage of the first airstrike

On November 28, 2025, *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* published an article alleging Hegseth had given a spoken order to kill the survivors of a September 2, 2025, strike led by SEAL Team 6 on a boat suspected of carrying drugs in the Caribbean Sea.[^cite_note-boat-159] The operation was overseen by Admiral [Frank M. "Mitch" Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_M._Bradley), from Fort Bragg in North Carolina. Bradley described the survivors as legitimate targets, as they could theoretically have contacted other traffickers to rescue them and their cargo, and subsequently ordered the second strike to complete Hegseth's order that everyone must be killed.[^cite_note-boat-159] Hegseth's explanation for the strike has shifted over time.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162] The [Office of Legal Counsel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Legal_Counsel) eventually argued that the strike was legal because its goal was to destroy the boat, not to kill the crew, reflecting a secret legal memo endorsing such strikes; the memo has been "fiercely criticized" by legal experts.[^cite_note-Guardian3Dec-163] The strike was the first in a series of strikes against suspected drug traffickers.[^cite_note-boat-159] Hegseth's alleged orders were characterized by several legal experts as murder and a war crime.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-boat-159][^cite_note-165]

The alleged orders were condemned by members of congress, including Representative [Seth Moulton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Moulton), who said "Mark my words: It may take some time, but Americans will be prosecuted for this, either as a war crime or outright murder."[^cite_note-boat-159] Both Senators [Tim Kaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Kaine) and [Mark Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Kelly) described the alleged orders as a war crime.[^cite_note-gold-166] Representative [Mike Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Turner) stated that "Obviously if that occurred, that would be very serious, and I agree that that would be an illegal act."[^cite_note-gold-166] On November 28, 2025, Senator [Roger Wicker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Wicker), chairman of the Senate Armed Services Committee wrote in a statement that the committee had directed inquiries about the orders to the defense department, and "we will be conducting vigorous oversight to determine the facts related to these circumstances."[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168] Hegseth subsequently defended his actions, writing on X that "As usual, the fake news is delivering more fabricated, inflammatory, and derogatory reporting to discredit our incredible warriors fighting to protect the homeland."[^cite_note-169]

### Removal of Anthropic AI

See also: [Anthropic–United States Department of Defense dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic%E2%80%93United_States_Department_of_Defense_dispute)

The Defense Secretary threatened [Anthropic](6206236.md), the owner of [Claude AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_AI) (the only AI authorized by the Department of Defense in early 2026), to designate the company a supply chain risk[^cite_note-\:3-170] and force the company to eliminate its restrictions on the use of its AI by the Defense Department or he will use the [Defense Production Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Production_Act_of_1950) to compel them.[^cite_note-\:6-171] The Defense Department gave Anthropic a deadline of February 27, 2026. The contract Sec. Hegseth wanted to cancel was worth \$200 million.[^cite_note-172] A senior defense official stated that the company will "pay a price for forcing our hand like this." Anthropic stated that they wanted assurances that the DoD would not use their AI tools to spy on Americans or develop weapons that could fire without human involvement.[^cite_note-\:3-170] The Defense Department also stated in February 2026 that Grok, OpenAI and Google were close to being approved for use.[^cite_note-\:6-171] Claude was used by the Pentagon during their January 2026 operation to capture [Nicolas Maduro](7161476.md).[^cite_note-173] In May 2026, the Defense Department announced AI use deals with [SpaceX](832774.md), [OpenAI](48795986.md), Google, [NVIDIA](39120.md), Reflection, Microsoft and [Amazon Web Services](1691376.md).[^cite_note-174]

On February 27, 2026, Hegseth used the [Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Acquisition_Supply_Chain_Security_Act) to declare Anthropic a supply chain risk and excluded it from all federal contracts.[^cite_note-175] President Trump also stated that every federal agency must stop using Anthropic's AI technology. [ChatGPT](72417803.md) signed a deal with the Defense Department the same day but their CEO [Sam Altman](22476815.md) said, "the AI system shall not be intentionally used for domestic surveillance of U.S. persons and nationals."[^cite_note-176] On March 6, 2026, Anthropic's CEO [Dario Amodei](74329563.md) stated that his company will challenge the DoD in court.[^cite_note-177] Anthropic's partners, including Microsoft, Amazon and Google stated their agreement with Amodei. On March 8, 2026, [OpenAI](48795986.md) (the parent company of ChatGPT) announced that their head of robotics, [Caitlin Kalinowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Kalinowski), had resigned stating, "surveillance of Americans without judicial oversight and lethal autonomy without human authorization are lines that deserved more deliberation than they got."[^cite_note-178]

### Military leadership resignations and removals

As defense secretary, Hegseth has undertaken an unprecedented purge of senior leadership with little explanation and often against the advice of top military leaders.[^cite_note-Jaffe_07112025-9] As of September 2026, more than 20 generals, admirals, or civilian leaders have been forced out or have resigned, and more had their promotions blocked. Over 80 generals and admirals have been impacted in total, amounting to 10% of the military's top officers.[^cite_note-CBS-02092026-187][^cite_note-188] The [NAACP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAACP), [National Women's Law Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women's_Law_Center), and other civil rights groups have accused Hegseth of blocking the promotions of black people and women.[^cite_note-Drenon_01102026-189] *[The New York Times](30680.md)* has reported the "purge does not appear to be driven by job performance, but rather is part of a broader effort by the defense secretary to fashion a force, particularly at senior levels, that is aligned with his political and ideological views, according to military officials".[^cite_note-190]

In February 2025, Hegseth relieved Chief of Naval Operations Adm. [Lisa Franchetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Franchetti).[^cite_note-CBS-02092026-187] In April 2026, Hegseth asked Army Chief of Staff Gen. [Randy George](67617755.md) to step down and retire.[^cite_note-191] The removals of Franchetti and George caused the longest breaks without a Senate-confirmed service chief in Army and Navy history.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193] On August 31, 2026, Secretary of the Army [Dan Driscoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_P._Driscoll) gave Hegseth his resignation[^cite_note-194] after a face to face meeting Driscoll had with President Trump where he stated his concerns over the number of army generals fired under Hegseth.[^cite_note-195] On September 30, Hegseth announced a 20% reduction of admirals and generals effective January 1, 2027 as nesessary to purge "woke" elements and restore a "warrior ethos". The announcement raised concerns over political interference in the Pentagon and sparked criticism of sidelining experience, discouraging dissent, and appointing political loyalists.[^cite_note-196] Hegseth stated the military would remove "ideological clowns" and that "patriotic cowboys are in with testosterone testing on top".[^cite_note-Drenon_01102026-189]

### Domestic issues

Hegseth holds strongly conservative views and has been regarded as a [Christian nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_nationalism#United_States) by critics.[^cite_note-200] In his book *[American Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Crusade)* (2020), Hegseth characterized "[Americanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americanism_\(ideology\))" as being opposition to movements such as [feminism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminism), [globalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalism), [Marxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism), and [progressivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism), equated [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) to a leftist demand, and expressed support for [gerrymandering](12987.md) to "screw [Democrats](5043544.md)".[^cite_note-Herman-197][^cite_note-Wilson2024-201] He described progressives and Democrats as enemies of freedom, the United States, and the [Constitution](31644.md). Hegseth has said that victory for America includes the end of, [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism), [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism), [Islamism](15012.md), [globalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalism), [secularism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism), "genderism", and [environmentalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism).[^cite_note-202] He has repeated [false claims of electoral fraud](65905202.md) in the [2020 presidential election](48410011.md) and spread conspiracy theories about [Antifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifa_\(United_States\)) involvement in the [January 6 attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_6_attack).[^cite_note-203] As defense secretary, Hegseth promoted [culture war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_war) issues, derided "secular humanism", and promoted conservative [evangelicalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism).[^cite_note-Cooper_30092026-186][^cite_note-204]

Hegseth initially supported [Florida](18933066.md) senator [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) in the [2016 Republican Party presidential primaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries), later favored [Texas](29810.md) senator [Ted Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Cruz), and ultimately endorsed [Donald Trump](4848272.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStelter202299-205] He defended Trump's policies in [his first term](52231341.md), including his interactions with [North Korean](21255.md) supreme leader [Kim Jong Un](5962511.md), the [2020–2021 US troop withdrawal from Afghanistan](66883576.md), and the [assassination of Qasem Soleimani](62726668.md).[^cite_note-Steinhauer-35] In 2016 on Fox News, Hegseth criticized [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) for her [email controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton_email_controversy), stating that her "recklessness in handling information" would usually lead to job firings and potential criminal charges, while also risking foreign access to sensitive information and harming relationships with allies.[^cite_note-211]

In a March 2012 interview with *[National Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Review)*, Hegseth advocated for [premium support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_\(United_States\)#Premium_support) in [Medicare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_\(United_States\)), opposed [contraception mandates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraceptive_mandate), and described the [Keystone Pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_Pipeline) as a choice between jobs and [environmental impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_oil_drilling), asserting he would "always side with jobs."[^cite_note-Bolduc-22] On *[Fox & Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_&_Friends)* in 2019, Hegseth described [climate change](5042951.md) as an [attempt at government control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_denial).[^cite_note-212] In March 2025, he canceled [climate change studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_science) and decried the phenomenon as "crap" on social media.[^cite_note-213] That month, he sought to eliminate [climate planning](2119179.md) from the [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense) but included an exception for extreme weather preparation.[^cite_note-214]

### Foreign policy



Hegseth and German Defence Minister [Boris Pistorius](69097811.md) at NATO Headquarters on February 12, 2025

In November 2009, Hegseth supported sending additional forces into [Afghanistan](737.md) during the [War in Afghanistan](19666611.md).[^cite_note-215] He advocated for withdrawing from Afghanistan in his interview with the *National Review*, but argued that special operators should remain in the country and that the [Afghan Army](212681.md) should be supported to avert a conflict.[^cite_note-Bolduc-22]

Hegseth has criticized [NATO](21133.md) and the [United Nations](31769.md).[^cite_note-AlJazeera-216][^cite_note-217] After the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), he called [Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putin) a [war criminal](70167888.md),[^cite_note-218] but he later told [Fox News](11121.md) host [Harris Faulkner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris_Faulkner) that the invasion was less significant than "[wokeness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woke#2019%E2%80%93present:_as_a_pejorative)" and crime.[^cite_note-219] He has criticized [United States military aid to Ukraine](70376708.md).[^cite_note-AlJazeera-216] In January 2020, he supported Donald Trump's threat of the destruction of Iranian cultural sites.[^cite_note-Steck-210][^cite_note-220]

Hegseth has supported the premiership of [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md). After Netanyahu was expected to be [criminally charged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_of_Benjamin_Netanyahu) in March 2019 for alleged bribery and fraud, he posted a video of Hegseth describing him as a "great friend to the United States".[^cite_note-221] He has argued that the [Chinese government](23239.md) is "building a military to defeat the United States" and repeated claims by Trump that "tens of thousands of Chinese nationals" have been sent to the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border).[^cite_note-222] In May 2020, Hegseth said the "[communist Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Chinese)" want to "end our civilization".[^cite_note-223]

### Military affairs

In a [Yale Political Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_Political_Union) speech in October 2008, Hegseth disagreed with "[Don't ask, don't tell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_ask,_don't_tell)", the United States's position on [homosexuality in the military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation_in_the_United_States_military) at the time, but noted that "[Radical Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Islam) is a far greater threat."[^cite_note-224] In a podcast interview with [Shawn Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Ryan_\(United_States_Navy\)) in November 2024, Hegseth stated that [women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_the_United_States_Armed_Forces) should not serve in [combat roles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_combat).[^cite_note-225]

As defense secretary, Hegseth promoted basing Pentagon policy on [culture war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_war) issues. During his 2025 speech in front of over 800 generals and admirals, Hegseth blamed them of having restrictive rules of engagement, allowing liberal ideas and "toxic ideological garbage", and accused them without evidence of lowering standards to let women in the military.[^cite_note-Jaffe_30092026-158] During his September 30, 2026 "State of the Force" address, Hegseth stated the military would have "No fatties, no trannies, no beardos, no weirdos, no wimps, no radicals",[^cite_note-Cooper_30092026-186] and that there would be no more "silly PowerPoint trainings or drag queen shows", "gender pronouns", or "identity months".[^cite_note-Jaffe_30092026-158]

Hegseth opposed [Operation Iron Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Iron_Triangle), a raid in August 2006 that resulted in the death of three Iraqi men, which he described as "atrocities" to an audience at the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia). He has criticized the US military for accusing soldiers of committing war crimes.[^cite_note-Philipps-Rosenberg-28] In an op-ed in *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* from March 2026, political philosopher [Jan-Werner Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Werner_M%C3%BCller) wrote that "Pete Hegseth \[was] promoting a [nihilist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihilism) cult of death".[^cite_note-The_Guardian_23_March_2026-226]

### Religion

In *In the Arena* (2016), Hegseth described his [Christian](5211.md) faith as initially "more out of diligent habit than deep conviction". Following the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md), he developed a hatred for [Islamic terrorism](566821.md), and "he found himself repelled by the \[Princeton University] [campus chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University_Chapel)'s 'gospel of [moral relativism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_relativism),' and disparaged his fellow students for focusing on peace and 'mutual understanding' rather than 'condemnations of Islamic terrorism'".[^cite_note-Craven-34] He told *Nashville Christian Family* that he experienced a religious transformation in 2018 after he and his wife, Jennifer, began attending the Colts Neck Community Church in [New Jersey](21648.md). Seeking to send their children to Jonathan Edwards Classical Academy, a [Christian school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_school), the Hegseths moved to [Nashville, Tennessee](22018.md), three years later. There, they joined the Pilgrim Hill Reformed Fellowship, a church in the [Communion of Reformed Evangelical Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communion_of_Reformed_Evangelical_Churches) (CREC).[^cite_note-Baker-Graham-240] Hegseth is a supporter of CREC co-founder [Douglas Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Wilson_\(theologian\)). In August 2025, he favorably shared a video from [CNN](62028.md) featuring Wilson, with Hegseth commenting on a video that included a pastor from Wilson's church calling for the repeal of [women's right to vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_right_to_vote), another pastor stating that husbands should cast votes for their entire [household](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_voting), and Wilson stating that women should not hold leadership positions in the military.[^cite_note-241]

Hegseth supports the idea of [holy war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_war) and has repeatedly praised the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades), claiming that people who enjoy the "benefits" of [western culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture) should "thank a crusader".[^cite_note-Wilson2024-201][^cite_note-Baker-Graham-240] Two of Hegseth's tattoos reference the Crusades.[^cite_note-SnopesTattoos-242]
