The **leader of the Labour Party** is the highest political office within the British [Labour Party](19279158.md). The current holder of the position is [Andy Burnham](414818.md) since 17 July 2026, following his victory in the [2026 Labour Party leadership election](83143566.md).[^cite_note-1]

The position of leader was officially codified in the Labour Party's constitution in 1922. Before this, from when Labour MPs were first elected at the [1906 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1906_United_Kingdom_general_election) and the [1922 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_United_Kingdom_general_election) (the first election that saw substantial gains for the Labour Party), the position of leader was known as [Chairman of the Parliamentary Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Labour_Party) (PLP).[^cite_note-A_History_of_the_British_Labour_Party-2] Following the [1970 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_United_Kingdom_general_election), the positions of party leader and PLP chair were separated.

In 1921, [J. R. Clynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._R._Clynes) became the first Leader of the Labour Party to have been born in [England](9316.md); all leaders before him had been born in [Scotland](26994.md). Following the [1924 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_United_Kingdom_general_election), [Ramsay MacDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsay_MacDonald) became the first party leader to become Prime Minister of the [United Kingdom](31717.md), leading a minority government which lasted nine months. Following the [1945 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_United_Kingdom_general_election), [Clement Attlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_Attlee) became the first leader to form a majority government. In 1983, [Neil Kinnock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Kinnock) became the first party leader to have been born in [Wales](69894.md). The only two party leaders not to have contested general elections (excluding acting leaders) are [George Lansbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lansbury), who resigned, and [John Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Smith_\(Labour_Party_leader\)), who died in office.[^cite_note-3]

To date, the only leaders to have led the party to victory in general elections are Ramsay MacDonald, Clement Attlee, [Harold Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Wilson), [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair) and [Keir Starmer](18681491.md). Out of the five, Wilson won four general elections out of five contested, in [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_United_Kingdom_general_election), [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_United_Kingdom_general_election), [February 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_1974_United_Kingdom_general_election) (forming a minority government) and [October 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_1974_United_Kingdom_general_election). Blair won three consecutive electoral victories in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_United_Kingdom_general_election), [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_United_Kingdom_general_election) (both [landslide victories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslide_victories)), and [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_United_Kingdom_general_election). Attlee, the first leader to lead Labour to a majority in [1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_United_Kingdom_general_election), also won in [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_United_Kingdom_general_election). Starmer led Labour to a landslide victory in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election), although his victory was tempered by a percentage share of the popular vote that was the lowest of any party forming a majority government in British electoral history. Both [James Callaghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Callaghan) and [Gordon Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Brown) also served as prime minister, but did not win general elections. Three Labour leaders subsequently stood against official Labour Party candidates at general elections, [George Barnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Barnes_\(British_politician\)), MacDonald and [Jeremy Corbyn](415036.md).

When the Labour Party forms the [Government of the United Kingdom](25318118.md), the leader is expected to serve as prime minister, [First Lord of the Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Lord_of_the_Treasury) and [Minister for the Civil Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_the_Civil_Service), as well as appointing and chairing the [Cabinet](162790.md). Concordantly, when the party is in [Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Majesty's_Most_Loyal_Opposition), the leader is expected to act as the [Leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(United_Kingdom\)) and to chair the [Shadow Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Opposition_Shadow_Cabinet_\(United_Kingdom\)).

## Selection process

The leader of the Labour Party, along with their deputy, is elected using an [alternative vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_vote) system.[^cite_note-4] Before 1980, the party leader was elected solely by members of the [Parliamentary Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Labour_Party) (PLP).

From 1980 to 2014 an electoral college was used to elect the leader, with a third of votes allocated to the party's [MPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Labour_Party_\(UK\)_MPs) and [MEPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_members_of_the_European_Parliament_2009%E2%80%932014), a third to individual members of the Labour Party, and a third to individual members of all affiliated organisations, including [socialist societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_societies) and [trade unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affiliated_trade_union).

Beginning with the [2015 leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Labour_Party_leadership_election_\(UK\)), the party now uses a "one member, one vote" system, in which the votes of MPs, party members and members of affiliated organisations are all weighted equally. While the votes of Labour MPs are no longer counted separately, leadership candidates must first receive the nomination of at least 20% of MPs in order to appear on the ballot.[^cite_note-5]

The PLP does not have the equivalent of the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\))'s [1922 Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_Committee) to call a binding [vote of confidence](208101.md) for the party leader; Jeremy Corbyn did not resign despite 80% of Labour MPs voting against him. A potential challenger to the current leader would need the support of 20% of MPs to call an election to choose a replacement; the current leader would automatically be on the ballot unless deciding otherwise.[^cite_note-crerar20250913-6]

## Retirement

It is not uncommon for a retired leader of the Labour Party to be granted a [peerage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peerage) upon their retirement, particularly if they served as [prime minister](24150.md); examples of this include [Clement Attlee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_Attlee), [Harold Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Wilson) and [James Callaghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Callaghan). However, [Neil Kinnock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Kinnock) was also elevated to the [House of Lords](13658.md), despite never being prime minister, and [Michael Foot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Foot) declined a similar offer.
