The **governor of California** is the [head of government](37570.md) of the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [California](5407.md). The [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_\(United_States\)) is the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the [California National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_National_Guard) and the [California State Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Guard).

Established in the [Constitution of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_California), the governor's responsibilities also include submitting the budget, ensuring that state laws are enforced, and making the annual [State of the State address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_State_address) to the [California State Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Legislature). The position was created in 1849, the year before California became a state.

The governor is now limited to two terms, regardless of whether they are consecutive. The current governor of California, is [Democrat](5043544.md) [Gavin Newsom](418947.md), who was inaugurated on January 7, 2019 and whose second term ends on January 1, 2027. [Jerry Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Brown) was the longest serving governor in Californian history, serving from 1975 until 1983, and again from 2011 until 2019.

## Responsibilities

Article 5 of the State Constitution lists the Powers & Responsibilities of the Office of Governor of California:

- Chief Executive of the State

- Ensures State Laws are faithfully enforced & executed

- Supreme Authority over State Agencies & Departments

- Appoints Officials to State Agencies, Boards & commissions

- Appoints state judges to vacant judicial seats

- Commander-In-Chief of the [California National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_National_Guard) (when not federalized) & [California State Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Guard)

- Can sign bills into law

- Can [veto](74661.md) bills entirely (override by 2/3 majority in Legislature)

- Use a line-item veto on budget bills

- Prepares & submits the statement budget every year

- Has Authority to approve or reject funding levels for government programs

- Convene the State Legislature if needed

- Deliver the State of the State Address to the State Legislature every year

- Can grant pardons, commute sentences or issue reprieves (cannot grant clemency involving impeachment, requires California Supreme Court approval)

- If a U.S. Senate seat become vacant due to death or resignation, the Governor can appoint a replacement

- Can use the “[Bully Pulpit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bully_pulpit)" to influence Public Opinion or Legislative Priorities

## Gubernatorial elections, oath, and term of office

### Qualifications

A candidate for governor must be a [U.S. citizen](19792942.md) and a registered voter within the state, must not have been convicted of a felony involving bribery, embezzlement, or extortion, and must not have served two terms since November 6, 1990.[^cite_note-2]

### Election and oath of office

Governors are elected by [popular](652563.md) [ballot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballot) following a [nonpartisan primary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonpartisan_primary) and serve terms of four years, with a limit of two terms, if served after November 6, 1990.[^cite_note-Sec'y-3] Governors take the following oath:

> I (Governor) do solemnly swear that I will support and defend the [Constitution of the United States](31644.md) and the Constitution of the State of California against all enemies foreign and domestic, that I will bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution of the United States and the Constitution of the State of California, that I take this obligation freely without any mental reservation or purpose of evasion and that I will well and faithfully discharge the duties upon which I am about to enter.

Governors take office on the first Monday after January 1 after their election.

### Gubernatorial removal



The [California Governor's Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Governor's_Mansion), official residence of the governor

Two methods exist to remove a governor.

#### Impeachment and removal by the legislature

The governor [can be impeached](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_in_California) for "misconduct in office" by a majority vote of the [State Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Assembly) and removed by a two-thirds supermajority vote of the [State Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Senate).

#### Recall by the voters

Petitions signed by California state voters equal to 12% of the last vote for the office of governor (with signatures from each of five counties equal to 1% of the last vote for governor in the county) can launch a gubernatorial [recall election](129203.md). The voters can then vote on whether or not to recall the incumbent governor, and on the same ballot can vote for a potential replacement. If a majority of the voters in the election vote to recall the governor, then the person who gains a plurality of the votes in the replacement race will become governor.

Only two governor recall attempts have ever gained enough signatures to make the ballot in California. The [2003 recall election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_California_gubernatorial_recall_election) began with a petition drive that forced Democratic governor [Gray Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_Davis) into a recall election, which he lost. He was replaced by Republican [Arnold Schwarzenegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Schwarzenegger). It was the first time that a California governor was voted out of office by Recall.[^cite_note-4]

In addition to the successful 2003 recall, current governor [Gavin Newsom](418947.md) faced a [recall election in 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_California_gubernatorial_recall_election), which he defeated.[^cite_note-5]

## Relationship with the lieutenant governor



Bronze statue of a [California grizzly bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_grizzly_bear) outside the governor's office, in the [California State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Capitol)

The [lieutenant governor of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Governor_of_California) is separately elected during the same election, not jointly as the [running mate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_mate) of the gubernatorial candidate. As such, California had governors and lieutenant governors of different parties for nearly 27 of the 33 years between 1978 and 2011, whereas previously, this had only occurred in 1875, 1887, 1895 and 1916–1917 due to the resignation or death of an incumbent governor or lieutenant governor.

This occasionally becomes significant, since the California Constitution provides that all the powers of the governor fall to the lieutenant governor whenever the governor is not in the state of California, with the lieutenant governor sometimes signing or vetoing legislation or making political appointments whenever the governor leaves the state.

In practice, there is a [gentlemen's agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentlemen's_agreement) for the lieutenant governor not to perform more than perfunctory duties while the governor is away from the state: This [agreement was violated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Curb#Political_career) when [Mike Curb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Curb) was in office, as from 1979 to 1983 he signed several executive orders at odds with the [Brown administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Brown) when Brown was out of the state. Court rulings have upheld the lieutenant governor's right to perform the duties and assume all of the prerogatives of governor while the governor is out of the state.[^cite_note-6]

The lieutenant governor is also the president of the California State Senate.

## Official residence and workplace



[Stanford Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leland_Stanford_Mansion) is the official reception center for the [California government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_California) and one of the official workplaces for the governor.

The official residence of the California governor is the [California Governor's Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Governor's_Mansion), in Sacramento. The mansion has served as the residence of 14 governors, while others have declined to reside in the mansion, preferring private residential arrangements. It is also one of the official workplaces for the governor.

The governor's primary official workplace is located within the [California State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Capitol) in Sacramento.

The [Stanford Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leland_Stanford_Mansion), in Sacramento, serves as one of the official workplaces for the governor, as well as the official reception center for the [California government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_California).
