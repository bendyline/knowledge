**Arizona**[^cite_note-13] is a landlocked state in the [Southwestern United States](179571.md), sharing the [Four Corners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Corners) region with [Colorado](5399.md), [New Mexico](21649.md), and [Utah](31716.md). It also borders [Nevada](21216.md) to the northwest and [California](5407.md) to the west, and shares [an international border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico-United_States_border) with the [Mexican states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_states) of [Sonora](83750.md) and [Baja California](199793.md) to the south and southwest. Its capital and [largest city](14649921.md) is [Phoenix](49121.md), which is the most populous state capital and [fifth-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population) in the United States. Arizona is divided into 15 [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_Arizona).

Arizona is the [sixth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area) state by area and the [14th-most-populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population) of the 50 states. It is the 48th state and last of the [contiguous states](83759.md) to be admitted to the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)), achieving statehood on February 14, 1912. Historically part of the territory of [Alta California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alta_California) and [Nuevo México](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_de_Nuevo_M%C3%A9xico) in [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain) ([Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire)), it became part of independent Mexico in 1821. After being defeated in the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War), Mexico ceded much of this territory to the United States in 1848, where the area became part of the [New Mexico Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico_Territory). The southernmost portion of the state was acquired in 1853 through the [Gadsden Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadsden_Purchase).

[Southern Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Arizona) is known for its [desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert) climate, with extremely hot summers and mild winters. [Northern Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Arizona) features forests of pine, [Douglas fir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_fir), and [spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spruce) trees; the [Colorado Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Plateau); mountain ranges (such as the [San Francisco Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Peaks)); as well as large, deep [canyons](78478.md), with much more moderate summer temperatures and significant winter snowfalls. There are [ski resorts](28478.md) in the areas of [Flagstaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagstaff,_Arizona), [Sunrise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunrise_Park_Resort), and [Tucson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson,_Arizona). In addition to the internationally known [Grand Canyon National Park](46986.md), which is one of the [world's seven natural wonders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Natural_Wonders_of_the_World), there are several [national forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._National_Forest), [national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_parks_\(United_States\)), and [national monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_monument_\(United_States\)).

Arizona is home to a diverse population. About one-quarter of the state[^cite_note-\:2-14][^cite_note-15] is made up of [Indian reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_reservations_in_Arizona) that serve as the home of [27 federally recognized Native American tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Arizona#List_of_contemporary_peoples), including the [Navajo Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navajo_Nation), the largest in the state and the country, with more than 300,000 citizens. Since the 1980s, the proportion of [Hispanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) has grown significantly owing to migration from Mexico and Central America. A substantial portion of the population are followers of the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) and [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints). Arizona's population and [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy) have grown dramatically since the 1950s because of inward migration, and the state is now a major hub of the [Sun Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Belt). Many large companies, such as [PetSmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PetSmart) and [Circle K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_K),[^cite_note-16] have headquarters in the state, and Arizona is home to major universities, including the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona), [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University), and [Northern Arizona University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Arizona_University).

## Etymology

The state's name appears to originate from an earlier Spanish name, *Arizonac*, derived from the [O'odham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohono_O'odham) name *alĭ ṣonak*, meaning 'small spring'. Initially this term was applied by Spanish colonists only to an area near the [silver mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_mining) camp of [Planchas de Plata, Sonora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planchas_de_Plata,_Sonora).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] To the European settlers, the O'odham pronunciation sounded like *Arissona*.[^cite_note-AZC070211-21] The area is still known as *alĭ ṣonak* in the O'odham language.[^cite_note-ood-12]

Another possible origin is the [Basque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_language) phrase *haritz ona* 'the good oak', as there were numerous Basque shepherds in the area.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] A native-born Mexican of Basque ancestry established the *[ranchería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancher%C3%ADa)* (small rural settlement) of Arizona between 1734 and 1736 in the current Mexican state of [Sonora](83750.md). It became notable after a significant discovery of silver there around 1737.[^cite_note-25]

## History

Main article: [History of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Arizona)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Arizona).

For thousands of years before the modern era, Arizona was home to many ancient [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) civilizations. [Hohokam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohokam), [Mogollon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogollon_culture), and [Ancestral Puebloan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_Puebloans) cultures were among those that flourished throughout the state. Many of their pueblos, cliffside dwellings, rock paintings and other prehistoric treasures have survived and attract thousands of tourists each year.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]



*La conquista del Colorado*, by [Augusto Ferrer-Dalmau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_Ferrer-Dalmau), depicts [Francisco Vázquez de Coronado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_V%C3%A1zquez_de_Coronado)'s 1540–1542 expedition. [García López de Cárdenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garc%C3%ADa_L%C3%B3pez_de_C%C3%A1rdenas) can be seen overlooking the [Grand Canyon](46989.md).

In 1539, [Marcos de Niza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_de_Niza), a Spanish [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan), became the first European to contact Native Americans. He explored parts of the present state and made contact with [native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) inhabitants, probably the [Sobaipuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobaipuri). The expedition of Spanish explorer [Coronado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_V%C3%A1squez_de_Coronado) entered the area in 1540–1542 during its search for [Cíbola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quivira_and_C%C3%ADbola).[^cite_note-28] One of the first settlers in Arizona was [José Romo de Vivar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Romo_de_Vivar).[^cite_note-Lainez_and_Torres-29]

[Father Kino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusebio_Kino) was the next European in the region under Spanish rule. A member of the [Society of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) ("[Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits)"), he led the development of a chain of missions in the region. He converted many of the Indians to Christianity in the [Pimería Alta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pimer%C3%ADa_Alta) (now southern Arizona and northern [Sonora](83750.md)) in the 1690s and early 18th century. Spain founded *presidios* ("fortified towns") at Tubac in 1752 and Tucson in 1775.[^cite_note-30]

When Mexico achieved its independence from the [Kingdom of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Spain) and its [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) in 1821, what is now Arizona became part of its Territory of *Nueva California*, ("New California"), also known as *[Alta California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alta_California)* ("Upper California").[^cite_note-31] Descendants of ethnic Spanish and [mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo) settlers from the colonial years still lived in the area at the time of the arrival of later European-American migrants from the United States.



[Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mexican_Republic) in 1824. [Alta California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alta_California) is the northwesternmost state.

During the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War) (1847–1848), the [U.S. Army](32087.md) occupied the national capital of [Mexico City](18987.md) and pursued its claim to much of northern Mexico, including what later became [Arizona Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Arizona_\(United_States\)) in 1863 and later the State of Arizona in 1912.[^cite_note-32] The [Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Guadalupe_Hidalgo) (1848) specified that, in addition to language and cultural rights of the existing inhabitants of former Mexican citizens being considered as inviolable, the sum of \$15 million in compensation (equivalent to \$558,173,076.92 in 2025) be paid to the Republic of Mexico.[^cite_note-33] In 1853, the U.S. acquired the land south below the [Gila River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gila_River) from Mexico in the [Gadsden Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadsden_Purchase) along the southern border area as encompassing the best future southern route for a transcontinental railway.[^cite_note-34]

What is now the state of Arizona was administered by the [United States government](195149.md) as part of the [Territory of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_New_Mexico) from 1850 until the southern part of that region seceded from the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)) to form the [Territory of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_Arizona).[^cite_note-35] This newly established territory was formally organized by the federal government of the [Confederate States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) on Saturday, January 18, 1862, when President [Jefferson Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Davis) approved and signed *An Act to Organize the Territory of Arizona*,[^cite_note-36] marking the first official use of the name "Territory of Arizona". The Southern territory supplied the Confederate government with men, horses, and equipment. Formed in 1862, [Arizona scout companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_A,_Arizona_Rangers) served with the [Confederate States Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_Army) during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War). Arizona has the westernmost military engagement on record during the Civil War with the [Battle of Picacho Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Picacho_Pass) (1862).[^cite_note-37]



[Geronimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geronimo) (far right) and his [Apache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache) warriors fought against both Mexican and American settlers.

The Federal government declared a new U.S. Arizona Territory, consisting of the western half of earlier New Mexico Territory, in Washington, D.C., on February 24, 1863.[^cite_note-38] These new boundaries would later form the basis of the state. The first territorial capital, Prescott, was founded in 1864 following a gold rush to central Arizona.[^cite_note-39] The capital was later [moved to Tucson, back to Prescott, and then to its final location in Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Territory_capitals) in a series of controversial moves as different regions of the territory gained and lost political influence with the growth and development of the territory.[^cite_note-40]

Although names including "Gadsonia", "Pimeria", "Montezuma" and "Arizuma" had been considered for the territory,[^cite_note-41] when 16th President [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) signed the final bill, it read "Arizona", and that name was adopted. ([Montezuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montezuma_\(mythology\)) was not derived from the [Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec) emperor, but was the sacred name of a divine hero to the [Akimel O'odham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akimel_O'odham) of the [Gila River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gila_River_Valley). It was probably considered – and rejected – for its sentimental value before Congress settled on the name "Arizona".)

[Brigham Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young), leader of [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) in [Salt Lake City, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City), sent [Mormons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormons) to Arizona in the mid- to late 19th century. They founded [Mesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesa,_Arizona), [Snowflake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowflake,_Arizona), [Heber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heber-Overgaard,_Arizona), [Safford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safford,_Arizona), and other towns. They also settled in the [Phoenix Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Valley) (or "Valley of the Sun"), [Tempe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempe,_Arizona), [Prescott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescott,_Arizona), and other areas. The Mormons settled what became [northern Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Arizona) and northern New Mexico. At the time these areas were in a part of the former [New Mexico Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico_Territory).

During the nineteenth century, a series of gold and silver rushes occurred in the territory, the best known being the 1870s stampede to the silver bonanzas of [Tombstone, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tombstone,_Arizona), in southeast Arizona, also known for its legendary outlaws and lawmen.[^cite_note-42] By the late 1880s, copper production eclipsed the precious metals with the rise of copper camps such as [Bisbee, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisbee,_Arizona), and [Jerome, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome,_Arizona).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] The boom and bust economy of mining also left hundreds of [ghost towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_towns) across the territory, but copper mining continued to prosper with the territory producing more copper than any other state by 1907, which earned Arizona the nickname "the Copper State" at the time of statehood.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] During the first years of statehood the industry experienced growing pains and labor disputes with the [Bisbee Deportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisbee_Deportation) of 1917, which was the result of a copper miners' strike.[^cite_note-47]

### 20th century to present

During the [Mexican Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Revolution) from 1910 to 1920, several battles were fought in the Mexican towns just across the border from Arizona settlements. Throughout the revolution, many Arizonans enlisted in one of the several armies fighting in Mexico. Only two significant engagements took place on U.S. soil between U.S. and Mexican forces: [Pancho Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancho_Villa)'s 1916 [Columbus Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Columbus_\(1916\)) in New Mexico and the [Battle of Ambos Nogales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ambos_Nogales) in 1918 in Arizona.

After Mexican federal troops fired on U.S. soldiers, the American garrison launched an assault into [Nogales, Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nogales,_Sonora). The Mexicans eventually surrendered after both sides sustained heavy casualties. A few months earlier, just west of Nogales, an Indian War battle had occurred, considered the last engagement in the [American Indian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Indian_Wars), which lasted from 1775 to 1918. U.S. soldiers stationed on the border confronted [Yaqui Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaqui_Indians) who were using Arizona as a base to raid the nearby Mexican settlements, as part of their wars against Mexico.

Arizona became a U.S. state on February 14, 1912, coinciding with [Valentine's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentine's_Day). Arizona was the [48th state admitted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_date_of_admission_to_the_Union) to the U.S. and the last of the [contiguous states](83759.md) to be admitted.[^cite_note-48]



[Eleanor Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_Roosevelt) at the Gila River relocation center, April 23, 1943

Cotton farming and copper mining, two of Arizona's most important statewide industries, suffered heavily during the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression).[^cite_note-49] During the 1920s and 1930s, tourism began to expand into a major economic pillar, developing into the prominent industry that characterizes the state today. Dude ranches, such as the K L Bar and Remuda in Wickenburg, along with the Flying V and Tanque Verde in Tucson, gave tourists the chance to take part in the flavor and activities of the "Old West". Several upscale hotels and resorts opened during this period, some of which are still top tourist draws. They include the [Arizona Biltmore Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Biltmore_Hotel) in central Phoenix (opened 1929) and the Wigwam Resort on the west side of the Phoenix area (opened 1936).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

Arizona was the site of German prisoner of war camps during World War II and [Japanese American internment camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_of_Japanese_Americans).[^cite_note-52] Because of wartime fears of a Japanese invasion of the [U.S. West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States) (which in fact materialized in the [Aleutian Islands Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleutian_Islands_Campaign) in June 1942), from 1942 to 1945, persons of Japanese descent were forced to reside in internment camps built in the interior of the country. Many lost their homes and businesses. The camps were abolished after World War II.[^cite_note-53]

The Phoenix-area [POW camp site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_war_camp) for Germans was purchased after the war by the [Maytag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maytag) family (of major [home appliance](185982.md) fame). It was developed as the site of the [Phoenix Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Zoo). A Japanese-American internment camp was on [Mount Lemmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lemmon), just outside the state's southeastern city of Tucson. Another [POW camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POW_camp) was near the [Gila River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gila_River) in eastern [Yuma County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuma_County,_Arizona). Arizona was also home to the [Phoenix Indian School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Indian_School_Park), one of several federal [Indian boarding schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_boarding_schools) designed to assimilate Native American children into mainstream European-American culture. Children were often enrolled in these schools against the wishes of their parents and families. Attempts to suppress native identities included forcing the children to cut their hair, to take and use English names, to speak only English, and to practice Christianity rather than their native religions.[^cite_note-54]

Numerous Native Americans from Arizona fought for the United States during World War II. Their experiences resulted in a rising activism in the postwar years to achieve better treatment and civil rights after their return to the state. After Maricopa County did not allow them to register to vote, in 1948 veteran Frank Harrison and Harry Austin, of the [Mojave-Apache Tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohave_people) at [Fort McDowell Indian Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_McDowell_Indian_Reservation), brought a legal suit, *[Harrison and Austin v. Laveen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_and_Austin_v._Laveen)*, to challenge this exclusion. The Arizona Supreme Court ruled in their favor.[^cite_note-HAvote-55]

Arizona's population grew tremendously with residential and business development after World War II, aided by the widespread use of [air conditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_conditioning), which made the intensely hot summers more comfortable. According to the *Arizona Blue Book* (published by the [Arizona Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Secretary_of_State)'s office each year), the state population in 1910 was 294,353. By 1970, it was 1,752,122. The percentage growth each decade averaged about 20% in the earlier decades, and about 60% each decade thereafter.

In the 1960s, [retirement communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retirement_communities) were developed. These age-restricted subdivisions catered exclusively to the needs of senior citizens and attracted many retirees who wanted to escape the harsh winters of the [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest) and the [Northeast](431669.md). [Sun City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_City,_Arizona), established by developer [Del Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Webb) and opened in 1960, was one of the first such communities. [Green Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Valley,_Arizona), south of Tucson, was another such community, designed as a retirement subdivision for Arizona's teachers. Many senior citizens from across the United States and Canada come to Arizona each winter and stay only during the winter months; they are referred to as [snowbirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowbird_\(people\)).

In March 2000, Arizona was the site of the first legally binding election ever held over the internet to nominate a candidate for public office.[^cite_note-56] In the 2000 Arizona Democratic Primary, under worldwide attention, [Al Gore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gore) defeated [Bill Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Bradley). Voter turnout in this state primary increased more than 500% over the 1996 primary.

In the 21st century, Arizona has frequently garnered national attention for its efforts to quell illegal immigration into the state. In 2004, voters passed [Proposition 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Arizona_Proposition_200), requiring proof of citizenship to register to vote. The [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md) struck this restriction down in 2013.[^cite_note-57] In 2010, Arizona enacted [SB 1070](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_SB_1070) which required all immigrants to carry immigration papers at all times, but the Supreme Court also invalidated parts of this law in *[Arizona v. United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_v._United_States)* in 2012.[^cite_note-58]

On January 8, 2011, a [gunman shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Tucson_shooting) congresswoman [Gabby Giffords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabby_Giffords) and 18 others at a gathering in Tucson. Giffords was critically wounded. The incident sparked national attention regarding incendiary political rhetoric.[^cite_note-59]

Three ships named [USS *Arizona*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Arizona) have been christened in honor of the state.

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Arizona)

The [Grand Canyon](46989.md)



Arizona municipalities

Arizona is in the Southwestern United States as one of the [Four Corners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Corners) states. Arizona is the sixth [largest state by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area), ranked after [New Mexico](21649.md) and before [Nevada](21216.md). Of the state's 113,998 square miles (295,000 km<sup>2</sup>), approximately 15% is privately owned. The remaining area is public forest and parkland, [state trust land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_Trust_Land) and Native American reservations. There are 24 [National Park Service](161535.md) maintained sites in Arizona, including the three national parks of [Grand Canyon National Park](46986.md), [Saguaro National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saguaro_National_Park), and the [Petrified Forest National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrified_Forest_National_Park).[^cite_note-60]

Arizona is well known for its [desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert) [Basin and Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basin_and_Range_Province) region in the state's southern portions, which is rich in a [landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape) of [xerophyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xerophyte) plants such as the [cactus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cactus). This region's topography was shaped by prehistoric [volcanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanism), followed by the cooling-off and related [subsidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidence). Its climate has exceptionally hot summers and mild winters. The state is less well known for its pine-covered north-central portion of the high country of the [Colorado Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Plateau) (see [Arizona Mountains forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Mountains_forests)).

Like other states of the [Southwest](179571.md) United States, Arizona is marked by high mountains, the Colorado plateau, and mesas. Despite the state's aridity, 27% of Arizona is forest,[^cite_note-61] a percentage comparable to modern-day Romania or Greece.[^cite_note-62] The world's largest stand of [ponderosa pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponderosa_pine) trees is in Arizona.[^cite_note-63]

The [Mogollon Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogollon_Rim) ([/moʊɡəˈjoʊn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)), a 1,998-foot (609 m) [escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escarpment), cuts across the state's central section and marks the southwestern edge of the Colorado Plateau. In 2002, this was an area of the [Rodeo–Chediski Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodeo%E2%80%93Chediski_Fire), the worst fire in state history until 2011.

Located in northern Arizona, the [Grand Canyon](46989.md) is a colorful, deep, steep-sided gorge, carved by the [Colorado River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_River_\(U.S.\)). The canyon is one of the [Seven Natural Wonders of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Natural_Wonders_of_the_World) and is largely contained in the [Grand Canyon National Park](46986.md) – one of the first national parks in the United States. President [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) was a major proponent of designating the Grand Canyon area as a National Park, often visiting to hunt [mountain lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cougar) and enjoy the scenery. The canyon was created by the Colorado River cutting a channel over millions of years, and is about 277 miles (446 km) long, ranges in width from 4 to 18 miles (6 to 29 km) and attains a depth of more than 1 mile (1.6 km). Nearly two billion years of the Earth's history have been exposed as the Colorado River and its tributaries cut through layer after layer of sediment as the Colorado Plateau uplifted.

Arizona is home to one of the most well-preserved [meteorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorite) impact sites in the world. Created around 50,000 years ago, the Barringer Meteorite Crater (better known simply as "[Meteor Crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteor_Crater)") is a gigantic hole in the middle of the high plains of the Colorado Plateau, about 25 miles (40 km) west of [Winslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winslow,_Arizona).[^cite_note-64] A rim of smashed and jumbled boulders, some of them the size of small houses, rises 150 feet (46 m) above the level of the surrounding plain. The crater itself is nearly a mile (1.6 kilometers) wide and 570 feet (170 m) deep.

Arizona is one of two U.S. states, along with Hawaii, that does not observe [Daylight Saving Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daylight_Saving_Time), though the large [Navajo Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navajo_Nation) in the state's northeastern region does.

[Phoenix](49121.md), in [Maricopa County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maricopa_County,_Arizona), is Arizona's capital and largest city. Other prominent cities in the Phoenix metro area include [Mesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesa,_Arizona) (Arizona's third largest city), [Chandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandler,_Arizona) (Arizona's fourth largest city), [Glendale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glendale,_Arizona), [Peoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoria,_Arizona), [Buckeye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckeye,_Arizona), [Sun City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_City,_Arizona), [Sun City West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_City_West,_Arizona), [Fountain Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain_Hills,_Arizona), [Surprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surprise,_Arizona), [Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert,_Arizona), [El Mirage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Mirage,_Arizona), [Avondale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avondale,_Arizona), [Tempe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempe,_Arizona), [Tolleson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolleson,_Arizona) and [Scottsdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottsdale,_Arizona), with a total metropolitan population of just over 4.7 million.[^cite_note-65] The average high temperature in July, 106 °F (41 °C), is one of the highest of any metropolitan area in the United States, offset by an average January high temperature of 67 °F (19 °C), the basis of its winter appeal.

[Tucson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson,_Arizona), with a metro population of just over one million, is the state's second-largest city. Located in [Pima County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pima_County,_Arizona), approximately 110 miles (180 km) southeast of Phoenix, it was incorporated in 1877, making it the oldest incorporated city in Arizona. It is home to the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona). Major incorporated suburbs of Tucson include [Oro Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oro_Valley,_Arizona) and [Marana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marana,_Arizona) northwest of the city, [Sahuarita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahuarita,_Arizona) south of the city, and [South Tucson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Tucson,_Arizona) in an enclave south of downtown. It has an average July temperature of 100 °F (38 °C) and winter temperatures averaging 65 °F (18 °C). [Saguaro National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saguaro_National_Park), just west of the city in the [Tucson Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson_Mountains), is the site of the world's largest collection of [Saguaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saguaro) cacti.

The [Prescott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescott,_Arizona) metropolitan area includes the cities of Prescott, [Cottonwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottonwood,_Arizona), [Camp Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Verde,_Arizona) and many other towns in the 8,123 square miles (21,000 km<sup>2</sup>) of [Yavapai County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yavapai_County) area. With 212,635 residents, this cluster of towns is the state's third largest metropolitan area. The city of Prescott (population 41,528) lies approximately 100 miles (160 km) northwest of the Phoenix metropolitan area. Situated in pine tree forests at an elevation of about 5,500 feet (1,700 m), Prescott enjoys a much cooler climate than Phoenix, with average summer highs around 88 °F (31 °C) and winter temperatures averaging 50 °F (10 °C).

[Yuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuma,_Arizona) is the center of the fourth-largest metropolitan area in Arizona. Located in [Yuma County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuma_County,_Arizona), it is near the borders of California and Mexico. It is one of the hottest cities in the United States, with an average July high of 107 °F (42 °C). For comparison, the same month's average in [Death Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Valley) is 115 °F (46 °C). The city features sunny days about 90% of the year. The Yuma [Metropolitan Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Statistical_Area) has a population of 160,000. Yuma attracts many winter visitors from all over the United States.

[Flagstaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagstaff,_Arizona), in [Coconino County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconino_County,_Arizona), is the largest city in northern Arizona, and is at an elevation of nearly 7,000 feet (2,100 m). With its large Ponderosa pine forests, snowy winter weather and picturesque mountains, it is a stark contrast to the desert regions typically associated with Arizona. It is sited at the base of the [San Francisco Peaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Peaks), the highest mountain range in the state of Arizona, which contains [Humphreys Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphreys_Peak), the highest point in Arizona at 12,633 feet (3,851 m). Flagstaff has a strong tourism sector, due to its proximity to numerous tourist attractions including: [Grand Canyon National Park](46986.md), [Sedona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedona,_Arizona), and [Oak Creek Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Creek_Canyon). Historic [U.S. Route 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_66) is the main east–west street in the town. The Flagstaff metropolitan area is home to 134,421 residents and the main campus of [Northern Arizona University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Arizona_University).

[Lake Havasu City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Havasu_City,_Arizona), in [Mohave County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohave_County,_Arizona), known as "Arizona's playground", was developed on the Colorado River and is named after Lake Havasu. Lake Havasu City has a population of about 57,000 people. It is famous for huge spring break parties, sunsets and the [London Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Bridge), relocated from London, England. Lake Havasu City was founded by real estate developer [Robert P. McCulloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_P._McCulloch) in 1963.[^cite_note-66] It has two colleges, [Mohave Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohave_Community_College) and ASU Colleges in Lake Havasu City.[^cite_note-67]

## Climate

Further information: [Climate change in Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Arizona)



[Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) climate map of Arizona.

Due to its large area and variations in elevation, the state has a wide variety of localized climate conditions. In the lower elevations the climate is primarily desert, with mild winters and extremely hot summers. Typically, from late fall to early spring, the weather is mild, averaging a minimum of 60 °F (16 °C). November through February are the coldest months, with temperatures typically ranging from 40 to 75 °F (4 to 24 °C), with occasional frosts.[^cite_note-wrcc-69]

About midway through February, the temperatures start to rise, with warm days, and cool, breezy nights. The summer months of June through September bring a dry heat from 90 to 120 °F (32 to 49 °C), with occasional high temperatures exceeding 125 °F (52 °C) having been observed in the desert area.[^cite_note-wrcc-69] Arizona's all-time record high is 128 °F (53 °C) recorded at [Lake Havasu City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Havasu_City,_Arizona) on June 29, 1994, and July 5, 2007; the all-time record low of −40 °F (−40 °C) was recorded at [Hawley Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawley_Lake,_Arizona) on January 7, 1971.[^cite_note-70]

Due to the primarily dry climate, large diurnal temperature variations occur in less-developed areas of the desert above 2,500 ft (760 m). The swings can be as large as 83 °F (46 °C) in the summer months. In the state's urban centers, the effects of [local warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island) result in much higher measured night-time lows than in the recent past.

Arizona has an average annual rainfall of 12.7 in (323 mm),[^cite_note-71] which comes during two rainy seasons, with [cold fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_front) coming from the Pacific Ocean during the winter and a [monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Monsoon) in the summer.[^cite_note-USGShyd-72] The monsoon season occurs toward the end of summer. In July or August, the [dewpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewpoint) rises dramatically for a brief period. During this time, the air contains large amounts of [water vapor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_vapor). Dewpoints as high as 81 °F (27 °C)[^cite_note-Wunderground_archive_of_PHX_airport_data-73] have been recorded during the [Phoenix](49121.md) monsoon season. This hot moisture brings [lightning](61344.md), [thunderstorms](70807.md), wind, and torrential, if usually brief, downpours. These downpours often cause [flash floods](244391.md), which can turn deadly. In an attempt to deter drivers from crossing flooding streams, the [Arizona Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Legislature) enacted the [Stupid Motorist Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stupid_Motorist_Law). It is rare for [tornadoes](37530.md) or [hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Arizona_hurricanes) to occur in Arizona.

Arizona's northern third is a [plateau](538005.md) at significantly higher altitudes than the lower desert, and has an appreciably cooler climate, with cold winters and mild summers, though the climate remains semiarid to arid. Extremely low temperatures are not unknown; cold air systems from the northern states and Canada occasionally push into the state, bringing temperatures below 0 °F (−18 °C) to the state's northern parts.[^cite_note-74]

Indicative of the variation in climate, Arizona is the state which has both the metropolitan area with the most days over 100 °F (38 °C) ([Phoenix](49121.md)), and the metropolitan area in the lower 48 states with the most days with a low temperature below freezing ([Flagstaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagstaff,_Arizona)).[^cite_note-75]

The [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) records Arizona's population as 7,151,502 in the 2020 census,[^cite_note-Census2010-78] a 12% increase since the [2010 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census).[^cite_note-Census2010-78]

Arizona remained sparsely settled for most of the 19th century.[^cite_note-80] The 1860 census reported the population of "Arizona County" to be 6,482, of whom 4,040 were listed as "Indians", 21 as "free colored", and 2,421 as "white".[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] Arizona's continued [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) has put an enormous stress on the state's water supply.[^cite_note-83] As of 2011, 61% of Arizona's children under age one belonged to racial groups of color.[^cite_note-84]

The population of metropolitan Phoenix increased by 45% from 1991 through 2001, helping to make Arizona the second fastest-growing state in the U.S. in the 1990s (the fastest was [Nevada](21216.md)).[^cite_note-85]

According to the 2010 United States census, Arizona had a population of 6,392,017. In 2010, [illegal immigrants](7124412.md) constituted an estimated 8% of the population. This was the second highest percentage of any state in the U.S.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

Metropolitan Phoenix (4.7 million) and Tucson (1.0 million) are home to about five-sixths of Arizona's people (as of the 2010 census). Metro Phoenix alone accounts for two-thirds of the state's population.

According to [HUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Housing_and_Urban_Development)'s 2022 [Annual Homeless Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Homeless_Assessment_Report_to_Congress), there were an estimated 13,553 [homeless](19394651.md) people living in Arizona.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

As of 2018, the top countries of origin for Arizona's immigrant population were Mexico, Canada, India, the [Philippines](23440.md) and China.[^cite_note-90]

## Economy

See also: [List of Arizona locations by per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Arizona_locations_by_per_capita_income)

This section is an excerpt from [Economy of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Arizona).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Economy_of_Arizona\&action=edit)]



Arizona's [Meteor Crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteor_Crater) is a tourist attraction.

The economy of Arizona had a total [gross state product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_state_product) in 2025 of \$598.1 billion.[^cite_note-117] The composition of the state's economy is moderately diverse, although health care, transportation and the government remain the largest sectors.[^cite_note-118] Early in its history, Arizona's economy relied on the "five C's": copper, cotton, cattle, [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus), and [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate).[^cite_note-119] Copper is still extensively mined from many expansive open-pit and underground mines, accounting for two-thirds of the nation's output.[^cite_note-120]

As of 2025, Arizona's largest trading partners include Mexico, Taiwan, Canada, and China.[^cite_note-Economy_of_Arizona_\:1-121]

Arizona's per capita personal income was \$68,283 in 2025, ranking 36th in the U.S.[^cite_note-122] The state's 2023 [median household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_household_income) was \$74,568, ranking 19th in the country and just below the U.S. national mean.[^cite_note-Economy_of_Arizona_u8582-123]

### Agriculture



[Romaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romaine_lettuce) farms in [Yuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuma,_Arizona)

Multiple crops are grown in Arizona, including [durum wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durum_wheat), [lettuce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaf_lettuce), [spinach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinach), [cantaloupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantaloupe), [broccoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broccoli), [cauliflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauliflower), [cabbage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabbage), and [watermelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watermelon).[^cite_note-quick-stats-124]

Federal [crop insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop_insurance) is available for grape (*[Vitis vinifera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitis_vinifera)* and other *[Vitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitis)* spp.) here.[^cite_note-Federal-insurance-gr-125] Together with [California's crop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_grape) it falls under special provisions of the relevant crop insurance statutes.[^cite_note-Federal-insurance-gr-125] [Insect pests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect_pest_of_grape?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disease_of_grape) are [covered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop_insurance_coverage), excluding Phylloxera (*[Daktulosphaira vitifoliae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daktulosphaira_vitifoliae)*) or failure to correctly [apply insect control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_of_insects) or [apply disease control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application_of_pesticide).[^cite_note-Federal-insurance-gr-125]

The whitefly [*Bemisia tabaci* B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bemisia_tabaci_B) was introduced through the [poinsettia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poinsettia) trade in the 1980s, displacing the previous [A biotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bemisia_tabaci_A).[^cite_note-White-Fly-126] In 2004 the [Q biotype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bemisia_tabaci_Q) (from the Mediterranean) was first found here, also on poinsettia.[^cite_note-White-Fly-126]

The Colorado potato beetle (*[Leptinotarsa decemlineata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leptinotarsa_decemlineata)*) is either native or an early [introduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduced_species) here.[^cite_note-L-dec-127] Unusually, the population here commonly feeds on Silverleaf nightshade (*[Solanum elaeagnifolium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solanum_elaeagnifolium)*), which is usually a less attractive host for this beetle.[^cite_note-L-dec-127] The CPB is an occasional pest of tomato.[^cite_note-L-dec-127]

### Public transportation, Amtrak, and intercity bus

See also: [List of passenger train stations in Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_passenger_train_stations_in_Arizona)

The Phoenix and Tucson metropolitan areas are served by public bus transit systems. Yuma and Flagstaff also have public bus systems. [Greyhound Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines) serves Phoenix, Tucson, Flagstaff, Yuma, and several smaller communities statewide.

A [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) system, called [Valley Metro Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_Metro_Rail), was completed in December 2008; it connects Central Phoenix with the nearby cities of Mesa and Tempe.[^cite_note-129]

In Tucson, the [Sun Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Link) streetcar system travels through the downtown area, connecting the main [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona) campus with Mercado San Agustin on the western edge of downtown Tucson. Sun Link, loosely based on the [Portland Streetcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Streetcar), launched in July 2014.[^cite_note-130]

[Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) *[Southwest Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Chief)* route serves the northern part of the state, stopping at [Winslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winslow_\(Amtrak_station\)), [Flagstaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagstaff_\(Amtrak_station\)), [Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Junction_\(Amtrak_station\)) and [Kingman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingman_\(Amtrak_station\)). The *[Texas Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Eagle)* and *[Sunset Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset_Limited)* routes serve South-Central Arizona, stopping at [Tucson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson_\(Amtrak_station\)), [Maricopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maricopa_\(Amtrak_station\)), [Yuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuma_\(Amtrak_station\)) and [Benson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benson_\(Amtrak_station\)). Phoenix lost Amtrak service in 1996 with the rerouting of the *Sunset Limited*, and now an Amtrak bus runs between Phoenix and the station in Maricopa. As of 2021, Amtrak has proposed to restore rail service between Phoenix and Tucson.[^cite_note-131]

### Capitol complex



The original [Arizona State Capitol](2181578.md) in [Phoenix](49121.md)

The capital of Arizona is [Phoenix](49121.md). The original [Capitol building](2181578.md), with its distinctive copper dome, was dedicated in 1901 (construction was completed for \$136,000 in 1900) when the area was a territory. Phoenix became the official state capital with Arizona's admission to the union in 1912.[^cite_note-132]

The [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_House_of_Representatives) and [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Senate) buildings were dedicated in 1960, and an Executive Office Building was dedicated in 1974 (the ninth floor of this building is where the Office of the Governor is located). The original Capitol building was converted into a museum.

The Capitol complex is fronted and highlighted by the richly landscaped [Wesley Bolin Memorial Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesley_Bolin_Memorial_Plaza), named after [Wesley Bolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesley_Bolin), a governor who died in office in the 1970s. The site also includes many monuments and memorials, including the anchor and signal mast from the [USS *Arizona*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Arizona_\(BB-39\)) (one of the U.S. Navy ships [sunk in Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor)) and a granite version of the [Ten Commandments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Commandments).

### State legislative branch

The [Arizona Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Legislature) is [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) and consists of a thirty-member Senate and a 60-member House of Representatives. Each of the thirty legislative districts has one senator and two representatives. Legislators are elected for two-year terms.[^cite_note-133]

Each Legislature covers a two-year period. The first session following the general election is known as the first regular session, and the session convening in the second year is known as the second regular session. Each regular session begins on the second Monday in January and adjourns *sine die* (terminates for the year) no later than Saturday of the week in which the 100th day from the beginning of the regular session falls. The President of the Senate and Speaker of the House, by rule, may extend the session up to seven additional days. Thereafter, the session can be extended only by a majority vote of members present of each house.

The majority party is the [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Republican_Party), which has held power in both houses since 1993. The [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Democratic_Party) picked up several legislative seats in recent elections, bringing both chambers one seat away from being equally divided as of 2021.[^cite_note-134]

Arizona state senators and representatives are elected for two-year terms and are limited to four consecutive terms in a chamber, though there is no limit on the total number of terms. When a lawmaker is term-limited from office, it is common for him or her to run for election in the other chamber.

Arizona's executive branch is headed by a [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Arizona), who is elected to a four-year term. The governor may serve any number of terms, though no more than two in a row. Arizona is one of the few states that has no governor's mansion. During their term, the governors reside within their private residence, with executive offices housed in the executive tower at the state capitol. The governor of Arizona is [Katie Hobbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Hobbs) (D).

Governor [Jan Brewer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Brewer) assumed office in 2009 after [Janet Napolitano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Napolitano) had her nomination by Barack Obama for [Secretary of Homeland Security](212926.md) confirmed by the Senate.[^cite_note-135] Arizona has had four female governors and a fifth currently serving, more than any other state.

Other elected executive officials include the [Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_\(U.S._state_government\)), [State Treasurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Treasurer), [State Attorney General](4316853.md), [Superintendent of Public Instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_education_agency), [State Mine Inspector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_Mine_Inspector), and a five-member [Corporation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Corporation_Commission). All elected officials hold a term of four years, and are limited to two consecutive terms (except the office of the State Mine Inspector, which is limited to four terms).[^cite_note-136]

Arizona has the distinction of having the most female governors of any state with five having served, the first and only state to have three consecutive female governors,[^cite_note-137] and in 1998 elected women to all five top, statewide elected offices with the election of [Jane Dee Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Dee_Hull) as Governor, [Betsey Bayless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betsey_Bayless) as Secretary of State, [Janet Napolitano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Napolitano) as Attorney General, [Carol Springer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Springer) as Treasurer, and [Lisa Graham Keegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Graham_Keegan) as Superintendent of Public Instruction.[^cite_note-138]

Arizona is one of five states that do not have a [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_governor_\(United_States\)#Arizona). The elected secretary of state is first in line to succeed the governor in the event of death, disability, resignation, or removal from office. If appointed, the Secretary of State is not eligible and the next governor is selected from the next eligible official in the line of succession, including the attorney general, state treasurer, and superintendent of public instruction. Since 1977, four secretaries of state and one attorney general have succeeded to the state's governorship.

On November 8, 2022, Arizona voters approved a state constitutional amendment (Proposition 131) that created the position and office of the lieutenant governor beginning with the 2026 elections. The position will be elected on a joint ticket with the governor. The lieutenant governor ascends to the governorship if the incumbent governor dies, resigns, or is removed (via impeachment conviction) from office. The proposition, through a law pre-passed by the state legislature, also tasks the governor with assigning a job to her or his running mate, such as chief of staff, the director of the state Department of Administration, or "any position" to which the governor can appoint someone by law.[^cite_note-139]

### State judicial branch

The [Arizona Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Supreme_Court) is the highest court in Arizona, consisting of a chief justice, a vice chief justice, and five associate justices. Justices are appointed by the governor from a list recommended by a bipartisan commission and must be sustained in office by election after the first two years following their appointment. Subsequent sustaining elections occur every six years. The supreme court has appellate jurisdiction in death penalty cases, but nearly all other appellate cases go through the [Arizona Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Court_of_Appeals) first. The court has original jurisdiction in a few other circumstances, as outlined in the state constitution. The court meets in the Arizona Supreme Court Building at the capitol complex (at the southern end of Wesley Bolin Plaza).

The Arizona Court of Appeals, subdivided into two divisions, is the intermediate court in the state. Division One is based in Phoenix, consists of nineteen judges, and has jurisdiction in the Western and Northern regions of the state, along with the greater Phoenix area. Division Two is based in Tucson, consists of nine judges, and has jurisdiction over the Southern regions of the state, including the Tucson area. Judges are selected in a method similar to the one used for state supreme court justices.

Each county of Arizona has a [superior court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Superior_Court), the size and organization of which are varied and generally depend on the size of the particular county.

From statehood through the late 1940s, Arizona was primarily dominated by the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Democratic_Party). During this time, the Democratic candidate for the presidency carried the state each election, the only exceptions being the elections of [1920](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_United_States_presidential_election), [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_United_States_presidential_election) and [1928](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_United_States_presidential_election) – all three were national [Republican](32070.md) landslides.

In 1924, Congress had passed a law granting citizenship and suffrage to all Native Americans, some of whom had previously been excluded as members of tribes on reservations. Legal interpretations of Arizona's constitution prohibited Native Americans living on reservations from voting, classifying them as being under "guardianship".[^cite_note-HAvote-55] This interpretation was overturned as being incorrect and unconstitutional in 1948 by the Arizona Supreme Court, following a lawsuit by World War II Indian veterans [Frank Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Harrison_\(soldier\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Harry Austin, both of the [Fort McDowell Yavapai Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_McDowell_Yavapai_Nation). The landmark case is *[Harrison and Austin v. Laveen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_and_Austin_v._Laveen)*. After the men were refused the opportunity to register in Maricopa County, they filed lawsuit against the registrar. The [National Congress of American Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_of_American_Indians), the [Department of Justice](52563.md), the [Department of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_the_Interior), and the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union) all filed *amicus curiae* (friends of the court) briefs in the case. The State Supreme Court established the rights of Native Americans to vote in the state; at the time, they comprised about 11% of the population.[^cite_note-HAvote-55] That year, a similar provision was overturned in New Mexico when challenged by another Indian veteran in court. These were the only two states that had continued to prohibit Native Americans from voting.[^cite_note-NAvote-142][^cite_note-HAvote-55]

Arizona voted Republican in every presidential election from 1952 to 1992, with [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) and [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) winning the state by particularly large margins. During this forty-year span, it was the only state not to be carried by a Democrat at least once.

Democrat [Lyndon Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_Johnson), in [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_United_States_presidential_election), lost the state by fewer than 5,000 votes to Arizona Senator and native [Barry Goldwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Goldwater). (This was the most closely contested state in an otherwise a landslide victory for Johnson that year.) Democrat [Bill Clinton](3356.md) ended this streak in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_United_States_presidential_election), when he won Arizona by a little over two percentage points (Clinton had previously come within less than two percent of winning Arizona's electoral votes in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_United_States_presidential_election)). From 2000 until 2016, the majority of the state continued to support Republican presidential candidates by solid margins. In the [2020 United States presidential election](48410011.md), [Joe Biden](145422.md) again broke the streak by becoming the first Democratic presidential candidate to win Arizona since 1996 by 0.3% of the vote.[^cite_note-143] Republican Donald Trump won the state in [2024](59568546.md).[^cite_note-144]

Since the mid 20th century, the Republican Party has also dominated Arizona politics in general. The fast-growing Phoenix and Tucson suburbs became reliably Republican areas from the 1950s onward. During this time, many "Pinto Democrats", or conservative Democrats from rural areas, became increasingly willing to support Republicans at the state and national level. While the state normally supports Republicans at the federal level, Democrats are often competitive in statewide elections. Two of the last six governors have been Democrats.

On March 4, 2008, Senator [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain) effectively clinched the Republican nomination for 2008, becoming the first major party presidential nominee from the state since Barry Goldwater in 1964. McCain won Arizona by eight percentage points in the 2008 General Election.

Arizona politics is dominated by a longstanding rivalry between its two largest counties, [Maricopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maricopa_County) and [Pima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pima_County) – home to Phoenix and Tucson, respectively. The two counties have almost 75 percent of the state's population and cast almost 80 percent of the state's vote. They also elect a substantial majority of the state legislature.

Maricopa County is home to almost 60 percent of the state's population, and most of the state's elected officials live there. Before [Joe Biden](145422.md) won Maricopa County in 2020, it had voted Republican in every presidential election since 1952. This includes the [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_United_States_presidential_election) run of native son Barry Goldwater; he would not have carried his home state without his 20,000-vote margin in Maricopa County.

In the 2024 General Election, former president Donald Trump won Maricopa County by 71,515 votes and won the state of Arizona by 187,382 votes, making the 5.5 percent statewide victory the largest percentage win of any of the seven 2024 swing states.[^cite_note-145]

Pima County, home to Tucson, and most of southern Arizona have historically voted more Democratic. While Tucson's suburbs lean Republican, they hold to a somewhat more moderate brand of Republicanism than is common in the Phoenix area.



[Arizona teacher's strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Arizona_teachers'_strike) and rally on April 26, 2018

In 2010, Arizona adopted [SB 1070](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_SB_1070), called the "toughest [immigration](15051.md) law" in the United States. A fierce debate erupted between supporters and detractors of SB 1070.[^cite_note-146] The [United States Supreme Court](31737.md) struck down portions of the Arizona law, which required all immigrants to carry immigration papers at all times, in *[Arizona v. United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_v._United_States)*.[^cite_note-147]

The [West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_West_Virginia_teachers'_strike) teachers' strike in 2018 inspired [teachers in other states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_education_workers'_strikes_in_the_United_States), including [Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Arizona_teachers'_strike), to take similar action.[^cite_note-148]

Arizona retains the death penalty. Authorized methods of execution include the [gas chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_chamber).[^cite_note-149]

In 2024, Arizona voters passed a [constitutional amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Arizona_Proposition_139) recognising abortion access within the Arizona state constitution,[^cite_note-150] becoming the southernmost continental state to codify abortion rights.[^cite_note-151]

### Same-sex marriage and civil unions

Arizona was initially the first U.S. state to reject a same-sex marriage ban in 2006 (proposition failed, 48%–52%) but passed a constitutional amendment prohibiting same-sex marriage two years later (56%–44%) with proponents enjoying a 10-to-1 advertising advantage and benefitting from a broadcast message from [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) Bishop [Thomas Olmsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Olmsted) of Phoenix, calling the issue "non-negotiable" for Catholics and played at every mass in its jurisdiction.[^cite_note-152] Several cities created civil unions in the years following the marriage ban.[^cite_note-153] Following a federal district court ruling in October 2014, Arizona's Attorney General stopped objecting to same-sex marriage, making Arizona the 31st state to legalize it.[^cite_note-154] Public opinion has shifted significantly, with 2023 polling showing overwhelming support for same-sex marriage among Arizonans.[^cite_note-155]

## Education

### Elementary and secondary education

See also: [List of school districts in Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_school_districts_in_Arizona) and [List of high schools in Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_schools_in_Arizona)



[Arizona School Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_school_districts_in_Arizona)

Public schools in Arizona are separated into about 220 local school districts which operate independently, but are governed in most cases by elected county school superintendents; these are in turn overseen by the Arizona State Board of Education and the [Arizona Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Department_of_Education). A state [Superintendent of Public Instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Superintendent_of_Public_Instruction) (elected in partisan elections every even-numbered year when there is not a presidential election, for a four-year term). In 2005, a School District Redistricting Commission was established with the goal of combining and consolidating many of these districts.[^cite_note-156]

### Higher education



The [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona) (the Mall) in [Tucson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson,_Arizona)



[Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University) (a biodesign building) in [Tempe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempe,_Arizona)



[Northern Arizona University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Arizona_University) (The Skydome) in [Flagstaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagstaff,_Arizona)

Arizona is served by three public universities: The [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona), [Arizona State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_University), and [Northern Arizona University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Arizona_University). These schools are governed by the [Arizona Board of Regents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Board_of_Regents).

Private higher education in Arizona is dominated by a large number of for-profit and "chain" (multi-site) universities.[^cite_note-157]

[Embry–Riddle Aeronautical University, Prescott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embry%E2%80%93Riddle_Aeronautical_University,_Prescott) and [Prescott College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescott_College) are Arizona's only non-profit four-year private colleges.[^cite_note-158]

Arizona has a wide network of two-year vocational schools and [community colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_colleges). These colleges were governed historically by a separate statewide board of directors but, in 2002, the state legislature transferred almost all oversight authority to individual community college districts.[^cite_note-159] The Maricopa County Community College District includes 11 community colleges throughout Maricopa County and is one of the largest in the nation.

### Visual arts and museums

See also: [List of museums in Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Arizona)

[Phoenix Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Art_Museum), on the historic [Central Avenue Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Avenue_Corridor) in Phoenix, is the Southwest's largest collection of visual art from across the world. The museum displays international exhibitions alongside the museum's collection of more than 18,000 works of American, Asian, European, Latin American, Western American, modern and contemporary art, and fashion design. With a community education mandate since 1951, Phoenix Art Museum holds a year-round program of festivals, live performances, independent art films and educational programs. The museum also has PhxArtKids, an interactive space for children; photography exhibitions through the museum's partnership with the [Center for Creative Photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Creative_Photography); the landscaped Sculpture Garden and dining at Arcadia Farms.[^cite_note-161]

Arizona is a recognized center of Native American art, with a number of galleries showcasing historical and contemporary works. The [Heard Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heard_Museum), also in Phoenix, is a major repository of Native American art. Some of the signature exhibits include a full Navajo hogan, the Mareen Allen Nichols Collection containing 260 pieces of contemporary jewelry, the [Barry Goldwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Goldwater) Collection of 437 historic [Hopi kachina dolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopi_kachina_dolls), and an exhibit on the 19th-century boarding school experiences of Native Americans. The Heard Museum has about 250,000 visitors a year.

[Sedona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedona,_Arizona), [Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome,_Arizona), and [Tubac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubac,_Arizona) are known as budding artist colonies, and small arts scenes exist in the larger cities and near the state universities.[^cite_note-162]

### Music

Main article: [Music of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Arizona)

Arizona is prominently featured in the lyrics of many [Country and Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_and_Western) songs, such as [Jamie O'Neal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_O'Neal)'s hit ballad "[There Is No Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_Is_No_Arizona)". [George Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Strait)'s "Oceanfront Property" uses "ocean front property in Arizona" as a metaphor for a sucker proposition. The line "see you down in Arizona Bay" is used in a [Tool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool_\(band\)) song in reference to the possibility (expressed as a *hope* by comedian [Bill Hicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Hicks)) that Southern California will one day fall into the ocean. [Glen Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Campbell), a notable resident, popularized the song "[By The Time I Get To Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By_The_Time_I_Get_To_Phoenix)".



[Standin' on the Corner Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standin'_on_the_Corner_Park) and mural in [Winslow, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winslow,_Arizona)

"Arizona" was the title of a popular song recorded by [Mark Lindsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Lindsay). Arizona is mentioned by the hit song "[Take It Easy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_It_Easy)", written by [Jackson Browne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_Browne) and [Glenn Frey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Frey) and performed by the [Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagles_\(band\)). Arizona is also mentioned in the Beatles' song "Get Back", credited to John Lennon and Paul McCartney; McCartney sings: "JoJo left his home in Tucson, Arizona, for some California grass." "[Carefree Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carefree_Highway_\(song\))", released in 1974 by [Gordon Lightfoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Lightfoot), takes its name from [Arizona State Route 74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_Route_74) north of Phoenix.[^cite_note-Crawdaddy-163]

Arizona's music scene is helped by emerging bands, as well as some well-known artists. The [Gin Blossoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gin_Blossoms), [Chronic Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_Future), [Roger Clyne and the Peacemakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Clyne_and_the_Peacemakers), [Jimmy Eat World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Eat_World), [Caroline's Spine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline's_Spine), and others began their careers in Arizona. Also, a number of [punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock) and rock bands got their start in Arizona, including [JFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JFA_\(band\)), [The Feederz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Feederz), [Sun City Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_City_Girls), [The Meat Puppets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meat_Puppets), [The Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Maine_\(band\)), [The Summer Set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Summer_Set), and more recently [Authority Zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authority_Zero) and [Digital Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Summer).

Arizona also has many singers and other musicians. Singer, songwriter and guitarist [Michelle Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Branch) is from [Sedona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedona,_Arizona). [Chester Bennington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_Bennington), the former lead vocalist of [Linkin Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linkin_Park), and [mash-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashup_\(music\)) artist [DJ Z-Trip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Z-Trip) are both from [Phoenix](49121.md). One of Arizona's better known musicians is [shock rocker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_rock) [Alice Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Cooper), who helped define the genre. [Maynard James Keenan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maynard_James_Keenan), the lead singer of the bands [Tool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool_\(band\)), [A Perfect Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Perfect_Circle), and [Puscifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puscifer), calls the town of [Cornville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornville,_Arizona) home.

Other notable singers include [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_music) singers [Dierks Bentley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dierks_Bentley) and [Marty Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_Robbins), [folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music) singer [Katie Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Lee_\(singer\)), [Fleetwood Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleetwood_Mac)'s [Stevie Nicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevie_Nicks), [CeCe Peniston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CeCe_Peniston), [Rex Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rex_Allen), 2007 *[American Idol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Idol_\(season_6\))* winner [Jordin Sparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordin_Sparks), and [Linda Ronstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Ronstadt).

Arizona is also known for its [heavy metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music) scene, which is centered in and around Phoenix. In the early to mid-1990s, it included bands such as [Job for a Cowboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_for_a_Cowboy), [Knights of the Abyss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_of_the_Abyss), [Greeley Estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeley_Estates), [Eyes Set To Kill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyes_Set_To_Kill), [blessthefall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blessthefall), [The Word Alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Word_Alive), [The Dead Rabbitts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dead_Rabbitts), and [Abigail Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abigail_Williams_\(band\)). The bands [Soulfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soulfly) and [Gatecreeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatecreeper) calls Phoenix home, and [Megadeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadeth) lived in Phoenix for about a decade. Around 2009, Phoenix began to host a burgeoning desert rock and sludge metal underground, (ala' Kyuss in 1990s California) led by bands like Wolves of Winter, Asimov, and Dead Canyon.

American composer [Elliott Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Carter) composed his first String Quartet (1950–51) while on sabbatical (from New York) in Arizona. The quartet won a [Pulitzer Prize](24230.md) and other awards and is now a staple of the string quartet repertoire.

### College sports

College sports are also prevalent in Arizona. The [Arizona State Sun Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_State_Sun_Devils) and the [Arizona Wildcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Wildcats) belong to the [Big-12 Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big-12_Conference) while the [Northern Arizona Lumberjacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Arizona_Lumberjacks) compete in the [Big Sky Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Sky_Conference) and the [Grand Canyon Antelopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canyon_Antelopes) compete in the [Mountain West Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_West_Conference). The rivalry between Arizona State Sun Devils and the Arizona Wildcats predates Arizona's statehood, and is the oldest rivalry in the [NCAA](60706.md).[^cite_note-167] The [Territorial Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Cup), first awarded in 1889 and certified as the oldest trophy in college football,[^cite_note-168] is awarded to the winner of the annual football game between the two schools.

Arizona also hosts several college football [bowl games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowl_game). The [Fiesta Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiesta_Bowl), originally held at [Sun Devil Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Devil_Stadium), is now held at [State Farm Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Farm_Stadium) in [Glendale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glendale,_Arizona). The Fiesta Bowl is part of the new [College Football Playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Football_Playoff) (CFP). University of Phoenix Stadium was also home to the [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_BCS_National_Championship_Game) and [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_BCS_National_Championship_Game) [BCS National Championship Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCS_National_Championship_Game).



A spring training game between the Cubs and White Sox at [HoHoKam Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HoHoKam_Park)

State Farm Stadium hosted the Final Four of the [NCAA Division I men's basketball tournament](507399.md) in 2017 and is scheduled to host it again in 2024.[^cite_note-169]

### Baseball

Arizona is a popular location for [Major League Baseball](38776.md) [spring training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_training), as it is home to the [Cactus League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cactus_League). Spring training was first started in Arizona in 1947, when Brewers owner Veeck sold them in 1945 but went on to purchase the Cleveland Indians in 1946. He decided to train the Cleveland Indians in [Tucson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson) and convinced the New York Giants to give [Phoenix](49121.md) a try. Thus, the Cactus League was born.[^cite_note-170]

On March 9, 1995, Arizona was awarded a franchise to begin to play for the 1998 season. A \$130 million franchise fee was paid to Major League Baseball and on January 16, 1997, the Diamondbacks were officially voted into the National League.

Since their debut, the [Diamondbacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Diamondbacks) have won five National League West titles, two National League Championship pennants, and most recently the [2001 World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_World_Series).[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]
