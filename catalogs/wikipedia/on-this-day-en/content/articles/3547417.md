The **National Assembly** ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Assembleia Nacional*) is the [unicameral](204304.md) [legislative body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_body) of [Cape Verde](18962637.md).

## History

### National People's Assembly (1975–1991)



National Assembly building

The country's [first legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Cape_Verdean_parliamentary_election) took place in June 1975. The body was known as the National People's Assembly and its members came from the [African Party for the Independence of Guinea and Cape Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Party_for_the_Independence_of_Guinea_and_Cape_Verde) (PAIGC), which was the sole political party allowed to field candidates. They elected PAIGC Secretary-general [Aristides Pereira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristides_Pereira) [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Cape_Verde) on 5 July 1975, when the country officially gained independence from [Portugal](23033.md).

[One-party](102459.md) [parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Cape_Verdean_parliamentary_election) were again held on 7 December 1980 with Pereira being re-elected unopposed by the Assembly on 12 February 1981. That same year the Cape Verdean branch of the PAIGC, which was also the ruling party in [Guinea-Bissau](12186.md), was renamed [African Party for the Independence of Cape Verde](1094346.md) (PAICV).

The [1985 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Cape_Verdean_parliamentary_election) for an enlarged 83-seat National People's Assembly took place on 7 December 1985. For the first time a few independent, PAICV-endorsed candidates won seats in the legislature.

In 1990, Cape Verde became one of the first African countries to abandon one-party rule and embrace [multiparty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiparty) [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy).

### National Assembly (1991–2016)

The [first multiparty National Assembly elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Cape_Verdean_parliamentary_election) took place on 13 January 1991. The ruling PAICV was soundly defeated by the opposition [Movement for Democracy](850326.md) (MpD), which won 56 out of 79 seats compared to the PAICV's 23. The elections were considered transparent, free, and fair.

In the [next election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Cape_Verdean_parliamentary_election), held on 17 December 1995, the number of Assembly seats was reduced from 79 to 72. The MpD won 50 seats and the PAICV won 21. The [Democratic Convergence Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Convergence_Party_\(Cape_Verde\)) (PCD) won the remaining seat.

After the [elections on 14 January 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Cape_Verdean_parliamentary_election), the Assembly has a total of 72 directly elected members who serve five-year terms. They are elected from 16 multi-member [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency) using the [D'Hondt method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Hondt_method) of [party-list proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party-list_proportional_representation).

Four parties and one coalition contested the election of 2001. They were the PAICV, MpD, [Democratic Renewal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Renewal_Party_\(Cape_Verde\)) (PRD), and the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Cape_Verde\)) (PSD). Three parties—the [Democratic Convergence Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Convergence_Party_\(Cape_Verde\)) (PCD), [Democratic and Independent Cape Verdean Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_and_Independent_Cape_Verdean_Union) (UCID), and the [Labour and Solidarity Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_and_Solidarity_Party) (PTS)—formed a coalition known as the [Democratic Alliance for Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_for_Change) (ADM). The election results were as follows:

**PAICV** - 49.50% of the vote and 40 seats\
&#x20;**MpD** - 40.55% of the vote and 30 seats\
&#x20;**ADM** - 6.12% of the vote and 2 seats\
&#x20;**PRD** - 3.38% of the vote and no seats\
&#x20;**PSD** - 0.45% of the vote and no seats

Eight women won seats in the National Assembly.

### National Assembly (2016–present)

Following the [parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verdean_parliamentary_election,_2016) in March 2016, the [Movement for Democracy](850326.md) party won a majority of the seats in the election. This was the first time the ruling party PAICV have lost their majority in the parliament. Following the election [Ulisses Correia e Silva](50229553.md) became Prime Minister and [Jorge Pedro Maurício dos Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Pedro_Maur%C3%ADcio_dos_Santos) was elected President of the National Assembly.[^cite_note-3] Following this defeat, the former ruling party the PAICV lost momentum and lost in the local and [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verdean_presidential_election,_2016) elections in the same year.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]
