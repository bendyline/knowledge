The **Philippine Army** (**PA**; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Hukbong Katihan ng Pilipinas*) is the main, oldest and largest branch of the [Armed Forces of the Philippines](23448.md) (AFP), responsible for [ground warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_warfare). As of 2025, it had an estimated strength of 110,000 personnel.[^cite_note-Marcos_installs_new_Army_chief,_vows_full_support-1] The service branch was established on December 21, 1935, as the [Army of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippine_Army#Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines).

The Philippine Army has been engaged in numerous combat operations, including the ongoing [Communist rebellion in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_rebellion_in_the_Philippines), the [Moro conflict](4602307.md) and, alongside other national military forces, in conflicts of international scope and on United Nations peacekeeping missions in various parts of the world.

## Background

Main articles: [History of the Philippine Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippine_Army) and [Military history of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_Philippines)

### Spanish era



Philippine carabiniers in the 1800s.

Majority of the soldiers that served the [Captaincy General of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_the_Philippines) are Philippine natives,[^cite_note-\:0-5] as opposed to their Native-American, Mestizo from the Americas or white counterparts who at first, are mostly [Criollos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criollo_people) from New Mexico, but then after [Mexican independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_independence), are mostly from the Peninsula. Many of them are exiles and are forced to join the military service in the Philippines for crimes committed at home.[^cite_note-6] Philippine natives, in most cases, join the military to pay debts. They participated in many conflicts and pacifications throughout the Spanish Empire. In many cases acting as intermediaries. The reward for service of an elite native are mostly land grant and encomiendas. The annexation of the capital - Manila to the Spanish Empire in 1571 was made possible with the help of the Visayan natives who views [Rajah Sulayman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah_Sulayman) as an enemy.[^cite_note-\:0-5]

### Philippine Revolution (1896–1898)

Main articles: [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution) and [Philippine Revolutionary Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolutionary_Army)



Philippine Revolutionary Army uniforms.

After three centuries of [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) rule, there were calls for social reforms and an end to the perceived oppressive friar rule. In 1896, [Andres Bonifacio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andres_Bonifacio) founded the [Katipunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katipunan) to prepare his band of Filipinos for armed revolt against the Spanish government. The Katipunan thus formed an army of [insurgents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgent).[^cite_note-7]

On March 22, 1897, almost a year after the outbreak of hostilities between the Katipuneros and the Spanish troops, [Emilio Aguinaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Aguinaldo) was elected as revolutionary president in the [Tejeros Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tejeros_Convention) and revolutionary forces were organized into the [Philippine Revolutionary Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolutionary_Army) (PRA). General [Artemio Ricarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemio_Ricarte) was named as its Captain General. This date marks the founding day of the PRA, and is considered by the Armed Forces of the Philippines to be the establishment date of the Philippine Army.[^cite_note-8]

On November 1, the [Republic of Biak-na-Bato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Biak-na-Bato) was established, with the PRA as its military arm. That republic was dissolved on December 14 by the [Pact of Biak-na-Bato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pact_of_Biak-na-Bato), with Aguinaldo and other senior leadership going into [exile in Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Junta). During the exile period, some elements of the PRA remained active in the Philippines under the [Central Executive Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Executive_Committee_\(Philippines\)) established by [Francisco Macabulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Macabulos). On May 19, 1898, during the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War), Aguinaldo returned to the Philippines, rekindled the revolution, [declared independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Declaration_of_Independence) from Spain, and became President of the [First Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic) which was established during the lull following Spanish surrender to American forces in the Philippines.

### Philippine–American War (1899–1902)

Main article: [Philippine–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine%E2%80%93American_War)



[Philippine revolutionary soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolutionary_Army) in 1899.



Philippine Scouts in formation during the American colonial period

The 1898 [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1898\)) formalized the end of the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War), with one of its provisions being [cession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cession) of the Philippines to America by Spain. Shortly thereafter, the [Philippine–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine%E2%80%93American_War) erupted between that nascent republic and occupying American forces, eventually resulting in American victory and the disbanding of the PRA.

During the final years of the Philippine–American War, with the notable successes by the all-Filipino Macabebe Scouts cavalry squadron (raised in 1899) under U.S. command against the PRA, the American President Theodore Roosevelt officially sanctioned the raising of the [Philippine Scouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Scouts) (PS) as part of the [United States Army](32087.md), with full effect starting from October 1901. Earlier, in August that same year, came the colonial civil government's decision to found the [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) (PC) as the national [gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie) force for law enforcement. Both of these organizations and their victories over the PRA; the Scouts were integrated into the U.S. Army,[^cite_note-9] and the Constabulary gradually took over the responsibility for suppressing hostile forces' activities from United States Army units.[^cite_note-10] This contributed to ending the conflict in 1902, even as resistance continued (inclusive of the Muslims of the south, resulting in the [Moro Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Rebellion)) through 1914.

Starting in 1910, one Philippine Scout soldier was sent to the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy) each year. Several of these graduates who had served with the Scouts, along with PC officers, formed part of the first officer corps of the revitalized [Philippine Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commonwealth_Army) established in 1935.[^cite_note-11]

### World War I (1914–1918)

Main article: [World War I](4764461.md)

In 1917 the [Philippine Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Assembly) created the [Philippine National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_National_Guard) with the intent to join the [American Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Expeditionary_Force). By the time it was absorbed into the [National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Army_\(USA\)) it had grown to 25,000 soldiers. However, these units did not see action. The first Filipino to die in World War I was Private [Tomas Mateo Claudio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomas_Mateo_Claudio) who served with the U.S. Army as part of the American Expeditionary Forces to Europe. He died in the [Battle of Chateau Thierry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ch%C3%A2teau-Thierry_\(1918\)) in France on June 29, 1918.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The Tomas Claudio Memorial College in Morong Rizal, Philippines, which was founded in 1950, was named in his honor.[^cite_note-14]

### Interwar period (1918–1935)

The Philippine National Guard unit of the U.S. Army was deactivated following World War I, then formally disbanded in 1921.[^cite_note-15] During most of the [Interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period), spanning about 20 years from 11 November 1918 to 1 September 1939, the Philippines had no armed forces other than the Philippine Scouts, the Constabulary, and some semimilitary units which were generally privately organized and had no connection with conventional military forces.[^cite_note-16]

## History

Main article: [History of the Philippine Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippine_Army)

### Commonwealth period (1935–1946)



Major General [Basilio Valdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilio_Valdes), [Vice Chief of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Chief_of_Staff_of_the_Armed_Forces_of_the_Philippines)**,** Philippine Army from 1935 to 1938. Later [Chief of Staff of the Armed Forces of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_Armed_Forces_of_the_Philippines) from 1939 to 1945.

The Philippine Army of today was initially organized under the [National Defense Act of 1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Act_of_1935) (Commonwealth Act No. 1) that formally created the Armed Forces of the Philippines. The act specified that insofar as may be practicable, original appointments by the President in grades above third lieutenant "shall be made from among those formerly holding Reserve Commissions in the United States Army, from among former officers of the Philippine Scouts and Constabulary, from among former officers of the National Guard and from such others who possess exceptional ability or special training and skill."[^cite_note-CA1-17][^cite_note-18]

After the establishment of the [Philippine Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines) on November 15, 1935, [President](271126.md) [Manuel L. Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_L._Quezon) sought the services of [General of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_of_the_Army_\(United_States\)) [Douglas MacArthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_MacArthur) (also [Philippine Army Field Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Marshal_\(Philippines\))) to evolve a national defense plan. The official rebirth of the Philippine Army occurred with the passage of *Commonwealth Act No. 1*, approved on December 21, 1935, which effected the organization of a *Council of National Defense* and of the *Army of the Philippines*. The act set forth the organizational structure of the army in some detail, set forth enlistment procedures, and established mobilization procedures.[^cite_note-CA1-17] Among the organizational arrangements was the stipulation that the Philippines were to be divided into military districts.

The development of the by now renewed Philippine Army was slow.[^cite_note-FallP-19] The year 1936 was devoted to the building of camps, organization of cadres, and the special training of instructors, drawn largely from the Constabulary, which joined the new force as the Constabulary Division. The commander of the Philippine Department provided Philippine Scouts as instructors and detailed U.S. Army officers to assist in the inspection, instruction, and administration of the program. By the end of the year instructors had been trained and camps established.

&#x20;The first group of 20,000 men was called up on January 1, 1937, and by the end of 1939 there were 4,800 officers and 104,000 men in the reserves.[^cite_note-FallP-19] Infantry training was given at camps scattered throughout the Philippines. Field artillery training was concentrated in the vicinity of the U.S. Army's [Fort Stotsenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Stotsenburg) near Angeles, about fifty miles north of [Manila](184334.md), and specialized training was given at [Fort William McKinley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_William_McKinley) just south of Manila. Coast artillery instruction was carried on at Fort Stotsenburg and at [Grande Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wint) in [Subic Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subic_Bay) by personnel supplied largely by the American commander at [Corregidor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corregidor).



Philippine Scouts operating a [37 mm gun M3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37_mm_gun_M3) at [Fort McKinley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Bonifacio).

With the threat of war with [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) becoming imminent, on July 26, 1941, a new U.S. command in the Far East was created, known as the [United States Army Forces Far East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Forces_Far_East) (USAFFE) under the command of General MacArthur. On the same date, U.S. President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt), issued a [Presidential Order](210541.md) (6 Fed. Reg. 3825) which called the Philippine Army into the service of the Armed Forces of the United States. The Presidential Order did not order all the military forces of the Philippine government into the service of the United States Armed Forces; only those units and personnel indicated in orders issued by a general officer of the United States Army were mobilized and made an integral part of the United States Army Forces Far East (USAFFE), and only those members of a unit who physically reported for duty were inducted.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-COMMONWEALTH-21] With an annual appropriation of 16 million pesos, the mobilized units trained new Filipino members in defending the nation and protecting its people.

### World War II

See also: [Japanese occupation of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Philippines) and [Philippine resistance against Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_resistance_against_Japan)

When World War II broke out in December 1941, two regular and ten reserve divisions of the Philippine Army undertook the defense of the Philippines. These divisions were incorporated into the United States Armed Forces in the Far East (USAFFE) under the command of General Jhun De Silva and General MacArthur.

Japanese forces invaded the Philippines after the bombing of [Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_Harbor) on the island of Oahu on December 7, 1941. At this time, two regular and ten reserve divisions of the Philippine Army undertook the defense of the [Philippines](23440.md). This included [North Luzon Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Luzon_Force) (under then Major General [Jonathan M. Wainwright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_M._Wainwright_\(general\))), [South Luzon Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Luzon_Force) activated December 13, 1941 under Brig. Gen. [George M. Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_M._Parker_\(general\)) Jr., the [Visayas-Mindanao Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayas-Mindanao_Force) under Colonel [W.F. Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_F._Sharp) in the southern islands (61st, [81st](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/81st_Division_\(Philippines\)), and 101st Divisions plus three other regiments),[^cite_note-22] and the Reserve Force. North Luzon Force included the [11th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Division_\(Philippines\)), [21st](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Division_\(Philippines\)), and [31st Divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31st_Division_\(Philippines\)), all reserve.[^cite_note-23] South Luzon Force include the 1st (regular) Division, and the 41st, 51st, and 71st (reserve) Divisions.[^cite_note-24] These divisions were incorporated into the United States Army Forces in the Far East (USAFFE).[^cite_note-25]

[2.95 inch QF Mountain gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2.95_inch_QF_Mountain_gun); [3-inch naval gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3-inch_gun); [6-pounder naval gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-pounder_gun); [Stokes Mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stokes_Mortar); [Brandt mle 27/31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandt_mle_27/31); [Canon d'Infanterie de 37 modèle 1916 TRP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_d'Infanterie_de_37_mod%C3%A8le_1916_TRP); [M2 Browning machine gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M2_Browning_machine_gun); [M1917 Browning machine gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1917_Browning_machine_gun); [AN/M2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1919_Browning_machine_gun#.30_AN/M2); [Thompson submachine gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thompson_submachine_gun); [M1911 pistol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1911_pistol); [M1917 revolver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1917_revolver); and the [Colt Model 1903 Pocket Hammerless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colt_Model_1903_Pocket_Hammerless).

After the Battle of Bataan, the Japanese began the siege and [Battle of Corregidor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Corregidor). Defending forces included regiments of the [Harbor Defenses of Manila and Subic Bays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor_Defenses_of_Manila_and_Subic_Bays), the [4th Marine Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Marine_Regiment) and other Philippine, U.S. Army and Navy units and soldiers. Japanese forces landed at Corregidor on May 5, 1942. The island's fall led to the surrender of all defending Filipino and American forces on May 6, 1942. About 4,000 of the 11,000 American and Filipino prisoners of war from the island were marched through the streets of Manila to incarceration at [Fort Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Santiago) in [Intramuros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramuros) and [Bilibid Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilibid_Prison) in [Muntinlupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muntinlupa), [Rizal](232458.md), which had become Japanese camps.

With the fall of Corregidor, Filipino and U.S. forces under U.S. command surrendered. After the surrender, thousands of Filipinos formerly under U.S. command (especially the former [Visayas-Mindanao Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayas-Mindanao_Force), which had seen little combat) evaded Japanese confinement and hid in the jungle. Every major island had guerrilla groups; Luzon had a dozen, including the Communist [Huks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huks). After initial clashes based on religious and political rivalries order was gradually restored, with most willing to trust the United States to grant independence in time.[^cite_note-27] Many of these groups worked under the control of General Douglas MacArthur's General Headquarters, [Southwest Pacific Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Pacific_Area). The [Japanese occupation of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Philippines) saw repeated combat between the Japanese imperial forces, their collaborators and Filipino guerrillas. The American and Allied liberation force which began landing on October 17, 1944, was aided by local Filipino soldiers and recognized guerrillas in the [liberation of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_Campaign_\(1944-45\)).

President [Sergio Osmeña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Osme%C3%B1a) and Major General [Basilio J. Valdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilio_J._Valdes) ordered the re-establishment of the army. The general headquarters of the Philippine Army and the United States Army Forces in the Far East moved to [Tacloban](746499.md), [Leyte](194750.md) on October 23. From October 17, 1944, to September 2, 1945, local Philippine Constabulary troops, guerrilla units and the American liberation forces fought Imperial Japanese and [Kempeitai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kempeitai) troops which were supported by the Bureau of Constabulary and [Makapili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makapili) militia.

After the restoration of the Commonwealth of the Philippines on October 20, 1944, President Sergio Osmeña, the government, military officials and cabinet returned from exile in the United States.

After the war, four military areas were activated to take the place of military districts. The Armed Forces were reorganized, thereby giving birth to its four major services. Headquarters National Defense Forces was renamed General Headquarters Armed Forces of the Philippines.

### Post-war era (1946-1965)

Service of the Philippine Army as part of the [United States Army](32087.md) terminated as of midnight, June 30, 1946, by authority of General Order #168, [U.S. Army Forces, Western Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army_Forces,_Western_Pacific?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-COMMONWEALTH-21] The next day, on July 1, President [Manuel Roxas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Roxas) issued Executive Order No. 94 s. 1947 which, among other things, reorganized the Philippine Army into a service branch of what was now called the Armed Forces of the Philippines.[^cite_note-28] This resulted in the formation of the [Philippine Air Force](27918720.md) and reformation of the [Philippine Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Navy) as separate organizations after long years as part of the Philippine Army.

In the early fifties and the mid-sixties, Philippine Army was thus involved in the [Korean War](16772.md) and, in civic action context, the [Vietnam War](32611.md), responding to the Philippine government decision to extend a helping hand to war-torn countries as part of its commitment as member of the United Nations.&#x20;

1950 would see the new army not just fighting Communist groups in Luzon but from August of that year, even the [Korean People's Army](21263.md) and their allies in the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) in the [Korean War](16772.md) as PA Battalion Combat Teams (BCTs) forming the bulk of the [Philippine Expeditionary Forces to Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Expeditionary_Forces_to_Korea) formed part of the UN forces, led by the US, that fought in the conflict. The decade saw the raising of the first active division of the Army, the 1st Infantry Division. With the victory over the Huks later in the 50s, the BCTs became active duty infantry battalions. Formed in the same time was the [1st Scout Ranger Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Scout_Ranger_Regiment), and in 1962 the PA raised its airborne and special forces formation, the [Special Forces Regiment (Philippines)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Forces_Regiment_\(Philippines\)) following the traditions of the US Army Special Forces (the Green Berets) and the 11th Airborne Division that helped liberate Southern Luzon and Manila at the closing stages of the Japanese occupation of the country.

### The Marcos dictatorship era (1965–1986)

Main article: [Military history of the Philippines during the Marcos dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_Philippines_during_the_Marcos_dictatorship)

The two-decade long Marcos presidency (which began in 1965 and lasted until 1986) marked the beginnings of at least two long-running conflicts that continued to plague later administrations:[^cite_note-Robles2016-29][^cite_note-DavideCommission1990-30] the Moro conflict and the [New People's Army](1262096.md) conflict.[^cite_note-asiafoundationpdf-31][^cite_note-DavideCommission1990-30]

The [New People's Army](1262096.md) (NPA) first took root in 1968 with the establishment of the Marxist-Leninist-Maoist [Communist Party of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Philippines) by [Jose Maria Sison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Maria_Sison) after he left the Marxist Leninist [Partido Komunista ng Pilipinas-1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partido_Komunista_ng_Pilipinas-1930). An alliance with a small armed group led by Bernabe Buscayno one year later created the [New People's Army](1262096.md), which was a relatively weak armed group until the declaration of Martial Law, which made activism illegal and thus radicalized a large number of moderate protesters into taking up arms and joining the NPA underground movement.[^cite_note-asiafoundationpdf-31][^cite_note-DavideCommission1990-30] Meanwhile, the [Moro conflict](4602307.md) began in earnest in 1974 with the formation of the [Moro National Liberation Front](2821886.md) (MNLF), having grown out of the discontent created by the [Jabidah Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabidah_Massacre) several years earlier.[^cite_note-BetweenIntegrationandSecession-32][^cite_note-TessVitug20130318-33][^cite_note-asiafoundationpdf-31] These conflicts became a major preoccupation of the PA throughout the Marcos regime and for decades afterwards, as the growth of the armed groups forced the army into engagements both large and small.[^cite_note-asiafoundationpdf-31] PA bases also became detention centers for [political detainees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_detainees_under_the_Marcos_dictatorship), becoming the venue for the regime's many [Human rights violations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship).[^cite_note-DavideCommission1990-30]

Notably, the Communist and Muslim rebellions forced the PA into establishing its 2nd Infantry Division, which led to the raising of more infantry divisions all over the country, as well as the formal raising of the Army's Special Operations Command and what is now today the Armor Division.

Towards the end of the end of the regime, it was largely a group of Philippine Army officers who planned the aborted [February 1986 Reform the Armed Forces Movement coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_1986_Reform_the_Armed_Forces_Movement_coup),[^cite_note-Lee2015-34][^cite_note-Berlin1982-35][^cite_note-Celoza1997-36]<sup>: "32" </sup> whose uncovering sparked the events which would become the civilian-led 1986 [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution) which unseated Marcos.[^cite_note-DavideCommission1990-30][^cite_note-Schock1999-37]

### Contemporary history

Contemporary history saw the Philippine Army involved in more major conflicts, such asthe [war on terror](13425800.md), the [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) and the [Iraq War](5043324.md), as well as missions with the [United Nations](31769.md), such as the [United Nations Disengagement Observer Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Disengagement_Observer_Force) in [Golan Heights](7515865.md) and the [United Nations Mission in East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Mission_in_East_Timor). By the 2000s, the Army acquired a small aviation capability for transport purposes, with plans to include attack and transport helicopters, a rocket artillery battery unit, and a land-based missile battery system unit.

## Vision

*By 2028, a world-class Army that is a source of national pride.*

## Mission

*To provide prompt, sustained, and decisive land warfare operations in support of the Filipino people and the nation's development.*

## Organization

The Philippine Army is administered through the Department of National Defense (DND). Under the AFP structure, the Chief of Staff, Armed Forces of the Philippines (CSAFP), a four-star [general](148537.md)/[admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral) (if the officer is a member of the Philippine Navy), is the most senior military officer. The senior Army officer is the **[Commanding General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commanding_General_of_the_Philippine_Army), Philippine Army (CG, PA)**, who usually holds the rank of [Lieutenant General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_General). He is assisted by the Vice-Commander of the Philippine Army (VC, PA), and the Chief of Staff, Philippine Army (CS, PA), in charge on organizational and administrative matters, both holding the ranks of [Major General](36301471.md).&#x20;

The Philippine Army consists of 11 infantry divisions, 1 armor division, 1 combined arms brigade, 1 artillery regiment, 5 engineer brigades, 1 aviation regiment, and 7 combat support units which are spread throughout the Philippine archipelago.

## Equipment

Main article: [List of equipment of the Philippine Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_equipment_of_the_Philippine_Army)

The Philippine Army has made use of its existing equipment to fulfill its mandate while modernization projects are underway. The [Republic Act No. 7898](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFP_Modernization_Act) declares the policy of the State to modernize the military to a level where it can effectively and fully perform its constitutional mandate to uphold the sovereignty and preserve the patrimony of the [republic](23440.md). The law, as amended, has set conditions that should be satisfied when the defense department procures major equipment and weapon systems for the army.
