**Genoa**[^cite_note-4] is the [sixth-largest city in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Italy) and the capital of the region of [Liguria](71864.md) in [northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy) [Italy](14532.md). As of 2026, 566,247 people live within the city's administrative limits.[^cite_note-population-3] While its [metropolitan city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Genoa) has 820,691 inhabitants,[^cite_note-population-3] more than 1.5 million people live in the wider metropolitan area stretching along the [Italian Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Riviera).[^cite_note-UrbanismiInItalia3-5]

On the [Gulf of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Genoa) in the [Ligurian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_Sea), Genoa has historically been one of the most important ports on the [Mediterranean](19006.md): it is the busiest port in Italy and the Mediterranean Sea and twelfth-busiest in the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

Genoa was the capital of [one of the most powerful maritime republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa) for over seven centuries, from the 11th century to 1797.[^cite_note-8] Particularly from the 12th century to the 15th century, the city played a leading role in the history of commerce and trade in Europe, becoming one of the largest naval powers of the continent and considered among the wealthiest cities in the world.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] It was also nicknamed *la Superba* ("the superb one") by [Petrarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarch) due to its glories on the seas and impressive landmarks.[^cite_note-11] The city has hosted massive shipyards and steelworks since the 19th century, and its solid financial sector dates back to the Middle Ages. The [Bank of Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Saint_George), founded in 1407, is the oldest known state deposit bank in the world and has played an important role in the city's prosperity since the middle of the 15th century.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

The [historical centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_center_of_Genoa), or old town, of Genoa is one of the largest and most-densely populated in Europe.[^cite_note-14] Part of it was also inscribed on the World Heritage List (UNESCO) in 2006 as [Genoa: Le Strade Nuove and the system of the Palazzi dei Rolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa:_Le_Strade_Nuove_and_the_system_of_the_Palazzi_dei_Rolli). Genoa's historical city centre is also known for its narrow lanes and streets called *caruggi* ('alleys').[^cite_note-15] Genoa is also home to the [University of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genoa), which has a history going back to the 15th century, when it was known as Genuense Athenaeum. The city's rich cultural history in [art](#Visual_arts), [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Genoa) and [cuisine](#Cuisine) allowed it to become the 2004 [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture). It is the birthplace of [Guglielmo Embriaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guglielmo_Embriaco), [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus), [Simonetta Vespucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simonetta_Vespucci), [Andrea Doria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Doria), [Niccolò Paganini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Paganini), [Giuseppe Mazzini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Mazzini), [Pope Benedict XV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV), [Renzo Piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renzo_Piano), [Fabrizio De André](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrizio_De_Andr%C3%A9) and [Grimaldo Canella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimaldo_Canella), founder of the [House of Grimaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Grimaldi), among others.

Genoa, which forms the southern corner of the Milan-Turin-Genoa industrial triangle of [Northwest Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Italy), is one of the country's major economic centres.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] A number of leading Italian companies are based in the city, including [Fincantieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fincantieri), [Leonardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_S.p.A.),[^cite_note-18] [Ansaldo Energia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansaldo_Energia),[^cite_note-19] [Ansaldo STS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansaldo_STS), [Erg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erg_\(company\)), [Piaggio Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio_Aerospace), [Mediterranean Shipping Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Shipping_Company) and [Costa Cruises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Cruises).

## Etymology

The city's modern name may derive from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) word *genua* ([\[ˈɡe.nu.aː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Latin); singular: *genu* [\[ˈɡe.nuː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Latin)), meaning 'knees', because of its position at the centre of the Ligurian coastal arch, which was deemed the "knee region" of a "[booted leg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Peninsula)".

An alternative origin for it is the [theonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theonym) of [Janus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janus), a Roman deity said to have two faces: a fore-turned one and a back-turned one, which was eventually used as a [metaphor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metaphor) for the city's two sides (or "faces"): the sea-facing southside and the mountains-facing northside. The Latin word *ianua* ([\[ˈjaː.nu.a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Latin)) is yet another proposed source of the name, related to Janus's name and meaning 'door' or 'passage' (into the Italian Peninsula). The Latin name *[oppidum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppidum) Genua* ([\[ˈop.pi.dum ˈɡe.nu.a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Latin); 'Genoa town') was recorded by [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) as part of the [Augustan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Italy#Augustan_organization) *Regio IX Liguria* (English: [Liguria](71864.md#Roman_times) the Ninth Region).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

Finally, the placename might have had an origin in the [Etruscan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_language), specifically in the term *Kainua*, which meant 'new city'. Such etymology was proposed after the discovery of an Etruscan pottery-sherd inscription that read *Kainua*; it suggests that the Latin name would have been an alteration of that older Etruscan name, whose original "New City" meaning was lost to the Roman tongue.[^cite_note-22]

## History

Main article: [History of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Genoa)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Genoa).

### Prehistory and Roman times

The city's area has been inhabited since the fifth or fourth millennium BC, making it one of the [oldest continuously inhabited cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldest_continuously_inhabited_cities) in the world.[^cite_note-23] In the fifth century BC the first town, or [oppidum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppidum), was founded probably by the [ancient Ligures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligures) (which gave the name to the modern region of [Liguria](71864.md)) at the top of the hill today called Castello (Castle), which is now inside the medieval old town.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] In this period the Genoese town, inhabited by the "Genuati" (a group of Ligure peoples), was considered "the emporium of the Ligurians", given its strong commercial character.[^cite_note-26]

The "Genoese oppidum" had an alliance with [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) through a *foedus aequum* (equal pact) in the course of the [Second Punic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Punic_War). The [Carthaginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginians) accordingly destroyed it in 209 BC. The town was rebuilt and, after the [Carthaginian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginian_Wars) ended in 146 BC, it received municipal rights. The original *castrum* then expanded towards the current areas of Santa Maria di Castello and the San Lorenzo promontory. Trade goods included skins, timber, and honey. Goods were moved to and from Genoa's hinterland, including major cities like [Tortona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortona) and [Piacenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piacenza). An amphitheater was also found there among other archaeological remains from the Roman period.[^cite_note-27]

### Middle Ages to early modern period

### 5th to 10th centuries

After the fall of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire), the [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths) occupied Genoa. After the [Gothic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_War_\(535%E2%80%93554\)), the [Byzantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) made it the seat of their [vicar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicar). When the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) invaded Italy in 568, Bishop [Honoratus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoratus_of_Mediolanum?action=edit\&redlink=1) of [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishopric_of_Milan) fled and held his seat in Genoa.[^cite_note-28] During this time and in the following century Genoa was little more than a small centre, slowly building its merchant fleet, which was to become the leading commercial carrier of the Western Mediterranean. In 934–35 the town was [thoroughly sacked and burned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_sack_of_Genoa) by a [Fatimid fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimid_fleet) under [Ya'qub ibn Ishaq al-Tamimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ya'qub_ibn_Ishaq_al-Tamimi).[^cite_note-29]

#### Rise of the Genoese Republic

Main article: [Republic of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa)

[Guglielmo Embriaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guglielmo_Embriaco) portrayed on the main façade of the [Palazzo San Giorgio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_San_Giorgio), Genoa



The port and fleet of Genoa in the early 14th century, by [Quinto Cenni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinto_Cenni)

Genoa started expanding during the [First Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Crusade). At the time the city had a population of about 10,000. Twelve [galleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galley), one ship and 1,200 soldiers from Genoa joined the crusade. The Genoese troops, led by noblemen de Insula and Avvocato, set sail in July 1097.[^cite_note-genfir-30] The Genoese fleet transported and provided naval support to the crusaders, mainly during the [siege of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Antioch) in 1098, when the Genoese fleet blockaded the city while the troops provided support during the siege.[^cite_note-genfir-30] In the [siege of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_\(1099\)) in 1099 [Genoese crossbowmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese_crossbowmen) led by [Guglielmo Embriaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guglielmo_Embriaco) acted as support units against the defenders of the city.

The Republic's role as a maritime power in the Mediterranean region secured many favorable commercial treaties for Genoese merchants. They came to control a large portion of the trade of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), [Tripoli](31232.md) (Libya), the [Principality of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Antioch), [Cilician Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Kingdom_of_Cilicia), and Egypt.[^cite_note-genfir-30] Although Genoa maintained free-trading rights in Egypt and Syria, it lost some of its territorial possessions after Saladin's campaigns in those areas in the late 12th century.[^cite_note-vas-31][^cite_note-anra-32]

### 13th and 14th centuries

The commercial and cultural rivalry of Genoa and Venice was played out through the thirteenth century. Thanks to the major role played by the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) in the [Fourth Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Crusade), Venetian trading rights were enforced in the eastern Mediterranean and Venice was able to gain control of a large portion of maritime commerce in the region.[^cite_note-vas-31]

To regain control of local commerce, the Republic of Genoa allied with [Michael VIII Palaiologos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_VIII_Palaiologos), emperor of [Nicaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Nicaea), who wanted to restore the Byzantine Empire by recapturing [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople). In March 1261 the treaty of the alliance was signed in [Nymphaeum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemalpa%C5%9Fa).[^cite_note-vas-31] On 25 July 1261, Nicaean troops under [Alexios Strategopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexios_Strategopoulos) recaptured Constantinople.[^cite_note-vas-31] As a result, the balance of favour tipped toward Genoa, which was granted free trade rights in the Nicene Empire.[^cite_note-vas-31] The islands of [Chios](17067.md) and [Lesbos](2633994.md) became commercial stations of Genoa as well as the city of [Smyrna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smyrna) (Izmir). In the same century the Republic conquered many settlements in [Crimea](163045.md), known as [Gazaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese_Gazaria), where the Genoese colony of [Caffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feodosiya) was established. The alliance with the restored Byzantine Empire increased the wealth and power of Genoa, and simultaneously decreased Venetian and Pisan commerce. The Byzantine Empire had granted the majority of free trading rights to Genoa.[^cite_note-navwa-33]

Around the 14th century, Genoa was also credited with the invention of blue [jeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeans). Genoa's jean fabric was a [fustian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fustian) textile of "medium quality and of reasonable cost", very similar to cotton [corduroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corduroy) for which Genoa was famous, and was "used for work clothes in general". The [Genoese navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese_navy) equipped its sailors with jeans, as they needed a fabric which could be worn wet or dry.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

During the [Catalan–Genoese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan%E2%80%93Genoese_War), [Genoa was besieged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Genoa_\(1331\)) and sacked by Guillem de Cervelló. As a result of the Genoese support to the [Aragonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon) rule in [Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sicily), Genoa was granted free trading and export rights in the Kingdom. Genoese bankers also profited from loans to the new nobility of Sicily. Corsica was formally annexed in 1347.[^cite_note-36]

### 15th and 16th centuries



View of Genoa, published in 1483



[Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus), posthumous portrait by [Ridolfo Ghirlandaio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridolfo_Ghirlandaio), c. 1520



[Andrea Doria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Doria)

In the 15th century two of the earliest banks in the world were founded in Genoa: the [Bank of Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Saint_George), founded in 1407, which was the oldest state deposit bank in the world at its closure in 1805 and the [Monte di Pietà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_of_piety) of Genoa founded in 1483. [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) was born in Genoa c. 1451, and donated one-tenth of his income from the discovery of the Americas for Spain to the [Bank of Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Saint_George) in Genoa for the relief of taxation on goods. Under the ensuing economic recovery, many aristocratic Genoese families, such as the Balbi, [Doria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doria_\(family\)), [Grimaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimaldi_Family), [Pallavicini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallavicini_family), and Serra, amassed tremendous fortunes. According to Felipe Fernandez-Armesto and others, the practices Genoa developed in the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) (such as chattel slavery) were crucial in the exploration and exploitation of the New World.[^cite_note-37]

Thereafter, Genoa became something of an associate of the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire), with Genoese bankers, in particular, financing many of the Spanish crown's foreign endeavors from their [counting houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counting_house) in [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville). [Fernand Braudel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernand_Braudel) has even called the period 1557 to 1627 the "age of the Genoese", "of a rule that was so discreet and sophisticated that historians for a long time failed to notice it" (Braudel 1984 p. 157). The Genoese bankers provided the unwieldy [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) system with fluid credit and a dependably regular income. But other historians have stressed how the Genoese participation in the Spanish Atlantic went well beyond financial flows, with Genoese merchants and entrepreneurs settling in different areas of the empire and building Genoese networks that lasted well into the 19th century.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] In return the less dependable shipments of American silver were rapidly transferred from Seville to Genoa, to provide capital for further ventures. Genoa's trade, however, remained closely dependent on control of Mediterranean sealanes, and the loss of [Chios](17067.md) to the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) (1566), struck a severe blow.[^cite_note-40]

### 17th century



A map of Italy in 1796

From the 17th century, the Genoese Republic started a period of slow decline. In May 1625 a French-Savoian army briefly laid siege to Genoa. Though it was eventually [lifted with the aid of the Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relief_of_Genoa), the French would later [bombard the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardment_of_Genoa) in May 1684 for its support of Spain during the [War of the Reunions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Reunions).[^cite_note-41] In-between, a [plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples_Plague) killed as many as half of the inhabitants of Genoa in 1656–57.[^cite_note-42]

### 18th century

In 1729, the Republic of Genoa had to cope with the beginning of the Corsican revolution for their independence. First led by [Luiggi Giafferi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luiggi_Giafferi) and Giacinto Paoli, this conflict culminated after 26 years of struggle, costly in economic and military terms for the Republic of Genoa, in a self proclaimed [Corsican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corsican_Republic) in 1755 under the leadership of [Pasquale Paoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasquale_Paoli), son of Giacinto Paoli.

The Republic of Genoa continued its slow decline well into the 18th century, losing its last [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) colony, the island fortress of [Tabarka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabarka), to the [Bey of Tunis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bey_of_Tunis) in 1742.[^cite_note-43]

The [Convention of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_of_Turin) of 1742, in which Austria allied with the [Kingdom of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia), caused some consternation in the Republic. Consequently, the Republic of Genoa signed a secret treaty with the Bourbon allies of [the Kingdom of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France), [the Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) and [the Kingdom of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Naples). On 26 June 1745, the Republic of Genoa declared war on the Kingdom of Sardinia. This decision would prove disastrous for Genoa, which later surrendered to the Austrians in September 1746 and was briefly occupied before a revolt liberated the city two months later.[^cite_note-44]

The Republic of Genoa, in a weak state and not capable of suppressing the Corsican struggle for independence, was forced to cede [Corsica](5714828.md) to [France](5843419.md) in the 1768 [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles_\(1768\)). Only a year later, [Napoleon Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_Bonaparte) was born in Corsica.

In 1780, the Confetteria Romanengo was founded in Genoa.[^cite_note-45]

The direct invasion of [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) (during the [Campaigns of 1796](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campaigns_of_1796_in_the_French_Revolutionary_Wars)) and his representatives in Genoa was the final act that led to the fall of the Republic in early June, who overthrew the old elites which had ruled the state for all of its history, giving birth to the [Ligurian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_Republic) on 14 June 1797, under the military occupation of Napoleonic France.

### 19th century

Main article: [First French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire)

During the [Siege of Genoa (1800)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Genoa_\(1800\)), 30,000 of Genoa's 160,000 inhabitants had died of starvation and disease. After Bonaparte's seizure of power in France, a more conservative constitution was enacted, but the Ligurian Republic's life was short—in 1805 it was annexed by France, becoming the *[départements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A9partement_in_France)* of [Apennins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apennins), [Gênes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%AAnes), and [Montenotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenotte_\(department\)).[^cite_note-\:0-46]

#### The annexation to the Kingdom of Sardinia



A map of Italy in 1843

Main articles: [Duchy of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Genoa) and [Kingdom of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia)

Following the fall of Napoleon, Genoa regained ephemeral independence, with the name of the *Repubblica genovese*, which lasted less than a year. However, the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) established the annexation of the whole territories of the former Genoese Republic to the [Kingdom of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia), governed by the [House of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Savoy), contravening the principle of restoring the legitimate governments and monarchies of the old Republic.[^cite_note-47]

In the 19th century, Genoa consolidated its role as a major seaport and an important steel and shipbuilding centre. In 1853, [Giovanni Ansaldo](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni%20Ansaldo) founded [Gio. Ansaldo & C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gio._Ansaldo_&_C.) whose shipyards would build many passenger ships. In 1854, the ferry company [Costa Crociere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Crociere) was founded. In 1861 the [Registro Italiano Navale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Registro_Italiano_Navale) Italian register of shipping was created, and in 1879 the [Yacht Club Italiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yacht_Club_Italiano). The owner [Raffaele Rubattino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffaele_Rubattino) in 1881 was among the founders of the ferry company [Navigazione Generale Italiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigazione_Generale_Italiana) which then become the [Italian Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Line).[^cite_note-49] In 1870 Banca di Genova was founded which in 1895 changed its name to [Credito Italiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credito_Italiano) and in 1998 became [Unicredit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicredit). In 1874 the city was completely connected by railway lines to France and the rest of Italy: [Genoa-Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin-Genoa_railway), [Genoa-Ventimiglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa-Ventimiglia_railway), [Genoa-Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa-La_Spezia-Genoa_railway). In 1884 [Rinaldo Piaggio](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinaldo%20Piaggio) founded [Piaggio & C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio) that produced locomotives and railway carriages and then in 1923 began aircraft production. In 1888 the Banca Passadore was established. In 1898 the insurance company called [Alleanza Assicurazioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alleanza_Assicurazioni) was founded.

### 21st century



[Renzo Piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renzo_Piano)



[Marco Bucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Bucci_\(politician\)), [mayor of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Genoa) (27 June 2017 – 10 December 2024) and currently [President of Liguria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Liguria)

In 2001, Genoa hosted the [27th G8 summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/27th_G8_summit). It was overshadowed by violent [anti-globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-globalization_movement) protests, with one protester killed.[^cite_note-52] In 2003, the [Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Italiano_di_Tecnologia) (IIT) was established. In 2004, the [European Union](9317.md) designated Genoa as the [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture) for that year, along with the French city of [Lille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lille). In 2015, work began to secure the Genoa area, hit by the floods of 2010, 2011 and 2014, with the reconstruction and expansion of the coverage of the [Bisagno stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisagno_\(stream\)).[^cite_note-53] Work also began on the completion of the underground stream channel of the Ferreggiano river, which flooded several times in various floods, including the most tragic one in 1970.[^cite_note-54] In 2017, the architect [Renzo Piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renzo_Piano) donated the design of the Levante Waterfront[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] to the Municipality of Genoa; this project involves a radical transformation of the [Fiera di Genova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palasport_di_Genova), with the creation of a new dock and an urban park, the continuation of [Corso Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corso_Italia_\(Genoa\)) toward Porta Siberia and the construction of residential structures. In 2018, the first planning and study works began for the realization of the Waterfront of Levante project.[^cite_note-57] From 21 April to 6 May 2018, [Euroflora 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euroflora) took place, an exhibition of flowers and ornamental plants for the first time in the Parchi di Nervi venue rather than the historic venue of the [Fiera di Genova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palasport_di_Genova). On 14 August 2018 the [Ponte Morandi](58143323.md) viaduct bridge for motor vehicles collapsed during a torrential downpour, leading to 43 deaths.[^cite_note-58] The bridge's remains were demolished in August 2019. The replacement bridge, the [Genoa-Saint George Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa-Saint_George_Bridge), was inaugurated in August 2020 during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). The tragedy of the collapse of the [Morandi Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morandi_Bridge) and its rapid reconstruction with a new viaduct designed by Renzo Piano, which occurred during the pandemic, facilitated by a redefinition of the implementing rules of public procurement, which has been defined as the Genoa model,[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] is expected to hasten the construction of the Levante Waterfront and other important work in the city.

In 2021, Genoa Mayor [Marco Bucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Bucci_\(politician\)) and [President of Liguria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Liguria) [Giovanni Toti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Toti) launched a new plan for the modernization and redevelopment of the entirety of Genoa, which has as its fulcrum Renzo Piano's Levante Waterfront project.[^cite_note-61] From 23 April 2022 to 8 May 2022, [Euroflora 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euroflora) took place for the second time at the Nervi Parks.[^cite_note-62] In 2023 Genoa was the finish of [The Ocean Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ocean_Race).[^cite_note-63] In 2024 Genoa was the 2024 European Capital of Sport.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] On 7 March 2024, Mayor Bucci presented the vision of Genoa 2030, a development and urban renewal plan for Genoa to be completed in 2030.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

## Geography

The city of Genoa covers an area of 243 square kilometres (94 sq mi) between the [Ligurian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_Sea) and the [Apennine Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apennine_Mountains). It stretches along the coast for about 30 kilometres (19 mi) from the neighbourhood of [Voltri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltri) to Nervi, and for 10 kilometres (6.2 mi) from the coast to the north along the valleys [Polcevera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_Polcevera) and [Bisagno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisagno_\(stream\)). The territory of Genoa is popularly divided into 5 main zones: the centre, the west, the east, the [Polcevera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polcevera) and the Bisagno Valley. Although much of the city centre is at a low elevation, the territory surrounding it is mountainous, with undeveloped land usually in steep terrain.

Genoa is adjacent to two popular Ligurian vacation spots: [Camogli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camogli) and [Portofino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portofino). In the metropolitan area of Genoa lies [Aveto Natural Regional Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aveto_Natural_Regional_Park).

### Climate

Genoa has a [hot-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*), that is bordering closely on a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfa*), with plentiful precipitation due to its location on a [common storm track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_low). Due to its position between the sea and mountains over 1000 meters high, each neighborhood of Genoa has specific climatic characteristics.

The average yearly temperature is around 20 °C (68 °F) during the day and 14 °C (57 °F) at night. In the coldest months, the average temperature is 12 °C (54 °F) during the day and 6 °C (43 °F) at night. In the warmest months – July and August – the average temperature is 28 °C (82 °F) during the day and 22 °C (72 °F) at night. The daily temperature range is limited, with an average range of about 6 °C (11 °F) between high and low temperatures. Genoa also sees significant moderation from the sea, in stark contrast to areas behind the Ligurian mountains such as [Parma](24231.md), where summers are hotter and winters are quite cold.

Annually, the average 2.9 of nights recorded temperatures of ≤0 °C (32 °F) (mainly in January). The coldest temperature ever recorded was −8 °C (18 °F) in February 2012; the highest temperature ever recorded during the day is 38.5 °C (101 °F) in August 2015. Average annual number of days with temperatures of ≥30 °C (86 °F) is about 8, four days in July and August.[^cite_note-ServizioMeteorologico-69]

Average annual temperature of the sea is 17.5 °C (64 °F), from 13 °C (55 °F) in the period January–March to 25 °C (77 °F) in August. In the period from June to October, the average sea temperature exceeds 19 °C (66 °F).[^cite_note-weather2travel-70]

Genoa is also a windy city, especially during winter when [northern winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramontane) often bring cool air from the [Po Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Valley) (usually accompanied by lower temperatures, high pressure and clear skies). Another typical wind blows from [southeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirocco), mostly as a consequence of Atlantic disturbances and storms, bringing humid and warmer air from the sea. Snowfall is sporadic, but does occur almost every year, albeit big amounts in the city centre are rare.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] Genoa often receives heavy rainfall in autumn from strong convection. Even so, the overall number of precipitation days is quite modest. There are on average 11.57 days annually with thunder, which is more common from May to October than other times of the year.[^cite_note-NCEI-73]

Annual average [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity) is 68%, ranging from 63% in February to 73% in May.[^cite_note-ServizioMeteorologico-69]

Sunshine hours total above 2,200 per year, from an average 4 hours of sunshine duration per day in winter to average 9 hours in summer.

## Main sights

Genoa's past as a powerful maritime republic has endowed it with a wealth of artistic and architectural treasures. Its extensive historic centre, innovative Renaissance and Baroque palaces, and revitalised port area offer a diverse range of attractions.

### Genoa: Le Strade Nuove and the system of the Palazzi dei Rolli



[Doge's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doge's_Palace,_Genoa), ancient seat of the government of the oligarchic republic

The city's most significant cultural legacy is the [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md) *[Genoa: Le Strade Nuove and the system of the Palazzi dei Rolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa:_Le_Strade_Nuove_and_the_system_of_the_Palazzi_dei_Rolli)*. This site recognizes the unique urban planning of the late 16th and early 17th centuries, when the Republic of Genoa, at the apex of its power, undertook a project to create a district of prestigious residences.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-86] The city's ruling aristocracy financed a series of new thoroughfares (the *Strade Nuove*), most notably [Via Garibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Garibaldi_\(Genoa\)) (formerly Strada Nuova or Via Aurea), [Via Cairoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Cairoli_\(Genoa\)) (Strada Nuovissima), and [Via Balbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Balbi_\(Genoa\)).[^cite_note-ReferenceB-87]

These magnificent homes were part of a public lodging system managed through official registers called the *Rolli*. When state visits occurred, a palace was chosen from these lists to host dignitaries such as princes or ambassadors, with the grandeur of the palace matched to the guest's rank.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-87] The palaces themselves are architectural marvels, adapting to the tight urban landscape with vertical solutions like spectacular open staircases, courtyards, and hanging gardens.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-86] The Flemish painter [Peter Paul Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Paul_Rubens) famously compiled a book of drawings of these palaces, presenting them as an exemplary model of living for European nobility.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-87]

Many of these palaces are now open to the public. Three of the most important on Via Garibaldi—[Palazzo Rosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Rosso), [Palazzo Bianco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Bianco), and [Palazzo Doria-Tursi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Doria-Tursi)—are collectively known as the **Musei di Strada Nuova**:[^cite_note-ReferenceC-88]

- **Palazzo Rosso** ("Red Palace") is a historic house museum retaining the original furnishings and art collections of the Brignole-Sale family amid frescoed rooms.

- **Palazzo Bianco** ("White Palace") is Liguria's main art gallery, featuring works by Genoese, Italian (including [Caravaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravaggio)), Flemish, and Spanish artists.

- **Palazzo Doria-Tursi** is the grandest of the three and houses an expansion of the gallery as well as decorative arts, coins, and the official weights and measures of the old Republic. It is also home to "Il Cannone", the famous violin owned by [Niccolò Paganini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Paganini).

  [^cite_note-ReferenceC-88]

Twice a year, during the spring and autumn **Rolli Days**, many of these palaces open their doors to the public, offering a glimpse into their historic treasures.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-87]

### Historic Centre



[Porta Soprana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Soprana?action=edit\&redlink=1), a restored 12th-century gate from the Barbarossa Walls.

With an area of 113 hectares, Genoa's [historic centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_center_of_Genoa) is one of the largest and most densely populated medieval centres in Europe. It is characterized by a maze of narrow alleys (*caruggi*) that unexpectedly open onto small squares. This dense urban fabric is a melting pot of cultures, where historic shops (*botteghe storiche*) stand alongside noble palaces and churches. The architectural styles are layered: a medieval wall might serve as the base for a 14th-century building, and Gothic loggias are now integrated into modern businesses. Many street names, like Via degli Orefici (Goldsmiths' Street), recall the craft guilds that once dominated the area.[^cite_note-89]

### Principal buildings

- **[Doge's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doge's_Palace,_Genoa)**: The ancient seat of government for the Republic, the Doge's Palace has a history reflecting the city's own. Its origins date to 1284, after Genoa's victory over Pisa at the Battle of Meloria, and it became the official residence of the [Doge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doge_of_Genoa) in 1339. The palace was transformed into a grand Mannerist structure in the late 16th century, but a fire in 1777 led to a Neoclassical reconstruction of its main façade. After extensive restoration, the palace reopened in 1992 as a major cultural centre, hosting art exhibitions, conferences, and events.

  [^cite_note-90]

- **[Cathedral of San Lorenzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_Cathedral)**: Genoa's cathedral is a magnificent building with a distinctive black-and-white striped façade. Founded around 1098, it was consecrated in 1118 and elevated to an archbishopric in 1133. Following a fire in 1296, it was partly rebuilt in the Gothic style. Later additions include the 15th-century Chapel of St. John the Baptist, which houses the relics of the city's patron saint, and a dome designed by [Galeazzo Alessi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galeazzo_Alessi) in the 16th century. Inside, a replica of an unexploded British naval shell that struck the church during a World War II bombardment serves as a memorial.

  [^cite_note-91]

- **[Porta Soprana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Soprana?action=edit\&redlink=1)**: The best-known survivor of the city's medieval fortifications, Porta Soprana was the main eastern entrance in the 12th-century "Barbarossa Walls," built to defend the Republic's independence from Emperor Frederick Barbarossa. An inscription on the gate declares to those entering: "If you bring peace, accost these doors, if you seek war, sad and defeated you will retreat." It was restored to its likely medieval appearance in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

  [^cite_note-92]

### Other City Sights



Royal Palace of Genoa, 16th century

The symbols of the city are the [Lanterna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighthouse_of_Genoa), a 77-metre-high (253 ft) tower visible from over 30 km (19 mi) away, and the monumental fountain of [Piazza De Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_De_Ferrari_\(Genoa\)), the city's main square. Nearby are the [Teatro Carlo Felice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Carlo_Felice), Genoa's principal opera house, and the Mazzini Gallery, a 19th-century glass-roofed arcade. A popular destination is the ancient seaside district of [Boccadasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boccadasse), a picturesque cove with colourful boats that marks the end of the city's main promenade, [Corso Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corso_Italia_\(Genoa\)). For scenic views, a public elevator provides access to the [Belvedere Castelletto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castelletto_\(Genoa\)), a panoramic viewpoint over the city.

### The Old Port and Modern Developments



[Palace of Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_San_Giorgio), built in 1260

The [Old Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Harbour_\(Genoa\)) (Porto Antico) area was revitalised for the 1992 Columbian Celebrations, largely to the designs of Genoese architect [Renzo Piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renzo_Piano). Its centrepiece is the [Aquarium of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquarium_of_Genoa), one of the largest in Europe. Piano also designed the [Bigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bigo_\(Genoa\)), a panoramic lift, and the [Biosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere_\(Genoa\)) (known as "Piano's bubble"), a glass sphere housing a tropical ecosystem. Nearby, the "[Matitone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matitone_\(Genoa\))" ("big pencil") is a prominent modern skyscraper in the San Benigno business district.

### Beyond the City Centre

The municipality of Genoa stretches along 33 km (21 mi) of coastline.

- **To the east**, [Nervi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervi) is a gateway to the Ligurian Riviera di Levante. It offers the Passeggiata Anita Garibaldi, a scenic coastal walk, and the Parchi di Nervi, a complex of historic villas that now house modern art museums.

- **To the west**, [Pegli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegli) is known for the historic [Villa Durazzo-Pallavicini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Durazzo-Pallavicini) and its romantic botanical garden.

- **Nearby attractions**, accessible by ferry from the [Old Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Harbour_\(Genoa\)), include coastal towns such as [Camogli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camogli), [Portofino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portofino), and the [Cinque Terre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinque_Terre). In the seabed off the coast of the nearby [San Fruttuoso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fruttuoso_Abbey) abbey lies the submerged bronze statue of the [Christ of the Abyss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_of_the_Abyss).

### Buildings and palaces



The Mirror Gallery of the Royal Palace

The main features of central Genoa include the [Piazza De Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piazza_De_Ferrari_\(Genoa\)), around which are the [Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Carlo_Felice) and the [Palace of the Doges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Ducale_\(Genoa\)). Nearby, just outside the medieval city walls, is located [Christopher Columbus House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus_House) where [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) is said to have lived as a child, although the current building is an 18th-century reconstruction of the original which was destroyed by the French naval bombing of 1684.

In the old port area called [Porto Antico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_Antico?action=edit\&redlink=1), is located [Palazzo di San Giorgio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_di_San_Giorgio). In the Middle Ages, this palace was the headquarters of the [Bank of Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Saint_George). In its prisons, [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo) and [Rustichello da Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rustichello_da_Pisa) composed [The Travels of Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Travels_of_Marco_Polo).



[Palazzo Rosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Rosso) in Via Garibaldi

[Strada Nuova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Garibaldi_\(Genoa\)) (now Via Garibaldi), in the old city, alongside [Via Cairoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Cairoli) and [via Balbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Balbi?action=edit\&redlink=1), was inscribed on the [World Heritage List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_List) in 2006. This district was designed in the mid-16th century to accommodate Mannerist palaces built by the city's most eminent families.

Of the many palaces built by the nobility in the city center of Genoa, 114 have not been substantially altered (see also [Rolli di Genova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolli_di_Genova)): among these, 42 *[Palazzi dei Rolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzi_dei_Rolli)* are inscribed on the [World Heritage List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_List).[^cite_note-93] The most famous are [Palazzo Rosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Rosso_\(Genoa\)), [Palazzo Bianco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Bianco_\(Genoa\)), [Palazzo Doria Tursi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Tursi), [Palazzo Gerolamo Grimaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Gerolamo_Grimaldi), [Palazzo Podestà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Nicolosio_Lomellino), [Palazzo Reale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Reale_\(Genoa\)), [Palazzo Angelo Giovanni Spinola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Angelo_Giovanni_Spinola), [Palazzo Pietro Spinola di San Luca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Pietro_Spinola_di_San_Luca), [Palazzo Spinola di Pellicceria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Spinola_di_Pellicceria), Palazzo Cicala. Palazzo Bianco, Palazzo Rosso and Palazzo Doria Tursi are also known as *Musei di Strada Nuova* and host the renowned art collection bequeathed to the city by the Genoese filantropist [Maria Brignole Sale De Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Brignole_Sale_De_Ferrari), Duchess of Galliera, as well as the violins of the Genoese violinist Niccolò Paganini.[^cite_note-94] The Flemish artist and diplomat [Peter Paul Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Paul_Rubens) wrote [Palazzi di Genova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzi_di_Genova) in 1622, a book with his own depiction of the palaces of Genoa in the 17th century.[^cite_note-95]

The [Genoese Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese_Renaissance?action=edit\&redlink=1) began with the construction of [Villa del Principe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_del_Principe) commissioned by [Andrea Doria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Doria): the architects were [Giovanni Angelo Montorsoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Angelo_Montorsoli) and [Giovanni Ponzello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Ponzello), the interior was painted by [Perino del Vaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perino_del_Vaga) and the garden fountain was realised by [Taddeo Carlone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taddeo_Carlone).[^cite_note-96]

In 1548 [Galeazzo Alessi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galeazzo_Alessi), with the project of [Villa Giustiniani-Cambiaso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Giustiniani-Cambiaso?action=edit\&redlink=1), designed a new prototype of Genoese palace that would be an inspiration to other architects working in Genoa as [Bartolomeo Bianco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartolomeo_Bianco), [Pietro Antonio Corradi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Antonio_Corradi?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Rocco Lurago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocco_Lurago), [Giovan Battista Castello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovan_Battista_Castello), and [Bernardino Cantone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardino_Cantone?action=edit\&redlink=1).



[Palazzo Spinola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Spinola_di_Pellicceria)

Scattered around the city are many [villas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villas_of_Genoa), built between the fifteenth and the twentieth centuries. Among the best known are: [Villa Brignole Sale Duchessa di Galliera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Brignole_Sale_Duchessa_di_Galliera), [Villa Durazzo-Pallavicini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Durazzo-Pallavicini), [Villa Doria Centurione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Doria_Centurione?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Villa Durazzo Bombrini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Durazzo_Bombrini?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Villa Serra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Serra?action=edit\&redlink=1), Villa Giustiniani-Cambiaso, [Villa Rossi Martini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Rossi_Martini?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Villa Imperiale Scassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Imperiale_Scassi?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Villa Grimaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Grimaldi,_Genoa?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Villa Negrone Moro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Negrone_Moro?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Villa Rosazza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Rosazza), [Villetta Di Negro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villetta_Di_Negro?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Villa delle Peschiere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_delle_Peschiere), [Villa Imperiale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Imperiale?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Villa Saluzzo Bombrini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Saluzzo_Bombrini?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Villa Grimaldi Fassio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Grimaldi_Fassio).



Loggia of [Villa del Principe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_del_Principe)

As it regards the 19th century remember the architects Ignazio Gardella (senior), and [Carlo Barabino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Barabino) which among other things, realises together with Giovanni Battista Resasco, the [Monumental Cemetery of Staglieno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumental_Cemetery_of_Staglieno). The cemetery is renowned for its statues and sepulchral monuments that preserve the mortal remains of notable personalities, including [Giuseppe Mazzini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Mazzini), [Fabrizio De André](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrizio_De_Andr%C3%A9), and [Constance Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constance_Lloyd) (Oscar Wilde's wife). In the first half of the 19th century they are completed the [Albergo dei Poveri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albergo_dei_Poveri_\(Genoa\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the [Acquedotto storico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquedotto_storico?action=edit\&redlink=1). In 1901 [Giovanni Antonio Porcheddu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Antonio_Porcheddu?action=edit\&redlink=1) realised the *Silos Granari*.



[Arco della Vittoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arco_della_Vittoria)

### Old Harbour



The [galleon Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptune_\(galleon\)) in the Old Harbour

The [Old Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Harbour_\(Genoa\)) ("Porto Antico" in Italian) is the ancient part of the port of Genoa. The harbour gave access to outside communities creating a good geographical situation for the city.[^cite_note-\:0-46] The city is spread out geographically along a section of the Liguria coast, which makes trading by ship possible. Before the development of car, train, and airplane travel, the main outside access for the city was the sea, as the surrounding mountains made trade north by land more difficult than coastal trade. Trade routes have always connected Genoa on an international scale, with increasingly farther reach starting from trade along Europe's coastline before the medieval period to today's connection across continents.[^cite_note-98] In its heyday the [Genoese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese_Navy) was a prominent power in the Mediterranean.

As the Genoa harbour was so important to the merchants for their own economic success, other nearby harbours and ports were seen as competition for a landing point for foreign traders. In the 16th century, the Genovese worked to destroy the local shipping competition, the Savona harbour.[^cite_note-\:0-46] Taking matters into their own hands, the Genoa merchants and the politically powerful in Genoa attacked the harbour of Savona with stones.[^cite_note-\:0-46] This action was taken to preserve the economic stability and wealth of the city during the rise in prominence of Savona. The Genovese would go as far as to war with other coastal, trading cities such as Venice,[^cite_note-\:0-46] to protect the trade industry.

[Renzo Piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renzo_Piano) redeveloped the area for public access, restoring the historical buildings (like the Cotton warehouses) and creating new landmarks like the [Aquarium of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquarium_of_Genoa), the [Bigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bigo_\(Genoa\)) and recently a [Biosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere_\(Genoa\)) ("la Bolla"). The main touristic attractions of this area are the famous Aquarium and the Museum of the Sea (MuMA). In 2007 these attracted almost 1.7 million visitors.[^cite_note-99]

### Walls and fortresses

Main article: [Walls of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walls_of_Genoa)



The Porta Soprana

The city of Genoa during its long history at least since the ninth century had been protected by different lines of [defensive walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_wall). Large portions of these walls remain today, and Genoa has more and longer walls than any other city in Italy. The main [city walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_walls) are known as "Ninth century walls", "Barbarossa Walls" (12th century), "Fourteenth century walls", "Sixteenth century walls" and "New Walls" ("Mura Nuove" in Italian). The more imposing walls, built in the first half of the 17th century on the ridge of hills around the city, have a length of almost 20 km (12 mi). Some fortresses stand along the perimeter of the "New Walls" or close them.

### Parks

Main article: [The Parks of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Parks_of_Genoa)



The gardens of [Villa Durazzo-Pallavicini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Durazzo-Pallavicini)

Genoa has 82,000 square metres (880,000 square feet) of public parks in the city centre, such as Villetta Di Negro which is right in the heart of the town, overlooking the historical centre. Many bigger green spaces are situated outside the centre: in the east are the Parks of [Nervi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervi) (96,000 square metres or 1,030,000 square feet) overlooking the sea, in the west the beautiful gardens of [Villa Durazzo Pallavicini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Durazzo-Pallavicini) and its [Giardino botanico Clelia Durazzo Grimaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giardino_botanico_Clelia_Durazzo_Grimaldi) (265,000 square metres or 2,850,000 square feet). The numerous [villas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villas_of_Genoa) and palaces of the city also have their own gardens, like Palazzo del Principe, Villa Doria, [Palazzo Bianco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Bianco_\(Genoa\)) and Palazzo Tursi, Palazzo Nicolosio Lomellino, [Albertis Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albertis_Castle), Villa Rosazza, Villa Croce, Villa Imperiale Cattaneo, Villa Bombrini, Villa Brignole Sale Duchessa di Galliera, Villa Serra and many more.[^cite_note-100]

The city is surrounded by natural parks such as [Parco naturale regionale dell'Antola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_naturale_regionale_dell'Antola), [Parco naturale regionale del Beigua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_naturale_regionale_del_Beigua), [Aveto Natural Regional Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aveto_Natural_Regional_Park) and the [Ligurian Sea Cetacean Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_Sea_Cetacean_Sanctuary) (a marine protected area).

### Aquarium of Genoa

Main article: [Aquarium of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquarium_of_Genoa)

The [Aquarium of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquarium_of_Genoa) (in [Italian](14708.md): *Acquario di Genova*) is the largest [aquarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquarium) in Italy and among the largest in Europe. Built for [Genoa Expo '92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_Expo_'92), it is an educational, scientific and cultural centre. Its mission is to educate and raise public awareness as regards conservation, management and responsible use of aquatic environments. It welcomes over 1.2 million visitors a year.

Control of the entire environment, including the temperature, filtration and lighting of the tanks was provided by local Automation Supplier Orsi Automazione, acquired in 2001 by [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens). The Aquarium of Genoa is co-ordinating the AquaRing EU project. It also provides scientific expertise and a great deal of content for AquaRing, including documents, images, academic content and interactive online courses, via its Online Resource Centre.[^cite_note-101]

Genoese painters active in the 14th century include [Barnaba da Modena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnaba_da_Modena) and his local followers [Nicolò da Voltri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%B2_da_Voltri) and at the same time, the sculptor [Giovanni Pisano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Pisano) reached Genoa to make the monument for [Margaret of Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_of_Brabant), whose remains are today housed in the [Museum of Sant'Agostino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Sant'Agostino?action=edit\&redlink=1).

In the 16th century along with the flourishing trade between the Republic of Genoa and Flanders also grew the cultural exchanges. The painters [Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_de_Wael) and [Cornelis de Wael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelis_de_Wael) lived in Genoa for a long time, where they played the role of a magnet for many Flemish painters like [Jaan Roos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Roos_\(painter\)), [Giacomo Legi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Legi), [Jan Matsys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Matsys), [Andries van Eertvelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andries_van_Eertvelt) and [Vincent Malo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Malo).

This creative environment also attracted the two most important Flemish painters, [Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubens) and [Van Dyck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Dyck), who along with [Bernardo Strozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Strozzi).[^cite_note-premier.net-102] gave life to the Genoese Painting School of the 17th century.

Much of the city's art is found in its churches and palaces, where there are numerous Renaissance, Baroque and Rococo frescos. They are rich in works of art the [Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_Cathedral), the [Chiesa del Gesù e dei Santi Ambrogio e Andrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiesa_del_Ges%C3%B9_e_dei_Santi_Ambrogio_e_Andrea?action=edit\&redlink=1) where [*The Circumcision*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Circumcision_\(Rubens\)) and the "*Miracles of St. Ignatius*" by [Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Paul_Rubens), the *Assunzione della Vergine* by [Guido Reni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Reni). The [Church of San Donato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Donato_\(Genoa\)) contains works of [Barnaba da Modena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnaba_da_Modena), [Nicolò da Voltri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%B2_da_Voltri) and [Joos van Cleve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joos_van_Cleve),[^cite_note-premier.net-102] the [Church of Santo Stefano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Stefano_\(Genoa\)) [The Stoning of St. Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Stoning_of_St._Stephen?action=edit\&redlink=1) by [Giulio Romano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giulio_Romano) and the [Church of Santa Maria Assunta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_Assunta,_Genoa) the sculptures by [Filippo Parodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filippo_Parodi) and [Pierre Puget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Puget), very interesting is the [Santa Maria di Castello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_di_Castello). But most of the works are kept in the Palaces like [Palazzo Bianco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Bianco) where "[*Ecce Homo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecce_Homo_\(Caravaggio,_Genoa\))" by [Caravaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravaggio), "[*Susannah and the Elders*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susannah_and_the_Elders_\(Veronese\))" by [Veronese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Veronese), and the *[Garden Party in Albaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_Party_in_Albaro?action=edit\&redlink=1)* by [Magnasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Magnasco) are kept; [Palazzo Rosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Rosso) with the [Portrait of Anton Giulio Brignole-Sale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait_of_Anton_Giulio_Brignole-Sale?action=edit\&redlink=1) by [van Dyck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_van_Dyck); [*Cleopatra morente*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dying_Cleopatra) by [Guercino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guercino) and works of [Dürer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer); [Bernardo Strozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Strozzi); [Mattia Preti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattia_Preti); [Veronese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Veronese); [Palazzo Spinola di Pellicceria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Spinola_di_Pellicceria) where the "*[Portrait of Giovanni Carlo Doria on Horseback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait_of_Giovanni_Carlo_Doria_on_Horseback)*" by [Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Paul_Rubens) and [*Ecce Homo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecce_Homo_\(Antonello_da_Messina,_Genoa\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) by [Antonello da Messina](254609.md) (see also [the series of Ecce Homo by Antonello da Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecce_Homo_\(Antonello_da_Messina\))) are kept; [Palazzo Tursi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Tursi) with the [Penitent Magdalene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penitent_Magdalene_\(Canova\)) by [Canova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Canova); and [Palazzo Reale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Reale) which contains works of [Strozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Strozzi), [Gaulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Gaulli), [Tintoretto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tintoretto), [van Dyck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_van_Dyck), [Simon Vouet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Vouet), and [Guercino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guercino).

The most important Genoese painters are: [Luca Cambiaso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Cambiasi); [Bernardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Castello) and [Valerio Castello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerio_Castello); [Giovanni Benedetto Castiglione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Benedetto_Castiglione); [Domenico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_Piola) and [Paolo Gerolamo Piola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Gerolamo_Piola); [Gregorio De Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorio_De_Ferrari); [Bernardo Strozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Strozzi); [Giovanni Battista Gaulli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Gaulli) and [Alessandro Magnasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Magnasco). Sculptors include [Filippo Parodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filippo_Parodi), the wood sculptor [Anton Maria Maragliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Maria_Maragliano), [Francesco Maria Schiaffino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Maria_Schiaffino) and [Agostino Carlini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agostino_Carlini) who was member of the [Royal Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Academy).

The famous humanist author, architect, poet and philosopher [Leon Battista Alberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Battista_Alberti) was born in Genoa on 14 February 1404. [Simonetta Vespucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simonetta_Vespucci), considered the most beautiful woman of her time, was also born in Genoa. She is portrayed in *[The Birth of Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Birth_of_Venus)* and [Primavera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primavera_\(painting\)) by [Sandro Botticelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandro_Botticelli) and in [*Portrait of Simonetta Vespucci*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait_of_Simonetta_Vespucci_\(Piero_di_Cosimo\)) by [Piero di Cosimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_di_Cosimo).

Genoa is also famous for its numerous tapestries which decorated the city's many salons. Whilst the patrician palaces and [villas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villas_of_Genoa) in the city were and still are austere and majestic, the interiors tended to be luxurious and elaborate, often full of tapestries, many of which were Flemish.[^cite_note-premier.net-102] Famous is the [Genoese lace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese_lace) called with its name of Turkish origin *macramè*. Very used in Genoa is the [cobblestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobblestone) called *Risseu* and a kind of [azulejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azulejo) called *laggioni*.

Genoa has been likened by many to a Mediterranean New York, perhaps for its high houses that in the Middle Ages were the equivalent of today's skyscrapers, perhaps for the sea route Genoa-New York which in past centuries has been travelled by millions of emigrants. The architect [Renzo Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renzo_Picasso) in his visionary designs reinforces this strange affinity between the two cities.

The [Monumental Cemetery of Staglieno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumental_Cemetery_of_Staglieno) features 19th- and early-20th-century sculptures, including the *[Monteverde Angel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monteverde_Angel)* by [Giulio Monteverde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giulio_Monteverde), alongside works by artists such as [Augusto Rivalta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_Rivalta), [Leonardo Bistolfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_Bistolfi), [Edoardo Alfieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edoardo_Alfieri), and [Santo Varni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Varni).

Amongst the most notable Genoese painters of the 19th century and of the first half of the 20th century are [Tammar Luxoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tammar_Luxoro), [Ernesto Rayper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Rayper), [Rubaldo Merello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubaldo_Merello), and Antonio Giuseppe Santagata. The sculptor [Francesco Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Messina) also grew up in Genoa.

In 1967 the Genoese historian, critic and curator [Germano Celant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germano_Celant) coined the term [Arte Povera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arte_Povera). [Enrico Accatino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Accatino) was another important art theorist and [Emanuele Luzzati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emanuele_Luzzati) was the production designer and illustrator like [Lorenzo Mongiardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Mongiardino), also a production designer and architect. Two other important artists are [Emilio Scanavino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Scanavino) and [Vanessa Beecroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanessa_Beecroft).

The yearly [International Cartoonists Exhibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cartoonists_Exhibition) was founded in 1972 in [Rapallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapallo), near Genoa. A notable figure is the illustrator and comics artist [Giovan Battista Carpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovan_Battista_Carpi).

### Literature



*[Golden Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Legend)*, 1290

"Anonymous of Genoa" was one of the first authors in Liguria and Italy who wrote verses in the Vernacular. It explained that in Genoa [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo) and [Rustichello da Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rustichello_da_Pisa), in the prisons of [Palazzo San Giorgio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_San_Giorgio), wrote [The Travels of Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Travels_of_Marco_Polo). The [Golden Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Legend) is a collection of [hagiographies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagiography) written by the Archbishop of Genoa [Jacobus de Voragine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobus_de_Voragine). To animate the Genoese literary environment of the 16th century were [Gabriello Chiabrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriello_Chiabrera) and [Ansaldo Cebà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansaldo_Ceb%C3%A0), the latter best known for his correspondence with [Sara Copia Sullam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Copia_Sullam). The city has been the birthplace of the historian [Caffaro di Rustico da Caschifellone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caffaro_di_Rustico_da_Caschifellone); of the poet "Martin Piaggio"; of the famous historian, philosopher and journalist [Giuseppe Mazzini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Mazzini); of the writer [Piero Jahier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_Jahier); of the poet Nobel Prize [Eugenio Montale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Montale). The writer and translator [Fernanda Pivano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernanda_Pivano), the journalist "Vito Elio Petrucci" and the poet [Edoardo Sanguineti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edoardo_Sanguineti), the literary critic [Carlo Bo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Bo) instead was born in [Sestri Levante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sestri_Levante) near Genoa. We have also remember the dialet poet [Edoardo Firpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edoardo_Firpo?action=edit\&redlink=1), the dialect "poeta crepuscolare" Giambattista Vigo, and the symbolist [Ceccardo Roccatagliata Ceccardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceccardo_Roccatagliata_Ceccardi?action=edit\&redlink=1).

The city of Genoa has been an inspiration to many writers and poets among whom: [Dino Campana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Campana), [Camillo Sbarbaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camillo_Sbarbaro), [Gaspare Invrea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspare_Invrea?action=edit\&redlink=1) who wrote "The mouth of the wolf" and [Giorgio Caproni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Caproni). Between the alleys of the historical centre there is the Old [Libreria Bozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libreria_Bozzi). The "Berio Civic Library" houses the precious manuscript entitled "The Durazzo Book of Hours". In the first half of the 20th century, the Mazzini Gallery's was a meeting place of many artists, writers and intellectuals among whom [Guido Gozzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Gozzano), [Salvatore Quasimodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Quasimodo), Camillo Sbarbaro, [Francesco Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Messina), [Pierangelo Baratono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierangelo_Baratono?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Eugenio Montale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenio_Montale). In the 1930s the [Circoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circoli) magazine was active in Genoa, and after World War II the "Il Gallo" magazine. Coveted and known from the 1960s to the 1980s was the Genoese literary lounge animated by the writer [Minnie Alzona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnie_Alzona?action=edit\&redlink=1). Dutch writer [Ilja Leonard Pfeijffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilja_Leonard_Pfeijffer) wrote "La Superba", a novel in which Genoa is prominently featured. This was followed by the autobiographical novel "Brieven uit Genua".

Since 1995, every June in Genoa the **Genoa International Poetry Festival** takes place, conceived by [Claudio Pozzani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Pozzani?action=edit\&redlink=1) with the help of [Massimo Bacigalupo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimo_Bacigalupo).

### Music

Main article: [Music of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Genoa)



The neoclassical [Teatro Carlo Felice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Carlo_Felice)

Genoa was a centre of [Occitan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_literature) in Italy and for this reason it developed an important school of [troubadours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troubadour): [Lanfranc Cigala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanfranc_Cigala), [Jacme Grils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacme_Grils), [Bonifaci Calvo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonifaci_Calvo), [Luchetto Gattilusio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luchetto_Gattilusio), [Guillelma de Rosers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillelma_de_Rosers), and [Simon Doria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Doria).

Genoa is the birthplace of the composer [Simone Molinaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_Molinaro), violinist and composer [Niccolò Paganini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Paganini), violinist [Camillo Sivori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camillo_Sivori) and composer [Cesare Pugni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Pugni). In addition, the famous violin maker [Paolo de Barbieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_de_Barbieri). Paganini's violin, [Il Cannone Guarnerius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Cannone_Guarnerius), is kept in Palazzo Tursi. The city is the site of the [Niccolò Paganini Music Conservatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Paganini_Music_Conservatory) which was originally established as the Scuola Gratuita di Canto in 1829.[^cite_note-103]

[Alessandro Stradella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Stradella), a composer of the middle baroque, lived in Genoa and was assassinated in 1682.

[Felice Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felice_Romani) was a poet who wrote many librettos for the opera composers like [Gaetano Donizetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaetano_Donizetti) and [Vincenzo Bellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincenzo_Bellini). [Giovanni Ruffini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Ruffini) was another poet known for writing the libretto of the opera *[Don Pasquale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Pasquale)* for its composer.

In 1847, [Goffredo Mameli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goffredo_Mameli) and [Michele Novaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michele_Novaro) composed "[Il Canto degli Italiani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Canto_degli_Italiani)".

In 1857, debuted the work of [Giuseppe Verdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Verdi) entitled *[Simon Boccanegra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Boccanegra)* inspired by the first Doge of Genoa, Simone Boccanegra.

Genoa is also the birthplace of the conductor [Fabio Luisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Luisi) and of many opera singers like [Giuseppe Taddei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Taddei), [Margherita Carosio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margherita_Carosio), [Luciana Serra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciana_Serra), [Ottavio Garaventa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottavio_Garaventa?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Luisa Maragliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luisa_Maragliano) and [Daniela Dessì](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniela_Dess%C3%AC).

The oldest theatre in Genoa was the [Teatro del Falcone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_del_Falcone?action=edit\&redlink=1). Active since the 16th century, it was the second public theatre in Italy, only preceded by the one in the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice).[^cite_note-beniculturali-104] It was followed by the Teatro delle Vigne which, however, along with other important theaters in the city (Teatro Margherita, [Teatro Paganini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Paganini), Teatro Colombo), was demolished between the 19th and 20th centuries, either to make way for urban expansion or due to damage caused by [bombing of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Genoa_in_World_War_II) during [World War II](32927.md).

The [Teatro Carlo Felice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Carlo_Felice), the main opera theatre in the city, was built in 1828 in the Piazza De Ferrari, and named for the monarch of the then [Kingdom of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Sardinia) (which included the present regions of [Sardinia](29376.md), Piedmont and [Liguria](71864.md)). The theatre was the centre of music and social life in the 19th century. On various occasions in the history of the theatre, presentations have been conducted by [Mascagni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Mascagni), [Richard Strauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Strauss), [Hindemith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Hindemith) and [Stravinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Stravinsky). Other prominent Genoese theaters are the [Teatro Nazionale di Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Nazionale_di_Genoa?action=edit\&redlink=1), Politeama Genovese, Teatro di Sant'Agostino and Teatro Gustavo Modena.

On the occasion of the [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) celebration in 1992, new musical life was given to the area around the old port, including the restoration of the house of [Paganini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Paganini) and presentations of the *[trallalero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trallalero)*, the traditional singing of Genoese dock workers.

The trallalero, traditional music in the Genoese dialect, is a [polyphonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphonic) vocal music, performed by five men and several songs. The [trallalero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trallalero) are ancient songs that have their roots in the Mediterranean tradition. Another aspect of the traditional Genoese music is the "Nostalgic Song". The principal authors and singers of the Nostalgic Song in Genoese dialect are [Mario Cappello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Cappello?action=edit\&redlink=1) who wrote the piece "[Ma se ghe penso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_se_ghe_penso)" (English: "But if I think about it"), a memory of Genoa by an emigrant to Argentina, [Giuseppe Marzari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Marzari?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Agostino Dodero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agostino_Dodero_\(composer\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) up to [I Trilli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Trilli?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Piero Parodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piero_Parodi?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Buby Senarega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buby_Senarega?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Franca Lai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franca_Lai?action=edit\&redlink=1). The traditional Nostalgic Song will have a great influence on the so-called *[Scuola Genovese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Genovese)* (Genoese School) of singer-songwriters that in some cases will mix the nostalgic feeling with pop and jazz atmospheres.

The singer [Natalino Otto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalino_Otto) started the swing genre in Italy and his friend and colleague [Pippo Barzizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pippo_Barzizza) was a composer, arranger, conductor and music director. Other musicians, composers and arrangers are [Angelo Francesco Lavagnino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelo_Francesco_Lavagnino), [Gian Piero Reverberi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Piero_Reverberi), [Gian Franco Reverberi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Franco_Reverberi), [Oscar Prudente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Prudente), [Pivio and Aldo De Scalzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pivio_and_Aldo_De_Scalzi).

Genoa in the second half of the 20th century was famous for an important school of Italian singer-songwriters, so-called *[Scuola Genovese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuola_Genovese)*, that includes [Umberto Bindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umberto_Bindi), [Luigi Tenco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Tenco), [Gino Paoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gino_Paoli), [Bruno Lauzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Lauzi), [Fabrizio de André](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrizio_de_Andr%C3%A9), [Ivano Fossati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivano_Fossati), [Angelo Branduardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelo_Branduardi)" and [Francesco Baccini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Baccini). [Nino Ferrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nino_Ferrer) was also born in Genoa. In the 70s there were formed in Genoa numerous bands of [Italian progressive rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_progressive_rock) like [New Trolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Trolls), [Picchio dal Pozzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picchio_dal_Pozzo), [Latte e Miele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latte_e_Miele), and Delirium. Today we point the band [Buio Pesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buio_Pesto) and [The Banshee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Banshee_\(band\)) band.

Some songs about the city of Genoa are part of Italian popular culture, like "[Via del Campo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_del_Campo)" and "La Città Vecchia", both by [Fabrizio de André](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrizio_de_Andr%C3%A9), "Genova per noi" by [Paolo Conte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Conte), "La Casa in Via del Campo" the song also sung by [Amalia Rodrigues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalia_Rodrigues) and "Piazza Alimonda" the song about the facts of [Genoa 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/27th_G8_summit) by [Francesco Guccini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Guccini).

[Fabrizio de André](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrizio_de_Andr%C3%A9) in 1984 released the album *[Crêuza de mä](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cr%C3%AAuza_de_m%C3%A4)*, totally written in Genoese dialect.

I Madrigalisti di Genova is a vocal and instrumental group formed in 1958 which specialised in medieval and Renaissance repertoire.

The city has numerous music festivals, among which are Concerts at [San Fruttuoso abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fruttuoso_abbey), Premio Paganini, I Concerti di San Torpete, International Music Festival Genova, We Love Jazz, Gezmatz Festival & Workshop, and Goa-Boa Festival. In the town of [Santa Margherita Ligure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Margherita_Ligure) the ancient abbey of Cervara is often the site of chamber music.

[Giovine Orchestra Genovese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovine_Orchestra_Genovese), one of the oldest concert societies in Italy, was founded in Genoa in 1912.

### Language

Main article: [Genoese dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese_dialect)

The Genoese dialect (*Zeneize*) is the most important dialect of the [Ligurian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_language_\(Romance\)), and is commonly spoken in Genoa alongside Italian. [Ligurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_language_\(Romance\)) is listed by [Ethnologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnologue) as a language in its own right, of the [Romance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages) branch, the [Ligurian Romance language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_\(Romance_language\)), and not to be confused with the [ancient Ligurian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_\(ancient_language\)). Like the languages of [Lombardy](43807.md), Piedmont, and surrounding regions, it is of [Gallo-Italic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo-Italic_languages) derivation.

### Flag



St. George's flag flying on the [Doge's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doge's_Palace,_Genoa) in Genoa



[Gonfalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonfalon) of Genoa

The **flag of Genoa** is a [St. George's Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George's_Cross), a red cross on a white field.&#x20;

Genoa's patron saint was Saint [Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_of_Rome) until at least 958, but the Genoese transferred their allegiance to [Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_George) (and Saint [John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Baptist)) at some point during the 11th or 12th century, most likely with the rising popularity of the [military saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_saint) during the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades). Genoa also had a banner displaying a cross since at latest 1218, possibly as early as 1113.[^cite_note-105] But the cross banner was not associated with the saint; indeed, the saint had his own flag, the *vexillum beati Georgii* (first mentioned in 1198), a red flag showing George and the dragon. A depiction of this flag is shown in the Genoese annals under the year 1227. The Genoese flag with the red cross was used alongside this "Saint George's flag", from at least 1218, known as the *insignia cruxata comunis Janue* ("cross ensign of the commune of Genoa").

The saint's flag was the city's main war flag, but the cross flag was used alongside it in the 1240s.

The [Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_George)'s flag (i.e. the flag depicting the saint) remained the main flag of Genoa at least until the 1280s. The flag now known as the "St. George's Cross" seems to have replaced it as Genoa's main flag at some point during the 14th century. The *[Book of Knowledge of All Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Knowledge_of_All_Kingdoms)* (c. 1385) shows it, inscribed with the word *iustiçia*, and described as:

> And the lord of this place has as his ensign a white pennant with a red cross. At the top it is inscribed with 'justice', in this manner.[^cite_note-106]

There was also a historiographical tradition claiming[^cite_note-108] that the [flag of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_England) derives from the Genoese flag, which derives from the [Knights Templar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Templar)'s red cross, during the [Third Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Crusade) in 1190, but it cannot be substantiated as historical.[^cite_note-109]

### Sports



[Luigi Ferraris Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Luigi_Ferraris)

There are two major football teams in Genoa: [Genoa C.F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_C.F.C.) and [U.C. Sampdoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.C._Sampdoria); the former is the oldest [football club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_club) operating in Italy (see [History of Genoa C.F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Genoa_C.F.C.)). The football section of the club was founded in 1893 by [James Richardson Spensley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Richardson_Spensley), an English doctor. [Genoa 1893](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_C.F.C.) has won [9 championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scudetto) (between 1898 and 1924) and 1 [Coppa Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coppa_Italia) (1936–37). [U.C. Sampdoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.C._Sampdoria) was founded in 1946 from the merger of two existing clubs, Andrea Doria (founded in 1895) and Sampierdarenese (founded in 1911). Sampdoria has won one Italian championship ([1990–91 Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990%E2%80%9391_Serie_A)), 4 Coppa Italia, 1 [UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) (1989–90) and 1 [Supercoppa Italiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercoppa_Italiana). Both Genoa C.F.C. and U.C. Sampdoria play their home games in the [Luigi Ferraris Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Luigi_Ferraris), which holds 36,536 spectators. Deeply felt is the derby called [Derby della Lanterna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derby_della_Lanterna).

The international tennis tournament [AON Open Challenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AON_Open_Challenger) takes place in Genoa.

In [rugby union](25405.md) the city is represented by CUS Genova Rugby, which is the rugby union team of the [University of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genoa) Sports Centre. CUS Genova had their peak in 1971–1973 when the team was runner-up of the Italian Serie A for three consecutive seasons and contested unsuccessfully the title to [Petrarca Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarca_Rugby). Amongst the CUS Genova players who represented [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_rugby_union_team) at international level the most relevant were [Marco Bollesan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Bollesan) and Agostino Puppo.

In 1947 was founded the [CUS Genova Hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CUS_Genova_Hockey) and in 1968 the basketball club [Athletic Genova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Genova). The city hosted the FIFA World Cup in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1990](157232.md), in 1988 the [European Karate Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_European_Karate_Championships) and in 1992 the [European Athletics Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_European_Athletics_Indoor_Championships). In 2003 the indoor sporting arena, [Vaillant Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaillant_Palace), was inaugurated.

The city lends its name to a particular type of a sailing boat so-called [Genoa sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_\(sail\)), in 2007 the city hosts the [Tall Ships' Races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tall_Ships'_Races).

Popular sauces of Genoese cuisine include [Pesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesto) sauce, garlic sauce called [Agliata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agliata), "Walnut Sauce" called [Salsa di noci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salsa_di_noci), [Green sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_sauce), [Pesto di fave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesto_di_fave?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Pasta d'acciughe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchovy_paste) and the meat sauce called [tócco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B3cco?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-110] not to be confused with the [Genovese sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genovese_sauce), that in spite of the name is typical of the [Neapolitan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_cuisine). The Genoese tradition includes many varieties of pasta as [Trenette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenette), [Corzetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corzetti), [Trofie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofie), [Pansoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pansoti), gnocchi and also: [Farinata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farinata), [Panissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panissa) and [Cuculli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuculli?action=edit\&redlink=1).

Key ingredient of Genoese cuisine is the [Prescinsêua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescins%C3%AAua) used among other things to prepare the [Savory spinach pie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savory_spinach_pie) and the [Barbagiuai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbagiuai) and still [Focaccia con le cipolle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focaccia_con_le_cipolle?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Farinata di ceci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farinata_di_ceci?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Focaccette al formaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focaccette_al_formaggio?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the [Focaccia con il formaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focaccia_con_il_formaggio?action=edit\&redlink=1) which means "Focaccia with cheese" that is even being considered for [European Union](9317.md) [PGI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_geographical_indication) status. Other key ingredients are many varieties of fish as [Sardines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardines), [Anchovies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchovies) (see also [Acciughe ripiene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acciughe_ripiene?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Acciughe sotto sale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acciughe_sotto_sale?action=edit\&redlink=1)), [Garfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garfish), [Swordfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swordfish), [Tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna), Octopus, [Squid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squid), [Mussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mussels), the *Stoccafisso* which means [Stockfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockfish) (see also [Brandacujun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandacujun?action=edit\&redlink=1)), the [Musciame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musciame) and [Gianchetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianchetti).

Other elements of Genoese cuisine include the *Ligurian Olive Oil*, the cheeses like [Brös](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C3%B6s), [U Cabanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Cabanin?action=edit\&redlink=1), [San Stè cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Stefano_d'Aveto), [Giuncata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuncata?action=edit\&redlink=1), the sausages like [Testa in cassetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testa_in_cassetta), [Salame cotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salame_cotto?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Genoa salami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_salami). Fresh pasta (usually [trofie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofie), [trenette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenette)) and "gnocchi" with pesto sauce are probably the most iconic among Genoese dishes. Pesto sauce is prepared with fresh [Genovese basil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genovese_basil), [pine nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_nut), grated [parmesan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parmesan) and pecorino mixed, garlic and [olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil) pounded together.\[^cite_note-[www.mangiareinliguria.it-111](http://www.mangiareinliguria.it-111/)] [Liguria wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liguria_wine) such as [Pigato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigato), [Riviera Ligure di Ponente Vermentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riviera_Ligure_di_Ponente_Vermentino?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Sciacchetrà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sciacchetr%C3%A0?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Rossese di Dolceacqua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rossese_di_Dolceacqua) and [Ciliegiolo del Tigullio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciliegiolo_del_Tigullio?action=edit\&redlink=1) are popular. Dishes of Genoese tradition include the [Tripe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripe) cooked in various recipes like Sbira, the [Polpettone di melanzane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polpettone_di_melanzane), the *Tomaxelle*, the [Minestrone alla genovese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minestrone_alla_genovese?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-Portofino_2023_f380-112] the [Bagnun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagnun), the fish-consisting *Ciuppin* (the precursor to San Francisco's [Cioppino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cioppino)), the [Buridda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buridda), the [Seppie in zimino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seppie_in_zimino?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the [Preboggion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preboggion).

Two sophisticated recipes of Genoese cuisine are: the [Cappon magro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappon_magro) and the [Cima alla genovese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cima_alla_genovese?action=edit\&redlink=1) (a song by [Fabrizio De André](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrizio_De_Andr%C3%A9) is titled '*A Çimma* and is dedicated to this Genoese recipe). Originating in Genoa is *Pandolce* that gave rise to [Genoa cake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_cake). The city lands its name to a special paste used to prepare cakes and pastries called [Genoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoise) and to the [Pain de Gênes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pain_de_G%C3%AAnes).

In Genoa there are many food markets in typical nineteenth-century iron structures as Mercato del Ferro, Mercato Dinegro, Mercato di Via Prè, Mercato di piazza Sarzano, Mercato del Carmine, Mercato della Foce, Mercato Romagnosi. The [Mercato Orientale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercato_Orientale?action=edit\&redlink=1) instead is in masonry and has a circular structure.

## Economy

The Genoa metropolitan area had a [GDP amounting to \$30.1 billion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_GDP) in 2011, or \$33,003 per capita.[^cite_note-Brookings-116]



San Benigno business district



[Genoa exhibition centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_International_Boat_Show)

Ligurian agriculture has increased its specialisation pattern in high-quality products (flowers, [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_wine), [olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil)) and has thus managed to maintain the gross value-added per worker at a level much higher than the national average (the difference was about 42% in 1999).[^cite_note-regportraits1-117] The value of flower production represents over 75% of the agriculture sector turnover, followed by animal farming (11.2%) and vegetable growing (6.4%).

Steel, once a major industry during the booming 1950s and 1960s, phased out after the late 1980s crisis, as Italy moved away from the heavy industry to pursue more technologically advanced and less polluting productions. So the Ligurian industry has turned towards a widely diversified range of high-quality and high-tech products (food, shipbuilding (in [Sestri Ponente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sestri_Ponente) and in metropolitan area – [Sestri Levante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sestri_Levante)), electrical engineering and electronics, petrochemicals, aerospace etc.). Nonetheless, the regions still maintain a flourishing shipbuilding sector (yacht construction and maintenance, cruise-liner building, military shipyards).[^cite_note-regportraits1-117]

In the services sector, the gross value-added per worker in Liguria is 4% above the national average. This is due to the increasing diffusion of modern technologies, particularly in commerce and tourism. A good motorway network (376 km (234 mi) in 2000) makes communications with the border regions relatively easy. The main motorway is located along the coastline, connecting the main ports of Nice (in France), Savona, Genoa and La Spezia. The number of passenger cars per 1000 inhabitants (524 in 2001) is below the national average (584). On average, about 17 million tonnes of cargo are shipped from the main ports of the region and about 57 million tonnes enter the region.[^cite_note-regportraits1-117] The [Port of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Genoa), with a trade volume of 58.6 million tonnes,[^cite_note-genova1-118] ranks first in Italy,[^cite_note-119] second in terms of [twenty-foot equivalent units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-foot_equivalent_units) after the [transshipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transshipment) port of [Gioia Tauro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gioia_Tauro), with a trade volume of over 2 million TEUs.[^cite_note-data-120] The main destinations for the cargo-passenger traffic are Sicily, Sardinia, Corsica, Barcelona, and the Canary Islands.

Major companies headquartered in Genoa include [Ansaldo Energia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansaldo_Energia), [Ansaldo STS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansaldo_STS), [Banca Carige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banca_Carige), [Costa Cruises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Cruises), [Erg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erg_\(company\)), [Piaggio Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio_Aerospace), [Registro Italiano Navale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Registro_Italiano_Navale), and [SLAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLAM_\(clothing\)).

## Education



[University of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genoa)'s main building

The first organised forms of higher education in Genoa date back to the 13th century when private colleges were entitled to award degrees in medicine, philosophy, Theology, Law, Arts.[^cite_note-121] Today the [University of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genoa), founded in the 15th century, is one of the largest in Italy, with 11 faculties, 51 departments and 14 libraries. In 2007–2008, the university had 41,000 students and 6,540 graduates.[^cite_note-122]

The main fields of research of the Institute are [Neuroscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroscience), [Robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics), [Nanotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanotechnology), [Drug discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_discovery). The central research labs and headquarters are located in Morego, in the neighbourhood of [Bolzaneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzaneto).[^cite_note-123] [Clemson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clemson_University), based in [South Carolina](27956.md), United States, has a villa in Genoa where architecture students and students in related fields can attend for a semester or year-long study program.

[Florida International University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_International_University) (FIU), based in Miami, Florida, United States, also has a small campus in Genoa, with the [University of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genoa) which offers classes within the [FIU School of Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_International_University_School_of_Architecture).

### Science



The Italian astronaut [Franco Malerba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Malerba)

Genoa is the birthplace of [Giovanni Battista Baliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Baliani) and [Vincentio Reinieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincentio_Reinieri), of the geneticist [Luigi Luca Cavalli-Sforza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Luca_Cavalli-Sforza), of the Nobel Prize astrophysicist [Riccardo Giacconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riccardo_Giacconi) and of the astronaut [Franco Malerba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Malerba). The city is home to the [Erzelli Hi-Tech Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzelli_Hi-Tech_Park), to the [Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Italiano_di_Tecnologia), to the Istituto idrografico della Marina and annually hosts the [Festival della Scienza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_della_Scienza). The city has an important tradition in the fields of the geology, paleontology, botany and naturalistic studies, among the most eminent personalities we remember: [Lorenzo Pareto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Pareto), [Luigi d'Albertis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_d'Albertis), [Enrico Alberto d'Albertis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Alberto_d'Albertis), [Giacomo Doria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Doria) and [Arturo Issel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arturo_Issel), we point the [Orto Botanico dell'Università di Genova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orto_Botanico_dell'Universit%C3%A0_di_Genova). Very important and renowned is the [Istituto Giannina Gaslini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Giannina_Gaslini).

In 1846 the city hosted the eighth Meeting of Italian Scientists and in 1902 [Luigi Carnera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Carnera) discovered an asteroid and called it "[485 Genua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/485_Genua)", dedicating it to the Latin name of Genoa.

#### Erzelli science technology park



Buildings in the Erzelli GREAT campus

The western area of Genoa hosts the [Erzelli GREAT Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzelli), an under-construction [science technology park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_park) which houses the [high-tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-tech) corporations [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens), [Ericsson](9424.md), [Esaote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esaote), and [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics) [laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratories) of the [Italian Institute of Technology (IIT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Italiano_di_Tecnologia).[^cite_note-124] The [Erzelli GREAT Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erzelli) [science park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_park) is undergoing a process of enlargement, and in the future will host the new Faculty of Engineering of [University of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genoa). The project has been struggling in recent years with enterprises laying off their employees and no real growth.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126]

## Transport

### Ports

Main article: [Port of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Genoa)



Panorama of the port of Genoa

Several cruise and ferry lines serve the passenger terminals in the old port, with a traffic of 3.2 million passengers in 2007.[^cite_note-127] [MSC Cruises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSC_Cruises) chose Genoa as one of its main home ports, in competition with the Genoese company [Costa Cruises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Cruises), which moved its home port to [Savona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savona). The quays of the passenger terminals extend over an area of 250,000 square metres (2,700,000 square feet), with 5 equipped berths for cruise vessels and 13 for ferries, for an annual capacity of 4 million ferry passengers, 1.5 million cars and 250,000 trucks.[^cite_note-128]

The historical maritime station of Ponte dei Mille is today a technologically advanced cruise terminal, with facilities designed after the world's most modern airports, to ensure fast embarking and disembarking of all latest generation ships carrying a thousand passengers. A third cruise terminal is currently under construction in the redesigned area of Ponte Parodi, once a quay used for grain traffic.

The *[Costa Concordia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Concordia)* cruise ship, owned by Costa Cruises, was docked at the port before being dismantled.[^cite_note-news-129]



A view of the commercial port of Genoa

### Air transport



Genoa Airport, built on an artificial peninsula

Main article: [Genoa Cristoforo Colombo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_Cristoforo_Colombo_Airport)

The [Airport of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_Cristoforo_Colombo_Airport) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **GOA**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **LIMJ**) (Italian: Aeroporto di Genova) also named Christopher Columbus Airport (Italian: Aeroporto Cristoforo Colombo) is built on an artificial peninsula, 4 [NM](21289.md) (7.4 km; 4.6 mi) west[^cite_note-AIP-130] of the city. The airport is currently operated by Aeroporto di Genova S.P.A., which has recently upgraded the airport complex that now connects Genoa with several daily flights to Rome, Naples, Paris, London, Madrid and Munich. In 2008, 1,202,168 passengers travelled through the airport,[^cite_note-131] with an increase of international destinations and charter flights.

### Public transport

Main article: [Railway stations in Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_stations_in_Genoa)



[Genova Brignole railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genova_Brignole_railway_station)



[Genova Piazza Principe railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genova_Piazza_Principe_railway_station)

The main railway stations are [Genoa Brignole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genova_Brignole_railway_station) in the east and [Genoa Principe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genova_Piazza_Principe_railway_station) in the west. Genoa Brignole is close to the business districts and the exhibition centre, while the Principe is close to the port, the university and the historical centre. From these two stations depart the main trains connecting Genoa to France, Turin, Milan and Rome.

Genoa's third most important station is [Genoa Sampierdarena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genova_Sampierdarena_railway_station), which serves the densely populated neighbourhood of Sampierdarena. 23 other local stations serve the other neighbourhoods on the 30-kilometre-long coast line from [Nervi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nervi) to [Voltri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltri) and on the northern line through [Bolzaneto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzaneto) and the Polcevera Valley.

The municipal administration of Genoa plans to transform these urban railway lines to be part of the rapid transit system, which now consists of the *Metropolitana di Genova* ([Genoa Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa_Metro)), a [light metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_metro) connecting Brin to the city centre. The metro line was extended to Brignole Station in December 2012. Trains currently pass through Corvetto station between De Ferrari and Brignole without stopping. A possible further extension towards the eastern densely populated boroughs was planned, but the municipal administration intends to improve the public transport by investing in new tram lines instead of completing the extension of the light metro.[^cite_note-132] The current stations of the metro line are Brin-Certosa, Dinegro, Principe, Darsena, San Giorgio, Sant'Agostino and De Ferrari; the line is 5.3 km (3.3 mi) long.

The city's hilly nature has influenced its public transport. The city is served by two [funicular railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular_railway) (the [Zecca–Righi funicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zecca%E2%80%93Righi_funicular), the [Sant'Anna funicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant'Anna_funicular)), the [Quezzi inclined elevator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezzi_inclined_elevator), the [Principe–Granarolo rack railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principe%E2%80%93Granarolo_rack_railway), and ten public [lifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevator). One such example is the mixed funicular and lift railway, the [Ascensore Castello d'Albertis-Montegalletto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascensore_Castello_d'Albertis-Montegalletto).[^cite_note-amtabout-133]

The city's metro, bus and [trolleybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Genoa) network is operated by [AMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMT_\(Genoa\)) (Azienda Mobilità e Trasporti S.p.A.). The Drin Bus is a [demand responsive transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demand_responsive_transport) service that connects the hilly, low-density areas of Genoa.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] The average time people spend commuting on public transit in Genova, for example to and from work, is 54 minutes on a weekday. 10% of public transit riders ride for more than 2 hours every day. The average time people wait at a stop or station for public transit is 12 minutes, while 13% of riders wait over 20 minutes on average every day. The average distance people usually ride in a single trip with public transit is 4 km, while 2% travel for over 12 km in a single direction.[^cite_note-137]
