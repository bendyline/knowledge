**Pierre-Auguste Renoir** ([/rɛnˈwɑːr/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-1] French: [\[pjɛʁ oɡyst ʁənwaʁ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); 25 February 1841 – 3 December 1919) was a leading French [Impressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressionism) artist. In his depiction of feminine beauty, Renoir has been described as "the final representative of a tradition which runs directly from [Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Paul_Rubens) to [Watteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Watteau)."[^cite_note-2]

He was the father of the actor [Pierre Renoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Renoir) (1885–1952), the filmmaker [Jean Renoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Renoir) (1894–1979) and the ceramic artist Claude Renoir (1901–1969). He was the grandfather of the filmmaker [Claude Renoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Renoir) (1913–1993), son of Pierre.

## Life

### Youth



Portrait of Renoir by [Frédéric Bazille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Bazille) (1867)

Pierre-Auguste Renoir was born in [Limoges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limoges), Haute-Vienne, France, in 1841. His father, Léonard Renoir, was a tailor of modest means, so, in 1844, Renoir's family moved to Paris in search of more favorable prospects. The location of their home, in rue d'Argenteuil in central Paris, placed Renoir in proximity to the Louvre. Although the young Renoir had a natural proclivity for drawing, he exhibited a greater talent for singing. His talent was encouraged by his teacher, [Charles Gounod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Gounod), who was the choirmaster at the Church of St Roch at the time. However, due to the family's financial circumstances, Renoir had to discontinue his music lessons and leave school at the age of thirteen to pursue an apprenticeship at a [porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcelain) factory.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-\:0-4]

Although Renoir displayed a talent for his work, he frequently tired of the subject matter and sought refuge in the galleries of the [Louvre](17546.md). The owner of the factory recognized his apprentice's talent and communicated this to Renoir's family. Following this, Renoir started taking lessons to prepare for entry into [Ecole des Beaux Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_des_Beaux-Arts_de_Paris). When the [porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcelain) factory adopted mechanical reproduction processes in 1858, Renoir was forced to find other means to support his learning.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Before he enrolled in art school, he also painted hangings for overseas missionaries and decorations on fans.[^cite_note-5]



Forty-three portraits of painters from the Gleyre studio (1856-68). [Petit Palais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petit_Palais). Renoir is third from the left on the fourth row

In 1862, he began studying art under [Charles Gleyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Gleyre) in Paris. There he met [Alfred Sisley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Sisley), [Frédéric Bazille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Bazille), and [Claude Monet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Monet).[^cite_note-6] At times, during the 1860s, he did not have enough money to buy paint. Renoir had his first success at the [Salon of 1868](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon_of_1868) with his painting *[Lise with a Parasol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lise_with_a_Parasol)* (1867), which depicted [Lise Tréhot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lise_Tr%C3%A9hot), his lover at the time.[^cite_note-OxfordArtOnline-7] Although Renoir first started exhibiting paintings at the [Paris Salon of 1864](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon_of_1864),[^cite_note-8] recognition was slow in coming, partly as a result of the turmoil of the [Franco-Prussian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Prussian_War).

During the [Paris Commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Commune) in 1871, while Renoir painted on the banks of the [Seine River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine), some [Communards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communards) thought he was a spy and were about to throw him into the river, when a leader of the *Commune*, [Raoul Rigault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Rigault), recognized Renoir as the man who had protected him on an earlier occasion.[^cite_note-9] In 1874, a ten-year friendship with Jules Le Cœur and his family ended,[^cite_note-Wadley,_page_15-10] and Renoir lost not only the valuable support gained by the association but also a generous welcome to stay on their property near [Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontainebleau) and [its scenic forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_of_Fontainebleau). This loss of a favorite painting location resulted in a distinct change of subjects.

### Adulthood

Renoir was inspired by the style and subject matter of the previous modern painters [Camille Pissarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Pissarro) and [Édouard Manet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Manet).[^cite_note-11] After a series of rejections by the Salon juries, he joined forces with Monet, Sisley, Pissarro, and several other artists to mount the [First Impressionist Exhibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Impressionist_Exhibition) in April 1874, in which Renoir displayed six paintings. Although the critical response to the exhibition was largely unfavorable, Renoir's work was comparatively well received.[^cite_note-OxfordArtOnline-7] That same year, two of his works were shown with [Paul Durand-Ruel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Durand-Ruel) in London.[^cite_note-Wadley,_page_15-10]



*[The Swing (La Balançoire)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Swing_\(Pierre-Auguste_Renoir\))*, 1876, oil on canvas, [Musée d'Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_d'Orsay), Paris

&#x20;Hoping to secure a livelihood by attracting portrait commissions, Renoir displayed mostly portraits at the [Second Impressionist Exhibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Impressionist_Exhibition) in 1876.[^cite_note-Brodskaya_114-12] He contributed a more diverse range of paintings the next year when the group presented its third exhibition; they included *Dance at Le Moulin de la Galette* and *The Swing*.[^cite_note-Brodskaya_114-12] Renoir did not exhibit in the fourth or fifth Impressionist exhibitions, and instead resumed submitting his works to the Salon. By the end of the 1870s, particularly after the success of his painting *[Mme Charpentier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marguerite_Charpentier) and her Children* (1878) at the [Salon of 1879](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon_of_1879), Renoir was a successful and fashionable painter.[^cite_note-OxfordArtOnline-7] It was also in 1879 that he met the man who was soon to become his main patron, [Paul Bérard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_B%C3%A9rard?action=edit\&redlink=1), who regularly invited him to paint and enjoy the Normandy seaside at the [Château de Wargemont.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Wargemont?action=edit\&redlink=1)



&#x20;*Dance at Le Moulin de la Galette* (*[Bal du moulin de la Galette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bal_du_moulin_de_la_Galette)*), 1876, [Musée d'Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_d'Orsay)

In 1881, he traveled to [Algeria](358.md), a country he associated with [Eugène Delacroix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eug%C3%A8ne_Delacroix),[^cite_note-13] then to [Madrid](41188263.md), to see the work of [Diego Velázquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez). Following that, he traveled to Italy to see [Titian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titian)'s masterpieces in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) and the paintings of [Raphael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael) in Rome. On 15 January 1882, Renoir met the composer [Richard Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Wagner) at his home in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo), Sicily. Renoir painted Wagner's portrait in just thirty-five minutes. In the same year, after contracting pneumonia which permanently damaged his respiratory system, Renoir convalesced for six weeks in Algeria.[^cite_note-Wadley,_page_25-14]

In 1883, Renoir spent the summer in [Guernsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guernsey), one of [the islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Islands) in the [English Channel](9230.md) with a varied landscape of beaches, cliffs, and bays, where he created fifteen paintings in little over a month. Most of these feature *Moulin Huet*, a bay in [Saint Martin's, Guernsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Martin's,_Guernsey). These paintings were the subject of a set of commemorative postage stamps issued by the Bailiwick of Guernsey in 1983. While living and working in Montmartre, Renoir employed [Suzanne Valadon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Valadon) as a model, who posed for him (*The Large Bathers*, 1884–1887; *[Dance at Bougival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_at_Bougival)*, 1883)[^cite_note-15] and many of his fellow painters; during that time, she studied their techniques and eventually became one of the leading painters of the day. In 1887, the year when [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria) celebrated her [Golden Jubilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Jubilee_of_Queen_Victoria), and upon the request of the queen's associate, Phillip Richbourg, Renoir donated several paintings to the "French Impressionist Paintings" catalog as a token of his loyalty.



*[Luncheon of the Boating Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luncheon_of_the_Boating_Party)*, 1880–1881, [The Phillips Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Phillips_Collection), Washington D.C.

In 1890, he married [Aline Victorine Charigot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aline_Charigot), a dressmaker twenty years his junior,[^cite_note-16] who, along with a number of the artist's friends, had already served as a model for *Le Déjeuner des canotiers* (*[Luncheon of the Boating Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luncheon_of_the_Boating_Party)*; she is the woman on the left playing with the dog) in 1881, and with whom he had already had a child, Pierre, in 1885.[^cite_note-Wadley,_page_25-14] After marrying, Renoir painted many scenes of his wife and daily family life including their children and their nurse, Aline's cousin [Gabrielle Renard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabrielle_Renard). The Renoirs had three sons: [Pierre Renoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Renoir) (1885–1952), who became a stage and film actor; [Jean Renoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Renoir) (1894–1979), who became a filmmaker of note; and Claude Renoir (1901–1969), who became a ceramic artist.

### Later years



Pierre-Auguste Renoir, c. 1910

Around 1892, Renoir developed [rheumatoid arthritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheumatoid_arthritis). In 1907, he moved to the warmer climate of "Les Collettes", a farm at the village of [Cagnes-sur-Mer](3546923.md), [Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provence-Alpes-C%C3%B4te_d'Azur), close to the [Mediterranean](19006.md) coast.[^cite_note-17] Renoir painted during the last twenty years of his life even after his arthritis severely limited his mobility. He developed progressive deformities in his hands and [ankylosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankylosis) of his right shoulder, requiring him to change his painting technique. It has often been reported that in the advanced stages of his arthritis, he painted by having a brush strapped to his paralyzed fingers,[^cite_note-18] but this is erroneous; Renoir remained able to grasp a brush, although he required an assistant to place it in his hand.[^cite_note-bmj-19] The wrapping of his hands with bandages, apparent in late photographs of the artist, served to prevent skin irritation.[^cite_note-bmj-19]

In 1919, Renoir visited the [Louvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre) to see his paintings hanging with those of the old masters. During this period, he created sculptures by cooperating with a young artist, [Richard Guino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Guino), who worked the clay. Due to his limited joint mobility, Renoir also used a moving canvas, or picture roll, to facilitate painting large works.[^cite_note-bmj-19]

Renoir's portrait of the Austrian actress [Tilla Durieux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilla_Durieux) (1914) contains playful flecks of vibrant color on her shawl that offset the classical pose of the actress and highlight Renoir's skill just five years before his death.

Renoir died in Cagnes-sur-Mer on 3 December 1919 at the age of 78.[^cite_note-20]

### Family legacy

Renoir's great-grandson, [Alexandre Renoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Renoir), has also become a professional artist. In 2018, the Monthaven Arts and Cultural Center in [Hendersonville, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendersonville,_Tennessee), United States, hosted *Beauty Remains*, an exhibition of his works. The exhibition title comes from a famous quotation by Renoir who, when asked why he continued to paint with his painful arthritis in his advanced years, replied "The pain passes, but the beauty remains."[^cite_note-21]

## Artworks



*[Two Sisters (On the Terrace)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Sisters_\(On_the_Terrace\))*, oil on canvas, 1881, [Art Institute of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Institute_of_Chicago)

Renoir's paintings are notable for their vibrant light and saturated color, most often focusing on people in intimate and candid compositions. The female nude was one of his primary subjects. However, in 1876, a reviewer in *[Le Figaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Figaro)* wrote "Try to explain to Monsieur Renoir that a woman's torso is not a mass of decomposing flesh with those purplish green stains that denote a state of complete putrefaction in a corpse."[^cite_note-Figaro-22] Yet in characteristic Impressionist style, Renoir suggested the details of a scene through freely brushed touches of colour, so that his figures softly fuse with one another and their surroundings.



*[Portrait of Irène Cahen d'Anvers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait_of_Ir%C3%A8ne_Cahen_d'Anvers)* (*La Petite Irène*), 1880, [Foundation E.G. Bührle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_E.G._B%C3%BChrle), Zürich

[^cite_note-23]

His initial paintings show the influence of the colorism of [Eugène Delacroix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eug%C3%A8ne_Delacroix) and the luminosity of [Camille Corot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste-Camille_Corot). He also admired the realism of [Gustave Courbet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Courbet) and [Édouard Manet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_Manet), and his early work resembles theirs in his use of black as a color. Renoir admired [Edgar Degas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Degas)' sense of movement. Other painters Renoir greatly admired were the 18th-century masters [François Boucher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Boucher) and [Jean-Honoré Fragonard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Honor%C3%A9_Fragonard).[^cite_note-24]

A fine example of Renoir's early work and evidence of the influence of Courbet's realism, is *[Diana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana_\(Renoir_painting\))*, 1867. Ostensibly a mythological subject, the painting is a naturalistic studio work; the figure carefully observed, solidly modeled and superimposed upon a contrived landscape. If the work is a "student" piece, Renoir's heightened personal response to female sensuality is present. The model was Lise Tréhot, the artist's mistress at that time, and inspiration for a number of paintings.[^cite_note-25]

In the late 1860s, through the practice of painting light and water *[en plein air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/En_plein_air)* (outdoors), he and his friend [Claude Monet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Monet) discovered that the color of shadows is not brown or black, but the reflected color of the objects surrounding them, an effect known today as [diffuse reflection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffuse_reflection). Several pairs of paintings exist in which Renoir and Monet worked side-by-side, depicting the same scenes (*La Grenouillère*, 1869).

One of the best-known Impressionist works is Renoir's 1876 *Dance at Le Moulin de la Galette ([Bal du moulin de la Galette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bal_du_moulin_de_la_Galette))*. The painting depicts an open-air scene, crowded with people at a popular dance garden on the *Butte Montmartre* close to where he lived. The works of his early maturity were typically Impressionist snapshots of real life, full of sparkling color and light.



One of [a series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blonde_Bather), *Blonde Bather* (1881), marked a distinct change in style following a trip to Italy. The work is part of the permanent collection of the [Clark Art Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Art_Institute).

By the mid-1880s, however, he had broken with the movement to apply a more disciplined formal technique to portraits and figure paintings, particularly of women. It was a trip to Italy in 1881 when he saw works by [Raphael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael), [Leonardo da Vinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_da_Vinci), [Titian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titian), and other [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) masters, that convinced him that he was on the wrong path. At that point he declared, "I had gone as far as I could with Impressionism and I realized I could neither paint nor draw".[^cite_note-26]

For the next several years he painted in a more severe style in an attempt to return to classicism.[^cite_note-27] Concentrating on his drawing and emphasizing the outlines of figures, he painted works such as *[Blonde Bather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blonde_Bather)* (1881 and 1882) and *The Large Bathers* (1884–1887; [Philadelphia Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Museum_of_Art)) during what is sometimes referred to as his "[Ingres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Auguste_Dominique_Ingres) period".[^cite_note-28]



*[Girls at the Piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girls_at_the_Piano)*, 1892, [Musée d'Orsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_d'Orsay), Paris

After 1890 he changed direction again. To dissolve outlines, as in his earlier work, he returned to thinly brushed color.

From this period onward he concentrated on monumental nudes and domestic scenes, fine examples of which are *[Girls at the Piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girls_at_the_Piano)*, 1892, and *[Grandes Baigneuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Grandes_Baigneuses_\(Renoir\))*, 1887. The latter painting is the most typical and successful of Renoir's late, abundantly fleshed nudes.[^cite_note-29]

A prolific artist, he created several thousand paintings. The warm sensuality of Renoir's style made his paintings some of the most well-known and frequently reproduced works in the history of art. The single largest collection of his works—181 paintings in all—is at the [Barnes Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnes_Foundation), in [Philadelphia](50585.md), United States.

### Catalogue raisonné

A five-volume *[catalogue raisonné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalogue_raisonn%C3%A9)* of Renoir's works (with one supplement) was published by [Bernheim-Jeune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernheim-Jeune) between 1983 and 2014.[^cite_note-30] Bernheim-Jeune is the only surviving major art dealer that was used by Renoir. The [Wildenstein Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildenstein_Institute) is preparing, but has not yet published, a critical catalogue of Renoir's work.[^cite_note-31] A disagreement between these two organizations concerning an unsigned work in [Picton Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picton_Castle) was at the centre of the second episode of the fourth season of the television series *[Fake or Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_or_Fortune)*.

### Posthumous prints

In 1919, [Ambroise Vollard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambroise_Vollard), a renowned art dealer, published a book on the life and work of Renoir, *La Vie et l'Œuvre de Pierre-Auguste Renoir*, in an edition of 1000 copies. In 1986, Vollard's heirs started reprinting the copper plates, generally, [etchings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etching) with hand applied [watercolor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watercolor_painting). These prints are signed by Renoir in the plate and are embossed "Vollard" in the lower margin. They are not numbered, dated or signed in pencil.

### Posthumous sales

A small version of *[Bal du moulin de la Galette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bal_du_moulin_de_la_Galette#Smaller_version)* sold for \$78.1 million 17 May 1990 at Sotheby's New York.[^cite_note-32]

In 2012, Renoir's *[Paysage Bords de Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paysage_Bords_de_Seine)* was offered for sale at auction but the painting was discovered to have been stolen from the [Baltimore Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Museum_of_Art) in 1951. The sale was cancelled.

### Painting thefts

Main article: [2026 Musée Renoir theft](84173201.md)

On 8 September 2011, the work *Madeleine Leaning on Her Elbow with Flowers in Her Hair* (1918) was stolen during an armed robbery from a home in [Houston](13774.md), [Texas](29810.md). A reward of up to \$50,000 was offered for information leading to the recovery of the painting.[^cite_note-33]

On 22 March 2026, four masked men stole Renoir's *[Les Poissons](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les%20Poissons)* (1917), along with two other works by [Cézanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9zanne) and [Matisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matisse), with a combined estimated value of €9 million (£7.7m; \$10.5m),[^cite_note-34] in a [heist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnani-Rocca_Foundation#2026_art_theft) at the [Magnani Rocca Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnani-Rocca_Foundation) villa near [Parma](24231.md), Italy.[^cite_note-\:0-4][^cite_note-35] In August 2026 it was announced that the paintings had been recovered.[^cite_note-amos-36]

On 8 September 2026, two thieves broke into the [Musée Renoir](84175718.md) at [Cagnes-sur-Mer](3546923.md) in France, and stole four Renoir paintings, before dropping two during their escape.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Each of the paintings had an estimated value of approximately €9 million (£7.7m; \$10.5m).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-amos-36] The two paintings taken were *[Portrait of Madame Colonna Romano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait_of_Madame_Colonna_Romano)* and *[Jeune Femme au puits](84181484.md).*[^cite_note-amos-36][^cite_note-38]
