**Willem-Alexander** (Dutch: [\[ˈʋɪləm aːlɛkˈsɑndər\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch); *Willem-Alexander Claus George Ferdinand*; born 27 April 1967) is [King of the Netherlands](23416611.md), reigning since 30 April 2013.

Willem-Alexander was born in [Utrecht](50333.md) during the reign of his maternal grandmother, [Queen Juliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Juliana), as the eldest child of [Princess Beatrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Beatrix) (later Queen) and [Prince Claus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Claus). He became [Prince of Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Orange) as [heir apparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_apparent) upon his mother's accession on 30 April 1980. He went to public primary and secondary schools in the Netherlands, and an international sixth-form college in [Wales](69894.md) UWC Atlantic College. He served in the [Royal Netherlands Navy](198478.md), and studied history at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University). He married [Máxima Zorreguieta Cerruti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1xima_Zorreguieta_Cerruti) in 2002, and they have three daughters: [Catharina-Amalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catharina-Amalia), [Alexia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Alexia_of_the_Netherlands), and [Ariane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Ariane_of_the_Netherlands). Willem-Alexander succeeded his mother as monarch upon her abdication on 30 April 2013. He is the first man to hold this position since the death of his great-great-grandfather [William III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_III_of_the_Netherlands) in 1890.

Willem-Alexander is interested in sports and international [water management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_management) issues. Until his accession to the throne, he was a member of the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) (1998–2013),[^cite_note-1] chairman of the Advisory Committee on Water to the Dutch [Minister of Infrastructure and the Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Infrastructure_and_the_Environment) (2004–2013),[^cite_note-2] and chairman of the [Secretary-General of the United Nations](162415.md)' [Advisory Board on Water and Sanitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN-Water#Members_and_partners_of_UN-Water) (2006–2013).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## Early life and education



Prince Willem-Alexander (left) at age 14 and his brother [Friso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Friso_of_Orange-Nassau) in 1982

Willem-Alexander was born on 27 April 1967 at Academic Hospital Utrecht (now known as the [University Medical Center Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Medical_Center_Utrecht)) in [Utrecht](50333.md). He is the first child of [Princess Beatrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Beatrix) (later Queen) and [Prince Claus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Claus),[^cite_note-biography-5] and the first grandchild of [Queen Juliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Juliana) and [Prince Bernhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Bernhard). He was the first male Dutch royal baby since the birth of [Prince Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander,_Prince_of_Orange) in 1851, and the first immediate male heir since Alexander's death in 1884.

From birth, Willem-Alexander has held the titles Prince of the Netherlands ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Prins der Nederlanden*), Prince of [Orange-Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Orange-Nassau), and [Jonkheer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonkheer) of [Amsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsberg).[^cite_note-biography-5] He was baptised as a member of the [Dutch Reformed Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Reformed_Church)[^cite_note-6] on 2 September 1967[^cite_note-40questions-7] in Saint Jacob's Church in [The Hague](30269.md).[^cite_note-8] His godparents are his maternal grandfather [Prince Bernhard of Lippe-Biesterfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Bernhard_of_Lippe-Biesterfeld), his paternal grandmother [Gösta Freiin von dem Bussche-Haddenhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroness_G%C3%B6sta_von_dem_Bussche-Haddenhausen), [Prince Ferdinand von Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_von_Bismarck), former Prime Minister [Jelle Zijlstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelle_Zijlstra), [Jonkvrouw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonkvrouw) Renée [Röell](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B6ell), and [Queen Margrethe II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Margrethe_II).[^cite_note-40questions-7]

He had two younger brothers: [Prince Friso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Friso) (1968–2013) and [Prince Constantijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Constantijn) (b. 1969). He lived with his family at the castle [Drakensteyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drakensteyn) in the hamlet [Lage Vuursche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lage_Vuursche) near [Baarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baarn) from his birth until 1981, when they moved to the larger palace [Huis ten Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huis_ten_Bosch) in The Hague. His mother, Beatrix, became Queen of the Netherlands in 1980, after his grandmother Juliana abdicated. He then received the title of [Prince of Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Orange) as [heir apparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heir_apparent) to the throne of the [Kingdom of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands) at the age of 13.[^cite_note-biography-5]

Willem-Alexander attended local state primary school Nieuwe Baarnse Elementary School in [Baarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baarn) from 1973 to 1979. He went to two different state secondary schools (the Baarns Lyceum in Baarn from 1979 to 1981 and the Eerste Vrijzinnig Christelijk Lyceum in [The Hague](30269.md) from 1981 to 1983) and the private sixth-form college [United World College of the Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_World_College_of_the_Atlantic) in [Wales](69894.md) (1983 to 1985), where he received his [International Baccalaureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Baccalaureate).[^cite_note-biography-5][^cite_note-parlement-9]

After his military service from 1985 to 1987, Willem-Alexander studied history at [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University) from 1987 onwards and received his [Master of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts) degree (*[doctorandus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorandus)*) in 1993.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] His final thesis was on the Dutch response to France's [decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_withdrawal_from_NATO_command) under President [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle) to leave [NATO](21133.md)'s integrated command structure.[^cite_note-biography-5]

Willem-Alexander speaks English, Spanish (his wife's native language), French, and German (his father's native language), in addition to his native Dutch.[^cite_note-12]

## Military training and career



Willem-Alexander in the navy uniform of [ensign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensign_\(rank\)) in 1986

Between secondary school and his university education, Willem-Alexander performed military service in the [Royal Netherlands Navy](198478.md) from August 1985 until January 1987. He received his training at the [Royal Netherlands Naval College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Naval_College) and in the frigates [HNLMS *Tromp*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_Tromp_\(F801\)) and [HNLMS *Abraham Crijnssen*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_Abraham_Crijnssen_\(F816\)), where he was an [ensign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensign_\(rank\)). In 1988 he received additional training in the ship [HNLMS *Van Kinsbergen*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HNLMS_Van_Kinsbergen_\(F809\)) and became a [lieutenant (junior grade)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_\(junior_grade\)) (*wachtofficier*).[^cite_note-13]

As a [reservist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservist) for the Royal Netherlands Navy, Willem-Alexander was promoted to [lieutenant commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_commander) in 1995, [commander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander) in 1997, [Captain at Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_at_Sea) in 2001, and [commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(rank\)) in 2005. As a reservist for the [Royal Netherlands Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Army), he was made a [major](201920.md) ([Grenadiers' and Rifles Guard Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenadiers'_and_Rifles_Guard_Regiment)) in 1995, and was promoted to [lieutenant colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel) in 1997, [colonel](199023.md) in 2001, and [brigadier general](206220.md) in 2005. As a reservist for the [Royal Netherlands Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Air_Force), he was made [squadron leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squadron_leader) in 1995 and promoted to [air commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_commodore) in 2005. As a reservist for the [Royal Marechaussee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Marechaussee), he was made brigadier general in 2005.[^cite_note-parlement-9]

Before his investiture as king in 2013, Willem-Alexander was honourably discharged from the armed forces. The government declared that the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) cannot be a serving member of the armed forces, since the government itself holds supreme command over the armed forces. As king, Willem-Alexander may choose to wear a military uniform with [royal insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_insignia), but not with his former rank insignia.[^cite_note-14]

## Reign

See also: [List of state visits made by Willem-Alexander of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_visits_made_by_Willem-Alexander_of_the_Netherlands)



Inauguration portrait of King Willem-Alexander

On 28 January 2013, Beatrix announced her intention to abdicate. On the morning of 30 April 2013 (*[Koninginnedag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koninginnedag)*), Beatrix signed the instrument of abdication at the *Moseszaal* (Moses Hall) at the [Royal Palace of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Palace_of_Amsterdam). Later that afternoon, Willem-Alexander was [inaugurated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inauguration_of_Willem-Alexander) as king before a joint session of the [States General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_General_of_the_Netherlands) in a ceremony held at the [Nieuwe Kerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwe_Kerk_\(Amsterdam\)).

As king, Willem-Alexander has weekly meetings with the prime minister and speaks regularly with ministers and state secretaries. He also signs all new Acts of Parliament and royal decrees. He represents the kingdom at home and abroad. At the [State Opening of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Opening_of_Parliament), he delivers the [Speech from the Throne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_from_the_Throne), which announces the plans of the government for the parliamentary year. The Constitution requires that the king appoint, dismiss and swear in all government ministers and state secretaries. As king, he is also the President of the [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_of_the_Netherlands), an advisory body that reviews proposed legislation. In modern practice, the monarch seldom chairs council meetings.[^cite_note-15]

At his accession at age 46, he was Europe's youngest monarch; one of the current youngest monarch in Europe, alongside [Frederik X](339155.md) of Denmark, [Felipe VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_VI) of Spain and [Guillaume V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_V,_Grand_Duke_of_Luxembourg) of Luxembourg. He is also the first male monarch of the Netherlands since the death of his great-great-grandfather [William III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_III_of_the_Netherlands) in 1890. Willem-Alexander was one of four new sovereign monarchs in 2013 along with [Pope Francis](1687680.md), Emir [Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani](6146151.md) of Qatar, and King [Philippe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_of_Belgium) of Belgium.

## Activities and social interests

Since 1985, when he became 18 years old, Willem-Alexander has been a member of the [Council of State of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_of_the_Netherlands). This is the highest council of the [Dutch political system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_government) and is chaired by the head of state (then Queen Beatrix).[^cite_note-16]

Willem-Alexander is interested in [water management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_management) and sports issues. He was an honorary member of the World Commission on Water for the 21st century and patron of the Global Water Partnership, a body established by the [World Bank](36752.md), the UN, and the Swedish Ministry of Development. He was appointed as the Chairperson of the United Nations Secretary General's Advisory Board on Water and Sanitation on 12 December 2006.[^cite_note-17]

On 10 October 2010, Willem-Alexander and Máxima went to the [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles)' capital, [Willemstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willemstad), to attend and represent [his mother, the Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrix_of_the_Netherlands), at the [Antillean Dissolution ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Netherlands_Antilles).

He was a patron of the Dutch Olympic Games Committee until 1998 when he was made a member of the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) (IOC). After becoming King, he relinquished his membership and received the Gold [Olympic Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Order) at the [125th IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/125th_IOC_Session).[^cite_note-OlympicOrder-18] To celebrate the 100th anniversary of the [1928 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_Summer_Olympics) held in Amsterdam, he had expressed support to bid for the [2028 Summer Olympics](12695048.md).[^cite_note-19]

He was a member of the supervisory board of [De Nederlandsche Bank](180516.md) (the Dutch central bank), a member of the Advisory Council of ECP (the information society forum for government, business and civil society), patron of Veterans' Day and held several other patronages and posts.[^cite_note-20]

### Other activities



Willem-Alexander with his family at the [2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Summer_Olympics), here supporting [Ellen van Dijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_van_Dijk)

Willem-Alexander is an avid pilot and has said that, had he not been a royal, he would have chosen a career as an airline pilot, ideally flying large aircraft such as the [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747).[^cite_note-\:0-21] During his mother's reign, he regularly flew the Dutch royal aircraft on trips.[^cite_note-22]

Willem-Alexander revealed in May 2017 that he had quietly been working as a [first officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_officer_\(aviation\)) with [KLM](16882.md) since the 1990s, flying [Fokker 70s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_70) for civilian [KLM Cityhopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLM_Cityhopper) flights twice a month, even after becoming king. He said he is rarely recognized while in uniform, although some passengers recognized his voice when he made announcements, despite never introducing himself by name.[^cite_note-\:0-21][^cite_note-23] Following the retirement of the Fokker 70, he transitioned to the [Boeing 737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737), and in 2025 transitioned to the [Airbus A321neo](32104573.md).[^cite_note-\:0-21][^cite_note-\:1-24]

Using the name "W. A. [van Buren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Buren_\(surname\))", one of the least-known titles of the [House of Orange-Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Orange-Nassau), he completed the 1986 Frisian [Elfstedentocht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elfstedentocht), a 200-kilometre-long (120 mi) distance [ice skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_skating) tour.[^cite_note-25] He ran the full [New York City Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Marathon) under the same pseudonym in 1992.[^cite_note-26]

## Privacy and the press



Willem-Alexander with Dutch Prime Minister [Mark Rutte](4321056.md), Austrian President [Alexander Van der Bellen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Van_der_Bellen) and Israeli President [Isaac Herzog](4645588.md) on 10 March 2024

In an attempt to strike a balance between privacy for the royal family and availability to the press, the [Netherlands Government Information Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Government_Information_Service) (RVD) instituted a media code on 21 June 2005 which essentially states that:[^cite_note-30]

- Photographs of the members of the royal house while performing their duties are always permitted.

- For other occasions (like holidays or vacations), the RVD will arrange a photo-op on condition that the press leave the family alone for the rest of the activity.

During a ski vacation in [Argentina](18951905.md), several photographs were taken of the prince and his family during the private part of their holiday, including one by [Associated Press](18935732.md) staff photographer Natacha Pisarenko, in spite of the media code, and after a photo opportunity had been provided earlier.[^cite_note-NRC_AP-31] The Associated Press decided to publish some of the photos, which were subsequently republished by several Dutch media. Willem-Alexander and the RVD jointly filed [lawsuit](113258.md) against the Associated Press on 5 August 2009, and the trial started on 14 August 2009 at the [district court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_court) in Amsterdam. On 28 August 2009, the district court ruled in favour of the prince and RVD, citing that the couple has a right to privacy, that the pictures in question add nothing to any public debate, and that they are not of any particular value to society since they are not photographs of his family "at work". Associated Press was sentenced to stop further publication of the photographs, on pain of a [€](9472.md)1,000 fine per violation with a [€](9472.md)50,000 maximum.[^cite_note-32]

In October 2020, Willem-Alexander apologised for a family holiday trip to Greece which had taken place while his country was under partial lockdown during the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_Netherlands).[^cite_note-Covid-Greece-33] He and his family cut their trip short, and in a two-minute video he stated that it "hurts to have betrayed" people's trust.[^cite_note-Covid-Greece-33] Earlier in August 2020, he and his wife were photographed with a restaurant owner during another trip to Greece, which was a violation of social distancing rules at the time.[^cite_note-Covid-Greece-33]

## Properties

From 2003 until 2019, Willem-Alexander and his family lived in Villa Eikenhorst on the De Horsten Estate in [Wassenaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassenaar).[^cite_note-34] After his mother abdicated and became Princess Beatrix once again, she moved to the castle of [Drakensteyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drakensteyn), after which the King and his family moved to the newly renovated monarch's palace of [Huis ten Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huis_ten_Bosch) in The Hague in 2019.[^cite_note-35]

Willem-Alexander has a villa near [Kranidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kranidi), Greece.[^cite_note-36]

### Villa in Machangulo

On 10 July 2008, the Prince of Orange and Princess Máxima announced that they had invested in a development project on the [Mozambican](19301.md) peninsula of [Machangulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machangulo).[^cite_note-37] The development project was aimed at building an ecologically responsible vacation resort, including a hotel and several luxury holiday homes for investors. The project was to invest heavily in the local economy of the peninsula (building schools and a local clinic) with an eye both towards responsible sustainability and maintaining a local staff.[^cite_note-38] After contacting Mozambican President [Armando Guebuza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armando_Guebuza) to verify that the Mozambican government had no objections, the couple decided to invest in two villas.[^cite_note-ADPres-39] In 2009, controversy erupted in parliament and the press about the project and the prince's involvement.[^cite_note-ADPres-39] Politician [Alexander Pechtold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pechtold) questioned the morality of building such a resort in a poor country like Mozambique. After public and parliamentary controversy, the royal couple announced that they had decided to sell the property in Machangulo once their house was completed.[^cite_note-NRC-40] In January 2012, it was confirmed that the villa had been sold.[^cite_note-NOS_sold-41]

Through his father, a member of the [House of Amsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Amsberg), he is descended from families of the lower [German nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_nobility), and through his mother, from several royal German–Dutch families such as the [House of Lippe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lippe), [Mecklenburg-Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Mecklenburg-Schwerin), the [House of Orange-Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Orange-Nassau), [Waldeck and Pyrmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldeck_and_Pyrmont), and the [House of Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hohenzollern). He is descended from the first king of the Netherlands, [William I of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_I_of_the_Netherlands), who was also a ruler in Luxembourg and several German states, and all subsequent Dutch monarchs.

Through his mother, Willem-Alexander also descends from [Paul I of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_I_of_Russia) and thus from German princess [Catherine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great) and Swedish King [Gustav I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_I). Through his father, he is also descended from several Dutch–Flemish families who left the [Low Countries](51402.md) during Spanish rule, such as the [Berenbergs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berenberg_family). His paternal great-great-grandfather Gabriel von Amsberg, a major-general of Mecklenburg, was recognized as noble as late as 1891, the family having adopted the "[von](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von)" in 1795.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

Willem-Alexander is a descendant of King [George II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_II_of_Great_Britain) and, more relevant for his succession rights, of his granddaughter [Princess Augusta of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Augusta_of_Great_Britain). Under the [British Act of Settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Settlement_1701), King Willem-Alexander forfeited his (distant) succession rights to the [throne of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throne_of_the_United_Kingdom) by marrying a Roman Catholic. This right has since been restored in 2015 under the [Succession to the Crown Act 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession_to_the_Crown_Act_2013).[^cite_note-44]

Finally, Willem-Alexander is also a distant descendent of [William the Silent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Silent), who is held to be the [Father of the Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Father_of_the_Nation) in the Netherlands, since [John William Friso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_William_Friso), a great-great-grandson of William the Silent, is known to be a common ancestor to all current European monarchs.

  [^cite_note-abdication-45]

Willem-Alexander's full title is: His Majesty King Willem-Alexander, King of the Netherlands, Prince of Orange-Nassau, etc., etc., etc.[^cite_note-abdication-45]

Willem-Alexander is the first Dutch king since [Willem III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_III_of_the_Netherlands) (d. 1890). Willem-Alexander had earlier indicated that when he became king, he would take the name *Willem IV*,[^cite_note-witteman97-46] but it was announced in January 2013 that his regnal name would be *Willem-Alexander*.[^cite_note-koninklijkhuis1-47]

[^cite_note-51]: &#x20;Per tradition, all children of a Dutch monarch are awarded the highest Dutch civil honour, the Grand Cross of the Order of the Netherlands Lion, on their eighteenth birthday,<sup>[\[50\]](#cite_note-50)</sup> including Willem-Alexander on 27 april 1985. He held this grade until he ascended the throne on 30 April 2013, at which point he became the Grand Master of the order.

[^cite_note-52]: &#x20;As a son of [Queen Beatrix of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Beatrix_of_the_Netherlands) at the time, Willem-Alexander was automatically appointed a Knight in the Order of the Golden Lion of Nassau at birth. He held this grade until he ascended the throne on 30 April 2013, at which point he became joint Grand Master of the order (with [Guillaume V, Grand Duke of Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_V,_Grand_Duke_of_Luxembourg) being the other since 2025).

[^cite_note-57]: &#x20;Willem-Alexander received the Officer's Cross on 6 December 2001, marked with "XV" insignia for 15 years of service.<sup>[\[51\]](#cite_note-53)</sup> It was upgraded with the "XX" insignia in 2006 for 20 years and with the "XXV" insignia in 2011 for 25 years of service.<sup>[\[52\]](#cite_note-54)</sup> In 2013, he was honorably discharged upon becoming king, as monarchs cannot serve as soldiers, and he no longer receives new year insignia.<sup>[\[53\]](#cite_note-55)</sup><sup>[\[54\]](#cite_note-56)</sup>
