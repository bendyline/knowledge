**Tauranga** (Māori pronunciation: [\[ˈtaʉɾaŋa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/M%C4%81ori) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/31/Tauranga_Pronunciation.wav/Tauranga_Pronunciation.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/31/Tauranga_Pronunciation.wav/Tauranga_Pronunciation.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, Māori language for "resting place" or "anchorage")[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] is a coastal city in the [Bay of Plenty Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Plenty_Region) and the [fifth-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_New_Zealand) of [New Zealand](4913064.md), with an urban population of 160,900  (June 2025),[^cite_note-NZ_population_data_2018-6] or roughly 3% of the national population. It was settled by Māori late in the 13th century and colonised by Europeans in the early 19th century. It was constituted as a city in 1963.[^cite_note-10]

The city lies in the northwestern corner of the Bay of Plenty, on the southeastern edge of [Tauranga Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauranga_Harbour). The city extends over an area of 141.91 square kilometres (54.79 sq mi), and encompasses the communities of [Bethlehem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem,_New_Zealand), on the southwestern outskirts of the city; [Greerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greerton), on the southern outskirts of the city; Matua, west of the central city overlooking Tauranga Harbour; Maungatapu; [Mount Maunganui](1166577.md), located north of the central city across the harbour facing the Bay of Plenty; [Otūmoetai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ot%C5%ABmoetai); [Papamoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papamoa), Tauranga's largest suburb, located in the Bay of Plenty; Tauranga City; [Tauranga South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauranga_South); and [Welcome Bay](51782732.md).

Tauranga is one of New Zealand's main centres for business, international trade, culture, fashion and horticultural science. The [Port of Tauranga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Tauranga) is New Zealand's largest port in terms of gross export tonnage and efficiency.[^cite_note-11] Tauranga is one of New Zealand's fastest-growing cities, with an 11% increase in population between the 2006 census and the 2013 census,[^cite_note-12] and 19% between the 2013 and 2018 census. Due to its rapid population growth, Tauranga has become New Zealand's fifth-largest city, overtaking [Dunedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin) and the [Napier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napier,_New_Zealand)-[Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings,_New_Zealand) urban areas.

## History

See also: [List of historic places in Tauranga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_historic_places_in_Tauranga)

### Settlement

The earliest known settlers were [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_people), who arrived in the 13th century at Tauranga in the [Tākitimu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C4%81kitimu) and the [Mātaatua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81taatua) [waka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waka_\(canoe\)).

At 9 am on Friday, 23 June 1826, [*Herald*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herald_\(1826_ship\)) was the first European ship to enter [Tauranga Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauranga_Harbour). The Revd [Henry Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Williams_\(missionary\)) conducted a Christian service at Otamataha Pā.[^cite_note-HW2-1-13][^cite_note-Fitzgerald2011-4-14][^cite_note-McCauley-15] In December 1826 and again in March 1827, the *Herald* travelled to Tauranga from the [Bay of Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Islands) to obtain supplies of potatoes, pigs and flax.[^cite_note-HW5-1-16][^cite_note-HW7-17] In 1835 a [Church Missionary Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_Missionary_Society) mission station was established at Tauranga by William Wade. Rev. [Alfred N. Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Nesbit_Brown) arrived at the CMS mission station in 1838.[^cite_note-LMR1874-18] John Morgan also visited the mission in 1838.[^cite_note-19]



View of waterfront in 1924

Europeans trading in [flax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phormium) were active in the Bay of Plenty during the 1830s; some were transient, others married local women and settled permanently. The first permanent non-Maori trader was [James Farrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Farrow_\(trader\)), who travelled to Tauranga in 1829, obtaining flax fibre for Australian merchants in exchange for [muskets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musket) and [gunpowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder). Farrow acquired a land area of 2,000 square metres (1⁄2 acre) on 10 January 1838 at [Otūmoetai Pā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ot%C5%ABmoetai) from the chiefs [Tupaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hori_Kingi_Tupaea), Tangimoana and Te Omanu, the earliest authenticated land purchase in the Bay of Plenty.[^cite_note-20]

In 1840, a [Catholic](606848.md) mission station was established. [Bishop Pompallier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Baptiste_Pompallier) was given land within the palisades of Otūmoetai Pā for a church and a [presbytery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbytery_\(residence\)). The mission station closed in 1863 due to land wars in the [Waikato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waikato) district.[^cite_note-21]

### New Zealand Wars

The [Tauranga Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauranga_Campaign) took place in and around Tauranga from 21 January to 21 June 1864, during the [New Zealand Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Wars). The [Battle of Gate Pā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauranga_Campaign#Battle_of_Gate_P%C4%81), the best known battle, was an attack on the well fortified [pā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81) and its Māori defenders on 29 April 1864 by British forces made up of approximately 300 men of the 43rd Regiment and a naval contingent. The British casualties were 31 dead (including 10 officers), and 80 wounded – the highest loss of life suffered by the British military in the New Zealand Wars. The Māori defenders abandoned the pā during the night with casualties estimated at 25 dead and an unknown number of wounded.[^cite_note-cowan42-22]

### Fires



Tauranga Hotel on fire, surrounded by a large crowd, 1936

A fire broke out in a store at 8\:00pm on 31 May 1881 and spread, destroying all the buildings in one block in the town and others in adjacent blocks. There was no fire brigade available.[^cite_note-23]

In November 1916, a large fire broke out on The Strand, destroying the Commercial Hotel and 11 other buildings.[^cite_note-24]

In 1936 another large fire occurred which started in the hotel's staff quarters and drew large crowds.[^cite_note-25]

### Modern era

Under the *Local Government (Tauranga City Council) Order 2003*,[^cite_note-26] Tauranga became legally a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_New_Zealand) for a second time, from 1 March 2004.

In August 2011, Tauranga received [Ultra-Fast Broadband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra-Fast_Broadband) as part of the [New Zealand Government](8899161.md)'s rollout.[^cite_note-27]

## Geography



Satellite view of Tauranga and surrounding area

Tauranga is located around a large harbour that extends along the western [Bay of Plenty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Plenty), and is protected by [Matakana Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matakana_Island) and the extinct volcano of [Mauao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Maunganui_\(mountain\)) (Mount Maunganui). [Ngāumuwahine River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C4%81umuwahine_River) is located 19 kilometres southwest of Tauranga.

Tauranga and the [Bay of Plenty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Plenty) are situated along a faultline and so experience (infrequent) seismic activity. There are a few volcanoes around the area, including both dormant volcanoes such as Mauao, nicknamed "The Mount" by locals, and active volcanoes such as [White Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whakaari/White_Island).

Tauranga is roughly the [antipode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipodes_on_Earth) of [Jaén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ja%C3%A9n,_Spain), Spain.

### Climate

Tauranga has an [oceanic or maritime temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) climate (cfb) in the [Köppen Climate Classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification). Though in the [Trewartha Climate Classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification) it is [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical) (cfbl)[^cite_note-29]

During the summer months the population swells as holidaymakers descend on the city, especially along the popular white coastal surf beaches from [Mount Maunganui](1166577.md) to [Papamoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papamoa).

## Demographics

In 1976 Tauranga was a medium-sized urban area with a population of around 48,000. The completion of a harbour bridge in 1988 brought Tauranga and The Mount closer (they amalgamated in 1989) and re-energised the economies of both parts of the enlarged city. By 1996 Tauranga's population had grown to 82,092 and by 2006 had reached 103,635.[^cite_note-34] By 2023, it had reached 152,844.[^cite_note-Census_2023-35]

In 2008 Tauranga overtook [Dunedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin) to become the [sixth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranked_list_of_New_Zealand_main_urban_areas) city in New Zealand by urban area, and the [ninth largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_New_Zealand) city by [Territorial Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Authorities_of_New_Zealand) area. With continuing growth it has now surpassed the Napier-Hastings area to become New Zealand's fifth-largest city.

Tauranga covers 141.91 km<sup>2</sup> (54.79 sq mi)[^cite_note-Area-5] and had an estimated population of 161,000 as of June 2025,[^cite_note-NZ_population_data_2018-6] with a population density of 1,135 people per km<sup>2</sup>.

Tauranga had a population of 152,844 in the [2023 New Zealand census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_New_Zealand_census), an increase of 15,714 people (11.5%) since the [2018 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_New_Zealand_census), and an increase of 37,683 people (32.7%) since the [2013 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_New_Zealand_census). There were 73,821 males, 78,558 females and 462 people of [other genders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-binary_gender) in 55,929 dwellings.[^cite_note-37] 2.5% of people identified as [LGBTIQ+](78174325.md). The median age was 39.4 years (compared with 38.1 years nationally). There were 29,604 people (19.4%) aged under 15 years, 26,316 (17.2%) aged 15 to 29, 66,786 (43.7%) aged 30 to 64, and 30,138 (19.7%) aged 65 or older.[^cite_note-Census_2023-35]

People could identify as more than one ethnicity. The results were 78.7% [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_New_Zealanders) ([Pākehā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81keh%C4%81)); 19.3% [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_people); 3.6% [Pasifika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasifika_New_Zealanders); 10.3% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_New_Zealanders); 1.8% Middle Eastern, Latin American and African New Zealanders (MELAA); and 2.1% other, which includes people giving their ethnicity as "New Zealander". English was spoken by 96.2%, Māori language by 4.5%, Samoan by 0.4% and other languages by 12.5%. No language could be spoken by 2.1% (e.g. too young to talk). [New Zealand Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Sign_Language) was known by 0.4%. The percentage of people born overseas was 25.2, compared with 28.8% nationally.

Religious affiliations were 31.3% [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_New_Zealand), 1.5% [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_New_Zealand), 0.4% [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_New_Zealand), 1.9% [Māori religious beliefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_of_M%C4%81ori_people), 0.6% [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_New_Zealand), 0.4% [New Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Age), 0.1% [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaism_in_New_Zealand), and 3.3% other religions. People who answered that they had [no religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion_in_New_Zealand) were 53.7%, and 6.9% of people did not answer the census question.

Of those at least 15 years old, 22,431 (18.2%) people had a bachelor's or higher degree, 64,740 (52.5%) had a post-high school certificate or diploma, and 29,052 (23.6%) people exclusively held high school qualifications. ## Government and politics

For elections to the [New Zealand Parliament](340639.md), the city of Tauranga is in the [Tauranga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauranga_\(New_Zealand_electorate\)), [Bay of Plenty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Plenty_\(New_Zealand_electorate\)), and [Waiariki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waiariki_\(New_Zealand_electorate\)) electorates.

Tauranga is located in the administrative area of the [Tauranga City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauranga_City_Council). The council consists of the [Mayor of Tauranga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Tauranga) and nine councillors. The mayor is elected by the city [at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-large), while the councillors are elected from nine wards (constituencies), each ward electing a single councillor. Elections are held via [single transferable vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_transferable_vote).[^cite_note-46]

The present nine wards were first established for the 2024 local elections. There are eight general wards (Mauao/Mount Maunganui, Arataki, Pāpāmoa, Welcome Bay, Matua-Otūmoetai, Bethlehem, Tauriko and Te Papa) and one [Māori ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_wards_and_constituencies) (Te Awanui, covering the entire city).[^cite_note-47]

Council elections are usually held every three years, most recently in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Tauranga_local_elections). The next local election for Tauranga is scheduled for 2028.[^cite_note-48]

In December 2020, the [Minister of Local Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Local_Government_\(New_Zealand\)) [Nanaia Mahuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanaia_Mahuta) announced that, due to alleged "dysfunction" within the elected council, the council would be replaced by commissioners until the 2022 local elections.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] However, then Tauranga MP [Simon Bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Bridges) said the appointment of commissioners was unnecessary and a "dramatic and draconian step."[^cite_note-51] An independent review by law firm [Russell McVeagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_McVeagh) found that Mahuta's decision may have been unlawful.[^cite_note-52] Her decision to reappoint the [crown commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauranga_City_Council#Commission_reappointment_controversy) for a second term in 2022 through to July 2024 was subject to a legal review by [Dentons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dentons) Kensington Swan who found her decision was challengeable on the grounds of unlawfulness and unreasonableness.[^cite_note-53]

## City facilities and attractions



View over Greater Tauranga, taken from the top of Mauao

Greater Tauranga is a lifestyle and tourism destination. It features many natural attractions and scenery ranging from the beaches and harbour environments to bush-clad mountains with waterfalls and lakes.



View of The Strand, looking across Tauranga Harbour 2026

Cultural attractions include the Tauranga Art Gallery, which opened in October 2007 and showcases local, national and international exhibitions in a range of media. On the 17th Avenue, the "Historic Village on 17th",[^cite_note-65] recreates a historic setting with original and replica buildings from early Tauranga housing arts and gift shops.

The [Baycourt Community and Arts Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baycourt_Community_and_Arts_Centre) is a multi-purpose performing arts and theatre facility located in the central business district.

Aviation interests are served with the [Classic Flyers Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Flyers_Museum) and the Gyrate Flying Club where you can experience flying a modern gyroplane; the "motorbike of the sky".[^cite_note-66]

Tauranga has many parks: one of the largest is [Memorial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Park,_Tauranga), and others include Yatton Park, Kulim Park, Fergusson Park and the large Tauranga Domain. The [Te Puna Quarry Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Puna_Quarry_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1) has become a regional attraction, known for being converted from a disused quarry into a community park.[^cite_note-67]

Due to the temperate climate, outdoor activities are very popular, including golf, tramping (hiking), mountain biking and white water rafting. The Bay of Plenty coastline has miles of golden sandy beaches, and watersports are very popular, including swimming, surfing, fishing, diving, kayaking and kitesurfing. Tourists also enjoy dolphin-watching on specially run boat trips.

The coastal suburb [Papamoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papamoa) and neighbouring [Mount Maunganui](1166577.md) are some of the more affluent areas in Tauranga. The region's beaches attract swimmers, surfers, kayakers and kitesurfers throughout the year.

Tauranga has many outlying islands and reefs that make it a notable tourist destination point for travelling scuba divers and marine enthusiasts.[^cite_note-68] Extensive marine life diversity is available to scuba divers all year round. Water temperatures range from 12 degrees Celsius in winter to 22–24 degrees Celsius in summer. Tauranga houses two professional dive instructor training centres, training NAUI, PADI and SSI dive leader systems.

## Infrastructure

### Hospitals

Tauranga Hospital is a public secondary regional hospital located in Tauranga South, with 360 beds including neonatal, geriatric, surgical, maternity and mental health care.[^cite_note-69] It provides elective and emergency healthcare across medical, surgical, paediatric, obstetric, gynaecological and psychiatric services. The main tertiary referral centre for Tauranga Hospital is [Waikato Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waikato_Hospital), located in [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_New_Zealand). As the site of the Bay of Plenty Clinical School, Tauranga Hospital provides training to medical students from the [University of Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Auckland), as well as selective and elective placements for nursing and midwifery students.[^cite_note-70]

Grace Hospital is Tauranga's only private specialist surgical hospital, located in Oropi. It accommodates 6 operating theatres, 48 inpatient beds, a two-bed HDU, a procedure room for minor surgery and two procedure rooms for endoscopy.[^cite_note-71]

### Utilities

[Powerco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powerco) operates the local distribution network in the city,[^cite_note-72] with electricity supplied from [Transpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transpower_New_Zealand_Limited)'s national grid at three substations: [Greerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greerton), [Kaitemako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaitemako) and [Matapihi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matapihi).

Natural gas arrived in Tauranga in 1982, following the completion of the high-pressure pipeline from the [Maui pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maui_gas_field) near [Te Awamutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Awamutu) to the city, now operated by [First Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Gas).[^cite_note-73] First Gas also operates the gas distribution network within the city.

From 24 October 2024, the [Tauranga City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauranga_City_Council) began [fluoridating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_fluoridation) the city's water supply in response to a directive from the Director-General of Health.[^cite_note-74]

### Rail

Tauranga has no passenger rail network, however it is a busy freight rail hub due to distribution from the Port of Tauranga.

#### Tauranga railway station



The first Tauranga timetable

Tauranga is located on the [East Coast Main Trunk Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_Main_Trunk_Railway) (ECMT). It had a passenger station from 1924 to 1986, 97.06 km (60.31 mi) from the start of the ECMT at [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_railway_station,_New_Zealand). There was also a small station in the centre of town at Strand, finally closed in 2001. Although regular passenger services have ended, it is one of [KiwiRail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KiwiRail)'s busiest lines, with up to 86 goods trains a week between [MetroPort Auckland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Tauranga#Company) and Tauranga[^cite_note-76] (Sulphur Point[^cite_note-77] and [Mount Maunganui](1166577.md#Mount_Maunganui_railway_station)).[^cite_note-78] Of the original buildings, the 1927 cargo shed is the only one to survive.

##### History

In 1882, a company was formed, the [Tauranga East Coast and Hot Lakes District Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauranga_East_Coast_and_Hot_Lakes_District_Railway), to build a railway from Tauranga to Rotorua. The company failed to raise sufficient capital for the venture and was dissolved in 1886.

In 1906 [William Hall Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hall-Jones), the [Minister of Public Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Works_\(New_Zealand\)) visited Tauranga and announced that a railway must come to Tauranga.[^cite_note-79] It took another 22 years to complete. The 38 mi (61 km) Tauranga-[Matatā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matat%C4%81) railway,[^cite_note-\:13-80] was built by the [Public Works Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Works_and_Development) (PWD) and opened in stages; Maunganui-[Te Puke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Puke#Railway) opened on 16 October 1913,[^cite_note-81] extended to [Paengaroa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paengaroa) by April 1917,[^cite_note-82] to [Matatā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matat%C4%81) about late July 1917,[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] to [Awakeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awakeri) on 12 November 1923[^cite_note-88] and the 4 mi (6.4 km) Te Maunga to Tauranga section on Friday, 20 June 1924.[^cite_note-\:16-89] The whole ECMT, from Auckland to [Tāneatua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C4%81neatua#History), was opened at the Strand on Wednesday 28 March 1928 by the Prime Minister, [Gordon Coates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Coates),[^cite_note-90] and transferred from PWD to [New Zealand Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Railways_Department) (NZR) on 18 June 1928 for the [Tahawai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahawai)-Tauranga section and Tauranga-Tāneatua, on 2 September 1928.[^cite_note-\:02222-91]

In 1913 a temporary railway wharf was built, but then it was not until 1923 that a station and wharf accommodation were mentioned. In 1925 an engine shed was built and, when the railway fully opened in 1928, there was a platform, cart approach, 60 ft (18 m) x 30 ft (9.1 m) goods shed, loading bank, cattle and sheep yards, 70 ft (21 m) [turntable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_turntable), engine shed, 50-ton coal store, passing loop for 68 wagons, 6,000 gallon water vats with 9 inch hose pipes at each end of the station and a [traverser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traverser_\(railway\)) at the wharf.[^cite_note-\:02222-91] PWD built a quay of about 710 ft (220 m), known as the Railway Wharf, with a spur line from the ECMT. The cargo shed was completed in 1927, although at first the wharf provided only 320 ft (98 m) of berthage.[^cite_note-\:02222-91] The cargo shed had sliding doors to the wharf and a landing for motor trucks on its Dive Crescent frontage, with some cargo transferred direct from ship to railway wagon, some into the shed and some to trucks.[^cite_note-\:12-92] Sometimes the wharf was too busy and ships had to wait.[^cite_note-93] In March 1965 *Poranui* was the last [Northern Steamship Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Steamship_Company) coaster to visit the Railway Wharf. After the [Tauranga Harbour Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauranga_Harbour_Bridge) was built in 1988 only small ships could reach the wharf.[^cite_note-\:12-92]

In 1935 [mixed trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_train) from Auckland reached Tauranga at 10am, 12.55pm and the [Tāneatua Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taneatua_Express) at 4.41pm. It had a 10 minute refreshment stop.[^cite_note-\:11-94] [Refreshment rooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_refreshment_room) opened on 5 December 1928.[^cite_note-95] They were 100 ft (30 m) long with a 56 ft (17 m) x 19 ft (5.8 m) counter room, 19 ft (5.8 m) x 16 ft (4.9 m) and kitchen, stores, staff, milk, ham, fuel and lumber rooms. The roof had [Marseilles tiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseilles_tile) and the 20 ft (6.1 m) wide verandah matched that of the station, with a total length of 250 ft (76 m).[^cite_note-96] Although passenger services ended in 1967, the rooms remained in 1969, to cater for the [Road Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Railways_Road_Services).[^cite_note-97] [Railway houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railways_Department's_Housing_Scheme) were built; in 1910 an 8-roomed house and [platelayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platelayer)' cottages, 1921 a house, 1922 the [stationmaster's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_master) house and 4 houses, 1925 2, 1926 cottages 1928 20 houses and state houses in 1953 (2), 1955 (6) and 1956 (7).[^cite_note-\:02222-91]

From 30 March 1928 a guard, driver, acting driver, acting fireman, [W<sup>W</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NZR_WW_class) engine, 2 passenger cars and a bogie van were transferred from Tahawai to Tauranga, to work all the trains between Tauranga and Waihi.[^cite_note-\:02222-91] Taxis met trains at Tauranga, as the CBD was a kilometre south of the station.[^cite_note-\:11-94] In 1933 [Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_plc) built a [private siding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siding_\(rail\)#Private_siding).[^cite_note-\:02222-91] In 1973 [kauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kauri) trees were felled when a new NZR telephone exchange was built.[^cite_note-98]

&#x20;Passenger services ended on 11 September 1967[^cite_note-\:0-99] and the main station was demolished in 1986.[^cite_note-100] The 1927 cargo shed remains and was refurbished as an events venue in 2023.[^cite_note-101]

#### Matapihi Railway Bridge

Tauranga Harbour Bridge, now known as [Matapihi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matapihi#Matapihi_railway_station) Railway Bridge,[^cite_note-107] No.71, is 461.49 m (1,514.1 ft) long.[^cite_note-\:10-108] It spans the [Waimapu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waimapu) Estuary on 14 x 85 ton spans of 105 ft (32 m), with 250,628 rivets, the last being ceremoniously driven in on 25 February 1924. It rests on 15 cylinders, of 25 ft (7.6 m) to 110 ft (34 m). The east end is straight, transitioning to a 12 ch (240 m) curve. At its highest point the bridge is 12 ft (3.7 m) above high water mark. Nearly 3,000 yd<sup>3</sup> (2,300 m<sup>3</sup>) of concrete and 100 tons of steel were used in the piers and abutments and 1,200 tons of steel in the girders. A warm water spring was found in construction.[^cite_note-109] The first pier had been built by 1916.[^cite_note-110] In 1918 *[The New Zealand Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Zealand_Herald)* questioned whether delays were due to wartime steel shortages, or money saving.[^cite_note-111] It was not until Sunday, 1 June 1924 that the bridge opened.[^cite_note-\:02222-91] The alternative road route between Tauranga and Matapihi was about 16 mi (26 km) long, but a walkway was deemed too expensive, until, in 1958 it was decided to attach a walkway to the bridge, as 30 people had died by falling from it,[^cite_note-112] or being hit by trains.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]

### Bus

Main transportation in the city is provided by the BayBus, with twelve routes servicing the city's population. Bay Hopper buses depart the central stops in Tauranga's CBD, Ohauiti, Mount Maunganui and Bayfair every 15 minutes, with the routes to Papamoa and Greerton half-hourly.[^cite_note-115] [Bee Cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bee_Card_\(New_Zealand\)) were introduced for fares on 27 July 2020.[^cite_note-116]

The city is also a waypoint for bus travel between cities, with the Bay Hopper, and Intercity having a daily schedule.

### Sea

Tauranga has a passenger ferry service that transports passengers from Tauranga CBD to Mount Maunganui's Salisbury wharf. It is a 2-Way service that costs [\$](161585.md)15 each way.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

## Education

Main article: [List of schools in the Bay of Plenty Region § Tauranga City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_the_Bay_of_Plenty_Region#Tauranga_City)

Tauranga is home to the Bay of Plenty Tertiary Education Partnership, made up of:

- [Toi Ohomai Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toi_Ohomai_Institute_of_Technology)

- The [University of Waikato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Waikato)

- [Te Whare Wānanga o Awanuiārangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te_Whare_W%C4%81nanga_o_Awanui%C4%81rangi)

  [^cite_note-119]

The organisations currently share two main campuses, but are planning a new central campus. Stage 1 was expected to be open in 2017, catering for 500 but with capacity for 700, which cost \$67.3 million.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121]

Tauranga's secondary schools are:

- [Tauranga Boys' College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauranga_Boys'_College), with about 2100 boys.

  [^cite_note-122]

- [Tauranga Girls' College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauranga_Girls'_College), with nearly 1500 girls.

  [^cite_note-123]

- [Otumoetai College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otumoetai_College), with nearly 1900 students.

  [^cite_note-124]

- [Bethlehem College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_Campus), a state integrated Christian school offering [kindergarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindergarten) and Year 1–13, with around 1500 students.

- [Aquinas College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquinas_College,_Tauranga) a state integrated coeducational Catholic school founded in 2003 for Years 7–13, with around 800 students.

- [Mount Maunganui College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Maunganui_College), a co-educational secondary school, with over 1500 students.

- [Pāpāmoa College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81p%C4%81moa_College), co-educational secondary school opened in 2011 for years 7–13.

- Te Wharekura o Mauao,

  [^cite_note-125]

  &#x20;a co-educational wharekura-ā-iwi total immersion Māori secondary school for Years 7–13, founded in 2010, with around 200 students.

- Te Kura Kaupapa Māori o Te Kura Kōkiri, a co-educational [kura kaupapa Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kura_kaupapa_M%C4%81ori) total immersion school for Years 1–13, founded in 2000, with around 140 students.

[ACG Tauranga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACG_Tauranga), the city's first fully [private](612857.md#New_Zealand) school,[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127] offers school to Year 12.[^cite_note-128]
