The **119th United States Congress** is the current meeting of the [legislative branch of the United States federal government](31756.md), composed of the [United States Senate](24909346.md) and the [United States House of Representatives](19468510.md). It convened on January 3, 2025, for the last 17 days of [Joe Biden's presidency](65732174.md) and continued during the first two years of [Donald Trump's second presidency](78298108.md).

Following the [2024 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_elections), the [Republican Party](32070.md) retained its slim majority in the [House of Representatives](19468510.md) (albeit with losing two net seats to end up with a three-seat majority). The Republican Party also won a three-seat majority in the [Senate](24909346.md) after winning four net seats. Upon Trump's [second inauguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_inauguration_of_Donald_Trump) on January 20, 2025, the Republican Party has an overall federal [government trifecta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_trifecta#United_States) for the first time since the [115th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/115th_United_States_Congress) (2017–2019), which was in session during Trump's [first term](52231341.md).[^cite_note-2]

The 119th Congress features the slimmest majority in the House for any party since the [72nd Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/72nd_United_States_Congress) (1931–1933), and the first openly [transgender](19904525.md) member of Congress in history, Representative [Sarah McBride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_McBride) ([D](5043544.md)-[DE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware)).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] It also features the fewest split Senate delegations since the passage of the [Seventeenth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventeenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution), which established the direct election of U.S. senators.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

&#x20;Despite a trifecta, the first year of the Congress saw the lowest number of House votes in a nonelection year since the [101st Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_United_States_Congress) in 1990.[^cite_note-7]

As of 2025, the 119th Congress been one of the least productive when it comes to passing bills.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## History

Further information: [2025 in United States politics and government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_in_United_States_politics_and_government)

In the [2024 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_elections), the [Republican Party](32070.md) retained control of the [House of Representatives](19468510.md) and gained control of the [Senate](24909346.md), while Republican nominee [Donald Trump](4848272.md) won the [presidential election](59568546.md), securing a second non-consecutive term.[^cite_note-10] The results of the election were attributed to economic conditions of voters and concerns over immigration, particularly the [Mexico–United States border crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border_crisis).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

The Senate flipped to a 53–47 Republican majority, and in their [leadership elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Senate_Republican_Conference_leadership_election), Senator [John Thune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Thune) of [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota) was elected to succeed Mitch McConnell, who had been in power for 18 years.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

The House assumed a 220–215 Republican majority, among the narrowest controlling majorities in House history with the [65th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/65th_United_States_Congress).[^cite_note-17] [Mike Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Johnson) was [re-elected as speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives_election) on the first ballot after initially not receiving enough votes on the roll call, with the vote remaining open until enough members changed votes to support him.[^cite_note-18]

On January 6, a joint session convened to [count the presidential Electoral College votes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States_Electoral_College_vote_count). The proceedings were peaceful, four years after the [January 6 Capitol attack](66307490.md), in which supporters of Trump entered the Capitol and disrupted [Joe Biden](145422.md)'s [certification as president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_States_Electoral_College_vote_count). In response to the attack and Trump's [efforts to overturn the 2020 election](65905202.md), Congress has passed [revisions to the Electoral Count Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_Count_Reform_and_Presidential_Transition_Improvement_Act_of_2022) that prevent the vice president from altering the results and significantly raises the bar for certification objections.[^cite_note-19]

Comprising 80% of the membership of the House of Representatives and 89% of the Senate, [Baby boomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_boomers) and [Generation X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_X) remained the largest generations represented in Congress after having comprised more than 80% of the membership of both chambers since at least the [115th United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/115th_United_States_Congress) and Baby boomers alone comprising the majority of the House of Representatives and the Senate since the [106th United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/106th_United_States_Congress) and the [111th United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/111th_United_States_Congress) respectively.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]
