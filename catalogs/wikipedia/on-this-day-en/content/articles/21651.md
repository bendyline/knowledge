**North Dakota** ( [/dəˈkoʊtə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/de/En-us-North_Dakota.ogg/En-us-North_Dakota.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/de/En-us-North_Dakota.ogg/En-us-North_Dakota.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*də-KOH-tə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key))[^cite_note-6] is a landlocked [U.S. state](18618239.md) in the [Upper Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Midwest), named after the indigenous [Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_people) and [Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux) peoples. It is bordered by the Canadian provinces of [Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan) and [Manitoba](18926.md) to the north and by the U.S. states of [Minnesota](19590.md) to the east, [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota) to the south, and [Montana](19978.md) to the west. North Dakota is part of the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains) region, characterized by broad [prairies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie), [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe), temperate [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna), [badlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badlands), and farmland. North Dakota is the [19th-largest state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area) by area, but with a population of just under 800,000, the [fourth-least populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population) and [fourth-least densely populated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_population_density). The [state capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States) is [Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismarck,_North_Dakota) and the [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_North_Dakota) is [Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo,_North_Dakota), which accounts for nearly a fifth of the state's population; both cities are among the fastest-growing in the U.S., although half of North Dakotans live in rural areas.

The area of North Dakota was inhabited for thousands of years by [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American) tribes, including the [Mandan, Hidatsa, and Arikara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandan,_Hidatsa,_and_Arikara) along the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River); the [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe) and [Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree) in the northeast; and several Sioux groups (the [Nakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assiniboine), [Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_people), and [Lakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakota_people)) in the rest of the state. European explorers and traders first arrived in the early 18th century, mostly in pursuit of furs.

The United States acquired the region in the early 19th century, gradually settling it amid [growing resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Indian_Wars) by increasingly displaced natives. The [Dakota Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Territory), established in 1861, became central to [American pioneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_pioneer), with the [Homestead Act of 1862](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Act_of_1862) precipitating significant population growth and development. The traditional fur trade declined in favor of farming, particularly of wheat. The [Dakota Boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Boom) of 1878 to 1886 saw giant farms stretched across the rolling prairies, with the territory becoming a regional economic power. The [Northern Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Pacific_Railway) and [Great Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_Railway_\(U.S.\)) railway companies competed for access to lucrative grain centers; farmers banded together in political and socioeconomic alliances that were central to the Midwest's broader [Populist Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populist_movement_\(United_States,_19th_Century\)). North and South Dakota were [admitted to the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admitted_to_the_Union) on November 2, 1889, as the 39th and 40th states. President [Benjamin Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Harrison) shuffled the statehood papers before signing them so that no one could tell which became a state first; consequently, the two states are officially numbered in alphabetical order.[^cite_note-7] In subsequent decades, interest rose in radical agrarian movements and economic cooperatives, of which one legacy is the [Bank of North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_North_Dakota), the nation's only state-run bank. This coincided with the gradual winding-down of the pioneer period, and the state was fully settled by around 1920.[^cite_note-8]



Logo for the State of North Dakota.

Beginning in the mid-20th century, North Dakota's rich [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource) became more critical to economic development; into the 21st century, [oil extraction](8082027.md) from the [Bakken formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakken_formation) in the northwest has played a [major role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_oil_boom) in the state's prosperity. Such development has led to population growth (along with high birth rates) and reduced unemployment. It is believed to contain North America's geographic center, in [Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby,_North_Dakota), and is home to what was once the tallest artificial structure in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md), the [KVLY-TV mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVLY-TV_mast).

## History

Main article: [History of North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_North_Dakota)

### Pre-colonial history

Native American people lived in what is now North Dakota for thousands of years before the arrival of Europeans. The known tribes included the [Mandan people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandan_people) (from around the 11th century),[^cite_note-auto-9] while the first [Hidatsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidatsa) group arrived a few hundred years later.[^cite_note-10] They both assembled in villages on tributaries of the Missouri River in what would become west-central North Dakota. [Crow Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crow_Indians) traveled the plains from the west to visit and trade with the related Hidatsas[^cite_note-auto-9] after the split between them, probably in the 17th century.[^cite_note-11]

Later came divisions of the [Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sioux): the [Lakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakota_people), the [Santee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_people) and the [Yanktonai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanktonai). The [Assiniboine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assiniboine) and the [Plains Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plains_Cree_people) undertook southward journeys to the village Indians, either for trade or for war.[^cite_note-auto-9][^cite_note-12] The [Shoshone Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoshone_Indians) in present-day [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming) and Montana may have carried out attacks on Indian enemies as far east as the Missouri.[^cite_note-13] A group of [Cheyennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheyenne) lived in a village of earth lodges at the lower [Sheyenne River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheyenne_River) ([Biesterfeldt Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biesterfeldt_Site)) for decades in the 18th century.

Due to attacks by Crees, Assiniboines and [Chippewas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chippewas) armed with firearms, they left the area around 1780 and crossed Missouri some time after.[^cite_note-14] A band of the few [Sotaio Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheyenne) lived east of Missouri River and met the uprooted Cheyennes before the end of the century. They soon followed the Cheyennes across [Missouri](19571.md) and lived among them south of [Cannonball River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannonball_River).[^cite_note-15]

Eventually, the Cheyenne and the Sutaio became one tribe and turned into mounted buffalo hunters with ranges mainly outside North Dakota. Before the middle of the 19th century, the [Arikara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arikara) entered the future state from the south and joined the Mandan and Hidatsa.[^cite_note-16] With time, a number of Indians entered into treaties with the United States. Many of the treaties defined the territory of a specific tribe.

### European exploration and colonization

The first European to reach the area was the [French-Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French-Canadian) trader [Pierre Gaultier, sieur de La Vérendrye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Gaultier_de_Varennes,_sieur_de_La_V%C3%A9rendrye), who led an exploration and trading party to the [Mandan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandan) villages in 1738 guided by Assiniboine Indians.[^cite_note-atlas_of_canada-17]

From 1762 to 1800, the region formed part of [Spanish Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Louisiana), part of New Spain, administered from Mexico City.[^cite_note-18]



[Fort Union Trading Post National Historic Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Union_Trading_Post_National_Historic_Site)

On October 1, 1800, the [Third Treaty of San Ildefonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Treaty_of_San_Ildefonso) was signed, and the territory of Spanish Louisiana was transferred to France as part of [French Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_France\)), which was later sold to the United States in the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase).

The northeastern portion of the state, corresponding to the [Red River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Valley) and the drainage basin to the [Hudson Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Bay) was, at the time, part of [Rupert's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert's_Land), a [British North American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America) territory. It remained under control of the [Hudson's Bay Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_Company) until the [Anglo-American Convention of 1818](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-American_Convention_of_1818), which set the border between it and the United States to the [49th parallel](630260.md).

### Settlement and statehood

Main article: [Dakota Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Territory)

European Americans settled in Dakota Territory only sparsely until the late 19th century, when railroads opened up the region. With the advantage of grants of land, they vigorously marketed their properties, extolling the region as ideal for agriculture.

Differences between the northern and southern part caused resentments between the settlers. The northern part was seen by the more populated southern part as somewhat disreputable, "too much controlled by the wild folks, cattle ranchers, fur traders" and too frequently the site of conflict with the indigenous population. The northern part was generally content with remaining a territory. However, following the territorial capital being moved from [Yankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankton,_South_Dakota) in the southern part to Bismarck, the southern part began to call for division. Finally, at the [1887 Dakota Territory division referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1887_Dakota_Territory_division_referendum), the voters approved splitting the territory into two. The division was done by the seventh standard parallel.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Other account(s) state that the real reason for the split was a political lure for four Republican senators instead of two from the Republican dominated Dakota Territory and in their push to split the territory, Republican congressmen also ignored the uncomfortable fact that much of the land in the anticipated state of South Dakota belonged to the Sioux.[^cite_note-ndstudies.gov-21]

Congress passed an omnibus bill for statehood for North Dakota, [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota), [Montana](19978.md), and [Washington](13015878.md), titled the [Enabling Act of 1889](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enabling_Act_of_1889), on February 22, 1889, during the administration of President [Grover Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grover_Cleveland). His successor, [Benjamin Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Harrison), signed the proclamations formally admitting North Dakota and South Dakota to the Union on November 2, 1889.[^cite_note-washington-22]

There was a rivalry between the two new states over which one would be admitted first. So Harrison directed Secretary of State [James G. Blaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_G._Blaine) to shuffle the papers and obscure from him which he was signing first to keep both the states happy and to avoid showing favor to either state. The actual order went unrecorded, thus no one knows which of the Dakotas was admitted first.[^cite_note-using-23][^cite_note-nd_boundaries-24][^cite_note-ndstudies.gov-21] However, since *North Dakota* alphabetically appears before *South Dakota*, its proclamation was published first in the Statutes At Large.

### 20th century

Unrest among wheat farmers, especially among Norwegian [immigrants](4599312.md), led to a populist political movement centered in the [Non Partisan League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non_Partisan_League) ("NPL") around the time of [World War I](4764461.md). The NPL ran candidates on the Republican ticket (but merged into the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_Democratic-Nonpartisan_League_Party) after [World War II](32927.md)). It tried to insulate North Dakota from the power of out-of-state banks and corporations.

In addition to founding the state-owned [Bank of North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_North_Dakota) and [North Dakota Mill and Elevator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_Mill_and_Elevator) (both still in existence), the NPL established a state-owned railroad line (later sold to the [Soo Line Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soo_Line_Railroad)). Anti-corporate laws virtually prohibited a corporation or bank from owning title to land zoned as farmland. These laws, still in force today, after having been upheld by state and federal courts, make it almost impossible to foreclose on farmland, as even after foreclosure, the property title cannot be held by a bank or mortgage company.[^cite_note-state-law-25] Furthermore, the Bank of North Dakota, having powers similar to a Federal Reserve branch bank, exercised its power to limit the issuance of subprime mortgages and their collateralization in the form of derivative instruments, and so prevented a collapse of housing prices within the state in the wake of 2008's financial crisis.[^cite_note-state_bank-26]



[North Dakota State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_State_Capitol), featuring an Art Deco tower

The original [North Dakota State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_State_Capitol) in Bismarck burned to the ground on December 28, 1930. It was replaced by a [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone)-faced [art-deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art-deco) skyscraper that still stands today.[^cite_note-capitol-27] A round of federal investment and construction projects began in the 1950s, including the [Garrison Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison_Dam) and the [Minot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minot_Air_Force_Base) and [Grand Forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Forks_Air_Force_Base) [Air Force](32090.md) bases.[^cite_note-timeline-28]

Western North Dakota saw a boom in [oil exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_exploration) in the late 1970s and early 1980s, as rising petroleum prices made development profitable.[^cite_note-nd_history_summary-29] This boom came to an end after petroleum prices declined.[^cite_note-nd_history_summary-29]

### 21st century

Main article: [North Dakota oil boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_oil_boom)

In 2010, the state had lower rates of unemployment than the national average,[^cite_note-businessinsider-30] and increased job and population growth.[^cite_note-startribune-31] Much of the growth has been based on development of the [Bakken oil fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakken_formation) in the western part of the state.[^cite_note-usatoday30.usatoday-32] Estimates as to the remaining amount of oil in the area vary, with some estimating over 100 years' worth.[^cite_note-33]

For decades, North Dakota's annual murder and violent crime rates were regularly the lowest in the United States. In recent years, however, while still below the national average, crime has risen sharply. In 2016, the violent crime rate was three times higher than in 2004, with the rise occurring mostly in the late 2000s, coinciding with the oil boom era. This happened at a time when the national violent crime rate declined slightly.[^cite_note-34] Workers in the oil boom towns have been blamed for much of the increase.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_North_Dakota)

See also: [List of North Dakota counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_North_Dakota_counties)

See also: [Geology of North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_North_Dakota)

North Dakota is located in the [Upper Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Midwest) region of the United States. It lies at the center of the [North American continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_continent) and borders [Canada](5042916.md) to the north. The geographic center of North America is near the town of [Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby,_North_Dakota). [Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismarck,_North_Dakota) is the capital of North Dakota, and [Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo,_North_Dakota) is the most populous city.



View of western North Dakota

North Dakota is in the U.S. region known as the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains). The state shares the [Red River of the North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_of_the_North) with [Minnesota](19590.md) to the east. [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota) is to the south, [Montana](19978.md) is to the west, and the Canadian provinces of [Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan) and [Manitoba](18926.md) are to the north. North Dakota is near the middle of [North America](21139.md) with a stone marker in [Rugby, North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby,_North_Dakota) marking the "Geographic Center of the North American Continent". With an area of 70,762 square miles (183,273 km<sup>2</sup>),[^cite_note-37] 69,001 square miles (178,712 km<sup>2</sup>) of which is land,[^cite_note-38] North Dakota is the 19th largest state.[^cite_note-39]

The western half of the state consists of the hilly [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains) as well as the northern part of the [Badlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badlands), which are to the west of the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River). The state's high point, [White Butte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Butte_\(North_Dakota\)) at 3,506 feet (1,069 m), and [Theodore Roosevelt National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt_National_Park)[^cite_note-40] are in the Badlands. The region is abundant in [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) including [natural gas](22131.md), [crude oil](23195.md) and [lignite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite) coal.[^cite_note-41]

The [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River) forms [Lake Sakakawea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Sakakawea), the third largest artificial lake in the United States, behind the [Garrison Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison_Dam).[^cite_note-42]

The central region of N.D. is divided into the [Drift Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drift_Prairie) and the [Missouri Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Plateau). The eastern part of N.D. consists of the flat [Red River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Valley), the bottom of glacial [Lake Agassiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Agassiz). Its fertile soil, drained by the meandering [Red River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_of_the_North) flowing northward into [Lake Winnipeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Winnipeg), supports a large agriculture industry.[^cite_note-43] [Devils Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devils_Lake_\(North_Dakota\)), the largest natural lake in the state, is also found in the east.[^cite_note-50states-44]



The [Little Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Missouri_River_\(North_Dakota\)) and [Theodore Roosevelt National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt_National_Park) appear with [Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) on the reverse of the 2016 [America the Beautiful quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_the_Beautiful_quarters).

Most of N.D. is covered in [grassland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassland); crops cover most of eastern North Dakota but become increasingly sparse in the center and farther west. Natural trees in North Dakota are found usually where there is good drainage, such as the ravines and valley near the [Pembina Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembina_Gorge_State_Park) and [Killdeer Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killdeer_Mountains), the [Turtle Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtle_Mountain_\(plateau\)), the hills around Devils Lake, in the dunes area of McHenry County in central North Dakota, and along the Sheyenne Valley slopes and the Sheyenne delta. This diverse terrain supports nearly 2,000 species of plants.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

Soil is North Dakota's most precious resource. It is the base of the state's great agricultural wealth. North Dakota also has enormous mineral resources. These mineral resources include billions of tons of lignite coal. In addition, North Dakota has large oil reserves. [Petroleum](23195.md) was discovered in the state in 1951 and quickly became one of North Dakota's most valuable mineral resources. In the early 2000s, the emergence of [hydraulic fracturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_fracturing) technologies enabled mining companies to extract huge amounts of oil from the Bakken shale rock formation in the western part of the state.[^cite_note-47]

## Demographics

### Population



North Dakota population density



North Dakota [population pyramid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_pyramid)

At the 2023 estimate, North Dakota's population was 783,926 on July 1, 2023, a 0.62% increase since the [2020 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-PopEstUS-53] North Dakota is the fourth least-populous state in the country; only [Alaska](624.md), [Vermont](32578.md), and [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming) have fewer residents.[^cite_note-\:3-54]

From fewer than 2,000 people in 1870, North Dakota's population grew to near 680,000 by 1930. Growth then slowed, and the population fluctuated slightly over the next seven decades, hitting a low of 617,761 in the 1970 census, with 642,200 in the 2000 census.[^cite_note-ndsupop-55] In the 21st Century North Dakota has experienced significant growth reaching a record population of 783,926 in 2023.[^cite_note-56] Except for [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas), the North Dakota population has a lesser percentage of minorities than in the nation as a whole.[^cite_note-quickfacts-57] As of 2011, 20.7% of North Dakota's population younger than age 1 were minorities.[^cite_note-58] The [center of population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_population) of North Dakota is in [Wells County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_County,_North_Dakota), near [Sykeston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykeston).[^cite_note-59]

According to [HUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Housing_and_Urban_Development)'s 2023 [Annual Homeless Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Homeless_Assessment_Report_to_Congress), there were an estimated 784 [homeless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeless) people in North Dakota.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

From the 1930s until the end of the 20th century, North Dakota's population gradually declined, interrupted by a couple of brief increases. Young adults with university degrees were particularly likely to leave the state. With the advancing process of mechanization of agricultural practices, and environmental conditions requiring larger landholdings for successful agriculture, subsistence farming proved to be too risky for families. Many people moved to urban areas for jobs.[^cite_note-80]

Since the late 20th century, one of the major causes of migration from North Dakota is the lack of skilled jobs for college graduates. Expansion of economic development programs has been urged to create skilled and high-tech jobs, but the effectiveness of such programs has been open to debate.[^cite_note-81] During the first decade of the 21st century, the population increased in large part because of jobs in the oil industry related to development of [unconventional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconventional_\(oil_&_gas\)_reservoir) [tight oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tight_oil) (shale oil) fields.[^cite_note-82] Elsewhere, the Native American population has increased as some reservations have attracted people back from urban areas.

According to the [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census), the racial and ethnic composition of North Dakota was 88.7% [non-Hispanic white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_white), 5.4% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American), 1.2% [Black or African American](2154.md), 1.0% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans), 0.1% [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander), 0.5% some other race, and 0.2% from [two or more races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_or_more_races).[^cite_note-83] At the 2019 [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey), North Dakota's racial and ethnic makeup was 83.6% non-Hispanic white, 2.9% Black or African American, 5.0% Native American and Alaska Native, 1.4% Asian, 0.4% Native Hawaiian or other Pacific Islander, 0.1% some other race, 2.7% multiracial, and 4.0% [Hispanic or Latin American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) of any race.[^cite_note-84]

North Dakota is one of the top resettlement locations for refugees proportionally. According to the U.S. Office of Refugee Resettlement, in 2013–2014 "more than 68 refugees" per 100,000 North Dakotans were settled in the state.[^cite_note-85] In fiscal year 2014, 582 refugees settled in the state.[^cite_note-86] Fargo Mayor Mahoney said North Dakota accepting the most refugees per capita should be celebrated given the benefits they bring to the state.[^cite_note-87] In 2015, Lutheran Social Services of North Dakota, the state's only resettlement agency, was "awarded \$458,090 in federal funding to improve refugee services".[^cite_note-88] 29.8% of immigrants in North Dakota are from Africa leading to a rapid increase in the black proportion of the population in recent decades from 0.6% in 2000 to 3.9% in 2020.[^cite_note-89]

[Immigration](15051.md) from outside the United States resulted in a net increase of 3,323 people, and migration within the country produced a net loss of 21,110 people.[^cite_note-census_cum-90] Of the residents of North Dakota in 2009, 69.8% were born in North Dakota, 27.2% were born in a different state, 0.6% were born in Puerto Rico, U.S. Island areas, or born abroad to American parent(s), and 2.4% were born in another country.[^cite_note-\:3-54] The age and gender distributions approximate the national average. In 2019, 4.1% were foreign-born residents.[^cite_note-91] The [Philippines](23440.md), [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan), [Nepal](171166.md), [Canada](5042916.md) and [Liberia](17791.md) are the top countries of origin for North Dakota's immigrants.[^cite_note-92]

### Languages

In 2010, 94.86% (584,496) of North Dakotans over 5 years old spoke [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) as their [primary language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_language). 5.14% (31,684) of North Dakotans spoke a language other than English. 1.39% (8,593) spoke [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), 1.37% (8,432) spoke [Spanish](26825.md), and 0.30% (1,847) spoke [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_language). Other languages spoken included [Serbo-Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbo-Croatian) (0.19%), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language) and [Japanese](15606.md) (both 0.15%), and [Native American languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_languages) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) (both 0.13%).[^cite_note-94] In 2000, 2.5% of the population spoke German in addition to English, reflecting early 20th century immigration.[^cite_note-US_Census-95]

In 1940, 56.18% (355,400) of North Dakotans spoke English, 20.34% (128,700) spoke German, 12.85% (81,300) spoke Norwegian, 1.99% (12,600) spoke [Swedish](26689.md), and 8.64% (54,640) spoke some other language.[^cite_note-Census_1940_languages-96]

The [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) determined 77% of the adult population was [Christian](5211.md) in 2014.[^cite_note-\:0-98] In contrast with many southern U.S. states, [mainline Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainline_Protestant) was the largest form of Protestantism practiced (28%). The largest mainline Protestant denomination in North Dakota was the [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America](248255.md), and the [United Methodist Church](32304.md) was the second largest. Evangelical Protestants, forming the second largest Protestant branch (22%), were also dominated by Lutherans; the [Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Church%E2%80%93Missouri_Synod) was the largest Evangelical denomination. Among the Christian population of North Dakota, the Roman Catholic Church was the single largest Christian denomination. According to the [Public Religion Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Religion_Research_Institute) in 2020, 75% of the adult population were Christian, with mainline Protestantism remaining the majority and [evangelical Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Protestantism) at 18% of the population.[^cite_note-\:2-99] In 2022, the Public Religion Research Institute determined 80% of the population were Christian.

Per the Pew Research Center in 2014, non-Christian religions accounted for 3% of the adult population, with [Islam](6037917.md) being the largest non-Christian religion. Other faiths such as [Unitarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarian_Universalism) and [New Agers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Age) collectively made up 1% of the practicing population. At the 2014 survey, 20% were unaffiliated with any religion, and 2% of North Dakotans were atheist; 13% of the population practiced nothing in particular.[^cite_note-\:0-98] The 2020 Public Religion Research Institute's survey determined 22% were unaffiliated with any religion,[^cite_note-\:2-99] and 12% in 2022.

The largest church bodies by number of adherents in 2010 were the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) with 167,349; the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America with 163,209; and the Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod with 22,003.\[^cite_note-[www.thearda.com-100](http://www.thearda.com-100/)] In 2006, North Dakota had the most churches per capita of any state.[^cite_note-religious_survey-101] Additionally, North Dakota had the highest percentage of church-going population of any state in 2006.[^cite_note-religious_survey-101] By 2020, the Association of Religion Data Archives numbered 164,843, remaining the largest Christian body by attendance; it had an adherence rate of 211.58 per 1,000 people.[^cite_note-102]

A 2001 survey indicated 35% of North Dakota's population was [Lutheran](23371382.md), and 30% was Catholic. Other religious groups represented were [Methodists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodists) (7%), [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists) (6%), the [Assemblies of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assemblies_of_God) (3%), [Presbyterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian) (1.27%),[^cite_note-103] and [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) (1%). Christians with unstated or other denominational affiliations, including other [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainline_Protestant) and [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) (LDS Church), totaled 3%, bringing the total Christian population to 86%. There were an estimated 920 Muslims and 730 Jews in the state in 2000.[^cite_note-104] Three percent of respondents answered "no religion" on the survey, and 6% declined to answer.[^cite_note-religious_survey-101]

## Economy

See also: [List of North Dakota companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_North_Dakota_companies) and [North Dakota locations by per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_locations_by_per_capita_income)



[Sunflowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower) in [Traill County, North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traill_County,_North_Dakota)

Agriculture is North Dakota's largest industry, although petroleum, [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing), and technology are also major industries.[^cite_note-105] Its growth rate is about 4.1%.[^cite_note-106] According to the [U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Bureau_of_Economic_Analysis) the economy of North Dakota had a gross domestic product of \$81.883 billion in 2025.[^cite_note-107] North Dakota's per capita personal income in 2025 was \$75,157.[^cite_note-108] The three-year [median household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_household_income) from 2013 to 2017 was \$61,285.[^cite_note-per-capita-income-109] In 2025, 98.8% of businesses in North Dakota were [small businesses](276495.md), and employed 57.6% of the state's work force.[^cite_note-110]

According to [Gallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_\(company\)) data, North Dakota led the U.S. in job creation in 2013 and has done so since 2009. The state has a [Job Creation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_Creation_Index) score of 40, nearly 10 points ahead of its nearest competitors.[^cite_note-O'Neal-_Job_creation-111] North Dakota has added 56,600 private-sector jobs since 2011, creating an annual growth rate of 7.32 percent.[^cite_note-Thomas-_job_creation-112][^cite_note-Saad-Job_Creation-113] According to statistics released in December 2020, by the [Bureau of Economic Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Economic_Analysis), North Dakota had the highest rate of annual growth in personal consumption expenditures of all 50 states, from 2009 to 2018.[^cite_note-\:1-114] During this time period, annual nominal personal income growth averaged 6% per year, compared to the U.S. average of 4.4%. North Dakota's personal income growth is tied to various private business sectors such as agriculture, energy development, and construction.[^cite_note-Yahoo_news-personal_income_growth-115][^cite_note-Graeber-personal_income_growth-116] North Dakota also had the highest growth in personal expenditures on housing and utilities of all states, reflecting the sharply increased demand for housing in the 2010s.

Just over 21% of North Dakota's total 2013 gross domestic product (GDP) of \$49.77 billion comes from natural resources and mining.[^cite_note-117]

North Dakota is the only state with a [state-owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned) bank, the [Bank of North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_North_Dakota) in [Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismarck,_North_Dakota), and a state-owned [flour mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flour_mill), the [North Dakota Mill and Elevator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_Mill_and_Elevator) in [Grand Forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Forks). These were established by the NPL before World War II.

As of 2012, Fargo is home to the second-largest campus of [Microsoft](19001.md) with 1,700 employees, and [Amazon.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon.com) employs several hundred in Grand Forks.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119]

As of May 2025, the state's [unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_in_the_United_States) rate was 2.5%.[^cite_note-120] With the exception of a five-month period in 2020, the unemployment rate remained below five percent each month since 1987.[^cite_note-121] At end of 2010, the state per capita income was ranked 17th in the nation, the biggest increase of any state in a decade from rank 38th.[^cite_note-122] The reduction in the unemployment rate and growth in per capita income is attributable to the [oil boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_oil_boom) in the state.[^cite_note-\:1-114] Due to a combination of oil-related development and investing in technology and service industries, North Dakota has had a budget surplus every year since the 2008 market crash.[^cite_note-123]

Since 1976, the highest that North Dakota's unemployment rate has reached is just 6.2%, recorded in 1983. Every U.S. state except neighboring South Dakota has had a higher unemployment rate during that period.[^cite_note-124]

### Agriculture

North Dakota's earliest industries were [fur trading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trading) and agriculture. Although less than 10% of the population is employed in the agricultural sector,[^cite_note-125] it remains a major part of the state's economy. With industrial-scale farming, it ranks 9th in the nation in the value of crops and 18th in total value of agricultural products sold. Large farms generate the most crops. The share of people in the state employed in agriculture is comparatively high: as of 2008, only two to three percent of the population of the United States is directly employed in agriculture.[^cite_note-126] North Dakota has about 90% of its land area in farms with 27,500,000 acres (111,000 km<sup>2</sup>) of cropland, the third-largest amount in the nation. Between 2002 and 2007, total cropland increased by about a million acres (4,000 km<sup>2</sup>); North Dakota was the only state showing an increase. Over the same period, 1,800,000 acres (7,300 km<sup>2</sup>) were shifted into soybean and corn monoculture production, the largest such shift in the United States.[^cite_note-usda_facts-127] Agriculturalists are concerned about too much monoculture, as it makes the economy at risk from insect or crop diseases affecting a major crop. In addition, this development has adversely affected habitats of wildlife and birds, and the balance of the ecosystem.



North Dakota Mill and Elevator postcard, ca. 1922



North Dakota State Seed Department on North Dakota State University campus.

The state is the largest producer in the U.S. of many cereal grains, including [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley) (36% of U.S. crop), [durum wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durum_wheat) (58%), hard red spring wheat (48%), [oats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oat) (17%), and combined wheat of all types (15%). It is the second leading producer of [buckwheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckwheat) (20%). As of 2007, corn became the state's largest crop produced, although it is only 2% of total U.S. production.[^cite_note-usda_facts-127] The [Corn Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_Belt) extends to North Dakota but is more on the edge of the region instead of in its center. Corn yields are high in the southeast part of the state and smaller in other parts of the state. Most of the cereal grains are grown for livestock [feed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fodder). The state is the leading producer of many oilseeds, including 92% of the U.S. [canola](71016451.md) crop, 94% of [flax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flax) seed, 53% of [sunflower seeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower_seed), 18% of [safflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safflower) seeds, and 62% of [mustard seed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_seed). Canola is suited to the cold winters and it matures fast. Processing of canola for oil production produces canola meal as a by-product. The by-product is a high-protein animal feed.

[Soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybeans) are also an increasingly important crop, with 400,000 acres (1,600 km<sup>2</sup>) additional planted between 2002 and 2007.[^cite_note-usda_facts-127] Soybeans are a major crop in the eastern part of the state, and cultivation is common in the southeast part of the state. Soybeans were not grown at all in North Dakota in the 1940s, but the crop has become especially common since 1998.[^cite_note-128] In North Dakota soybeans have to mature fast, because of the comparatively short [growing season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growing_season). Soybeans are grown for livestock feed.

North Dakota is the second leading producer of [sugarbeets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarbeet), which are grown mostly in the [Red River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Valley). The state is also the largest producer of honey, dry edible peas and beans, [lentils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lentil), and the third-largest producer of potatoes.[^cite_note-usda_facts-127]

### Energy

See also: [List of power stations in North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_North_Dakota)



[Oil well](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_well) in western North Dakota

The [energy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_industry) is a major contributor to the economy. North Dakota has both coal and oil reserves. On average, the state's oil production grew at an average annual rate of 48.4% from 2009 to 2018. During these years, oil production increased each year from 2009 to 2015, with 2016 marked by a slight decline and a return to growth since.[^cite_note-\:1-114] As of [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_in_the_United_States), [Shale gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale_gas) was produced and [Lignite coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite_coal) reserves in Western North Dakota were used to generate about 90% of the electricity consumed, and electricity was also exported to nearby states.[^cite_note-130] In 2009, North Dakota had the second largest lignite coal production in the U.S.[^cite_note-131] [Lignite coal is the lowest grade coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite#As_fuel) (latent high moisture content and low energy density). There are larger and higher grade coal reserves ([anthracite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthracite), [bituminous coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bituminous_coal) and [subbituminous coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subbituminous_coal)) in other U.S. states.

Oil was discovered near [Tioga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tioga,_North_Dakota) in 1951, generating 53 million barrels (8,400,000 m<sup>3</sup>) of oil a year by 1984.[^cite_note-132] Recoverable oil reserves have jumped dramatically recently. The oil reserves of the [Bakken Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakken_Formation) may hold up to 400 billion barrels (6.4×10<sup>10</sup> m<sup>3</sup>) of oil, 25 times larger than the reserves in the [Arctic National Wildlife Refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_National_Wildlife_Refuge).[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134] A report issued in April 2008 by the [U.S. Geological Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Geological_Survey) estimated the oil recoverable by current technology in the Bakken formation is two orders of magnitude less, in the range of 3 billion barrels (480×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> m<sup>3</sup>) to 4.3 billion barrels (680×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> m<sup>3</sup>), with a mean of 3.65 billion barrels (580×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> m<sup>3</sup>).[^cite_note-135]

The northwestern part of the state has been the center of the [North Dakota oil boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_oil_boom). The [Williston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williston,_North_Dakota), [Tioga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tioga,_North_Dakota), [Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley,_North_Dakota) and [Minot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minot)-[Burlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlington,_North_Dakota) communities are having rapid growth that strains housing and local services. As of 30 November 2022, the state is the 2nd-largest oil producer in the U.S., with an average of 1,097,716 barrels (174,522.9 m<sup>3</sup>) per day while producing 3,029,032 thousand cubic feet (85,772,600 m<sup>3</sup>) per day of natural gas for a total of 1,619,963 barrels (257,553.5 m<sup>3</sup>) of oil equivalent ([BOE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrel_of_oil_equivalent)).[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138]

Main article: [Wind power in North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_North_Dakota)

The [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains) region, which includes the state of North Dakota, has been referred to as "the Saudi Arabia of [wind energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_energy)".[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

There, wind speeds seldom go below 10 miles per hour (16 km/h).

### Tourism

North Dakota is considered the least visited state, owing, in part, to its not having a major tourist attraction.[^cite_note-142] Nonetheless, tourism is North Dakota's third largest industry, contributing more than \$3 billion into the state's economy annually. Outdoor attractions such as the 144-mile (232 km) [Maah Daah Hey Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maah_Daah_Hey_Trail) and activities such as fishing and hunting attract visitors. The state is known for the Lewis & Clark Trail and being the winter camp of the Corps of Discovery.[^cite_note-143] Areas popular with visitors include [Theodore Roosevelt National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt_National_Park) in the western part of the state. In 2024, the park received 732,000 recreational visitors.[^cite_note-144]

Regular events in the state that attract tourists include *[Norsk Høstfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsk_H%C3%B8stfest)* in [Minot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minot), billed (as of 2006) as North America's largest [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia) festival;[^cite_note-145] the [Medora Musical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medora_Musical); and the [North Dakota State Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_State_Fair). The state also receives a significant number of visitors from the neighboring Canadian provinces of Manitoba and Saskatchewan, particularly when the exchange rate is favorable.[^cite_note-146]

International tourists have also come to visit the [Oscar-Zero Missile Alert Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar-Zero_Missile_Alert_Facility).[^cite_note-147]

### Native American First Nations



[Paul Kane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Kane) witnessed and participated in the annual [bison hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bison_hunt) of the [Métis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tis_people_\(United_States\)) in June 1846 on the prairies in Dakota.

In the 21st century, North Dakota has an increasing population of Native Americans, who as of 2025 made up 5.3% of the population.[^cite_note-158] By the early 19th century the territory was dominated by Siouan-speaking peoples, whose territory stretched west from the Great Lakes area. The word "Dakota" is a Sioux (Lakota/Dakota) word meaning "allies" or "friends".

The primary historic tribal nations in or around North Dakota, are the Lakota and the Dakota ("[The Great Sioux Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Sioux_Nation)" or "Oceti Sakowin", meaning the seven council fires), the [Blackfoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackfoot), the [Cheyenne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheyenne), the [Chippewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chippewa) (known as [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe) in Canada), and the [Mandan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandan). There are six [Indian reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_reservation) in North Dakota--[Spirit Lake Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_Lake_Tribe), [Standing Rock Sioux Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Rock_Sioux_Reservation), [Sisseton Wahpeton Oyate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisseton_Wahpeton_Oyate), [Fort Berthold Indian Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Berthold_Indian_Reservation), [Turtle Mountain Indian Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtle_Mountain_Indian_Reservation), and [The Mandan, Hidatsa, and Arikara Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandan,_Hidatsa,_and_Arikara_Nation).

### Pow wows

Social gatherings known as "[powwows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powwows)" (or wacipis in Lakota/Dakota) continue to be an important part of Native American culture and are held regularly throughout the state. Throughout Native American history, powwows were held, usually in the spring, to rejoice at the beginning of new life and the end of the winter cold. These events brought Native American tribes together for singing and dancing and allowed them to meet with old friends and acquaintances, as well as to make new ones. Many powwows also held religious significance for some tribes. Today, powwows are still a part of the Native American culture and are attended by Natives and non-Natives alike. In North Dakota, the United Tribes International Powwow held each September in the capital of [Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismarck,_North_Dakota), is one of the largest powwows in the United States.

A [pow wow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pow_wow) is an occasion for parades and Native American dancers in regalia, with many dancing styles presented. It is traditional for male dancers to wear regalia decorated with beads, quills, and eagle feathers; male [grass dancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass_dance) wear colorful fringe regalia, and male [fancy dancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fancy_dance) wear brightly colored feathers. Female dancers dance much more subtly than male dancers. Fancy female dancers wear cloth, beaded moccasins, and jewelry, while the [jingle dress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingle_dress) dancer wears a dress made of metal cones. Inter-tribal dances during the powwow, allow everyone (even spectators) to take part in the dancing.

### Norwegian and Icelandic influences



Norwegian settlers in front of their sod house in North Dakota in 1898

Around 1870 many European immigrants from Norway settled in North Dakota's northeastern corner, especially near the Red River. [Icelanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_American) also arrived from Canada.[^cite_note-159] [Pembina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembina,_North_Dakota) was a town of many [Norwegians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_American) when it was founded; they worked on family farms. They started Lutheran churches and schools, greatly outnumbering other denominations in the area. This group has unique foods such as *[lefse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefse)* and *[lutefisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutefisk)*. The continent's largest Scandinavian event, *[Norsk Høstfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norsk_H%C3%B8stfest)*, is celebrated each September in [Minot's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minot,_North_Dakota) [North Dakota State Fair Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_State_Fair_Center?action=edit\&redlink=1), a local attraction featuring art, architecture, and cultural artifacts from all five Nordic countries. The Icelandic State Park in [Pembina County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembina_County) and an annual Icelandic festival reflect immigrants from that country, who are also descended from Scandinavians.

Old World folk customs have persisted for decades in North Dakota, with the revival of techniques in weaving, silver crafting, and wood carving. Traditional turf-roof houses are displayed in parks; this style originated in Iceland. A [stave church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stave_church) is a landmark in Minot. [Norwegian-Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian-Americans) constitute nearly one-third or 32.3% of Minot's total population and 30.8% of North Dakota's total population.

### Germans from Russia

See also: [Germans from Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans_from_Russia)

Some months after she had become empress of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) in 1762, [Catherine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great) invited Ethnic Germans to migrate to Russia. Many came, settled for several generations and became [Russia Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_Germans). They grew dissatisfied in the nineteenth century because of economic problems and because of the revocation of religious freedoms for [Mennonites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Mennonite) and [Hutterites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutterite), in particular after in 1871, the exemption from military service in the [Imperial Russian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army) was revocated. Most Mennonites and Hutterites [migrated to America](15051.md) in the late [1870s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1870s_in_the_United_States). By 1900, about 100,000 had immigrated to the U.S., settling primarily in North Dakota, South Dakota, [Kansas](16716.md), and [Nebraska](21647.md). The south-central part of North Dakota became known as "the German-Russian triangle". By 1910, about 60,000 ethnic Germans from Russia lived in Central North Dakota. These individuals were Lutherans, Mennonites, Hutterites and Roman Catholics who had kept most of their German customs of the time when their ancestors immigrated to Russia. They were committed to agriculture. Traditional iron cemetery grave markers were a famous art form practiced by ethnic Germans.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161]

### Fine and performing arts

North Dakota's major [fine art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_art) museums and venues include the [Chester Fritz Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_Fritz_Auditorium), [Empire Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Arts_Center), the [Fargo Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo_Theatre), [North Dakota Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_Museum_of_Art), and the [Plains Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plains_Art_Museum). The [Bismarck-Mandan Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismarck-Mandan_Symphony_Orchestra), [Fargo-Moorhead Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo-Moorhead_Symphony_Orchestra), [Greater Grand Forks Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Grand_Forks_Symphony_Orchestra), [Minot Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minot_Symphony_Orchestra) and Great Plains Harmony Chorus are full-time professional and semi-professional musical ensembles who perform concerts and offer educational programs to their communities.

## Media

See also: [List of newspapers in North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_North_Dakota), [List of radio stations in North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_radio_stations_in_North_Dakota), and [List of television stations in North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_television_stations_in_North_Dakota)

The state has 10 daily newspapers, the largest being *[The Forum of Fargo-Moorhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forum_of_Fargo-Moorhead)*. Other weekly and monthly publications (most of which are fully supported by advertising) are also available. The most prominent of these is the [alternative weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_weekly) *[High Plains Reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Plains_Reader)*.

The state's oldest radio station, [WDAY-AM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WDAY-AM), was launched on May 23, 1922.[^cite_note-165] North Dakota's three major [radio markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_market) center around [Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo,_North_Dakota), [Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismarck,_North_Dakota), and [Grand Forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Forks), though stations broadcast in every region of the state. Several new stations were built in [Williston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williston,_North_Dakota) in the early 2010s. North Dakota has 34 AM and 88 FM radio stations.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168] [KFGO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KFGO_\(AM\)) in Fargo has the largest audience.[^cite_note-169]

[Broadcast television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrestrial_television) in North Dakota started on April 3, 1953, when KCJB-TV (now [KXMC-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KXMC-TV)) in Minot started operations.[^cite_note-170] North Dakota's television [media markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_market) are [Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo,_North_Dakota)-[Grand Forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Forks) (117th largest nationally), including the eastern half of the state, and [Minot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minot)-[Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismarck,_North_Dakota) (152nd), making up the western half of the state.[^cite_note-171] There are currently [31 full-power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_television_stations_in_North_Dakota) television stations, arranged into 10 networks, with 17 [digital subchannels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_subchannels).

[Public broadcasting](178808.md) in North Dakota is provided by Prairie Public, with statewide [television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Public_Television) and [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_Public_Radio) networks affiliated with [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS) and [NPR](29697232.md). [Public access television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_access_television) stations open to community programming are offered on cable systems in [Bismarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismarck,_North_Dakota), [Dickinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickinson,_North_Dakota), [Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo,_North_Dakota), and [Jamestown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown,_North_Dakota).

There were 142 schools in North Dakota cities and 4,722 [one room schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_room_schools) in the state in 1917. ## Emergency services

The North Dakota Department of Emergency Services[^cite_note-174] provides 24/7 communication and coordination for more than 50 agencies. In addition, "it administers federal disaster recovery programs and the Homeland Security Grant Program".[^cite_note-175] In 2011, the Department selected Geo-Comm, Inc.[^cite_note-176] "for the Statewide Seamless Base Map Project", which will facilitate "identifying locations 9–1–1 callers" and route emergency calls based on locations.[^cite_note-177] In 1993 the state adopted the [Burkle addressing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burkle_addressing_system) numbering rural roads and buildings to aid in the delivery of emergency services.[^cite_note-century-code-178]

## Transportation

See also: [List of North Dakota numbered highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_North_Dakota_numbered_highways), [List of North Dakota railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_North_Dakota_railroads), and [Aviation in North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_in_North_Dakota)



Interstate 94 in North Dakota, near Gladstone

Transportation in North Dakota is overseen by the [North Dakota Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_Department_of_Transportation). The major [Interstate highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_highways) are [Interstate 29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_29) and [Interstate 94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_94), with I-29 and I-94 meeting at [Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo,_North_Dakota), with I-29 oriented north to south along the eastern edge of the state, and I-94 bisecting the state from east to west between Minnesota and Montana. A unique feature of the North Dakota Interstate Highway system is virtually all of it is paved in concrete, not [blacktop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blacktop), because of the extreme weather conditions it must endure. [BNSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNSF) and the [Canadian Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Railway) operate the state's largest rail systems. Many branch lines formerly used by BNSF and Canadian Pacific Railway are now operated by the [Dakota, Missouri Valley and Western Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota,_Missouri_Valley_and_Western_Railroad) and the [Red River Valley and Western Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Valley_and_Western_Railroad).[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180]

North Dakota's principal airports are the [Hector International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hector_International_Airport) (FAR) in Fargo, [Grand Forks International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Forks_International_Airport) (GFK), [Bismarck Municipal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bismarck_Municipal_Airport) (BIS), [Minot International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minot_International_Airport) (MOT) and [Williston Basin International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williston_Basin_International_Airport) (XWA) in Williston.

[Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak)'s [Empire Builder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Builder) runs through North Dakota, making stops at [Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fargo_\(Amtrak_station\)) (2\:13 am westbound, 3\:35 am eastbound), [Grand Forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Forks_\(Amtrak_station\)) (4\:52 am westbound, 12\:57 am eastbound), [Minot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minot_\(Amtrak_station\)) (around 9 am westbound and around 9\:30 pm eastbound), and four other stations.[^cite_note-181] It is the descendant of the famous line of the same name run by the [Great Northern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_Railway_\(U.S.\)), which was built by the tycoon [James J. Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_J._Hill) and ran from [St. Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Paul,_Minnesota) to [Seattle](11388236.md).

Intercity bus service is provided by [Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Bus_Lines) and [Jefferson Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Lines). [Public transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transit) in North Dakota includes daily [fixed-route bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport_bus_service) systems in [Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MATBUS_\(Fargo-Moorhead\)), [Bismarck-Mandan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bis-Man_Transit), [Grand Forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_Area_Transit), and [Minot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minot_City_Transit), [paratransit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paratransit) service in 57 communities, along with multi-county rural transit systems.[^cite_note-182]

## Law and government

As with the federal government of the United States, political power in North Dakota state government is divided into three branches: executive, legislative, and judicial.[^cite_note-183]

The [Constitution of North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_North_Dakota) and the [North Dakota Century Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_Century_Code) form the formal law of the state; the *North Dakota Administrative Code* incorporates additional rules and policies of state agencies.[^cite_note-184]

In a 2020 study, North Dakota was ranked as the 8th easiest state for citizens to vote in.[^cite_note-185]

### Legislative

The [North Dakota Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_Legislative_Assembly) is a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) body consisting of the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_Senate) and the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_House_of_Representatives). The state has 47 districts, each with one senator and two representatives. Both senators and representatives are elected to four-year terms. The state's legal code is named the [North Dakota Century Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_Century_Code).

### Judicial

North Dakota's court system has four levels, one of which is dormant. Municipal courts serve the cities. Decisions from municipal courts are generally appealable to district court. Most cases start in the [district courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts_of_North_Dakota), which are courts of general jurisdiction. There are 42 district court judges in seven judicial districts.[^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190] Appeals from final district court decisions are made to the [North Dakota Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_Supreme_Court). An intermediate court of appeals was provided for by statute in 1987, but the North Dakota Court of Appeals has only heard 65 cases since its inception. The North Dakota Court of Appeals is essentially dormant, but capable of meeting if the North Dakota Supreme Court's case load necessitates the reestablishment of intermediate review.[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192]

### Indian tribes and reservations



Administrative building for the Standing Rock Sioux Reservation

Historically, North Dakota was populated by the [Mandan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandan), [Hidatsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidatsa), [Lakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakota_people), and [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe), and later by the [Sanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arikara_people) and [Métis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tis_people_\(United_States\)). Today, five federally recognized tribes within the boundaries of North Dakota have independent, sovereign relationships with the federal government and territorial reservations:

- [Mandan, Hidatsa, and Arikara Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandan,_Hidatsa,_and_Arikara_Nation), [Fort Berthold Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Berthold_Reservation);

- [Sisseton Wahpeton Oyate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisseton_Wahpeton_Oyate), [Lake Traverse Indian Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Traverse_Indian_Reservation);

- [Standing Rock Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Rock_Sioux), [Standing Rock Sioux Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Rock_Sioux_Reservation);

- [Spirit Lake Tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_Lake_Tribe), [Spirit Lake Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_Lake_Reservation); and

- [Turtle Mountain Band of Chippewa Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtle_Mountain_Band_of_Chippewa_Indians), [Turtle Mountain Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtle_Mountain_Reservation).

The major political parties in North Dakota are the [Democratic-NPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic-NPL) and the [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Dakota_Republican_Party). As of 2007, the [Constitution Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Party_\(United_States\)) and the [Libertarian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_Party_\(United_States\)) are also organized parties in the state.

At the state level, the [governorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_North_Dakota) has been held by the Republican Party since 1992, along with a majority of the state legislature and statewide officers. Dem-NPL showings were strong in the 2000 governor's race, and in the 2006 legislative elections, but the League has not had a major breakthrough since the administration of former state governor [George Sinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Sinner).

The Republican Party presidential candidate usually carries the state by a considerable margin; in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_North_Dakota), [Donald Trump](4848272.md) won nearly 67% of the vote. Of all the Democratic presidential candidates since 1892, only [Grover Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grover_Cleveland) ([1892](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1892_United_States_presidential_election), one of three votes), [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson) ([1912](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_United_States_presidential_election) and [1916](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1916_United_States_presidential_election)), [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt) ([1932](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_United_States_presidential_election) and [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_United_States_presidential_election)), and [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson) ([1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_United_States_presidential_election)) received [Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_\(United_States\)) votes from North Dakota.

On the other hand, Dem-NPL candidates for North Dakota's federal Senate and House seats won every election between 1982 and 2008, and the state's federal delegation was entirely Democratic from 1987 to 2011. However, both of the current U.S. senators, [John Hoeven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hoeven) and [Kevin Cramer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Cramer), are Republicans, as is the sole House member, [Julie Fedorchak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julie_Fedorchak).

A [six-week abortion ban is active](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbeat_bill) in North Dakota.

### State taxes

North Dakota has a slightly [progressive income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_income_tax) structure; the five brackets of state [income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax) rates are 1.1%, 2.04%, 2.27%, 2.64%, and 2.90% as of 2017.[^cite_note-194] In 2005 North Dakota ranked 22nd highest by per capita state taxes.[^cite_note-195] The [sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax) in North Dakota is 6% for most items.[^cite_note-sales_tax-196] The state allows municipalities to institute local sales taxes and special local taxes, such as the 1.75% supplemental sales tax in Grand Forks.[^cite_note-197] [Excise taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excise_taxes) are levied on the purchase price or market value of aircraft registered in North Dakota. The state imposes a [use tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_tax) on items purchased elsewhere but used within North Dakota. Owners of [real property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_property) in North Dakota pay [property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) to their county, municipality, school district, and special taxing districts.[^cite_note-198]

The [Tax Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_Foundation) ranks North Dakota as the state with the 20th most "business friendly" tax climate in the nation.[^cite_note-taxfound-199] [Tax Freedom Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_Freedom_Day) arrives on April 1, 10 days earlier than the national Tax Freedom Day.[^cite_note-taxfound-199] In 2006, North Dakota was the state with the lowest number of returns filed by taxpayers with an [adjusted gross income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjusted_gross_income) of over \$1M—only 333.[^cite_note-200]
