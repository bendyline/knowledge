**Talkalakh** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): تَلْكَلَخ, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Talkalaḵ*) is a city in western [Syria](7515849.md) administratively belonging to the [Homs Governorate](7445154.md) as the capital of the [Talkalakh District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talkalakh_District) just north of the border with [Lebanon](17771.md) and west of [Homs](77533.md). According to the [Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau_of_Statistics_\(Syria\)) (CBS) Talkalakh had a population of 18,412 in 2004.[^cite_note-CBS-1] Its inhabitants are predominantly [Sunni Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim), while the surrounding villages are mostly inhabited by [Alawites](436616.md). Most of the city's [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim) residents have fled as a result of the ongoing [Syrian civil war](30741795.md).[^cite_note-2]

People in the city depend on trade and public services as the main source of income. It has six [mosques](19894.md) and two main small squares: Al Hurria and Al Saha Al Amma Squares.

## History

During the [Ottoman](22278.md) era, between the 18th and 19th centuries, Talkalakh was home to the wealthy and influential Danadisha (also spelled Dandashi) clan.[^cite_note-3] They stiffly competed with Hammadi family and the al-Jundi family of [Homs](77533.md) and [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama).[^cite_note-4]

The [Arab horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_horse) [O'Bajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Bajan) was born in this town in 1880.

### Syrian civil war

On May 15, 2011, as a part of the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md), the [Syrian Army](3423927.md) [shelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Talkalakh_\(May_2011\)) Talkalakh in response to attacks on the army that occurred a day before in the city. Seven civilians were killed and at least 2,000 residents tried to flee from the city into Lebanon.[^cite_note-5]

A resident claimed that [Shabiha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabiha) militias were targeting the [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) inhabitants, assuring that "The city of Talkalakh is empty of people. Most of them have fled to Lebanon,".[^cite_note-6] The remainder of its Sunni residents continued protesting against the government.[^cite_note-7]

On 12 February 2013, a CNN report from inside Talkalakh revealed that the town itself was under rebel control, though government forces were only a matter of yards away, surrounding the town. Nevertheless, there was no fighting in or around the town thanks to a tenuous ceasefire between the warring sides brokered by a local sheikh and an Alawite member of parliament.[^cite_note-8] The ceasefire ended in June 2013, when government forces drove out the rebels and took control of the town.[^cite_note-9]

Since the end of the Syrian Civil War an estimated 12,396 people have returned to Talkalakh from abroad.[^cite_note-10]
