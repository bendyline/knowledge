**Mahdia Governorate** ([Tunisian Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_Arabic_language): ولاية المهدية; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Gouvernorat de Mahdia*) is in central-eastern Tunisia, named after [its largest town](1086128.md) and administrative centre. It comprises an area of coastal relative lowland, but extends further inland than its coastal length. It is one of the twenty-four [governorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorates_of_Tunisia) (provinces). It covers an area of 2,966 km², and has a population of 410,812 (as at the 2014 census).[^cite_note-2] Four other governorates are its neighbours - clockwise from south, [Sfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sfax_Governorate), [Kairouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kairouan_Governorate), [Sousse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sousse_Governorate) and [Monastir Governorates](4974375.md). The governorate of Mahdia was created by the decree of March 9, 1974 after having been part of the governorate of Sousse.

## Economic summary



Swordfish on a roundabout in Mahdia. The tourist industry is reflected in the area by luxury accommodation, restaurants and impressive works of art.

[Mahdia](1086128.md), the administrative centre, is a coastal resort with prominent weaving and fish processing industries.[^cite_note-Lexic-3]

The other coastal urban centre is the small town of [Chebba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chebba), on the headland of a bay. [Ksour Essef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ksour_Essef) is midway between these towns and approximately 2.5 km inland. [El Djem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Djem) is 13 km inland and a mid-sized town on a major crossroads of roads in the district and having Tunisia's main north-south railway. The nearest international airport is connected by road and rail and is 15 km north of Mahdia (the town), [Monastir Habib Bourguiba International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastir_Habib_Bourguiba_International_Airport).

### Synopsis of history



Ship's anchor of the [Mahdia shipwreck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdia_shipwreck), many of the artefacts of which are in the [Bardo National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardo_National_Museum_\(Tunis\)) in [Tunis](57659.md).

Main articles: [Mahdia](1086128.md), [Chebba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chebba), [El Djem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Djem), and [Ksour Essef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ksour_Essef)

The two main coastal cities are of historic note to Greek, Roman, and early Muslim societies. They include the [Mahdia shipwreck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdia_shipwreck) – a sunken ship found off Mahdia's shore, containing Greek art treasures – dated to about 80 BC, the early part of Roman rule in this region. The old part of Mahdia corresponds to the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) city called **Aphrodisium** and, later, called **Africa** (a name perhaps derived from the older name),[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] or **Cape Africa**.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-Tissot-9]

## Climate

Main article: [Tunisia § Climate](30188.md#Climate)

The climate is [semiarid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiarid) throughout particularly in the summer and due to the high [albedo effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albedo_effects) compared to provinces of the far north, nighttime temperatures tend to fall more, to a similar temperature to average upland parts of the north. Much of the developed area benefits from the Sousse area's advanced water distribution network from dammed rivers and lakes including those hundreds of miles to the west which provides water for domestic use and pools.
