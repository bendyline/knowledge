The **Qatar Armed Forces** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): القوات المسلحة القطرية, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Al-Quwwat Al-Musallahah Al-Qatariyyah*) are the military forces of the [State of Qatar](8238258.md). Since 2015, Qatar has implemented mandatory military conscription with an average of 2,000 graduates per year.[^cite_note-4] As of 2010, Qatar's defence expenditures added up to a total of \$1.913 billion, about 1.5% of the [national GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_national_product), according to the [SIPRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_International_Peace_Research_Institute).[^cite_note-milexdata.sipri.org-5] Qatar has recently signed defence pacts with the [United States](3434750.md) in 2002[^cite_note-6] and 2013,[^cite_note-7] with the [United Kingdom](31717.md) in 2020,[^cite_note-8] and with [France](5843419.md) in 1994.

Qatar plays an active role in the collective defense efforts of the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md); the other five members are [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), [Kuwait](7515890.md), [Bahrain](18933277.md), the [UAE](69328.md), and [Oman](22316.md). Qatar also hosts the largest American [military base](3830783.md) in the Middle East and in 2017 inaugurated a military attache office in Washington.[^cite_note-9]

[SIPRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIPRI) states that Qatar's plans to transform and significantly enlarge its armed forces have accelerated in 2014, and in 2010-14 Qatar was the 46th largest arms importer in the world. Orders in 2013 for 562 tanks and 75 self-propelled guns from Germany were followed in 2014 by a number of other contracts, including 34 combat helicopters and 3 [AEW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_early_warning) aircraft from the US, and 6 tanker aircraft from Spain.[^cite_note-10] As of 2016, Qatar maintains advanced anti-air and anti-ship capabilities with deliveries of [Patriot PAC-3 MSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_PAC-3) batteries,[^cite_note-11] [Exocet MM40 Block 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exocet_MM40) and [Marte ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otomat) anti-ship missiles.[^cite_note-12]

Reports indicate that between 2014 and 2024, Qatar significantly enhanced its military capabilities, with defense spending rising by 434%. These accounts also suggest that a substantial portion of the arms trade during this period was marred by corruption, including instances of bribery.[^cite_note-13]

On 8 November 2024, for the first time a [NATO](21133.md) military committee visited Qatar's military education and training facilities.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

## History

The armed forces were founded in 1971 after the country gained independence from the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-16]

With a personnel strength of 16,500, Qatar's armed forces are the second-smallest in the Middle East.[^cite_note-17] France has provided approximately 80% of Qatar's arms inventory. Qatar took part in the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) of 1991, with a battalion at the [Battle of Khafji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khafji).[^cite_note-18] It also hosted the U.S. [614th Tactical Fighter Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/614th_Tactical_Fighter_Squadron)[^cite_note-19] and the Canadian Air Task Group with 26 CF-18s both at Doha.

Since the Gulf War, Qatar has pursued a limited program of force modernization. Qatar has spent \$12 billion to buy [MIM-104 Patriot](52024.md) [surface to air missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_to_air_missile). In July 2008, the US [Defense Security Cooperation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Security_Cooperation_Agency) announced Qatar's official request for logistics support, training, and associated equipment and services. The total value of the support arrangements could be as high as \$400 million.

In March 2011, Qatar announced the participation of its Air Force in the enforcement of the [Libyan no-fly zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_no-fly_zone).[^cite_note-20]

### Saudi Arabian-led intervention in Yemen

See also: [Saudi Arabian-led intervention in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen)

According to [Aljazeera](30204052.md) news, in December 2016, Qatar deployed 1,000 ground troops in Yemen to fight on behalf of the ousted president [Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdrabbuh_Mansur_Hadi). Qatari soldiers, backed by 200 armoured vehicles and 30 Apache helicopters, headed to Yemen's Marib province.[^cite_note-21]

The Armed Forces of Qatar have suffered 4 killed and 2 wounded during their deployment in Yemen.[^cite_note-22]

### Humanitarian Aid

**Gaza**

Following the start of Israeli military operations in the enclave after the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md), a significant number of Palestinians in Gaza, estimated at 2.2 million, are now confronted with the imminent risk of famine. The Qatari Armed Forces have played a significant role in offering assistance to the Palestinians in Gaza. Thus far, a grand total of 91 aircraft have been dispatched, each laden with numerous tons of much-needed assistance. In a significant development, on April 8, 2024, they accomplished the successful evacuation of the 22nd group of injured Palestinians, marking a significant milestone since the commencement of the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

**Afghanistan**

On May 16, 2024, a Qatar Armed Forces aircraft delivered 42 tons of food and shelter from the [Qatar Red Crescent Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Red_Crescent_Society) and [Qatar Charity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Charity) to Mazar-i-Sharif, Afghanistan.Qatar Charity (QC) also sent three aircraft delivering sixty tons of relief goods to Balkh flood victims via the Qatar Emiri Air Force airlift. Additionally, Qatar Red Crescent Society (QRCS) will build 10 health centers in Afghanistan to serve hundreds of thousands of women and children.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

**Sudan**

On August 14, 2024, two Qatari Armed Forces aircraft arrived at Port Sudan Airport, delivering 27 tons of relief aid and shelter supplies to support flood victims in Sudan. The aid was provided by the [Qatar Fund for Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Fund_for_Development) (QFFD), [Qatar Charity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Charity), and the [Qatar Red Crescent Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Red_Crescent_Society). This is part of Qatar's ongoing humanitarian air bridge to Sudan, initiated in May 2023 in response to the country's ongoing conflict and natural disasters.[^cite_note-27]

### 2026 Iran war

On 2 March 2026, Qatari downed two Iranian [Su-24 bombers](382335.md) during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-28] One Iranian fighter jet pilot was killed in the strike. Three Iranian fighter jet pilots were captured by the armed forces.[^cite_note-29]

## Military branches

### Qatari Emiri Land Force



Qatar Armed Forces in training.

The Qatar Emiri Land Force is the largest [land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_warfare) [service branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch) of the Qatar Armed Forces. Initially outfitted with British weaponry, Qatar shifted much of its procurement to France during the 1980s in response to French efforts to develop closer relations. The tank battalion was equipped with French-built [AMX-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMX-30) main battle tanks, before later being replaced by German [Leopard 2A7's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard_2).[^cite_note-Janes-30] Other armored vehicles include French [AMX-10P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMX-10P) APCs and the French [VAB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A9hicule_de_l'Avant_Blind%C3%A9), adopted as the standard wheeled combat vehicle. The artillery unit has a few French 155 mm self-propelled howitzers. The principal antitank weapons are French [MILAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MILAN) and [HOT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HOT_\(missile\)) wire-guided missiles.

Qatar had also illicitly acquired a few [Stinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIM-92_Stinger) shoulder-fired SAMs, possibly from Afghan rebel groups, at a time when the United States was trying to maintain tight controls on Stingers in the Middle East. When Qatar refused to turn over the missiles, the United States Senate in 1988 imposed a ban on the sale of all weapons to Qatar. The ban was repealed in late 1990 when Qatar satisfactorily accounted for its disposition of the Stingers.



Qatar Armed Forces in [National Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_National_Day) celebrations on the [Doha Corniche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Corniche).

Qatari tank battalion fought in the Gulf war in 1991, their AMX-30s took part in the [battle of Khafji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khafji). Qatari contingent, composed mostly of Pakistani recruits, acquitted itself well during the war.[^cite_note-31]

Qatar signed a contract with the German defence company Krauss-Maffei Wegmann (KMW) for the delivery of 24 artillery systems [PzH 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PzH_2000) and 62 LEOPARD 2 main battle tanks.[^cite_note-32]

The US DSCA announces that Qatar wants to join its neighbor the UAE, and field 2 medium-range [THAAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/THAAD) batteries of its own.

Their request is worth up to \$6.5 billion, and includes up to 12 THAAD Launchers, 150 THAAD missiles, 2 THAAD Fire Control and Communications units, 2 AN/TPY-2 THAAD Radars, and 1 Early Warning Radar (EWR). The USA would also sell them the required trucks, generators, electrical power units, trailers, communications equipment, fire unit test & maintenance equipment, system integration and checkout, repair and return, training, and other support.[^cite_note-33]

### Qatari Emiri Navy

Main article: [Qatari Emiri Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatari_Emiri_Navy)

The Qatari Emiri Navy (QEN), also called the Qatari Emiri Naval Forces (QENF), is the naval branch of the armed forces of the State of Qatar.

### Qatar Emiri Air Force

Main article: [Qatar Emiri Air Force](4014890.md)

The [Qatar Emiri Air Force](4014890.md) was formed in 1974, three years after achieving independence from Great Britain in 1971. Initially equipped with ex-RAF [Hawker Hunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Hunters), the air force soon began expansion with six Dassault/Dornier [Alpha Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault-Breguet/Dornier_Alpha_Jet) in 1979. Fourteen [Dassault Mirage F1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_F1) were delivered between 1980 and 1984. After the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), Qatar's air force infrastructure was upgraded by France for \$200 million, leading to the order of nine single seat [Mirage 2000-5DEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000) multi-role combat aircraft and three two seat [Mirage 2000-5DDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000) combat trainers in August 1994. Deliveries started in December 1997, and involved the buy back of the remaining 11 Mirage F1s by France that were later sold on to Spain.[^cite_note-35] The current commander of the Qatar Emiri Air Force is Brigadier General Mubarak Mohammed Al Kumait Al Khayarin.

British pilots in Oman remain on duty with the air force, and French specialists are employed in a maintenance capacity. Nevertheless, an increasing number of young Qataris have been trained as pilots and technicians. The past decade saw Qatar increase its air force with 96 planes from three different countries: the [F-15](11715.md) from the US, France's [Rafale](33731380.md), and the [Eurofighter Typhoon](167667.md) from the UK.[^cite_note-36]

Its units include:

- No. 1 Fighter Wing&#x20;

  - No. 7 Air Superiority Squadron – [Dassault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault) [Mirage 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_2000)&#x20;

    - 9 single-seat Mirage 2000-5EDA

    - 3 2000-5DDA trainers

  - No. 11 Close Support Squadron – [Dassault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault)/[Dornier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornier_Flugzeugwerke) [Alpha Jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Jet)

- No. 2 Rotary Wing&#x20;

  - No. 6 Close Support Squadron – [Eurocopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocopter) [SA342](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SA342)

  - No. 8 Anti-Surface Vessel Squadron – [Westland Sea King Commando Mk 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Sea_King)

  - No. 9 Multi-Role Squadron – [Westland Sea King Commando Mk 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Sea_King)

- [Qatar Amiri Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Amiri_Flight) – [C-17 Globemaster III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-17_Globemaster_III)
