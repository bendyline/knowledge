**West Yorkshire** is a [metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_counties_of_England) and [ceremonial county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England) in the [Yorkshire and the Humber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_and_the_Humber) region of England. It borders [North Yorkshire](143759.md) to the north and east, [South Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Yorkshire) and [Derbyshire](71070.md) to the south, [Greater Manchester](79819.md) to the south-west, and [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire) to the west. The city of [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds) is the largest settlement.

The county has an area of 2,029 km<sup>2</sup> (783 sq mi) and had an estimated population of 2,435,236 in 2024. The centre of the county is urbanised. Leeds is in the north-east, the city of [Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefield) in the south-east, [Huddersfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield) in the south-west, and the city of [Bradford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford) in the north-west. The outer areas of the county are rural. For local government purposes the county comprises five [metropolitan boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_borough): [Bradford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Bradford), [Calderdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calderdale), [Kirklees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirklees), [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Leeds), and [Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Wakefield), which collaborate through [West Yorkshire Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire_Combined_Authority). The county was created in 1974, prior to which the area was within the [West Riding of Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Riding_of_Yorkshire).

The western part of West Yorkshire is in the [South Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pennines), and contains a small part of the [Peak District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_District) National Park. It is characterised by steep valleys and is the source of the [River Calder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Calder,_West_Yorkshire), which flows past Wakefield before meeting the [Aire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Aire), which flows through Leeds, near [Castleford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castleford). The landscape becomes flatter in the east, and the eastern boundary of the county is on the edge of the [Vale of York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_of_York).

Remnants of strong coal, wool and iron ore industries remain in the county, having attracted people over the centuries, and this can be seen in the buildings and architecture. Several railways and the [M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_motorway), [M621](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M621_motorway), [M606](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M606_motorway), [A1(M)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1\(M\)_motorway) and [M62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M62_motorway) motorways traverse the county.

### Local

For local government purposes West Yorkshire is divided into five [metropolitan boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_borough): [Bradford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Bradford), [Calderdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calderdale), [Kirklees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirklees), [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Leeds), and [Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Wakefield). Their councils are the principal bodies responsible for the provision of local government services in the county.[^cite_note-\:2-4][^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-\:3-6][^cite_note-\:4-7]

The councils collaborate through [West Yorkshire Joint Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire_Joint_Services) and the [West Yorkshire Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire_Combined_Authority) (see below).[^cite_note-8]

#### History

The [Local Government Act 1888](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1888) reformed English local government by creating [administrative counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_counties_of_England), [county boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_borough), and elected county councils and county borough councils to govern them. County boroughs largely consisted of those settlements with a population over 50,000, and administrative counties covered smaller towns and rural areas.[^cite_note-\:04-9] The three ridings of Yorkshire were reconstituted as the [administrative counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_counties_of_England) of [County of York, East Riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_York,_East_Riding), [County of York, North Riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_York,_North_Riding), and [County of York, West Riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_York,_West_Riding). Within what would later become West Yorkshire, [Bradford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford), [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_West_Yorkshire), [Huddersfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield), and [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds) were made county boroughs.[^cite_note-\:04-9]

The [Local Government Act 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972), which came into effect in 1974, reformed English local government again. It introduced [metropolitan counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_county), which covered the largest urban areas, and [non-metropolitan counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_county) elsewhere.[^cite_note-\:2-4][^cite_note-\:6-10] The metropolitan county of West Yorkshire was created from the central part of the West Riding and divided into five metropolitan boroughs.[^cite_note-\:04-9][^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-\:3-6] The provision of local government services was divided between [West Yorkshire County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire_County_Council) and the district councils of the five boroughs.[^cite_note-\:1-5] In [Local Government Act 1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1985), which came into effect the following year, abolished the county council and transferred many of its powers and responsibilities the borough councils. It also created or allowed to be created joint bodies, notably the [West Yorkshire Passenger Transport Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire_Passenger_Transport_Authority), in which all the borough councils participated.[^cite_note-\:4-7]

### Regional

The [West Yorkshire Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire_Combined_Authority) is a strategic authority established in 2014. It allows the five metropolitan borough councils to collaborate.[^cite_note-\:5-11] The authority was restructured in 2021 to be led by a [directly elected mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_authority_mayor), the [Mayor of West Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_West_Yorkshire), and at the same time gained additional powers over areas including education, housing, and planning.[^cite_note-12] The current mayor is [Tracy Brabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy_Brabin), who was [first elected in 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_West_Yorkshire_mayoral_election) and successfully [defended her seat in 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_West_Yorkshire_mayoral_election).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

As part of the establishment of the combined authority, the West Yorkshire Passenger Transport Executive was abolished and its functions transferred to the new authority.[^cite_note-\:5-11]

## Geography



Geology of Yorkshire



[Ilkley Moor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkley_Moor)

The county borders, going anticlockwise from the west: [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire), [Greater Manchester](79819.md), [Derbyshire](71070.md), [South Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Yorkshire) and [North Yorkshire](143759.md). The terrain of the county mostly consists of the [Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennines) and its foothills which dominate the west of the county and gradually descend into the [Vale of York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_of_York) and [Humberhead Levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humberhead_Levels) in the east. Geologically, it lies almost entirely on rocks of carboniferous age which form the inner Southern Pennine fringes in the west[^cite_note-16] and the Yorkshire coalfield further eastwards.[^cite_note-17] In the extreme east of the metropolitan county there are younger deposits of [Magnesian Limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesian_Limestone).[^cite_note-18] Areas in the west such as Bradford and Calderdale are dominated by the scenery of the eastern slopes of the [South Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pennines), dropping from upland in the west down to the east, and dissected by many steep-sided valleys while a small part of the northern [Peak District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_District) extends into the south west of Kirklees. Large-scale industry, housing, public and commercial buildings of differing heights, transport routes and open countryside conjoin. The dense network of roads, canals and railways and urban development, confined by valleys creates dramatic interplay of views between settlements and the surrounding hillsides, as shaped the first urban-rural juxtapositions of [David Hockney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hockney). Where most rural the land crops up in the such rhymes and folklore as [On Ilkla Moor Baht 'at](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_Ilkla_Moor_Baht_'at), date unknown, the early 19th century novels and poems of the [Brontë family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bront%C3%AB_family) often in and around [Haworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haworth) and long-running light comedy-drama *[Last of the Summer Wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_of_the_Summer_Wine)* in the 20th century.

The carboniferous rocks of the Yorkshire coalfield further east have produced a rolling landscape with hills, escarpments and broad valleys in the outer fringes of the Pennines. In this landscape there is widespread evidence of both current and former industrial activity. There are numerous derelict or converted mine buildings and recently landscaped former spoil heaps. The scenery is a mixture of built up areas, industrial land with some dereliction, and farmed open country. Ribbon developments along transport routes including canal, road and rail are prominent features of the area although some remnants of the pre industrial landscape and semi-natural vegetation still survive. However, many areas are affected by urban fringe pressures creating fragmented and downgraded landscapes and ever present are urban influences from major cities, smaller industrial towns and former mining villages. In the [Magnesian Limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesian_Limestone) belt to the east of the Leeds and Wakefield areas is an elevated ridge with smoothly rolling scenery, dissected by dry valleys. Here, there is a large number of country houses and estates with parkland, estate woodlands, plantations and game coverts. The rivers [Aire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Aire) and [Calder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Calder,_West_Yorkshire) drain the area, flowing from west to east.

## History

See also: [History of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Leeds) and [History of Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Yorkshire)

Wakefield's Parish Church was raised to cathedral status in 1888 and after the elevation of Wakefield to diocese, Wakefield Council immediately sought city status and this was granted in July 1888.[^cite_note-19] However the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution), which changed West and South Yorkshire significantly, led to the growth of Leeds and Bradford, which became the area's two largest cities (Leeds being the largest in Yorkshire). Leeds was granted city status in 1893 and Bradford in 1897. The name of [Leeds Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_Town_Hall) reflects the fact that at its opening in 1858 Leeds was not yet a city, while Bradford renamed its Town Hall as City Hall in 1965.[^cite_note-20]

West Yorkshire was formed as a metropolitan county in 1974, by the [Local Government Act 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972), and corresponds roughly to the core of the [historic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_counties_of_England) [West Riding of Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Riding_of_Yorkshire) and the county boroughs of Bradford, Dewsbury, Halifax, Huddersfield, Leeds, and Wakefield.

West Yorkshire Metropolitan County Council inherited the use of [West Riding County Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Hall,_Wakefield) at [Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefield), opened in 1898, from the West Riding County Council in 1974. Since 1987 it has been the headquarters of Wakefield City Council.[^cite_note-22]

The county initially had a two-tier structure of local government with a strategic-level county council and five districts providing most services.[^cite_note-23] In 1986, throughout England the metropolitan county councils were abolished. The functions of the county council were devolved to the boroughs; joint-boards covering fire, police and public transport; and to other special joint arrangements.[^cite_note-24] Organisations such as the [West Yorkshire Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire_Police) (governed by the [West Yorkshire Police and Crime Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire_Police_and_Crime_Commissioner)) continue to operate on this basis.

Although the county council was abolished, West Yorkshire continues to form a [metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_and_non-metropolitan_counties_of_England) and [ceremonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England) county with a [Lord Lieutenant of West Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Lieutenant_of_West_Yorkshire) and a [High Sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Sheriff_of_West_Yorkshire).

### Green belt

Further information: [South and West Yorkshire Green Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_and_West_Yorkshire_Green_Belt)

West Yorkshire contains [green belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_belt_\(United_Kingdom\)) interspersed throughout the county, surrounding the West Yorkshire Urban Area. It was first drawn up in the 1950s. All the county's districts contain large portions of green belt.

### Parish

West Yorkshire has close ties with Lancashire in terms of history, local identity and infrastructure including with the [War of the Roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Roses) and [Lancashire and Yorkshire Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire_and_Yorkshire_Railway).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Up until the 19th century, the town of [Todmorden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todmorden) was in Lancashire but was moved into Yorkshire.[^cite_note-27] In the 1974 boundary review, the towns of [Earby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earby) and [Barnoldswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnoldswick) were moved into the [Pendle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Pendle) district of Lancashire.[^cite_note-28] The civil parish of [Saddleworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddleworth) in [Oldham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Oldham) was the only part of West Riding of Yorkshire to be moved into the county of Greater Manchester. The villages in the parish border the towns of [Huddersfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield) and [Holmfirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmfirth). There is a strong identity debate with Saddleworth residents who still maintain close connections with Yorkshire including the Saddleworth White Rose Society.[^cite_note-29]

West Yorkshire is ethnically diverse, hosting large populations of multiple ethnic minority groups. Most notably, the city of [Bradford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford) is well known for its large concentration of [British Pakistanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Pakistanis), the highest by percentage in the country. [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds) and [Kirklees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirklees) also have large British Pakistani populations. Kirklees also hosts a large population of [British Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indians). West Yorkshire is home to a large Eastern European population, particularly [British Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poles_in_the_United_Kingdom). Ethnic minorities totalled to over 21% of West Yorkshire's population in 2011.[^cite_note-36]

### Industries

Leeds has since attracted investment from financial institutions, to become a recognised financial centre, with many [banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank), [building societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_societies) and [insurance companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance_companies) having offices in the city. Wakefield has also attracted many service-based industries, in particular [call centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_centres). Two of the big four supermarkets are from West Yorkshire. [Morrisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morrisons) is based in Bradford, while [Asda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asda) is based in Leeds.

West Yorkshire grew up around several industries. Wakefield, Castleford, Pontefract and South and East Leeds were traditional [coal mining](208413.md) areas.

Wool

[Bradford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford), [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_West_Yorkshire) and [Huddersfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield) grew through the development of [woollen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolen) mills. Leeds' traditional industry was the manufacturing of cloth while heavier engineering industries facilitated growth in South Leeds.

The [Heavy Woollen District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_Woollen_District) covered towns such as [Dewsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewsbury), [Batley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batley), [Morley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morley,_West_Yorkshire), [Ossett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossett,_West_Yorkshire), [Cleckheaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleckheaton) and [Heckmondwike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckmondwike). The woollen and cloth industries declined throughout the twentieth century.

Rhubarb

The [Rhubarb Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhubarb_Triangle) is wholly in West Yorkshire and still produces the vegetable in considerable quantities.[^cite_note-37] Twelve farmers who farm within the Rhubarb Triangle applied to have the name "Yorkshire forced rhubarb" added to the list of foods and drinks that have their names legally protected by the European Commission's Protected Food Name scheme.[^cite_note-Application-38] The application was successful and the farmers in the Rhubarb Triangle were awarded Protected Designation of Origin status (PDO) in February 2010. Food protected status accesses European funding to promote the product and legal backing against other products made outside the area using the name. Other protected names include [Stilton cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stilton_cheese), [Champagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champagne_\(wine\)) and Parma Ham.

Coal

The last pit in West Yorkshire to close was Hay Royds Colliery at [Denby Dale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denby_Dale) in 2012 after a flood.[^cite_note-39]

### Film and television productions

Several films and television series have been filmed in West Yorkshire's historic areas, particularly around the town of [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_West_Yorkshire).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] For example, portions of the BBC television series *[Happy Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Valley_\(TV_series\))* were filmed in Huddersfield; in addition to exteriors, some of the studio filming was done at North Light Film Studios at Brookes Mill, Huddersfield. As well, interiors for the BBC's *[Jamaica Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_Inn_\(2014_TV_series\))*, for the BBC's *[Remember Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remember_Me_\(TV_series\))* and for ITV series *[Black Work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Work)*, were also filmed at the studios.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] More recently, many of the exteriors of the BBC series *[Jericho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jericho_\(2016_TV_series\))* were filmed at the nearby Rockingstone Quarry and some interior work was done at North Light Film Studios.[^cite_note-46]

### Tourism



[Titus Salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titus_Salt)'s mill in [Saltaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltaire), [Shipley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipley,_West_Yorkshire), is an [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md).

See also: [§ Places of interest](#Places_of_interest)

Urban tourism include national and internationally significant sports stadiums, museums, theatre and galleries. The Royal Armouries is in Leeds, as is the [Leeds Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_Playhouse) (formerly the West Yorkshire Playhouse), [Opera North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_North) and The Grand Theatre. The [First Direct Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Direct_Arena) in Leeds seats around 15,000 people. [Sheffield Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_Arena) is also popular, as are the [Bradford Alhambra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford_Alhambra), St Georges Hall and the [National Science and Media Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Science_and_Media_Museum) in Bradford.&#x20;

Leeds is the most popular shopping destination in West Yorkshire, and the wider region, with several notable retail destinations. Briggate and The Headrow are the most important shopping streets, while [Trinity Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Leeds), [Kirkgate Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkgate_Market) (the biggest indoor market in Europe) and the [White Rose Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Rose_Centre) are regionally dominant shopping destinations. [Victoria Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Leeds) is nationally known for hosting many 'first outside of London' labels such as [Harvey Nichols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Nichols) and [Anthropologie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropologie).&#x20;

Leeds is also a popular weekend destination for nightlife, with several famous bars and clubs across a variety of different districts. The city's gay village is the largest in Yorkshire.

A wide range of towns and cities are connected by rail to [Leeds railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_railway_station), which is the busiest station in northern England and the main national gateway to West Yorkshire. The [M62 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M62_motorway) is the main east-west motorway in the county, connecting the largest towns and cities of West Yorkshire, while the M1 forms a key north-south axis from Leeds to Wakefield and beyond towards London.

Outside of the main cities, signposted walks follow rivers and the escarpment of the Pennines, which is scaled in meandering stages and tunnels by the recreational [Leeds-Liverpool Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds-Liverpool_Canal) and [Rochdale Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochdale_Canal), navigable by barge, canoe or kayak. The [Yorkshire Sculpture Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Sculpture_Park) and [The Hepworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hepworth) in Wakefield are major national art attractions, while [Haworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haworth) is visited for being the home of the [Bronte Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronte_Sisters). [Ilkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkley), [Otley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otley), [Hebden Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebden_Bridge) and [Holmfirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holmfirth) are popular smaller towns featuring farmer's markets, breweries, tea rooms, stately homes and are popular with hikers.

## Transport

See also: [Transport in Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Leeds)



[Leeds Bradford Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_Bradford_Airport)

Major national road transport routes pass through West Yorkshire, principally the [M62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M62_motorway), [M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_motorway), and [A1(M)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1\(M\)_motorway). The regional rail network is centred on [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_railway_station), with long-distance intercity services to destinations across Great Britain provided by [LNER](57431689.md), [CrossCountry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CrossCountry), and [TransPennine Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransPennine_Express)..[^cite_note-47] Most regional and local train services along the [Airedale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airedale_line), [Wharfedale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharfedale_line), [Calder Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calder_Valley_line), [Huddersfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield_line), [Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefield_line), and [Pontefract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontefract_line) lines are provided by [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Trains).[^cite_note-48] [Open-access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-access_operator) [train operating company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_operating_company) [Grand Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_\(train_operating_company\)) also provides services from [Bradford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford) to [London](17867.md) via [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_West_Yorkshire).[^cite_note-49]

[Leeds Bradford Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_Bradford_Airport), located in [Yeadon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeadon,_West_Yorkshire), is Yorkshire’s largest airport. [Doncaster Sheffield Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doncaster_Sheffield_Airport), formerly the other major passenger airport serving Yorkshire, closed in November 2022.[^cite_note-50] Leeds Bradford Airport is served by [Ryanair](262878.md), [Jet2.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet2.com), [TUI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TUI_Airways), [Wizz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizz_Air) [Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizz_Air), [Aer Lingus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aer_Lingus), [Aurigny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurigny), [KLM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLM_Cityhopper), [SunExpress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SunExpress), and [EasyJet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EasyJet), with easyJet due to end its services from the airport in January 2027.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] In 2024, Leeds Bradford Airport served more than 4.24 million passengers.[^cite_note-53]

Towards the end of her first mayoral term in West Yorkshire, [Tracy Brabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy_Brabin) announced her decision to improve bus services in the region by introducing [bus franchising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_Services_Act_2017) rather than continuing with an enhanced partnership under the existing privatised model.[^cite_note-54] In May 2025, the [Weaver Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaver_Network) brand was unveiled as the future identity for public transport in West Yorkshire, progressively replacing the [West Yorkshire Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire_Metro) brand ahead of the launch of franchised bus services.[^cite_note-55] The first two bus operating contracts for the new Weaver Network were awarded in September 2026 to [FirstGroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FirstGroup), covering Huddersfield and parts of Leeds, and [Transdev Blazefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transdev_Blazefield), covering Dewsbury.[^cite_note-56]

In October 2021, £830 million of funding was announced for the [West Yorkshire Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire_Combined_Authority) through the City Region Sustainable Transport Settlement, including funding to develop [mass transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transit) proposals for the region.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] Consultation was undertaken between July and September 2024 on proposed tram routes linking Bradford and Leeds, and [St. James Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_James's_University_Hospital), central Leeds and [White Rose Shopping Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Rose_Centre).[^cite_note-59] Following a review by the [National Infrastructure and Service Transformation Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Infrastructure_and_Service_Transformation_Authority), the programme was moved to a more sequential approach intended to reduce delivery risk, contributing to a delay in the expected timetable for the scheme.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

[Rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) is also big in West Yorkshire. The teams who are, or have been, in the [Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_League) are [Bradford Bulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford_Bulls), [Castleford Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castleford_Tigers), [Halifax Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax_R.L.F.C.), [Huddersfield Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield_Giants), [Leeds Rhinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_Rhinos), and [Wakefield Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefield_Trinity). Any combination of these teams playing against each other would be called a West Yorkshire derby even if the rivalry is not as great as other rivalries between teams in the area. The main rugby union club in the county is [Yorkshire Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Carnegie).

[Elland Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elland_Road) is the largest stadium in the area, hosting [Leeds United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_United_F.C.). The [Headingley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headingley_Stadium), a stadium complex also in [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds), consists of a cricket and a rugby ground. The cricket ground is home of the [Yorkshire County Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_County_Cricket_Club) and the rugby ground is home to Leeds Rhinos. In [Huddersfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huddersfield), the [John Smith's Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Smith's_Stadium) is home to Huddersfield Town and Huddersfield Giants. In [Bradford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford), [Valley Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_Parade) is the home of Bradford City, whereas the [Odsal Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odsal_Stadium) is the home of the Bradford Bulls. Other stadiums include [Wheldon Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jungle_\(Wheldon_Road\)) (Castleford), [The Shay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shay) (Halifax), [Belle Vue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_Vue_\(Wakefield\)) (Wakefield), [Mount Pleasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pleasant,_Batley) (Batley), [Crown Flatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Flatt) (Dewsbury), [Post Office Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_Office_Road) (Featherstone), [John Charles Centre for Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Charles_Centre_for_Sport) (Hunslet) and [Cougar Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cougar_Park) (Keighley).

There are two racecourses in West Yorkshire: [Pontefract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontefract_Racecourse) and [Wetherby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetherby_Racecourse).

West Yorkshire also used to host regular [speedway](230182.md) meetings, having the [Halifax Dukes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax_Dukes) and the [Bradford Dukes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford_Dukes) teams. [Odsal Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odsal_Stadium) used to host [BriSCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BriSCA_Formula_1_Stock_Cars) stock cars. Leeds has a hill climb event at [Harewood speed Hillclimb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harewood_speed_Hillclimb).
