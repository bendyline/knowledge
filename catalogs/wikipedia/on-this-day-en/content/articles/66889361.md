# Archaeology of ancient Egypt

&#x20;The **archaeology of ancient Egypt** is the study of the [archaeology](18951655.md) of [Egypt](8087628.md), stretching from [prehistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory) through three millennia of documented history. Egyptian archaeology is one of the branches of [Egyptology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptology).

[Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon)'s [invasion of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_campaign_in_Egypt_and_Syria) in 1798 led to the Western passion for Egyptian antiquities. In the modern era, the [Ministry of State for Antiquities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Council_for_Antiquities) controls excavation permits for Egyptologists. The field can now use [geophysical methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geophysical_survey_\(archaeology\)) and other applications of modern sensing techniques. [John Romer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Romer_\(Egyptologist\)), [Zahi Hawass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahi_Hawass), [Sarah Parcak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Parcak), [Toby Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toby_Wilkinson) are some of the prominent Egyptologists making excavations in Egypt today.[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:1-2][^cite_note-\:2-3][^cite_note-4]

## History

English Egyptologist [William Matthew Flinders Petrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flinders_Petrie), known as the "father of Egyptian archaeology",[^cite_note-5] introduced the archaeological techniques of field preservation, recording, and excavation methodology in the mid-1880s. Many highly educated amateurs also travelled to Egypt, including women such as [Harriet Martineau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Martineau), Headley Fellow and [Florence Nightingale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Nightingale). In 1882, [Amelia Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amelia_Edwards) and [Reginald Stuart Poole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reginald_Stuart_Poole), an employee from the Department of Coins and Medals at the [British Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum), decided to create the [Egypt Exploration Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt_Exploration_Society) as a way to raise funds for more excavations in the [Nile Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_Delta), which had rarely been visited.[^cite_note-\:1-2][^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:2-3]

### First excavations

The [Rosetta Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone) was discovered there in July 1799 by French officer [Pierre-François Bouchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-Fran%C3%A7ois_Bouchard) during the Napoleonic campaign in Egypt. It was the first ancient Egyptian bilingual text recovered in modern times, and it aroused widespread public interest with its potential for deciphering this previously untranslated [hieroglyphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_hieroglyphics) script. [Jean-François Champollion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Fran%C3%A7ois_Champollion) later broke the code of the Rosetta Stone.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

[Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon)'s men tried but failed to dig up and remove the statue of [Younger Memnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Younger_Memnon) to France during his 1798 expedition. During this attempt that the hole on the right of the torso (just above Ramesses's right nipple) is said to have been made. In 1815, following an idea mentioned to him by his friend [Johann Ludwig Burckhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Ludwig_Burckhardt) of digging up the statue and bringing it to Britain, the British consul general [Henry Salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Salt_\(egyptologist\)) hired the adventurer [Giovanni Belzoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Belzoni) in [Cairo](6293.md) for this purpose. Using his hydraulics and engineering skills, he had it pulled on wooden rollers with ropes to the bank of the [River Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Nile) opposite [Luxor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxor) by hundreds of workmen. However, no boat was yet available to take it up to [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), so Belzoni carried out an expedition to [Nubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubia), returning by October. With French collectors also in the area possibly looking to acquire the statue, he then sent workmen to [Esna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esna) to obtain a suitable boat and in the meantime carried out further excavations in [Thebes](65811.md). He finally loaded the products of these digs, plus the Memnon, onto this boat and got it to Cairo by 15 December 1816. There he received and obeyed orders from Henry Salt to unload all but the Memnon, which was then sent on to Alexandria and London without him.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## Archaeological timeline

### 19th century

The great temple of [Ramesses II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_II) at [Abu Simbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Simbel) was discovered in 1813 by the Swiss [Orientalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientalism) and traveler [Johann Ludwig Burckhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Ludwig_Burckhardt). An enormous pile of sand almost completely covered the facade and its colossal statues, blocking the entrance for four more years. The Paduan explorer Belzoni reached its interior on 4 August 1817.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

A life-size [sarcophagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcophagus) of the [19th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteenth_Dynasty_of_Egypt) Pharaoh [Seti I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seti_I) was rediscovered in 1817 by Belzoni in the [Valley of the Kings](3036884.md).[^cite_note-12]

The 3,200-year-old [Statue of Ramesses II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Ramesses_II) was discovered in 1820 by [Giovanni Battista Caviglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Battista_Caviglia) at the [Great Temple of Ptah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Ptah_\(Karnak\)) near [Memphis, Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Egypt). The statue was found broken in six pieces and earlier attempts at restoration had failed. In 1955, [Egyptian Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Egypt) [Gamal Abdel Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser) [moved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_relocation) it to [Bab Al-Hadid Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramses_Square) in Cairo.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

The [mummy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mummy) of Seti I was discovered by [Émil Brugsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89mile_Brugsch) on June 6, 1881, in the mummy cache (tomb [DB320](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DB320)) at [Deir el-Bahri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_el-Bahri), and since then, it has been kept at the [Egyptian Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Museum) in [Cairo](6293.md).[^cite_note-15]

The mummy of [Amenhotep II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amenhotep_II) was discovered in his original sarcophagus in March 1898, by [Victor Loret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Loret) in the [KV35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KV35) tomb in the Valley of the Kings.[^cite_note-16]

### 20th century

The tomb of [Nefertari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nefertari), [QV66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QV66), was discovered by [Ernesto Schiaparelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Schiaparelli) in 1904. A flight of steps cut out of the rock gives access to the antechamber, which is decorated with paintings based on chapter seventeen of the *[Book of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_the_Dead)*. The astronomical ceiling represents the heavens and is painted in dark blue, with a myriad of golden five-pointed stars. The east wall of the antechamber is interrupted by a large opening flanked by the representation of [Osiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osiris) to the left and [Anubis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anubis) on the right; this, in turn, leads to the side chamber, decorated with offering-scenes, preceded by a vestibule in which paintings portray Nefertari presented to the deities, who welcome her. On the north wall of the antechamber is the stairway down to the burial-chamber, a vast quadrangular room covering a surface area of about 90 square metres (970 sq ft), its astronomical ceiling supported by four pillars, entirely decorated.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

The entrance to [KV55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KV55) was uncovered by [Edward R. Ayrton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_R._Ayrton) on 6 January 1907. Its discovery was brought to the attention of [Theodore M. Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_M._Davis) the following day. The tomb was first entered on 9 January by Ayrton, Davis, [Joseph Lindon Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Lindon_Smith), and (as the representative of the antiquities service) [Arthur Weigall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Weigall). On 11 January 1907, the findings were photographed. Ayrton then began clearing the tomb. On 25 January 1907, the coffin and mummy were investigated *in situ*.[^cite_note-19]

In 1921, while excavating south of the tomb, [Howard Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Carter) discovered several items that seem to have originated in KV55. These included a jasper burnisher and some fragments of copper rosettes from a [funerary pall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pall_\(funeral\)).[^cite_note-20]

After a systematic search, beginning in 1915, Carter discovered the actual tomb of [Tutankhamun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutankhamun) ([KV62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KV62)) in November 1922. By February 1923, the antechamber had been cleared of everything but two sentinel statues. A day and time were selected to unseal the tomb with about twenty appointed witnesses that included [Lord Carnarvon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Herbert,_5th_Earl_of_Carnarvon), several Egyptian officials, museum representatives and the staff of the Government Press Bureau. On 17 February 1923, at just after two o'clock, the seal was broken. There were 5,398 items found in the tomb, including a solid gold coffin, [face mask](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mask_of_Tutankhamun), thrones, archery bows, [trumpets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutankhamun's_trumpets), a [lotus chalice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_chalice), two [Imiut fetishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imiut_fetish), [gold toe stalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_finger_and_toe_stall), furniture, food, wine, sandals, and fresh linen underwear. Howard Carter spent ten years cataloging the items.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

In 1954, Egyptian archaeologist [Kamal el-Mallakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamal_el-Mallakh) discovered a ship buried in pieces right beside the [Great Pyramid of Khufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Pyramid_of_Giza). It was largely built of [Lebanon cedar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedrus_libani) planking in the "shell-first" construction technique, using unpegged [tenons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortise_and_tenon) of [Christ's thorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ's_thorn). The ship was built with a flat bottom composed of several planks, but no actual [keel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keel), with the planks and frames lashed together with [Halfah grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halfah_grass) and has been reconstructed from 1,224 pieces which had been laid in a logical, disassembled order in the pit beside the pyramid.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

Though [KV5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KV5) was partially excavated as early as 1825, its true extent was discovered in 1995 by [Kent R. Weeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_R._Weeks) and his exploration team. It has proven to be the largest tomb in the Valley of the Kings, and originally contained the mummified remains of some of this king's estimated 52 sons. Approximately 150 corridors and tomb chambers have been located in this tomb as of 2006, and the tomb may contain as many as 200 corridors and chambers.[^cite_note-25]

### 21st century

In June 2000,[^cite_note-26] the [European Institute for Underwater Archaeology (IEASM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Europ%C3%A9en_d'Arch%C3%A9ologie_Sous-Marine), directed by [Franck Goddio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franck_Goddio), in cooperation with the Egyptian Ministry for Antiquities discovered the ancient sunken city of [Thonis-Heracleion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heracleion) in today's Abu Qir Bay. The statues of a colossal king and queen are on display at the [Grand Egyptian Museum](5359115.md). Other discovered artefacts are exhibited at the [Bibliotheca Alexandrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliotheca_Alexandrina) and Alexandria National Museum (ANM). The excavations are documented through several publications[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

In June 2011, an archaeological team led by [French](5843419.md) Egyptologists [Pierre Tallet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Tallet) and Gregory Marouard, organized by the [French Institute of Oriental Archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Institute_of_Oriental_Archaeology) (*IFAO*), restarted work at the site. Among other materials, a collection of hundreds of papyrus fragments was found in 2013 dating back 4500 years. The [papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papyrus) is currently exhibited at the [Egyptian Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Museum) in [Cairo](6293.md). The Egyptian archaeologist [Zahi Hawass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahi_Hawass) called this ancient papyrus "the greatest discovery in Egypt in the 21st century".[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

In March 2017, the Egyptian-German team of archaeologists unearthed an eight-meter 3,000-year-old statue that included a head and a torso thought to depict [Pharaoh Ramses II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_II). According to Khaled El-Enany, the Egyptian Antiquities Minister, the statue was more likely thought to be King [Psammetich I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psamtik_I). Excavators also revealed an 80 cm-long part of a [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) statue of Pharaoh [Seti II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seti_II) while excavating the site.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In August 2017, archaeologists from the Ministry of Antiquities announced the discovery of five mud-brick tombs at Bir esh-Shaghala, dating back nearly 2000 years. Researchers also revealed worn masks gilded with gold, several large jars and a piece of pottery with unsolved ancient Egyptian writing on it.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

In November 2017(2000-10-25), the Egyptian mission in cooperation with the [European Institute for Underwater Archaeology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Europ%C3%A9en_d'Arch%C3%A9ologie_Sous-Marine) announced the discovery of three 2,000-year-old sunken shipwrecks dated back to the Roman Era in Alexandria's [Abu Qir Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Qir_Bay).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

In April 2018, the [Egyptian Ministry of Antiquities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Antiquities_\(Egypt\)) announced the discovery of the head of the bust of Roman Emperor [Marcus Aurelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius) at the [Temple of Kom Ombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Kom_Ombo) in [Aswan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aswan) during work to protect the site from groundwater.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

In April 2018, the Egyptian Ministry of Antiquities announced the discovery of the shrine of the god [Osiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osiris) Ptah Neb, dating back to the [25th dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-fifth_Dynasty_of_Egypt) in the [Temple of Karnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnak) in Luxor.

In July 2018, a German-Egyptian researchers' team head by Ramadan Badry Hussein of the [University of Tübingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_T%C3%BCbingen) reported the discovery of an extremely rare gilded burial mask that probably dates from the [Saite-Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Period_of_ancient_Egypt) period in a partly damaged wooden [coffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffin) in [Saqqara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqqara). The last time a similar mask was found was in 1939. The eyes were covered with [obsidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsidian), [calcite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcite), and black hued [gemstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemstone), possibly [onyx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onyx). "The finding of this mask could be called a sensation. Very few masks of precious metal have been preserved to the present day, because the tombs of most Ancient Egyptian dignitaries were looted in ancient times", said Hussein.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

In July 2018, archaeologists led by Zeinab Hashish announced the discovery of a 2.000-year-old 30-ton black granite [sarcophagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcophagus) in [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria). It contained three damaged skeletons in red-brown sewage water. According to archaeologist [Mostafa Waziri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostafa_Waziri), the skeletons looked like a family burial with a middle-aged woman and two men. Researchers also revealed a small gold artifact and three thin sheets of gold.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

In September 2018, a sandstone sphinx statue was discovered at the temple of Kom Ombo in Aswan. The statue, measuring approximately 28 cm (11 in) in width and 38 cm (15 in) in height, likely dates to the [Ptolemaic dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_dynasty).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

In September 2018, several dozen caches of mummies dating 2,000 years back were found in Saqqara by a team of Polish archaeologists led by [Kamil Kuraszkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamil_Kuraszkiewicz?action=edit\&redlink=1) from the Faculty of Oriental Studies of the [University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warsaw).[^cite_note-47]

In November 2018, an Egyptian archaeological mission located seven ancient Egyptian tombs at the ancient necropolis of [Saqqara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqqara) containing a collection of scarab and cat mummies dating back to the [Fifth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Dynasty_of_Egypt) and [Sixth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_Dynasty_of_Egypt) Dynasties. Three of the tombs were for cats, some dating back more than 6,000 years, while one of four other [sarcophagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcophagus) was unsealed. With the remains of cat mummies were unearthed 100 gilded and wooden statues of cats, and one in [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze), dedicated to the cat goddess [Bastet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastet). In addition, funerary items dating back to the [12th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_Dynasty_of_Egypt) were found beside the skeletal remains of the cats.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

In November 2018, France's [University of Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Strasbourg) announced the discovery of two sarcophagi thought to be more than 3,500 years old with two perfectly preserved mummies and approximately 1,000 funerary statues in the [Assasseef valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El-Assasif) near Luxor. One of the tombs with paintings where the female body found, was opened to the public in front of the international media, but the other one was previously opened by Egyptian antiquities officials.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

According to the [Al-Ahram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ahram), in January 2019, archaeologists headed by [Mostafa Waziri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostafa_Waziri) revealed a collection of 20 tombs dated back to the [Second Intermediate Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Intermediate_Period_of_Egypt) in Kom Al-Khelgan. The burials contained the remains of animals, amulets, and scarabs carved from faience, round and oval pots with handholds, flint knives, broken and burned pottery. All burials included skulls and skeletons in the bending position and were not very well-preserved.[^cite_note-53]

In February 2019, fifty mummy collections wrapped in linen, stone coffins or wooden sarcophagi dated back to the [Ptolemaic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Kingdom) were discovered by Egyptian archaeologists in the [Tuna el-Gebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna_el-Gebel) site in Minya. Twelve of the graves in four burial chambers 9 m (30 ft) deep, belonged to children. One of the remains was the partly uncovered skull enclosed in linen. Egypt's Minister of Tourism and Antiquities announced the discovery of the collective graves of senior officials and high clergy of the god Djehuty ([Thoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoth)) in [Tuna el-Gebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna_el-Gebel) in January 2020. An archaeological mission headed by [Mustafa Waziri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostafa_Waziri) reported that 20 [sarcophagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcophagi) and [coffins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffin) of various shapes and sizes, including five sarcophagi made of [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) and carved with [hieroglyphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_hieroglyphs) texts, as well as 16 tombs and five well-preserved wooden coffins were unearthed by their team.[^cite_note-54]

On 13 April 2019, an expedition led by a member of the [Czech](5321.md) Institute of [Egyptology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptology), Mohamed Megahed, discovered a 4,000-year-old tomb near Egypt's Saqqara [Necropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necropolis). Archaeologists confirmed that the tomb belonged to an influential person named Khuwy, who lived in Egypt during the [5th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Dynasty_of_Egypt)."The L-shaped Khuwy tomb starts with a small corridor heading downwards into an antechamber and from there a larger chamber with painted reliefs depicting the tomb owner seated at an offerings table", reported Megahed. Some paintings maintained their brightness over a long time in the tomb. Mainly made of white limestone bricks, the tomb had a tunnel entrance generally typical for pyramids. Archaeologists say that there might be a connection between Khuwy and pharaoh because the mausoleum was found near the pyramid of Egyptian Pharaoh [Djedkare Isesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djedkare_Isesi), who ruled during that time.[^cite_note-55]

Archaeologists discovered 35 mummified remains of Egyptians in a tomb in Aswan in April 2019. Italian archaeologist [Patrizia Piacentini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrizia_Piacentini), professor of [Egyptology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptology) at the [University of Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Milan), and Khaled El-Enany, the Egyptian minister of antiquities reported that the tomb where the remains of ancient men, women and children were found, dates back to the [Greco-Roman period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_world) between 332 BC and 395 AD. While the findings assumed belonging to a mother and a child were well preserved, others had suffered major destruction. Beside the mummies, artefacts including painted funerary masks, vases of bitumen used in mummification, pottery and wooden figurines were revealed. Thanks to the [hieroglyphics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_hieroglyphs) on the tomb, it was detected that the tomb belongs to a tradesman named Tjit.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

In July 2019, ancient granite columns and a smaller Greek temple, treasure-laden ships, along with bronze coins from the reign of [Ptolemy II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_II), pottery dating back to the third and fourth centuries BC were found at the sunken city of [Heracleion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heracleion). The investigations were conducted by Egyptian and European divers led by underwater archaeologist [Franck Goddio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franck_Goddio). They also uncovered the ruins of the city's main temple off of Egypt's north coast.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

In September 2019, archaeologists announced the discovery of a 2,200-year-old temple believed to belong to the [Ptolemy IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_IV_Philopator) Philosopher of the [Ptolemaic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Kingdom) in Kom Shakau village of Tama township. Researchers also revealed limestone walls carved with inscriptions of Hapi, the Nile god, and inscriptions with fragments of text featuring the name of Ptolemy IV.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

In October 2019, Egyptian archaeologists headed by [Zahi Hawass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahi_Hawass) revealed an ancient "industrial area" used to manufacture decorative artefacts, furniture and pottery for royal tombs in Luxor's Valley of the Monkeys. The site contained a big kiln to fire ceramics and 30 ateliers. According to Zahi Hawass, each atelier had a different aim – some of them were used to make pottery, others used to produce gold artefacts and others still to churn out furniture. About 75 meters below the valley, several items believed to have adorned wooden royal coffins, such as inlaid beads, silver rings and gold foil were unearthed. Some artefacts depicted the wings of deity [Horus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horus).[^cite_note-62]

In October 2019, the Egyptian archaeological mission unearthed thirty well-preserved wooden [coffins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffin) (3,000-year-old) in front of the [Mortuary Temple of Hatshepsut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortuary_Temple_of_Hatshepsut) in [El-Assasif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El-Assasif) Cemetery. The coffins contained mummies of twenty-three adult males, five adult females and two children, who are believed to be from the middle class. According to Hawass, mummies were decorated with mixed carvings and designs, including scenes from [Egyptian gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egyptian_deities), [hieroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hieroglyph), and the *[Book of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_the_Dead)*, a series of spells that allowed the soul to navigate in the [afterlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afterlife). Some of the coffins had the names of the dead engraved on them.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

In January 2020, Egypt's [Ministry of Antiquities](40143408.md) announced the discovery of 16 tombs of high priests of the god [Djehuty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoth) with about 20 [sarcophagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcophagus) and 5 well-preserved wooden [coffins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffin) at Al-Ghoreifa area in [Tuna el-Gebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna_el-Gebel), dating back to the 26th Dynasty.[^cite_note-Saraceni-67]

Another discovery is an [amphora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphora) jar stamped with the name of [Meritaten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritaten), daughter of [Akhenaten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhenaten) and [Nefertiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nefertiti), indicating royal wine production and the region's early occupation possibly tied to Akhenaten's rule.[^cite_note-livescience.com-134]

In April 2025, archaeologists led by Dr. [Zahi Hawass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahi_Hawass) uncovered the tomb of Prince Waser-If-Re, son of [Userkaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Userkaf), the founder of [Egypt's Fifth Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Dynasty_of_Egypt), at the [Saqqara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqqara) necropolis southwest of [Cairo](6293.md). The tomb features a pink granite false door, measuring 4.5 meters in height and 1.15 meters in width, inscribed with the prince's titles, including "Hereditary Prince," "Royal Scribe," "Vizier," and "Chanting Priest". According to the archaeologists, this is the first discovery of such a large pink granite false door at [Saqqara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqqara), signifying the prince's high status.[^cite_note-livescience.com-134][^cite_note-135] Additional findings include a red granite offering table, a black granite statue from the [26th Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-sixth_Dynasty_of_Egypt), and a group of 13 seated pink granite statues believed to represent the prince's wives. In May 2025, three tombs of prominent statesmen were discovered in the Dra Abu el-Naga necropolis in the southern Egyptian city of [Luxor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxor). In June 2025, archaeologists announced the discovery of three [Old Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Kingdom_of_Egypt) tombs at the [Qubbet el-Hawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qubbet_el-Hawa) necropolis in [Aswan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aswan). In September 2025, archaeologists announced the discovery of a previously unknown complete copy of the [Canopus Decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decree_of_Canopus) at the Tell el-Fara‘un site in [El-Husseiniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Husseiniya), [Sharqia Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharqia_Governorate). In January 2026, a joint Egyptian-Chinese archaeological mission announced the identification of a man-made reservoir at the Montu Temple precinct within the [Karnak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnak) Temple Complex in [Luxor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxor). In February 2026, a study by researchers from [Newcastle University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_University) and the [Academy of Fine Arts Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Fine_Arts_Vienna) reidentified a 5,300-year-old artifact from [Predynastic Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Egypt) as the oldest securely attested rotary metal [drill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drill) in the [Nile Valley](21244.md). ## Ministry of Antiquities (Egypt)

An edict issued by the Egyptian governor [Muhammad Ali Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_of_Egypt) on August 15, 1835, banned the export and trade of all Egyptian antiques to another country. The decree helped to protect monuments and curb the smuggling of ancient Egyptian artifacts.[^cite_note-190]

The [Ministry of Tourism & Antiquities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Antiquities_\(Egypt\)) (officially called the "Antiquities Authority") is the Egyptian government organization which serves to protect and preserve the heritage and ancient history of Egypt. It was established in 1858.

In December 2019 it was merged into the Ministry of Tourism, with Khaled El-Anany retaining his function.[^cite_note-\:3-191]

From 2009 to 2014, the ministry worked with the [Getty Conservation Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getty_Conservation_Institute) on the management and conservation of the [tomb of Tutankhamun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KV62).[^cite_note-\:3-191]

In September 2025, Tourism and Antiquities Minister Sherif Fathy reported that a 3,000-year-old gold bracelet, containing a [lapis lazuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapis_lazuli) bead and once belonging to [Amenemope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amenemope_\(pharaoh\)), a pharaoh of Egypt's [21st Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-first_Dynasty_of_Egypt) was stolen from the restoration laboratory of the [Egyptian Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Museum), [Cairo](6293.md) and subsequently sold and melted down. The stolen bracelet was first sold to a silver-shop owner, then passed to a gold jeweler, and eventually to a worker at a gold foundry.[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194]
