**Ayta al-Jabal** or **Aayta Ej Jabal** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): عيتا الجبل, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *ʿAytā al-Jabal*), also **Ayta az-[Zutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zutt)** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): عيتا الزط, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *ʿAytā az-Zuṭṭ*),[^cite_note-2] is a municipality in [Nabatiye Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabatiye_Governorate), in the [Bint Jbeil District](12027489.md) of southern Lebanon, about 112 kilometres (70 mi) from [Beirut](37428.md). The village is situated in the southern outskirts of the town of [Tibnin](6787186.md), in the heart of the [Lebanese Shia Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Shia_Muslims) community of [Jabal Amel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Amel). The village sits on an elevation of 680 metres (2,230 ft) above sea level.

## History

### Antiquity

Ayta al-Jabal is identified with *Beth 'Ayit* ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): בית עיט), a place referenced in the *[Baraita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baraita)* on the "Boundaries of the [Land of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Israel)" as part of the delineation of the northwestern border of Jewish resettlement following the return from [Babylonian exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_captivity). Scholarly analysis suggests that this text likely describes a later era, possibly the [Hasmonean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasmonean_dynasty) or [Herodian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodian_kingdom) periods, during the 2nd or 1st century BCE.[^cite_note-3]

### Ottoman period

In the 1596 [Ottoman tax records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defter), the village, named '*Ayta al-Gajar*, was located in the [Ottoman](22278.md) *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* (subdistrict) of [Tibnin](6787186.md) under the [Liwa of Safad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safad_Sanjak), with a population of 12 households and 3 bachelors, all [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim). The villagers paid a fixed tax-rate of 25% on agricultural products, such as [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) (2,600 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e)), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley) (1,400 akçe), olive trees (500 akçe), goats and beehives (400 akçe), in addition to "occasional revenues" (137 akçe) and a press for olive oil or grape syrup (12 akçe); a total of 5,049 akçe. Part of the revenue went to a [waqf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waqf).[^cite_note-Hutteroth-4][^cite_note-5]

&#x20;In 1856 it was named *Aithat et Tut* on [Kiepert's map of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiepert_maps_of_Palestine_and_Jerusalem)/Lebanon published that year,[^cite_note-6] while in 1875, [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) passed by and noted: “to my left, beyond a [wadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi), \[is] the village of *A'ïtha*, on a high hill; it does not look very considerable and is inhabited by [Metualis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metualis)."[^cite_note-7]



Ayta al-Jabal shown amongst the villages surrounding [Tebnine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tebnine) in the 1880s [PEF Survey of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine).

In 1881, the [Palestine Exploration Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s [*Survey of Western Palestine* (SWP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine) described the village (which it called '*Aita ez Zut*): "A village, built of stone, containing about fifty [Metawileh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metawileh), situated on a hill-top, with figs, olives, and arable land around. There are two [cisterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistern) in the village."[^cite_note-8]

### French rule

The current Bint Jbeil province was created in 1922 by [French mandatory authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Lebanon).[^cite_note-9]

## Demographics

In 2014 [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Lebanon) made up 99.54% of registered voters in Ayta al-Jabal. 98.13% of the voters were [Shiite Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Shia_Muslims).[^cite_note-10]
