Relations between Romania and the United States were formally established in 1880, with the appointment of [Eugene Schuyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Schuyler), a renowned and talented diplomat and historian, as the first American diplomatic representative to Romania.[^cite_note-1] After Romania [left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Revolution) the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) in 1989, US-Romanian relations have matured into a strategic partnership that encompasses a wide range of political, military, economic and cultural issues. The US supported Romania's entry into [NATO](21133.md), setting the stage for further integration into Europe. Today, Romania is a strong ally of the United States, and the two countries work together to build democracy, fight terrorism, and promote regional security and stability. United States is Observer bureau of the [BSEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_the_Black_Sea_Economic_Cooperation) and both countries are Observer bureau of the [CBSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_Baltic_Sea_States).

In addition to close historical and cultural ties, Romania is one of the most consistently [pro-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-American) nations in Europe and in the world. According to a 2018 European poll, 78% of [Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians) view the United States favorably. This is the second-highest pro-American sentiment in the [EU](9317.md), after Poland.[^cite_note-Eurobarometer-2] Another poll showed that a very large percentage of Romanians, 87%, want future US ambassadors to Bucharest to continue supporting the fight against [corruption](41107761.md) in Romania. The same poll showed that 74% of Romanians want the United States to remain the main strategic partner of Romania.[^cite_note-3]

## History

### Early history

The earliest contacts between the two countries date to the 1830s when [James Buchanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Buchanan) sought to create new trading routes on the [Danube](21209639.md) and in the [Black Sea](3386.md). In 1839, the American [consul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consul_\(representative\)) to [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) urged the [Department of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_State) to form consular offices in the [Danubian Principalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danubian_Principalities) with the role of increasing trade in the Danube harbors. Though not approved by the Department of State, the first [consulate](6770059.md) of the United States in the Principalities was established in [Galați](256639.md) in 1844. The first career consul to Galați was appointed by President Buchanan in 1858 and was approved by *[Domnitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domnitor)* [Alexandru Ioan Cuza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Ioan_Cuza) a year later, on 10 November 1859. Other consulates were opened in [Ismail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izmail), [Brăila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%83ila), and [Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ia%C8%99i), with the first consulate of Bucharest opening in 1864. The United States openly backed Romania during its [War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_War_of_Independence) and recognized its independence on 14 October 1878, in a letter written by President [Rutherford B. Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutherford_B._Hayes) and delivered to Bucharest by the American consul to Galați.[^cite_note-Partnership-4][^cite_note-5]



The American Hospital at the [Roznovanu Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roznovanu_Palace) in Iași, 1918

The official diplomatic relations between Romania and the United States started in 1880 when [Eugene Schuyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Schuyler) was appointed as diplomatic agent and consul general of the United States of America to Romania. This was followed by the official recognition of the new [Kingdom of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Romania) on 7 April 1881.[^cite_note-history-6] Before [World War I](4764461.md), the Romanian imports to America were declared subject to the "[most favored nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_favored_nation)" clause in an executive order issued by President [William Howard Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Howard_Taft). A similar act was endorsed by the Romanian authorities in 1912.[^cite_note-Partnership-4] Although American support for Romania during the war was limited, an [American Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Red_Cross) mission was sent to the country to provide humanitarian aid starting in 1917.[^cite_note-7] In 1918, Romania's situation was also addressed by President [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson) in his [Fourteen Points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourteen_Points), where it was demanded that the occupying [Central Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Powers) armies should evacuate the country. Wilson's support for [self-determination](29269.md) also helped in establishing the eventual [union with Transylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Transylvania_with_Romania).[^cite_note-8]

In the United States, Romanians had also [volunteered to join the US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volunteered_to_join_the_US_Army?action=edit\&redlink=1) in fighting on the [Western Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Front_\(World_War_I\)). Although the Romanian volunteer unit could not be established before the end of the war, the attempt strengthened the relationship between the two countries. Furthermore, the initiative succeeded in increasing the Romanian enlistments in the army, with 3,200–3,500 Romanians ending up serving in various American units in France, while some volunteers from [Youngstown, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youngstown,_Ohio) managed to form a company size unit – the 112th Trench Mortar Battery.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

### Interwar period

Following the end of the Great War, the relations between Romania and the United States continued to increase. In 1921, [Peter Augustus Jay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Augustus_Jay_\(diplomat\)) was appointed as the first minister plenipotentiary to Romania.[^cite_note-history-6] The cultural relations between the two countries also improved with publicized conference tours of important cultural figures taking place, while the number of Romanian students in top American universities increased as well.[^cite_note-11] On the economic level, American companies continued to invest in Romania such as the [Ford Motor Company](30433662.md) opening an [assembly plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Romania) in the country,[^cite_note-12] and the "Româno-Americana" Society under [Standard Oil of New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil_of_New_Jersey) expanding its operations.[^cite_note-13]

### World War II



[Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt),\
[US president](24113.md) from 1933 to 1945



[Ion Antonescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Antonescu),\

On 5 June 1942, in the midst of [World War II](32927.md), the United States declared war on Axis-aligned Romania, in response to Romania having declared war on the United States on December 12 of the previous year.[^cite_note-history-6] The US declared war on Hungary and Bulgaria the same day. The declaration of war passed both houses of [Congress](31756.md) unanimously, by votes of 361–0 in the [House of Representatives](19468510.md) and 73–0 in the [Senate](24909346.md).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

After the war declaration, the United States led a [bombing campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Allied_campaign_in_Romania) over the country which aimed to knock out Romania's oil exports to [Nazi Germany](21212.md). The most notable raid of this campaign was the low-level raid on [Ploiești](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ploie%C8%99ti), code-named [Operation Tidal Wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Tidal_Wave), on 1 August 1943, which saw the loss of over 50 [B-24 Liberator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-24_Liberator) bombers.[^cite_note-16] In the aftermath of the raids, oil transports to Germany were severely affected although Romania's oil production failed to be destroyed.[^cite_note-17] Allied casualties amounted to 1,500 [POW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POW)[^cite_note-18] and 1,706 [KIA](368513.md). After Romania [changed sides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_Romanian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) in 1944, the American prisoners were repatriated during [Operation Reunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Reunion).

Relations remained strained during the [Cold War](325329.md) while Romania was [under communist influence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Romania). Bilateral relations began to improve in the early 1960s with the signing of an agreement providing for partial settlement of American property claims. Cultural, scientific, and educational exchanges were initiated, and in 1964 the legations of both nations were promoted to full embassies.[^cite_note-State_Dept-19]



[Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford), Richard Nixon and Nicolae Ceaușescu in 1973

After [Communist Party general secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretary_of_the_Romanian_Communist_Party) [Nicolae Ceaușescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Ceau%C8%99escu) began to distance Romania from [Soviet foreign policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_foreign_policy), as in Romania's continued [diplomatic relations with Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel-Romania_relations) and [denunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceau%C8%99escu's_speech_of_21_August_1968) of the 1968 [invasion of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact_invasion_of_Czechoslovakia), President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) paid an official visit to Romania in August 1969. Despite political differences, diplomacy continued between US and Romanian leaders throughout the 1970s, culminating in the 1978 state visit to Washington by President Ceaușescu and his wife [Elena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Ceau%C8%99escu).[^cite_note-State_Dept-19]

In 1972, a consular convention to facilitate the protection of citizens and their property in both countries was signed. [Overseas Private Investment Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Private_Investment_Corporation) (OPIC) facilities were granted, and Romania became eligible for US Export-Import Bank credits.[^cite_note-State_Dept-19]



Ceaușescu with [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter), 12 April 1978&#x20;

A trade agreement signed in April 1975 accorded [most favored nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_favored_nation) (MFN) status to Romania under section 402 of the Trade Reform Act of 1974 (the [Jackson-Vanik amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson-Vanik_amendment) that links MFN to a country's performance on emigration). This status was renewed yearly after a congressional review confirmed a presidential determination that stated Romania was making progress toward freedom of emigration.[^cite_note-State_Dept-19] In 1984, Romania became the only [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact) country to ignore the Soviet demands and participate in the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics) held in Los Angeles that year.[^cite_note-20]

In the mid-1980s, criticism of Romania's [deteriorating human rights record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Romania), particularly regarding the mistreatment of religious and ethnic minorities, spurred attempts by Congress to withdraw MFN status. In 1988, to preempt congressional action, Ceaușescu renounced MFN treatment, calling Jackson-Vanik and other human rights requirements unacceptable interference in Romanian sovereignty.[^cite_note-State_Dept-19]



[Emil Constantinescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Constantinescu) and [Bill Clinton](3356.md) in Bucharest on 11 July 1997. During the visit Clinton declared Romania "free of communism".

After welcoming the [revolution of December 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Revolution) with a visit by Secretary of State [Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Baker) in February 1990, the US Government expressed concern that opposition parties had faced discriminatory treatment in the [May 1990 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_general_election,_1990), in which the [National Salvation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Salvation_Front_\(Romania\)) won a sweeping victory. The slow progress of subsequent political and economic reform increased that concern, and relations with Romania declined sharply after the [June 1990 Mineriad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_1990_Mineriad), where an anti-NSF sit-in was suppressed by Romanian police. Anxious to cultivate better relationships with the US and Europe, and disappointed at the poor results from its gradualist economic reform strategy, the [Stolojan government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolojan_I_Cabinet) implemented economic reform and conducted free and fair parliamentary and presidential elections in September 1992. Encouraged by the conduct of local elections in February 1992, US Deputy Secretary of State [Lawrence Eagleburger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Eagleburger) visited in May 1992. Congress restored MFN in November 1993 in recognition of Romania's progress in instituting political and economic reform. In 1996, the US Congress voted to extend permanent MFN graduation to Romania.[^cite_note-State_Dept-19]

### Present relations



Romanian president [Traian Băsescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traian_B%C4%83sescu) with US president [George W. Bush](3414021.md), 2005

As Romania's policies became unequivocally [pro-Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture), the United States moved to deepen relations. President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) visited Bucharest in 1997. The two countries initiated cooperation on shared goals, including economic and political development, defense reform, and non-traditional threats (such as trans-border crime and non-proliferation).[^cite_note-State_Dept-19]

Following the attacks of [11 September 2001](5058690.md), Romania was fully supportive of the US in the global [war on terror](13425800.md). Romania was part of the American-led "[Coalition of the Willing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_of_the_willing_\(Iraq_War\))" that supported the [invasion of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Iraq) in 2003. Romania was invited to join the North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) in November 2002 and formally joined NATO on 29 March 2004, after depositing its instruments of treaty ratification in Washington, D.C. President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) helped commemorate Romania's NATO accession when he visited Bucharest in November 2002. On that occasion, he congratulated the [Romanian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_people) on building democratic institutions and a [market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy) after the [fall of communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_communism). Romanian troops served alongside US troops in Afghanistan and were among the last to withdraw from Iraq.[^cite_note-State_Dept-19]

In March 2005, President [Traian Băsescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traian_B%C4%83sescu) made his first official visit to Washington to meet with President Bush, Secretary of State [Condoleezza Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condoleezza_Rice), Secretary of Defense [Donald Rumsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Rumsfeld), and other senior US officials. In December 2005, Secretary Rice visited Bucharest to meet with President Băsescu and to sign a bilateral defense cooperation agreement that would allow for the joint use of Romanian military facilities by US troops. The first proof of principle exercise took place at Mihail Kogălniceanu Air Base from August to October 2007.

Romania formally terminated its mission in Iraq on 4 June 2009, and pulled out its troops. On 23 July, the last Romanian soldiers left Iraq.[^cite_note-21] Three Romanian soldiers had been killed during their mission, and at least eight were wounded.

In 2011, the United States and Romania issued the "Joint Declaration on Strategic Partnership for the 21st Century Between the United States of America and Romania." The two countries identified key areas for enhanced cooperation, focusing on their political-military relationship, law-enforcement cooperation, trade and investment opportunities, and energy security. The United States and Romania are mutually committed to supporting human rights, strengthening the rule of law, and increasing prosperity in both countries. Romania and the United States also have ties in the form of business, arts and academic programs, including the [Future Leaders Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Leaders_Exchange) (FLEX) for high school students and a [Fulbright Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright_Program) managed by the bilateral Fulbright Commission. Romania's promotion of greater cooperation among its Black Sea neighbors in the areas of defense, law enforcement, energy, economic development, and the environment complements the US goal of enhancing stability in this sensitive and vital region.[^cite_note-history-6][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

In October 2013, the Romanian Government allowed the United States military to use the Mihail Kogălniceanu Air Base for US troop withdrawal from Afghanistan after the closure of the [Transit Center at Manas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_Center_at_Manas).[^cite_note-24]



Romanian president [Klaus Iohannis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Iohannis) with American president [Joe Biden](145422.md), 2024

In 2024, the [US Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Government) informed its Romanian counterpart that the Romanian Armed Forces can access funds for the modernization programs through the [Foreign Military Financing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Military_Financing) program. Under this program, the Romanian side can access up to 4 billion dollars in direct loans as well as up to 8 billion dollars attracted by contracting loans from the [financial markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_market).[^cite_note-25]

Romania joined the [Visa Waiver Program](421825.md) in January 2025.[^cite_note-26] At the [61st](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/61st_Munich_Security_Conference) [Munich Security Conference](1464620.md) in February 2025, vice president [JD Vance](53396477.md) [held a speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_JD_Vance_speech_at_the_Munich_Security_Conference) in which he criticised Romania for annulling the result of its [2024 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Romanian_presidential_election) following the surprise victory of [Călin Georgescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83lin_Georgescu) in the first round.

Following the [Iran war](82533582.md), Romania allowed United States to use the [Mihail Kogălniceanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RoAF_57th_Air_Base) and [Câmpia Turzii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RoAF_71st_Air_Base) air bases to deploy [refueling aircraft](237949.md) participating in the ongoing military operations in Iran.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

## Economic relations

Following the 1989 revolution, Romania's economy began to transition from state control to capitalism. The country worked to create a legal framework consistent with a market economy and investment promotion. Romania became a member of the European Union in 2007. In 1992, the United States and Romania signed a [bilateral investment treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilateral_investment_treaty) (BIT), which came into force in 1994. In 2003, before Romania's accession to the EU, the United States and Romania amended the BIT, which remains in effect. Romania attracts US investors interested in accessing the European market, with relatively low costs and a well-educated, tech-savvy population being major draws. In Romania, major US firms operate in the energy, manufacturing, information technology and telecommunications, services, and consumer products sectors. Top Romanian exports to the United States include machinery, vehicle parts, steel, and metallic items, and fertilizers.[^cite_note-history-6]

## U.S military deployments to Romania

### 99th Military Base Deveselu

Main article: [Deveselu Military Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deveselu_Military_Base)



The Aegis Ashore Missile Defense Complex Romania



Satellite view of the base

The [Romanian Air Force](2106520.md) 91st Air Base was closed in 2003, forcing approximately 200 personnel into early retirement; about 15 still live in the commune in the "airmen neighborhood".[^cite_note-31]

The airbase near [Deveselu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deveselu) was selected for the [NATO missile defense system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_missile_defense_system) employing [Aegis Ashore Ballistic Missile Defense System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegis_Ashore) and the inauguration ceremony was held in December 2015. The system uses the [SM-3 Block I.B. interceptor](15787389.md).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] There are about 500 Romanian soldiers, 250 U.S. troops, and other personnel working at the base.[^cite_note-34]

On 29 April 2022, a ceremony was held at the base with the occasion of the 10th anniversary of its establishment. On this occasion, the [military colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_colours#Romania) of the 99th Military Base Deveselu were decorated with the [Order of Military Virtue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Virtue_Medal).[^cite_note-35]

### 57th Air Base Mihail Kogălniceanu

Main article: [RoAF 57th Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RoAF_57th_Air_Base)



&#x20;US Air Force [B-52H Stratofortress](18933037.md) taxis at Mihail Kogălniceanu Air Base

The base has been used by the [US Military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Military) since 1999. In 2003, it became one of four Romanian military facilities that have been used by US military forces as a staging area for the [invasion](201936.md) of and [counter-insurgency efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_insurgency_\(2003%E2%80%932011\)) in Iraq, operated by the [458th Air Expeditionary Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/458th_Air_Expeditionary_Group). It was intended to become one of the main operating bases of [United States Army Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Europe)'s Joint Task Force East (JTF-E), a rotating task force initially to be provided by the [US 2nd Cavalry Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._2nd_Cavalry_Regiment), which was to grow to a brigade-sized force eventually. The JTF-E concept was reduced to the Army-only [Task Force East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_Force_East), but the base still retains an important role, given added weight by the [annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation). As of 2024, the base hosts over 4,500 American soldiers.[^cite_note-36]

During the first three months of the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md), the airport was transited by 1,300 cargo and personnel transports towards Iraq, comprising 6,200 personnel and about 11,100 tons of equipment.[^cite_note-rol2013-37]

Starting in 2019, the [United States Army](32087.md) has stationed units at the base on nine-month rotations.[^cite_note-38] In 2022, elements of the [101st Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Airborne_Division) arrived at the base, an event which marked the first deployment of the 101st Division to Europe in nearly 80 years.[^cite_note-39] As part of the [Bomber Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomber_Task_Force) mission, [B-52 Stratofortress](18933037.md) bombers operated from the base for the first time in 2024.[^cite_note-40] Also in 2024, construction work began on expanding the base, which will become the largest NATO base in Europe, able to house over 10,000 American and NATO soldiers and civilians.[^cite_note-41]

### 71st Air Base Câmpia Turzii

Main article: [RoAF 71st Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RoAF_71st_Air_Base)

The first [United States Air Force](32090.md) use of the Câmpia Turzii Air Base happened in 2008 with the deployment of [F-15E Strike Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15E_Strike_Eagle) in support of the air policing missions for the [Bucharest Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Bucharest_summit) that happened in the same year.[^cite_note-42] In 2021, the base began modernization with the help of the United States through the [William M. (Mac) Thornberry National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_M._\(Mac\)_Thornberry_National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2021) part of the [European Deterrence Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Deterrence_Initiative) program.[^cite_note-43] Further base upgrades were completed in 2023.[^cite_note-44]

Since 2021, the US Air Force has also stationed [MQ-9 Reaper](4769988.md) drones at the base.[^cite_note-45] The drones are operated by the [731st Expeditionary Attack Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/731st_Expeditionary_Attack_Squadron).[^cite_note-46]
