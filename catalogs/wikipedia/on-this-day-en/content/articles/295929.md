**Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): حمد بن خليفة آل ثاني; 1 January 1952 – 12 July 2026) was [Emir of Qatar](399230.md) from 1995 until his abdication in 2013. He was also [prime minister of Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Qatar) from 1995 to 1996.

Hamad received his education in Qatar before attending the United Kingdom's [Royal Military Academy Sandhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_Academy_Sandhurst). After graduating in 1971, he held several senior posts in the [Qatar Armed Forces](25194.md), including as chief of staff and minister of defense. Hamad seized power from his father, [Khalifa bin Hamad Al Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalifa_bin_Hamad_Al_Thani), in a bloodless [palace coup d'état in 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Qatari_coup_d'%C3%A9tat). During his 18-year rule, he promoted the development of [Qatar's natural gas](22562134.md), in particular [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) (LNG), which reached 77 million tonnes in annual production. This made Qatar the [richest country in the world per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(PPP\)_per_capita) with the average income in the country being US\$86,440 a year per person.[^cite_note-indy13-1] As emir of Qatar, Hamad ruled the country as an [absolute monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_monarchy), with supreme authority over all [branches of state power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers), and no [political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) were allowed.[^cite_note-\:2-2][^cite_note-\:02-3]

During his reign, several sports and diplomatic events took place in Qatar, including the [2006 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Asian_Games), [2011 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup), [2012 UN Climate Change Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference), [Doha Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha_Agreement_\(2008\)), [Fatah–Hamas Doha Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatah%E2%80%93Hamas_Doha_Agreement), and the decision to hold the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) in the country. He established the [Qatar Investment Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Investment_Authority). By 2013, it had invested over \$100 billion around the world, including [The Shard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shard), [Barclays Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barclays_Bank), [Heathrow Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathrow_Airport), [Harrods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrods), [Paris Saint-Germain F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_F.C.), [Volkswagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen), [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens), and [Royal Dutch Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Shell).[^cite_note-mem-4] Hamad's investment strategy is credited with Qatar becoming the first Arab and Muslim country to host the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md).[^cite_note-AA-12072026-5] Qatar began hosting two U.S. military bases while also maintaining relations with [Iran](14653.md). Hamad sought closer ties with both Iran and Israel compared to his father's approach.[^cite_note-\:5-6] Hamad founded news media group [Al Jazeera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Arabic) in 1996, which became known as the first news channel in the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world) to provide critical coverage of Arab governments, though it has avoided criticizing Qatar's own government. He also played a part in negotiations between the U.S. and the [Taliban](30635.md).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

In June 2013, Hamad, in a brief televised address, announced that he would hand power to his fourth son, [Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani](6146151.md), who was born to his second wife, [Moza bint Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moza_bint_Nasser).[^cite_note-9] Following his abdication, he became known as the Father Emir in Qatari terminology. He is credited with setting the foundation of the modern Qatari state, including the adoption of its first permanent [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Qatar) and holding elections for the advisory [Central Municipal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Municipal_Council); for presiding over a massive expansion in state revenue and national wealth due to LNG exports; and for his sweeping economic, cultural, and social reforms; which contributed to Qatar's rise on the international stage.[^cite_note-AA-12072026-5]

## Early life, education, and career

Hamad was born in [Doha](26214389.md), Qatar on 1 January 1952. His mother died soon after his birth and he was raised by his uncle.[^cite_note-dic25jun-10] He received his early education in Qatar.[^cite_note-AA-12072026-5]

He graduated from the British [Royal Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_Academy_Sandhurst) at [Sandhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandhurst,_Berkshire) in 1971, and was then commissioned as a [lieutenant colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel) in Qatar's [armed forces](25194.md).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-aps17sep-12] A few months later he returned to Qatar and was made commander of a mobile brigade, which later became a force called "Hamad Brigade".[^cite_note-aps17sep-12] In 1972, Hamad had the rank of general, and became army chief of staff.[^cite_note-aps17sep-12] Next he was appointed commander-in-chief of Qatar's armed forces with the rank of [major general](36301471.md).[^cite_note-indy13-1][^cite_note-aps17sep-12] In 1977 he was named minister of defense.[^cite_note-aps17sep-12] He was eventually promoted to the ranks of [lieutenant general](201916.md)[^cite_note-AA-12072026-5] and full [general](148537.md).[^cite_note-UPI-27061995-13] His tenure as minister of defense coincided with the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), and Hamad met United States defense secretary [Dick Cheney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Cheney) in 1991, during discussions about the postwar security architecture for the [Arab states of the Persian Gulf](3088555.md).[^cite_note-14] Hamad also promoted Qatar's participation in international sports, founded the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Qatar_Open) tennis tournament, and led the Supreme Council for Youth Welfare from 1979 to 1991.[^cite_note-UPI-27061995-13]

Hamad was appointed Heir Apparent of Qatar in 1977.[^cite_note-b-15] In the early 1980s, he led the Supreme Planning Council, which sets Qatar's basic economic and social policies. Starting in 1992, Hamad's father began gradually handing over responsibility for the day-to-day running of the country,[^cite_note-CSM1-16] including the development of Qatar's oil and natural gas resources, rendering him the effective ruler of the country.[^cite_note-voa-17] However, his father ultimately retained control over state finances.[^cite_note-voa-17] Hamad came to be regarded as the *de facto* leader of the country, while his father's role as emir was considered a formality. He began to change Qatar's foreign policy from other Gulf Arab states by increasing contact and trade with [Israel](9282173.md), calling for the lifting of [international sanctions against Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions_against_Iraq), and improving relations with [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-UPI-27061995-13] Qatar became the first Gulf monarchy to re-establish relations with [Iraq](7515928.md) after the Gulf War.[^cite_note-18] Hamad met Iranian foreign minister [Ali Akbar Velayati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Akbar_Velayati) in 1994, who described [Iran's relations with Qatar](52167955.md) as "fraternal and good".[^cite_note-19] Hamad also maintained cooperation with the United States, which included the building of a military base in Qatar, despite its criticisms of Qatar's increasing ties with Iraq and Iran.[^cite_note-UPI-27061995-13][^cite_note-20]

## Reign (1995–2013)



Sheikh Hamad in 1997

After securing the support of most senior members of his family, as well as that of the [United States](3434750.md), Hamad staged [a bloodless coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Qatari_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) on 27 June 1995 when his father [Khalifa bin Hamad Al Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalifa_bin_Hamad_Al_Thani) was on vacation in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), Switzerland, at which point he officially became the Emir of Qatar.[^cite_note-\:12-21][^cite_note-CSM1-16][^cite_note-Current_Leaders-22] The deposition came after a falling out between Hamad and his father, who had tried regaining some of the authority that he gave to Hamad starting in 1992.[^cite_note-voa-17] Thereafter, his father lived in exile in France and [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md)[^cite_note-Current_Leaders-22] until he returned to Qatar in 2004. On 11 July 1995, Hamad announced his first cabinet, which included himself concurrently serving as [prime minister](3596637.md), minister of defense, and commander-in-chief of the armed forces.[^cite_note-23] He remained as prime minister until 29 October 1996, when he named his half-brother [Abdullah bin Khalifa Al Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_bin_Khalifa_Al_Thani) to the post and asked him to form a government.[^cite_note-24]

Hamad then engaged an American law firm to freeze his father's bank accounts abroad in order to deter a possible countercoup.[^cite_note-CSM1-16] However, a [counter-coup was attempted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Qatari_coup_attempt) against Hamad in February 1996 under the leadership of former Economy Minister [Hamad bin Jassim bin Hamad Al Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamad_bin_Jassim_bin_Hamad_Al_Thani). The coup failed, and several of Qatar's traditional Arab allies were implicated in the plot, namely Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain and Egypt.[^cite_note-25]

In a break with traditional role, his second wife Sheikha [Moza bint Nasser Al-Missned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moza_bint_Nasser) became a visible advocate for education and children's causes.[^cite_note-CSM1-16] In 1995, the couple founded the [Qatar Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Foundation).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

### Autocracy and political reform

During Hamad's reign, the country enacted its first permanent [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Qatar), following the [2003 Qatari constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Qatari_constitutional_referendum).[^cite_note-\:2-2] While Hamad "hailed the event as a great step toward bringing democracy to the country", the constitution actually gave the emir "near-absolute powers", and in practice Hamad ruled an [autocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocracy) regime in Qatar.[^cite_note-\:13-28][^cite_note-\:2-2][^cite_note-\:3-29] In 1999, Qatar held elections for the first time, to the advisory [Central Municipal Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Municipal_Council), in which women were also allowed to vote and stand as candidates.[^cite_note-AJ-12072026-1-30][^cite_note-\:03-31] This made Qatar the first Gulf country to allow women to vote.[^cite_note-32]

Mehran Kamrava of [Georgetown University in Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University_in_Qatar) has suggested that Qatar's highly publicized campaign of [political liberalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratization) was meant to help stabilize the regime under Hamad, especially through support from the West, and that once sufficient stabilization was achieved, "both the rhetoric and the pretext of democratization were abandoned".[^cite_note-\:2-2] Other scholars have also called Hamad's domestic political reforms "largely performative" steps, intended mostly to bolster Qatar's international image without enacting meaningful change,[^cite_note-\:82-33] or said that Qatar's "political trajectory reflects a model of selective and symbolic democratization—embracing electoral forms while avoiding their full democratic substance."[^cite_note-34] Although the ministry of information was abolished in 1998, with the press occasionally allowed to criticize the economy or the status of [foreign workers](384142.md), journalists continue to face a "draconian system of censorship".[^cite_note-\:2-2][^cite_note-\:3-29][^cite_note-35] It has been noted in scholarship that the Qatari government "made great play" of the abolition of the Ministry of Information promising greater press freedom, and that in reality the step was a "highly symbolic gesture" intended to signal reform to international partners while attempting to "carefully, selectively, and pre-emptively remain in control of content".[^cite_note-36]

### Oil and gas wealth



US president [George W. Bush](3414021.md) and Hamad bin Khalifa in the [Oval Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Office), 2003

Hamad was able to focus on turning [Qatar](8238258.md) from a small desert backwater into a major world power by continuing to exploit the [country's vast oil fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_industry_in_Qatar) and discovering and tapping the world's [third largest gas reserves](22562134.md).[^cite_note-lem-37] By 2010 [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) (LNG) production had reached 77 million tons, making Qatar the richest country in the world. With fewer than two million inhabitants, the average income in the country shot to \$86,440 per year per person. Qatar expert [Olivier Da Lage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Da_Lage) said: "When he came to power in 1995, Sheikh Hamad had a goal to place Qatar on the world map by exploiting the gas resources which his father did not develop for fear it would change the emirate's society. Eighteen years on, he has finished the job – Qatar has acquired the financial clout to command respect from neighboring countries and Western governments alike".[^cite_note-indy13-1]

Since he had been the crown prince, Hamad promoted the development of natural gas, and in particular the [North Field](9037100.md), the largest gas field in the world, as an alternative to Qatar's dependence on crude oil exports.[^cite_note-UPI-27061995-13][^cite_note-UPI-28061995-38] State revenue began growing massively when the North Field started exporting LNG in 1996; its [value added](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_added) increased from US\$3 billion to \$110 billion over the course of his reign. By 2006 Qatar had become the world's largest LNG exporter. During Hamad's reign, Qatar's GDP increased by 24 times, and per capita GDP increased by nearly six times. Hamad also adopted the [Qatar National Vision 2030](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_National_Vision_2030) plan, aiming to transform Qatar into a knowledge-based economy.[^cite_note-AA-12072026-5]

### Foreign relations



Sheikh Hamad with [Margareta of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margareta_of_Romania) and her husband [Prince Radu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Radu_of_Romania), 2010

The foreign policy of Qatar was primarily determined by a circle of four; Hamad, his wife [Moza bint Nasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moza_bint_Nasser), crown prince Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, and prime minister and foreign minister [Hamad bin Jassim bin Jaber Al Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamad_bin_Jassim_bin_Jaber_Al_Thani). Despite this, the Norwegian Centre for Conflict Resolution claimed that Hamad was fully in charge of every foreign policy decision.[^cite_note-\:6-60]

Due to Qatar's historically tenuous relationship with regional powers such as Iran and Saudi Arabia, Hamad sought to court the United States as a counterweight to any potential threats. In the [aftermath of the September 11 attacks](171761.md), [United States Central Command](338482.md) established their regional headquarters in Doha's [Al Udeid Air Base](3830783.md).[^cite_note-\:6-60]

The Emir made a \$100 million donation for the relief of [New Orleans](53842.md) following [Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina) in 2005.[^cite_note-61] He was a key person in the ceasefire during the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War) and contributed significantly in the relief of damaged areas.

As emir, Hamad oversaw a tightening of ties with Iran, as opposed to other Arab Gulf neighbours. In 2010, Qatar and Iran signed a defense cooperation agreement[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] and an internal security pact,[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] and Hamad traveled to Tehran to meet with both the [President of Iran](233913.md), [Mahmoud Ahmadinejad](7907317.md), and its [supreme leader](36814344.md), [Ali Khamenei](385653.md), exchanging pledges of cooperation and friendship.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] In late 2012, Hamad paid \$57 million in ransom to free 57 [IRGC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRGC) fighters captured by an armed opposition group in southern Damascus, and Ahmadinejad later told that the Emir refused to accept reimbursement from the Iranian government, stating that he paid the ransom "for the sake of the friendship between the two peoples".[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

In 2012, the Emir proposed deploying Arab troops to reduce killings in the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md).[^cite_note-70] He provided two military bases for foreign troops, [Al Udeid Air Base](3830783.md) and [Camp As Sayliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_As_Sayliyah).[^cite_note-71]

Despite the prevalence of [anti-Israel sentiment within the Arab world](7960202.md), he had previously maintained friendly relations with Israel.[^cite_note-Current_Leaders-22] He met Israeli foreign minister [Tzipi Livni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzipi_Livni) on 25 September 2007 in New York City. This marked the first real attempt by any leader in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) to pursue dialogue with Israel.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] However, Qatar severed diplomatic ties with Israel in 2009 in response to Israel's actions during the [Gaza war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_\(2008%E2%80%932009\)).[^cite_note-74] The emir also expressed his objection to [Israeli settlement](15123.md) policy, especially the [Judaization of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaization_of_Jerusalem).[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

#### Palestine and Syria

In October 2012, the Emir made a landmark visit to Gaza by being the first head of state to go there since the [2006 election](38945340.md) of Hamas and the [imposition of a blockade](19224310.md) by Israel.[^cite_note-77] He took a flight to Egypt before being driven into Gaza. Palestine's interior ministry was said to have a "well-prepared plan" to provide security for the emir during his stay.[^cite_note-78] In October 2012, Hamad visited Gaza and pledged US\$400 million in humanitarian aid to [Hamas](13913.md), to build infrastructure projects and hospitals.[^cite_note-79]

In December 2012, *The New York Times* accused the Qatari government of funding the [Al-Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front), a U.S. government [designated terrorist organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_designated_terrorist_organizations).[^cite_note-nytimes.com-80] Others have noted the Emir's visit to Gaza and meeting with Hamas, which houses a militant wing, [Izz ad-Din al-Qassam Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izz_ad-Din_al-Qassam_Brigades).[^cite_note-ft.com-81] However, Qatar denies these allegations, stating that its policy was to help facilitate constructive engagement between Hamas and the [Palestinian Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority).[^cite_note-82]

In 2004, 2010, 2014, and 2017, the [Qatari government](25190.md) introduced new [anti-terror laws](4700071.md) to combat terrorism, terrorism financing and related crimes. In 2019 the Qatari government introduced a new [anti-money laundering](19390.md) and [counter terror financing](4308598.md) laws.[^cite_note-83]

### Culture and education

In one of his first acts as emir in 1995, Hamad established the Qatar Foundation for Education, Science and Community Development.[^cite_note-AA-12072026-5] In the arts, Hamad established the [Qatar Museums Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Museums_Authority) in 2005 which built the [I. M. Pei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._M._Pei) designed [Museum of Islamic Art Doha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Islamic_Art,_Doha). Since its opening, [Qatar](8238258.md) has become the world's biggest contemporary art buyer,[^cite_note-\:1-84] famously purchasing [Cézanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9zanne)'s *[The Card Players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Card_Players)&#x20;*&#x69;n 2012 for over US\$250 million.[^cite_note-85] The art acquisition efforts were often represented by Sheikh Saud bin Mohammed Al Thani, president of Qatar's National Council for Culture, Arts and the Heritage.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

Throughout his reign, Hamad played an active role in promoting sports and athletics in Qatar and significantly increasing its prominence on the international stage. Doha became a foremost sporting capital in the world, hosting several major international tournaments. He oversaw the development of advanced sporting infrastructure, as well the creation of [a women's tennis league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Qatar_Open) in 2003 and the [National Sports Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Sports_Day#Qatar) in 2011. The "defining sporting achievement" of his reign was considered to have occurred in December 2010, when Qatar was awarded the right to host the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md).[^cite_note-sports-88]

### Abdication



Sheikh Hamad with [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry) in 2014

On 25 June 2013, Hamad handed over power to his son [Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani](6146151.md) in a televised speech.[^cite_note-New_Emir_BBC-100][^cite_note-Nordland-101] In regards to the shift in power, Hamad said: "The time has come to open a new page in the journey of our nation that would have a new generation carry the responsibilities".[^cite_note-investvine-102]

## Health and death

Hamad was believed to have suffered from poor health for several years. Hamad underwent his first kidney transplant in 1997[^cite_note-103] and at least one more at a later date.[^cite_note-Nordland-101] He later went on [dialysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_dialysis).

In December 2015, he was flown to [Zurich](40334603.md), Switzerland, for treatment after breaking his leg while on vacation in Morocco's [Atlas Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Mountains).[^cite_note-GuardDec15-104][^cite_note-\:4-105]

Hamad died in Doha on 12 July 2026, at the age of 74.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-\:5-6] The Qatari government declared four days of [national mourning](2111211.md) with flags at half mast.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] The Indian government announced a day of national mourning.[^cite_note-109] The Maldivian government announced flags to fly at half-mast for a day.[^cite_note-110]

### Funeral

Hamad's funeral was held on the same day of his death, after evening prayers, at the [Imam Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Muhammad_ibn_Abd_al-Wahhab_Mosque) in Doha.[^cite_note-\:8-111] Heads of state such as President of Turkey [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan), President of Syria [Ahmed al-Sharaa](44007699.md) and Prime Minister of Italy [Giorgia Meloni](29228976.md) came to pay their respects.[^cite_note-112] Foreign minister of [Iran](14653.md), [Abbas Araghchi](21951274.md), also came to pay his respects, even amidst harsh criticism from the [GCC](220950.md) on Iran's ongoing attacks on neighbouring countries.[^cite_note-113] Among other dignitaries present was also [FIFA](11049.md) president [Gianni Infantino](9286117.md), who notably left the United States just a day before the semi finals of the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) to attend the event.[^cite_note-114] Hamad was said to have been "buried in a simple grave" at the Lusail Cemetery north of Doha.[^cite_note-\:8-111]

## Legacy

Often called the "founding father of modern Qatar",[^cite_note-\:5-6] Hamad is regarded as having laid the foundation of the modern Qatari state,[^cite_note-AJ-12072026-1-30] and began efforts to increase Qatar's soft power, with major investments in education, scientific research, sports, and media.[^cite_note-AJ-12072026-2-115] In their obituary of Hamad, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* stated that under his reign, Hamad oversaw Qatar's transformation from a "little-known desert peninsula in the Persian Gulf" into a country with "vast wealth and global influence".[^cite_note-\:5-6]

Following his abdication, he became known as the Father Emir in Qatari terminology.[^cite_note-AA-12072026-5] He is credited with his efforts in the adoption of Qatar's first permanent [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Qatar),[^cite_note-AA-12072026-5] and holding elections to the advisory Central Municipal Council.[^cite_note-\:03-31] He was also noted for presiding over a massive expansion in state revenue and national wealth from LNG exports,[^cite_note-AA-12072026-5] and for strengthening ties between Qatar and the Western world.[^cite_note-\:2-2] Aside from politics and economic expansion, Hamad was also credited with making Qatar into a "global sporting hub" by increasing the country's involvement and performance in a number of international competitions.[^cite_note-sports-88] He was also noted for founding [Al Jazeera](30204052.md), which *[The Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Los_Angeles_Times)* described as a "major force in global media" and a "powerful voice in Arab media".[^cite_note-LAobit-116]

## Investments

In 2013, Hamad established the [Qatar Investment Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Investment_Authority), which has invested over \$100 billion around the world, including [The Shard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shard), [Barclays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barclays), [Heathrow Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathrow_Airport), [Harrods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrods), [Paris Saint-Germain F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_F.C.), [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens) and [Royal Dutch Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_plc).[^cite_note-117] It also holds about 17% stake in the [Volkswagen Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Group), [Porsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche), [Hochtief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hochtief), as well as investments in [Sainsbury's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sainsbury's).[^cite_note-118]

Hamad was listed as owner of Afrodille S.A., which had a bank account in Luxembourg and shares in two South African companies.[^cite_note-offshore-119] He also held a majority of the shares in Rienne S.A. and Yalis S.A., which held a term deposit with the [Bank of China](303789.md) in Luxembourg.[^cite_note-offshore-119] A relative owned 25% of these: Sheikh [Hamad bin Jassim Al Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamad_bin_Jassim_bin_Hamad_Al_Thani), Qatar's former prime minister and foreign minister.[^cite_note-offshore-119]
