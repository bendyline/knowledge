**London Business School** (**LBS**) is a [business school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_school) and a [member institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_institutions_of_the_University_of_London) of the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London). LBS was established in 1964 and awards post-graduate degrees ([master's degrees](170346.md) in [management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management) and [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance), [MBAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration) and [PhDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD_in_management)).

London Business School's main campus is in [Marylebone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marylebone), [London](17867.md), with the main site at [Sussex Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex_Place), adjacent to [Regent's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent's_Park), and other nearby sites at the Taunton Centre on Taunton Place and the [Sammy Ofer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sammy_Ofer) Centre in the former [Marylebone Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marylebone_Town_Hall). LBS also has an international satellite campus in [Dubai](211583.md), [United Arab Emirates](69328.md).

## History



Sussex Place, main campus of the London Business School in London

The [Robbins Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robbins_Report) committee recommended in the early 1960s that at least two major postgraduate business schools should be created, and the Franks Report (led by [Oliver Franks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Franks,_Baron_Franks)) subsequently recommended that one be founded in London and one in Manchester. An industry appeal raised £5 million, with a matching grant from the government, to establish these schools. The initial plan for the London school was for it to be a joint effort between the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) and [Imperial College London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_College_London).[^cite_note-4] However, this plan did not develop and London Business School was instead established in 1964 under the name of the *London Graduate School of Business Studies*.[^cite_note-5] The first meeting of the board was held in November 1964, with the first dean being appointed in April 1965 and the first two professors in May 1965. The executive education programme started in February 1966[^cite_note-LBS40-6] The MBA (called an MSc until 1985) also launched in 1966. Despite Franks' recommendation that the business schools should offer 12-month MBA courses, this was a 2-year course after the US model.[^cite_note-Lock-7] The part-time MBA followed in 1983.[^cite_note-LBS40-6]

The school was incorporated as a [company limited by guarantee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_limited_by_guarantee) in 1965 and became an "institution having recognised teachers" of the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London).[^cite_note-LBS50-8] In 1968, the school joined the [Sloan Fellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellows) MSc programme, funded by the [Alfred P. Sloan Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_P._Sloan_Foundation), with an initial intake of 17 students.[^cite_note-LBS50-8] The first doctoral programme was established in 1969, with backing from the [Ford Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Foundation).[^cite_note-9]

The school was initially based in the fourth to tenth floors of 28 [Northumberland Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumberland_Avenue) while a permanent site was chosen. The first choice for a permanent site, between [Bayswater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayswater) and [Edgware Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgware_Road) in central London, but this encountered opposition from [London County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_County_Council) and was eventually dropped. Eventually, the decision was made to lease 1–26 Sussex Place from the [Crown Estate Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Estate_Commission). The neoclassical frontage, designed by [John Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Nash_\(architect\)), had to be retained, but the rest of the building was rebuilt for the school. This was completed in 1970 and opened by [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II) in November of that year. The new building combined accommodation and teaching facilities, allowing the school to begin running residential executive courses.[^cite_note-Hollow-10]

The Park Road building (now the Plowden Building), adjacent to the Sussex Place site and also leased from the Crown Estate, was redeveloped from 1977 following a capital campaign. Initial plans to demolish part of the terrace and replace it with a modernist building were dropped after opposition from the local council. Construction eventually began in 1981 with the facade along Park Road being retained, as had been done with Sussex Place, but the interior being rebuilt. It opened in 1982, with a later formal opening by [Prince Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Charles) in May 1983.[^cite_note-Hollow-10][^cite_note-LBS50-8]

The school operated as a graduate school of the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London) until 1986, when it was incorporated by [royal charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_charter), which gave LBS the right to confer its own degrees (held in abeyance until 2010) and became a college of the university under the name *London Business School*.[^cite_note-11] The school won a Queen's Award for Export in 1992.[^cite_note-LBS50-8] The executive MBA was launched in 1992,[^cite_note-LBS50-8] and the Masters in Finance programme the following year.[^cite_note-LBS60-12] A global executive MBA in partnership with [Columbia Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Business_School) was launched in 2001.[^cite_note-LBS50-8]

In 1995, the school acquired a block of houses on Taunton Place and raised £8 million to convert this to an academic complex including a library, seminar rooms and offices, as well as an underground recreation centre.[^cite_note-Hollow-10]

In 2007 a campus was opened in [Dubai](211583.md).[^cite_note-LBS60-12] An Asian-focused version of the global executive MBA was launched in 2009 in partnership with Columbia Business School and the [University of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Hong_Kong), along with the Masters in Management programme.[^cite_note-LBS50-8] In 2016, LBS launched a new programme, the Masters in Financial Analysis, aimed at recent graduates who wish to work in finance.

In 2012, the school leased [Marylebone Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marylebone_Town_Hall), located close to the Sussex Place site, from [Westminster City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_City_Council)[^cite_note-LBS60-12][^cite_note-LBS50-8] and restored it with the objective of expanding its teaching facilities by 70 per cent.[^cite_note-LBS_Expansion-13] The building was renamed "The Sammy Ofer Centre" following a donation from the Sammy Ofer Foundation for the development of the building,[^cite_note-LBS60-12], and opened in 2017.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-Hollow-10] In 2016, the main building on Sussex Place was renamed the Ratcliffe Building in honour of alumnus [Jim Ratcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Ratcliffe).[^cite_note-15] In 2019, the school acquired the lease of 27 Sussex Place, adjacent to the existing buildings, from the [Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Obstetricians_and_Gynaecologists). This became the North Building.[^cite_note-campus-16] Following the donation from David and Molly Pyott and their foundation, the North Building will be renamed “The David and Molly Pyott Building” by the end of 2026. The donation of 25 million pounds, which the school itself describes as one of the largest in its history, will also fund an MBA scholarship programme over the next decade.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-\:1-18]

## Campus



The Ratcliffe Building at the main Sussex Place campus in London



The Marylebone Town Hall, second campus in London

The main campus is at [Sussex Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sussex_Place) in [Marylebone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marylebone), on the perimeter of [Regent's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent's_Park). It consists of three main buildings: the Ratcliffe Building, the original main building of the school, at 1–26 Sussex Place, facing [Regent's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent's_Park) across the Front Lawn; the Plowden Building, backing onto the Ratcliffe Building and facing Park Road; and the North Building, at 27 Sussex Place,[^cite_note-campus-16][^cite_note-Campus_Map-20] which is to be renamed the David and Molly Pyott Building following renovations scheduled for completion in late 2026.[^cite_note-\:1-18]

The Taunton Centre is at 25 Taunton Place, around 150m West of the Plowden Building.[^cite_note-campus-16]

The school also has a teaching hub at the Sammy Ofer Centre in the Old [Marylebone Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marylebone_Town_Hall).[^cite_note-21]

The school does not have any [halls of residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halls_of_residence) of its own in London but, as part of the University of London, students are eligible for University of London intercollegiate halls.[^cite_note-22]

LBS has an [international satellite campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_branch_campus) in the [Dubai International Financial Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubai_International_Financial_Centre) in [Dubai](211583.md), [United Arab Emirates](69328.md). This offers an executive MBA and executive education programmes.[^cite_note-23]

### Teaching

The school's full-time [Master of Business Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration) degree programme takes 15 to 21 months,[^cite_note-28] and it also offers a one-year MBA for graduates with a Masters in Management or equivalent,[^cite_note-29] as well as executive (part-time) MBA degrees (in London or Dubai),[^cite_note-30] and a global executive MBA in partnership with [Columbia Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Business_School).[^cite_note-31] The school also offers other one-year master's degrees,[^cite_note-32] including the [Master in Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_in_Management)[^cite_note-33] and [Master in Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_in_Finance) degrees[^cite_note-34] and the [Sloan Masters in Leadership and Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan_Fellows),[^cite_note-35] as well as a PhD programme.[^cite_note-36]

### Research

The school houses two research institutes: the Institute of Entrepreneurship and Private Capital and the Wheeler Institute for Business and Development.[^cite_note-44] The school submitted 106 [full time equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_time_equivalent) academics to the [business and management studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_and_management_research) panel of the 2021 [Research Excellence Framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Excellence_Framework). In the [Times Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_Higher_Education) analysis of results from this panel, the school was ranked third in the UK for average quality of work submitted, and 15th for research power.[^cite_note-45]
