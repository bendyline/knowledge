**Beersheba** ([/bɪərˈʃiːbə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*beer-SHEE-bə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)), officially **Be'er-Sheva** ([/bɛərˈʃɛvə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*bair-SHEV-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)),[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-4] is the largest city in the [Negev](142273.md) desert of southern [Israel](9282173.md). Often referred to as the "Capital of the Negev", it is the centre of the fourth-most populous metropolitan area in Israel, the [eighth-most populous Israeli city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Israel) with a population of 223,587,[^cite_note-Israelpopulations-1] and the second-largest city in the area (after [Jerusalem](16043.md)), with a total area of 117,500 dunams (117.5 square kilometres).

Human habitation near present-day Beersheba dates back to the fourth millennium BC. In the Bible, Beersheba marks the southern boundary of [ancient Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Israel), as mentioned in the phrase "[From Dan to Beersheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_Dan_to_Beersheba)." Initially assigned to the [Tribe of Judah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Judah), Beersheba was later reassigned to [Simeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Simeon). During the [monarchic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Judah), it functioned as a royal city but eventually faced destruction at the hands of the [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire).[^cite_note-\:02-3] The [Biblical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_archaeology) site of Beersheba is [Tel Be'er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Be'er_Sheva), lying some 4 kilometres (2+1⁄2 miles) distant from the modern city, which was established at the start of the 20th century by the [Ottomans](22278.md).[^cite_note-Berman-5] The city was captured by the British-led [Australian Light Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Light_Horse) troops in the [Battle of Beersheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Beersheba_\(1917\)) during [World War I](4764461.md).

The population of the town was completely changed in 1948–49 during the [First Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War). Beersheba had been almost entirely Muslim, and the 1947 [UN Partition Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine) designated it to be part of the [Arab state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_state). It was occupied by the [Egyptian army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_army) from May 1948 until October 1948 when it was captured by the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) and part of the Arab population [was expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestinian_expulsion_and_flight).[^cite_note-\:0-6] Today, the metropolitan area is composed of approximately equal Jewish and Arab populations, with a large portion of the Jewish population made up of the descendants of [Sephardi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi_Jews) and [Mizrahi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizrahi_Jews) who [fled, relocated or were expelled from Arab countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_exodus_from_the_Muslim_world) after Israel's founding in 1948, as well as smaller communities of [Bene Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bene_Israel) and [Cochin Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochin_Jews) from India. Second and third waves of immigration have taken place since 1990, bringing Russian-speaking immigrants from the former [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) as well as [Beta Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_Israel) immigrants from [Ethiopia](187749.md). The Soviet immigrants have made the game of [chess](5134.md) a major sport in Beersheba, and it is now Israel's national chess center, with more [chess grandmasters](12304.md) per capita than any other city in the world.[^cite_note-7]

Beersheba is home to [Ben-Gurion University of the Negev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben-Gurion_University_of_the_Negev). This city also serves as a center for [Israel's high-tech and developing technology industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_Israel).[^cite_note-globes2-8]

## Etymology

&#x20;The [Book of Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Genesis) gives two [etymologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology) for the name *Be'er Sheba*. [Genesis 21\:28-31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genesis_21) relates:

> Then Abraham set seven ewes apart. And [Abimelech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abimelech) said to Abraham, "What mean these seven ewes, which you have set apart? And \[Abraham] said, "That you are to take these seven (*sheba*) ewes from me, to be for me a witness that I have dug this well (*bǝ'er*)." Therefore the name of that place was Be'er Sheba, for there the two of them had sworn (*nishbǝ'u*).

[Genesis 26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genesis_26) relates:

> And Isaac redug the wells which had been dug in the days of Abraham his father, and which the Philistines had sealed after the death of Abraham, and he used the same names as had his father ... And they arose in the morning, and they swore (*wa-yishabǝ'u*) each to his fellow, and Isaac sent them off, and they departed him in peace. On that same day, Isaac's men came to him to tell him of the well which they had dug, and they said to him, "We found water." And he called it *Shib'a* ("seven" normally, possibly "oath" or a proper noun); therefore the name of the city is Be'er Sheba to this day.[^cite_note-10]

The original Hebrew name could therefore relate to the oath of [Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham) and [Abimelech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abimelech) ('well of the oath') or the seven ewes in that oath ('well of the seven'), as related in [Genesis 21\:31](https://mechon-mamre.org/p/pt/pt0121.htm#31), and/or to the oath of [Isaac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac) and Abimelech in [Genesis 26\:33](https://mechon-mamre.org/p/pt/pt0126.htm#33). Alternatively, [Obadiah Sforno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obadiah_Sforno) suggested that the well is called Seven because it was the seventh dug; the narrative of Genesis 26 includes three wells dug by Abraham which are reopened by Isaac ([Esek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esek), [Sitnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitnah), and [Rehoboth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehoboth_\(Bible\))), for a total of six, after which Isaac goes to Beersheba, the seventh well.[^cite_note-11]

The double name of Shib'a and Beersheba is referenced again by the [Masoretic Text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masoretic_Text) in [Joshua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Joshua) 19\:2,[^cite_note-12] usually translated "Beersheba or Sheba"; however the [Septuagint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septuagint) reads "Beersheba and Samaa (Σαμαὰ)" which fits with MT [1 Chron.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_Chronicles) 4\:28.

[Abraham ibn Ezra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_ibn_Ezra) and [Samuel b. Meir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_ben_Meir) suggest the two etymologies refer to two different cities.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

During the Ottoman administration, the city was referred as بلدية بئرالسبع, "*Belediyet Bi'r alsab'" (Palestinian Arabic meaning "Well of the Seven"), "Baladiyyat Bi'russab" ([Modern Standard Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Standard_Arabic))*.[^cite_note-15]

## Hebrew Bible

Beersheba is mainly dealt with in the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible) in connection with the [Patriarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchs_\(Bible\)) [Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham) and [Isaac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac), who both dug a well and close peace treaties with King [Abimelech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abimelech) of [Gerar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerar) at the site. Hence it receives its name twice, first after Abraham's dealings with Abimelech ([Genesis 21\:22–34](https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Genesis%2021\:22%E2%80%9334\&version=nrsv)), and again from [Isaac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac) who closes his own covenant with Abimelech of Gerar and whose servants also dig a well there ([Genesis 26\:23–33](https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Genesis%2026\:23%E2%80%9333\&version=nrsv)). The place is thus connected to two of the three [wife–sister narratives in the Book of Genesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wife%E2%80%93sister_narratives_in_the_Book_of_Genesis).

According to the Hebrew Bible, Beersheba was founded when Abraham and Abimelech settled their differences over a well of water and made a covenant (see [Genesis 21\:25–34](https://bible.oremus.org/?passage=Genesis%2021\:25%E2%80%9334\&version=nrsv)). Abimelech's men had taken the well from Abraham after he had previously dug it so Abraham brought sheep and cattle to Abimelech to get the well back. He set aside seven lambs to swear that it was he that had dug the well and no one else. Abimelech conceded that the well belonged to Abraham and, according to this account, Beersheba means "Well of Seven" or "Well of the Oath".[^cite_note-16]

Beersheba is further mentioned when Isaac built an altar in Beersheba (Genesis 26\:23–33). Later, [Jacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob) had his dream about a stairway to heaven after leaving Beersheba. (Genesis 28\:10–15 and 46\:1–7). Beersheba was the territory of the [tribe of Simeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Simeon) and [Judah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Judah) ([Joshua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Joshua) 15\:28 and 19\:2). The sons of the prophet [Samuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_\(Bible\)) were judges in Beersheba ([I Samuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Books_of_Samuel) 8\:2). [Saul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul), Israel's first king, built a fort there for his campaign against the [Amalekites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalek) (I Samuel 14\:48 and 15\:2–9). The prophet [Elijah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elijah) took refuge in Beersheba when [Jezebel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezebel_\(Bible\)) ordered him killed ([I Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Books_of_Kings) 19\:3). The prophet [Amos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_\(prophet\)) mentions the city in regard to idolatry ([Amos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Amos) 5\:5 and 8\:14).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-17] Following the [Babylonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_Empire) conquest and subsequent [enslavement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_captivity) of many [Israelites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelites), the town was abandoned. After the Israelite slaves returned from [Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon), they resettled the town. According to the Hebrew Bible, Beersheba was the southernmost city of the territories settled by Israelites, hence the expression "[from Dan to Beersheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/From_Dan_to_Beersheba)", used to describe the whole kingdom.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-17]

[Zibiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zibiah), the [consort](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/consort) of King [Ahaziah of Judah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahaziah_of_Judah) and the mother of King [Jehoash of Judah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehoash_of_Judah),[^cite_note-kings-18] was from Beersheba.

## History

The city has been destroyed and rebuilt many times. Considered unimportant for centuries, Be'er Sheva regained notoriety under [Byzantine rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) (in the 4th–7th century), when it was a key point on the [Limes Palestinae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limes_Palestinae?action=edit\&redlink=1), a defense line built against the desert tribes; however, it fell to the Arabs in the 7th century and to the Turks in the 16th century.

It long remained a watering place and small trade centre for the nomadic Bedouin tribes of the [Negev](142273.md), despite Turkish efforts at town planning and development around 1900. Its capture in 1917 by the British Army opened the way for their conquest of Palestine and Syria. After being taken by Israeli troops in October 1948, Beersheba was rapidly settled by new immigrants and has since developed as the administrative, cultural, and industrial centre of the Negev. It is one of the largest cities in Israel outside of metropolitan [Tel Aviv](31453.md), [Jerusalem](16043.md), and [Haifa](55606.md).

### Chalcolithic

Human settlement in the area dates from the [Copper Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic). The inhabitants lived in caves, crafting metal tools and raising cattle.[^cite_note-19] Findings unearthed at [Tel Be'er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Be'er_Sheva), an [archaeological site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_site) east of modern-day Beersheba, suggest the region has been inhabited since the [4th millennium BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_millennium_BC) (between 5000 and 6,000 years ago).[^cite_note-20]

### Iron Age



Tel Sheva archaeological site

Main article: [Tel Be'er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Be'er_Sheva)

[Tel Be'er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Be'er_Sheva), an archaeological site containing the ruins of an ancient town believed to have been the Biblical Beersheba, lies a few kilometers east of the modern city. The town dates to the early [Israelite period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_ancient_Israel_and_Judah), around the 10th century BC. The site was possibly chosen due to the abundance of water, as evidenced by the numerous wells in the area. According to the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible), the wells were dug by [Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham) and [Isaac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac) when they arrived there. The streets were laid out in a grid, with separate areas for administrative, commercial, military, and residential use. It is believed to have been the first planned settlement in the region, and is also noteworthy for its elaborate water system; in particular, a huge [cistern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cistern) carved out of the rock beneath the town.

### Persian period

During the Persian rule 539 BC–c. 332 BC, Beersheba was at the south of [Yehud Medinata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yehud_Medinata) autonomous province of the Persian [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire). During that era, the city was rebuilt[^cite_note-ynetenc-21] and a citadel had been constructed.[^cite_note-openau-22] Archeological finds from between 359 and 338 BC have been made, finding pottery and an [ostracon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostracon).[^cite_note-openau-22]

### Hellenistic period

During the [Hasmonean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasmonean) rule, the city was not attributed great importance as it was not mentioned when conquered from [Edom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edom) or described in the [Hasmonean wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maccabean_Revolt).[^cite_note-ynetenc-21]

### Roman and Byzantine periods

Around 64-63 BC, the Roman general [Gnaeus Pompeius Magnus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnaeus_Pompeius_Magnus) made Beersheba, known as **Birosaba**, the southern part of the [Judea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea_\(Roman_province\)) province.[^cite_note-Avni-23] During the [Herodian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_the_Great) there was a small settlement in Beersheba. Remains of a Jewish village dating back to the 1st century AD were discovered in the Rakafot neighborhood in the north of the city.[^cite_note-24]

In the following years, the town served as front-line defence against [Nabatean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabatean) attacks and was on the [limes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limes_\(Roman_Empire\)) belt, which in this region is attributed to the time of [Vespasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vespasian) (1st century AD).[^cite_note-limes-25] The city become the centre of an [eparchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eparchy) around 268.[^cite_note-limes-25] During the Roman and Byzantine periods, the city developed significantly and the burial grounds on the outskirts of the city became residential areas. The inhabitants, which consisted of [Nabataeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabataeans), Jews and other ethnicities, spoke primarily Greek and lived from olive oil production, viticulture, agricultural and other trades.[^cite_note-Golan-26]

After the reforms of Diocletian, the town became part of the province of [Palaestina Tertia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaestina_Tertia) and grew to an approximate size of 60 hectares during its peak in the 6th century.[^cite_note-Avni-23] Beersheba was described in the [Madaba Map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madaba_Map) and [Eusebius of Caesarea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusebius_of_Caesarea) as a large village with a Roman garrison.[^cite_note-27] The camp was later identified in aerial photographs taken during the First World War and other structures associated with the camp, such as a bath house and dwellings, were found in later excavations.[^cite_note-Golan-26]

During the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) period, at least six churches were built there, one of which is the largest church to have been excavated in the Negev. Some of the churches were still in use until the [Umayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) period but it remains uncertain whether they continued beyond the early eight century.[^cite_note-Avni-23] Monasticism is also attested in historical documents and one structure has been identified as a monastery.[^cite_note-Golan-26] [Barsanuphius of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barsanuphius_of_Gaza) corresponded with a certain monk of Beersheba, John, who might be identified with [John the Prophet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Prophet), who between 525 and 527 moved to the [monastery of Seridus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastery_of_Seridus) and together with Barsanuphius wrote over 850 letters on spiritual direction.[^cite_note-28]

### Early Muslim period

During the early [Muslim period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilad_al-Sham), some of the Byzantine buildings continued to be used, but there was a slow decline of the city, which was manifested in the demolition of the public buildings and their transformation into a source of raw material for [secondary construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_construction?action=edit\&redlink=1). In the second half of the 8th century, the city was apparently abandoned.[^cite_note-29]

### Mamluk period

In 1483, during the late [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate_\(Cairo\)) era, the pilgrim [Felix Fabri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Fabri) noted Beersheba as a city. Fabri also noted that Beersheba marked the southern-most border of "the Holy Land".[^cite_note-30]

### Ottoman period



Beersheba in 1901



Beersheba from the south in 1902



Beersheba, 1917

The present-day city was built to serve as an administrative center by the [Ottoman](22278.md) administration for the benefit of the Bedouin at the outset of the 20th century and was given the name of *Bir al-Sabi* (well of the seven). Until [World War I](4764461.md), it was an overwhelmingly Muslim township with some 1,000 residents.[^cite_note-Reiter-31] Ben-David and Kressel have argued that the Bedouin traditional market was the cornerstone for the founding of Beersheba as capital of the Negev during this period,[^cite_note-Kressel_Ben-David_1996-32]<sup>: 3 </sup> and Negev Bedouin. Anthropologist and educationalist [Aref Abu-Rabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aref_Abu-Rabia?action=edit\&redlink=1), who worked for the [Israeli Ministry of Education and Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(Israel\)), described it as "the first Bedouin city".[^cite_note-Abu-Rabia-33]<sup>: ix </sup>

In June 1899, the Ottoman government ordered the creation of the Beersheba sub-district (*kaza*) of the district (*mutasarrıflık*) of [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutasarrifate_of_Jerusalem), with Beersheba to be developed as its capital.[^cite_note-Avci-34] Implementation was entrusted to a special bureau of the Ministry of the Interior.[^cite_note-Avci-34] The British incorporation of [Sinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula) into [Egypt](8087628.md) led to a need for the Ottomans to consolidate their hold on southern Palestine.[^cite_note-Avci-34] There was also a desire to encourage the Bedouin to become sedentary, with a predicted increase of tranquility and tax revenue.[^cite_note-Avci-34] The first governor (*[kaymakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaymakam)*), Isma'il Kamal Bey, lived in a tent lent by the local [sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh) until the government house (*Saraya*) was built.[^cite_note-AbuRabia-35] Kamal was replaced by Muhammed Carullah Efendi in 1901, who in turn was replaced by Hamdi Bey in 1903.[^cite_note-Avci-34] The governor in 1908 was promoted to 'adjoint' (*mutassarrıf muavin*) to the governor of the Jerusalem district, which placed him above the other sub-district governors.[^cite_note-Avci-34]

A visitor to Beersheba in May 1900 found only a ruin, a two-storey stone [khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_\(inn\)), and several tents.[^cite_note-Robinson1901-36] By the start of 1901 there was a barracks with a small garrison as well as other buildings.[^cite_note-37] The [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary)-Czech orientalist[^cite_note-38] [Alois Musil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Musil) noted in August 1902:

Bir es-Seba grows from day to day; This year, instead of the tents, we found stately houses along a beautiful road from the Sarayah to the bed of the wadi. In the government building a garden has been laid out, and all sorts of trees have been planted which are sure to prosper, for the few shrubs planted two years ago by the steam mill at the south-east end of the road have grown considerably. The lively construction activity is also causing a lively exploitation of the ruins.

[^cite_note-Musil-39]

By 1907, there was a large village, military post, a residence for the *kaymakam* and a large mosque.[^cite_note-Robinson1908-40] The population increased from 300 to 800 between 1902 and 1911, and by 1914 there were 1,000 people living in 200 houses.[^cite_note-Avci-34]

A plan for the town in the form of a [grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_plan) was developed by a Swiss and a German architect and two others.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] The grid pattern can be seen today in Beersheba's Old City. Most of the residents at the time were [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) from [Hebron](38577.md) and the Gaza area, although Jews also began settling in the city. Many Bedouin abandoned their nomadic lives and built homes in Beersheba.[^cite_note-ariel-43]

### First World War and British Mandate



Beersheba 1938



[Beersheba Turkish Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beersheba_Turkish_Railway_Station)

During [World War I](4764461.md), the Ottomans built a [military railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_to_Beersheba) from the [Hejaz line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz_Railway) to Beersheba, inaugurating the station on October 30, 1915.[^cite_note-cotterell-ch3-44] The celebration was attended by the Ottoman army commander [Jamal Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Djemal) and other senior government officials. The train line was captured by [Allied forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) in 1917, towards the end of the war. Today, it forms part of the [Israeli railway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Railways).

Beersheba played an important role in the [Sinai and Palestine Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_and_Palestine_Campaign) in World War I. The [Battle of Beersheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Beersheba_\(1917\)) was part of a wider British offensive in aimed at breaking the Turkish defensive line from [Gaza](55387.md) to Beersheba. The Ottoman army engaged in three battles with the British forces near Gaza between March 26 and November 7, 1917.[^cite_note-45] Having failed in the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_Gaza) and [Second Battles of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Gaza), the British succeeded in the [Third Battle of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Gaza). On October 31, 1917, three months after taking [Rafah](584373.md), [General Allenby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Allenby)'s troops breached the line of Turkish defense between Gaza and Beersheba.[^cite_note-46] Approximately five-hundred soldiers of the Australian [4th Light Horse Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Light_Horse_Regiment_\(Australia\)) and the [12th Light Horse Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Light_Horse_Regiment_\(Australia\)) of the 4th [Light Horse Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Light_Horse), led by [Brigadier General William Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Grant_\(general\)), with only horses and bayonets, charged the Turkish trenches, overran them and captured the wells in what has become known as the [Battle of Beersheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Beersheba_\(1917\)), called the "last successful cavalry charge in British military history."[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] On the edge of Beersheba's Old City is a [Commonwealth War Graves Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_War_Graves_Commission) Cemetery containing the graves of Australian, New Zealand and British soldiers. The town also contains [a memorial park dedicated to them](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Soldier_Park).

During the [Palestine Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), Beersheba was a major administrative center. The British constructed a railway between [Rafah](584373.md) and Beersheba in October 1917 which opened to the public in May 1918, serving the Negev and settlements south of [Mount Hebron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hebron).[^cite_note-49] In 1928, at the beginning of the tension between the Jews and the Arabs over control of Palestine and wide-scale rioting which left 133 Jews dead and 339 wounded, many Jews abandoned Beersheba, although some returned occasionally. After an Arab attack on a Jewish bus in 1936, which escalated into the [1936–39 Arab revolt in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%9339_Arab_revolt_in_Palestine), the remaining Jews left.[^cite_note-50]

At the time of the [1922 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine), Beersheba had a population of 2,356 (2,012 Muslims, 235 Christians, 98 Jews and 11 [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze)).[^cite_note-Census1922-51] At the time of the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine), Beersheba had 545 occupied houses and a population of 2,959 (2,791 [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim), 152 Christians, 11 Jews and five [Baháʼí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith)).[^cite_note-Census1931-52] The 1938 village survey did not cover Beersheba due to the area's largely nomadic population and the Rural Property Tax Ordinance not being applied there.[^cite_note-53] The 1945 village survey conducted by the Palestine Mandate government found 5,570 (5,360 Muslims, 200 Christians and 10 others).[^cite_note-54]

### 1948 Palestine war

See also: [1948 Palestine war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestine_war), [Operation Yoav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Yoav), and [Battle of Beersheba (1948)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Beersheba_\(1948\))



Beersheba was proposed to be in the Arab State in final version of the [United Nations Partition Plan for Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine)



Harel Brigade assembling in Beersheba prior to Operation Horev, 25 December 1948



[Israel Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Philharmonic_Orchestra) performing for Israeli soldiers in Beersheba, November 1948



Monument to the [Negev Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negev_Brigade), [Danny Karavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Karavan)

In 1947, the [United Nations Special Committee on Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Special_Committee_on_Palestine) (UNSCOP) proposed that Beersheba be included within the Jewish state in their partition plan for Palestine.[^cite_note-UNSCOP-55] However, when the [UN's Ad Hoc Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_Hoc_Committee_on_the_Palestinian_Question) revised the plan, they moved Beersheva to the Arab state on account of it being primarily Arab.[^cite_note-UNSCOP-55][^cite_note-56] Egyptian forces had been stationed at Beersheva since May 1948.

After the Arab states [invaded Palestine and declared war on the newly founded Jewish state of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), [Yigal Allon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yigal_Allon) proposed the conquest of Beersheba,[^cite_note-57] which was approved by Prime Minister [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion). According to Israeli historian [Benny Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Morris), Allon ordered the "conquest of Beersheba, occupation of outposts around it, \[and] demolition of most of the town."[^cite_note-Morris467-58] The objective was to break the Egyptian blockade of Israeli convoys to the Negev. The Egyptian army did not expect an offensive and fled en masse.[^cite_note-googleil-59] Israel bombed the town on October 16.[^cite_note-Abu-Rabia_2-60]

At 4\:00 am on October 21, the 8th Brigade's 89th [battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion) and the [Negev Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negev_Brigade)'s 7th and 9th battalions moved in. Some troops advanced from the [Mishmar HaNegev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishmar_HaNegev) junction, 20 kilometres (12 mi) north of Beersheba and others from the Turkish train station and [Hatzerim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatzerim). By 9\:45, Beersheba was in Israeli hands. Around 120 Egyptian soldiers were taken prisoner. All of the Arab inhabitants who had resisted were expelled.[^cite_note-Reiter-31] The remaining Arab civilians, 200 men and 150 women and children, were taken to the police fort and, on October 25, the women, children, disabled and elderly were driven by truck to the Gaza border. The Egyptian soldiers were interned in [POW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POW) camps. Some men lived in the local mosque and were put to work cleaning, however, when it was discovered that they were supplying information to the Egyptian army, they were also deported.[^cite_note-Morris467-58]

The town was subject to large-scale looting by the [Haganah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haganah), and by December, in one calculation, the total number of Arabs driven out from Beersheva and surrounding areas reached 30,000 with many ending up in [Jordan](7515964.md) as refugees.[^cite_note-Abu-Rabia_2-60][^cite_note-61]

In February 2026 *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)* reported on a newly discovered court testimony made by [Yisrael Carmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yisrael_Carmi), a battalion commander in the 7th Brigade, wherein he described the capture of Beersheba and stated that the method was to kill civilians who resisted expulsion:[^cite_note-62]

> "I conquered the city," Carmi testified. "In mopping up that area, I gave an order to annihilate anyone who appeared in the street, whether they resisted or did not resist. An order was given to destroy everything. After the conquest of the police station – after the surrender – the murder stopped. Until then everyone was killed – women and children and everyone. Then an order was given to the people to go to [Hebron](38577.md). Anyone who didn't go was 'removed'" (quotation marks in the original).

Following [Operation Yoav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Yoav), a 10-kilometer radius exclusion zone around Beersheba was enforced into which no Bedouin were allowed.[^cite_note-63] In response, the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) passed two resolutions on the November 4 and 16 demanding that Israel withdraw from the area.[^cite_note-64]

### Israel

#### First four decades

Following the conclusion of the war, the [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements) formally granted Beersheba to Israel. The town was then transformed into an Israeli city with only an exiguous Arab minority.[^cite_note-Reiter-31] Beersheba was deemed strategically important due to its location with a reliable water supply and at a major crossroads, northeast to Hebron and [Jerusalem](16043.md), east to the [Dead Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Sea) and [al Karak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Karak), south to [Aqaba](328399.md), west to Gaza and southwest to [Al-Auja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auja_al-Hafir) and the border with [Egypt](8087628.md).[^cite_note-googleil-59]

After a few months, the town's war-damaged houses were repaired. As a post-independence wave of Jewish immigration to Israel began, Beersheba experienced a population boom as thousands of immigrants moved in. The city rapidly expanded beyond its core, which became known as the "Old City", as new neighborhoods were built around it, complete with various housing projects such as apartment buildings and houses with auxiliary farms, as well as shopping centers and schools. The Old City was turned into a city center, with shops, restaurants, and government and utility offices. An industrial area and one of the largest cinemas in Israel were also built in the city. By 1956, Beersheba was a booming city of 22,000.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] In 1959, during the [Wadi Salib riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Salib_riots), riots spread quickly to other parts of the country, including Beersheba.[^cite_note-67]

[Soroka Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soroka_Medical_Center) opened its doors in 1960. By 1968, the population had grown to 80,000.[^cite_note-68] The University of the Negev, which would later become Ben-Gurion University of the Negev, was established in 1969. The then [Egyptian president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Presidents_of_Egypt) [Anwar Sadat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_Sadat) visited Beersheba in 1979. In 1983, its population was more than 110,000. During the [1990s post-Soviet aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990s_post-Soviet_aliyah), the city's population greatly increased as many immigrants from the former Soviet Union settled there.

#### Urban development in the 21st century



Beersheba in the mid-1980s

As part of its [Blueprint Negev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blueprint_Negev) project, the [Jewish National Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_National_Fund) funded major redevelopment projects in Beersheba. One such project is the Beersheba River Walk, a 900-acre (3.6-square-kilometre) riverfront park stretching along 8 kilometers of the riverside and containing a 15-acre (6.1-hectare) manmade boating lake, a 12,000-seat amphitheater, green spaces, playgrounds, and a bridge along the route of the city's [Mekorot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekorot) water pipes.[^cite_note-park-69] The Beersheba River had previously been used as a dumping site and filled with untreated wastewater. After the renovation, the river was transformed and now flows with high-quality purified wastewater.[^cite_note-\:0-6] At the official entrance to the river park is the Beit Eshel Park, which consists of a park built around a courtyard with historic remains from the settlement of [Beit Eshel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Eshel).[^cite_note-blueprint-70]



Panorama of Beersheba



Pipes Bridge, 2012



Modern Beersheba

Four new shopping malls were also built. Among them is Kanyon Beersheba, a 115,000-square-metre (1,240,000-square-foot) ecologically planned mall with pools for collecting rainwater and lighting generated by solar panels on the roof. It will be situated next to an 8,000-meter park with bicycle paths.[^cite_note-blueprint-70][^cite_note-71] In addition, the first ever farmer's market in Israel was established as an enclosed, circular complex with 400 spaces for vendors surrounded by parks and greenery.[^cite_note-blueprint-70]

A new central bus station was built in the city. The station has a glass-enclosed complex also containing shops and cafés.[^cite_note-blueprint-70]

Some \$10.5 million was also invested in renovating Beersheba's Old City, preserving historical buildings and upgrading infrastructure.[^cite_note-72] The Turkish Quarter was also redeveloped with newly cobbled streets, widened sidewalks, and the restoration of Turkish homes into areas for dining and shopping.[^cite_note-park-69]

In 2011, city hall announced plans to turn Beersheba into the "water city" of Israel.[^cite_note-73] One of the projects, "Beersheva beach", is a 7-[dunam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam#Syria,_Israel,_Palestine,_Jordan,_Lebanon_and_Turkey) fountain opposite city hall.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] Other projects included fountains near the [Soroka Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soroka_Medical_Center) and in front of the Shamoon College of Engineering.

In the 1990s, as skyscrapers began to appear in Israel, the construction of high-rise buildings began in Beersheba.[^cite_note-76] Today, downtown Beersheba has been described as a "clean, compact, and somewhat sterile-looking collection of high-rise office and residential towers."[^cite_note-77] The city's tallest building is Rambam Square 2, a 32-story apartment building.[^cite_note-78] Many additional high-rise buildings are planned or are under construction, including skyscrapers.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] There are further plans to build luxury residential towers in the city.[^cite_note-82]

In December 2012, a plan to build 16,000 new housing units in the Ramot Gimel neighborhood was scrapped in favor of creating a new [urban forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_forest), which spans 1,360 acres (550 ha) and serves as the area's "green lung", as part of the plans to develop a "green band" around the city. The forest includes designated picnic areas, biking trails, and walking trails. According to Mayor [Ruvik Danilovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruvik_Danilovich), Beersheba still has an abundance of open, underdeveloped spaces that can be used for urban development.[^cite_note-83]

In 2017, a new urban building plan was approved for the city, designed to raise the city's population to 340,000 by 2030. Under the plan, 13,000 more housing units will be built, along with industrial and business developments occupying a total of four million square meters. A second public hospital is also planned.[^cite_note-globes-84] Planning for the [Beersheba Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beersheba_Light_Rail) also began.[^cite_note-85] In 2019, the construction of a new public hospital, which will be named after [Shimon Peres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimon_Peres), was approved. The hospital will be a 345-acre (140 ha) complex that will feature 1,900 beds, commerce, hotel, alternative medicine, and paramedical services, and research centers, with the possibility of apartment units for medical faculty employees, students, and senior housing. It will be linked to the rest of the city by a light rail system.[^cite_note-86]

In 2021, an outline plan was approved for the construction of 34,000 housing units in the city to increase the population to 400,000, as well as the construction of 4 million square meters of office and commercial space, 3 million square meters of industrial space, 2.7 million square meters of space in public buildings, and 370,000 square meters of space for the tourism industry. One of the primary goals of the plan is to boost connections between neighborhoods through a continuous network of streets which will be shaded and give preference to public transport and pedestrians. Under the plan, construction in the city center will be boosted and Rager Boulevard, which the plan identifies as the city's main avenue, will be turned from a multi-lane road into an urban avenue with expanded residential construction alongside it.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

#### Security incidents in the city

On October 19, 1998, sixty-four people were wounded in a [grenade](20353360.md) attack.[^cite_note-89] On August 31, 2004, sixteen people were killed in [two suicide bombings on commuter buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beersheba_bus_bombings) in Beersheba for which [Hamas](13913.md) claimed responsibility. On August 28, 2005, another [suicide bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_bomber) attacked the central bus station, seriously injuring two security guards and 45 bystanders.[^cite_note-90] During [Operation Cast Lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_\(2008%E2%80%9309\)), which began on December 27, 2008, and lasted until the ceasefire on January 18, 2009, Hamas fired 2,378 rockets (such as [Grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BM-21_Grad) rockets) and mortars, from Gaza into southern Israel, including Beersheba. The rocket attacks have continued, but have been only partially effective since the introduction of the [Iron Dome](18342427.md) rocket defense system.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

In 2010, an Arab attacked and injured two people with an axe.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] In 2012, a Palestinian from [Jenin](50997.md) was stopped before a stabbing attack in a "safe house".[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] On October 18, 2015, a lone gunman [shot and killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Beersheba_bus_station_shooting) a soldier guarding the Beersheva bus station before being gunned down by police.[^cite_note-100] In September 2016, the [Shin Bet](311752.md) thwarted a [Palestinian Islamic Jihad](13093253.md) terror attack at a wedding hall in Beersheba.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

On March 22, 2022, a convicted [Islamic State](9087364.md) supporter carried out a [stabbing and vehicle-ramming attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Beersheba_attack), killing four people and injuring two others.[^cite_note-103]

During the [Gaza war](74998519.md), the city became the target of several rocket attacks.[^cite_note-104]

During the [Iran–Israel war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War) in 2025, the city was targeted by Iran. On June 19, the [Soroka Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soroka_Medical_Center) was struck by an Iranian ballistic missile, destroying the hospital's surgical ward, causing widespread destruction to nearby buildings and injuring at least 80 people.[^cite_note-105] On June 24, after the ceasefire agreement came into effect, Iran launched missiles towards a residential building in the city, killing 5 civilians and injuring 20.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

On March 29, 2026, 11 people were injured as a result of a missile strike during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), with a further 20 treated for "acute anxiety" by [Magen David Adom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magen_David_Adom).[^cite_note-108]

## Emblem of Beersheba



Beersheva emblem on a 1965 stamp

Since 1950, Beersheba has changed its municipal emblem several times. The 1950 emblem, designed by Abraham Khalili, featured a [tamarix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamarix) tree, a factory and water flowing from a pipeline.[^cite_note-city_sign-109] In 1972, the emblem was modernized with the symbolic representation of the [Twelve Tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Tribes) and a tower.[^cite_note-city_sign-109] Words from the Bible are inscribed: Abraham "planted a tamarisk tree in Beersheba." (Genesis 21\:33) Since 2012, it has incorporated the number seven as part of the city rebranding.

## Geography



Dry riverbed in Nahal Ashan park

Beersheba is located on the northern edge of the Negev desert 115 kilometres (71 mi) south-east of [Tel Aviv](31453.md) and 120 kilometres (75 mi) south-west of Jerusalem. The city is located on the main route from the center and north of the country to [Eilat](66162.md) in the far south. The [Beersheba Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beersheba_Valley?action=edit\&redlink=1) has been populated for thousands of years, as it has available water, which flows from the Hebron hills in the winter and is stored underground in vast quantities.[^cite_note-geog-110] The main river in Beersheba is [Nahal Be'er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahal_Be'er_Sheva), a stream that flows year round and occasionally floods in the winter. The Kovshim and Katef streams are other important wadis that pass through the city. Beersheba is surrounded by several satellite towns, including [Omer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omer,_Israel), [Lehavim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehavim), and [Meitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meitar), and the Bedouin localities of [Rahat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahat), [Tel as-Sabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_as-Sabi), and [Lakiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakiya). Just northwest of the city (near Ramot neighborhood) is a region called Goral hills (heb\:גבעות גורל lit: hills of fate), the area has hills with up to 500 metres (1,600 feet) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level) and low as 300 metres (980 feet) above sea level.[^cite_note-111] Due to heavy construction the flora unique to the area is endangered. Northeast of the city (north to the Neve Menahem neighborhood) there are [Loess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loess) plains and dry river bands.

### Climate

Beersheba has a [hot arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_arid_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BWh*) bordering upon a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) (*BSh*) though with [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) influences. The city has characteristics of both Mediterranean and desert climates. Summers are hot and dry, and winters are mild. Rainfall is highly concentrated in the winter season. In summer, the temperatures are high in daytime and nighttime with an average high of 34.7 °C (94 °F) and an average low of 21.4 °C (71 °F). Winters have an average high of 17.7 °C (64 °F) and average low of 7.1 °C (45 °F). Snow is very rare; a snowfall on February 20, 2015, was the first such occurrence in the city since 2000.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

Precipitation in summer is rare, most rainfalls come in winter between September and May, but the annual amount is low, averaging 195.1 millimeters (7.7 in) per year. There are sandstorms in summer. Haze and fog are common in winter, as a result of high humidity.

## Economy



Negev Mall Tower

The largest employers in Beersheba are [Soroka Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soroka_Medical_Center),[^cite_note-131] the municipality, [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) and [Ben-Gurion University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben-Gurion_University_of_the_Negev). A major [Israel Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Aerospace_Industries) complex is located in the main industrial zone, north of [Highway 60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_60_\(Israel\)). Numerous electronics and chemical plants, including [Teva Pharmaceutical Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teva_Pharmaceutical_Industries), are located in and around the city.

Beersheba is emerging as a high-tech center, with an emphasis on cyber security.[^cite_note-globes2-8] A large high-tech park was built near the [Be'er Sheva North Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be'er_Sheva_North_Railway_Station) in 2012[^cite_note-autogenerated2-132] and a fifth commercial building begun to be constructed. [Deutsche Telekom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Telekom), [Elbit Systems](1341669.md), [EMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMC_Corporation), [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin), [Ness Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ness_Technologies), [WeWork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WeWork) and [RAD Data Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAD_Data_Communications) have opened facilities there, as has a cyberincubator run by [Jerusalem Venture Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Venture_Partners).[^cite_note-133] A [Science park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_park) funded by the RASHI-SACTA Foundation, Beersheba Municipality and private donors was completed in 2008.[^cite_note-autogenerated2-132] Another high-tech park is located north of the city near [Omer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omer,_Israel).

An additional three industrial zones are located on the southeastern side of the city – Makhteshim, Emek Sara and Kiryat Yehudit – and a light industry zone between Kiryat Yehudit and the Old City.

## Neighborhoods

Main article: [Neighborhoods of Beersheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_of_Beersheba)

After Israeli independence, Beersheba became a "laboratory" for [Israeli architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Israel).[^cite_note-haaretz.com-138] *Mishol Girit,* a neighborhood built in the late 1950s, was the first attempt to create an alternative to the standard public housing projects in Israel. *Hashatiah* (literally, "the carpet"), also known as *Hashekhuna ledugma* (the model neighborhood), was hailed by architects around the world.[^cite_note-haaretz.com-138] Today, Beersheba is divided into seventeen residential neighborhoods in addition to the Old City and Ramot, an umbrella neighborhood of four sub-districts. Many of the neighbourhoods are named after letters of the [Hebrew alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_alphabet), which also have numerical value, but descriptive place names have been given to some of the newer neighborhoods.

## Culture



Keren Cinema, first movie theater in the Negev

In 1953, Cinema Keren, the Negev's first movie theater, opened in Beersheba. It was built by the [Histadrut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histadrut) and had seating for 1,200 people.[^cite_note-139] Beersheba is the home base of the Israel Sinfonietta, founded in 1973. Over the years, the Sinfonietta has developed a broad repertoire of symphonic works, concerti for solo instruments and large choral productions, among them [Handel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handel)'s *[Israel in Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_in_Egypt),* masses by [Schubert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schubert) and [Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozart), [Rossini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rossini)'s "Stabat Mater" and [Vivaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Vivaldi)'s "Gloria". World-famous artists have appeared as soloists with the Sinfonietta, including [Pinchas Zukerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinchas_Zukerman), [Jean-Pierre Rampal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Rampal), [Shlomo Mintz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shlomo_Mintz), [Gary Karr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Karr), and [Paul Tortelier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tortelier).[^cite_note-140] In the 1970s, a memorial commemorating fallen Israeli soldiers designed by the sculptor [Danny Karavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dani_Karavan) was erected on a hill north-east of the city.[^cite_note-141] The [Beersheba Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beersheba_Theater) opened in 1973. The Light Opera Group of the Negev, established in 1980, performs musicals in English every year.[^cite_note-142]

Landmarks in the city include "[Abraham's well](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham's_well)", a well dating to at least the 12th century CE (now inside a visitors center), and the old Turkish railway station, now the focus of development plans.[^cite_note-143] The Artists House of the Negev, in a Mandate-era building, showcases artwork connected in some way to the Negev.[^cite_note-144]

The [Negev Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negev_Museum_of_Art) reopened in 2004 in the Ottoman Governor's House, and an art and media center for young people was established in the Old City.

In 2009, a new tourist and [information center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visitor_center), Gateway to the Negev, was built.[^cite_note-Lubliner-145]

In 2024, [Midbarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midbarium), a desert zoo and amusement park was opened, replacing the [NegevZoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NegevZoo).[^cite_note-jp-146]

### Great Mosque of Beersheba

The Great Mosque of Beersheba in 1948

The Great Mosque of Beersheba in 1948

In 1906, during the [Ottoman era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_era), the Great Mosque of Beersheba was built with donations collected from the Bedouin residents in the Negev. It was used actively as a mosque until the city fell to Israeli forces in 1948.[^cite_note-Al_Akhbar_2014-147] The mosque was used until 1953 as the city's courthouse. From then until the 1990s, when it was closed for renovations, the building housed an archeological museum, which the city intended to turn into the archeological branch of the Negev Museum.[^cite_note-148] In 2011, however, the [Supreme Court of Israel](1666510.md), sitting as the [High Court of Justice](1666510.md#High_Court_of_Justice), ordered the property to be turned into a museum of [Islam](6037917.md) without reverting to a place of worship.[^cite_note-149]

## Transportation

Main article: [Transport in Beersheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Beersheba)

Beersheba is the central transport hub of southern Israel, served by roads, railways and air. Beersheba is connected to [Tel Aviv](31453.md) via [Highway 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_40_\(Israel\)), the second longest highway in Israel, which passes to the east of the city and is called the Beersheba bypass because it allows travellers from the north to go to southern locations, avoiding the more congested city center. From west to east, the city is divided by [Highway 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_25_\(Israel\)), which connects to [Ashkelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkelon) and the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) to the northwest, and [Dimona](9103.md) to the east. Finally, Highway 60 connects Beersheba with Jerusalem and the Shoket Junction, and goes through the [West Bank](33209.md). On the local level, a partial [ring road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beltway) surrounds the city from the north and east, and Road 406 (Rager Blvd.) goes through the city center from north to south.

[Metrodan Beersheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrodan_Beersheba), established in 2003, had a fleet of 90 buses and operates 19 lines in the city between 2003 and 2016, most of which depart from the Beersheba Central Bus Station.[^cite_note-150] These lines were formerly operated by the municipality as the 'Be'er Sheva Urban Bus Services'. Inter-city buses to and from Beersheba are operated by [Egged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egged_Bus_Cooperative), [Dan BaDarom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_BaDarom) and [Metropoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropoline).[^cite_note-151] The intercity bus service was transferred to [Dan Be'er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Be'er_Sheva?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 25'th of November 2016 and Metrodan Beersheva had been shut down. With the change to Dan Be'er Sheva the company introduced electronic payment stopping pay at the driver which was common in Beersheba.[^cite_note-152]



Mexico Bridge from railway station to Ben-Gurion University

[Israel Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Railways) operates two stations in the city that form part of the [railway to Beersheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_to_Beersheba): the old [Be'er Sheva North University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be'er_Sheva_North_Railway_Station) station, adjacent to Ben Gurion University and [Soroka Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soroka_Medical_Center), and the new [Be'er Sheva Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be'er_Sheva_Center_Railway_Station) station, adjacent to the central bus station. Between the two stations, the railway splits into two, and also continues to Dimona and [the Dead Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Sea) factories. An extension is planned to Eilat[^cite_note-eilat_rail-153] and [Arad](310435.md).

The Be'er Sheva North University station is the terminus of the line to Dimona. All stations of Israel Railways can be accessed from Beersheba using [transfer stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interchange_station) in Tel Aviv and [Lod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lod). Until 2012, the [railway line to Beersheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_to_Beersheba) used a slow single-track configuration with sharp curves and many [level crossings](193595.md) which limited train speed. Between 2004 and 2012 the line was double tracked and rebuilt using an improved alignment and all its level crossings were [grade separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_separation). The rebuilding effort cost [NIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_new_shekel) 2.8 billion and significantly reduced the travel time and greatly increased the train frequency to and from Tel Aviv and [Kiryat Motzkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiryat_Motzkin) to Beersheba.[^cite_note-154] In addition, Beersheba will be linked to Tel Aviv and Eilat by a new passenger and freight [high-speed railway system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_speed_railway_to_Eilat).[^cite_note-155]

The [Beersheba Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beersheba_Light_Rail) is currently planned as a [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) system for the city of Beersheba and outlying communities. There have been plans for a light rail system in Beersheba for many years, and a light rail system appears in the master plan for the city.[^cite_note-156] An agreement was signed for the construction of a light rail system in 1998, but was not implemented. In 2008, the [Israeli Finance Ministry](7088294.md) contemplated freezing the [Tel Aviv Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Light_Rail) project and building a light rail system in Beersheba instead, but that did not happen. In 2014, mayor [Ruvik Danilovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruvik_Danilovich) announced that the light rail system will be built in the city.[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] In 2017, the Ministry of Transport gave the Beersheba municipality approval to proceed with preliminary planning on a light rail system.[^cite_note-160] In August 2023, the light rail was officially approved. It is expected to be completed by 2033.[^cite_note-161]

### Roundabouts



&#x20;Harp statue, Artzieli Square, 2019

In Be'er Sheva, there are over 250 roundabouts, giving the city its nickname of "Roundabout Capital of Israel". Many roundabouts, part of Be'er-Sheva's urban oasis project, include fountains, landscaping and sculptures by well-known artists (such as [Menashe Kadishman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menashe_Kadishman)'s The Horse Circle and [Jeremy Langford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Langford_\(sculptor\))'s The Drip Circle). Some commemorate famous people and international and local organizations, or mark important events. Some are named after the twin cities of Beer Sheva.[^cite_note-162]

Well-known roundabouts are: [Ilan Ramon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilan_Ramon) Circle, [Phantom](11759.md) Circle near the Air Force Technical School, Champions Square near [Turner Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Stadium) and [Conch Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conch_Arena), [Chess](5134.md) Circle, Harp Circle near the Municipal Conservatory and the Be'er-Sheva Performing Arts Center, College Circle, [Ben Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Gurion) Circle, Light Circle, [Freemasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasons) Circle, [Shofarot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shofar) Circle, [Twin Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(1973%E2%80%932001\)) Circle.

## Sports

[Hapoel Be'er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hapoel_Be'er_Sheva_F.C.) plays in the [Israeli Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Premier_League), the top tier of [Israeli football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Israel), having been promoted in the [2008–2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Liga_Leumit) [Liga Leumit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Leumit) season. The club has won the Israeli championship five times, in 1975, 1976, 2016, 2017 and 2018, as well as the [State Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_State_Cup) in 1997, 2020 and 2022. Beersheba has two other local clubs, [Maccabi Be'er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maccabi_Be'er_Sheva_F.C.) (based in [Neve Noy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_of_Beersheba#Neve_Noy)) and [F.C. Be'er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F.C._Be'er_Sheva) (based in the north of [Dalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_of_Beersheba#Dalet)), a continuation of the defunct [Beitar Avraham Be'er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beitar_Avraham_Be'er_Sheva_F.C.). Hapoel play at the [Turner Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Stadium).

Beersheba has a basketball club, [Hapoel Be'er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hapoel_Be'er_Sheva_B.C.). The team plays at The [Conch Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conch_Arena), which seats 3,000.

Beersheba has become Israel's national [chess](5134.md) center; thanks to Soviet immigration, it is home to the largest number of [chess grandmasters](12304.md) of any city in the world.[^cite_note-163] The city hosted the [World Team Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Team_Chess_Championship) in 2005, and chess is taught in the city's kindergartens.[^cite_note-164] The Israeli chess team won the silver medal at the [2008 Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/38th_Chess_Olympiad)[^cite_note-165] and the bronze at the [2010 Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/39th_Chess_Olympiad). The chess club was founded in 1973 by Eliyahu Levant, who served as its director for the next 40 years.[^cite_note-166]

The city has the second largest [wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling) center (AMI wrestling school) in Israel.  The center is run by Leonid Shulman and has approximately 2,000 students, most of whom are from Russian immigrant families since the origins of the club are in the [Nahal Beka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_of_Beersheba#Nahal_Beka) immigrant absorption center. Maccabi Be'er Sheva has a freestyle wrestling team, whilst Hapoel Be'er Sheva has a Greco-Roman wrestling team. In the [2010 World Wrestling Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_World_Wrestling_Championships), AMI students won five medals.[^cite_note-167] Cricket is played under the auspices of [Israel Cricket Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Cricket_Association). Beersheba is also home to a [rugby](25405.md) team, whose senior and youth squads have won several national titles (including the recent Senior National League 2004–2005 championship).[^cite_note-168] Beersheba's tennis center, which opened in 1991, features eight lighted courts, and the Beersheba (Teyman) airfield is used for [gliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gliding).

## Environmental awards

In 2012, the Beersheba "ring trail", a 42-kilometer hiking trail around the city, won third place in the annual environmental competition of the European Travelers Association.[^cite_note-169]

## Voting Patterns

In the [2022 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Israeli_legislative_election), 66 percent of voters in Beersheba voted for the parties of the right-wing coalition led by [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md), while 30 percent voted for the parties of the center-left coalition led by [Yair Lapid](2448072.md) and 1 percent voted for the Arab parties.[^cite_note-170]
