**HD 80606** and **HD 80607** are two [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star) comprising a [binary star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binary_star) system. They are 215 [light-years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-year) away in the [constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation) of [Ursa Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursa_Major). Both stars orbit each other at an average distance of 1,200 [astronomical units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_unit). The binary system is listed as **Struve 1341** in the [Struve Catalogue of Double Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Struve_Catalogue_of_Double_Stars); however, this designation is not in wide use and the system is usually referred to by the [HD designations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Draper_Catalogue) of its constituent stars. An [extrasolar planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrasolar_planet) has been confirmed to orbit HD 80606 in a [highly elliptical orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highly_elliptical_orbit).

## Stellar system

The binary system components are very similar [G-type main-sequence stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-type_main-sequence_star) with similar properties to the [Sun](26751.md), although both are older than the Sun and approaching the very end of their [main sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_sequence) lives.[^cite_note-dr3a-4][^cite_note-dr3b-2]

## Planetary system



The orbital motion of HD 80606 b

The variable radial velocity of HD 80606 was first noticed in 1999 from observations with the 10 m Keck 1 telescope at the [W. M. Keck Observatory](203314.md) in Hawaii by the G-Dwarf Planet Search, a survey of nearly 1,000 nearby [G dwarfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-type_main-sequence_star) to identify extrasolar planet candidates. The star was then followed up by the [Geneva Extrasolar Planet Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Extrasolar_Planet_Search) team using the [ELODIE spectrograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELODIE_spectrograph) mounted on the 1.93 m telescope at the [Haute-Provence Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Provence_Observatory). The discovery of [HD 80606 b](16796898.md) was announced on April 4, 2001.[^cite_note-Naef2001-11][^cite_note-eso0114-13] Its orbit is misaligned with the star's rotation at 53 degrees.[^cite_note-moutou2009-14][^cite_note-roberts2012-15] Additional studies using the Spitzer Space Telescope in the infrared, and the Very Large Array in the millimeter radio, have shown that the highly eccentric planet 'b' orbiting HD 80606 grazes the parent star at its closest passage to produce difficult-to-detect stellar lobing, severe 'space weather', aurorae and other non-thermal activity.[^cite_note-spitzer-16][^cite_note-weather-17][^cite_note-lazio2010-18] At the time, its orbit was the most [eccentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eccentricity_\(orbit\)) orbit of any extrasolar planet known.[^cite_note-19] It has an eccentricity of 0.9336,[^cite_note-fossey2009-7] comparable to that of [Comet Halley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comet_Halley) in the [Solar System](26903.md). The eccentricity may be a result of the [Kozai mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozai_mechanism), which would occur if the planet's orbit is significantly inclined to that of the binary stars. This conclusion is reinforced by the detection of the misalignment, an expected result of the Kozai mechanism.[^cite_note-moutou2009-14]

In a simulation of a ten-million-year span, the planet "sweeps clean" most test particles within 1.75 AU of HD 80606. The 8\:1 resonance hollows out another [Kirkwood gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkwood_gap) at 1.9 AU. There cannot be any habitable planets in this system. Also, observation has ruled out planets heavier than 0.7 [Jupiter masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter_mass) with a period of one year or less.[^cite_note-Wittenmyer2007-20]
