*The Cannon Shot* (1670) by [Willem van de Velde the Younger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_van_de_Velde_the_Younger), showing a late Dutch 17th-century [ship of the line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_the_line)

A **warship** or **combatant ship** is a [naval ship](53159.md) that is used for [naval warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_warfare). Usually they belong to the [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) branch of the [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_forces) of a [nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation),[^cite_note-UNCLOSW-1] though they have also been operated by individuals, [cooperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative) and [corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporation). As well as being armed, warships are designed to withstand damage and are typically faster and more maneuverable than [merchant ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_ships). Unlike a merchant ship, which carries cargo, a warship typically carries only weapons, ammunition and supplies for its crew.

In wartime, the distinction between warships and merchant ships is often blurred. Until the 17th century it was common for merchant ships to be pressed into naval service, and not unusual for more than half of a [fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_fleet) to be composed of merchant ships—there was not a large difference in construction, unlike the difference between a heavily armoured [battleship](4054.md) and an ocean liner. Until the threat of [piracy](50715.md) subsided in the 19th century, it was normal practice to arm larger [merchant ships](875717.md) such as [galleons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galleon). Warships have also often been used as [troop carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troopship) or supply ships, such as by the [French Navy](383256.md) in the 18th century or the [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy) during the Second World War. In war since the early 20th century, merchant ships were often armed and used as [auxiliary warships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_merchantman), such as the [Q-ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-ship) of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) and the [armed merchant cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_merchant_cruiser) of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War).

## Types and classes

The main types of warships today are, in order of decreasing size: [aircraft carriers](2219.md) – [amphibious assault ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship) – [cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser) – [destroyers](8779.md) – [frigates](11464.md) – [corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette) – [fast attack boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_attack_craft).[^cite_note-2] A more extensive list follows:

See also: [List of naval ship classes in service § Warships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_naval_ship_classes_in_service#Warships)

- [Submarine](28825.md), are ships capable of staying submerged for days. Modern submarines can stay underwater for months, with food supplies as the only limiting factor.&#x20;

  - [Fleet submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_submarine) is a type of submarine with the speed, range, and endurance to operate as part of a navy's battle [fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_fleet).

  - [Ballistic missile submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile_submarine) is a submarine capable of deploying [submarine-launched ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine-launched_ballistic_missiles) (SLBMs) with [nuclear warheads](21785.md).

  - [Cruise missile submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_missile_submarine) are submarines equipped with [cruise missiles](6590.md).

  - [Attack submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_Submarine) is a submarine with the purpose of attacking other submarines.

  - [Coastal submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_submarine) or littoral submarine is a small, maneuverable type of submarine with shallow draft well suited to navigation of coastal channels and harbors.

  - [Midget submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midget_submarine) is any submarine under 150 tons, typically operated by a crew of one or two but sometimes up to six or nine.

  - [Submarine aircraft carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_aircraft_carrier) is a submarine equipped with aircraft for observation or attack missions. These submarines saw their most extensive use during World War II, although their operational significance remained small.

  - [Cruiser submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser_submarine) were a type of a very large submarine designed to remain at sea for extended periods in areas distant from base facilities.

- [Amphibious warfare ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare_ship) are warships employed to land and support ground forces, such as [marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marines), on enemy territory during an [amphibious assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault).&#x20;

  - [Amphibious assault ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ship) is a type of amphibious warfare ship employed to land and support ground forces with ship-deployed [helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_helicopter) and [V/STOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V/STOL) aircraft on enemy territory by an amphibious assault.&#x20;

    - [Landing helicopter dock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_helicopter_dock) is a multipurpose amphibious assault ship that is capable of operating as a [helicopter carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_carrier) and also has a [well deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well_deck) for supporting [landing crafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_craft).

  - [Amphibious transport dock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_transport_dock) is an amphibious warfare ship, that embarks, transports, and lands elements of a landing force for expeditionary warfare missions.

  - [Dock landing ship](641435.md) is an amphibious warfare ship with a [well dock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Well_dock) to transport and launch [landing craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_craft) and [amphibious vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_vehicle#Armored).

  - [Landing craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_craft) are small and medium seagoing watercraft, such as [boats](3996.md) and [barges](4177.md), used to convey a landing force (infantry and vehicles) from the sea to the shore during an amphibious assault.&#x20;

    - [Landing craft utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_craft_utility) is a type of landing craft used by amphibious forces to transport equipment and troops to the shore. They are capable of transporting tracked or wheeled vehicles and marines from amphibious assault ships to [beachheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beachhead).

    - [Landing craft mechanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_craft_mechanized) is a landing craft designed for carrying [vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_vehicles) during amphibious assaults.

    - [Landing ship, tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_ship,_tank) is the naval designation for ships first developed during [World War II](32927.md) to support amphibious operations by carrying [tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanks), [armoured fighting vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_fighting_vehicle), [transport vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_vehicle), cargo, and landing [troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troops) directly onto shore with no [docks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dock) or [piers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier).

  - [Landing craft support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_craft_support) were two distinct classes of amphibious warfare vessels used by the [United States Navy](20518076.md) during World War II to support landing crafts.

- [Capital ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_ship), the largest and most important ships in a nation's fleet. These were previously battlecruisers, battleships, and aircraft carriers, but the first two warship types are now no longer used.&#x20;

  - [Aircraft carrier](2219.md), a warship primarily armed with [carrier-based aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrier-based_aircraft).&#x20;

    - [Fleet carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_carrier) is an aircraft carrier designed to operate with the main fleet of a nation's navy.

    - [Light aircraft carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_aircraft_carrier) is an aircraft carrier that is smaller than the standard carriers of a navy.

    - [Escort carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escort_carrier), also called a "jeep carrier" or "baby flattop" is a slow type of aircraft carrier used during WWII.

    - [Anti-submarine warfare carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare_carrier) is a type of small aircraft carrier whose primary role is as the nucleus of an [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) [hunter-killer group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-killer_group).

    - [Aircraft cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_cruiser) (also known as aviation cruiser or cruiser-carrier) is a type of warship that combines the features of the aircraft carrier and a surface warship, such as a cruiser or battleship.&#x20;

      - [Helicopter cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_cruiser).

    - [Helicopter carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_carrier), an aircraft carrier especially suited to carry [helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_helicopter) and [V/STOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V/STOL) aircraft.

    - [Seaplane tender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplane_tender), a [boat](3996.md) or [ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship) that supports the operation of [seaplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplane). Some of these vessels, also known as seaplane carriers, could not only carry seaplanes but also provided all the facilities needed for their operation; these ships are regarded by some as the first [aircraft carriers](2219.md) and became obsolete at the end of the Second World War.

  - [Battleship](4054.md), a large, heavily armored warship equipped with many powerful guns. A term which generally post-dates sailing warships.&#x20;

    - [Ironclad battleship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironclad_battleship), battleships built before the [pre-dreadnought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-dreadnought_battleship) in the late 1850s to the early 1890s.

    - [Pre-dreadnought battleship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-dreadnought_battleship), sea-going battleships built to a common design before the launch of dreadnoughts, between the mid-1880s to the early 1900s. Pre-dreadnoughts commonly featured a mixed main battery composed of several different caliber guns.

    - [Dreadnought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreadnought), an early 20th-century battleship, which set the pattern for all subsequent battleship construction. Dreadnoughts differ from pre-dreadnoughts in that they feature an all-big-gun main battery. The advantage lies in that if all the big guns have the same characteristics, only one firing solution will be needed to aim them all.&#x20;

      - [Fast battleships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_battleship) were battleships which emphasized speed without – in concept – undue compromise of either armor or armament.

  - [Battlecruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlecruiser), a ship with battleship-level armament and cruiser-level armour; typically faster than a battleship because the reduction in armour allowed mounting of more powerful propulsion machinery, or the use of a more slender hull shape with a lower drag coefficient.

- [Cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser), a fast, independent warship. Traditionally, cruisers were the smallest warships capable of independent action. As of 2024, only two countries operated active duty vessels formally classed as cruisers: the [United States](3434750.md) and [Russia](25391.md).&#x20;

  - [Guided missile cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile_cruiser) are cruisers armed with [anti-ship missiles](140725.md).

  - [Torpedo cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo_cruiser) is a type of cruiser that is armed primarily with [torpedoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedoe).

  - [Armored cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armored_cruiser) was a type of warship of the late 19th and early 20th centuries. It was designed like other types of cruisers to operate as a long-range, independent warship, capable of defeating any ship apart from a battleship and fast enough to outrun any battleship it encountered.

  - [Large cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_cruiser) is the class of the [battlecruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlecruiser)-sized [Alaska-class cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska-class_cruiser) of the [United States Navy](20518076.md) during [World War II](32927.md).

  - [Heavy cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_cruiser) was a type of cruiser, a naval warship designed for long range and high speed, armed generally with naval guns of roughly 203 mm (8 in) caliber; its parameters were dictated by the [Washington Naval Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Naval_Treaty) of 1922 and the [London Naval Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Naval_Treaty) of 1930.&#x20;

    - [Pocket battleship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_battleship), nickname for the *Deutschland*-class heavy cruisers.

  - [Light cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_cruiser) is a type of small or medium-sized warship. The term is a shortening of the phrase "light [armored cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armored_cruiser)", describing a small ship that carried armor in the same way as an armored cruiser: a protective belt and deck.

  - [Scout cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scout_cruiser) was a type of warship of the early 20th century, which were smaller, faster, more lightly armed and armoured than protected cruisers or light cruisers, but larger than contemporary destroyers. They were used for [scouting](146911.md).

  - [Protected cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_cruiser) is a type of naval cruiser of the late-19th century, gained their description because an armored deck offered protection for vital machine-spaces from fragments caused by shells exploding above them.

  - [Unprotected cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unprotected_cruiser) was a type of naval cruiser in use during the early 1870s [Victorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era) or [pre-dreadnought era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-dreadnought_battleship).

  - [Coastal defence ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_defence_ship) were a type of cruiser-sized warship built for the purpose of coastal defense.

- [Destroyer](8779.md), a fast and highly maneuverable warship, traditionally incapable of independent action. Originally developed to counter the threat of [torpedo boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo_boat), they are now the largest independent warship generally seen on the ocean.&#x20;

  - [Guided missile destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile_destroyer) are destroyers armed with [anti-ship missiles](140725.md).

  - [Escort destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escort_destroyer) was a small warship built to full naval standards which was optimised for air-defence and anti-submarine duties in wartime, but which retained many of the capabilities of a traditional fleet destroyer, enabling it to conduct operations in conjunction with main fleet units as well as carrying out convoy escort and ASW patrols.

  - [Destroyer escort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destroyer_escort) was the [United States Navy](20518076.md) mid-20th-century classification for a 20-knot (37 km/h; 23 mph), warship designed with the endurance necessary to escort mid-ocean convoys of merchant marine ships similar to frigates.

- [Frigate](11464.md), originally a medium-sized sailing ship. Although they date back to the 17th century, frigates in modern navies are typically used to protect merchant ships and other warships.&#x20;

  - [Armoured frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_frigate) are frigates with armour which was added to ships based on existing frigate and ship of the line designs. The additional weight of the armour on these first ironclad warships meant that they could have only one gun deck, and they were technically frigates, even though they were more powerful than existing ships-of-the-line and occupied the same strategic role.

  - [Guided missile frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile_frigate) are frigates armed with [anti-ship missiles](140725.md).

- [Corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette) were small ships during the Age of Sail. The concept was revived again in WWII as a merchant convoy escort and anti-submarine ship. Today they are used for [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare) and patrolling.

  [^cite_note-3]

  - [Littoral Combat Ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral_Combat_Ship) is a [United States Navy](20518076.md) classification of warships with the size and role of corvettes.

- [Fast attack crafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_attack_craft) are a small, fast, agile, offensive, often affordable type of warships armed with [anti-ship missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-ship_missiles), guns or [torpedoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedoes).&#x20;

  - [Missile boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_boat) are small, fast warship armed with [anti-ship missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-ship_missiles).

  - [Torpedo boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo_boat), are small, fast surface vessels designed for launching [torpedoes](31424.md).&#x20;

    - [Torpedo ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo_ram) is a type of torpedo boat combining a [ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_ram) with [torpedo](31424.md) tubes.

    - [Motor torpedo boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_torpedo_boat) is a type of fast torpedo boat, especially of the mid-20th century.

- [Patrol vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrol_vessel) are relatively small naval vessels generally designed for coastal defence, border protection, immigration law-enforcement, search and rescue duties. They may be broadly classified as inshore patrol vessels or offshore patrol vessels.

- Mine warfare vessels:&#x20;

  - [Minesweeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minesweeper_\(ship\)) are small warships designed to remove or detonate [naval mines](22102.md).

  - [Minehunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minehunter) are naval vessels that seek, detect, and destroys individual naval mines.

  - [Mine countermeasures vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mine_countermeasures_vessel) are a type of naval ships designed for the location of and destruction of naval mines which combines the role of a minesweeper and minehunter in one hull.

  - [Minelayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minelayer) are naval vessels that plant [naval mines](22102.md) offshore.

- [Fire ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_ship), a vessel of any sort set on fire and sent into an anchorage or fleet with the intention of causing destruction and chaos. Exploding fire ships are called [hellburners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellburners). The development of [unmanned surface vehicles](7808557.md) has revived the use of fire ships.

- [Naval drifters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_drifter) are boats built along the lines of a commercial [fishing drifter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drifter_\(fishing_boat\)) but fitted out for naval purposes.

- [Naval trawlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_trawler) are vessels built along the lines of [fishing trawlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_trawlers) but fitted out for naval purposes.

- [Armed merchantman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_merchantman) is a type of [merchant ship](875717.md) equipped with [naval guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_guns), usually for defensive purposes, either by design or after the fact.

- [Commerce raider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commerce_raider), any armed vessel—privately or government-owned—sanctioned to raid a nation's merchant fleet.&#x20;

  - [Merchant raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_raider) are disguised commerce raiders.

- [Gunboats](274471.md) are naval watercraft designed for the express purpose of carrying one or more guns to [bombard coastal targets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shore_bombardment).&#x20;

  - [River gunboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_gunboat) is a type of gunboat for riverine use.

  - [Flat-iron gunboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat-iron_gunboat) were a number of classes of coastal gunboats generally characterized by small size, low freeboard, the absence of masts, and the mounting of a single non-traversing large gun, aimed by pointing the vessel.

  - [Torpedo gunboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo_gunboat) were a form of gunboat armed with [torpedoes](31424.md) and designed for hunting and destroying smaller torpedo boats.

  - [Motor gunboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_gunboat) is a type of a fast gunboat armed with [machine-guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine-guns) and [autocannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autocannons).

- [Monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monitor_warship_type), a type of small, heavily gunned warships with shallow draft designed for [shore bombardment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_gunfire_support).&#x20;

  - [River monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_monitor), a type of [monitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monitor_warship_type) used in rivers.

  - [Breastwork monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breastwork_monitor) was a modification of the monitor by [Sir Edward Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Edward_Reed) of the [Royal Navy](26061.md).

- [Q-ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-ship), also known as Q-boats, decoy vessels, special service ships, or mystery ships, were heavily armed merchant ships with concealed weaponry, designed to lure submarines into making surface attacks. This gave Q-ships the chance to open fire and sink them.

- [Submarine chaser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_chaser) was a small warship used in [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare).

- [Armed yachts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_yacht) were modified [yachts](34291.md) that were armed with weapons and were typically in the service of a navy.

- [Balloon carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balloon_carrier) was a type of ship equipped with a [hot-air balloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-air_balloon) tied to the ship with a rope or [cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire_rope), which was used for [observation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation_balloon). This type of ship was later replaced by seaplane tenders and aircraft carriers.

- [Sloop-of-war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloop-of-war) was a sailing vessel category later revived in WWII as a convoy escort ship.&#x20;

  - [Screw sloop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screw_sloop) was a [propeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propeller)-driven sloop-of-wars used during the mid-19th Century.

- [Ironclad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironclad), a wooden warship with external iron plating.&#x20;

  - [Casemate ironclad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casemate_ironclad) were a type of ironclad gunboats used in the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War).

  - [Central battery ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_battery_ship) in European continental navies, was a development of the (high-freeboard) broadside ironclad of the 1870s

  - [Turret ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turret_ship) was a 19th-century type of warship, the earliest to have their guns mounted in a revolving [gun turret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_turret), instead of a [broadside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadside_\(naval\)) arrangement.

  - [Floating battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_battery) is a kind of armed watercraft, often improvised or experimental, which carries heavy armament but has few other qualities as a warship.

- [Ship of the line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_the_line), a sailing warship capable of standing in the [line of battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_battle). A direct predecessor to the later battleship.

- [Cottonclad warships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottonclad_warship) were [steam-powered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamship) warships with bales of [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) lining as armour used in the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War). The armaments consisted of a [ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_ram), random numbers of different [cannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon) and [sharpshooters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharpshooter).

- [Brig of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brig) is a [brig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brig_\(ship\)) armed for use by a [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy).

- [Bomb vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb_vessel) were wooden sailing ships which carried [mortars](324499.md) instead of [cannons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannons).

- [Dispatch boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispatch_boat) were small boats, and sometimes large ships, tasked to carry military dispatches from ship to ship or from ship to shore or, in some cases from shore to shore. ## History and evolution of warships

### First warships

See also: [Oared vessel tactics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oared_vessel_tactics)

The first evidence of ships being used for warfare comes from [Ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt), specifically the northern [Nile River](21244.md) most likely to defend against [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) peoples. The [galley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galley) warship most likely originated in [Crete](6591.md), an idea which was soon copied and popularized by the [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicians). In the time of [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia), [Ancient Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Persia), [Phoenicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia), [Ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) and the [Ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), warships were always [galleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galley) (such as [biremes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bireme), [triremes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trireme) and [quinqueremes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinquereme)): long, narrow vessels powered by banks of [oarsmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oar) and designed to ram and sink enemy vessels, or to engage them [bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow_\(watercraft\))-first and follow up with boarding parties. The development of [catapults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catapult) in the 4th century BC and the subsequent refinement of this technology enabled the first fleets of [siege engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_engine) - equipped warships by the [Hellenistic age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_age). During [late antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_antiquity), [ramming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramming) fell out of use and the [galley tactics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galley_tactics) against other ships used during the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) until the late 16th century focused on boarding.

### The Age of Sail

Main article: [Age of Sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Sail)

See also: [Naval tactics in the Age of Sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_tactics_in_the_Age_of_Sail), [Gunboat](274471.md), [Corvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette), [Sloop-of-War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloop-of-War), [Frigate](11464.md), and [Ship of the line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_the_line)

[Naval artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_artillery) was redeveloped in the 14th century, but [cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon) did not become common at sea until the guns were capable of being reloaded quickly enough to be reused in the same battle. The size of a ship required to carry a large number of cannons made oar-based propulsion unfeasible, and warships came to rely primarily on [sails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sail). The sailing [man-of-war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man-of-war) emerged during the 16th century.

By the middle of the 17th century, warships were carrying increasing numbers of cannons on their [broadsides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadside_\(naval\)) and [tactics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_tactics_in_the_Age_of_Sail) evolved to bring each ship's firepower to bear in a [line of battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_of_battle). The man-of-war now evolved into the [ship of the line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_of_the_line). In the 18th century, the [frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing_frigate) and [sloop-of-war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloop-of-war) – too small to stand in the line of battle – evolved to escort [convoy](42990.md) trade, scout for enemy ships and [blockade](293054.md) enemy coasts.[^cite_note-4] Also during this time, the [Ship's wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship's_wheel) came more into common usage instead of the [whipstaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whipstaff) for steering the ship.

### Steel, steam and shellfire

See also: [Naval tactics in the Age of Steam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_tactics_in_the_Age_of_Steam)

See also: [Steam frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_frigate), [Screw sloop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screw_sloop), [Ironclad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironclad), [Casemate ironclad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casemate_ironclad), [Monitor (warship)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monitor_\(warship\)), [Central battery ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_battery_ship), [Turret ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turret_ship), and [Torpedo boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo_boat)

During the 19th century a revolution took place in the means of [marine propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_propulsion), [naval armament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_armament) and [construction of warships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_architecture). [Marine steam engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_steam_engine) were introduced, at first as an auxiliary force, in the second quarter of the 19th century. The [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) gave a great stimulus to the development of guns. The introduction of explosive [shells](337004.md) soon led to the introduction of [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron), and later [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel), [naval armour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_armour) for the sides and decks of larger warships. The first [ironclad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironclad) warships, the French [*Gloire*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_ironclad_Gloire) and British [*Warrior*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Warrior_\(1860\)), made wooden vessels obsolete. Metal soon entirely replaced wood as the main material for warship construction.

From the 1850s, the sailing ships of the line were replaced by steam-powered [battleships](4054.md), while the sailing [frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing_frigate) were replaced by steam-powered [cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser). The armament of warships also changed with the invention of the rotating [barbettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbette) and [turrets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_turrets), which allowed the guns to be aimed independently of the direction of the ship and allowed a smaller number of larger guns to be carried.

The final innovation during the 19th century was the development of the [torpedo](31424.md) and development of the [torpedo boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo_boat). Small, fast torpedo boats seemed to offer an alternative to building expensive fleets of battleships.

### Pre-dreadnought era

Main article: [Pre-Dreadnought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Dreadnought)

See also: [Destroyer](8779.md), [Unprotected cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unprotected_cruiser), [Protected cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_cruiser), and [Armoured cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_cruiser)

[Pre-dreadnought battleships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-dreadnought_battleship) were sea-going battleships built between the mid- to late 1880s and 1905, before the launch of HMS Dreadnought in 1906. The pre-dreadnought ships replaced the ironclad battleships of the 1870s and 1880s. Built from steel, protected by case-hardened steel armour, and powered by coal-fired triple-expansion steam engines, pre-dreadnought battleships carried a main battery of very heavy guns in fully enclosed rotating turrets supported by one or more secondary batteries of lighter weapons. The role of [corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette), [sloops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloop-of-war) and [frigates](11464.md) were taken by new types of ships like [destroyers](8779.md), [protected cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_cruiser) and [armoured cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_cruiser).&#x20;

### 1906 onward

See also: [Light cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_cruiser), [Heavy cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_cruiser), and [Battlecruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlecruiser)

#### The dreadnought era

Main article: [Dreadnought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreadnought)

Another revolution in capital warship design began shortly after the start of the 20th century, when Britain launched the [Royal Navy](26061.md)'s all-big-gun [battleship](4054.md) [*Dreadnought*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Dreadnought_\(1906\)) in 1906. Powered by [steam turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_turbine), it was bigger, faster and more heavily gunned than [any existing battleships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-dreadnought_battleship), which it immediately rendered obsolete. It was rapidly followed by similar ships in other countries. The Royal Navy also developed the first [battlecruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlecruiser). Mounting the same heavy guns as the [dreadnoughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreadnought) on an even larger [hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_\(watercraft\)), battlecruisers sacrificed armour protection for speed. Battlecruisers were faster and more powerful than all existing cruisers, but much more vulnerable to shellfire than contemporary battleships. The [torpedo-boat destroyer](8779.md) was developed at the same time as the dreadnoughts. Bigger, faster and more heavily gunned than the [torpedo boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo_boat), the destroyer evolved to protect the [capital ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_ship) from the menace of the torpedo boat.

At this time, Britain also introduced the use of [fuel oil](392547.md) to power steam warships, instead of coal. Oil produced twice as much power per unit weight as coal, and was much easier to handle.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBacon1901246-5][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDahl200151-6] Tests were conducted by the Royal Navy in 1904 involving the torpedo-boat destroyer [*Spiteful*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Spiteful_\(1899\)), the first warship powered solely by fuel oil.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnon.190427-7][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELyon200580-8] These proved its superiority, and all warships procured for the Royal Navy from 1912 were designed to burn fuel oil.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELyon200597-9][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESiegel2002181-10]

By this time, the term [flagship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagship), that is, a ship carrying the flag of a commanding admiral or [commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_\(rank\)), had been in use for centuries. [Royal Navy](26061.md) ships with the opposite status, commissioned warships in active service that were not currently serving as the [flagship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagship) of a flag officer were increasingly described as "private" ships, akin to [private soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_soldier#Etymology).[^cite_note-11]

#### Obsolescence of battleships

See also: [List of ships of the Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ships_of_the_Second_World_War)

During the lead-up to the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), [Germany](21212.md) and the United Kingdom once again emerged as the two dominant [Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic) sea powers. The German navy, under the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles), was limited to only a few minor surface ships. But the clever use of deceptive terminology, such as *[Panzerschiffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutschland-class_cruiser)* deceived the British and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic) commands. They were surprised when ships such as [*Admiral Graf Spee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_cruiser_Admiral_Graf_Spee), [*Scharnhorst*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_battleship_Scharnhorst), and [*Gneisenau*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_battleship_Gneisenau) raided [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) supply lines. The greatest threat however, was the introduction of the [Kriegsmarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kriegsmarine)'s largest vessels, [*Bismarck*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_battleship_Bismarck) and [*Tirpitz*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_battleship_Tirpitz). *Bismarck* was heavily damaged and sunk/scuttled after a series of sea battles in the north Atlantic in 1941, while *Tirpitz* was destroyed by the [Royal Air Force](25679.md) in 1944. The British [Royal Navy](26061.md) gained dominance of the European theatre by 1943.



[Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) battleship [*Yamato*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_battleship_Yamato) under air attack off [Kure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kure) on 19 March 1945

The Second World War brought massive changes in the design and role of several types of warships. For the first time, the [aircraft carrier](2219.md) became the clear choice to serve as the main capital ship within a naval task force. World War II was the only war in history in which battles occurred between groups of carriers. World War II saw the first use of [radar](25676.md) in combat. It brought the first naval battle in which the ships of both sides never engaged in direct combat, instead sending aircraft to make the attacks, as in the [Battle of the Coral Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Coral_Sea).



Russian [Typhoon-class submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon-class_submarine)

#### Cold War-era

Modern warships are generally divided into seven main categories, which are: [aircraft carriers](2219.md), [cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser), [destroyers](8779.md), [frigates](11464.md), [corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette), [submarines](28825.md), and [amphibious warfare ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_warfare_ship).&#x20;

Battleships comprise an eighth category, but are not in current service with any navy in the world. Only the deactivated American [*Iowa*-class battleships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa-class_battleship) still exist as potential combatants, and battleships in general are unlikely to re-emerge as a ship class without redefinition. The [*Kirov*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirov-class_battlecruiser) [battlecruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlecruiser) is a guided missile cruiser that straddles the line between a heavy cruiser and a battleship. They are often called battlecruisers by Western defence commentators.[^cite_note-12]

The [destroyer](8779.md) is generally regarded as the dominant surface-combat vessel of most modern blue-water navies. However, the once distinct roles and appearances of [cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruiser), [destroyers](8779.md), [frigates](11464.md), and [corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette) have blurred. Most vessels have come to be armed with a mix of anti-surface, anti-submarine and anti-aircraft weapons. Class designations no longer reliably indicate a displacement hierarchy, and the size of all vessel types has grown beyond the definitions used earlier in the 20th century. Another key difference between older and modern vessels is that all modern warships are "soft", without the thick armor and bulging anti-torpedo protection of World War II and older designs.

Most [navies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) also include many types of support and [auxiliary vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_vessel), such as [minesweepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minesweeper_\(ship\)), [patrol boats](536773.md) and [offshore patrol vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OPV_\(naval\)).

By 1982 the [United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea](31940.md) (UNCLOS) treaty negotiations had produced a legal definition of what was then generally accepted as a late-twentieth century warship. The UNCLOS definition was : "A warship means a ship belonging to the armed forces of a State bearing the external marks distinguishing such ships of its nationality, under the command of an officer duly commissioned by the government of the State and whose name appears in the appropriate service list or its equivalent, and manned by a crew which is under regular armed forces discipline."[^cite_note-UNCLOSW-1]

##### Development of the submarine

Main article: [Submarine](28825.md)



American [submarine](28825.md) [USS *Plunger*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Plunger_\(SS-2\))

The first practical submarines were developed in the late 19th century, but it was only after the development of the [torpedo](31424.md) that submarines became truly dangerous (and hence useful). By the end of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) submarines had proved their potential. During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) Nazi Germany's fleet of [U-boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-boat) (submarines) almost starved Britain into submission and inflicted [huge losses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_happy_time) on US coastal shipping. The success of submarines led to the development of new [anti-submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine) [convoy](42990.md) escorts during the First and Second World Wars, such as the [destroyer escort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destroyer_escort). Confusingly, many of these new types adopted the names of the smaller warships from the [age of sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_sail), such as [corvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette), [sloop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloop-of-war) and [frigate](11464.md).

##### Development of the aircraft carrier

See also: [Seaplane tender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplane_tender) and [Aircraft carrier](2219.md)

A [seaplane tender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplane_tender) is a ship that supports the operation of [seaplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplane). Some of these vessels, known as seaplane carriers, could not only carry seaplanes but also provided all the facilities needed for their operation; these ships are regarded by some as the first aircraft carriers and appeared just before the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War).

A major shift in naval warfare occurred with the introduction of the [aircraft carrier](2219.md). First at [Taranto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Taranto) and then at [Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor), the aircraft carrier demonstrated its ability to strike decisively at enemy ships out of sight and range of surface vessels. By the end of the Second World War, the carrier had become the dominant warship.

##### Development of the amphibious assault ship

[*Shinshū Maru*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_landing_craft_carrier_Shinsh%C5%AB_Maru) was a ship of the [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army) during World War II. She was the world's first purpose-built [landing craft carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_craft_carrier) ship, and a pioneer of modern-day [amphibious assault ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_assault_ships). During some of her operations, she was known to have used at least four cover names, R1, GL, MT, and Ryujo Maru.

An amphibious warfare ship is an amphibious vehicle warship employed to land and support ground forces, such as [marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marines), on enemy territory during an amphibious assault. Specialized shipping can be divided into two types, most crudely described as ships and craft. In general, the ships carry the troops from the port of embarkation to the drop point for the assault and the craft carry the troops from the ship to the shore. Amphibious assaults taking place over short distances can also involve the shore-to-shore technique, where landing craft go directly from the port of embarkation to the assault point. Amphibious assault ships have a well deck with landing craft which can carry [tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank) and other [armoured fighting vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_fighting_vehicle) and also have a deck like a [helicopter carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_carrier) for helicopters and [V/STOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V/STOL) aircraft.
