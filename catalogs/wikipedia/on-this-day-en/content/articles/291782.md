A **naval base**, **navy base**, or **military port** is a [military base](843566.md), where [warships](263960.md) and [naval ships](53159.md) are docked when they have no mission at sea or need to restock. Ships may also undergo repairs. Some naval bases are temporary homes to [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_aviation) that usually stay on ships but are undergoing maintenance while the ship is in port.[^cite_note-1]

In the [United States](3434750.md), the [United States Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy)'s General Order No. 135 issued in 1911 as a formal guide to naval terminology described a naval station as "any establishment for building, manufacturing, docking, repair, supply, or training under control of the Navy. It may also include several establishments". A naval base, by contrast, was "a point from which naval operations may be conducted".[^cite_note-2]

In most countries, naval bases are expressly named and identified as such.

One peculiarity of the [Royal Navy](26061.md) and certain other navies which closely follow British naval traditions is the concept of the [stone frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_frigate): a naval base on land that is named like a ship. Certain facilities were often initially housed on [hulks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulk_\(ship_type\)) as a cost-saving measure and were later moved to land but kept their traditional names.
