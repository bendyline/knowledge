The **Belgium national football team**[^cite_note-5] has represented [Belgium](3343.md) in men's international [football](10568.md) since [their maiden match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89vence_Copp%C3%A9e_Trophy) in 1904. The squad is under the global jurisdiction of [FIFA](11049.md) and is governed in Europe by [UEFA](32332.md)—both of which were co-founded by the Belgian team's supervising body, the [Royal Belgian Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Belgian_Football_Association). Periods of regular Belgian representation at the highest international level, from 1920 to 1938, from 1980 to 2002 and again from 2014 onwards, have alternated with mostly unsuccessful qualification rounds. Most of Belgium's home matches are played at the [King Baudouin Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Baudouin_Stadium) in [Brussels](3708.md).

Belgium's national team have participated in three quadrennial major football competitions. It appeared in fifteen [FIFA World Cups](11370.md) and seven [UEFA European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship), and featured at three Olympic football tournaments, including the [1920 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics) which they won. Other notable performances are victories over four [reigning world champions](11370.md#Results)—West Germany, Brazil, Argentina and France—between 1954 and 2002. Belgium has long-standing football rivalries with its Dutch and French counterparts, having played both teams nearly every year from 1905 to 1967. The squad has been known as the Red Devils since 1906; its fan club is named "1895".

Belgium finished in third place as hosts at [UEFA Euro 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1972). After that, they experienced two [golden generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_generation). In the first period, which lasted from the 1980s to the early 1990s, the team finished as runners-up at [UEFA Euro 1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1980) and fourth in the [1986 FIFA World Cup](157231.md). In the second, under the guidance of [Marc Wilmots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Wilmots) and later [Roberto Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Mart%C3%ADnez) in the 2010s, Belgium topped the [FIFA World Ranking](719095.md) for the first time in November 2015 and finished third at the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md). To date, Belgium is the only national team to top the FIFA ranking without having won a World Cup or continental trophy.

## History

Main article: [History of the Belgium national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Belgium_national_football_team)

### Early history

Belgium was one of the first mainland European countries to play association football, with the earliest recorded example of its practice in Belgium dating back to 1863.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

Football team in uniform

The first Belgium A-squad in 1901 featured four Englishmen

On 11 October 1900, [Beerschot AC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Beerschot_V.A.C.) honorary president Jorge Díaz announced that [Antwerp](32149462.md) would host a series of challenge matches between Europe's best football teams.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuldemontDeps199564-8] After some organisational problems, on 28 April 1901, Beerschot's pitch hosted [its first tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1901_Coupe_Vanden_Abeele), in which a Belgian selection and a Dutch team made up of players from third-level sides led by ex-footballer [Cees van Hasselt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cees_van_Hasselt) contested the [Coupe Vanden Abeele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_vs_Netherlands_Cups#Coupe_Vanden_Abeele).[^cite_note-Vanden-9] Naturally, the hosts had little trouble claiming the cup, defeating the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) by 8–0.[^cite_note-Vanden-9][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFraipontsWillocx2003-10][^cite_note-TrouwCVA-11] Belgium then beat the Netherlands in all three follow-up matches;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHubert198013-12] [FIFA does not recognize these results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_national_football_team_results_\(unofficial_matches\)) because Belgium fielded some English players, such as [Herbert Potts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Potts), who scored 12 of "Belgium's" 17 goals.[^cite_note-Vanden-9][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHubert198013-12]

On 1 May 1904, the Belgians played their first official match, against [France](250197.md) at the [Stade du Vivier d'Oie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_du_Vivier_d'Oie) in Uccle; their draw left the Évence Coppée Trophy unclaimed.[^cite_note-UEFABelFra1904-13] Twenty days later, the [football boards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_association) of both countries were among the seven FIFA founders.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParrishNauright2014xv-15] At that time, the Belgian squad was chosen by a committee chaired by [Édouard de Laveleye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89douard_de_Laveleye), who usually drew from the country's six or seven major clubs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoin1945-16] Belgium would play twice a year against the Netherlands beginning from 1905 onwards, generally once in Antwerp and once in [Rotterdam](26049.md). From these beginnings until 1925, [Belgian-Dutch cup trophies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_vs_Netherlands_Cups) would be awarded in the "[Low Countries derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Countries_derby)".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFraipontsWillocx2003-10]

In 1906, the national team received the nickname *Red Devils* because of their red jerseys,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuldemontDeps199565-17] and four years later, Scottish ex-footballer [William Maxwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Maxwell_\(footballer\)) replaced the UBSSA committee as their manager.[^cite_note-manrsssf-18] From 1912, UBSSA governed football only and was renamed UBSFA.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-RBFAhist-21][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHenshaw197975-22] During the Great War, the national team only played [unrecognized friendlies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_national_football_team_results_%E2%80%93_unofficial_matches), with matches in and against France.[^cite_note-DT210316-23]

### Olympic gold and World Cup struggles

A successful penalty kick, seen from the back of the net

In the 1920 Olympic football final at the [Olympisch Stadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympisch_Stadion_\(Antwerp\)) in Antwerp, Robert Coppée scored a penalty kick for Belgium

At the [1920 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Summer_Olympics), in their first official Olympics appearance, the *Red Devils* won the gold medal on home soil after [a controversial final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics#Gold_medal_match) in which their Czechoslovak opponents left the pitch. In the three 1920s Summer Olympic, they achieved fair results (four wins in seven matches), and played their first intercontinental match, against [Argentina](454699.md).[^cite_note-results-24]

However, over the following decade, Belgium lost all of their matches at the first three FIFA World Cup final tournaments.[^cite_note-results-24] According to historian Richard Henshaw, "\[t]he growth of \[football] in Scandinavia, Central Europe, and South America left Belgium far behind".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHenshaw197976-25] Although [World War II](32927.md) hindered international football events in the 1940s, the Belgian team remained active with unofficial matches against squads of other [allied nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II).[^cite_note-26]

Belgium qualified for only one of eight major tournaments during the 1950s and the 1960s: the [1954 World Cup](60523.md). The day before the tournament began, the RBFA was among the three UEFA founders.[^cite_note-27] Dutch journalists considered the draw of the 1954 Belgian team in their opener against [England](9904.md) to be the most surprising result of that match day, even more than [Switzerland](681862.md)'s victory over the Italian "football stars".[^cite_note-AD180654-28] However, Belgium were eliminated after a loss to [Italy](362466.md) in the second (and last) group match.[^cite_note-rsssf54-29] Two bright spots in these decades were wins against World Cup holders: [West Germany](250204.md) in 1954, and [Brazil](149286.md) in 1963.[^cite_note-results-24] Between these, Belgium defeated Hungary's [Golden Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Team) in 1956.[^cite_note-results-24] The combination of failure in competitive matches, and success in exhibition matches, gave the Belgians the mock title of "world champions of the friendlies".[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBernhartHoutman2014-31]



The Belgium squad at the start of a friendly game against the Netherlands, 1966

The team's performance improved during the early 1970s, under manager [Raymond Goethals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Goethals). Fully dressed in white, as the White Devils,[^cite_note-WhiteDevil-32] Belgium achieved their first victories at the World Cup in [1970](157227.md) and the Euros in [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1972), their debut.[^cite_note-rsssf70-33][^cite_note-rsssf72-34] En route to Euro 1972, they eliminated [reigning European champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_UEFA_European_Championship_records_and_statistics#General_statistics_by_tournament) Italy by winning the [two-legged quarter-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1972_quarter-finals) on [aggregate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#A). At the end stage, they finished third by winning the consolation match against Hungary.[^cite_note-rsssf72-34] In 1973, the denial of a match-winning goal in their last [1974 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) match for UEFA Group 3 cost Belgium their appearance at the final,[^cite_note-35] causing Belgium to become the only nation ever to miss a World Cup final round despite not allowing a goal during the qualifiers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnyder2001-36] The next two attempts to reach a major final ([Euro 1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1976) and the [1978 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup)) were also fruitless.[^cite_note-rsssf76-37][^cite_note-rsssf78-38]



The Belgian national team before a training session in 1977

### Golden age

Beginning with a second-place finish at Euro 1980,[^cite_note-rsssf80-39] the 1980s and the early 1990s are generally considered as Belgium's first golden age.[^cite_note-40] Coached by [Guy Thys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Thys), they achieved their spot in [the 1980 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1980_final) with an unbeaten record in the group phase; in the final, they narrowly lost the title to West Germany 1–2.[^cite_note-rsssf80-39] Starting with the [1982 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup), and ending with the [2002 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), the national team qualified for six consecutive World Cups.[^cite_note-BBC230514-41] During this period, managers Guy Thys, [Paul Van Himst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Van_Himst) and [Robert Waseige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Waseige) each guided Belgium past the first round.[^cite_note-rsssf82-42][^cite_note-rsssf94-43][^cite_note-rsssf2002-44] In addition to receiving individual [FIFA recognitions](3482503.md),[^cite_note-AllStar-45][^cite_note-FIFAWCAwards-46] the team reached the semi-finals of the [1986 World Cup](157231.md) after eliminating the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team) and [Spain](609825.md) in the [knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage).[^cite_note-47] [Argentina](454699.md) went through to the final after a 2–0 victory, in which their star player [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona) scored both goals.[^cite_note-48]



[Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona) and [Argentina](454699.md) won 2–0 to knock Belgium out of the 1986 World Cup

After reaching the [Euro 1980 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1980_final), they were unsuccessful at subsequent European Championships, with early exits from their appearances in [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1984) and [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000).[^cite_note-rsssf84det-49][^cite_note-rsssf2000-50] During the late 1990s, they played three friendly tournament in Morocco, Cyprus and Japan,[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] sharing the [1999 Kirin Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirin_Cup_Soccer) with Peru in the latter.[^cite_note-53] Before the 2002 World Cup, Belgium defeated reigning world and European champions France.[^cite_note-results-24] During that World Cup, Belgium defeated [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team) and tied with co-host [Japan](993546.md) and [Tunisia](1029953.md) to reach the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage).[^cite_note-rsssf2002-44] There they were eliminated by eventual world champions [Brazil](149286.md). Brazil coach [Luiz Felipe Scolari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luiz_Felipe_Scolari) would later state that their match against Belgium was the most difficult of the tournament.[^cite_note-54]

After the 2002 World Cup, the team weakened with the loss of more veterans and coach Waseige.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] They missed out five successive major tournaments from [UEFA Euro 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004) until [UEFA Euro 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012), and went through an equal number of head coaches.[^cite_note-HN060612-57] A 2005 win over reigning European champions [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece_national_football_team) meant nothing but a small comfort.[^cite_note-results-24] In between, a promising new generation was maturing at the [2007 European U-21 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_UEFA_European_Under-21_Championship); [Belgium's squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_national_under-21_football_team) qualified for the following year's [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics) in [Beijing](18603746.md),[^cite_note-58] where the [Young Red Devils squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_team_squads#Belgium) finished fourth. Seventeen of them appeared in the senior national team,[^cite_note-AllRedD-59] albeit without making an immediate impact. Belgium finished in second (and last) place at the [Kirin Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirin_Cup_Soccer) in May 2009,[^cite_note-60] and lost against 125th-ranked [Armenian team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia_national_football_team) in September 2009.[^cite_note-FIFA_World_Rankings-4] After [Georges Leekens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Leekens)' second stint as national manager,[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-DS130512-62] his assistant [Marc Wilmots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Wilmots) became the caretaker in May 2012.[^cite_note-DS150512-63]

### Second golden age

After two matches as interim coach, Wilmots agreed to replace Leekens as manager.[^cite_note-64] Following his appointment, the team's results improved,[^cite_note-65] such that some foreign media regarded it as another Belgian golden generation.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] The young Belgian squad qualified as unbeaten [group winners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_A) for the [2014 World Cup](656933.md),[^cite_note-69] and earned Belgium's second-ever place in the World Cup quarter-finals with a four-match winning streak.[^cite_note-rsssf2014-70]

Belgian defender maneuvering around the Algerian goal

Belgium (in red) playing [Algeria](1029528.md) at the [Mineirão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineir%C3%A3o) at the 2014 World Cup

Belgium qualified for [UEFA Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016) with a match to spare in October 2015,[^cite_note-71] and took No. 1 in the FIFA World Ranking for the first time in November 2015,[^cite_note-72] to stay first for five months.[^cite_note-FIFA_World_Rankings-4][^cite_note-73] At the Euros, Belgium were eliminated in the quarter-finals by [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_football_team).[^cite_note-FIFA_World_Rankings-4][^cite_note-EC2016Wal-74] This prompted the RBFA to dismiss Wilmots.[^cite_note-75] In [2018 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), they were seeded first in their group,[^cite_note-draw_format-76][^cite_note-77] and made the [final tournament](13327177.md) under Spanish manager [Roberto Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Mart%C3%ADnez), becoming the first European team besides hosts [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team) to do so.[^cite_note-78] At the World Cup, Belgium won all their [group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_G) matches, against [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_national_football_team), [Tunisia](1029953.md) and [England](9904.md), progressing to the knockout stage as group winners.[^cite_note-79] In the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Round_of_16) they trailed 2–0 against [Japan](993546.md), but eventually won 3–2 with a 94th minute winner by [Nacer Chadli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacer_Chadli).[^cite_note-80] The Red Devils defeated Brazil 2–1 in the [quarter-finals](13327177.md#Quarter-finals),[^cite_note-81] and would be eliminated in the [semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Semi-finals) by eventual champions France. They eventually won the third place play-off against England.[^cite_note-82] On 16 November 2019, for the first time in its history the team topped the [World Football Elo Ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Football_Elo_Ratings), after a 1–4 away win over Russia during the [Euro 2020 qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_qualifying).[^cite_note-World_Football_Elo_Ratings-83]

Being considered one of the biggest contenders for the [European trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020), the tournament was a complete disappointment for Belgium. Being drawn in Group B alongside Russia, [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) and [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland_national_football_team), Belgium easily conquered the group with three wins.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] In the [knockout phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_knockout_stage), Belgium faced reigning champions [Portugal](576328.md) in the round of 16 with a strike from [Thorgan Hazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorgan_Hazard) to give Belgium a 1–0 win.[^cite_note-87] In the quarter-finals, Belgium faced Italy, failing to take revenge for their 2016 loss, suffering a 1–2 defeat, with the goal being scored by [Romelu Lukaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romelu_Lukaku), ending Belgium's campaign on a sad note.[^cite_note-88]

At the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md) in [Qatar](8238258.md), Belgium were drawn into [Group F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_F) alongside [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team), [Morocco](1024865.md) and [Canada](723688.md). Despite starting their campaign well with a 1–0 victory over Canada, they suffered a shock 2–0 defeat to Morocco, and following a 0–0 draw with Croatia in their final group game, Belgium were knocked out of the tournament at the group stage for the first time since [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-89] Following their elimination from the tournament, Martínez announced that he would be standing down as head coach after six years in charge of the national team.[^cite_note-90]

In February 2023, it was announced that [Domenico Tedesco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_Tedesco) had been appointed as the new head coach of the Belgian national team, replacing Roberto Martinez on a contract lasting until the end of [Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024).[^cite_note-91] At the finals, Belgium were eliminated in the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_knockout_stage), losing 1–0 to France.[^cite_note-92] Tedesco left the post in January 2025, with [Rudi Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudi_Garcia) being appointed the new head coach with a contract through the end of the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md). Belgium reached the quarter finals in the tournament, after beating [Senegal](1017436.md) (3-2 ET) and co-host nation, the United States (4-1), in the knockout stage. They ended up losing 2-1 to Spain, being the only team to score, [Charles De Ketelaere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_De_Ketelaere) (‘41), against the eventual champion in the entire tournament.

In home matches, the team's [outfield players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#O) traditionally wear the colours of the [Belgian flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Belgium): black, yellow and red.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-HistFK1-94][^cite_note-GOAAAL80-95] Red dominates the strip and is often the sole jersey colour.[^cite_note-HistFK1-94][^cite_note-GOAAAL80-95] The away colours are usually white, black or both;[^cite_note-GOAAAL-96] in 2014, the squad introduced a third, yellow kit.[^cite_note-WC2014shirts-97] Their shirts are often trimmed with *tricolores* at the margins.[^cite_note-GOAAAL80-95][^cite_note-GOAAAL70-98] Since 1981, the RBFA emblem has been the national team's badge;[^cite_note-GOAAAL80-95][^cite_note-fotocoll81-99] the previous badge was a yellow lion on a black shield,[^cite_note-HistFK1-94][^cite_note-GOAAAL80-95] similar to the escutcheon of the [national coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Belgium).[^cite_note-100] On 8 November 2019, the [Royal Belgian Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Belgian_Football_Association) revealed a new logo, which preserved the main elements of the previous one: the royal crown, the [wreath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wreath) and the Belgian tricolor.[^cite_note-101]

For their first unofficial match in 1901, the Belgian team wore white jerseys with tricoloured bands on the upper arms.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFraipontsWillocx2003-10] Around their third unofficial match in 1902, the choice was made for a "shirt with national colours ... \[that would indicate,] with a stripe, the number of times every player has participated in an encounter".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHubert198012-102] Since 1904, Belgium's classic all-red jersey design has been altered twice. In 1904–05, the squad briefly wore satin shirts with three horizontal bands in red, yellow and black; according to sports journalist [Victor Boin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Boin), the shirts set "the ugliness record".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoin1945-16] During the 1970s, manager Raymond Goethals chose an all-white combination to improve the team's visibility during evening matches.[^cite_note-WhiteDevil-32][^cite_note-bruzzz-103]

Six clothing manufacturers have supplied the official team strip. [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) is the producer since 2014,[^cite_note-104] and closed a sponsorship deal with the RBFA until 2026;[^cite_note-105] it was also the supplier from 1974 to 1980, and from 1983 to 1991.[^cite_note-sponsortimeline-106] Former kit manufacturers are [Umbro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbro) (early 1970s),[^cite_note-bruzzz-103][^cite_note-sponsortimeline-106] [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_Sportswear) (1981–1982),[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-sponsortimeline-106] [Diadora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diadora) (1992–1998),[^cite_note-sponsortimeline-106] [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) (1999–2009) and Burrda (2010–2014).[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-sponsortimeline-106][^cite_note-110]

### Media coverage

Journalist, seated in the stands and speaking into a microphone

Gust De Muynck's live coverage during Belgium–Netherlands in 1931

The first live coverage of a Belgian sporting event occurred on 3 May 1931, when journalist [Gust De Muynck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gust_De_Muynck) commentated on the football match between Belgium and the Netherlands on radio.[^cite_note-111] Later, football broadcasts were also televised. As 60 per cent of Belgians speak [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language) and 40 per cent speak [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language),[^cite_note-CIA-Bel-112] commentaries for the national team matches are provided in both languages. The matches are not broadcast in [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language)—Belgium's third official language.[^cite_note-CIA-Bel-112] During Belgium's tournament appearances in the 1980s and the early 1990s, [Rik De Saedeleer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rik_De_Saedeleer) crowned himself the nation's most famous football commentator with his emotional and humorous reports.[^cite_note-113]

Initially the matches were transmitted mainly on public television channels: the former BRTN (now [VRT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlaamse_Radio-_en_Televisieomroeporganisatie)) in Dutch, and the [RTBF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTBF) in French. Since 1994, commercial channels such as [vtm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTM_\(TV_channel\)) and its sister channel [Kanaal 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTM_2), and [VIER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Play4_\(TV_channel\)) in [Flanders](10878.md), have purchased broadcasting rights.[^cite_note-RBFAhist-21] The Euro 2016 [round of 16 match against Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_knockout_phase#Hungary_vs_Belgium) was the most-watched programme in Belgian television history, with an audience of over 4 million viewers out of 11.3 million Belgians.[^cite_note-CIA-Bel-112][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

In April 2014, the VRT started transmitting a nine-piece, behind-the-scenes documentary about the national team filmed during the 2014 World Cup qualifiers, titled *Iedereen Duivel* (*Everybody Devil*).[^cite_note-116] Cable broadband provider [Telenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telenet_\(provider\)) broadcast an eight-part documentary about individual players titled *Rode Helden* (*Red Heroes*).[^cite_note-117]

### Side activities

Old cartoon of an association football match with the goalkeeper in the middle who jumps and hits the ball

Illustration of Belgium's game against France in April 1918: some of such unofficial wartime matches served as charity fundraisers

Multiple events were organised for the fans during the squad's peak popularity in the 2010s. During the 2014 World Cup qualifiers, a string of interactive events called the *Devil Challenges* were organised.[^cite_note-118] The premise was that small groups of international players would do a favour in return for each of the five comprehensive chores their supporters completed ("colour Belgium red", "gather 500,000 [decibels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decibel)", etc.), all of which were accomplished.[^cite_note-119] In June 2013, the Belgian national team's first ever Fan Day attracted over 20,000 supporters;[^cite_note-HLN020613-120] a second was held after the 2014 World Cup.[^cite_note-121] On the days of Belgium's 2014 World Cup group matches, large dance events titled *Dance with the Devils* took place in three Belgian cities.[^cite_note-122] This activity was repeated during Belgium's Euro 2016 group matches.[^cite_note-123]

Occasionally, the Belgian team directly supported charity. Between 1914 and 1941 they played at least five unofficial matches of which the returns were for charitable purposes: two against France,[^cite_note-DT210316-23][^cite_note-FraBelWWIa-124] and three against the Netherlands.[^cite_note-DerbyBelNed-125][^cite_note-BelNed1941-126] In 1986, when the Belgian delegation reached the World Cup semi-finals, the squad started a project titled Casa Hogar, an idea of delegation leader [Michel D'Hooghe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_D'Hooghe).[^cite_note-127] Casa Hogar is a home for street children in the Mexican industrial city of [Toluca](17551726.md), to which the footballers donated part of their tournament bonuses.[^cite_note-128] In August 2013, the national team supported four social projects through the charity fund Football+ Foundation, by playing an A-match with a plus sign on the shoulders of their jerseys and auctioning the shirts.[^cite_note-129]

In the 21st century, several national team players acted up against discrimination. In 2002, the national squad held its first anti-[racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism_in_association_football) campaign in which they posed with slogans.[^cite_note-130] A home Euro 2012 qualifier was given the theme of respect for [diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_diversity) in 2010; this UEFA-supported action was part of the European [FARE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fare_network) Action Week.[^cite_note-131] Ex-Red Devil [Dimitri Mbuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitri_Mbuyu)—the first black Belgium player (in 1987)[^cite_note-AllRedD-59][^cite_note-132]—was engaged as godfather, and other foreign, current, and former footballers who played in the [Belgian top division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Pro_League) participated.[^cite_note-133] In 2018, four national team players spoke up against [homophobic violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence_against_LGBT_people), in a video clip made by organisation [Kick It Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kick_It_Out_\(organisation\)).[^cite_note-134]

### Nickname, logo and mascot



"Belgian Red Devils" airbus with trident logo, pictured in 2016

After a 1905 match, a Dutch reporter wrote that three Belgian footballers "work\[ed] as devils".[^cite_note-RN160505-135] A year later [Léopold FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9opold_FC) manager Pierre Walckiers nicknamed the players Red Devils, inspired by their jersey colour, and the achievement of three successive victories in 1906.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuldemontDeps199565-17][^cite_note-results-24] Because of their white home shirts in the 1970s, they were temporarily known as the White Devils.[^cite_note-WhiteDevil-32] Since 2012, the team logo is a red trident (or three-pronged pitchfork),[^cite_note-136] an item that is often associated with the devil.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERussell1987129-137] Apart from that, the national squad has also had four official [anthropomorphous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropomorphism) mascots. The first was a lion in team kit named Diabolix,[^cite_note-mascot-138] a reference to the central symbol in the Belgian coat of arms that appeared on the team jerseys from 1905 to 1980.[^cite_note-GOAAAL80-95][^cite_note-fotocoll1905-139] In accordance with their epithet, the next mascots were a red super-devil and two fan-made modern devils; the one introduced in 2018 was named "Red".[^cite_note-mascot-138][^cite_note-mascot2-140] A trident logo, referring to a devil, is also used frequently.[^cite_note-141]

### Supporters

> "Cycling is the traditional national sport of Belgium, but soccer is the most popular."

— Historian Richard Henshaw, 1979

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHenshaw197975-22]

Fans of the Belgian national team display the country's tricolour national flag, usually with an emphasis on the red element. In 2012, local supporter clubs merged into one large Belgian federation named "1895" after the foundation year of the RBFA. One year later, 1895 had 24,000 members.[^cite_note-142] The nationwide interest in the national team has also been reflected by the occasional presence of [Belgian monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Belgium) at their matches since 1914.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145] One of the greatest moments for the Belgian team and their [12th man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_man_\(football\)) was in 1986 when the Belgian delegation at the World Cup received a warm "welcome home". When the World Cup semi-finalists appeared on the balcony of [Brussels Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels_Town_Hall), the adjoining Grand Place square was filled with an ecstatic crowd that cheered as though their squad had won a major tournament.[^cite_note-146]

Belgian fans wearing red at the 2018 World Cup in Kaliningrad

Crowd of Belgian fans in [Kaliningrad Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaliningrad_Stadium) at the 2018 World Cup

The team's deterioration after the 2002 World Cup led to their absence from the end stages of the next five major tournaments, and strained their popularity. Between 2004 and 2010, local journalists called the Belgian footballing nation "mortally ill".[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] Of the fans that kept supporting their squad in bad times, Ludo Rollenberg was one of the most loyal. He attended the team's matches worldwide since 1990, missing only the 1999 Japanese [Kirin Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirin_Cup_Soccer) and two other matches by 2006,[^cite_note-149] and was the only supporter to attend their matches in Armenia in 2009.[^cite_note-150]

Just before the kick-off of a 2014 World Cup home qualifier, Belgium's footballers saw a [tifo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tifo) banner, sized 10.5 by 11.5 metres (34 by 38 ft) depicting a devil in the national colours.[^cite_note-151] The presence of many Belgian players in top leagues abroad, such as the [Premier League](11250.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWillems2013-152] and promising results under Marc Wilmots, increased fans' enthusiasm and belief in a successful World Cup campaign.[^cite_note-HLN020613-120][^cite_note-153] Because of this popularity, two Belgian monuments were decorated in national colours for the 2014 FIFA World Cup; the [Manneken Pis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manneken_Pis) statue received a child-sized version of the new Belgian uniform,[^cite_note-154] and facets of the [Atomium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomium)'s upper sphere were covered in black, yellow and red vinyl.[^cite_note-155]

### Rivalries

See also: [Low Countries derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Countries_derby) and [Belgium–France football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium%E2%80%93France_football_rivalry)

Match phase with two outfield players from each side

Illustration of a Netherlands–Belgium cup match at Rotterdam's Schuttersveld pitch in 1905

Belgium's main football rivals are its neighbors [the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) and [France](250197.md), with which it shares close cultural and political relations.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157]

#### Netherlands

The matchup between the Belgian and [Dutch team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) is known as the Low Countries [derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#D), as of September 2025 they have played each other in 129 official matches.[^cite_note-DerbyBelNed-125] Belgium won the first four—unofficial—matches against the Netherlands,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHubert198012-102] but lost their first FIFA-recognised contest.[^cite_note-results-24] The two national teams played each other biannually between 1905 and 1964, except during the World Wars.[^cite_note-results-24] They have met 18 times in major tournaments, and have played at least [35 friendly cup matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_Countries_derby#Belgium_vs._Netherlands_Cups): in Belgium for the *Coupe Vanden Abeele*, and in the Netherlands for the *Rotterdamsch Nieuwsblad-Beker*.[^cite_note-TrouwCVA-11][^cite_note-RN160505-135] The overall balance favours the Netherlands, with 57 wins against 41 Belgian victories.[^cite_note-DerbyBelNed-125] The Low Countries' squads co-operated in fundraising initiatives between 1925 and 1941; they played seven unofficial matches for charity, FIFA and the Belgian Olympic Committee.[^cite_note-DerbyBelNed-125][^cite_note-BelNed1941-126][^cite_note-158]

#### France

The clash between Belgium and France is nicknamed *le Match Sympathique* in French ("the Friendly Match");[^cite_note-159] they have contested 78 official matches as of September 2025.[^cite_note-belfrares-160] The first match between Belgium and France, the *Évence Coppée* Trophy played in 1904, was the first official match for both teams and the first official football match between independent countries on the European continent.[^cite_note-belfrares-160] Belgium maintained the better record with most wins over the succeeding 100 years, but since 2018 the gap has closed with 5 successive losses to France.[^cite_note-belfrares-160]

## Stadium

Main articles: [Belgium national football team home stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_national_football_team_home_stadium) and [King Baudouin Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Baudouin_Stadium)

Aerial photo of packed stadium

Stadium interior, photographed from the grandstand

The Jubilee Stadium on the [Heysel/Heizel Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heysel_Plateau) in 1935 (left) and the [King Baudouin Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Baudouin_Stadium) in 2013 (right)

Numerous former and current [venues in 11 urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_national_football_team_home_stadium#List_of_home_stadiums) have hosted Belgium's home matches.[^cite_note-results-24] Most of these matches have been played in [Brussels](3708.md) on the [Heysel/Heizel Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heysel_Plateau), on the site of the present-day [King Baudouin Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Baudouin_Stadium)—a multipurpose facility with a seating capacity of 50,122.[^cite_note-161] Its field also hosts the team's final trainings before domestic matches. Since 2007, most physical preparation takes place at the National Football Centre in [Tubize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubize),[^cite_note-162] or at [Anderlecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSC_Anderlecht)'s training ground in the [Neerpede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderlecht) quarter.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164] Apart from Belgian home friendlies, at the international level Belgium's national stadium has also hosted six European Championship matches.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166]

In 1930, for the country's centennial, the venue was inaugurated as the Jubilee Stadium with an unofficial match between Belgium and the Netherlands.[^cite_note-DerbyBelNed-125] At that time, the stadium had a capacity of 75,000.[^cite_note-167] In 1946, it was renamed Heysel Stadium after its city quarter. This new name became associated with the tragedy preceding the [1985 European Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_European_Cup_final) between [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_FC) and [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.); [39 spectators died](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heysel_Stadium_disaster) after riots in the then antiquated building.[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEParrishNauright2014xxviii-169] Three years after the disaster, plans were unveiled for a renovation;[^cite_note-170] in 1995, after two years of work, the modernised stadium was named after the late [King Baudouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baudouin_of_Belgium).[^cite_note-171] In May 2013, the Brussels-Capital Region announced that the King Baudouin Stadium would be replaced by [Eurostadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostadium), elsewhere on the Heysel Plateau;[^cite_note-172] in 2018, however, the plans for the new stadium were cancelled definitively.[^cite_note-173]

## Results and fixtures

See also: [Belgium national football team results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_national_football_team_results) and [Belgium national football team results (2020–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_national_football_team_results_\(2020%E2%80%93present\))

As of 2 October 2026, the complete official match record of the Belgian national team comprises [871 matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_national_football_team_results): 388 wins, 184 draws and 299 losses.[^cite_note-fifa_unrec-3][^cite_note-results-24][^cite_note-178] During these matches, the team scored 1,581 times and conceded 1,337 goals. Belgium's highest winning margin is nine goals, which has been achieved on four occasions: against [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team) in 1994 (9–0), twice against [San Marino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marino_national_football_team) in 2001 (10–1) and 2019 (9–0), and against [Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_national_football_team) in 2017 (9–0).[^cite_note-results-24] Their longest winning streak is 12 wins, and their highest unbeaten record is 23 consecutive official matches.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-results-24]

### Coaching history

Further information: [List of Belgium national football team managers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Belgium_national_football_team_managers)

Since 1904, the RBFA, 26 permanent managers and two caretaker managers have officially been in charge of the national team;[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-manrsssf-18][^cite_note-HN060612-57] this includes one national footballer selector.[^cite_note-manrsssf-18] As of January 2025, a crew of over 30 RBFA employees guides the player group, including French head coach [Rudi Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudi_Garcia), and assistant coaches [Andreas Hinkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Hinkel) and Luke Benstead.[^cite_note-staff-180] Under [Marc Wilmots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Wilmots), Belgium reached the top FIFA ranking spot in 2015, which earned him the title of Best Coach of the Year at the 2015 [Globe Soccer Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe_Soccer_Awards).[^cite_note-184] Guy Thys received [*World Soccer* magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Soccer_\(magazine\))'s Manager of the Year in 1986 after results at the World Cup and Euros.[^cite_note-185] Under Spanish coach [Roberto Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Mart%C3%ADnez), the team reached a best-ever third place finish at the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md).

Rather than developing innovative team formations or styles of play, Belgium's managers applied conventional tactics. At the three World Cups in the 1930s, the Red Devils were aligned in a contemporary [2–3–5 "pyramid"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_\(association_football\)).[^cite_note-rsssf30-186][^cite_note-rsssf34-187][^cite_note-rsssf38-188] In 1954, [Doug Livingstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Livingstone)'s squad played in a [3–2–5 "WM" arrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_\(association_football\)) during World Cup matches.[^cite_note-rsssf54-29] Throughout most of their tournament matches in the 1970s, 1980s and 1990s, the team played in a [4–4–2 formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_\(association_football\)).[^cite_note-rsssf70-33][^cite_note-rsssf82-42][^cite_note-rsssf94-43] Since Raymond Goethals' stint in the 1970s, a key strength of the Belgian squad has been their systematic use of the [offside trap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offside_\(association_football\)),[^cite_note-189] a defensive tactic that was already intensively applied in the 1960s by Anderlecht coach [Pierre Sinibaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Sinibaldi).[^cite_note-190] According to football journalist Wim De Bock, "master tactician" Goethals represented the "conservative, defensive football of the Belgian national team"; he added that in the 1970s, the contrast between the Belgian playing style and the Netherlands' [Total Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Football) "could not be bigger".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDe_Bock2013-191]

At the 1998 World Cup, Georges Leekens chose a [4–3–3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_\(association_football\)) arrangement for Belgium's second and third group matches.[^cite_note-rsssf98-192] Robert Waseige said that "above all, \[his] 4–4–2 system \[was] holy", in the sense that he left good attackers on the bench to keep his favourite formation.[^cite_note-193] Wilmots opted for the 4–3–3 line-up again,[^cite_note-194] with the intention of showing dominant football against any country.[^cite_note-195]

### Notable

Player on the pitch in national team outfit

[Paul Van Himst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Van_Himst) in 1964&#x20;

In the team's first decade, striker [Robert De Veen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_De_Veen) scored 26 goals in 23 international appearances.[^cite_note-RSSSF_BelIntRec-197] Richard Henshaw described [Alphonse Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphonse_Six) as "Belgium's greatest player in the prewar period ... \[who] was often called the most skillful forward outside Great Britain".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHenshaw197976-25] The key player of the victorious 1920 Olympic squad was [Robert Coppée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Copp%C3%A9e), who scored a [hat-trick](411541.md) against Spain's [Ricardo Zamora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Zamora),[^cite_note-FIFA1920-198] and the penalty in the final.[^cite_note-1920finalFIFA-199] Other Belgian strikers in the interwar period were former top scorer [Bernard Voorhoof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Voorhoof) and "Belgium's football grandmaster" [Raymond Braine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Braine),[^cite_note-AllRedD-59][^cite_note-200] considered "one of the greatest players of the era".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurray199863-201]

Other players in the 1940s and 1950s included [centre-back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defender_\(association_football\)) [Louis Carré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Carr%C3%A9_\(footballer\)) and attackers [Jef Mermans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jef_Mermans), [Pol Anoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9opold_Anoul) and [Rik Coppens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Coppens);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHenshaw197976-25] at the 1954 World Cup, Anoul shone with three goals,[^cite_note-rsssf54-29] and newspaper *[L'Équipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'%C3%89quipe)* named Coppens the event's best centre forward.[^cite_note-202] The 1960s and the early 1970s were the glory days of forward and four-time [Belgian Golden Shoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Golden_Shoe) Paul Van Himst,[^cite_note-GoScho-203] later elected Belgian [UEFA Golden Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Jubilee_Awards) of 1954–2003 and Belgium's Player of the Century by [IFFHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_Football_History_&_Statistics).[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205]



[Eden Hazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Hazard), former team [captain](633462.md) and third top scoring player for Belgium

[^cite_note-206]

At the [1965 Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Ballon_d'Or), Van Himst ranked fourth, achieving Belgium's highest ever position at the European football election.[^cite_note-207] Decades after Coppens and Van Himst had retired from playing football, a journalist on a Flemish television show asked them "Who \[from both of you] was the best, actually?". Coppens replied: "I will ask Paul that ... If Paul says it was me, then he's right".[^cite_note-208] In 1966, striker [Raoul Lambert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Lambert) and defending midfielder [Wilfried Van Moer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilfried_Van_Moer) joined the national team;[^cite_note-AllRedD-59] while the UEFA praised Lambert for his skills at Euro 1972,[^cite_note-209] Van Moer won three Golden Shoes and equalled Van Himst's fourth rank at the [1980 Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Ballon_d'Or).[^cite_note-GoScho-203][^cite_note-210]

Belgium has seen two talented waves since 1980, from which several players in defensive positions gained international fame. In the 1980s and the early 1990s, goalkeepers [Jean-Marie Pfaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Marie_Pfaff) and [Michel Preud'homme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Preud'homme) were elected best [custodians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#C) at the FIFA World Cup,[^cite_note-AllStar-45][^cite_note-FIFAWCAwards-46] while FIFA recognised midfielders [Jan Ceulemans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Ceulemans) and [Enzo Scifo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Scifo) as the propelling forces of Belgium's 1986 World Cup squad.[^cite_note-AllStar-45][^cite_note-211] In 2002, after all players of this generation had retired,[^cite_note-AllRedD-59] Marc Wilmots became Belgium's top scorer at the World Cup with five goals.[^cite_note-rsssf2002-44][^cite_note-rsssf98-192]

During the 10 years from 2002 to 2012 in which Belgium failed to qualify for a major tournament, another [golden generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_generation#Belgium_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)) emerged, many of whom gained both prime individual and team awards in top European clubs and competitions.[^cite_note-212] These include defender [Jan Vertonghen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Vertonghen), Belgium's most capped player of all time, defender [Vincent Kompany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Kompany),[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-SoccerwayKompany-214] midfielder [Kevin De Bruyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_De_Bruyne), one of the best attacking midfielders in the world and his generation;[^cite_note-SporzaDeBruyne-215] and winger [Eden Hazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Hazard), who has been praised as one of [Chelsea](7473.md)'s greatest-ever players[^cite_note-216] and one of his era's best footballers in the world.[^cite_note-217] Hazard is ranked third after [Romelu Lukaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romelu_Lukaku) and [Kevin De Bruyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_De_Bruyne) on Belgium's all-time scoring leaderboard.[^cite_note-PLEHazard-218][^cite_note-SoccerwayEHazard-219] Honourable mentions of this golden generation are [Thibaut Courtois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thibaut_Courtois), [Dries Mertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dries_Mertens), and [Toby Alderweireld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toby_Alderweireld).[^cite_note-220] These players helped Belgium finish at the third place at the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md), the team's best result at the tournament, and reach No. 1 on FIFA's rankings twice, since 2015.[^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222]

### FIFA World Cup

Main article: [Belgium at the FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_at_the_FIFA_World_Cup)

Match phase with aerial play

United States–Belgium in 1930 was the joint first ever World Cup match

Belgium failed to progress past the first round in their first five World Cup participations. After two scoreless defeats at the [inaugural World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup) in 1930,[^cite_note-rsssf30-186] the team scored in their first round knockout matches in the [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1938 editions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-rsssf34-187][^cite_note-rsssf38-188] In 1954, they drew with England 4–4 after extra time,[^cite_note-rsssf54-29] and in 1970, they won their first World Cup match, against [El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Salvador_national_football_team) (3–0).[^cite_note-rsssf70-33]

From 1982 until 2002, Belgium qualified for six successive World Cups, advancing past the first round five times.[^cite_note-BBC230514-41] In the 1982 FIFA World Cup opener, Belgium beat [defending champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup) Argentina 1–0. Their tournament ended in the second group stage, after a [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team) hat-trick by [Zbigniew Boniek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zbigniew_Boniek) and a 0–1 loss against the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team).[^cite_note-rsssf82-42]

At Mexico 1986, the Belgian team achieved their then best-ever World Cup run at the time. In the knockout phase as [underdogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#U) they beat the Soviet Union after [extra time](633242.md) 4–3;[^cite_note-228] the unnoticed offside position of Jan Ceulemans, during the initial ninety minutes, allowed him to equalise the match at 2–2 and enter extra time.[^cite_note-229] They also beat [Spain](609825.md), in a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md) after a 1–1 draw, but lost to eventual champions Argentina in the semi-finals 2–0, and France in the third-place match 4–2.[^cite_note-rsssf86-230]

In 1990, Belgium dominated periods of their round of 16 match against England;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEdworthy1997138–139-231] Enzo Scifo and Jan Ceulemans hit the [woodwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#W).[^cite_note-232] [David Platt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Platt)'s [volley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volley_\(association_football\)) in the final minute of extra time, described as "nearly blind" by Richard Witzig,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWitzig2006167-233] avoided an apparently goalless draw and led to the sudden elimination of the Belgians.[^cite_note-rsssf90-234]

In 1994, a 3–2 defeat to defending champions [Germany](250204.md) saw Belgium go out in the second round again.[^cite_note-rsssf94-43] Afterwards, the entire Belgian delegation criticised referee [Kurt Röthlisberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_R%C3%B6thlisberger) for not awarding a penalty for a [foul](3090014.md) on Belgian [Josip Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Weber).[^cite_note-235] Three draws in the group stage of the 1998 World Cup were insufficient for Belgium to reach the knockout stage.[^cite_note-rsssf98-192] With two draws, the 2002 tournament started poorly for Belgium, but they won the decisive group match against Russia 3–2. In the second round, they faced eventual champions Brazil; Belgium lost 2–0 after Marc Wilmots' headed opening goal was disallowed due to a "phantom foul" on [Roque Júnior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_J%C3%BAnior), as Witzig named it.[^cite_note-rsssf2002-44][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWitzig2006289-236]

In 2014, Belgium beat all their group opponents by only one goal,[^cite_note-rsssf2014-70] and played an entertaining round of 16 match against the [United States](582488.md),[^cite_note-237] in which American goalkeeper [Tim Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Howard) made 15 saves.[^cite_note-239] However, they defeated the U.S. 2–1 in extra time.[^cite_note-rsssf2014-70] In a balanced quarter-finals, Argentina eliminated Belgium, with a 1–0 victory.[^cite_note-240]

At the [2018 World Cup](13327177.md), Belgium started with five consecutive victories (including [group wins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_G) over [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_national_football_team), [Tunisia](1029953.md) and England). In the round of 16 match against [Japan](993546.md), Belgium were down 2–0,[^cite_note-241][^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243] eventually turning the tide and winning 3–2 with goals from [Jan Vertonghen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Vertonghen) and late substitutes [Marouane Fellaini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marouane_Fellaini) and [Nacer Chadli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacer_Chadli). Belgium then defeated World Cup favourites Brazil 2–1 on the back of an early [Fernandinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernandinho_\(footballer,_born_May_1985\)) [own goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_goal) and a goal by [Kevin De Bruyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_De_Bruyne),[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245] and reached the [semi-finals](13327177.md).[^cite_note-246] Belgium lost to [France](250197.md) 0–1 in the semi-finals, but rebounded to win 2–0 in their second victory over England in the tournament to secure third place and the best ever World Cup result for the Belgian national team.

### UEFA European Championship

Main article: [Belgium at the UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_at_the_UEFA_European_Championship)

Scheme of football pitch with the line-ups of a red team in 4-4-2 formation against a white team in 5-3-2 formation

Line-ups of the [Euro 1980 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1980_final): Belgium (red) against West Germany

Belgium have qualified for the [UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) seven times, and has hosted or co-hosted the event twice; they were chosen to accommodate [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1972)[^cite_note-250] and [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000), co-hosting the latter with the Netherlands.[^cite_note-rsssf2000-50]

At Euro 1972, Belgium finished third after losing 1–2 against West Germany and beating Hungary 2–1.[^cite_note-rsssf72-34] The team's best continental result is runners-up at [Euro 1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1980) in Italy, losing to West Germany. West Germany's [Horst Hrubesch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horst_Hrubesch) scored first, but [René Vandereycken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Vandereycken) equalised courtesy of a penalty. Two minutes before the regular playing time ended, Hrubesch scored again denying Belgium a first European title.[^cite_note-rsssf80-39]

At Euro 1984, in their last and decisive group match against [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team), the Belgian team took a 0–2 lead, but the Danes won the match 3–2.[^cite_note-rsssf84det-49] Sixteen years later, Belgium reappeared at the Euros as co-hosts, qualifying automatically, but the Belgians were eliminated in the group stage.[^cite_note-rsssf2000-50]

Belgium exited in the quarter-finals of [UEFA Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016), losing to Wales.[^cite_note-EC2016Wal-74]

### Olympic Games

Main article: [Belgium at the Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_at_the_Olympics)

Aerial play with two Belgian players and the Luxembourg keeper trying to touch the ball

Hectic phase during the goal-rich Olympic win against [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_national_football_team) in 1928 (5–3)

The football tournament for senior men's national teams took place in six Summer Olympics between 1908 and 1936. The Belgian squad participated in all three [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics) in the 1920s and won the gold medal at home at the 1920 edition.[^cite_note-results-24][^cite_note-rsssfOG20-251] Two other Belgian delegations appeared at the Olympics. At the [1900 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1900_Summer_Olympics), [a Belgian representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1900_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_team_squads#Universit%C3%A9_de_Bruxelles) with mainly students won bronze,[^cite_note-rsssfOG00-252] and at [the 2008 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics), Belgium's U-23 selection placed fourth.[^cite_note-rsssfOG2008-253]

[Belgium's 1920 Olympic squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_team_squads#_Belgium) was given a [bye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bye_\(sports\)) into the quarter-finals, where they won 3–1 against Spain, and reached the semi-finals, where they beat the Netherlands 3–0. In the first half of the final against Czechoslovakia, the Belgians led 2–0.[^cite_note-rsssfOG20-251] Forward Robert Coppée converted a disputed early [penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_kick_\(association_football\)), and the action in which attacker [Henri Larnoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Larnoe) doubled the score was also a matter of debate.[^cite_note-ISOH-254][^cite_note-FIFA1920-198] After the [dismissal](3090014.md) of the Czechoslovak left-back Karel Steiner, the discontented visitors left the pitch in the 40th minute. Afterwards, the away team reported their reasons for protest to the Olympic organisation;[^cite_note-ISOH-254] these complaints were dismissed and the Czechoslovaks were disqualified.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHenshaw197976-25] The 2–0 score was allowed to stand and Belgium were crowned the champions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHenshaw197976-25]
