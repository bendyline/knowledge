# Capital city

"Capital cities" redirects here. For the band, see [Capital Cities (duo)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Cities_\(duo\)). For the capital city of a county, see [County seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat). For other uses, see [Capital City (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_City_\(disambiguation\)).



[Tokyo](30057.md), the capital of [Japan](15573.md), and the [most populous metropolitan area](14649921.md) in the world

A **capital city**, or just **capital**, is the [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality) holding primary status in a [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country), [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)), [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province), [department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_\(administrative_division\)), or other subnational division, usually as its [seat of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_of_government). A capital is typically a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) that physically encompasses the government's offices and meeting places; the status as capital is often designated by law or a [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution). In some [jurisdictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction), including several countries, different branches of government are in different settlements, sometimes meaning there are [multiple official capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_with_multiple_capitals). In some cases, a distinction is made between the official (constitutional) capital and the seat of government.

English-language media often use the name of the capital [metonymically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metonymy) to refer to the government sitting there. For example, "London–Washington relations" is understood to mean diplomatic relations between the United Kingdom and the United States.[^cite_note-1]



[Rome](25458.md), as the capital of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) (and present-day [Italy](14532.md)), acquired the nickname of *[Caput Mundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caput_Mundi)* ('Capital of the world').

## Terminology and etymology

The word *capital* derives from the Latin word *caput* (genitive *capitis*), meaning 'head', later borrowed from [Medieval Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Latin) *_capitālis_* ('of the head').[^cite_note-2] The Latin phrase *[Roma Caput Mundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caput_Mundi)* (lit. 'Rome head of the world') was already used by the poet [Ovid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovid) in the 1st century BC.[^cite_note-3] It originates out of a [classical European](252905.md) understanding of the [known world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumene): [Europe](9239.md), [North Africa](21714.md), and [Southwest Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Asia). The phrase is related to the enduring power of the city first as the capital of the [Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) and the [Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), and later as the centre of the [Catholic Church](606848.md).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

In several English-speaking [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_state), the terms [county town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_town) and [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) are also used in lower [administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division). In some [unitary states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state), subnational capitals may be known as 'administrative centres'. The capital is often the largest city of its constituent, though [not always](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_whose_capital_is_not_their_largest_city).



[Beijing](18603746.md), as the last of the [Four Great Ancient Capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_capitals_of_China) of [China](5405.md), has served as the country's political centre for most of the past eight centuries.

## Origins

Historically, the major economic centre of a state or region has often become the focal point of political power, and became a capital through [conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_\(military\)) or [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation).[^cite_note-7] Historical examples are [ancient Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon), [ancient Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Athens), [ancient Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), [Abbasid Baghdad](4492.md#History), [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople), [Chang'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'an), and [ancient Cusco](72735.md). The modern capital city has not always existed: in medieval Western Europe, an [itinerant (wandering) government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itinerant_court) was common.[^cite_note-8]



[Tehran](57654.md), the capital of [Iran](14653.md) since the [Qajar Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran), and the most populous city in [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia).

[^cite_note-9]

[^cite_note-10]

The capital city attracts politically motivated people and those whose skills are needed for efficient [administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administration_\(government\)) of national or imperial governments, such as [lawyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawyer), [political scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_scientist), [bankers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking), [journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalist), and [public policy makers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_policy_\(law\)). Some of these cities are or were also [religious centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_city),[^cite_note-11] e.g. [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) (more than one religion), [Rome](25458.md)/[Vatican City](32408.md) (the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church)), [Jerusalem](16043.md) (more than one religion), Babylon, Moscow (the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church)), Belgrade (the [Serbian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church)), Paris, and Beijing. In some countries, the capital has been changed for [geopolitical](163225.md) reasons; [Finland](10577.md)'s first city, [Turku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turku), which had been the country's most important city since the Middle Ages and became capital in 1809, lost its position during the [Grand Duchy of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Finland) in 1812, when [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki) was made the current capital of Finland by the Russian Empire.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

The convergence of political and economic or cultural power is by no means universal. Traditional capitals may be economically eclipsed by provincial rivals as is the case with [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) by [Shanghai](27643.md), [Quebec City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_City) by [Montreal](7954681.md), and several [US state capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_state_capitals). The [decline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Societal_collapse) of a dynasty or culture could also mean the extinction of its capital city, as occurred at Babylon[^cite_note-14] and [Cahokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahokia). "Political nomadism" was practiced in [ancient Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Near_East) to increase ties between the ruler and the subjects.[^cite_note-15]

Although many capitals are defined by constitution or legislation, many long-time capitals have no such legal designation, including [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern), [Edinburgh](9602.md), [Lisbon](18091.md), [London](17867.md), [Paris](22989.md), and [Wellington](33804.md). They are recognized as capitals as a matter of convention, and because all or almost all the country's central political institutions, such as government departments, supreme court, legislature, embassies, etc., are located in or near them.

The earliest capital existing is [Damascus](8914.md) which has existed since c.2500BC.[^cite_note-16]

## Modern capitals



[London](17867.md), the capital of [England](9316.md) and the [United Kingdom](31717.md), and the largest [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_metropolitan_area) in [Western Europe](33800.md)



[Paris](22989.md), the capital of [France](5843419.md), and the largest metropolitan area in the [European Union](9317.md)



[Cairo](6293.md), the capital of [Egypt](8087628.md), and the largest metropolitan area in [Africa](5334607.md)



[Moscow](19004.md), the capital of [Russia](25391.md), and the former capital of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)

Many modern capital cities are located near the centre of the country, so that they are more accessible to its population and have better protection from possible invasions. (See also [§ Capitals in military strategy](#Capitals_in_military_strategy)) The location may also be based on a compromise between two or more cities or other political divisions, historical reasons, or enough land was needed to deliberately build a new [planned city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_city) for the capital.[^cite_note-17] The majority of national capitals are also the largest city in their respective countries. Modern examples are [Berlin](3354.md), [Cairo](6293.md), [London](17867.md), [Madrid](41188263.md), [Mexico City](18987.md), [Moscow](19004.md), [Paris](22989.md), [Rome](25458.md), [Jakarta](16275.md), [Metro Manila](72340.md), [Seoul](19159283.md), and [Tokyo](30057.md).[^cite_note-18]

[Counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_the_United_Kingdom) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) have historic county towns, which are often not the largest settlement within the county and often are no longer administrative centres, as many historical counties are now only ceremonial, and administrative boundaries are different. The number of new capitals in the world increased substantially since the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) period, especially with the founding of independent nation-states since the eighteenth century.[^cite_note-19]

In [Canada](5042916.md), there is a [federal capital](22219.md), while the ten [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Canada) and three [territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territories_of_Canada#Territories) each have capital cities. The states of such countries as [Mexico](3966054.md), [Brazil](3383.md) (including the famous cities of [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md) and [São Paulo](390875.md), capitals of their respective states), and [Australia](4689264.md) also each have capital cities. For example, the six state capitals of Australia are [Adelaide](1148.md), [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane), [Hobart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobart), [Melbourne](17306237.md), [Perth](24355.md), and [Sydney](27862.md). In Australia, the term "capital cities" is regularly used to refer to those six state capitals plus the federal capital [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra), and [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory), the capital of the [Northern Territory](21638.md). [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md) is the capital city of the [Emirate of Abu Dhabi](8821238.md) and also of the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) overall.

In unitary states which consist of multiple constituent nations, such as the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and the [Kingdom of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Denmark), each will usually have its own capital city. Unlike in [federations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation), there is usually not a separate national capital, but rather the capital city of one constituent nation will also be the capital of the state overall, such as [London](17867.md), which is the capital of [England](9316.md) and of the United Kingdom. Similarly, each of the [autonomous communities of Spain](55002.md) and [regions of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) has a capital city, such as [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville) and [Naples](55880.md), while [Madrid](41188263.md) is the capital of the [Community of Madrid](69802.md) and of the [Kingdom of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Spain) as a whole and [Rome](25458.md) is the capital of [Italy](14532.md) and of the region of [Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio).

In the [Federal Republic of Germany](11867.md), each of its constituent [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_state) (or [*Länder*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany), plural of *Land*) has its own capital city, such as [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden), [Wiesbaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiesbaden), [Mainz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainz), [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf), [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), and [Munich](19058.md), as do all of the republics of the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation). The national capitals of Germany and Russia (the [Stadtstaat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-state#Non-sovereign_city-states) of [Berlin](3354.md) and the [federal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_cities_of_Russia) of [Moscow](19004.md)) are also constituent states of both countries in their own right. Each of the [states of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Austria) and [cantons of Switzerland](180752.md) also have their own capital cities. [Vienna](55866.md), the national capital of [Austria](26964606.md), is also one of the states, while [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern) is the (*de facto*) capital of both [Switzerland](26748.md) and of the [Canton of Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Bern).

These cities satisfy one or both of the following criteria:

1. A deliberately [planned city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_city) that was built expressly to house the [seat of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_of_government), superseding a capital city that was in an established [population center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_population). There have been various reasons for this, including overcrowding in that major metropolitan area, and the desire to place the capital city in a location with a better climate (usually a less tropical one).

2. A town that was chosen as a compromise among two or more cities (or other political divisions), none of which was willing to concede to the other(s) the privilege of being the capital city. Usually, the new capital is geographically located roughly equidistant between the competing population centres.

### Compromise locations



The [Australian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Parliament) opened in the small town of [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra) in 1927 as a compromise between the largest cities, [Sydney](27862.md) and [Melbourne](17306237.md).

Some examples of the second situation (compromise locations) are:

- [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra), Australia, chosen as a compromise location between Melbourne and Sydney.

- [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), United States, founded as a compromise between more urbanized [Northern states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_United_States) and agrarian [Southern](179553.md) [slave states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_states_and_free_states) to share national power. The [Compromise of 1790](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compromise_of_1790) resulted in the passage of the [Residence Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residence_Act), which approved the creation of a national capital on the [Potomac River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac_River) on land ceded from [Maryland](18858.md) and [Virginia](32432.md).

  [^cite_note-Crew124-20]

- [Frankfort, Kentucky](57834.md), midway between [Louisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky) and [Lexington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Kentucky).

- [Ottawa](22219.md), Ontario, Canada, along the boundary between the two former colonies that formed the core of pre-[Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) Canada: primarily English-speaking [Upper Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Canada) and primarily French-speaking [Lower Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Canada). Today, this border separates the two most populous of Canada's ten modern provinces, [Ontario](22218.md) and [Quebec](7954867.md).

- [Tallahassee, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallahassee,_Florida), chosen as the midpoint between [Pensacola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensacola,_Florida) and [St. Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Augustine,_Florida), Florida's two largest cities at the time.

- [Wellington](33804.md) became the [capital city of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_of_New_Zealand) in 1865. It lies at the southern tip of the [North Island](62046.md) of New Zealand, the smaller of New Zealand's two main islands (which subsequently became the more populous island),

  [^cite_note-21]

  &#x20;immediately across [Cook Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Strait) from the [South Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Island). The previous capital, [Auckland](18660332.md), lies much further north in the North Island; the move followed a long argument for a more central location for parliament.

  [^cite_note-22]

- [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua), Nicaragua, chosen to appease rivals in [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n_\(Nicaragua\)) and [Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada_\(Nicaragua\)), which also were associated with the liberal and conservative political factions respectively.

- [Jefferson City, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_City,_Missouri), was selected as the state capital in 1821, the year after Missouri was admitted to the Union, due to its central location within the state. It is almost halfway between Missouri's two largest cities, [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri) in the west and [St. Louis](27687.md) in the east, although Kansas City was not incorporated until 1850.

Changes in a nation's political regime sometimes result in the designation of a new capital. [Akmola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astana) (renamed Astana in 1998) became the capital of [Kazakhstan](16642.md) in 1997, following the [collapse of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1991. [Naypyidaw](2270051.md) was founded in [Burma](19457.md)'s interior as the former capital, [Rangoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangoon), was claimed to be overcrowded.[^cite_note-pedrosa-23]

## Unusual capital city arrangements

See also: [List of countries with multiple capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_with_multiple_capitals)



The [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Switzerland), the seat of [Switzerland](26748.md)'s judiciary, is in [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne), although the executive and legislature are located in [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern).



[Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_House,_Singapore) in [Singapore](27318.md); as a [city-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-state), Singapore requires no specific capital.



The [Blue Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Palace), the official residence of [Montenegro](20760.md)'s president, is in [Cetinje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetinje), although the executive and legislature are located in [Podgorica](172136.md).

A few nation-states have multiple capitals, and there are also several states that have no capital. Some have a city as the capital but with most government agencies elsewhere.

There is also a [ghost town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_town) which is currently the *[de jure](43594.md)* capital of a territory: [Plymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth,_Montserrat) in [Montserrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montserrat).

- [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize): [Belmopan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmopan) was designated the national capital of the then [British Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Honduras) in 1971, but most government offices and embassies are still located in [Belize City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize_City).

- [Canary Islands](5717.md) ([Spain](26667.md)): Until 1927, the capital of the [Province of Canarias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Canary_Islands) was [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife). When the Canary Islands became an autonomous community in 1982, Santa Cruz de Tenerife and [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria) were both given capital status.

### Capitals that are not the seat of government

There are several countries where, for various reasons, the official capital and [seat of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_of_government) are separated:

- [Benin](3459.md): [Porto-Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto-Novo) is the official capital, but [Cotonou](56647.md) is the seat of government.

- [Bolivia](3462.md): [Sucre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucre) is the [constitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bolivia) capital, and the [Supreme Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Tribunal_of_Justice_\(Bolivia\)) is located in Sucre, making it the judicial capital. The *[Palacio Quemado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palacio_Quemado)*, the [Plurinational Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurinational_Legislative_Assembly) and [Plurinational Electoral Organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurinational_Electoral_Organ) are located in [La Paz](42821.md), making it the seat of government.

- [Ivory Coast](57744.md): [Yamoussoukro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamoussoukro) was designated the national capital in 1983, but most government offices and embassies are still located in [Abidjan](21371767.md).

- [Netherlands](21148.md): [Amsterdam](844.md) is the constitutional national capital even though the [Cabinet of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_Netherlands), the [States General of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_General_of_the_Netherlands), the [High Council of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoge_Raad_der_Nederlanden), the [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_of_the_Netherlands), and the [Noordeinde Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noordeinde_Palace) of the [King](100384.md) are all located in [The Hague](30269.md), as are all the [embassies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_in_the_Netherlands). (*For more details see: [Capital of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_of_the_Netherlands)*.)

Some historical examples of similar arrangements, where the recognized capital was not the official seat of government:

- [Kingdom of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England): The traditional capital was the [City of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London), while [Westminster](37432.md), outside of the boundaries of the City of London, was the seat of government. They are both today part of the urban core of [Greater London](45367389.md).

- [Kingdom of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France): The traditional capital was [Paris](22989.md), though from 1682 to 1789 the seat of government was at the [Palace of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Versailles), located in a rural area southwest of Paris.

## Capital as symbol



[Mariehamn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariehamn), capital city of [Åland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%85land), a [demilitarized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demilitarized) archipelago with [self-governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance)

With the rise of the modern [nation-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state), the capital city has become a [symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol) for the [state](23604120.md) and its [government](12229.md), and imbued with political meaning. Unlike [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) capitals, which were declared wherever a [monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch) held their court, the selection, relocation, founding, or capture of a modern capital city is a highly symbolic event. For example:

- The ruined and almost uninhabited [Athens](1216.md) was made capital of [newly independent Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Greece) in 1834, four years after the country gained its independence, with the [romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism) notion of reviving the glory of [Ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece).

  [^cite_note-43]

  &#x20;Similarly, following the [Cold War](325329.md) and [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification), [Berlin](3354.md) once again became the capital of Germany.

  [^cite_note-44]

  &#x20;Other restored capital cities include [Moscow](19004.md) after the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution).

- A symbolic relocation of a capital city to a geographically or demographically peripheral location may be for either [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_geography) or [strategic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_geography) reasons (sometimes known as a *forward capital* or *spearhead capital*). [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) moved his government from [Moscow](19004.md) to [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md) to give the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) a [European](9239.md) orientation.

  [^cite_note-45]

  &#x20;The economically significant city of [Nafplio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nafplion) became the first capital of [Greece](12108.md), when Athens was an unimportant village.

  [^cite_note-46]

  &#x20;The [Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) emperors moved their capital to [Beijing](18603746.md) from the more central [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) to help supervise the border with the Mongols. During the 1857 rebellion, [Indian rebels](14533.md) considered [Delhi](37756.md) their capital, and [Bahadur Shah Zafar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahadur_Shah_Zafar) was proclaimed emperor, but the ruling [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India) had their capital in [Calcutta](47905.md). In 1877, the British formally held a '[Durbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Durbar)' in Delhi, proclaiming [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria) as '[Empress of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_of_India)'. Delhi finally became the colonial capital after the [Coronation Durbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Durbar) of King-Emperor [George V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_V) in 1911, continuing as independent India's capital from 1947. Other examples include [Abuja](56823.md), [Astana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astana), [Brasília](4752.md), [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki), [Islamabad](51153.md), [Naypyidaw](2270051.md), and [Yamoussoukro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamoussoukro).

- The selection or founding of a "neutral" capital city, one unencumbered by regional or political identities, was meant to represent the unity of a new state when [Ankara](802.md), [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern), [Brasília](4752.md), [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra), [Madrid](41188263.md), [Ottawa](22219.md) and [Washington](108956.md) became capital cities. Sometimes, the location of a new capital city was chosen to terminate actual or potential squabbling between various entities, such as in the cases of Brasília, Canberra, Ottawa, Washington, Wellington and [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua).

- The British-built town of [New Delhi](51585.md) represented a simultaneous break and continuity with the past, the location of Delhi being where many imperial capitals were built (Indraprastha, Dhillika, and Shahjahanabad) but the actual capital being the new British-built town designed by [Edwin Lutyens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Lutyens).

- Wellington, on the southwestern tip of the [North Island](62046.md) of New Zealand, replaced the much more northerly city of [Auckland](18660332.md) to place the national capital close to the [South Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Island) and hence to placate its residents, many of whom had sympathies with separatism.

- During the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), tremendous resources were expended to defend Washington, D.C., which bordered on the [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) (with the [Commonwealth of Virginia](32432.md)), from Confederate attack even though the relatively small federal government could easily have been moved elsewhere. Likewise, great resources were expended by the Confederacy in defending the Confederate capital from attack by the Union, in its exposed location of [Richmond, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia), barely 100 miles (160 km) south of Washington, D.C.

  [^cite_note-47]

- Two national capitals refer to another sovereign state. The name of [Tallinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallinn), the capital of [Estonia](28222445.md), is thought to be derived from *Taani linn*, originally meaning "Danish Castle" and now "Danish Town" in [Estonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonian_language), named after the [Toompea Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toompea_Castle), which [Denmark](76972.md) controlled in 1219–1227, 1238–1332 and in 1340–1346.

  [^cite_note-48]

  &#x20;[Port of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Spain), the capital of [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md), was named so in [Spanish](26825.md) by the first settlers from [Spain](26667.md) in the 16th century.

  [^cite_note-49]

  &#x20;See [List of national capital city name etymologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_capital_city_name_etymologies) for more.

## Capitals in military strategy



[Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople), the capital of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), was the final part of the empire to fall to the [Ottoman Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turks) due to its strong defences.

The capital city is usually but not always a primary target in a war, as capturing it usually guarantees capture of much of the enemy government, victory for the attacking forces, or at the very least demoralization for the defeated forces.

In [ancient China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_China), where governments were massive centralized bureaucracies with little flexibility on the provincial level, a [dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Dynasties) could easily be toppled with the fall of its capital. In the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms) period, both [Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_Han) and [Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Wu) fell when their respective capitals of [Chengdu](170375.md) and [Jianye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jianye_District) fell. The [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) relocated its capital from [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing) to [Beijing](18603746.md), where they could more effectively control the generals and troops guarding the borders from [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) and [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchus). The Ming was destroyed when [Li Zicheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Zicheng) took their seat of power, and this pattern repeats itself in Chinese history, until the fall of the traditional [Confucian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucian) monarchy in the 20th century. After the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty)'s collapse, decentralization of authority and improved transportation and communication technologies allowed both the [Chinese Nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Nationalists) and [Chinese Communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communists) to rapidly relocate capitals and keep their leadership structures intact during the great crisis of [Japanese invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War).

National capitals were arguably less important as military objectives in other parts of the world, including the West, because of socioeconomic trends toward localized authority, a strategic modus operandi especially popular after the development of [feudalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism) and reaffirmed by the development of democratic and capitalistic philosophies. In 1204, after the Latin [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Crusade) captured the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) capital, [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople), Byzantine forces were able to regroup in several provinces; provincial noblemen managed to reconquer the capital after 60 years and preserve the empire for another 200 years after that. The [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) forces sacked various [American](3434750.md) capitals repeatedly during the [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) and [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), but American forces could still carry on fighting from the countryside, where they enjoyed support from local governments and the traditionally independent civilian frontiersmen. Exceptions to these generalizations include highly centralized states such as [France](5843419.md), whose centralized bureaucracies could effectively coordinate far-flung resources, giving the state a powerful advantage over less coherent rivals, but risking utter ruin if the capital were taken.
