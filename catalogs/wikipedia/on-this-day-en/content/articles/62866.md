The&#x20;**&#x20;United States Department of Energy** (**DOE**) is an [executive department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_executive_departments) of the [U.S. federal government](195149.md) that oversees national energy policy and [energy production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_production), the research and development of [nuclear power](22153.md), the military's [nuclear weapons program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons_of_the_United_States), [nuclear reactor](22151.md) production for the [United States Navy](20518076.md), energy-related research, and [energy conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conservation).

The DOE was created in 1977 in the aftermath of the [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis). It sponsors more physical science research than any other U.S. federal agency, the majority of which is conducted through its system of [National Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy_National_Laboratories).[^cite_note-EnergySC-3][^cite_note-4] The DOE also directs research in [genomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genomics), with the [Human Genome Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Genome_Project) originating from a DOE initiative.[^cite_note-5]

The department is headed by the [secretary of energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_Energy), who reports directly to the [president of the United States](24113.md) and is a member of the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States). The current Secretary of energy is [Chris Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Wright_\(energy_executive\)), who has served in the position since February 2025.[^cite_note-6] The department's headquarters are in southwestern [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), in the [James V. Forrestal Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_V._Forrestal_Building), with additional offices in [Germantown, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germantown,_Maryland).

## History

### Formation and consolidation

In 1942, during [World War II](32927.md), the United States started the [Manhattan Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Project) to develop the [atomic bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bomb) under the [U.S. Army Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army_Corps_of_Engineers). After the war, in 1946, the [Atomic Energy Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Atomic_Energy_Commission) (AEC) was created to control the future of the project.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] The [Atomic Energy Act of 1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_Energy_Act_of_1946) also created the framework for the first [National Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Energy_national_laboratories). Among other nuclear projects, the AEC produced fabricated uranium fuel cores at locations such as [Fernald Feed Materials Production Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernald_Feed_Materials_Production_Center) in [Cincinnati, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati,_Ohio).[^cite_note-NCLO950-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The [Energy Reorganization Act of 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Reorganization_Act_of_1974) split the responsibilities of the AEC into the new [Nuclear Regulatory Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Regulatory_Commission), which was charged with regulating the nuclear power industry, and the [Energy Research and Development Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Research_and_Development_Administration), which was assigned to manage the nuclear weapon, naval reactor, and energy development programs.[^cite_note-12]

The [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis) called attention to the need to consolidate energy policy.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] In 1977, President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) signed into law the [Department of Energy Organization Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Energy_Organization_Act), which established the Department of Energy.[^cite_note-Relyea2003-16] The new agency, which began operations on October 1, 1977, consolidated the [Federal Energy Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Energy_Administration), the [Energy Research and Development Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Research_and_Development_Administration), the [Federal Power Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Power_Commission), and programs of various other agencies. Former Secretary of Defense [James Schlesinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Schlesinger), who served under Presidents Nixon and Ford during the [Vietnam War](32611.md), was appointed as the first secretary.

President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) proposed the Department of Energy with the goal of promoting energy conservation and energy independence, and developing alternative sources of energy to reduce the use of [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel).[^cite_note-17] With the future of international energy uncertain for America, Carter acted quickly to have the department come into action in the first year of his presidency. This was an extremely important issue of the time as the oil crisis was causing shortages and [inflation](38286.md).[^cite_note-18] With the [Three Mile Island accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Mile_Island_accident), Carter was able to intervene with the help of the department. Through the DOE, Carter was able to make changes within the [Nuclear Regulatory Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Regulatory_Commission), including improving management and procedures, since nuclear energy and weapons are responsibilities of the department.[^cite_note-19]

### Weapon plans stolen

Main article: [Wen Ho Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wen_Ho_Lee)

In December 1999, the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) began investigating how China acquired plans for a specific nuclear device. [Wen Ho Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wen_Ho_Lee), a scientist at [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory), was accused of passing nuclear secrets to the Chinese government. Before any formal charges, federal officials, including Energy Secretary [Bill Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Richardson), publicly identified Lee as a suspect. The U.S. Congress held hearings on the DOE’s handling of the case, with some senators proposing that nuclear security be managed by an independent agency.[^cite_note-slate-20] Of the 59 charges brought against Lee, all but one were later dropped after investigators concluded the leaked plans could not have come from him. Lee later received a \$1.6 million settlement from the federal government and several news organizations.[^cite_note-cnn-21]

In the wake of the scandal[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] and allegations that direct management by the Department of Energy had resulted in U.S. nuclear secrets being leaked to China, the [National Nuclear Security Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Nuclear_Security_Administration) was proposed.[^cite_note-24] Originally proposed to be independent, NNSA was instead chartered as a semiautonomous agency within the Department of Energy to be headed by an administrator reporting to the Secretary of Energy.

### Loan guarantee program of 2005

In 2001, [American Solar Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Solar_Challenge) was sponsored by the DOE and the [National Renewable Energy Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Renewable_Energy_Laboratory).[^cite_note-25] After the 2005 race, the DOE discontinued its sponsorship.[^cite_note-26]

Title XVII of [Energy Policy Act of 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Policy_Act_of_2005) authorizes the DOE to issue loan guarantees to eligible projects that "avoid, reduce, or sequester air pollutants or anthropogenic emissions of [greenhouse gases](21350772.md)" and "employ new or significantly improved technologies as compared to technologies in service in the United States at the time the guarantee is issued".[^cite_note-27] In loan guarantees, a conditional commitment requires to meet an equity commitment, as well as other conditions, before the loan guarantee is completed.[^cite_note-28]

In September 2008, the DOE, the [Nuclear Threat Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Threat_Initiative) (NTI), the [Institute of Nuclear Materials Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Nuclear_Materials_Management) (INMM), and the [International Atomic Energy Agency](14984.md) (IAEA) partnered to develop and launch the [World Institute for Nuclear Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Institute_for_Nuclear_Security) (WINS), an international [non-governmental organization](46539.md) designed to provide a forum to share best practices in strengthening the security and safety of [nuclear](2181563.md) and [radioactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive) materials and facilities.[^cite_note-29]

In December 2024, the Loan Programs Office announced it would extend the largest loan ever sanctioned – a \$15 billion (US) low-interest loan to support the modernization of [Pacific Gas & Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Gas_and_Electric_Company)’s hydroelectric power structure, enhance transmission lines critical for renewable energy integration, data center operations, and the growing fleet of electric vehicles. Initially requested as a \$30 billion (US) loan, the amount was reduced due to concerns over the company’s repayment capacity.[^cite_note-30]

In June 2026, the DOE announced \$17.5 billion in loans to speed the building of 10 new large nuclear reactors across the U.S. The reactors will use Westinghouse’s AP1000 design, each capable of generating 1.1 GW of electricity, with the combined output from all reactors projected to power nearly 10 million American households.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

### Genesis Mission and AI partnerships (2025)

In November 2025, President Donald Trump signed an executive order launching the [Genesis Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genesis_Mission), an initiative involving the Department of Energy and U.S. national laboratories to apply artificial intelligence and advanced computing to accelerate scientific research and support energy and national security-related capabilities.[^cite_note-33] On December 18, 2025, the Department of Energy announced collaboration agreements with 24 organizations to support the initiative.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

The department is under the control and supervision of a [United States Secretary of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_Energy), a political appointee of the [President of the United States](24113.md). The Energy Secretary is assisted in managing the department by a [United States Deputy Secretary of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Deputy_Secretary_of_Energy), also appointed by the president, who assumes the duties of the secretary in the secretary's absence. The department also has three under secretaries, each appointed by the president, who oversee the major areas of the department's work. The president also appoints seven officials with the rank of Assistant Secretary of Energy who have line management responsibility for major organizational elements of the department. The Energy Secretary assigns their functions and duties.

### Symbolism in the seal

Excerpt from the [Code of Federal Regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_of_Federal_Regulations), in [Title 10: Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_Code_of_Federal_Regulations):[^cite_note-40]

The official seal of the Department of Energy "includes a green shield bisected by a gold-colored lightning bolt, on which is emblazoned a gold-colored symbolic [sun](26751.md), [atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom), [oil derrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_derrick), [windmill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windmill), and [dynamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamo). It is crested by the white head of an [eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle), atop a white rope. Both appear on a blue field surrounded by [concentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentric) circles in which the name of the agency, in gold, appears on a green background."

"The [eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle) represents the care in planning and the purposefulness of efforts required to respond to the Nation's increasing demands for [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy). The [sun](26751.md), [atom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atom), [oil derrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_derrick), [windmill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windmill), and [dynamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamo) serve as representative technologies whose enhanced development can help meet these demands. The rope represents the cohesiveness in the development of the technologies and their link to our future capabilities. The [lightning](61344.md) bolt represents the power of the natural forces from which energy is derived and the Nation's challenge in harnessing the forces."

"The color scheme is derived from nature, symbolizing both the source of energy and the support of man's existence. The blue field represents [air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air) and [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water), green represents [mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) resources and the [earth](9228.md) itself, and gold represents the creation of energy in the release of natural forces. By invoking this [symbolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol), the color scheme represents the Nation's commitment to meet its energy needs in a manner consistent with the [preservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_\(ethic\)) of the [natural environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_environment)."

## Budget

On May 7, 2009 President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) unveiled a \$26.4 billion budget request for DOE for fiscal year (FY) 2010, including \$2.3 billion for the DOE [Office of Energy Efficiency and Renewable Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Energy_Efficiency_and_Renewable_Energy) (EERE). That budget aimed to substantially expand the use of [renewable energy](25784.md) sources while improving energy transmission infrastructure. It also proposed significant investments in [hybrids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_electric_vehicle) and [plug-in hybrids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_hybrid), [smart grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_grid) technologies, and scientific research and innovation.[^cite_note-energy1-52]

As part of the \$789 billion economic stimulus package in the [American Recovery and Reinvestment Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Recovery_and_Reinvestment_Act) of 2009, Congress provided Energy with an additional \$38.3 billion for fiscal years 2009 and 2010, adding about 75 percent to Energy's annual budgets. Most of the stimulus spending was in the form of grants and contracts. For fiscal year 2013, each of the operating units of the Department of Energy operated with the following budgets:[^cite_note-53]

| Division | Funding |
| --- | --- |
| Nuclear Security | \$11.5 |
| Energy and Environment | \$9.5 |
| Science | \$4.9 |
| Management | \$0.25 |
| Other | \$0.85 |
| **Total** | **\$28** |

In March 2018, Energy Secretary Rick Perry testified to a Senate panel about the Trump administration's DOE budget request for fiscal year 2019. The budget request prioritized nuclear security while making large cuts to [energy efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient_energy_use) and renewable energy programs. The proposal was a \$500 million increase in funds over fiscal year 2017. It "promotes innovations like a new Office of Cybersecurity, Energy Security, and Emergency Response (CESER) and gains for the Office of Fossil Energy. Investments would be made to strengthen the National Nuclear Security Administration and modernize the nuclear force, as well as in weapons activities and advanced computing." However, the budget for the Office of Energy Efficiency and Renewable Energy would be lowered to \$696 million under the plan, down from \$1.3 billion in fiscal year 2017. Overall, the department's energy and related programs would be cut by \$1.9 billion.[^cite_note-54]

## Programs and contracts

### Energy Savings Performance Contract

Main article: [Energy Savings Performance Contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Savings_Performance_Contract)

Energy Savings Performance Contracts (ESPCs) are contracts under which a contractor designs, constructs, and obtains the necessary financing for an energy savings project, and the federal agency makes payments over time to the contractor from the savings in the agency's utility bills. The contractor guarantees the energy improvements will generate savings, and after the contract ends, all continuing cost savings accrue to the federal agency.[^cite_note-55]

### Energy Innovation Hubs

Energy Innovation Hubs are [multi-disciplinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymath), meant to advance highly promising areas of energy science and technology from their early stages of research to the point that the risk level will be low enough for industry to commercialize the technologies.[^cite_note-energy1-52] The Consortium for Advanced Simulation of Light Water Reactors (CASL) was the first DOE Energy Innovation Hub established in July 2010, for the purpose of providing advanced modeling and simulation (M\&S) solutions for commercial nuclear reactors.[^cite_note-56]

The 2009 DOE budget includes \$280 million to fund eight Energy Innovation Hubs, each of which is focused on a particular energy challenge. Two of the eight hubs are included in the [EERE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Energy_Efficiency_and_Renewable_Energy) budget and will focus on integrating smart materials, designs, and systems into buildings to better conserve energy and on designing and discovering new concepts and materials needed to convert solar energy into electricity. Another two hubs, included in the DOE Office of Science budget, were created to tackle the challenges of devising advanced methods of energy storage and creating fuels directly from sunlight without the use of plants or microbes. Yet another hub was made to develop "smart" materials to allow the electrical grid to adapt and respond to changing conditions.[^cite_note-energy1-52]

In 2012, the DOE awarded \$120 million to the [Ames Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ames_Laboratory) to start a new EIH, the Critical Materials Institute, which will focus on improving the supply of [rare earth elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earth_element).[^cite_note-NetworkWorld_2013-57]

### Advanced Research Projects Agency-Energy

See also: [ARPA-E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARPA-E)

ARPA-E was officially created by the [America COMPETES Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_COMPETES_Act), authored by Congressman [Bart Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bart_Gordon),[^cite_note-58] within the United States Department of Energy (DOE) in 2007, though without a budget. The initial budget of about \$400 million was a part of the [economic stimulus bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Recovery_and_Reinvestment_Act_of_2009) of February 2009.[^cite_note-59]

### High Performance Computing for Energy Innovation (HPC4EI)

**[HPC4EI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPC4EI)** is an initiative that leverages [supercomputing](37153.md) resources from DOE national laboratories to support industry partnerships in advancing energy-efficient manufacturing, materials development, and decarbonisation technologies.[^cite_note-HPC4EI-Site-60][^cite_note-DOE-FY22-61][^cite_note-62]
