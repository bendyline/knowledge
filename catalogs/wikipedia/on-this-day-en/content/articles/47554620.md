## 2007

- As of January 6, 2007, after the [Fall of Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Mogadishu) and [Kismayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Kismayo) to the TFG, the leaders of the Shabaab were in hiding still at large.

  [^cite_note-ETHIOPIAN-TROOPS-TO-LEAVE-SOMALIA-WITHIN-WEEKS-6]

  &#x20;A member of the disbanded group said they once numbered about 1,000 (lower than other claims by former members), but they do not have any weapons any more. Still, there was support for the call of leaders to maintain *jihad* against the Ethiopians and secular government.

  [^cite_note-DISBANDED-MILITANT-YOUTH-GROUP-IN-SOMALIA-SUPPORT-AL-QAIDA-MESSAGE-7]

- January 19: Pro-Islamic Courts Union website featured a video describing the reformation of the ICU into the "Popular Resistance Movement in the Land of the Two Migrations" (PRM), alternatively translated and referred to in press reports as the "[Somali People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_people)'s Insurgent Movement" (SPIM) or "Somali People's Resistance Movement" (SPRM). On January 24, [Sheikh Abdikadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Abdikadir?action=edit\&redlink=1) was announced to be its commander of the [Banadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banadir) region.

  [^cite_note-SOMALIA-ENTERS-A-REVOLUTIONARY-CYCLE-8]

- January 31: Al-Shabaab made a video warning [African Union peacekeepers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMISOM) to avoid coming to Somalia, claiming "Somalia is not a place where you will earn a salary—it is a place where you will die."

  [^cite_note-SOMALI-EXTREMISTS-POST-VIDEO-WARNING-TO-PEACEKEEPERS-9]

- February 9: 800 Somali demonstrators in north [Mogadishu](60154.md), where Islamist support was strongest, burned U.S., Ethiopian, and [Ugandan](31816.md) flags in protest of the proposed [African Union](10634933.md) (AU) led and United Nations endorsed [peacekeeping](84617.md) mission, known as [AMISOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_to_Somalia). "Abdirisaq", a masked representative of the resistance group, the PRM, said Ethiopian troops would be attacked in their hotels.

## 2008

Main article: [War in Somalia (2006–2009)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Somalia_\(2006%E2%80%932009\))

- February 28: [United States Department of State](31975.md) designates al-Shabaab as a [Foreign Terrorist Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._State_Department_list_of_Foreign_Terrorist_Organizations) in accordance with section 219 of the U.S. [Immigration and Nationality Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_and_Nationality_Act_of_1965) (INA).

  [^cite_note-dos-13]

- August: **[Battle of Kismayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kismayo_\(2008\))** – After several days of fighting in which scores of deaths were reported, al-Shabaab fighters defeated the militia of [Barre Adan Shire Hiiraale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barre_Adan_Shire_Hiiraale) and took control of the port city. [Kismayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kismayo) had been held by the TFG since January 2007.

  [^cite_note-14]

  &#x20;The fighting in Kismayo is reported to have [displaced](477535.md) an estimated 35,000 people. After the withdrawal of Hiiraale's fighters, al-Shabaab commence a peaceful disarmament process targeting local armed groups that had been contributing to insecurity in Kismayo.

  [^cite_note-15]

  &#x20;The group has been blamed or claimed responsibility for, among other attacks, the **[February 2008 Bosaso bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2008_Bosaso_bombings)** and the **[2008 Hargeisa–Bosaso bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Hargeisa%E2%80%93Bosaso_bombings)**.

  [^cite_note-16]

  [^cite_note-17]

  &#x20;By late 2008, it was estimated that the group controlled the whole of southern Somalia, except for some pockets of Mogadishu. This was more territory than that controlled by the [Islamic Courts Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Courts_Union) at the height of their power.

  [^cite_note-18]

- October 27: **[Stoning of Aisha Ibrahim Duhulow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoning_of_Aisha_Ibrahim_Duhulow)** – After being sentenced to [public execution](4733727.md) by al-Shabaab courts, Aisha Ibrahim Duhulow was stoned to death by around 50 militants in the southern port town of Kismayo. Initial reports of the killing stated that she was a 23-year-old woman found guilty of [adultery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adultery). However, Duhulow's father asserted that she was only 13, under the age of marriage eligibility, and that she was arrested and executed after trying to report that she had been [raped](7980471.md).

  [^cite_note-19]

  &#x20;The stoning took place in a public stadium with roughly 1,000 bystanders in it, several of whom attempted to intervene but were in the process shot by the militants.

  [^cite_note-BBC2-20]

  [^cite_note-AM-21]

- December: [Anwar al-Awlaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_al-Awlaki) sends a [communique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communique) to al-Shabaab, congratulating them. He thanks them for "giving us a living example of how we as Muslims should proceed to change our situation. The ballot has failed us, but the bullet has not". In conclusion, he writes: "if my circumstances would have allowed, I would not have hesitated in joining you and being a soldier in your ranks".

## 2009

Main article: [2009 timeline of the Somali Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_timeline_of_the_Somali_Civil_War)

- January: UN-sponsored peace talks concluded in [Djibouti](17207794.md) with Ethiopians agreeing to withdraw from Somalia and [Islamist](15012.md) leader [Sharif Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_Ahmed) "agreed to stop fighting."

- January 31: Shiekh Sharif Ahmed is elected present of the Transitional Federal Government.

  [^cite_note-24]

  &#x20;Opposing any negotiated settlement with Ethiopia, al-Shabaab declares war on him.

  [^cite_note-MostFailedNYer-25]

- February 22: [2009 African Union base bombings in Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_African_Union_base_bombings_in_Mogadishu): Al-Shabaab carried out a [suicide](18835454.md) [car bomb](155282.md) attack against an [African Union](10634933.md) [military base](843566.md) in [Mogadishu](60154.md), killing at least six [Burundian](21490998.md) [peacekeepers](84617.md).

  [^cite_note-26]

- May: **[Battle of Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_\(2009\))** – Al-Shabaab, along with allied group [Hizbul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hizbul_Islam), launched a major offensive to take over Mogadishu, leaving hundreds killed and injured and tens of thousands displaced. The group made large gains, taking over most of the capital.

- June 18: **[2009 Beledweyne bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Beledweyne_bombing)** – Al-Shabaab claimed the bombing of the Hotel Medina in [Beledweyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beledweyne), which killed 35 people including Somali security minister [Omar Hashi Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Hashi_Aden).

- June 18: U.S. Diplomatic Security Daily cable, 09STATE63860, includes the death of [Omar Hashi Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Hashi_Aden) and al Shabaab's shift of tactics to include suicide bombings.

  [^cite_note-27]

- July 8: A video message featuring an American commander in al-Shabaab, Abu Mansur al-Amriki, is released in which he responds and denounces U.S. President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama)'s June 2009 Cairo speech to Arabs and Muslims.

  [^cite_note-28]

- July 26: Al-Shabaab members ban [samosas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samosa) (*sambusas*) in regions they control, deeming the snack too Christian on account of its triangular, allegedly [Holy Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity)-like shape.

  [^cite_note-29]

- August 4: **[Holsworthy Barracks terror plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holsworthy_Barracks_terror_plot)** – Four men allegedly connected with al-Shabaab in [Melbourne](17306237.md), Australia were charged over a plan to storm the [Holsworthy Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holsworthy_Barracks) with [automatic weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_weapon); and shoot [army](2795.md) personnel or others until they were killed or captured.

  [^cite_note-30]

  &#x20;Al-Shabaab has denied any connection with the men.

  [^cite_note-31]

  &#x20;It has subsequently been listed as a terrorist organisation in Australia.

  [^cite_note-32]

- August 11: Reuters reports residents in Marka complain "al Shabaab has been rounding up anyone seen with a silver or gold tooth and taking them to a masked man who then rips them out using basic tools." Residents told Reuters that al-Shabaab declared that since gold and silver teeth "are used for fashion and beauty," they are against Islam.

  [^cite_note-33]

- September 14: Members of the group were killed in a raid targeting [Saleh Ali Saleh Nabhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saleh_Ali_Saleh_Nabhan), who was also killed.

  [^cite_note-34]

  [^cite_note-35]

- September 17: **[2009 African Union base bombings in Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_African_Union_base_bombings_in_Mogadishu)** – The group claims a second bombing of an AU base, which killed 17 peacekeepers.

- September 20: Al-Shabaab released a 48-minute video, "Labayka Ya Usama," ("Here I am at Your Service, Usama") on transnational [jihadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadi) websites. The video is framed around [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden)'s March 2009 audio message "Fight On, O' Champions of Somalia," and features footage of African Union "atrocities" in Somalia and al-Shabaab units undergoing [military training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_training). Bin Laden and the Amir (leader) of al-Shabaab, Ahmed Abdi Godane, also known as Abu al-Zubayr, criticize Somalia's interim [president](516101.md), [Sharif Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_Ahmed), and the [religious scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_scholar) of Somalia ('ulama al-Sumaal) for [apostasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostasy). [Abu Mansoor al-Amriki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Mansoor_Al-Amriki), the American field commander in the group, is also briefly featured.

  [^cite_note-36]

- October 15: Al-Shabaab began publicly whipping women for wearing [bras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bra) that they claim violate Islam as they are deceptive. They sent gunmen into the streets of Mogadishu to round up any women who appear to be being deceptive. The women were then inspected by other women to see if they are being deceptive, if they are then they are ordered to stop.

  [^cite_note-37]

- November 1: Al-Shabaab announced the establishment of al-Quds Brigade, a [military unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_unit) specifically tasked with attacking [Israel](9282173.md) and Jewish interests in Africa. In a rally held the previous week in Mogadishu, a top al-Shabaab official said, "It is time to go for open war against Israel in order to drive them from the holy cities."

  [^cite_note-38]

- December 3: **[2009 Hotel Shamo bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Hotel_Shamo_bombing)** – Suspected of being behind the bombing of Hotel Shamo in Mogadishu, which kills 24, including three [government](36817379.md) ministers.

## 2011

- February 4: Al-Shabaab launch terrestrial news channel "Al-Kata'ib" to broadcast propaganda. The first footage shown is a recording of confessions of an alleged foreign spy captured in Somalia

  [^cite_note-56]

- March 5: Al-Shabaab loses control of the border town of Bulo Hawo in a joint offensive conducted by government forces working with AMISOM; the militia had controlled the city for two years beforehand. It was also reported that al-Shabaab was resisting against UN/Government forces for control of three of Mogadishu's sixteen districts, with six still remaining in their control.

  [^cite_note-57]

- March 16: [Abdikadir Yusuf Aar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdikadir_Yusuf_Aar) aka [Sheikh Qalbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdikadir_Yusuf_Aar) a senior al-Shabab official serving as the group's leader in [Juba](38364812.md) and [Gedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gedo) region was killed in [Mogadishu](60154.md).

  [^cite_note-58]

  [^cite_note-59]

- April 3: Al-Shabaab loses control of the town Dhobley near the [Kenyan](188171.md) border. [TFG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Federal_Government) forces together with [Raskamboni movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raskamboni_movement) had been fighting for several days before they took control of the town with support from helicopters of the [Kenya Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Air_Force).

  [^cite_note-Hiiraan_Online-60]

  [^cite_note-Shabelle-61]

  &#x20;The same day as al-Shabab lost control of the town Hassan Abdurrahman Gumarey, an al-Shabaab official was killed in action ([KIA](368513.md)) in Dhobley.

  [^cite_note-Shabelle2-62]

- June 11: Wanted al-Shabaab operative and [al-Qaeda](1921.md) collaborator [Fazul Abdullah Mohammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fazul_Abdullah_Mohammed) is killed by security forces of the [SNA](3972951.md) in [Afgooye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afgooye) northwest of Mogadishu; one other terrorist was killed and \$40,000 worth of U.S. dollars are recovered.

- July 5: Al-Shabaab officially lifts its ban on some [aid agencies](1064581.md), but upholds it later in the month vis-a-vis certain organizations. As an explanation for this discrepancy, the group's spokesman Sheikh Ali Dhere indicates that the group has no issue with allowing both Muslim and non-Muslim individuals from helping the drought-impacted people as long as those groups harbor no ulterior motives in doing so. Dhere adds that his organization believes that many aid agencies are exaggerating their relief requirements so as to satisfy their own selfish objectives. He also suggests that the actual nature of many of the relief operations are twofold: first, some of the aid workers are in effect attacking as "spies", while others, including the UN, he charges have a tacit political agenda not in keeping with what they claim to be doing. In addition, Dhere alleges that aid agencies that are providing assistance in neighboring countries are attempting to siphon away the various Muslim peoples of Somalia in order to more easily indoctrinate them into Christianity. Al-Shabaab members are reported to have intimidated, kidnapped and killed some aid workers, leading to a suspension of humanitarian operations and an exodus of relief agents.

  [^cite_note-Aasasrbsagft-63]

  &#x20;As a result, AU troops step up efforts in late July 2011 to protect civilians and aid workers from attacks.

  [^cite_note-64]

- August 6: The Transitional Federal Government's troops and their AMISOM allies reportedly manage to capture all of Mogadishu from the al-Shabaab militants. Witnesses report al-Shabaab vehicles abandoning their bases in the capital for the south-central city of [Baidoa](1648752.md). The group's spokesman Sheikh Ali Mohamud Rage describes the exodus as a tactical retreat, and vows to continue the insurgency against the national government. Observers suggest that the pullout may at least in part have been caused by internal ideological rifts in the rebel organization.

  [^cite_note-65]

- August 9: An ideological split reportedly emerges within al-Shabaab's leadership. Muktar Ali Robow, Hassan Dahir and other southern commanders who hail from the areas of the country worst-hit by the effects of the drought, reportedly want to extend relief efforts to the impacted peoples. However, they are overruled by Ahmed Abdi Godane, a northern commander credited with strengthening the group's ties with al-Qaeda. Observers suggest that the move is a manifestation of Godane's increasing paranoia since the assassination of his close ally Fazul Abdullah Mohammed, the latter of whom Godane suspects was set up by his foes within the organization.

  [^cite_note-Csfdafbtas-66]

  &#x20;Hassan Dahir also proposes that the group change its tactics by "abandoning Mogadishu to launch Taliban style attacks in Pakistan and Afghanistan", but is rebuffed by leaders within the organization.

  [^cite_note-Aswpbirao-67]

- October 4: A truck carrying explosive was driven into a government ministry in Somalia killing 139 and injuring 93. The group has claimed responsibility for these attacks.

  [^cite_note-68]

- October 16: A [coordinated operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Linda_Nchi) against al-Shabaab begins between the [Somali military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Somalia) and the [Kenyan military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Defence_Forces), as Kenyan troops cross over into Somalia after having met with Somali military officials.

  [^cite_note-69]

- October 20: Two women, Amina Farah Ali and Hawo Mohamed Hassan, both from [Rochester, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_Minnesota) were arrested for sending money and fighters to aid the group.

  [^cite_note-70]

- November 26: Martyrship video of [Mansur Nasir al Bihani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansur_Nasir_al_Bihani), an al-Qaeda veteran of Afghanistan, who trained al Shaabab fighters, is *"killed in a clash with American forces off the coast of Somalia."*

  [^cite_note-LongWarSonOf2011-12-30-71]

- December 31: The Transitional Federal Government retakes control of the central town of [Beledweyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beledweyne) from the al-Shabaab militants. [Somali National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Somalia) (SNA) soldiers and around 3,000 allied [Ethiopian army](9405.md) troops attacked the city in the early morning, capturing it after hours of fighting. Around 20 people were killed in the battle, mainly consisting of Ethiopian soldiers and al-Shabaab insurgents.

## 2012

- January 6: US Army veteran Craig Baxam is charged in Maryland with attempting to lend material support to al-Shabaab. Baxam had been en route to Somalia when he was detained in Kenya on December 23 and repatriated to the United States.

  [^cite_note-73]

## 2024

- January 10: Al-Shabaab extremists captured a [United Nations](31769.md) helicopter after it made an emergency landing in al-Shabaab territory killing one person and capturing five others in the process. The seventh person on the helicopter became presumably dead after they went missing. After capturing the helicopter, extremists looted the helicopter and confiscated important items before burning it.

  [^cite_note-231]

- January 16: An al-Shabaab suicide bomber detonated his [suicide vest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_belt) in front of the al-Hindi restaurant outside the [Mayor of Mogadishu's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_Hussein_Jimaale) office in [Mogadishu](60154.md), [Somalia](27358.md), killing three people and injuring two others.

  [^cite_note-232]

- January 21: Three al-Shabaab militants are killed by a [United States](3434750.md) [drone strike](38212231.md) near [Kismayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kismayo), [Somalia](27358.md), after a request from the [Somali Government](36817379.md).

  [^cite_note-233]

- February 6: [2024 Mogadishu market bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mogadishu_market_bombing)&#x20;

  - Ten people are killed and at least twenty others are injured after four bombings inside the [Bakaara Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakaara_Market) in [Mogadishu](60154.md), [Somalia](27358.md). Al-Shabaab is the suspected perpetrators.

    [^cite_note-234]

- February 7: Three people are killed and at least ten others are injured after a suicide bombing in [Afgooye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afgooye), [Somalia](27358.md). Al-Shabaab is suspected as the perpetrator.

  [^cite_note-235]

- February 10: Five people are killed and ten others are injured after an al-Shabaab attacker opened fire at a military base before the attacker was killed in a shootout.

  [^cite_note-236]

- March 14: [2024 Mogadishu SYL Hotel attack and siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mogadishu_SYL_Hotel_attack_and_siege)&#x20;

  - An al-Shabaab suicide bomber detonated an explosive device outside the SYL Hotel in [Mogadishu](60154.md), [Somalia](27358.md), before five gunmen raided the hotel in a 13-hour siege. In total, fourteen people were killed, including all six attackers.

- March 19: 39 al-Shabaab leaders are killed after two [Somali National Army](3972951.md) airstrikes in [Afgooye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afgooye), [Somalia](27358.md).

  [^cite_note-237]

- March 23: [2024 Busley army base attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Busley_army_base_attack)&#x20;

  - On March 23, 2024, al-Shabaab insurgents attacked the [Somali Armed Forces](27366.md) base in Busley, [Lower Shabelle](185139.md), [Somalia](27358.md). Al-Shabaab claims they killed 57 soldiers, while the Somali Government claims 10 soldiers died in the attack. Ten insurgents were also killed.

    [^cite_note-238]

- June 8: [2024 El Dher attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_El_Dher_attack)&#x20;

  - Al-Shabaab militants attacked four military bases and overran the town of El Dher for several hours killing at least 25 people, including five [Somali National Army](3972951.md) soldiers.

- July 13: [2024 Mogadishu prison attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mogadishu_prison_attack)&#x20;

  - Eight people are killed and twenty-one others are injured after a shootout between Somali Armed Forces soldiers and al-Shabaab militants after militants tried to escape from prison.

    [^cite_note-239]

- July 14: [Top Coffee bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Coffee_bombing)&#x20;

  - Nine people were killed by a car bomb outside a Mogadishu café that was showing the [Euro 2024 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_final).

    [^cite_note-240]

- July 22: At least 35 [Somali Armed Forces](27366.md) soldiers and over 80 al-Shabaab militants were killed during three attacks on military bases near [Kismayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kismayo), [Jubaland](38364812.md), [Somalia](27358.md).

  [^cite_note-241]

- August 2: [2024 Lido Beach attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Lido_Beach_attack)&#x20;

  - At least 56 people, including a soldier and six attackers are killed after a [suicide bombing](18835454.md) and [mass shooting](31883778.md) at the Beach View Hotel on [Lido Beach, Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lido_Beach,_Mogadishu), [Somalia](27358.md).

    [^cite_note-242]

- August 16: A roadside explosion occurred in the Sariitow village area near [Awdiinle town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awdiinle), west of [Baidoa](1648752.md) in [South West State](483484.md), resulting in at least 12 deaths and nine injuries. The blast targeted a pickup truck used by security forces. The al-Shabaab militant group claimed responsibility for the attack.

  [^cite_note-243]

- August 17: [2024 Mogadishu tea shop bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mogadishu_tea_shop_bombing)&#x20;

  - At least twenty people were killed and over ten others were injured when a [suicide bomber](18835454.md) detonated an [improvised explosive device](265112.md) at a tea shop in [Mogadishu](60154.md), [Somalia](27358.md).

    [^cite_note-244]

  - Separate Incident: At least four people were killed and several others injured in a bomb explosion at a [khat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khat) market in the city center of [Afgooye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afgooye), [Lower Shabelle](185139.md). Eyewitnesses reported that two bombs were thrown into the market as traders gathered on the busy streets.

    [^cite_note-245]

- August 21: [Al-Shabaab](8726463.md) claimed responsibility for a [suicide car bombing](155282.md) that killed 10 people, including seven [NISA](81014.md) security officers and two civilians. Several other people were also injured. The explosion happened near a security checkpoint on the busy [Mogadishu](60154.md)–[Afgoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afgooye) highway.

  [^cite_note-246]

- August 31: Al-Shabaab bombed several businesses in [Mogadishu](60154.md), [Somalia](27358.md).

  [^cite_note-247]

- September 14: Five people are killed and eight others are injured in multiple roadside explosions in [Mogadishu](60154.md), [Somalia](27358.md). Al-Shabaab are the suspected perpetrators.

  [^cite_note-248]
