There are two [international borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_borders) between [Canada](5042916.md) and the [United States](3434750.md): Canada's border with the [northern tier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Tier_\(United_States\)) of the [contiguous United States](83759.md) to its south (6,416 kilometers or 3,987 miles[^cite_note-International_Boundary_Commission-1]), and with the U.S. state of [Alaska](624.md) to its northwest (2,475 kilometers or 1,538 miles[^cite_note-International_Boundary_Commission-1]). It is the longest international border in the world by total length, and the section between Canada and the contiguous United States is the second-longest continuous international border in the world after the [Kazakhstan–Russia border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan%E2%80%93Russia_border).

The boundary (including boundaries on the Pacific coast, [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes), and Atlantic coasts) is 8,891 kilometers (5,525 mi)[^cite_note-International_Boundary_Commission-1] long. The bi-national [International Boundary Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Boundary_Commission) deals with matters relating to marking and maintaining the boundary, and the [International Joint Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Joint_Commission) deals with issues concerning boundary waters. The agencies responsible for facilitating legal passage through the international boundary are the [Canada Border Services Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Border_Services_Agency) (CBSA) and [U.S. Customs and Border Protection](757040.md) (CBP).

## History

### 18th century



The [45th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_parallel_north) (marked in red) was established as a border between the [Province of Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Quebec_\(1763%E2%80%931791\)) and the United States in the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1783\)), following the line of a 1763 declaration by King George III.

The [Treaty of Paris of 1783](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1783\)) ended the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) between [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) and the United States. In the second article of the Treaty, the parties agreed on all boundaries of the United States, including the northern boundary along what was then [British North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America). The agreed-upon boundary included the line from the northwest angle of [Nova Scotia](21184.md) to the northwesternmost head of the [Connecticut River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_River). It proceeded down along the middle of the river to the [45th parallel of north latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_parallel_north).

The parallel had been established in 1763 by [King George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_George_III) as the boundary between the provinces of [Quebec](7954867.md) and [New York](8210131.md) (including what would later become the [State of Vermont](32578.md)).[^cite_note-shapes-2] It was [surveyed and marked by John Collins and Thomas Valentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins%E2%80%93Valentine_line) from 1771 to 1773.[^cite_note-3] The [St. Lawrence River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Lawrence_River) and the Great Lakes became the boundary further west, between the United States and what is now [Ontario](22218.md). Northwest of [Lake Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Superior), the boundary followed rivers to the [Lake of the Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_of_the_Woods). From the [northwesternmost point of the Lake of the Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwesternmost_point_of_the_Lake_of_the_Woods), the boundary was agreed to go straight west until it met the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River). That line never meets the river, for [the river's source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Itasca) is farther south, so the border was eventually drawn from the lake's northwestern point south to the 49th Parallel.

#### Jay Treaty (1794)

The [Jay Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Treaty) of 1794 (effective 1796) created the [International Boundary Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Boundary_Commission), which was charged with surveying and mapping the boundary. It also provided for the removal of British forces from [Detroit](8687.md), as well as other frontier outposts on the U.S. side. The Jay Treaty was superseded by the [Treaty of Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Ghent) (effective 1815), concluding the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), which included pre-war boundaries.

### 19th century



Signing of the [Treaty of Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Ghent) in 1814, which ended the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812) and returned the border to its pre-war state. Subsequent agreed treaties saw the border demilitarized and most boundary disputes resolved.

Signed in December 1814, the Treaty of Ghent ended the War of 1812, returning the boundaries of British North America and the United States to the state they were in before the war. In the following decades, the United States and the United Kingdom concluded several treaties that settled the major boundary disputes between the two, enabling the border to be [demilitarized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demilitarized_zone). The [Rush–Bagot Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush%E2%80%93Bagot_Treaty) of 1817 provided a plan for demilitarizing the two combatant sides in the War of 1812 and also laid out preliminary principles for drawing a border between British North America and the United States.

#### London Convention (1818)

The [Treaty of 1818](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_1818) saw the expansion of both British North America and the United States, with their boundary extending westward along the [49th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_parallel_north), from the [Northwest Angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Angle) at Lake of the Woods to the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains).[^cite_note-4] While the [Laurentian Divide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurentian_Divide) had previously been agreed to as a border, the flatness of the terrain made it difficult to locate this line.[^cite_note-5] The treaty extinguished British claims to the south of the 49th in the [Red River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Valley), which was part of [Rupert's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert's_Land). The treaty also extinguished U.S. claims to land north of the 49th in the watershed of the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River), which was part of the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase). The border vista theoretically follows the 49th parallel, but in practice, the 19th-century surveyed border markers vary by several hundred feet in spots.[^cite_note-6]

#### Webster–Ashburton Treaty (1842)



Disputed territory between [British North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America) and [Maine](19977.md) marked in pink. The dispute was settled in the [Webster–Ashburton Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webster%E2%80%93Ashburton_Treaty) in 1842. The green line on the map marks the final border.

Disputes over the interpretation of the border treaties and mistakes in surveying required additional negotiations, which resulted in the [Webster–Ashburton Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webster%E2%80%93Ashburton_Treaty) of 1842. The treaty resolved the [Aroostook War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aroostook_War), a dispute over the boundary between [Maine](19977.md), [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick), and the [Province of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Canada). The treaty redefined the border between [New Hampshire](21134.md), Vermont, and New York on the one hand, and the Province of Canada on the other, resolving the [Indian Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Indian_Stream) dispute and the [Fort Blunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Montgomery_\(Lake_Champlain\)) dilemma at the outlet to [Lake Champlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Champlain).

The part of the 45th parallel that separates Quebec from the U.S. states of Vermont and New York had first been surveyed from 1771 to 1773 and markers placed (the [Collins–Valentine line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins%E2%80%93Valentine_line)) after it had been declared the boundary between New York (including what later became Vermont) and Quebec. It was surveyed again after the War of 1812. The U.S. federal government began to construct fortifications just south of the border at [Rouses Point, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouses_Point,_New_York), on Lake Champlain. After a significant portion of the construction was completed, measurements revealed that at that point, the actual 45th parallel was three-quarters of a mile (1.2 km) south of the surveyed line. The fort, which became known as "[Fort Blunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Blunder)", was in Canada, which created a dilemma for the U.S. that was not resolved until a provision of the treaty left the border on the meandering line as surveyed. The border along the [Boundary Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Waters) in present-day Ontario and [Minnesota](19590.md) between Lake Superior and the Northwest Angle was also redefined.[^cite_note-WA-7][^cite_note-Lass-8]

#### Oregon Treaty (1846)

Main article: [Oregon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Treaty)



Map of the disputed [Oregon Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Country), with the American and British claims marked. The dispute was settled in the Oregon Treaty, placing the boundary along the [49th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_parallel_north), excluding [Vancouver Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Island).

An [1844 boundary dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_boundary_dispute) during the [Presidency of James K. Polk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_James_K._Polk) led to a call for the northern boundary of the U.S. west of the Rockies to be [54°40′N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/54%C2%B040'_or_fight) related to the southern boundary of Russia's [Alaska Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_America). However, the United Kingdom wanted a border that followed the [Columbia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River) to the Pacific Ocean. The dispute was resolved in the [Oregon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Treaty) of 1846, which established the 49th parallel as the boundary through the Rockies.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

#### Boundary surveys (mid–19th century)

The Northwest Boundary Survey (1857–1861) laid out the land boundary. However, the water boundary was not settled for some time. After the [Pig War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_War_\(1859\)) in 1859, arbitration in 1872 established the border between the [Gulf Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Islands) and the [San Juan Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_Islands).

The [International Boundary Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Commission_Trail) (or the "Northern Boundary Survey" in the U.S.) began in 1872.[^cite_note-11] Its mandate was to establish the border as agreed to in the Treaty of 1818. Archibald Campbell led the way for the United States, while Donald Cameron, supported by chief astronomer [Samuel Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Anderson_\(surveyor\)), headed the British team. This survey focused on the border from the Lake of the Woods to the summit of the Rocky Mountains.[^cite_note-12]

### 20th century



An International Boundary Commission reference monument at the [Pigeon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigeon_River_\(Minnesota%E2%80%93Ontario\))&#x20;

In 1903, following a dispute that arose because of the [Klondike Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klondike_Gold_Rush), a joint United Kingdom–Canada–U.S. tribunal established the [boundary of southeast Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_boundary_dispute).[^cite_note-13]

On April 11, 1908, it was agreed under Article IV of the "Treaty Between the United States of America and the United Kingdom Concerning the Boundary Between the United States and the Dominion of Canada from the Atlantic Ocean to the Pacific Ocean" to survey and delimit the boundary between Canada and the U.S. through the St. Lawrence River and Great Lakes by modern surveying techniques, which accomplished several changes to the border.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] In 1925, the International Boundary Commission's temporary mission became permanent for maintaining the survey and mapping of the border, maintaining boundary monuments and buoys, and keeping the border clear of brush and vegetation for 6 m (20 ft), establishing a "[border vista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada-United_States_international_border_vista)" extending for 3 m (10 ft) on each side of the line.[^cite_note-16] In 1909, under the [Boundary Waters Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Waters_Treaty), the [International Joint Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Joint_Commission) was established for Canada and the U.S. to investigate and approve projects that affect the waters and waterways along the border.

### 21st century

As a result of the 2001 [September 11 attacks](5058690.md), the U.S. declared a level 1 alert at its borders, which required intrusive inspections of all crossing vehicles and passengers, resulting in considerable border congestion.[^cite_note-TA-17][^cite_note-McCarten-18][^cite_note-Brister-19] Canada's [Chrétien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Chr%C3%A9tien) administration worked with the U.S. [Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_W._Bush) administration to make the border both more secure and less of an impediment for high-value goods and low-risk travelers; and on December 12, 2001, the *[Smart Border Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Border_Declaration)* was signed.[^cite_note-TA-17][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-Gray-21][^cite_note-22] The agreement pioneered border innovations that have become common worldwide, such as cargo and passenger preclearance, the [Free and Secure Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_Secure_Trade) (FAST) program, and the [NEXUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEXUS) trusted traveler program.[^cite_note-McCarten-18][^cite_note-TA-17] The cooperation established by the *Smart Border* initiative made it easier to restrict border traffic during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) in 2020.[^cite_note-McCarten-18][^cite_note-23]

#### 2020–2021 closure



[Poker Creek–Little Gold Creek Border Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poker_Creek%E2%80%93Little_Gold_Creek_Border_Crossing) at the Alaska–Yukon border, closed as a result of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). A sign beside the gate alerts travelers that the border is also a boundary between time zones.

On March 21, 2020, in response to the [COVID-19 pandemic in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Canada) and [United States](63136490.md), the governments of both nations agreed to close the border for "non-essential" travel for an initial period of 30 days.[^cite_note-24] The closure was extended 15 times. In mid-June 2021, the Canadian government announced it would ease some entry requirements for fully vaccinated foreign nationals, permanent residents, and Canadian citizens starting on July 5.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] The closure finally expired on July 21. In mid-July, the Canadian government announced that fully vaccinated American citizens and permanent residents could visit Canada starting August 9. The American government reopened its land border to fully vaccinated Canadian citizens effective November 8. The 2020–21 closure was reportedly the first long-term blanket closure of the border since the War of 1812.[^cite_note-31]

Business advocacy groups, noting the substantial economic impact of the closure on both sides of the border, called for more nuanced restrictions in place of the blanket ban on non-essential travel.[^cite_note-32] The Northern Border Caucus, a group in the [U.S. Congress](31756.md) composed of members from border communities, made similar suggestions to the governments of both countries.[^cite_note-33] Beyond the closure itself, US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) also initially suggested the idea of deploying United States military personnel near the border with Canada in connection with the pandemic. He later abandoned the idea following vocal opposition from Canadian officials.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

## Security

### Law enforcement approach

The International Boundary is commonly said to be the world's "longest undefended border"—it is not militarized, although civilian law enforcement is present.[^cite_note-\:4-36] It is illegal to cross the border outside border controls, as anyone crossing the border must be checked under immigration[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] and customs laws.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] The relatively low level of security measures contrasts with the [United States–Mexico border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border), one-third as long as the Canada–U.S. border, which is actively patrolled by U.S. Customs and Border Protection personnel to prevent [drug trafficking](218449.md) and [illegal immigration](7124412.md).

Parts of the International Boundary cross through mountainous terrain or heavily forested areas, but significant portions also cross remote prairie farmland and the Great Lakes and Saint Lawrence River, in addition to the maritime components of the boundary at the [Atlantic](698.md), [Pacific](23070.md), and [Arctic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean). The border runs through the middle of the [Mohawk Nation at Akwesasne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akwesasne) and even divides some buildings in communities in New England and Quebec. The [US Customs and Border Protection](757040.md) identifies the chief issues along the border as domestic and international terrorism, smuggling of illegal drugs and products such as [tobacco](30942.md) to evade customs duties, and illegal immigration.[^cite_note-GAO2019-41] A June 2019 U.S. [Government Accountability Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Office) report identified specific staffing and resource shortfalls faced by the CBP on the Northern border that adversely affect enforcement actions; the U.S. Border Patrol and [CBP Air and Marine Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBP_Air_and_Marine_Operations) identified an insufficient number of agents along the northern border for land, air, and maritime missions.[^cite_note-GAO2019-41] There are eight [U.S. Border Patrol](567453.md) sectors, on the Canada–U.S. border, each covering a designated "area of responsibility"; the sectors, from west to east, are based in [Blaine, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaine,_Washington), [Spokane, Washington](138268.md), [Havre, Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havre,_Montana), [Grand Forks, North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Forks,_North_Dakota), [Detroit, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit,_Michigan), [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York), [Swanton, Vermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swanton_\(town\),_Vermont), and [Houlton, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houlton,_Maine).[^cite_note-GAO2019-41]



A joint meeting between Canada Border Service Agency and U.S. Customs and Border Protection senior executives

Following the September 11 attacks in the United States, security along the border was dramatically tightened by the two countries in both populated and rural areas. Both nations are also actively involved in detailed and extensive tactical and strategic intelligence sharing. In December 2010, Canada and the United States were negotiating an agreement titled "Beyond the Border: A Shared Vision for Perimeter Security and Competitiveness" which would give the U.S. more influence over Canada's border security and immigration controls, and more information would be shared by Canada with the U.S.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-\:4-36]

### Security measures



Canadian border crossing sign with lights to alert those crossing to report to customs remotely. Remote customs exist in areas where staffed border crossings are unavailable.

Residents of both nations who own property adjacent to the border are forbidden to build within the 6-meter-wide (20 ft) boundary vista without permission from the International Boundary Commission. They are required to report such construction to their respective governments.

All persons crossing the border are required to report to the customs agency of the country they have entered. Where necessary, fences or vehicle blockades are used. In remote areas, where staffed border crossings are not available, there are hidden [sensors](235757.md) on roads, trails, railways, and wooded areas, which are located near crossing points.[^cite_note-43] There is no [border zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_zone);[^cite_note-44] the U.S. Customs and Border Protection routinely sets up checkpoints as far as 100 miles (160 km) into U.S. territory.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

In August 2020, the United States constructed 3.8 km (2.4 mi) of short cable fencing along the border between [Abbotsford, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbotsford,_British_Columbia), and [Whatcom County, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whatcom_County,_Washington).[^cite_note-49]

#### Identification

See also: [Western Hemisphere Travel Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hemisphere_Travel_Initiative)



A [Canada Border Services Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Border_Services_Agency) officer and a [U.S. Customs and Border Protection](757040.md) officer reviewing documents of [NEXUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEXUS) applicants in 2015

Before 2007, American and Canadian citizens were only required to produce a birth certificate and a driver's license/government-issued identification card when crossing the Canada–United States border.[^cite_note-cbcident-50]

However, in late 2006, the [U.S. Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) (DHS) announced the final rule of the [Western Hemisphere Travel Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hemisphere_Travel_Initiative) (WHTI), which pertained to new identification requirements for travelers entering the United States. This rule, which marked the first phase of the initiative, was implemented on January 23, 2007, specifying six forms of identification acceptable for crossing the U.S. border (depending on mode):[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

- a valid [passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passport)—required to enter by air;

- a [United States passport card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_passport_card);

- an [enhanced driver's license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_driver's_license)—issued by the U.S. States of [Michigan](18859.md), [Minnesota](19590.md), [New York](8210131.md), [Vermont](32578.md), and [Washington](13015878.md), as well as the Canadian Provinces of [British Columbia](3392.md), [Manitoba](18926.md), [Ontario](22218.md), and [Quebec](7954867.md);

  [^cite_note-53]

- a trusted traveler program card (i.e. [NEXUS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEXUS), [FAST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_Secure_Trade), or [SENTRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_Electronic_Network_for_Travelers_Rapid_Inspection));

- a valid [Merchant Mariner Credential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_Mariner_Credential)—to be used when traveling in conjunction with official maritime business; and

- a valid U.S. [military identification card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_identity_card)—to be used when traveling on official orders.



Signage indicating a dedicated lane specifically for NEXUS card holders at a Canada-US land border crossing

The requirement of a passport or an enhanced form of identification to enter the United States by air went into effect in January 2007, and went into effect for those entering the U.S. by land and sea in January 2008.[^cite_note-cbcident-50] Although the new requirements for land and sea entry went into legal effect in January 2008, their enforcement did not begin until June 2009.[^cite_note-cbcident-50] Since June 2009, every traveler arriving via a land or sea port-of-entry (including [ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferries)) has been required to present one of the above forms of identification to enter the United States.

Conversely, to cross into Canada, a traveler must also carry identification, as well as a valid visa (if necessary) when crossing the border.[^cite_note-cbsaident-54] Forms of identification include a valid passport, a Canadian Emergency Travel Document, an enhanced driver's license issued by a Canadian province or territory, or an enhanced identification/photo card issued by a Canadian province or territory.[^cite_note-cbsaident-54] Several other documents may be used by Canadians to identify their citizenship at the border, although such documents must be supported with additional photo identification.[^cite_note-cbsaident-54]

Canadian and American citizens who are members of a trusted traveler program such as FAST or NEXUS, may present their FAST or NEXUS card as an alternate form of identification when crossing the international boundary by land or sea, or when arriving by air from only Canada or the United States.[^cite_note-cbsaident-54] Although permanent residents of Canada and the United States are eligible for FAST or NEXUS, they are required to travel with a passport and proof of permanent residency upon arrival at the Canadian border.[^cite_note-cbsaident-54] American permanent residents who are NEXUS members also require an Electronic Travel Authorization when crossing the Canadian border.[^cite_note-cbsaident-54]

### Security issues

#### Smuggling



A [United States Border Patrol](567453.md) agent tracking someone in harsh winter conditions on the northern border. The agent is armed with an [M14 rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M14_rifle)

Smuggling of [alcoholic beverages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_drink) (["rum running"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rum-running)) was widespread during the 1920s, when [Prohibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition) was in effect nationally in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_the_United_States) and parts of [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_Canada).

In more recent years, Canadian officials have brought attention to [drug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcotic), [cigarette](38327.md), and [firearm](11966.md) smuggling from the United States, while U.S. officials have made complaints of drug smuggling via Canada. In July 2005, law enforcement personnel arrested three men who had built a 110-meter (360 ft) tunnel under the border between British Columbia and [Washington](13015878.md), intended for the use of smuggling [marijuana](1481886.md), the first such tunnel known on this border.[^cite_note-55] From 2007 to 2010, 147 people were arrested for smuggling marijuana on the property of a [bed-and-breakfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bed_and_breakfast) in Blaine, Washington, but agents estimate that they caught only about 5% of smugglers.[^cite_note-macdonald201007_08-56]



[United States Coast Guardsman](32223.md) aboard a [Royal Canadian Mounted Police](69080.md) vessel during shiprider exercises, in which RCMP and USCG officers jointly crew vessels patrolling shared waterways to combat cross-border crime.

Because of its location, [Cornwall, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall,_Ontario) experiences ongoing smuggling—mostly of tobacco and firearms from the United States. The neighboring [Mohawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohawk_people) territory of Akwesasne straddles the Ontario–Quebec–New York borders, where its [First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada) sovereignty prevents [Ontario Provincial Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Provincial_Police), [Sûreté du Québec](375500.md), [Royal Canadian Mounted Police](69080.md), Canada Border Services Agency, [Canadian Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Coast_Guard), United States Border Patrol, [United States Coast Guard](32223.md), and [New York State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Police) from exercising jurisdiction over exchanges taking place within the territory.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

#### 2009 border occupation

In May 2009, the Mohawk people of Akwesasne occupied the area around the [Canada Border Services Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Border_Services_Agency) port of entry building to protest the Canadian government's decision to arm its border agents while operating on Mohawk territory. The north span of the [Seaway International Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaway_International_Bridge) and the CBSA inspection facilities were closed. During this occupation, the Canadian flag was replaced with the flag of the Mohawk people. Although U.S. Customs remained open to southbound traffic, northbound traffic was blocked on the U.S. side by both American and Canadian officials. The Canadian border at this crossing remained closed for six weeks. On July 13, 2009, the CBSA opened a temporary inspection station at the north end of the north span of the bridge in the city of Cornwall, allowing traffic to once again flow in both directions.[^cite_note-59]

The Mohawk people of Akwesasne have staged ongoing protests at this border. In 2014, they objected to a process that made their crossing more tedious, believing it violated their treaty rights of free passage. When traveling from the U.S. to [Cornwall Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall_Island_\(Ontario\)), they must first cross a second bridge into Canada for inspection at the new Canadian border station. Discussions between intergovernmental agencies were being pursued on the feasibility of relocating the Canadian border inspection facilities on the U.S. side of the border.[^cite_note-60]

#### 2017 border crossing crisis



Members of the [Royal Canadian Mounted Police](69080.md) waiting for migrants to cross at [Roxham Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roxham_Road) in August 2017

In August 2017, the border between Quebec and New York saw an influx of up to 500 irregular crossings each day by individuals seeking asylum in Canada.[^cite_note-61] In response Canada increased border security and immigration staffing in the area, reiterating that crossing the border irregularly did not affect a person's asylum status.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] From the beginning of January 2017 up until the end of March 2018, the RCMP intercepted 25,645 people crossing the border into Canada from an unauthorized point of entry. [Public Safety Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Safety_Canada) estimates another 2,500 came across in April 2018 for a total of just over 28,000.[^cite_note-64]

### Yukon

The [Canadian territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_territory) of [Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon) shares its entire northwestern border with the U.S. state of Alaska, beginning at the [Beaufort Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaufort_Sea) at [69°39′N 141°00′W  /  69.650°N 141.000°W  / 69.650; -141.000](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=69_39_N_141_00_W_) and proceeding southwards along the [141st meridian west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/141st_meridian_west). At 60°18′N, the border proceeds away from the 141st meridian west in a southeastward direction, following the [Saint Elias Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Elias_Mountains). South of the [60th parallel north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60th_parallel_north), the border continues into British Columbia.[^cite_note-65]

### British Columbia



[0 Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/0_Avenue) on the Canadian side and the border marker

[British Columbia](3392.md) has two international borders with the United States: with the state of Alaska in the northwest and with the [contiguous United States](83759.md) along the southern edge of the province, including (west to east) Washington, [Idaho](14607.md), and [Montana](19978.md).[^cite_note-\:1-66]

British Columbia's border with Alaska, continuing from Yukon's, proceeds through the Saint Elias Mountains, followed by [Mount Fairweather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Fairweather) at [58°54′N 137°31′W  /  58.900°N 137.517°W  / 58.900; -137.517](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=58_54_N_137_31_W_) (near the [Fairweather Glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairweather_Glacier)), where the border heads northwestward towards the [Coast Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Mountains).[^cite_note-\:1-66] At [59°48′N 135°28′W  /  59.800°N 135.467°W  / 59.800; -135.467](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=59_48_N_135_28_W_) (near [Skagway, Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skagway,_Alaska)), the border begins a general southeastward direction along the Coast Mountains. The border eventually reaches the [Portland Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Canal) and follows it outward to the [Dixon Entrance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixon_Entrance), which takes the border down and out into the Pacific Ocean, terminating it upon reaching [international waters](394357.md). BC's (Canada) border along the contiguous U.S. begins southwest of [Vancouver Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Island) and northwest of the [Olympic Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Peninsula), at the terminus of international waters in the Pacific Ocean and the northwest corner of the American state of Washington.[^cite_note-\:1-66] It follows the [Strait of Juan de Fuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Juan_de_Fuca) eastward, turning northeastward to enter [Haro Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haro_Strait). The border follows the strait in a northward direction but turns sharply eastward through [Boundary Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Pass), separating the Canadian Gulf Islands from the American San Juan Islands. Upon reaching the [Strait of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Georgia), the border turns due north and then towards the northwest, bisecting the strait until the 49th parallel north. After making a sharp turn eastbound, the border follows this parallel across the [Tsawwassen Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsawwassen,_British_Columbia), separating [Point Roberts, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Roberts,_Washington) from [Delta, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta,_British_Columbia), and continues into Alberta.

### Prairies



Boundary markers and a 25 foot (7.6 m) swath cut into the forest marking the Alberta–Montana border

The entire Canada–U.S. border in the provinces of both Alberta and Saskatchewan lies along the 49th parallel north.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-\:2-68] Both provinces share borders with the state of Montana, while, farther east, Saskatchewan also shares a border with [North Dakota](21651.md).[^cite_note-\:2-68] On the American side, the states of Montana, North Dakota, and Minnesota all lie on this part of the border.

Along with the U.S. states of North Dakota and Minnesota (west to east), nearly the entire Canada–U.S. border in [Manitoba](18926.md) lies along the 49th parallel north.[^cite_note-69] At the province's eastern end, however, the border briefly enters the Lake of the Woods, turning north at [48°59′N 95°09′W  /  48.983°N 95.150°W  / 48.983; -95.150](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=48_59_N_95_09_W_) where it continues into the land along the western end of Minnesota's Northwest Angle, the only part of the United States, other than Alaska, that is north of the 49th parallel. The border reaches Ontario at [49°23′N 95°09′W  /  49.383°N 95.150°W  / 49.383; -95.150](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=49_23_N_95_09_W_). Procedures to cross through Manitoba to reach and return from this territory, which involve telephoning U.S. Customs, have been arranged.[^cite_note-70]

### Ontario



The [Horseshoe Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseshoe_Falls) and the [Niagara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_River) marks the Ontario–New York border. Lakes and waterways make up most of Ontario's borders with the United States.

The province of Ontario shares its border (west to east) with the U.S. states of Minnesota, [Michigan](18859.md), [Ohio](22199.md), [Pennsylvania](23332.md), and New York. The largest provincial international border, most of the border is mostly a water boundary. It begins at the north-westernmost point of Minnesota's Northwest Angle. (49°23′N 95°09′W). From here, it proceeds eastward through the Angle Inlet into the Lake of the Woods, turning southward at [49°19′N 94°48′W  /  49.317°N 94.800°W  / 49.317; -94.800](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=49_19_N_94_48_W_) (near [Dawson Township, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawson_Township,_Manitoulin_District,_Ontario)) where it continues into the [Rainy River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainy_River_\(Minnesota%E2%80%93Ontario\)).[^cite_note-\:3-71] The border follows the river to [Rainy Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainy_Lake), then subsequently through various smaller lakes, including Namakan Lake, [Lac la Croix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lac_La_Croix_First_Nation), and [Sea Gull Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Gull_Lake). The border then crosses the [Height of Land Portage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_of_Land_Portage) over the divide between the [Hudson Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Bay) drainage basin and that of the Great Lakes. The boundary then follows the [Pigeon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigeon_River_\(Minnesota-Ontario\)), which leads it out into Lake Superior. The border continues through Lake Superior and [Whitefish Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitefish_Bay), into the St. Mary's River (Michigan–Ontario), then the [North Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Channel_\(Ontario\)). At [45°59′N 83°26′W  /  45.983°N 83.433°W  / 45.983; -83.433](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=45_59_N_83_26_W_) (between [Drummond Township, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drummond_Township,_Michigan) to the west and [Cockburn Island (Ontario)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockburn_Island_\(Ontario\)) to the east), the border turns southward into the [False Detour Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_Detour_Channel), from which it reaches [Lake Huron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Huron). Through the lake, the border heads southward until reaching the [St. Clair River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Clair_River), leading it to [Lake St. Clair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_St._Clair). The border proceeds through Lake St. Clair, reaching the [Detroit River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_River), which leads it to [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie), where it begins turning northeast. From Lake Erie, the border leads into the [Niagara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_River), which takes it into [Lake Ontario](17947.md). From here, the boundary heads northwestward until it reaches [43°27′N 79°12′W  /  43.450°N 79.200°W  / 43.450; -79.200](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=43_27_N_79_12_W_), where it makes a sharp turn towards the northeast. The border then reaches the St. Lawrence River, proceeding through it until finally, at [45°00′N 74°40′W  /  45.000°N 74.667°W  / 45.000; -74.667](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=45_00_N_74_40_W_) (between [Massena, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massena,_New_York) and Cornwall, Ontario), the border splits from the river and continues into Quebec.[^cite_note-\:3-71]

### Quebec

The province of Quebec borders (west to east) the U.S. states of New York, Vermont, New Hampshire, and Maine, beginning where the Ontario-New York border ends in the St. Lawrence River at the 45th parallel north.[^cite_note-72] The Quebec-New York border heads inland towards the east, remaining on or near the parallel, becoming the border of Vermont. At [45°00′N 71°30′W  /  45.000°N 71.500°W  / 45.000; -71.500](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=45_00_N_71_30_W_) (the tripoint of Vermont, New Hampshire, and Quebec), the border begins to follow various natural features of the [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains) as it turns into the border of Maine. It continues to do so until [46°25′N 70°03′W  /  46.417°N 70.050°W  / 46.417; -70.050](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=46_25_N_70_03_W_) (near [Saint-Camille-de-Lellis, Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Camille-de-Lellis,_Quebec), on the Canadian side, and unorganized territory on the American side), where it heads north, then northeastward at [46°41′N 69°59′W  /  46.683°N 69.983°W  / 46.683; -69.983](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=46_41_N_69_59_W_) (near [Lac-Frontière, Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lac-Fronti%C3%A8re,_Quebec)). Finally, at [47°27′N 69°13′W  /  47.450°N 69.217°W  / 47.450; -69.217](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=47_27_N_69_13_W_) (near [Pohénégamook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poh%C3%A9n%C3%A9gamook), Quebec), the border heads toward [Beau Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beau_Lake), going through it and continuing into New Brunswick.

### New Brunswick



A plaque marking the Maine-New Brunswick border over the [St. Croix River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Croix_River_\(Maine_%E2%80%93_New_Brunswick\)).

The entire border of New Brunswick is shared with the U.S. state of Maine, beginning at the southern tip of Beau Lake at [47°18′N 69°03′W  /  47.300°N 69.050°W  / 47.300; -69.050](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=47_18_N_69_03_W_) (between [Rivière-Bleue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivi%C3%A8re-Bleue), Quebec, and [Saint-François Parish, New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Fran%C3%A7ois_Parish,_New_Brunswick)), subsequently proceeding to the [Saint John River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_John_River_\(New_Brunswick\)).[^cite_note-73] The border moves through the River until [47°04′N 67°47′W  /  47.067°N 67.783°W  / 47.067; -67.783](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=47_04_N_67_47_W_) (between [Hamlin, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlin,_Maine), and [Grand Falls, New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Falls,_New_Brunswick)), where it splits from the river. It heads southward to [45°56′N 67°47′W  /  45.933°N 67.783°W  / 45.933; -67.783](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border\&params=45_56_N_67_47_W_) (near [Amity, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amity,_Maine)), from whence it follows the Monument Brook further south into the [Chiputneticook Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiputneticook_Lakes), which subsequently leads to the border of the [St. Croix River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Croix_River_\(Maine_%E2%80%93_New_Brunswick\)). The border proceeds through the St. Croix to [Passamaquoddy Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passamaquoddy_Bay), which then leads it to [Grand Manan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Manan_Island) into the middle of the [Bay of Fundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Fundy). Here, the border turns towards the south and terminates upon reaching international waters.

## Border crossings and straddlings

Main article: [List of Canada–United States border crossings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border_crossings)

### Airports



Entrance to a U.S. pre-clearance area at [Vancouver International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_International_Airport). Pre-clearance areas are situated in several Canadian airports to help expedite the customs process.

United States Customs and Border Protection maintains pre-clearance facilities at nine Canadian airports with nonstop air service to the United States: [Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_International_Airport); [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_International_Airport); [Halifax Stanfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax_Stanfield_International_Airport); [Montreal–Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montr%C3%A9al%E2%80%93Trudeau_International_Airport); [Ottawa Macdonald–Cartier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Macdonald%E2%80%93Cartier_International_Airport); [Toronto-Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Bishop_Toronto_City_Airport), [Toronto–Pearson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Pearson_International_Airport), [Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_International_Airport); and [Winnipeg James Armstrong Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_James_Armstrong_Richardson_International_Airport). These procedures expedite travel by allowing flights originating in Canada to land at a U.S. airport without being processed as an international arrival. Canada does not maintain equivalent personnel at U.S. airports due to the limited number of Canada-bound flights from U.S. locations. Both New York LaGuardia (LGA) and Washington National (DCA) airports lack immigration and customs inspection facilities. However, U.S. pre-clearance facilities in Toronto and Montreal permit nonstop service to New York and Washington.

#### Cross-border airports



Ramp facilities at Piney Pinecreek Border Airport. The left-hand building and ramp are north of the border in Canada; those on the right are in the United States.

The Canada–U.S. border has six airports and eleven [seaplane bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplane_base) whose runways straddle the borderline. Such airports were built before the [U.S. entry into World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_United_States_during_World_War_II) as a way to legally transfer U.S.-built aircraft, such as the [Lockheed Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Hudson), to Canada under the provisions of the [*Lend-Lease Act*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lend-Lease). In the interest of maintaining neutrality, U.S. military pilots were forbidden to deliver combat aircraft to Canada. As a result, the aircraft were flown to the border, where they landed, and then were towed on their wheels over the border by tractors or horses overnight. The next day, the planes were crewed by [RCAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Air_Force) pilots and flown to other locations, typically air bases in [Eastern Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Canada) and [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Newfoundland). The planes were flown to the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and deployed in the [Battle of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Britain).[^cite_note-74]

[Piney Pinecreek Border Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piney_Pinecreek_Border_Airport) is located in [Piney, Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piney,_Manitoba), and [Pinecreek, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinecreek,_Minnesota). The northwest–southeast-oriented runway straddles the border, and there are two ramps: one in the U.S. and one in Canada. The airport is owned by the [Minnesota Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Department_of_Transportation).[^cite_note-\:0-75]

[International Peace Garden Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Peace_Garden_Airport) is located in [Boissevain, Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boissevain,_Manitoba), and [Dunseith, North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunseith,_North_Dakota), adjacent to the [International Peace Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Peace_Garden). The runway is entirely within North Dakota, but a ramp extends across the border to allow aircraft to access Canadian customs. ### Land border crossings



[Ambassador Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador_Bridge) is a [suspension bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspension_bridge) that connects [Detroit, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit,_Michigan), with [Windsor, Ontario](33596.md).The bridge is North America's busiest international crossing in terms of trade volume, carrying more than one-quarter of all merchandise trade between Canada and the United States.

Currently, there are 119 legal land border crossings between the United States and Canada, 26 of which take place at a bridge or tunnel. Only 2 of the 119 crossings are one-way: the [Churubusco–Franklin Centre Border Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churubusco%E2%80%93Franklin_Centre_Border_Crossing), where travelers may enter only the United States; and the [Four Falls Border Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Falls_Border_Crossing), where travelers may enter only Canada. Six roads have [unstaffed road crossings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border_crossings#Unstaffed_road_crossings) and do not have border inspection services in one or both directions, where travelers are legally allowed to cross the border. Those who cross are required to report to customs, which are stationed farther within.

#### Rail crossings

There are 39 [railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border_crossings#Rail_crossings) that cross the U.S.–Canada border, nine of which are no longer in use. Eleven of these cross the border at a bridge or tunnel. Only three international rail lines currently carry passengers. At Vancouver's [Pacific Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Central_Station), passengers must pass through U.S. pre-clearance and pass their baggage through an [X-ray machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-ray_machine) before boarding the [Seattle](11388236.md)-bound [Amtrak *Cascades*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_Cascades) train, which makes no further stops before crossing the border at Blaine, Washington. [^cite_note-83] Pre-clearance facilities are not available for the popular [*Adirondack*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adirondack_\(train\)) (New York City to Montreal) or [*Maple Leaf*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_Leaf_\(train\)) (New York City to Toronto) trains, since these lines have stops between Montreal or Toronto and the border. Instead, passengers must clear customs at a stop located at the actual border.

#### Trail crossings

The [Fourth Connecticut Lake Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Connecticut_Lake_Trail) (New Hampshire/Quebec) crosses several times while following the border vista before heading back to the United States. The [Pacific Crest Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Crest_Trail) crosses into [E. C. Manning Provincial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._C._Manning_Provincial_Park) in the remote [North Cascades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Cascades) mountains. Crossing into Canada was possible with an advance permit until 2025, when CBSA cancelled the program. It was never possible to enter the U.S. along the trail. Hikers must pass through an official border crossing.[^cite_note-84]

### Seaports



A ferry departing [Wolfe Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfe_Island_\(Ontario\)), Ontario, for [Cape Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Vincent), [New York](8210131.md)

There are 13 international [ferry crossings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Canada%E2%80%93United_States_border_crossings#Ferry_crossings) operating between the U.S. and Canada. Two of them carry passengers only, and one carries only rail cars. Four of the ferries operate only on a seasonal basis. Similar to the pre-clearance facilities at Canadian airports, arrangements exist at major Canadian [seaports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) that handle sealed direct import shipments into the U.S. Along the East Coast, ferry services operate between the province of New Brunswick and the state of Maine, while on the West Coast, they operate between British Columbia and the states of Washington and Alaska. There are also several ferry services in the Great Lakes operating between the province of Ontario and the states of Michigan, New York, and Ohio. The ferry between Maine and Nova Scotia ended its operations in 2009, resuming again in 2014. Seasonal vessel inspection stations are operated at tourist destinations such as [Heart Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_Island), New York, and [Rockport, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockport,_Ontario). At landings unmanned by border personnel, telephoning the appropriate border agency may be sufficient to meet legal requirements.[^cite_note-85]

## Boundary divisions

### Practical exclaves

To be a true international [exclave](4848945.md), *all* potential paths of travel from the exclave to the home country must cross over only the territory of a different country or countries. Like exclaves, practical exclaves are not contiguous with the *land* of the home country and have *land* access only through another country or countries. Unlike exclaves, they are not *entirely* surrounded by foreign territory. Hence, they are exclaves *for practical purposes*, without meeting the strict definition.

The term *pene-exclave,* also known as a "functional" or "practical" exclave,[^cite_note-EV-86]<sup>: 31 </sup> was defined by G. W. S. Robinson (1959) as "parts of the territory of one country that can be approached conveniently — in particular by wheeled traffic — only through the territory of another country."[^cite_note-Robinson-87]<sup>: 283 </sup> Thus, a pene-exclave has land borders with other territories but is not surrounded by the other's land or territorial waters.[^cite_note-Melamid-88]<sup>: 60 </sup> Catudal (1974) and Vinokurov (2007)[^cite_note-EV-86]<sup>: 31–33 </sup> provide examples to further elaborate, including Point Roberts, Washington: "Although physical connections by water with Point Roberts are entirely within the sovereignty of the United States, land access is only possible through Canada."[^cite_note-HMC-89]<sup>: 113 </sup> Practical exclaves can exhibit continuity of state territory across territorial waters but a discontinuity on land, such as in the case of Point Roberts.[^cite_note-EV-86]<sup>: 47 </sup>

#### Practical exclaves of Canada



[Campobello Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campobello_Island) is a practical exclave of Canada, with land access to the mainland being only via Maine.

The western portion of the Akwesasne reserve is a practical exclave of Canada because of the St. Lawrence River to the north, the [St. Regis River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Regis_River) to the east, and New York State, U.S., to the south. To travel by land elsewhere in Canada, one must drive through New York State. [Campobello Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campobello_Island) is another practical exclave located at the entrance to Passamaquoddy Bay, adjacent to the entrance to [Cobscook Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobscook_Bay), and within the Bay of Fundy. The island is part of [Charlotte County, New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_County,_New_Brunswick), but is physically connected by the [Franklin Delano Roosevelt Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Delano_Roosevelt_Bridge) with [Lubec, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubec,_Maine), the easternmost tip of the continental United States. Premier, British Columbia, is an abandoned mining site accessible only through Hyder, Alaska.

### Split features

Between Quebec and Vermont, [Province Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_Island) is a piece of land that primarily lies in Canada, though a small portion of the island is situated in the U.S. state, lying south of the 45th parallel with a border vista marking the international boundary. [Canusa Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canusa_Street) in Beebe Plain, Vermont, is the only place where the border lies in the middle of a street. Between North Dakota and Manitoba, the international border splits a peninsula within a lake on the border of [Rolette County, North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolette_County,_North_Dakota), and the [Wakopa Wildlife Management Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakopa_Wildlife_Management_Area), Manitoba.[^cite_note-92] Likewise, [Lake Metigoshe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Metigoshe), lying in the [Township of Roland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Township,_Bottineau_County,_North_Dakota), borders the municipality of [Winchester, Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_of_Deloraine-Winchester). The border splits a shoreline, putting Canadian cabins on one side and the beach and boat docks for those cabins on the U.S. side, while land access is only through Canada.
