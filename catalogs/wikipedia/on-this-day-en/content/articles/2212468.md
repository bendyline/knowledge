**King Fahd International Airport** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مطار الملك فهد الدولي; abbr. KFIA) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **DMM**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **OEDF**), also known as **Dammam International Airport** or simply **Dammam Airport** or **King Fahd Airport**, is the international airport serving [Dammam](479214.md), [Saudi Arabia](349303.md). The airport is located 31 kilometres (19 miles) northwest of downtown Dammam and is named after the former [King of Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Saudi_Arabia), [Fahd ibn Abdulaziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahd_of_Saudi_Arabia) (1921–2005). The airport serves the entire [Eastern Province](468721.md) of Saudi Arabia and is one of the four primary international airports in the kingdom.

After its construction, it became a US [airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_military_deployments) used primarily during the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War), but the airport has been overseeing commercial operations since 28 November 1999 and has since expanded to provide connections to 43 destinations. Before King Fahd International, the primary airport serving the region was the much busier [Dhahran International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhahran_International_Airport), which has since been converted for military use and is now designated the [King Abdulaziz Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdulaziz_Air_Base). Since 1 July 2017, the airport has been operated and managed by the Dammam Airports Company (DACO).[^cite_note-kfia.gov.sa-2][^cite_note-3] Commercial transport was only halted once throughout the history of the airport when, on 21 March 2020, the [Saudi Press Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Press_Agency) (SPA) announced the suspension of all domestic and international travel both within and to and from the kingdom [due to the COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_commercial_air_transport).[^cite_note-4] Domestic operations were reinitiated on 31 May 2020,[^cite_note-5] and international operations resumed on 17 May 2021.[^cite_note-6]

The third largest airport in the kingdom by passenger volume, more than 10 million passengers use King Fahd International each year, and 37 airlines operate flights in and out of the airport. The airport serves as a hub to [Flynas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flynas) and [Flyadeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flyadeal). It previously served as a hub to [Saudia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudia) as well as the now defunct [Sama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama_\(airline\)) airline and [SaudiGulf Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SaudiGulf_Airlines). In addition to these airlines, Saudi Aramco Aviation, the airline operated by [Saudi Aramco](290527.md), the state-owned oil giant, uses it to transport employees in and out of strategic locations such as [Yanbu](629638.md), [Tanajib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanajib) and [Shaybah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaybah).[^cite_note-kfia.gov.sa-2]

The airport is served by two runways; both 4 kilometres (2.5 mi) long, and consists of three terminal buildings: the Passenger Terminal serves mainstream passengers, the Aramco Terminal is used exclusively by Aramco employees to board Saudi Aramco Aviation flights and the Royal Terminal is reserved for use by the Saudi royal family. The busiest route operated between Dammam and another city is round trip to [Dubai](211583.md), with 70 weekly flights, an average of 10 flights a day.

## History





**Top**: [A-10 Thunderbolt IIs](12502446.md) parked on the taxiway of King Fahd Int'l Airport \
**Bottom**: A [MIM-104 Patriot](52024.md) missile battery near the airport. The terminal building and mosque can be seen in the background.

The airport is named for [King Fahd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahd_of_Saudi_Arabia) (r. 1982–2005), under whose reign it was constructed and inaugurated. As part of the government-run [Airports Development Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airports_Development_Program), the planning stages for the third and final international airport began in 1976.[^cite_note-7] The site master plan was created by architecture firm [Yamasaki & Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamasaki_&_Associates) and [Boeing](18933266.md) and completed in 1977,[^cite_note-8] with construction beginning in 1983.[^cite_note-9] The basic infrastructure of the airport was complete by the end of 1990, which allowed the U.S-led coalition forces to use the airport during the Gulf War in early 1991 for the storage of military aircraft, including 144 [A-10 Thunderbolt IIs](12502446.md), among other aircraft such as the [AH-64 Apaches](37746.md) and [CH-47 Chinooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_CH-47_Chinook) of the [101st Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/101st_Airborne_Division), before operations were transferred to the [Ahmad al-Jaber Air Base](5306887.md) in [Kuwait](7515890.md).

The [General Authority of Civil Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Authority_of_Civil_Aviation) of Saudi Arabia inaugurated the King Fahd International Airport and opened it to commercial traffic on 28 November 1999, and all airlines transferred their operations from the [Dhahran International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhahran_International_Airport), which had been in use until then. Dhahran International has since been converted for military usage and was designated the [King Abdulaziz Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdulaziz_Air_Base).

As part of the [Saudi Vision 2030](50299103.md) and the National Transformation Program, King Fahd International was corporatized in July 2017 under the Dammam Airports Company (DACO), which operates and maintains the airport.[^cite_note-10] In an effort to mitigate the spread of [COVID-19](63030231.md), all domestic and international flights were suspended until further notice on 21 March 2020. Following strict curfews and lowering in case numbers, domestic flights were allowed to operate once again on 31 May 2020. International flights finally resumed on 18 May 2021.

The terminal can only be accessed via Route 605, a secondary expressway linking the cities of [Khobar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khobar) and [Dammam](479214.md) in the south, and [Qatif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatif) in the north; to the airport. Route 6466, a minor road and [spur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spur_route) of Highway 40, links the highway to Route 605 and the airport. SAPTCO offers bus connections from Khobar and Dammam to the airport. Taxis are available at fixed prices to every major city and town in the kingdom, with rideshare companies such as [Careem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Careem) providing similar services.
