The **Islamabad Capital Territory**[^cite_note-9] is a [federal territory](446017.md) of [Pakistan](23235.md), centred around [Islamabad](51153.md), the capital of Pakistan. It is located on the northern edge of the [Pothohar Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pothohar_Plateau), at the foot of the [Margalla Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margalla_Hills), in the northwestern [Punjab region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab).[^cite_note-10] The Territory shares borders with the province of [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) in the west and the other districts of the [province of Punjab](24751.md) in the remaining directions. It covers an area of 906.5 square kilometres (350.0 mi<sup>2</sup>) and, according to [2023 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_census), has a population of over 2.3 million.\[^cite_note-Pakistan_Bureau_of_Statistics\_([www.pbs.gov.pk)-2023-6](http://www.pbs.gov.pk\)-2023-6/)]

Islamabad Capital Territory has been a part of [Punjab](24751.md) Province ever since, though federally administered. The latest official political map of Pakistan released in 2020, as well as many older official maps, shows it as part of Punjab Province.[^cite_note-11]

The area was separated from [Rawalpindi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_District) in 1967 to form a separate territory administered by the [federal government](1897833.md). The territory is represented in the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Pakistan) by [NA-52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NA-52_\(Islamabad-I\)), [NA-53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NA-53_\(Islamabad-II\)), and [NA-54](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NA-54_\(Islamabad-III\)) constituencies and by four seats in [the Senate](761025.md).

## History

In 1960, land was transferred from the [Rawalpindi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_District) of [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_\(Pakistan\)) province to replace the [Karachi Federal Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Federal_Capital_Territory) and establish Pakistan's new capital. According to the 1960s master plan, the territory included much of what was [Rawalpindi](343524.md), and composed of the following parts:[^cite_note-Routledge-2013-12]

- [Rawalpindi](343524.md), 259 square kilometres (100 sq mi)

- [Islamabad Urban](51153.md), 220.15 square kilometres (85.00 sq mi)

- [Margalla Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margalla_Hills), 220.15 square kilometres (85.00 sq mi)

- Islamabad Rural, 466.20 square kilometres (180.00 sq mi)

  [^cite_note-CDA_Facts-5]

However, Rawalpindi was eventually excluded from the Islamabad master plan in the 1980s.[^cite_note-Routledge-2013-12]

## Geography



The five zones and sectors of Islamabad

The Islamabad Capital Territory is composed of eight types of areas: administrative, commercial, diplomatic, educational, green, industrial, residential, and rural.

### Zones



Map showing the five zones of the Islamabad Capital Territory

The territory is divided into five zones in two groups:[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

- Zone I: for urban development and federal government institutions

- Zone II: for urban development

- Zone III: for rural development

- Zone IV: for rural development

- Zone V: for rural development

Zone I consists mainly of all the developed residential sectors, while Zone II consists of the under-developed residential sectors. Each residential sector is identified by a letter of the alphabet and a number, and covers an area of approximately 4 square kilometres. The sectors are lettered from A to I, and each sector is divided into four numbered sub-sectors.[^cite_note-Matthew_S._Hull-2012-15] Zone IV is the largest in area.

### Sectors

Main article: [Sectors of Islamabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sectors_of_Islamabad)

Series A, B, and C are still underdeveloped. The D series has seven sectors (D-11 to D-17), of which only sector D-12 is completely developed. This series is located at the foot of Margalla Hills.[^cite_note-TheIslamabad.com-16] The E Sectors are named from E-7 to E-17. Many foreigners and diplomatic personnel are housed in these sectors.[^cite_note-TheIslamabad.com-16] In the revised Master Plan of the city, CDA has decided to develop a park on the pattern of [Fatima Jinnah Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatima_Jinnah_Park) in sector E-14. Sectors E-8 and E-9 contain the campuses of [Bahria University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahria_University), [Air University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_University_\(Pakistan\)), and the [National Defence University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_University,_Islamabad).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] The F and G series contains the most developed sectors. F series contains sectors F-5 to F-17; some sectors are still under-developed. F-5 is an important sector for the software industry in Islamabad, as the two software technology parks are located here. The entire F-9 sector is covered with Fatima Jinnah Park. The [Centaurus complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Centaurus_\(building\)) will be one of the major landmarks of the F-8 sector.[^cite_note-TheIslamabad.com-16] G sectors are numbered G-5 through G-17. Some important places include the Jinnah Convention Center and [Serena Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Hotels) in G-5, the [Red Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Masjid,_Islamabad) in G-6, and the Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences, the largest medical complex in the capital, located in G-8.[^cite_note-TheIslamabad.com-16]

The H sectors are numbered H-8 through H-17. The H sectors are mostly dedicated to educational and health institutions. [National University of Sciences and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Sciences_and_Technology,_Pakistan) covers a major portion of sector H-12.[^cite_note-TheIslamabad.com-16] The I sectors are numbered from I-8 to I-18. With the exception of I-8, which is a well-developed residential area, these sectors are primarily part of the industrial zone. Currently two sub-sectors of I-9 and one sub-sector of [I-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-10,_Islamabad) are used as industrial areas. CDA is planning to set up Islamabad Railway Station in Sector I-18 and Industrial City in sector I-17.[^cite_note-TheIslamabad.com-16] Zone III consists primarily of the Margalla Hills and [Margalla Hills National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margalla_Hills_National_Park). [Rawal Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawal_Lake) is in this zone. Zone IV and V consist of Islamabad Park, and rural areas of the city. The [Soan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soan_River) flows into the city through Zone V.

## Climate

Main article: [Climate of Islamabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Islamabad)



Islamabad's deciduous trees colours in autumn, Atatürk Avenue

### Seasons

The climate of Islamabad has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): Cwa), with five seasons: winter (November–February), spring (March and April), summer (May and June), rainy [monsoon](31096097.md) (July and August), and autumn (September and October).

### Temperatures

The temperatures range from 13 °C (55 °F) in January to 38 °C (100 °F) in June. The hottest month is June, where average highs routinely exceed 38 °C (100.4 °F), while the coolest month is January. The highest recorded temperature was 46.6 °C (115.9 °F) on 23 June 2005 while the lowest temperature was −6 °C (21.2 °F) on 17 January 1967.[^cite_note-Best_Housing_Societies-2010-20][^cite_note-21] Winters generally feature dense fog in the mornings and sunny afternoons. In the city, temperatures stay mild, with snowfall over the higher elevations points on nearby hill stations, notably [Murree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murree) and [Nathia Gali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathia_Gali).

### Precipitation

The wettest month is July, with heavy rainfalls and evening [thunderstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderstorms) with the possibility of [cloudburst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloudburst) and flooding. Highest monthly rainfall of 743.3 millimetres (29.26 in) was recorded during July 1995.[^cite_note-Climate_Records-22] On 23 July 2001, Islamabad received a record breaking 620 millimetres (24 in) of rainfall in just 10 hours. It was the heaviest rainfall in Islamabad in the past 100 years and the highest rainfall in 24 hours as well.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] The city has also experienced snowfall on a number of occasions. Islamabad's [micro-climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micro-climate) is regulated by three artificial reservoirs: [Rawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawal_lake), [Simli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simli_lake), and [Khanpur Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanpur_Dam). The latter is located on the [Haro River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haro_River) near the town of [Khanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanpur_Tehsil,_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa), about 40 kilometres (25 mi) from Islamabad. Simli Dam is 30 kilometres (19 mi) north of Islamabad.

### Vegetation

Around 220 acres (89 ha) of the city consists of the [Margalla Hills National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margalla_Hills_National_Park), while the Loi Bher Forest is situated along the [Islamabad Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_Highway), covering an area of 1,087 acres (440 ha).[^cite_note-25]

## Government

Main article: [Government of the Islamabad Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Islamabad_Capital_Territory)

The government of the Islamabad Capital Territory is provided by the federal [Government of Pakistan](1897833.md) and by a number of territorial bodies. Article 258 of the [Constitution of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Pakistan) stipulates that the [President of Pakistan](419460.md) is responsible for establishing the government of the federal capital and the [Parliament of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Pakistan) makes laws for the territory.[^cite_note-30] Although the federal government retains strategic power over the territory, the day to day government is carried out by a number of bodies, which have been established over the years.

The [Capital Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Development_Authority_\(Islamabad\)) (CDA), established on 14 June 1960, was responsible for building the capital and continues to have some government roles. The [Islamabad Capital Territory Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_Capital_Territory_Administration) (ICTA) was established in 1980, and is led by a Chief Commissioner, who holds powers similar to a provincial government. The [Islamabad High Court](15668259.md), established in 2007 (dissolved in 2008, re-established in 2010) is the senior court of the territory and is equivalent to the four provincial high courts. The [Islamabad Metropolitan Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_Metropolitan_Corporation) (MCI), established in 2015, is the municipal authority and is equivalent to a [district government](639402.md), as the upper tier of the local government in the territory. The CDA has been gradually transferring many of its roles to the MCI.

At the most local level, there are 101 [union councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_council_\(Pakistan\)), though this is due to increase to 125 councils.

### Union councils

At the most local level, the Territory comprises 101 [union councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_council_\(Pakistan\)), though this is due to increase to 125 Union councils.[^cite_note-31]

According to [2023 Pakistani census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_census), there are 1,154,540 Punjabi, 415,838 Pashto, 358,922 Urdu, 140,780 Hindko, 51,920 Kashmiri, 46,270 Saraiki, 21,362 Sindhi, 10,315 Balti, 7,099 Shina, 5,016 Koshistani, 4,503 Balochi, 1,095 Mewati, 668 Brahvi, 182 Kalasha and 64,734 others, of total 2,283,244 speakers.

After Islamabad's establishment, people from all around Pakistan shifted here which led to a diverse demographics. [Punjabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabis) constitute the largest ethnolinguistic population in the territory, with many having settled here from Punjab province as well. [Pothwari dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahari-Pothwari), which has been regarded by many as the dialect of Punjabi, has historically been the indigenous language of the region; and Punjabi still continues to be the most-widely spoken first language in Islamabad, with Punjabi-speakers forming a majority of the population. Punjabi is spoken in the form of many dialects and varieties, including the [Majhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majhi_dialect), [Dhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhani_dialect), [Shahpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahpuri_dialect), [Jhangvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhangvi_dialect), and [Doabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doabi_dialect) dialects.

### Religion

See also: [Hinduism in Islamabad Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Islamabad_Capital_Territory)

Religion in Islamabad Territory, Pakistan

[^cite_note-35]

1. [Islam](6037917.md) (97.1%)

2. [Christianity](5211.md) (2.80%)

3. [Ahmadiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadiyya) (0.10%)

[Islam](6037917.md) is the largest and most practiced religion in the territory, with just over 97% of the population adhering to the faith. [Christianity](5211.md) is the second largest religion and forms 2.8% of the population. [Ahmadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadis) make up 0.11% of the population. Other minorities, like [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism_in_Pakistan) and [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Pakistan), form just 0.2% combined.\[^cite_note-Pakistan_Bureau_of_Statistics\_([www.pbs.gov.pk)-2023-6](http://www.pbs.gov.pk\)-2023-6/)]

Hindu population in Islamabad is very small as per census, around 900,\[^cite_note-Pakistan_Bureau_of_Statistics\_([www.pbs.gov.pk)-2023-6](http://www.pbs.gov.pk\)-2023-6/)] but other sources estimates it to be around 3000.[^cite_note-36] The territory also hosts the historic important and sacred [Ram mandir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Mandir,_Saidpur).[^cite_note-37]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Islamabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Islamabad)



[Islamabad Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_Stock_Exchange)



[Zarai Taraqiati Bank Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarai_Taraqiati_Bank_Limited)

Islamabad is a net contributor to the Pakistani economy, as whilst having only 0.8% of the country's population, it contributes 1% to the country's GDP.[^cite_note-42] [Islamabad Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_Stock_Exchange), founded in 1989, is Pakistan's third largest stock exchange after [Karachi Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachi_Stock_Exchange) and [Lahore Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Stock_Exchange).[^cite_note-43] The exchange has 118 members with 104 [corporate bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_corporate) and 18 individual members. The average daily turnover of the stock exchange is over one million shares.[^cite_note-44] As of 2012, Islamabad LTU (Large Tax Unit) was responsible for Rs 371 billion in tax revenue, which amounts to 20% of all the revenue collected by Federal Board of Revenue.[^cite_note-45]

Islamabad has seen an expansion in [information and communications technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_communication_technologies) with the addition two [Software Technology Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_park), which house numerous national and foreign technological and information technology companies. The tech parks are located in Evacuee Trust Complex and Awami Markaz. Awami Markaz houses 36 [IT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology) companies while Evacuee Trust house 29 companies.[^cite_note-46] Call centres for foreign companies have been targeted as another significant area of growth, with the government making efforts to reduce taxes by as much as 10% to encourage foreign investments in the information technology sector. Most of Pakistan's state-owned companies like [Pakistan International Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_International_Airlines), [Pakistan Television Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Television_Corporation), [Pakistan Telecommunication Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Telecommunication_Company), [Oil & Gas Development Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_&_Gas_Development_Company) and [Zarai Taraqiati Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarai_Taraqiati_Bank) are based in Islamabad. Headquarters of all major telecommunication operators such as PTCL, Mobilink, Telenor, Ufone, and China Mobile are located in Islamabad. Being an expensive city, the prices of most of fruits, vegetable and poultry items increased in Islamabad during the year 2015-2020[^cite_note-47]

## Transport

Main article: [Transport in Islamabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Islamabad)

### Airport

Islamabad is connected to major destinations around the world through the [Islamabad International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamabad_International_Airport). The airport is the largest in Pakistan, handling 9 million passengers per annum. The airport was built at a cost of \$400 million and opened on 3 May 2018, replacing the former [Benazir Bhutto International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benazir_Bhutto_International_Airport). It is the first [greenfield airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenfield_airport) in Pakistan with an area of 3,600-acre (15 km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-48]

### Metrobus

The [Rawalpindi-Islamabad Metrobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi-Islamabad_Metrobus) is a 24 km (14.9 mi) [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) system that serves the twin cities of [Rawalpindi](343524.md) and Islamabad in Pakistan. It uses dedicated bus lanes for all of its route covering 24 bus stations.

### Motorways

All major cities and towns are accessible through regular trains and bus services running mostly from the neighbouring city of Rawalpindi. [Lahore](125315.md), [Yarik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarik), [Dera Ismail Khan](696749.md) and [Peshawar](66230.md) are linked to Islamabad through a network of motorways, which has significantly reduced travelling times between these cities. [M-2 Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-2_motorway_\(Pakistan\)) is 367 km (228 mi) long and connect Islamabad and Lahore.[^cite_note-National_Highway_Authority_Pakistan-49] [M-1 Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-1_motorway_\(Pakistan\)) connects Islamabad with Peshawar and is 155 km (96 mi) long.[^cite_note-National_Highway_Authority_Pakistan-49] Islamabad is linked to Rawalpindi through the [Faizabad Interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faizabad_Interchange), which has a daily traffic volume of about 48,000 vehicles.[^cite_note-50] [M-14 Motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M-14_motorway_\(Pakistan\)) connects Islamabad to [Dera Ismail Khan](696749.md) and [Yarik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarik). It is 285 km (177 mi) long.
