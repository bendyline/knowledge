**Borneo** ([/ˈbɔːrnioʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is the [third-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area) island in the world, with an area of 748,168 km<sup>2</sup> (288,869 sq mi), and population of 23,053,723 (2020 national censuses).[^cite_note-detik20-1][^cite_note-citypop-2][^cite_note-statistics.gov.my-3] Situated at the geographic centre of [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia), it is one of the [Greater Sunda Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Sunda_Islands), located north of [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_Island), west of [Sulawesi](28740.md), and east of [Sumatra](28679.md). The island is crossed by the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator), which divides it roughly in half. In [Indonesia](14579.md), the island is also known as **Kalimantan**, which is also the name of the [Indonesian region located on the island](165470.md). The rest of the island consists of [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia) and [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei).

The [geology of Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_history_of_Borneo) was formed beginning in the [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic). It formed part of [Sundaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundaland), a region connected to mainland [Asia](689.md), until it became isolated by sea level rise at the end of the [Last Glacial Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period). With seven unique [ecoregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion), including large expanses of some of the oldest [tropical rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo_lowland_rain_forests) in the world, Borneo is [rich in biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_of_Borneo) and [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) species.

Archaeological evidence indicates that Borneo has been inhabited by humans for tens of thousands of years. The island is home to hundreds of [Indigenous communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_communities), cultures and languages, many of which have traditionally been grouped under the broad term "[Dayak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayak_people)". Trading settlements on Borneo were connected to regional maritime networks by the first millennium CE.

Several empires, kingdoms and [sultanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanates) subsequently emerged around the island's coasts. This includes the Javanese [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit), the [Sultanate of Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Brunei), which held influence over much of coastal Borneo and parts of the southern [Philippines](23440.md) at its peak in the 15th century,[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] and the [Sultanate of Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu), which became an important maritime power in the [Sulu Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sea) and northeastern Borneo. The island's interior, however, remained politically diverse and was largely governed by numerous Indigenous communities and local systems of authority rather than by a single state. Parts of the island were later colonised by [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Borneo), [Netherlands](23476997.md) and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Empire).

Since [decolonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Asia), Borneo has been politically divided among three sovereign states. Approximately 73% of the island forms part of [Indonesia](14579.md), comprising the five provinces collectively known as [Kalimantan](165470.md). About 26% consists of the [Malaysian](3607937.md) states of [Sabah](28678.md) and [Sarawak](28258.md), while the sovereign state of [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei) occupies a small area on the island's northwestern coast. Brunei is the only sovereign state whose principal territory lies wholly on Borneo; Indonesia and Malaysia both extend far beyond the island.[^cite_note-6] The Malaysian federal territory of [Labuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labuan) is situated on a small island just off the coast of Borneo.

The majority of Borneo's inhabitants reside in coastal cities. It is the site of Indonesia's planned future capital, [Nusantara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusantara_\(city\)). Major economic sectors include [oil and gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_and_gas), [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), [timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timber_industry) and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism). Industrial [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_Borneo) in Indonesian and Malaysian Borneo for timber and agricultural conversion has taken place during the past century.

## Etymology

### Borneo

The name *Borneo* is closely associated with [ Brunei Darussalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei), the historic sultanate and trading polity on the island. Early European travellers and cartographers recorded variants such as *Bornei*, *Burnei*, *Burneo* and *Borneo* when referring to Brunei, and the name was subsequently applied more broadly to the island.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] The name may derive from the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) word *_váruṇa_* (वरुण), meaning either "water" or [Varuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varuna), the Hindu god of rain and oceans.[^cite_note-9]

The term has been used as early as the late 9th century by [Fan Chuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_Chuo_\(Tang_dynasty\)) in his book on [Nanzhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanzhao) in the 860s. The term Bóní (勃泥) was used to describe trading colonies in the south.[^cite_note-10] In 977, [Chinese records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China) use the term *Bo-ni* to refer to Borneo. In 1225, it was also mentioned by the Chinese official [Chau Ju-Kua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhao_Rukuo).[^cite_note-11] In modern Chinese, the island is called 婆羅洲 ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 婆罗洲; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Póluó Zhōu*), the first two characters being phonetic and the third referring to a large landmass.[^cite_note-12]

### Kalimantan

The Javanese manuscript *[Nagarakretagama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagarakretagama)*, written by [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit) court poet [Mpu Prapanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpu_Prapanca) in 1365, mentions the island as *Nusa Tanjungnagara*, which means the "island of the [Tanjungpura Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjungpura_Kingdom)".[^cite_note-13]

When the sixteenth-century [Portuguese explorer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_explorer) [Jorge de Menezes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_de_Menezes) made contact with the indigenous people of Borneo, they referred to their island as *Pulu K'lemantang*, and this second word became **[Kalimantan](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Kalimantan#Indonesian)**, the name for modern-day Borneo in the Indonesian language.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The term *kelamantan* is used in Sarawak to refer to a group of people who consume [sago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sago) in the northern part of the island.[^cite_note-17]

[John Crawfurd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Crawfurd) noted in his 1865 *Descriptive Dictionary of the Indian Islands* that "(...) Borneo has sometimes been called by the Malays Kalamantan.", which he traces back to the name of a type of mango (*[Mangifera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangifera)*), though he added that the name, meaning *Isle of Mangoes*, was little-known and not popular.[^cite_note-18] A mango variety locally called *klemantan* is still widely found in rural [Ketapang](17473479.md) and surrounding areas of West Kalimantan.[^cite_note-19]

Another source states that it derives from the Sanskrit word *kalamanthana*, meaning "burning weather", possibly to describe the island's hot and humid tropical weather.[^cite_note-20] In the Indianized Malay era the name *Kalamanthana* was derived from the Sanskrit terms *[kala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kala_\(time\))* (time or season) and *manthana* (churning, kindling, or creating fire by friction),[^cite_note-21] which possibly describes the hot weather.[^cite_note-22]

## Geography

### Geology

See also: [Geological history of Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_history_of_Borneo)



Location of Borneo in [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia).

Borneo was formed through [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) accretion of microcontinental fragments, [ophiolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophiolite) terranes and [island arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_arc) crust onto a [Paleozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleozoic) continental core. At the beginning of the [Cenozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenozoic), Borneo formed a [promontory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promontory) of [Sundaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundaland) which partly separated from Asian mainland by the [proto-South China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonics_of_the_South_China_Sea).[^cite_note-Geology_of_Borneo-23] The oceanic part of the proto-South China Sea was [subducted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction) during the [Paleogene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleogene) period and a large [accretionary complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accretionary_complex) formed along the northwestern of the island of Borneo.

In the [early Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Miocene) uplift of the accretionary complex occurred as a result of underthrusting of thinned continental crust in northwest.[^cite_note-Geology_of_Borneo-23] The uplift may have also resulted from shortening due to the counter-clockwise rotation of Borneo between 20 and 10 [mega-annum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mega-annum) (Ma) as a consequence of Australia–Southeast Asia collision.[^cite_note-Geology_of_Borneo-23] Large volumes of [sediment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sediment) were shed into basins, which scattered offshore to the west, north and east of Borneo as well into a [Neogene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neogene) basin which is currently exposed in large areas of eastern and southern [Sabah](28678.md).

In southeast Sabah, the [Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene) to recent island arc terranes of the [Sulu Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Archipelago) extend onshore into Borneo with the older [volcanic arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_arc) was the result of southeast dipping subduction while the younger volcanics are likely resulted from northwest dipping subduction the [Celebes Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebes_Sea).[^cite_note-Geology_of_Borneo-23]



Marine life off the coast of Borneo, in the Sulu Sea

Before sea levels rose at the end of the [last ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period), Borneo was part of the mainland of Asia, forming, with [Java](69336.md) and [Sumatra](28679.md), the upland regions of a peninsula that extended east from present day [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochina). The [South China Sea](74209.md) and [Gulf of Thailand](198107.md) now submerge the former low-lying areas of the peninsula. Deeper waters separating Borneo from neighbouring [Sulawesi](28740.md) prevented a land connection to that island, creating the divide known as [Wallace's Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace's_Line) between Asian and Australia-[New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea) biological regions.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The island today is surrounded by the South China Sea to the north and northwest, the [Sulu Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sea) to the northeast, the Celebes Sea and the [Makassar Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makassar_Strait) to the east, and the [Java Sea](467143.md) and [Karimata Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karimata_Strait) to the south. To the west of Borneo are the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula) and Sumatra. To the south and east are islands of Indonesia: [Java](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_\(island\)) and Sulawesi, respectively. To the northeast are the [Philippine Islands](23440.md). With an area of 743,330 square kilometres (287,000 sq mi), it is the third-largest island in the world, and is the largest island of Asia (the largest continent). Its highest point is [Mount Kinabalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kinabalu) in Sabah, Malaysia, with an elevation of 4,095 m (13,435 ft).[^cite_note-highest_summit-26]



[Lake Sentarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Sentarum_National_Park), Kapuas Hulu Regency, West Kalimantan

The largest river system is the [Kapuas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapuas_River) in [West Kalimantan](453636.md), with a length of 1,143 km (710 mi).[^cite_note-TanMerdikawati2015-27] Other major rivers include the [Mahakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahakam_River) in [East Kalimantan](453651.md) (980 km (610 mi) long),[^cite_note-28] the [Barito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barito_River), [Kahayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahayan_River), and [Mendawai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendawai_River) in [South Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kalimantan) (1,090 km (680 mi), 658 km (409 mi), and 616 km (383 mi) long respectively),[^cite_note-29] [Rajang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajang_River) in Sarawak (565 km (351 mi) long)[^cite_note-30] and [Kinabatangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinabatangan_River) in Sabah (560 km (350 mi) long).[^cite_note-31] Borneo has significant cave systems. In Sarawak, the Clearwater Cave has one of the world's longest underground rivers while [Deer Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deer_Cave) is home to over three million [bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat), with [guano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guano) accumulated to over 100 metres (330 ft) deep.[^cite_note-32] The [Gomantong Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomantong_Caves) in Sabah has been dubbed as the "Cockroach Cave" due to the presence of millions of cockroaches inside the cave.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] The [Gunung Mulu National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunung_Mulu_National_Park) in Sarawak and [Sangkulirang-Mangkalihat Karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangkulirang-Mangkalihat_Karst) in East Kalimantan which particularly a [karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst) areas contains thousands of smaller caves.[^cite_note-35]

### Ecology

See also: [Biodiversity of Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_of_Borneo), [Fauna of Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_of_Borneo), [Flora of Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_of_Borneo), [List of endemic birds of Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_endemic_birds_of_Borneo), and [List of mammals of Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mammals_of_Borneo)



The [critically endangered](7451605.md) [Bornean orangutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornean_orangutan), a great ape [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic) to Borneo

The Borneo [rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest) is estimated to be around 140 million years old, making it one of the oldest rainforests in the world.[^cite_note-36] The current dominant tree group, the [dipterocarps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipterocarpaceae), has dominated the [Borneo lowland rain forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo_lowland_rain_forests) for millions of years.[^cite_note-37] It is the centre of the evolution and distribution of many endemic species of plants and animals, and the rainforest is one of the few remaining [natural habitats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_\(ecology\)) for the endangered [Bornean orangutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornean_orangutan). It is an important refuge for many endemic forest species, including the [Borneo elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo_elephant), the [eastern Sumatran rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Sumatran_rhinoceros), the [Bornean clouded leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornean_clouded_leopard), the [Bornean rock frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staurois_tuberilinguis), the [hose's palm civet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hose's_palm_civet) and the [dayak fruit bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayak_fruit_bat).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]



[NASA](18426568.md) satellite image of Borneo on 19 May 2002

[Peat swamp forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo_peat_swamp_forests) occupy the entire coastline of Borneo.[^cite_note-40] The soil of the peat swamp is comparatively infertile, while it is known to be the home of various bird species such as the [hook-billed bulbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hook-billed_bulbul), [helmeted hornbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmeted_hornbill) and [rhinoceros hornbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinoceros_hornbill).[^cite_note-birds-41] There are about 15,000 species of [flowering plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flowering_plant) with 3,000 species of trees (267 species are [dipterocarps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipterocarp)), 221 species of terrestrial mammals and 420 species of resident birds in Borneo.[^cite_note-42] There are about 440 freshwater fish species in Borneo (about the same as Sumatra and Java combined).[^cite_note-43] The [Borneo river shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyphis_fowlerae) is known only from the [Kinabatangan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinabatangan_River).[^cite_note-44] In 2010, the [World Wide Fund for Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Fund_for_Nature) (WWF) stated that 123 species have been discovered in Borneo since the "[Heart of Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_of_Borneo)" agreement was signed in 2007.[^cite_note-45]

The WWF has classified the island into seven distinct [ecoregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion). Most are lowland regions:[^cite_note-Borneo_forests-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

- [Borneo lowland rain forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo_lowland_rain_forests) cover most of the island, with an area of 427,500 square kilometres (165,100 sq mi).

- [Borneo peat swamp forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo_peat_swamp_forests)

- *[Kerangas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerangas)* or Sundaland heath forests

- [Southwest Borneo freshwater swamp forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Borneo_freshwater_swamp_forests) are found in the island's western and southern lowlands

- [Sunda Shelf mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunda_Shelf_mangroves)

- The [Borneo montane rain forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo_montane_rain_forests) lie in the central highlands of the island, above the 1,000 metres (3,300 ft) elevation.

- The highest elevations of [Mount Kinabalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kinabalu) are home to the [Kinabalu montane alpine meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinabalu_montane_alpine_meadows), a subalpine and alpine shrubland notable for its numerous endemic species, including many orchids.

According to analysis of data from [Global Forest Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Forest_Watch),[^cite_note-49] the Indonesian portion of Borneo lost 10.7 million hectares (26 million acres) of tree cover between 2002 and 2019, of which 4 million hectares (9.9 million acres) was [primary forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_forest), compared with Malaysian Borneo's 4.4 million hectares (11 million acres) of tree cover loss and 1.9 million hectares (4.7 million acres) of primary forest cover loss. As of 2020, Indonesian Borneo accounts for 72% of the island's tree cover, Malaysian Borneo 27%, and Brunei 1%. Primary forest in Indonesia accounts for 44% of Borneo's overall tree cover.[^cite_note-50]

#### Environmental issues

See also: [Deforestation in Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_Borneo), [Southeast Asian haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Asian_haze), [Conservation in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_in_Indonesia), and [Conservation in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_in_Malaysia)



Logging near [Crocker Range National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocker_Range_National_Park). Borneo has lost more than half of its rainforests in the past half century.

[^cite_note-51]

The island historically had extensive rainforest cover, but the area was reduced due to heavy logging by the Indonesian and Malaysian [wood industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_industry), especially with the large demands of raw materials from industrial countries along with the conversion of forest lands for large-scale agricultural purposes.[^cite_note-Borneo_forests-46] Half of the annual global [tropical timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_timber) acquisition comes from Borneo. [Palm oil](57561.md) plantations have been widely developed and are rapidly encroaching on the last remnants of primary rainforest.[^cite_note-52] [Forest fires](56106.md) since 1997, started by the locals to clear the forests for plantations were exacerbated by an exceptionally dry [El Niño](317311.md) season, worsening the annual shrinkage of the rainforest.[^cite_note-53] During these fires, hotspots were visible on [satellite images](1184557.md) and the resulting [haze](998156.md) frequently affected Brunei, Indonesia and Malaysia. The haze could also reach southern [Thailand](30128.md), [Cambodia](334751.md), [Vietnam](202354.md) and the [Philippines](23440.md) as evidenced on the [2015 Southeast Asian haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Southeast_Asian_haze).[^cite_note-54]

A study in 2018 found that [Bornean orangutans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornean_orangutan) declined by 148,500 individuals from 1999 to 2015.[^cite_note-55]

## History

### Early history



[Dayaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayak_people), a collective term used to describe [Indigenous people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_people) of Borneo, depicted in an 1864 illustration in *[The Illustrated London News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Illustrated_London_News)*.

In the [Samang Buat Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samang_Buat_Cave?action=edit\&redlink=1), in [Lahad Datu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahad_Datu_District), Sabah, archaeological evidence suggests that human settlement dates back to around 46,000 years ago, confirming Lahad Datu as one of the most important prehistoric centres in Southeast Asia.[^cite_note-56] Intriguingly, tools found in [Mansuli Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansuli_Valley?action=edit\&redlink=1), also in Lahad Datu, have been dated as early as 235,000 years ago.[^cite_note-57]

In [Tingkayu Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tingkayu_Valley?action=edit\&redlink=1), Kunak, Sabah, archaeological excavations revealed traces of a Palaeolithic community dating from approximately 28,000–30,000 years ago. The site, once a prehistoric lake basin, yielded Hoabinhian stone tools that indicate human activity in the area.[^cite_note-58]

In [Niah Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niah_National_Park), Sarawak, human presence has been dated to around 40,000 years ago. Recent studies in the Trader Cave section of the complex discovered microlithic tools and human remains, dated between 55,000 and 65,000 years ago, making it one of the most important early modern human sites in Southeast Asia.[^cite_note-59]



[Cave painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_painting) in [Lubang Jeriji Saléh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubang_Jeriji_Sal%C3%A9h).

In November 2018, scientists reported the discovery of the oldest known [figurative art painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figurative_art), over 40,000 (perhaps as old as 52,000) years old, of an unknown animal, in the cave of [Lubang Jeriji Saléh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubang_Jeriji_Sal%C3%A9h) on the island of Borneo.[^cite_note-NYT-20181107-cz-60][^cite_note-NAT-20181107-61] It has been proposed, based on house construction styles, linguistic and genetic evidence, that [Madagascar](18964.md) may have been first populated from southern Borneo.[^cite_note-62]

According to ancient Chinese (977),[^cite_note-63]<sup>: 129 </sup> Indian and Japanese manuscripts, western coastal cities of Borneo had become trading ports by the [first millennium AD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_millennium).[^cite_note-64] In Chinese manuscripts, gold, [camphor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camphor), [tortoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortoise) shells, [hornbill ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornbill_ivory), [rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinoceros) horn, [crane crest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crane_\(bird\)), [beeswax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beeswax), [lakawood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakawood) (a scented heartwood and root wood of a thick [liana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liana), *Dalbergia parviflora*), [dragon's blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon's_blood), [rattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rattan), edible [bird's nests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird's_nest_soup) and various spices were described as among the most valuable items from Borneo.[^cite_note-Broek1962-65] The [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_India) named Borneo *[Suvarnabhumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suvarnabhumi)* (Land of Gold), and also *Karpuradvipa* (Camphor Island). The [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people) named Borneo *Puradvipa* (Diamond Island). [Archaeological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological) findings in the Sarawak river delta reveal that the area was a thriving centre of trade between India and China from the 6th century until about 1300.[^cite_note-Broek1962-65]



Territorial loss of the [thalassocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalassocracy) of the [Sultanate of Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruneian_Empire) from 1400 to 1890 due to the beginning of [Western imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_imperialism)

Stone pillars bearing inscriptions in the [Pallava script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallava_script), found in [Kutai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutai) along the [Mahakam River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahakam_River) in [East Kalimantan](453651.md) and dating to around the second half of the 4th century, constitute some of the oldest evidence of [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) influence in Southeast Asia.[^cite_note-66] By the 14th century, Borneo became a [vassal state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal_state) of [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit) (in present-day Indonesia),[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] later changing its allegiance to the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) of China.[^cite_note-69] Pre-Islamic Sulu, then known as [Lupah Sūg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupah_Sug), stretched from [Palawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palawan_\(island\)) and the [Sulu Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Archipelago) in the Philippines, to [Sabah](28678.md), Eastern, and [Northern Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Kalimantan) in Borneo.[^cite_note-70] The Sulu Empire rose as a rebellion and reaction against the Majapahit, which had briefly occupied its territory. [Islam](6037917.md) arrived in the 10th century,[^cite_note-71] brought by Muslim traders who later converted many indigenous peoples in the coastal areas.[^cite_note-72]

The Sultanate of Brunei declared independence from Majapahit following the death of the Majapahit emperor in the mid-14th century. During its golden age under the [Bolkiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolkiah) from the 15th to the 17th century, the Bruneian sultanate ruled almost the entire coastal area of Borneo (lending its name to the island due to its influence in the region) and several islands in the southwestern Philippines.[^cite_note-history-73] During the 1450s, [Shari'ful Hashem Syed Abu Bakr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharif_ul-H%C4%81shim_of_Sulu), an Arab born in [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor),[^cite_note-74] arrived in Sulu from [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca). In 1457, he founded the [Sultanate of Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu); he styled himself *Paduka Maulana Mahasari Sharif Sultan Hashem Abu Bakr*.[^cite_note-75] Following its independence in 1578 from Bruneian control,[^cite_note-76] the Sultanate of Sulu began to expand its [thalassocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalassocracy) to parts of northern Borneo.[^cite_note-thalassocracy-77][^cite_note-Hussainmiya2006-78] Both sultanates who ruled northern Borneo had traditionally engaged in trade with China by means of the frequent Chinese [junks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junk_\(ship\)).[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] Outside of the two thalassocratic states, Borneo's interior remained free from the rule of any kingdoms.[^cite_note-81]

### British and Dutch control

Main articles: [British Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Borneo) and [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md)



[British flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Jack) hoisted for the first time on the island of [Labuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labuan), on 24 December 1846.

After the [fall of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Malacca_\(1511\)) in 1511, Portuguese merchants traded regularly with Borneo, and especially with Brunei from 1530.[^cite_note-Lach1994-82] Having visited Brunei's capital, the Portuguese described the place as surrounded by a [stone wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_wall).[^cite_note-83] While Borneo was seen as rich, the Portuguese did not make any attempts to conquer it.[^cite_note-Lach1994-82] The Spanish had sailed from Spanish America and conquered the Brunei's provinces in the Philippines and incorporated it into the Mexico-Centered [Viceroyalty of New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Spain). The Spanish visit to Brunei led to the [Castilian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilian_War) in 1578. The British began to trade with [Sambas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sambas) of southern Borneo in 1609, while the Dutch only began their trade in 1644: to Banjar and Martapura, also in the southern Borneo.[^cite_note-84] The Dutch tried to settle the island of [Balambangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balambangan_Island), north of Borneo, in the second half of the 18th century, but withdrew by 1797.[^cite_note-Dutch_Borneo-85] In 1812, the sultan in southern Borneo ceded his forts to the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company). The British, led by [Stamford Raffles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford_Raffles), then tried to establish an intervention in Sambas but failed. Although they managed to defeat the sultanate the next year and declared a blockade on all ports in Borneo except Brunei, [Banjarmasin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjarmasin) and [Pontianak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontianak,_Indonesia), the project was cancelled by the British governor-general [Lord Minto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Elliot-Murray-Kynynmound,_1st_Earl_of_Minto) in India as it was too expensive.[^cite_note-Dutch_Borneo-85] At the beginning of British and Dutch exploration on the island, they described the island of Borneo as full of [head hunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headhunting), with the indigenous in the interior practising [cannibalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_cannibalism),[^cite_note-Dutch_and_British_explorations-86] and the waters around the island infested with [pirates](50715.md), especially between the north eastern Borneo and the southern Philippines.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] The [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_race) and [Sea Dayak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iban_people) pirates preyed on maritime shipping in the waters between Singapore and Hong Kong from their haven in Borneo,[^cite_note-89] along with the attacks by [Illanuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illanun) of the [Moro pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_pirates) from the southern Philippines, such as in the [Battle off Mukah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_off_Mukah).[^cite_note-90]



Map of the island, divided between the British and the Dutch, 1898. The present boundaries of Malaysia, Indonesia, and Brunei are largely inherited from British and Dutch colonial rules.

The Dutch began to intervene in the southern part of the island upon resuming contact in 1815, posting *[residents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_\(title\))* to Banjarmasin, Pontianak and Sambas and *assistant-residents* to Landak and Mampawa.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] The Sultanate of Brunei in 1842 granted large parts of land in Sarawak to the British adventurer [James Brooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Brooke), as a reward for his help in quelling a local rebellion. Brooke established the [Raj of Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raj_of_Sarawak) and was recognised as its rajah after paying a fee to the sultanate. He established a monarchy, and the Brooke dynasty (through his nephew and great-nephew) ruled Sarawak for 100 years; the leaders were known as the [White Rajahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Rajahs).[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] Brooke also acquired the island of [Labuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labuan) for [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) in 1846 through the [Treaty of Labuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Labuan) with the sultan of Brunei, [Omar Ali Saifuddin II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Ali_Saifuddin_II) on 18 December 1846.[^cite_note-95] The region of northern Borneo came under the administration of [North Borneo Chartered Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo_Chartered_Company) following the acquisition of territory from the Sultanates of Brunei and Sulu by a German businessman and adventurer named [Baron von Overbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Overbeck), before it was passed to the British Dent brothers ([Alfred Dent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Dent) and Edward Dent).[^cite_note-Hussainmiya2006-78][^cite_note-96] Further expansion by the British continued into the Borneo interior.[^cite_note-97] This led the 26th sultan of Brunei, [Hashim Jalilul Alam Aqamaddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashim_Jalilul_Alam_Aqamaddin) to appeal the British to halt such efforts, and as a result a Treaty of Protection was signed in 1888, rendering Brunei a British protectorate.[^cite_note-98]



[Dayak people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayak_people) during an [Erau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erau) ceremony in [Tenggarong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenggarong)

Before the acquisition by the British, the Americans also managed to establish their temporary presence in northwestern Borneo after acquiring a parcel of land from the Sultanate of Brunei. A company known as [American Trading Company of Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Trading_Company_of_Borneo) was formed by [Joseph William Torrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_William_Torrey), [Thomas Bradley Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Bradley_Harris) and several Chinese investors, establishing a colony named "Ellena" in the [Kimanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimanis) area.[^cite_note-99] The colony failed and was abandoned, due to denials of financial backing, especially by the US government, and to diseases and riots among the workers.[^cite_note-100] Before Torrey left, he managed to sell the land to the German businessman, Overbeck.[^cite_note-101] Meanwhile, the Germans under William Frederick Schuck were awarded a parcel of land in northeastern Borneo of the Sandakan Bay from the Sultanate of Sulu where he conducted business and exported large quantities of arms, [opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium), textiles and tobacco to Sulu before the land was also passed to Overbeck by the sultanate.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]



Arab-Malay [Sultan of Pontianak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontianak_Sultanate) in 1930

Prior to the recognition of Spanish presence in the Philippine archipelago, a protocol known as the [Madrid Protocol of 1885](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Protocol_of_1885) was signed between the governments of the United Kingdom, Germany and Spain in [Madrid](41188263.md) to cement Spanish influence and recognise their sovereignty over the Sultanate of Sulu—in return for Spain's relinquishing its claim to the former possessions of the sultanate in northern Borneo.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] The British administration then established the first railway network in northern Borneo, known as the [North Borneo Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo_Railway).[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] During this time, the British sponsored a large number of Chinese workers to migrate to northern Borneo to work in European plantation and mines,[^cite_note-108] and the Dutch followed suit to increase their economic production.[^cite_note-109] By 1888, North Borneo, Sarawak and Brunei in northern Borneo had become British [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate).[^cite_note-PrescottTriggs2008-110] The area in southern Borneo was made Dutch protectorate in 1891.[^cite_note-Dutch_and_British_explorations-86] The Dutch who already claimed the whole Borneo were asked by Britain to delimit their boundaries between the two colonial territories to avoid further conflicts.[^cite_note-PrescottTriggs2008-110] The British and Dutch governments had signed the [Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Dutch_Treaty_of_1824) to exchange trading ports in [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula) and [Sumatra](28679.md) that were under their controls and assert spheres of influence. This resulted in indirectly establishing British- and Dutch-controlled areas in the north (Malay Peninsula) and south (Sumatra and Riau Islands) respectively.[^cite_note-111]

In 1895, [Marcus Samuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Samuel,_1st_Viscount_Bearsted) received a concession in the Kutei area of east Borneo, and based on [oil seepages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_seep) in the [Mahakam River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahakam_River) [delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta), Mark Abrahams struck oil in February 1897. This was the discovery of the [Sanga Sanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanga-Sanga,_Kutai_Kartanegara) Oil Field, a refinery was built in [Balikpapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balikpapan), and discovery of the [Samboja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samboja) Oil Field followed in 1909. In 1901, the Pamusian Oil Field was discovered on [Tarakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarakan), and the [Bunyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunyu) Oil Field in 1929. [Royal Dutch Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Shell) discovered the [Miri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miri,_Malaysia) Oil Field in 1910, and the [Seria oil field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seria_oil_field) in 1929.[^cite_note-dy-112][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]

### World War II

See also: [Japanese occupation of British Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_British_Borneo) and [Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Dutch_East_Indies)



Japanese troops march through the streets of [Labuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_Labuan) on 14 January 1942.



American [support craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_craft) moving towards [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_Labuan) and Brown beach to assist the landing of members of the Australian [24th Infantry Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24th_Brigade_\(Australia\)) on the island during [Operation Oboe Six](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Oboe_Six), 10 June 1945.

During World War II, Japanese forces gained control and occupied most areas of Borneo from 1941 to 1945. In the first stage of the war, the British saw the Japanese advance to Borneo as motivated by political and territorial ambitions rather than economic factors.[^cite_note-115] The occupation drove many people in the coastal towns to the interior, searching for food and escaping the Japanese.[^cite_note-Lim2008-116] The Chinese residents in Borneo, especially with the [Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) in [Mainland China](55079.md) mostly resisted the Japanese occupation.[^cite_note-117] Following the formation of resistance movements in northern Borneo such as the [Jesselton Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesselton_Revolt), many innocent indigenous and Chinese people were executed by the Japanese for their alleged involvement.[^cite_note-118]

In Kalimantan, the Japanese also killed many Malay intellectuals, executing all the Malay sultans of West Kalimantan in the [Pontianak incidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontianak_incidents), together with Chinese people who were already against the Japanese for suspecting them to be threats.[^cite_note-threats-119] Sultan Muhammad Ibrahim Shafi ud-din II of [Sambas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sambas) was executed in 1944. The sultanate was thereafter suspended and replaced by a Japanese council.[^cite_note-120] The Japanese also set-up *Pusat Tenaga Rakjat* (PUTERA)[^cite_note-121] in the Indonesian archipelago in 1943, although it was abolished the following year when it became too nationalistic.[^cite_note-Meyer2000-122] Some of the Indonesian nationalist like [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno) and [Hatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Hatta) who had returned from Dutch exile began to co-operate with the Japanese. Shortly after his release, Sukarno became president of the Central Advisory Council, an advisory council for south Borneo, [Celebes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebes_Islands), and [Lesser Sunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Sunda_Islands), set up in February 1945.[^cite_note-Meyer2000-122]

After the [fall of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Singapore), the Japanese sent several thousand of British and Australian [prisoners of war](25008.md) to camps in Borneo such as [Batu Lintang camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batu_Lintang_camp). From the [Sandakan camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakan_camp) site, only six of some 2,500 prisoners survived after they were forced to march in an event known as the [Sandakan Death March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandakan_Death_March).[^cite_note-123] In addition, of the total of 17,488 [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people) labourers brought in by the Japanese during the occupation, only 1,500 survived mainly due to starvation, harsh working conditions and maltreatment.[^cite_note-Lim2008-116] The Dayak and other indigenous people played a role in [guerrilla warfare](12720.md) against the occupying forces, particularly in the [Kapit Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapit_Division). They temporarily revived headhunting of Japanese toward the end of the war,[^cite_note-124] with Allied [Z Special Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z_Special_Unit) provided assistance to them.[^cite_note-125] Australia contributed significantly to the liberation of Borneo.[^cite_note-126] The [Australian Imperial Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Australian_Imperial_Force) was sent to Borneo to fight off the Japanese.[^cite_note-127] Together with other Allies, the island [was completely liberated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo_campaign_\(1945\)) in 1945.

### Recent history



[Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno) visiting [Pontianak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontianak), West Kalimantan, in 1963.

In May 1945, officials in Tokyo suggested that whether northern Borneo should be included in the proposed new country of Indonesia should be separately determined based on the desires of its indigenous people and following the disposition of [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya).[^cite_note-128] Sukarno and [Mohammad Yamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Yamin) meanwhile continuously advocated for a [Greater Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Indonesia) republic.[^cite_note-129] Towards the end of the war, Japan decided to give an early independence to a new proposed country of Indonesia on 17 July 1945, with an Independence Committee meeting scheduled for 19 August 1945.[^cite_note-Meyer2000-122] However, following the [surrender of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan) to the Allied forces, the meeting was shelved. Sukarno and Hatta continued the plan by unilaterally declaring independence, although the Dutch tried to retake their colonial possession in Borneo.[^cite_note-Meyer2000-122]

The southern part of the island achieved its independence through the [Proclamation of Indonesian Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_Indonesian_Independence) on 17 August 1945. The southern part saw [guerrilla conflicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalimantan_Physical_Revolution) followed by Dutch blockades to cut supplies for nationalist within the region.[^cite_note-130] While nationalist guerrillas supporting the inclusion of southern Borneo in the new Indonesian republic were active in Ketapang, and to lesser extent in Sambas where they rallied with the red-white flag which became the [flag of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Indonesia), most of the Chinese residents in southern Borneo expected to be liberated by [Chinese Nationalist troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Revolutionary_Army) from mainland China and to integrate their districts as an overseas province of [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%9349\)).[^cite_note-Heidhues2003-131] Meanwhile, Sarawak and Sabah in northern Borneo became separate British crown colonies in 1946.[^cite_note-Porritt1997-132][^cite_note-cc-133]



The [Queen's Own Highlanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_Own_Highlanders_\(Seaforth_and_Camerons\)) 1st Battalion conduct a patrol to search for enemy positions in the jungle of Brunei.

In 1961, Prime Minister [Tunku Abdul Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunku_Abdul_Rahman) of the independent [Federation of Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Malaya) desired to unite Malaya, the British colonies of [Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_Sarawak), [North Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_North_Borneo), [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_Singapore) and the protectorate of [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei) under the proposed [Federation of Malaysia](3607937.md).[^cite_note-134] The idea was heavily opposed by the governments in both Indonesia and the Philippines as well from communist sympathisers and nationalists in Borneo.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] Sukarno, as the president of the new republic, perceiving the British trying to maintain their presence in northern Borneo and the Malay Peninsula, decided to launch a military infiltration, later known as the *[confrontation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konfrontasi)*, from 1962 to 1969.[^cite_note-137] As a response to the growing opposition, the British deployed their armed forces to guard their colonies against Indonesian and communist revolts.[^cite_note-138] Australia and New Zealand also participated in these measures.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140]

The Philippines opposed the newly proposed federation, claiming the eastern part of North Borneo (today the Malaysian state of Sabah) as part of its territory as a former possession of the [Sultanate of Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu).[^cite_note-141] The Philippine government mostly based their claim on the Sultanate of Sulu's [cession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cession) agreement with the British North Borneo Company, as by now the sultanate had come under the jurisdiction of the Philippine republican administration, which therefore should inherit the Sulu former territories. The Philippine government also claimed that the heirs of the sultanate had ceded all their territorial rights to the republic.[^cite_note-142]



The proposed flag of the [North Borneo Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo_Federation), an attempt to establish a sovereign state by unifying North Borneo, Brunei, and Sarawak by [A. M. Azahari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._M._Azahari)

The Sultanate of Brunei at first welcomed the proposal of a new larger federation.[^cite_note-143] Meanwhile, the [Brunei People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei_People's_Party) led by [A.M. Azahari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.M._Azahari) desired to reunify Brunei, Sarawak and North Borneo into one federation known as the [North Borneo Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo_Federation) ([Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language): *Kesatuan Negara Kalimantan Utara*), where the [sultan of Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sultans_of_Brunei) would be the head of state for the federation—though Azahari had his own intention to abolish the Brunei monarchy, to make Brunei more democratic, and to integrate the territory and other former British colonies in Borneo into Indonesia, with the support from the latter government.[^cite_note-144] This directly led to the [Brunei Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei_Revolt), which thwarted Azahari's attempt and forced him to escape to Indonesia. Brunei withdrew from being part of the new Federation of Malaysia due to some disagreements on other issues while political leaders in Sarawak and North Borneo continued to favour inclusion in a larger federation.[^cite_note-145]

With the continuous opposition from Indonesia and the Philippines, the [Cobbold Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobbold_Commission) was established to discover the feeling of the native populations in northern Borneo; it found the people greatly in favour of federation, with various stipulations.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] The federation was successfully achieved with the inclusion of northern Borneo through the [Malaysia Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Agreement) on 16 September 1963.[^cite_note-148] To this day, the area in northern Borneo is still subjected to attacks by Moro pirates since the 18th century and militant from groups such as [Abu Sayyaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Sayyaf) since 2000 in the frequent [cross border attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_border_attacks_in_Sabah). During the administration of Philippine president [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos), Marcos made some attempts to destabilise the state of Sabah,[^cite_note-149] although his plan failed and resulted in the [Jabidah massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabidah_massacre) and later the [insurgency in the southern Philippines](4602307.md).[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151]

Brunei remained a British protected state after 1963. Following the 1979 Treaty of Friendship and Cooperation with the United Kingdom, [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei) resumed full responsibility for its international affairs and defence and became fully independent on 1 January 1984.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153]

In August 2019, Indonesian president [Joko Widodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joko_Widodo) announced a [plan to move the capital of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_of_Indonesia#Move_to_Nusantara) from [Jakarta](16275.md) to a newly established location in the [East Kalimantan](453651.md) province in Borneo.[^cite_note-154]

Brunei occupies only a small proportion of Borneo's present-day land area, but the [Sultanate of Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Brunei) played a substantially larger role in the island's pre-colonial history. For several centuries it was one of the principal maritime states of northern Borneo, and its political and commercial influence extended beyond the boundaries of modern Brunei. The survival of the Sultanate through the colonial period also resulted in Brunei becoming the only pre-colonial Bornean monarchy to emerge as a separate sovereign state following decolonisation.[^cite_note-155]

## Demographics



Visual map of different ethnic groups on Borneo.



Population Density of Borneo.

The [demonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonym) for Borneo is **Bornean**.[^cite_note-156]

Borneo had 23,053,723 inhabitants (in 2020 Censuses),[^cite_note-detik20-1][^cite_note-citypop-2] a population density of 30.8 inhabitants per square kilometre (80 inhabitants per square mile). Most of the population lives in coastal cities, although the hinterland has small towns and villages along the rivers.

Borneo is home to a number of different [Indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) and distinct languages and cultures. [*Dayak*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayak_people) is inconsistently used as a collective term for non-Muslim [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) Indigenous peoples from Borneo, encompassing the [Iban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iban_people) and [Bidayuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidayuh) in [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia), and the [Kayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayan_people_\(Borneo\)), [Kenyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyah_people) and [Ngaju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngaju_people) of [Kalimantan](165470.md).[^cite_note-\:0-157] Historically, many Dayaks lived in communal [longhouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longhouse), lacked [class structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_structure) and practiced [shifting cultivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shifting_cultivation) for subsistence.[^cite_note-\:0-157]

## Administration

The island of Borneo is divided administratively by three countries.

## Economy



Seria Oil Refinery, Brunei Darussalam

Borneo's economy depends mainly on agriculture, logging and mining, [oil](23195.md) and gas, and [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism).[^cite_note-Borneo_economy-168] Brunei's economy is highly dependent on the oil and gas production sector, and the country has become one of the largest oil producers in Southeast Asia. The Malaysian states of Sabah and Sarawak are both top exporters of [timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumber).[^cite_note-Borneo_economy-168] Sabah is also known as the agricultural producer of [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_rubber), [cacao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theobroma_cacao), and vegetables, and for its fisheries, while Sabah, Sarawak and Labuan export [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) (LNG) and petroleum. The Indonesian provinces of Kalimantan are mostly dependent on mining sectors despite also being involved in logging and oil and gas explorations.[^cite_note-Borneo_economy-168]
