The **2030 Winter Olympics** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Jeux olympiques d'hiver de 2030*), officially known as the **XXVI Olympic Winter Games**[^cite_note-1] ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *XXVI<sup>es</sup> Jeux Olympiques d'hiver*) and branded as **Alpes 2030**, is an upcoming international winter [multi-sport event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-sport_event) scheduled to take place from 1 to 17 February 2030 in the [French Alps](961317.md) region as main hosts.

As part of the new Olympic bid process, the Future Host Commission of the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) (IOC) nominated the French Alps as its preferred candidate on 29 November 2023. The French Alps' bid was approved during the [142nd IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/142nd_IOC_Session) in [Paris](22989.md), France ahead to the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics) on 24 July 2024. These will be the first Olympic Games to be officially hosted by a region rather than a host city.[^cite_note-2]

The events will take place in two French regions, mainly in the [Metropolis of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_of_Lyon) and in the departments of [Savoie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoie) and [Haute-Savoie](90605.md) in the [Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes](45093325.md) region, as well as in the [Hautes-Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hautes-Alpes) department in the [Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provence-Alpes-C%C3%B4te_d'Azur) region. Speed skating is scheduled to be held in [Heerenveen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heerenveen), Netherlands. These will be the fourth Winter Olympics, and the seventh Olympic Games overall, to be hosted by France.

## Bidding process

Further information: [Bids for the 2030 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bids_for_the_2030_Winter_Olympics)

Under the new bidding process established under Olympic Agenda 2020, the IOC's Future Host Commission (FHC) for the Winter Olympic Games engages in ongoing, non-committal "continuous dialogue" with parties that are interested in hosting a future edition. The Commission then recommends preferred candidates to be invited to "targeted dialogue" with the IOC Executive Board to formalise their bid, after which it is presented to the IOC's delegates for final approval.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]



View of [Mont Blanc](44768.md) from [Les Arcs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Arcs) ski resort



The [Albertville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albertville) pylon, a remnant of the [1992 Olympic Winter Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Olympic_Winter_Games), where the Olympic flag arrived after the [2026 Winter Olympics](38416091.md).

During the 135th IOC Session in January 2020, FHC chair [Octavian Morariu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octavian_Morariu) identified that [Barcelona](4443.md)—[Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaragoza), [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City) (which hosted in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Winter_Olympics), but was understood to prefer to host the [2034](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2034_Winter_Olympics) so that it would not take place immediately after the [2028 Summer Olympics](12695048.md) in Los Angeles), and [Sapporo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapporo) (which hosted in [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Winter_Olympics)) had entered into continuous dialogue regarding hosting a future Winter Olympics in 2030 or later.[^cite_note-6] Spain dropped out in 2022 amid disputes between the autonomous [Catalonian](6822.md) and [Aragonian](39443.md) governments.[^cite_note-spain-withdrawn-7] In March 2023, Switzerland revealed that it had entered into continuous dialogue.[^cite_note-8] In July 2023, the [French National Olympic and Sports Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Olympic_and_Sports_Committee) announced its intent to enter into continuous dialogue with the FHC for a bid focusing on the French Alps.[^cite_note-9] Sweden also proposed a [Stockholm](26741.md)–[Åre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%85re) bid, after the cities lost the 2026 Games to Milan–Cortina.[^cite_note-10]

On 29 November 2023, per the recommendation of the Future Host Commission, the IOC Executive Board invited the [French National Olympic and Sports Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Olympic_and_Sports_Committee) to targeted dialogue, proposing the French Alps as preferred host of the 2030 Winter Olympics.[^cite_note-11] Switzerland would enter into "privileged dialogue" for potentially hosting in 2038.[^cite_note-12] The French Alps were conditionally ratified as hosts during the [142nd IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/142nd_IOC_Session) on 24 July 2024 in Paris, France;[^cite_note-\:2-13][^cite_note-14] as per the new bid process, the vote was in the form of a [referendum](51582.md) to the 95 IOC delegates.[^cite_note-\:2-13][^cite_note-\:1-15][^cite_note-16]

The decision to award the Games was made under the condition that financial guarantees from the French government and local authorities be secured by October 2024; usually, these are secured as part of the bid process, but this was hindered by the [then-ongoing French political crisis](77741666.md) following the [2024 French legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_legislative_election).[^cite_note-\:3-17] In October 2024, prime minister [Michel Barnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Barnier) sent a letter to IOC president [Thomas Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Bach) to confirm the French government's financial support.[^cite_note-\:3-17]

### Handover ceremony

During the [2026 Winter Olympics closing ceremony](76159922.md) in [Verona](263991.md), Italy, the French Alps were introduced as the next Winter Games host region, with the raising of the flag of France and a special performance of "[La Marseillaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Marseillaise)" in the arena.[^cite_note-18]

## Development and preparations

### Venues

The venues of the Games will be split between clusters in [Lyon](8638634.md), [Briançon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian%C3%A7on), [Haute-Savoie](90605.md), and [Savoie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoie). Five [Olympic villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_village) are slated to be constructed for the Games, with one each in Lyon (in the suburb of [Oullins-Pierre-Bénite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oullins-Pierre-B%C3%A9nite)[^cite_note-19]), Briançon, and Haute-Savoie, and two in Savoie (locations to be determined).



Map of the 2030 Winter Games venues in the French Alps



Lyon will host indoor ice events during the Games.

In March 2026, it was reported that president of the regional council of Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur, [Renaud Muselier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaud_Muselier) had threatened to withdraw Nice, have their events held in Lyon instead if newly elected mayor of Nice, [Éric Ciotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ric_Ciotti) does not accept to the original plans. Renaud had considered [Parc Olympique Lyonnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_Olympique_Lyonnais) to host the opening and closing ceremonies, the neighbouring [LDLC Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LDLC_Arena) hosting ice hockey, and [Eurexpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurexpo) hosting the rest of the ice sports.[^cite_note-20] On 1 April, the recently reelected mayor of Lyon, [Grégory Doucet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%A9gory_Doucet), would announce that his city was ready to host ice sports.[^cite_note-21] One day later, the president of the regional council of Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, [Fabrice Pannekoucke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrice_Pannekoucke), denounced Grégory's "cuckoo approach".[^cite_note-22]

On 28 April 2026, the organising committee announced that after exploring the possibility of having ice hockey held on temporary rinks at [Allianz Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allianz_Riviera) (as doing such would require its tennant, [OGC Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OGC_Nice), to vacate for up to nine months), as well as [Stade Charles-Ehrmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Charles-Ehrmann) and [Stade Marcel-Volot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Marcel-Volot?action=edit\&redlink=1), and determining them to be too costly or logistically challenging to implement, they would move the matches to existing venues outside of Nice.[^cite_note-23] A bidding phase was launched two days later; Lyon and [Paris](22989.md) expressed interest prior, while [Albertville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albertville), [Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gap,_Hautes-Alpes), [Grenoble](50972.md) and [Marseille](40888948.md) were invited to bid.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] During a meeting in May 2026, the IOC warned the organisers that they would step in and remove ice hockey from Nice if the men's and women's events were to be geographically dispersed, in the consideration of minimising travel and carbon emissions.[^cite_note-26] This reprimand occurred after the [International Ice Hockey Federation](16760449.md) complained about the distance between venues.[^cite_note-27]

On 29 May 2026, the organising committee announced that Lyon was now "the best solution" to host ice events after Nice mayor Eric Ciotti rejecting the conversion of Allianz Riviera for ice hockey, and pressure from the IOC to adopt a more compact venue cluster.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] Meanwhile in June 2026, a tentative Lyon venue plan was announced, with LDLC Arena and Eurexpo listed for ice hockey, [Halle Tony Garnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_Tony_Garnier) for figure skating and short-track speed skating, and [Palais des Sports de Gerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_des_Sports_de_Gerland) for curling. The final venue plan will be announced later in 2026.[^cite_note-31] On 22 June 2026, the IOC Executive Board approved the first set of changes to the venue masterplan. The board also approved hosting long-track speed skating at Thialf. On 16 September, the final venue masterplan was approved. In Lyon Metropolis, Halle Tony Garnier would now host preliminary ice hockey games, and figure skating and short-track speed skating would be moved to Eurexpo. In addition, the events for alpine skiing were allocated.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

#### Ceremonies venue

A venue for the ceremonies has currently not yet been decided, but organising committee head [Edgar Grospiron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Grospiron) suggested in an interview with *[Le Monde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Monde)* that the ceremonies may be held in the [Metropolis of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_of_Lyon); the organising committee's headquarters are based at [Parc Olympique Lyonnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_Olympique_Lyonnais) in [Décines-Charpieu](5146934.md).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] The President of the [Regional Council of Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Council_of_Provence-Alpes-C%C3%B4te_d'Azur), [Renaud Muselier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaud_Muselier), had also shared the idea of hosting the closing ceremony at a part of the [Lac de Serre-Ponçon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lac_de_Serre-Pon%C3%A7on), but this has not been looked into by the organising committee.[^cite_note-37]

Towards the end of June 2026, the Mayor of Lyon, [Grégory Doucet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%A9gory_Doucet), suggested having an open-air opening ceremony within the city, similar to [what was done in Paris for the 2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics_opening_ceremony).[^cite_note-38]The president of the regional council of Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur, [Renaud Muselier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaud_Muselier), also suggeted having the closing ceremony held at the [Stade Vélodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_V%C3%A9lodrome) in [Marseille](40888948.md).[^cite_note-39]

#### Speed skating venue

At that time, the only sport that had not yet have a confirmed venue is long-track speed skating; in April 2025, Grospiron told *[Le Figaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Figaro)* that it would be too expensive to build a new venue for speed skating, and explained that the organising committee was aiming to "reinvent the Games".[^cite_note-40] The committee has explored various options, including renovating existing facilities in [Albertville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albertville) or [Grenoble](50972.md), or hosting the event outside of the country at either [Oval Lingotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Lingotto) in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), Italy (which previously hosted long-track speed skating at the [2006 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Winter_Olympics), and is the closest existing venue outside of the host regions),[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-NOS_May2024-42] or [Thialf](2447040.md) in [Heerenveen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heerenveen), Netherlands.[^cite_note-NOS_April2025-43][^cite_note-NOS_May2024-42][^cite_note-OF-44] On 11 May 2026, the organising committee decided to pursue exclusive talks with Heerenveen about hosting the speed skating events.[^cite_note-45] On 29 June, Thialf was confirmed as the speed skating venue, meaning that speed skating will take place outside of France.[^cite_note-46]

### Sports

The Games will feature a record 126 medal events in 8 sports comprising 17 disciplines, including 56 events for women, 55 events for men, and 15 mixed events.&#x20;

At the [142nd IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/142nd_IOC_Session) in July 2024, the IOC's delegates ratified the core Winter Olympic programme of biathlon, bobsleigh, curling, ice hockey, luge, skating, and skiing.[^cite_note-50] On 9 June 2026, the organising committee proposed the inclusion of [ski mountaineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_mountaineering_at_the_Winter_Olympics) as an optional event, returning from its debut in 2026.[^cite_note-51] The proposal was ratified at the 146th IOC Session on 25 June 2026; individual events will be added alongside the existing sprint and team relay events contested in 2026.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

The full event programme was announced on 7 July 2026; several new events and disciplines will be added, including [freeride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeriding_\(winter_sport\)) freestyle skiing and snowboard, [synchronized skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synchronized_skating), single mixed relay in biathlon, mixed team ski cross in freestyle skiing, women's super team in ski jumping, mixed team parallel giant slalom in snowboard, and men's and women's team sprint in speed skating. After having been featured in every Winter Olympics since [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Winter_Olympics). [Nordic combined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_combined) was dropped from the Winter Olympics programme due to declining interest.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

## Marketing

### Emblem

The emblem of the Games was unveiled on 18 June 2026 during an event at Briançon.[^cite_note-75] The emblem is a stylised mountain peak with a blue, purple, and red gradient, formed by rays of light; it is stated to represent the "promise of clarity, warmth and renewal" and the "convergence" of nations at its summit. The Paralympic emblem uses a similar design, but with the mountain formed in [negative space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_space) by lights shining behind it. Alongside the emblem, the branding of the Games was also shortened from "French Alps 2030"/"Alpes Françaises 2030" to simply "Alpes 2030".[^cite_note-76] The emblems were designed by Paris based design agency Saint-Lazare.[^cite_note-77] The project is called *Under the light of the mountains, we can inspire the world* [^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

## Concerns and controversies

### Impacts of climate change

The impacts of [climate change](5042951.md) have become a central focus of the International Olympic Committee in planning the Winter Olympics. According to the IOC, the number of [NOCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Olympic_Committee) capable of hosting the Winter Olympics—which require access to snow competition venues with adequate temperature and snowfall—will decline to "practically just 10-12" by mid-century.[^cite_note-131] As a result of these challenges, the decision on the 2030 Winter Olympics host city was delayed until 24 July 2024 to allow the IOC more time to carefully plan the future of the Winter Olympics.[^cite_note-\:0-132][^cite_note-133]

### Organisational issues

The president of the Games' organising committee, former Olympian [Edgar Grospiron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Grospiron), was appointed in 2025. The organising committee has been plagued with infighting, with the Games' director general, chief operating officer, communications director, and chief of the remuneration committee resigning between 2025 and 2026.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] In addition, the cost of the event has been a source of controversy, especially in amidst the sharp increase in the amount of French public debt.[^cite_note-136] The Games' budget is an estimated US\$4.04 billion, an increase from the original preliminary estimate of US\$2.3 billion.[^cite_note-137] The proposed Nice ice arena has faced criticism due to rising costs and budgetary concerns in the lead-up to the [2026 Nice municipal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Nice_municipal_election); it is slated to cost nearly €138 million.[^cite_note-\:5-138]

The election of [Éric Ciotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ric_Ciotti) as mayor of Nice in 2026, and his subsequent opposition to the conversion of the Allianz Riviera stadium to become an ice hockey rink for the 2030 Winter Olympics, caused the ice events to be moved to [Lyon](8638634.md).[^cite_note-139]
