The **prime minister of Kenya** was a post in the Kenyan government. The first prime minister of Kenya was [Jomo Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomo_Kenyatta), who became prime minister in 1963. In 1964, when Kenya became a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic), the post of prime minister was abolished and Jomo Kenyatta became [president](392265.md). Following a [power-sharing agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Kenya_riots) in February 2008, the role was recreated in April 2008 and held by [Raila Odinga](2917410.md). The position was again abolished by the [2010 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Kenya) after the [2013 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Kenyan_general_election).

The [proposed 2021 constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Kenyan_constitutional_referendum_attempt) would have decided whether to constitutionally reestablish the office. The process of the proposed amendments to the constitution, was declared unconstitutional by the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Kenya) of Kenya On 31 March 2022. The court also barred the [Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Electoral_and_Boundaries_Commission) from proceeding with the referendum.[^cite_note-1]

A similar office, that of [Prime Cabinet Secretary](72147772.md), was established by Executive Order No. 1 of 2022 on 27 September 2022.

## History

On 1 June 1963, the [Dominion of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Kenya) had her first prime minister.[^cite_note-2] The head of state remained [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II) who was represented through a governor-general in Kenya.[^cite_note-\:63-3] The governor-general could then select a prime minister from whichever political party had the majority in the House of Representatives; the prime minister would be head of government; [Jomo Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomo_Kenyatta) was elected as the first prime minister of Kenya on 27 May 1963.[^cite_note-4] The prime minister office was only effective up to 12 December 1964, before Kenya became a republic; Kenyatta became president.

Following the [2007–2008 Kenyan crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%932008_Kenyan_crisis), H.E. the late President [Mwai Kibaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwai_Kibaki) and Hon. [Raila Odinga](2917410.md) reached an agreement to form a coalition government on 28 February 2008.[^cite_note-Key-5] Kibaki was to remain the head of state, while Odinga would have a new role of prime minister. Both would appoint half the cabinet. Both leading coalitions produced a deputy prime minister: [Musalia Mudavadi](2777215.md) for [ODM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Democratic_Movement), and [Uhuru Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhuru_Kenyatta) for [PNU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_National_Unity_\(Kenya\)).[^cite_note-6] The prime minister office was reestablished through the [National Accord and Reconciliation Act 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Accord_and_Reconciliation_Act_2008).[^cite_note-7]

The post was abolished in 2013 after the [Constitution of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Kenya) was changed, which did not provide for the office of prime minister, making Kenya a purely presidential system.[^cite_note-8]

Efforts have been initiated to reintroduce the office through constitutional amendment through the [Building Bridges Initiative constitutional referendum attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Kenyan_constitutional_referendum_attempt).[^cite_note-9] On 31 March 2022, the [Supreme Court of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Kenya) upheld the rulings of the lower courts, ruling that, "The Constitution Amendment Bill of 2020 is unconstitutional," because President Kenyatta \[Uhuru] initiated the amendments through his creation of the Presidential Taskforce on Building Bridges to Unity Advisory, and vocal endorsement of the legislation crafted based on their findings.[^cite_note-\:2-10][^cite_note-citizen.digital-11]
