# Astronaut

For other uses, see [Astronaut (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut_\(disambiguation\)).



[NASA](18426568.md) astronaut [Bruce McCandless II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_McCandless_II) using a [Manned Maneuvering Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manned_Maneuvering_Unit) outside [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle) [*Challenger*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger) on shuttle mission [STS-41-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-41-B) in 1984.

An **astronaut** (from the [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) ἄστρον (*astron*), meaning 'star', and ναύτης (*nautes*), meaning 'sailor') is a person trained, equipped, and deployed by a [human spaceflight program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_human_spaceflight_programs) to serve as a commander or crew member of a [spacecraft](37910.md). Although generally reserved for professional space travelers, the term is sometimes applied to anyone who travels into space, including scientists, politicians, journalists, and [space tourists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_tourists).[^cite_note-fact-1][^cite_note-utah-2] In the United States, it is a designated term used by three agencies: [NASA](18426568.md), the [FAA](11186.md), and the military.[^cite_note-nbc-3] The term is also used for people who are trained to fly in a spacecraft[^cite_note-collins-4][^cite_note-cambridge-5] after passing certain training courses,[^cite_note-esa-6] regardless of their experience of space travel.[^cite_note-collins-4][^cite_note-ieee-7][^cite_note-keane-8]

"Astronaut" technically applies to all human space travelers regardless of nationality. However, astronauts fielded by [Russia](25391.md) or the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) are typically known instead as **cosmonauts** (from the Russian "kosmos" (космос), meaning "space", also borrowed from Greek κόσμος).[^cite_note-vocabulary-9] Comparatively recent successes of the [China Manned Space Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Manned_Space_Program) have led to the rise of the informal term **taikonaut** (from the [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese) "tàikōng" (太空), meaning "space"); Chinese official media refers to [its astronauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_astronauts) and their foreign counterparts as *hángtiānyuán* (航天员, meaning "celestial navigator" or literally "[heaven-sailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight) staff"). The [Canadian Space Agency](328618.md), [European Space Agency](10363.md), and [JAXA](716184.md) refer to their crew as *astronauts* regardless of launch vehicle; historically other nationalities launched by the Soviet [Interkosmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interkosmos) adopted the *cosmonaut* term.

As of April 2026, 781 humans have flown in space, many of these to [space stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_stations).[^cite_note-\:2-10] Over 290 individuals have visited the [International Space Station](15043.md);[^cite_note-11] 105 individuals visited the *[Mir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir)* station.[^cite_note-12] Until 2002, astronauts were sponsored and trained exclusively by governments, either by the military or by civilian space agencies. With the suborbital flight of the privately funded [SpaceShipOne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceShipOne) in 2004, a new category of astronaut was created: the [commercial astronaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_astronaut).

## Definition



[Alan Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Shepard) aboard [*Freedom 7*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury-Redstone_3) (1961)

The word "astronaut" is sometimes used in a strictly defined sense, such as in the United States, where it is a designated term used by three agencies: NASA, the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md), and the military, with slightly different criteria for each. NASA and the military only use the term for their own employees who meet specific criteria.[^cite_note-nbc-3] In Europe, the [European Astronaut Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Astronaut_Corps) calls graduates of their training program "ESA astronauts", being "active ESA staff having successfully completed Basic Astronaut training recognised by ESA or who have participated in a mission to space".[^cite_note-esa-6] People who have completed this training are generally described as astronauts in the press,[^cite_note-ieee-7][^cite_note-keane-8] and the [Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collins_English_Dictionary) and [Cambridge English Dictionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_English_Dictionary) define "astronaut" as people who are trained to fly in a spacecraft.[^cite_note-collins-4][^cite_note-cambridge-5]

In addition, the criteria for what constitutes [human spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight) vary, with some focus on the point where the atmosphere becomes so thin that [centrifugal force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrifugal_force), rather than [aerodynamic force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodynamic_force), carries a significant portion of the weight of the flight object. The [Fédération Aéronautique Internationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9d%C3%A9ration_A%C3%A9ronautique_Internationale) (FAI) Sporting Code for astronautics recognizes only flights that exceed the [Kármán line](738149.md), at an altitude of 100 kilometers (62 mi).[^cite_note-fai-13] In the United States, professional, military, and commercial astronauts who travel above an altitude of 80 kilometres (50 mi)[^cite_note-14] are awarded [astronaut wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut_Badge).

As of 22 May 2026, 781 people from [55 countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_space_travel_by_nationality) have reached 100 km (62 mi) or more in altitude.[^cite_note-\:2-10] Of these, [28 people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_who_flew_to_the_Moon), all but Canadian [Jeremy Hansen](22811438.md) from the United States, have traveled beyond [low Earth orbit](47568.md), with three of them—[Jim Lovell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Lovell), [John Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Watts_Young) and [Eugene Cernan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Cernan)—doing so twice.[^cite_note-hundred-15] There have been ten crewed missions beyond low Earth orbit: [Apollo 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_8) and [Apollo 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_10) orbited the [Moon](19331.md) without landing, [Apollos 11–12 and 14–17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_landing#Human_Moon_landings_\(1969%E2%80%931972\)) landed on the Moon, and [Apollo 13](1770.md) and [Artemis II](41391511.md) looped around the Moon.

As of 8 November 2023, 676 humans had flown to space under the U.S. definition[^cite_note-16]. Of eight [X-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-15) pilots who exceeded 50 miles (80 km) in altitude, only one, [Joseph A. Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_A._Walker), exceeded 100 kilometers (about 62.1 miles) and he did it two times, becoming the first person in space twice.[^cite_note-stats1-17] Space travelers have spent over 41,790 [man-days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_hour) (114.5-man-years) in space, including over 100 astronaut-days of [spacewalks](9792.md).[^cite_note-stats-18][^cite_note-void-19] As of 2026, the man with the longest cumulative time in space is [Oleg Kononenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Kononenko), who has spent over 1100 days in space.[^cite_note-20] [Peggy A. Whitson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peggy_Whitson) holds the record for the most time in space by a woman, at 695 days.[^cite_note-paw-21]

### Schirra definition

&#x20;The veteran American astronaut, [Wally Schirra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wally_Schirra) (1923–2007), had firm views on the criteria that should apply for membership of the [Society of Experimental Test Pilots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Experimental_Test_Pilots) (SETP) – and on the definition of an astronaut. He devoted a whole chapter (*My Ultimate Peer Group*) of his 1988 autobiography, *Schirra's Space*, to a discussion of the subject.[^cite_note-Schirra-22] He argued that aircraft personnel not piloting an aircraft are not aviators, and applied the strict criterion that anyone in space not in control of the flight of the spacecraft is not an astronaut:[^cite_note-Schirra-22]

> My world as a test pilot is the [fighter](10929.md) world. You don't see [bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomber) in my inventory. \[...] Before the [shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle)—in [Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Mercury), [Gemini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Gemini) and [Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program)—astronauts were [aviators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_pilot). \[...] But then NASA began putting others on board, people they called [mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_specialist) and [payload specialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payload_specialist). Now I think of them as similar to members of a bomber crew—a [bombardier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_\(aircrew\)), a [navigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigator). The specialists have important duties to perform, but they should not be confused with [pilots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_pilot). Nor should people who don't fly the spacecraft be called astronauts.

## Terminology

See also: [Astronaut ranks and positions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut_ranks_and_positions)

In 1959, when both the United States and Soviet Union were planning, but had yet to launch humans into space, NASA Administrator [T. Keith Glennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._Keith_Glennan) and his Deputy Administrator, [Hugh Dryden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Latimer_Dryden), discussed whether spacecraft crew members should be called *astronauts* or *cosmonauts*. Dryden preferred "cosmonaut", on the grounds that flights would occur in and to the broader *[cosmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmos)*, while the "astro" prefix suggested flight specifically to the [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star).[^cite_note-23] Most NASA [Space Task Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Task_Group) members preferred "astronaut", which survived by common usage as the preferred American term.[^cite_note-24] When the Soviet Union launched the first man into space, [Yuri Gagarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Gagarin) in 1961, they chose a term – космонавт – which [anglicizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicization) to "cosmonaut".[^cite_note-\:0-25][^cite_note-\:1-26]

### Astronaut



The first sixteen NASA astronauts to be selected, February 1963. Back row: [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_H._White), [McDivitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_McDivitt), [Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Young_\(astronaut\)), [See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_See), [Conrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Conrad), [Borman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Borman), [Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Armstrong), [Stafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_P._Stafford), [Lovell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Lovell). Front row: [Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._Gordon_Cooper), [Grissom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Grissom), [Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Carpenter), [Schirra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wally_Schirra), [Glenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Glenn), [Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Shepard), [Slayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deke_Slayton).

A professional space traveler is called an *astronaut*.[^cite_note-27] The first known use of the term "astronaut" in the modern sense was by [Neil R. Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_R._Jones) in his 1930 short story "The Death's Head Meteor". The word itself had been known earlier; for example, in [Percy Greg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Greg)'s 1880 book *[Across the Zodiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Across_the_Zodiac)*, "astronaut" referred to a spacecraft. In *[Les Navigateurs de l'infini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Navigateurs_de_l'infini)* (1925) by [J.-H. Rosny aîné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.-H._Rosny_a%C3%AEn%C3%A9), the word *astronautique* ([astronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronautics)) was used. The word may have been inspired by "aeronaut", an older term for an air traveler first applied in 1784 to [balloonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balloon_\(aircraft\)). An early use of "astronaut" in a non-fiction publication is [Eric Frank Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Frank_Russell)'s poem "The Astronaut", appearing in the November 1934 *Bulletin of the [British Interplanetary Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Interplanetary_Society)*.[^cite_note-28]

The first known formal use of the term [astronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronautics) in the scientific community was the establishment of the annual [International Astronautical Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronautical_Congress) in 1950, and the subsequent founding of the [International Astronautical Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronautical_Federation) the following year.[^cite_note-IAFpage-29]

[NASA](18426568.md) applies the term astronaut to any crew member aboard NASA spacecraft bound for Earth orbit or beyond. NASA also uses the term as a title for those selected to join its [Astronaut Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Astronaut_Corps).[^cite_note-biopage-30] The [European Space Agency](10363.md) similarly uses the term astronaut for members of its [Astronaut Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Astronaut_Corps).[^cite_note-ESApage-31]

### Cosmonaut

The first eleven Soviet cosmonauts to fly, July 1965. Back row, left to right: [Leonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Leonov), [Titov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gherman_Titov), [Bykovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valery_Bykovsky), [Yegorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yegorov), [Popovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Popovich); front row: [Komarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Komarov), [Gagarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Gagarin), [Tereshkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentina_Tereshkova), [Nikolayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriyan_Nikolayev), [Feoktistov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Feoktistov), [Belyayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Belyayev).

An astronaut employed by the [Russian Federal Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federal_Space_Agency) (or its predecessor, the [Soviet space program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_space_program)) is called a *cosmonaut*,[^cite_note-biopage-30] an [Anglicization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicization) of *kosmonavt* (Russian: космонавт Russian pronunciation: [\[kəsmɐˈnaft\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian)).[^cite_note-space_traveller-32] Other countries of the former [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) use variations of the Russian kosmonavt, such as the [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *kosmonauta* ([Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) also used *astronauta* as a synonym).[^cite_note-33]

Coinage of the term космонавт has been credited to Soviet aeronautics (or "[cosmonautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmonautics)") pioneer [Mikhail Tikhonravov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Tikhonravov) (1900–1974).[^cite_note-\:0-25][^cite_note-\:1-26] The first cosmonaut was [Soviet Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Force) pilot [Yuri Gagarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Gagarin), the first person in space. He was part of the first six Soviet citizens, with [German Titov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Titov), [Yevgeny Khrunov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevgeny_Khrunov), [Andriyan Nikolayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriyan_Nikolayev), [Pavel Popovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Popovich), and [Grigoriy Nelyubov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigoriy_Nelyubov), who were given the title of pilot-cosmonaut in January 1961.[^cite_note-34] [Valentina Tereshkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentina_Tereshkova) was the first female cosmonaut and the first and youngest [woman to have flown in space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_space) with a solo mission on the [Vostok 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostok_6) in 1963.[^cite_note-35] On 14 March 1995,[^cite_note-36] [Norman Thagard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Thagard) became the first American to ride to space on board a Russian launch vehicle, and thus became the first "American cosmonaut".[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

### Taikonaut

Main articles: [People's Liberation Army Astronaut Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Army_Astronaut_Corps) and [China Manned Space Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Manned_Space_Program)

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of Chinese astronauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_astronauts).

In Chinese, the term *Yǔ háng yuán* (宇航员, "cosmos navigating personnel") is used for astronauts and cosmonauts in general,[^cite_note-rus-39][^cite_note-rus2-40] while *hángtiān yuán* (航天员, "[Tian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tian)-navigating personnel") is used for Chinese astronauts. Here, *hángtiān* (航天 or [spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight)) is strictly[^cite_note-41] defined as the navigation of [outer space](177602.md) within the local [star system](240963.md), i.e. [Solar System](26903.md). The phrase *tàikōng rén* (太空人, "spaceman") is often used in Hong Kong and [Taiwan](25734.md).[^cite_note-42]

The term *taikonaut* is used by some English-language news media organizations for professional [space travelers from China](343674.md).[^cite_note-43] The word has featured in the [Longman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longman) and [Oxford English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary) dictionaries, and the term became more common in 2003 when China sent its first astronaut [Yang Liwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Liwei) into space aboard the *[Shenzhou 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_5)* spacecraft.[^cite_note-44] This is the term used by [Xinhua News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhua_News_Agency) in the English version of the Chinese *[People's Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Daily)* since the advent of the Chinese space program.[^cite_note-45] The origin of the term is unclear; as early as May 1998, Chiew Lee Yih (趙裡昱) from [Malaysia](3607937.md) used it in [newsgroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usenet_newsgroup).[^cite_note-chiew-46][^cite_note-chiew2-47]

### Other terms

Main article: [Spaceflight participant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight_participant)

With the rise of [space tourism](67896.md), [NASA](18426568.md) and the [Russian Federal Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federal_Space_Agency) agreed to use the term "[spaceflight participant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight_participant)" to distinguish those space travelers from professional astronauts on missions coordinated by those two agencies.



[Finnish American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_American) astronaut [Timothy Kopra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Kopra)

While no nation other than Russia (and previously the Soviet Union), the United States, and China have launched a crewed spacecraft, several other nations have sent people into space in cooperation with one of these countries, e.g. the Soviet-led [Interkosmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interkosmos) program. Inspired partly by these missions, other synonyms for astronaut have entered occasional English usage. For example, the term *spationaut* (French: *spationaute*) is sometimes used to describe French space travelers, from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) word *spatium* for "space". The [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language) term *angkasawan* (deriving from *[angkasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akasha)* meaning 'space') was used to describe participants in the [Angkasawan program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angkasawan_program) (note its similarity with the [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language) term *antariksawan*). Plans of the [Indian Space Research Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Space_Research_Organisation) to launch its crewed [Gaganyaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaganyaan) spacecraft have spurred at times public discussion if another term than *astronaut* should be used for the crew members, suggesting *vyomanaut* (from the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) word *vyoman* meaning 'sky' or 'space') or *gagannaut* (from the Sanskrit word *gagan* for 'sky').[^cite_note-Ananthaswamy_2010-48][^cite_note-Mukunth_2018-49] In [Finland](10577.md), the NASA astronaut [Timothy Kopra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Kopra), a [Finnish American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Americans), has sometimes been referred to as *sisunautti*, from the [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_language) word *[sisu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisu)*.[^cite_note-50] Across Germanic languages, the word for "astronaut" typically translates to "space traveler", as it does with German's *Raumfahrer*, Dutch's *ruimtevaarder*, Swedish's *rymdfarare*, and Norwegian's *romfarer*.

For its [2022 Astronaut Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_European_Space_Agency_Astronaut_Group), the European Space Agency envisioned recruiting an astronaut with a physical disability, a category they called "parastronauts", with the intention but not guarantee of spaceflight.[^cite_note-51] The categories of disability considered for the program were individuals with lower limb deficiency (either through amputation or congenital), leg length difference, or a short stature (less than 130 centimetres or 4 feet 3 inches).[^cite_note-52] On 23 November 2022, [John McFall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McFall_\(athlete\)) was selected to be the first ESA parastronaut;[^cite_note-53] he has rejected the use of the term.[^cite_note-54]

As of 2021 in the United States, astronaut status is conferred on a person depending on the authorizing agency:

- one who flies in a vehicle above 50 miles (80 km) for NASA or the military is considered an *astronaut* (with no qualifier)

- one who flies in a vehicle to the International Space Station in a mission coordinated by NASA and [Roscosmos](406573.md) is a *spaceflight participant*

- one who flies above 50 miles (80 km) in a non-NASA vehicle as a crewmember and demonstrates activities during flight that are essential to public safety, or contribute to human space flight safety, is considered a *commercial astronaut* by the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md)

  [^cite_note-55]

- one who flies to the International Space Station as part of a "privately funded, dedicated commercial spaceflight on a commercial launch vehicle dedicated to the mission ... to conduct approved commercial and marketing activities on the space station (or in a commercial segment attached to the station)" is considered a *private astronaut* by NASA

  [^cite_note-56]

  &#x20;(as of 2020, nobody has yet qualified for this status)

On July 20, 2021, the FAA issued an order redefining the eligibility criteria to be an astronaut in response to the private suborbital spaceflights of [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos) and [Richard Branson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Branson).[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] The new criteria states that one must have "\[d]emonstrated activities during flight that were essential to public safety, or contributed to human space flight safety" to qualify as an astronaut. This new definition excludes Bezos and Branson.

The first human in space was Soviet [Yuri Gagarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Gagarin), who was launched on 12 April 1961, aboard [Vostok 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostok_1) and orbited around the Earth for 108 minutes. The first woman in space was Soviet [Valentina Tereshkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentina_Tereshkova), who launched on 16 June 1963, aboard [Vostok 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostok_6) and orbited Earth for almost three days.

[Alan Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Shepard) became the first American and second person in space on 5 May 1961, on a 15-minute sub-orbital flight aboard *[Freedom 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury-Redstone_3)*. The first American to orbit the Earth was [John Glenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Glenn), aboard *[Friendship 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendship_7)* on 20 February 1962. The first American woman in space was [Sally Ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Ride), during [Space Shuttle *Challenger*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger)'s mission [STS-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-7), on 18 June 1983.[^cite_note-ride1-59] In 1992, [Mae Jemison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mae_Jemison) became the first African American woman to travel in space aboard [STS-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-47).

Cosmonaut [Alexei Leonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Leonov) was the first person to conduct an [extravehicular activity](9792.md) (EVA), (commonly called a "spacewalk"), on 18 March 1965, on the Soviet Union's Voskhod 2 mission. This was followed two and a half months later by astronaut [Ed White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_White_\(astronaut\)) who made the first American EVA on NASA's Gemini 4 mission.[^cite_note-60]

The first crewed mission to orbit the Moon, [Apollo 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_8), included American [William Anders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Anders) who was born in Hong Kong, making him the first Asian-born astronaut in 1968.

The Soviet Union, through its [Intercosmos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercosmos) program, allowed people from multiple other countries, mostly Soviet-allied but also including from France and [Austria](26964606.md), to participate in [Soyuz TM-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_TM-7) and [Soyuz TM-13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_TM-13), respectively. This made the [Czechoslovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) [Vladimír Remek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladim%C3%ADr_Remek) the first cosmonaut/astronaut from a country other than the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_space_program) or the [United States](18426568.md) to fly to space in 1978 on a [Soyuz-U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz-U) rocket.[^cite_note-enc-61]

On 23 July 1980, [Pham Tuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pham_Tuan) of [Vietnam](202354.md) became the first [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_people) in space when he flew aboard [Soyuz 37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_37).[^cite_note-tuan-62] Also in 1980, [Cuban](1288758.md) [Arnaldo Tamayo Méndez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnaldo_Tamayo_M%C3%A9ndez) became the first person of black African descent, as well as the first Hispanic astronaut. In 1983, [Guion Bluford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guion_Bluford) became the first African American to fly into space. In April 1985, the Taiwanese-American [Taylor Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Wang) became the first ethnic Chinese person in space.[^cite_note-taylorbio-63][^cite_note-taylor-64]

With the increase of seats on the Space Shuttle, the U.S. also began taking international astronauts. In 1983, [Ulf Merbold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulf_Merbold) of West Germany became the first non-US citizen to fly in a US spacecraft. In 1984, [Marc Garneau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Garneau) became the first of eight [Canadian astronauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_astronauts) to fly in space (through 2010).[^cite_note-65] The first person born in Africa to fly in space was [Patrick Baudry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Baudry) of France, in 1985.[^cite_note-mendez-66][^cite_note-baudry-67] In same NASA flight as the Frenchman was the Saudi Arabian [Prince Sultan Bin Salman Bin AbdulAziz Al-Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Salman_al-Saud), who became the first Muslim and Arab astronaut.[^cite_note-MC-68] In 1985, [Rodolfo Neri Vela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolfo_Neri_Vela) became the first Mexican-born person in space.[^cite_note-vela-69] In 1991, [Helen Sharman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Sharman) became the first Briton to fly in space.[^cite_note-bbcsharmon-70]

In 2001, American [Dennis Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Tito) became the first [space tourist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_tourist), after paying a fee for a trip aboard Russian spacecraft Soyuz. In 2002, another private tourist, the South African [Mark Shuttleworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Shuttleworth), became the first citizen of an African country to fly into space.[^cite_note-mark-71]

On 15 October 2003, [Yang Liwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Liwei) became China's first astronaut on its own spacecraft, the [Shenzhou 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_5).

### Age milestones

The youngest person to reach space is [Oliver Daemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Daemen), who was 18 years and 11 months old when he made a [suborbital](455295.md) spaceflight on [Blue Origin NS-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_NS-16).[^cite_note-72] Daemen, who was a commercial passenger aboard the [New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard), broke the record of Soviet [cosmonaut](664.md) [Gherman Titov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gherman_Titov), who was 25 years old when he flew [Vostok 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostok_2). Titov remains the youngest human to reach [orbit](568972.md); he rounded the planet 17 times. Titov was also the first person to suffer [space sickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_sickness) and the first person to sleep in space, twice.[^cite_note-age-73][^cite_note-titov-74]

The oldest person to reach space is [William Shatner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shatner), who was 90 years old when he made a suborbital spaceflight on [Blue Origin NS-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_NS-18).[^cite_note-75] The oldest person to reach orbit is [John Glenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Glenn), one of the [Mercury 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_7), who was 77 when he flew on [STS-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-95).[^cite_note-glenn-76]

### Duration and distance milestones

The longest time spent in space was by Russian [Valeri Polyakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeri_Polyakov), who spent 438 days there.[^cite_note-stats-18] As of 2006, the most spaceflights by an individual astronaut is seven, a record held by both [Jerry L. Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_L._Ross) and [Franklin Chang-Diaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_Chang-Diaz). The farthest distance from Earth an astronaut has traveled is 406,771 km (252,756 mi), when [Reid Wiseman](23437180.md), [Victor Glover](39700888.md), [Christina Koch](39700916.md), and [Jeremy Hansen](22811438.md) flew around the Moon during the [Artemis II](41391511.md) lunar flyby mission in 2026.[^cite_note-77] The previous record was 401,056 km (249,205 mi), when [Jim Lovell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Lovell), [Jack Swigert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Swigert), and [Fred Haise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Haise) went around the Moon during the [Apollo 13](1770.md) emergency in 1970.[^cite_note-stats-18]

### Civilian and non-government milestones

The first civilian in space was [Valentina Tereshkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentina_Tereshkova)[^cite_note-Valentina_Vladimirovna_TERESHKOVA-78] aboard [Vostok 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostok_6) (she also became the first woman in space on that mission). Tereshkova was only honorarily inducted into the USSR's Air Force, which did not accept female pilots at that time. A month later, [Joseph Albert Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Albert_Walker) became the first American civilian in space when his [X-15 Flight 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-15_Flight_90) crossed the 100 kilometers (54 nautical miles) line, qualifying him by the international definition of spaceflight.[^cite_note-Civilians_in_Space-79][^cite_note-Space.com_Joseph_A_Walker-80] Walker had joined the US Army Air Force but was not a member during his flight. The first people in space who had never been a member of any country's armed forces were both [Konstantin Feoktistov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Feoktistov) and [Boris Yegorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Yegorov) aboard [Voskhod 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voskhod_1).

The first non-governmental space traveler was [Byron K. Lichtenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byron_K._Lichtenberg), a researcher from the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](18879.md) who flew on [STS-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-9) in 1983.[^cite_note-lichten-81] In December 1990, [Toyohiro Akiyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyohiro_Akiyama) became the first paying space traveler and the first journalist in space for [Tokyo Broadcasting System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Broadcasting_System), a visit to [Mir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir) as part of an estimated \$12 million (USD) deal with a Japanese TV station, although at the time, the term used to refer to Akiyama was "Research Cosmonaut".[^cite_note-smith-82][^cite_note-bbc1-83][^cite_note-de-84] Akiyama suffered severe [space sickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_adaptation_syndrome) during his mission, which affected his productivity.[^cite_note-bbc1-83]

The first self-funded [space tourist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_tourist) was [Dennis Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Tito) on board the Russian spacecraft Soyuz TM-3 on 28 April 2001.

## Training



[Elliot See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliot_See) during water egress training with NASA (1965)

Main article: [Astronaut training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut_training)

See also: [Astronaut ranks and positions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut_ranks_and_positions)

The first NASA astronauts were selected for training in 1959.[^cite_note-comet-88] Early in the space program, military jet test piloting and engineering training were often cited as prerequisites for selection as an astronaut at NASA, although neither John Glenn nor Scott Carpenter (of the [Mercury Seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_Seven)) had any university degree, in engineering or any other discipline at the time of their selection. Selection was initially limited to military pilots.[^cite_note-training-89][^cite_note-nolen-90] The earliest astronauts for both the US and the USSR tended to be [jet fighter](10929.md) pilots, and were often test pilots.

Once selected, NASA astronauts go through twenty months of training in a variety of areas, including training for [extravehicular activity](9792.md) in a facility such as NASA's [Neutral Buoyancy Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_Buoyancy_Laboratory).[^cite_note-fact-1][^cite_note-training-89] Astronauts-in-training (astronaut candidates) may also experience short periods of [weightlessness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weightlessness) ([microgravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micro-g_environment)) in an aircraft called the "[Vomit Comet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vomit_Comet)," the nickname given to a pair of modified [KC-135s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-135) (retired in 2000 and 2004, respectively, and replaced in 2005 with a [C-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Douglas_C-9)) which perform [parabolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parabola) flights.[^cite_note-comet-88] Astronauts are also required to accumulate a number of flight hours in high-performance jet aircraft. This is mostly done in [T-38 jet aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-38_Talon) out of [Ellington Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellington_Field), due to its proximity to the [Johnson Space Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_Space_Center). Ellington Field is also where the [Shuttle Training Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuttle_Training_Aircraft) is maintained and developed, although most flights of the aircraft are conducted from [Edwards Air Force Base](107530.md).

Astronauts in training must learn how to control and fly the Space Shuttle; further, it is vital that they are familiar with the International Space Station so they know what they must do when they get there.[^cite_note-nasa-20040527-91]

### NASA candidacy requirements

Unless otherwise noted, the following data are incorporated from the Astronaut Requirements article by NASA

- The candidate must be a citizen of the United States.

- The candidate must complete a master's degree in a [STEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STEM) field, including engineering, [biological science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_science), [physical science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_science), [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) or mathematics.

- The candidate must have at least two years of related professional experience obtained after degree completion or at least 1,000 hours [pilot-in-command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot-in-command) time on [jet aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_aircraft).

- The candidate must be able to pass the NASA long-duration flight astronaut physical.

- The candidate must also have skills in leadership, teamwork and communications.

The master's degree requirement can also be met by:

- Two years of work toward a doctoral program in a related science, technology, engineering or math field.

- A completed [Doctor of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Medicine) or [Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Osteopathic_Medicine) degree.

- Completion of a nationally recognized test pilot school program.

[Mission Specialist Educators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educator_Astronaut_Project), or "Educator Astronauts", were first selected in 2004; as of 2007, there are three NASA Educator astronauts: [Joseph M. Acaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_M._Acaba), [Richard R. Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_R._Arnold), and [Dorothy Metcalf-Lindenburger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Metcalf-Lindenburger).[^cite_note-nexgen-93][^cite_note-ed-94] [Barbara Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Morgan), selected as back-up teacher to [Christa McAuliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christa_McAuliffe) in 1985, is considered to be the first Educator astronaut by the media, but she trained as a mission specialist.[^cite_note-morgan1-95] The Educator Astronaut program is a successor to the [Teacher in Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teacher_in_Space) program from the 1980s.[^cite_note-fly-96][^cite_note-nasa-97]

## Health risks of space travel

See also: [Effect of spaceflight on the human body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effect_of_spaceflight_on_the_human_body) and [Space medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_medicine)



[Gennady Padalka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gennady_Padalka) performing ultrasound on [Michael Fincke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Fincke) during ISS [Expedition 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_9)

Astronauts are susceptible to a variety of health risks including [decompression sickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decompression_sickness), [barotrauma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barotrauma), [immunodeficiencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunodeficiencies), loss of [bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone) and [muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle), loss of [eyesight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyesight), [orthostatic intolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthostatic_intolerance), [sleep disturbances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_disturbances), and [radiation](25856.md) injury.[^cite_note-NYT-20140127-98][^cite_note-Wired-20120723-99][^cite_note-Mader-2011-100][^cite_note-Puiu-20111109-101][^cite_note-CNN-20120109-102][^cite_note-Space-20120313-103][^cite_note-Radiology-20120313-104][^cite_note-105][^cite_note-WIRED-20140212-106][^cite_note-SKR-20171103-107] A variety of large scale medical studies are being conducted in space via the [National Space Biomedical Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Space_Biomedical_Research_Institute) (NSBRI) to address these issues. Prominent among these is the [Advanced Diagnostic Ultrasound in Microgravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Diagnostic_Ultrasound_in_Microgravity) Study in which astronauts (including former ISS commanders [Leroy Chiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leroy_Chiao) and [Gennady Padalka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gennady_Padalka)) perform [ultrasound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasound) scans under the guidance of remote experts to diagnose and potentially treat hundreds of medical conditions in space. This study's techniques are now being applied to cover professional and Olympic [sports injuries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_injuries) as well as ultrasound performed by non-expert operators in medical and high school students. It is anticipated that remote guided ultrasound will have application on Earth in emergency and [rural care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_health) situations, where access to a trained physician is often rare.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]

A 2006 Space Shuttle experiment found that *[Salmonella typhimurium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmonella_typhimurium)*, a bacterium that can cause [food poisoning](531611.md), became more virulent when cultivated in space.[^cite_note-ASU-20070923-111] More recently, in 2017, bacteria were found to be more resistant to [antibiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotic) and to thrive in the near-weightlessness of space.[^cite_note-GZM-20170913-112] [Microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism) have been observed to survive the [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum) of outer space.[^cite_note-Dose-113][^cite_note-Horneck-114]

On 31 December 2012, a [NASA](18426568.md)-supported study reported that [human spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight) may harm the brain and accelerate the onset of [Alzheimer's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alzheimer's_disease).[^cite_note-PLOS-20121231-115][^cite_note-spaceref-116][^cite_note-NasaWatch-20130103-117]

In October 2015, the [NASA Office of Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_Office_of_Inspector_General) issued a [health hazards report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effect_of_spaceflight_on_the_human_body) related to [space exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_space_exploration), including a [human mission to Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_mission_to_Mars).[^cite_note-AP-20151029-118][^cite_note-NASA-20151029oig-119]

Over the last decade, flight surgeons and scientists at NASA have seen a pattern of vision problems in astronauts on long-duration space missions. The syndrome, known as [visual impairment intracranial pressure (VIIP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_impairment_due_to_intracranial_pressure), has been reported in nearly two-thirds of space explorers after long periods spent aboard the International Space Station (ISS).[^cite_note-120]

On 2 November 2017, scientists reported that significant changes in the position and structure of the brain have been found in astronauts who have taken [trips in space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight), based on [MRI studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging). Astronauts who took longer space trips were associated with greater brain changes.[^cite_note-NEJM-20171102-121][^cite_note-QTZ-20171103-122]

Being in space can be physiologically [deconditioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deconditioning) on the body. It can affect the [otolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otolith) organs and adaptive capabilities of the [central nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_nervous_system). [Zero gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_gravity) and [cosmic rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_rays) can cause many implications for astronauts.[^cite_note-123]

In October 2018, [NASA](18426568.md)-funded researchers found that lengthy journeys into [outer space](177602.md), including travel to the [planet Mars](14640471.md), may substantially damage the [gastrointestinal tissues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrointestinal_tract) of astronauts. The studies support earlier work that found such journeys could significantly damage the brains of astronauts, and [age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ageing) them prematurely.[^cite_note-TI-20181002-124]

Researchers in 2018 reported, after detecting the presence on the [International Space Station](15043.md) (ISS) of five *[Enterobacter bugandensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterobacter)* bacterial strains, none [pathogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathogen) to humans, that [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism) on ISS should be carefully monitored to continue assuring a medically healthy environment for astronauts.[^cite_note-EA-20181122-125][^cite_note-BMC-20181123-126]

A study by Russian scientists published in April 2019 stated that astronauts facing space [radiation](25856.md) could face temporary hindrance of their [memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory) centers. While this does not affect their intellectual capabilities, it temporarily hinders formation of new cells in brain's memory centers. The study conducted by Moscow Institute of Physics and Technology (MIPT) concluded this after they observed that mice exposed to neutron and gamma radiation did not impact the rodents' intellectual capabilities.[^cite_note-127]

A 2020 [study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_trial) conducted on the brains of eight male Russian cosmonauts after they returned from long stays aboard the [International Space Station](15043.md) showed that long-duration [spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight) causes many [physiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiological) adaptions, including macro- and [microstructural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microstructure) changes. While scientists still know little about the effects of [spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight) on brain structure, this study showed that space travel can lead to new [motor skills (dexterity)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_motor_skill), but also slightly weaker [vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_perception), both of which could possibly be long lasting. It was the first study to provide clear evidence of [sensorimotor neuroplasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroplasticity), which is the brain's ability to change through growth and reorganization.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

## Food and drink

Main article: [Space food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_food)



Astronauts making and eating hamburgers on board the ISS, 2002

[^cite_note-130]

An astronaut on the International Space Station requires about 830 [g](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram) (29 [oz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ounce)) mass of food per meal each day (inclusive of about 120 g or 4.2 oz packaging mass per meal).

Space Shuttle astronauts worked with nutritionists to select menus that appealed to their individual tastes. Five months before flight, menus were selected and analyzed for nutritional content by the shuttle dietician. Foods are tested to see how they will react in a reduced gravity environment. Caloric requirements are determined using a basal energy expenditure (BEE) formula. On Earth, the average American uses about 35 US gallons (130 L) of water every day. On board the ISS astronauts limit water use to only about three US gallons (11 L) per day.[^cite_note-131]

## Insignia



NASA Astronaut lapel pin

In Russia, cosmonauts are awarded [Pilot-Cosmonaut of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot-Cosmonaut_of_the_Russian_Federation) upon completion of their missions, often accompanied with the award of [Hero of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_the_Russian_Federation). This follows the practice established in the USSR where cosmonauts were usually awarded the title [Hero of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_the_Soviet_Union).

At NASA, those who complete astronaut candidate training receive a silver [lapel pin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut_Badge#NASA_Astronaut_Pins). Once they have flown in space, they receive a gold pin. U.S. astronauts who also have active-duty military status receive a special qualification badge, known as the [Astronaut Badge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut_Badge), after participation on a spaceflight. The [United States Air Force](32090.md) also presents an Astronaut Badge to its pilots who exceed 50 miles (80 km) in altitude.

## Deaths

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of spaceflight-related accidents and incidents § Astronaut fatalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_spaceflight-related_accidents_and_incidents#Astronaut_fatalities).



Space Mirror Memorial

As of 2020, eighteen astronauts (fourteen men and four women) have died during four space flights. By nationality, thirteen were American, four were Russian ([Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)), and one was Israeli.

As of 2020, eleven people (all men) have died training for spaceflight: eight Americans and three Russians. Six of these were in crashes of training jet aircraft, one drowned during water recovery training, and four were due to fires in pure oxygen environments.

Astronaut [David Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Scott) left a memorial consisting of a statuette titled *[Fallen Astronaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallen_Astronaut)* on the surface of the Moon during his 1971 [Apollo 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_15) mission, along with a list of the names of eight of the astronauts and six cosmonauts known at the time to have died in service.[^cite_note-132]

The [Space Mirror Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Mirror_Memorial), which stands on the grounds of the [Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center_Visitor_Complex), is maintained by the Astronauts Memorial Foundation and commemorates the lives of the men and women who have died during spaceflight and during training in the space programs of the United States. In addition to twenty NASA career astronauts, the memorial includes the names of an [X-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-15) test pilot, a [U.S. Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Air_Force) officer who died while training for a then-classified military space program, and a civilian [spaceflight participant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight_participant).
