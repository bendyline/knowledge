The **S\&P 500** (**Standard and Poor's 500**)[^cite_note-6] is a [stock market index](23408957.md) tracking the stock performance of 500 companies listed on [stock exchanges](28168.md) in the [United States](3434750.md) that are considered to have large [market capitalizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization). The S\&P 500 is a subset of the [S\&P 1500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S\&P_1500). It is maintained by [S\&P Dow Jones Indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S\&P_Dow_Jones_Indices) and its components are selected by a committee. For a list of the criteria for addition to the index, see [S\&P 1500 § Selection criteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S\&P_1500#Selection_criteria).[^cite_note-Methodology-4]

The index is a [public-float-weighted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalization-weighted_index#Free-float_weighting)/[capitalization-weighted index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalization-weighted_index). The index includes approximately 83% of the total [market capitalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization) of U.S. [public companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_company), with an aggregate market capitalization of more than \$61.1 trillion as of December 31, 2025.[^cite_note-sp500r-2] The median market capitalization of the components of the index is \$41.8 billion and components range in market capitalization from \$5.6 billion to \$4.8 trillion.[^cite_note-7]

Products linked to the S\&P 500 include [index funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_fund) ([exchange-traded funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange-traded_fund)/ETFs and [mutual funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_funds)) as well as [derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_\(finance\)) ([options](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_\(finance\)) and [futures contracts](242855.md)), which are available for trading in many countries with the goal of replicating the performance of the index. Also available are modified index funds; they replicate the performance of the index with modifications such as the use of [covered call](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covered_call) strategies, equal weighting, performance buffers, [leverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverage_\(finance\)), inclusion of only growth or value stocks, or exclusion of certain sectors, all with the goal of changing the risk/return and [yield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yield_\(finance\)). The [SPDR S\&P 500 ETF Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPDR_S\&P_500_ETF_Trust), with approximately \$800 billion in assets, has the highest average daily volume of all S\&P 500 ETFs. In total, there was approximately \$13 trillion in assets tracking the index as of December 31, 2024.[^cite_note-8]

The component companies that have increased their dividends over 25 consecutive years are known as the [S\&P 500 Dividend Aristocrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S\&P_500_Dividend_Aristocrats).[^cite_note-9] Companies in the S\&P 500 derive a collective 72% of revenues from the United States and 28% from other countries.[^cite_note-10] The index is one of the factors in the computation of the [Conference Board Leading Economic Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_Board_Leading_Economic_Index), which is used to forecast the direction of the economy.[^cite_note-11] The index is associated with many [ticker symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ticker_symbol), including ^GSPC,.[^cite_note-12] INX,[^cite_note-13] and SPX, depending on market or website.[^cite_note-14]

## History



Though there is large variability in month-to-month changes in the S\&P 500 (gray lines), a seasonal pattern emerges when the monthly change values are averaged (bold line).

[^cite_note-YahooFinance_SPX-15]

In 1860, [Henry Varnum Poor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Varnum_Poor) published the *History of Railroads and Canals in the United States,* a reference guide to the railroad industry for investors; in 1868 he published the book's follow-up, *[Poor's Manual of the Railroads of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poor's_Manual_of_the_Railroads_of_the_United_States)*.[^cite_note-history-16] In 1923, Standard Statistics Company (founded in 1906 as the [Standard Statistics Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S\&P_Global_Ratings#History)) began rating [mortgage bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortgage_bond)[^cite_note-history-16] and developed its first stock market index consisting of the stocks of 233 U.S. companies, computed weekly.[^cite_note-keydates-1] Three years later, it developed a 90-stock index, computed daily.[^cite_note-keydates-1] In 1941, Poor's publishing company merged with Standard Statistics Company to form [Standard & Poor's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_&_Poor's).[^cite_note-history-16][^cite_note-17]

On Monday, March 4, 1957, the index was expanded to its current extent of 500 companies and was renamed the S\&P 500 Stock Composite Index.[^cite_note-keydates-1] In 1962, Ultronic Systems became the compiler of the S\&P indices including the S\&P 500 Stock Composite Index, the 425 Stock Industrial Index, the 50 Stock Utility Index, and the 25 Stock Rail Index.[^cite_note-18] On August 31, 1976, [The Vanguard Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vanguard_Group) offered the first [mutual fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_fund) to [retail investors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retail_investor) that tracked the index.[^cite_note-keydates-1] On April 21, 1982, the [Chicago Mercantile Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Mercantile_Exchange) began trading futures based on the index.[^cite_note-keydates-1] On July 1, 1983, the [Chicago Board Options Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Board_Options_Exchange) began trading [options](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_\(finance\)) based on the index.[^cite_note-keydates-1] Beginning in 1986, the index value was updated every 15 seconds, or 1,559 times per trading day, with price updates disseminated by [Reuters](18998750.md). Prior to this, it had been updated once every minute.[^cite_note-19]

On January 22, 1993, the [Standard & Poor's Depositary Receipts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_&_Poor's_Depositary_Receipts) [exchange-traded fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange-traded_fund) issued by [State Street Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Street_Corporation) began trading.[^cite_note-keydates-1] On September 9, 1997, [CME Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CME_Group) introduced the S\&P E-mini futures contract.[^cite_note-keydates-1] In 2005, the index transitioned to a public float-adjusted capitalization-weighting.[^cite_note-20] Friday, September 17, 2021, was the final trading date for the original SP big contract which began trading in 1982.[^cite_note-21]

## Subsets

S\&P Dow Jones Indices provides several indices which are primarily market cap-based subsets of the S\&P 500, all categorized within the [megacap segment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megacap_stock):[^cite_note-22]

- [S\&P 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S\&P_100)

- S\&P 500 Top 50

- S\&P 500 Top 20

  [^cite_note-24]

- S\&P 500 Top 10

  [^cite_note-25]

The constituents of the S\&P 100 are chosen by an index committee, typically from among "the largest companies in the S\&P 500 that have listed options" while also considering [sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_sector) balance.[^cite_note-method-p13-26] By contrast, the "Top" indices are entirely rules-based, basically taking the specified number of very largest (by float-adjusted market capitalization) companies from the S\&P 500, though each index has some level of buffering to favor retaining current constituents, presumably to decrease turnover.[^cite_note-method-p13-26] The S\&P 500 Top 50 Index is comparable to the [Russell Top 50 Mega Cap Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Top_50_Mega_Cap_Index). The S\&P 500 Top 10 Index is comparable to the Russell Top 10 Index.[^cite_note-27]

S\&P DJI also offers a nominally-related "S\&P 500 [Magnificent 7](57174306.md#Magnificent_Seven) Index".[^cite_note-28]

As of 2026, the S\&P 500 is fairly top-heavy, with the S\&P 500 Top 20 comprising nearly half of the index's total market cap (see chart).&#x20;

## Performance

Since its inception in 1926, the index's [compound annual growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_annual_growth_rate)—including [dividends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dividend)—has been approximately 9.8% (6% after [inflation](38286.md)), with the [standard deviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_deviation) of the return, calculated on a monthly basis, over the same time period being 20.81%. While the index has declined in several years by over 30%,[^cite_note-34] it has posted annual increases 70% of the time,[^cite_note-love-35] with 5% of all trading days resulting in record highs.[^cite_note-36]
