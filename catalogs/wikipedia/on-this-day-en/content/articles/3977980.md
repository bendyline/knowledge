The **ICC Women's Cricket World Cup** is the quadrennial international championship of the [One Day International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_One_Day_International) format with 50 [overs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over_\(cricket\)) per team. It is organised by the [International Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council).

Until 2005, when the two organisations merged, it was administered by a separate body, the [International Women's Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Women's_Cricket_Council). The [first World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Women's_Cricket_World_Cup) was held in England in 1973, two years before [the inaugural men's tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Cricket_World_Cup). The event's early years were marked by funding difficulties, which meant several teams had to decline invitations to compete and caused gaps of up to six years between tournaments. However, since 2005, World Cups have been hosted at regular four-year intervals.

Qualification for the World Cup is through the [ICC Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Women's_Championship) and the [World Cup Qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Cricket_World_Cup_Qualifier). The [1997 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Women's_Cricket_World_Cup) was contested by eleven teams[^cite_note-1] and since then no new teams have debuted in the tournament. Since 2000 the number of teams in the World Cup has been fixed at eight. However, in March 2021, the ICC decided that the tournament would expand to 10 teams from the 2029 edition.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

The thirteen World Cups played have been held in five countries, with India and England having hosted the event three times. [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_women's_national_cricket_team) is the most successful team, having won seven titles and failing to make the final on only four occasions. [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_women's_cricket_team) (four titles), [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_women's_national_cricket_team) and [India](2281524.md) (one title each) are the only other teams to have won the event, while the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies_women's_cricket_team) and [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_women's_cricket_team) (once each) have each reached the final without going on to win.

## History

### First World Cup

Women's international cricket was first played in 1934, when a party from England toured Australia and New Zealand. The first [Test match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Test_cricket) was played on 28–31 December 1934, and was won by England.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeyhoe_FlintRheinberg1976175–180-4] The first Test against New Zealand followed early the following year. These three nations remained the only Test-playing teams in women's cricket until 1960, when South Africa played a number of matches against England.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeyhoe_FlintRheinberg1976175–180-4] [Limited overs cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_overs_cricket) was first played by [first-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_cricket) teams in England in 1962.[^cite_note-5] Nine years later, the first international one day match was played in men's cricket, when [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_cricket_team) took on [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_national_cricket_team) at the [Melbourne Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Cricket_Ground).[^cite_note-6]

Talks began in 1971 about holding a World Cup for women's cricket, led by [Jack Hayward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Hayward).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeyhoe_FlintRheinberg1976168-7] South Africa, under pressure from the world for their apartheid laws, were not invited to take part in the competition.[^cite_note-wch-8] Both of the other two Test-playing nations, Australia and New Zealand were invited. Hayward had previously organised tours of the West Indies by England women and it was from this region that the other two competing nations were drawn; [Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_national_women's_cricket_team) and [Trinidad & Tobago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago_national_women's_cricket_team). To make up the numbers England also fielded a "[Young England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_England_women's_cricket_team)" team, and an "[International XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_XI_women's_cricket_team)" was also included.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeyhoe_FlintRheinberg1976168-7] Five South Africans were invited to play for the International XI as a means of compensation for the team not being invited but these invitations were later withdrawn.[^cite_note-wch-8]

The [inaugural tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Women's_Cricket_World_Cup) was held at a variety of venues across England in June and July 1973,[^cite_note-1973results-9] two years before the first men's [Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_World_Cup) was played.[^cite_note-10] The competition was played as a round-robin tournament and the last scheduled match was England against Australia. Australia went into the game leading the table by a solitary point; they had won four matches and had one abandoned. England had also won four matches but they had lost to New Zealand.[^cite_note-1973results-9][^cite_note-11] As a result, the match also served as a de facto final for the competition. England won the match, held at [Edgbaston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgbaston_Cricket_Ground) Birmingham, by 92 runs to win the tournament.[^cite_note-12]

## Editions and results

Fifteen teams have appeared at the Women's Cricket World Cup at least once, excluding qualification tournaments. Three teams have competed at every tournament: England, Australia and New Zealand. They were the only sides to have won a title until 2025, when India won their first title.
