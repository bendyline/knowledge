**Naval Support Facility Diego Garcia**, abbreviated as **NSF Diego Garcia**,[^cite_note-1] is a [British Indian Ocean Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Ocean_Territory) facility used by the [Royal Navy](26061.md) and leased to the [United States Navy](20518076.md), located on the atoll [Diego Garcia](8630.md) in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md). [Camp Thunder Cove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Thunder_Cove) is part of the facility, and is operated by the [United States Armed Forces](32212.md) and [British Armed Forces](31730.md).[^cite_note-2]

The base was built between 1971 and 1976 following the [expulsion of 2,000 Chagosians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Chagossians), descendants of imported plantation workers, from Diego Garcia and other islands in the [Chagos Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagos_Archipelago).[^cite_note-3] In October 2024, the British government announced a deal that would see sovereignty over the Chagos Islands transferred to Mauritius and the Navy Support Facility Diego Garcia leased to the British government for an initial period of [99 years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/99-year_lease).[^cite_note-4] In September 2026, [Wes Streeting](17169295.md) announced that the Chagos Islands deal would be placed under review following direct criticism from [Donald Trump](4848272.md).[^cite_note-5]

## Mission

NSF Diego Garcia provides Base Operating Services to tenant commands located on the island. The command's mission is "To provide logistic support to operational forces forward deployed to the Indian Ocean and Persian Gulf AORs in support of national policy objectives."[^cite_note-6]

As of January 2012, the facility supported the following tenant commands:

- [Maritime Pre-Positioning Ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Prepositioning_ship) Squadron TWO

- Branch Health Clinic

- Naval Computer And Telecommunications Station Far East Detachment Diego Garcia

- [Naval Mobile Construction Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Mobile_Construction_Battalion) Detachment

- [Naval Media Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Media_Center) Detachment Diego Garcia

- [Military Sealift Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Sealift_Command) Office Diego Garcia

- Mission Support Facility

- Fleet Logistics Center Diego Garcia

- [NAVFAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Facilities_Engineering_Command) Public Works Department

## History

In 1971, the local population of Diego Garcia, the [Chagossians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagossians), was [forcibly expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Chagossians) from the island to make way for the base.[^cite_note-7]

The Navy tasked the [Seabees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabees) of [Naval Mobile Construction Battalions 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Mobile_Construction_Battalion_1), [40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Mobile_Construction_Battalion_40), 62, 71, [133](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Mobile_Construction_Battalion_133) and [Amphibious Construction Battalion 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_Construction_Battalion_2) with the construction of the base.[^cite_note-DG-8] On 23 January 1971 the first men of NMCB 40 arrived on site to begin what became an extended project.[^cite_note-DG-8] Naval Support Facility Diego Garcia, was established as the senior United States Navy command on the island on 1 October 1977. At the time the NAVCOMMSTA was the primary tenant, but as new major facilities were completed, most notably the expanded anchorage and mooring area and the extended airfield, other tenants were commissioned.[^cite_note-Natural_Resources_Management_Plan_2005-9] In 1980, the United States Navy established the [Near-Term Prepositioned Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Sealift_Command#Prepositioning_Program) of 16 ships. Then NTPF became the Afloat Prepositioning Force (AFP) and eventually [Maritime Prepositioning Ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Prepositioning_ship) Squadron Two (MPSRON 2) consisting of 20 deep-water pre-positioned logistics ships anchored in the lagoon.[^cite_note-10]

In 1981, the [naval air facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_air_facility) was commissioned. It was decommissioned in 1987 and its responsibilities returned to the Naval Support Facility Diego Garcia.[^cite_note-Natural_Resources_Management_Plan_2005-9]

In 1982, construction activities were transferred from the Seabees to a consortium of civilian contractors, Raymond International, and Brown and Root and [Mowlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mowlem), a joint venture. Raymond had deep-draft wharf and waterfront skills, Brown and Root had concrete and infrastructure skills, and [Mowlem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mowlem) was an English firm known for taking on challenging projects. The majority of the projects were completed by 1988.[^cite_note-Edis_90-11]

On 26 March 1982, Barbara Shuping and five other women were assigned to the Naval Support Facility Diego Garcia. They were the first women to live on the island since the Chagossians were expelled in 1971.[^cite_note-12]

In 1985, the new port facilities were completed and the [USS *Saratoga*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Saratoga_\(CV-60\)) was the first aircraft carrier to tie up.[^cite_note-13] Two years later, the Air Force's [Strategic Air Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Air_Command) began deploying [Boeing B-52](18933037.md) bombers and [aerial refueling](237949.md) aircraft to the newly completed airfield facilities.[^cite_note-Edis_94-14]

Following the [Iraqi invasion of Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_invasion_of_Kuwait) in August 1990, three ships of COMPSRON 2 sortied, delivering a [Marine Expeditionary Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Expeditionary_Brigade) to Saudi Arabia for participation in the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War). Other COMPSRON 2 ships offloaded the ammunition and fuel on Diego Garcia that were required for the American bomber fleet that deployed to the airfield. Subsequently, B-52G bombers flew more than 200 17-hour bombing missions over 44 days and dropped more than 800,000 short tons (730,000 metric tons) of bombs on the [Iraqi Armed Forces](4627429.md) in Iraq and Kuwait.[^cite_note-Edis_94-14] One of the B-52s, #59-2593, crashed from mechanical failures just north of the island with the loss of three of its six-man crew.[^cite_note-15]

Beginning on 7 October 2001, the United States again commenced military operations from Diego Garcia using [B-1](4952.md), [B-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman_B-2_Spirit), and B-52 bombers to attack [Taliban](30635.md) and [al-Qaeda](1921.md) targets in Afghanistan following the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) on the [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(1973%E2%80%932001\)) and [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon). The base played an important role in the [invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Afghanistan) until the U.S.-led coalition could establish [forward bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_operating_base) in the country because many countries closer to Afghanistan such as [Turkey](11125639.md), [Pakistan](23235.md), and [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) refused to allow United States air bases in their territory to be used for combat operations.[^cite_note-16] On 12 December 2001, a B-1 bomber was lost to mechanical failures just after takeoff from the island, but the crew survived and was rescued by the [USS *Russell*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Russell_\(DDG-59\)).[^cite_note-17] Four B-2 Shelter System Extra Large Deployable Aircraft Hangar Systems were erected at Diego Garcia to support the bombers' operations.[^cite_note-gs-18][^cite_note-stripes-19] Combat operations resumed in the spring of 2003, with MPSRON 2 sortieing to the Persian Gulf for the [Iraq War](5043324.md), and bombing operations began again, this time against Iraq.[^cite_note-20] Bomber operations ceased from Diego Garcia on 15 August 2006.[^cite_note-realhistory-21]

Iranian strikes in February 2026 destroyed much of [Naval Support Activity Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Support_Activity_Bahrain) during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), making it ineffective as a US Navy major logistics hub. This caused NSF Diego Garcia to be chosen as the alternative logistics hub, though about 2,200 miles (1,900 nmi) from where aircraft carrier strike groups operated in the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md).[^cite_note-nyt-20260814-22]

On 20 March 2026, Iran launched two ballistic missiles towards [Diego Garcia](8630.md), though both failed to hit the island. While one of the missiles broke apart mid-flight, another was intercepted by a [SM-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM-3) air defence missile launched by a US warship.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] Iran's Ministry of Foreign Affairs on Monday denied the allegations that it was behind the attack and that the missile launches were a “false flag” operation. He suggested that Israel might have been behind them. NATO Secretary-General [Mark Rutte](4321056.md) told [CBS News](359551.md) that the alliance “cannot confirm” Israel's claim that the projectiles targeting the Diego Garcia airbase were Iranian intercontinental ballistic missiles.[^cite_note-25] On 23 March, elements of the [31st Marine Expeditionary Unit](5885654.md) trained for beach reconnaissance on the island.[^cite_note-26]

## Military contracting

[KBR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KBR_\(company\)) runs base operations support services at Naval Support Facility Diego Garcia.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] Serco Inc., of [Herndon, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herndon,_Virginia), operates and maintains the Ground-based Electro-Optical Deep Space Surveillance ([GEODSS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GEODSS)), which tracks deep-space satellites.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

Recent construction in support of US military activities on Diego Garcia has included:

- Black Construction/Mace International JV building a 34-metre antenna facility (expected completed by April 2021) and two new 13-metre radomes (expected completed by February 2021).

  [^cite_note-34]

- Black Construction/Mace International JV repairing deep-draft wharf infrastructure (expected completed by September 2023).

  [^cite_note-35]

- San Juan-Black & Veatch International Ltd. JV, of [Montrose, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montrose,_Colorado), repairing the north parking apron (expected completed by May 2022).

  [^cite_note-36]

- SJC-BVIL moving underground the power and telephone lines that run from the Navy ammunition area to the Air Force ammunition area along DG1 (expected completed by September 2022).

  [^cite_note-37]

Supplementary work at the facility includes Poole Fire Protection of [Olathe, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olathe,_Kansas), testing and inspecting fire protection systems (June 2020 – June 2025);[^cite_note-38] Jacobs/B\&M JV architect-engineer services, specifically design, engineering, specification writing, cost estimating (July 2020 – July 2025);[^cite_note-39] and InSynergy Engineering Inc. utility system studies (September 2020 – September 2025).[^cite_note-40]
