The **Germany national football team** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Deutsche Fußballnationalmannschaft*) represents [Germany](11867.md) in men's international [football](10568.md) and played its first match in 1908.[^cite_note-First_game-8] The team is governed by the [German Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Football_Association) (*Deutscher Fußball-Bund (DFB)*), founded in 1900, by members the single largest national sports federation in the world.[^cite_note-13] Between 1949 and 1990, separate German national teams were recognised by [FIFA](11049.md) due to [Allied occupation and division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Occupation_Zones_in_Germany): the DFB's team representing the [Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Germany) (commonly referred to as West Germany in English between 1949 and 1990), the [Saarland team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland_national_football_team) representing the [Saar Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saar_Protectorate) (1950–1956) and the [East Germany team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany_national_football_team) representing the [German Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) (1952–1990). The latter two were absorbed along with their records;[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] the present team represents the reunified Federal Republic. The official name and code "Germany FR (FRG)" was shortened to "Germany (GER)" following [reunification in 1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification).

Germany is one of the most successful national teams in international competitions, having won four [FIFA World Cups](11370.md) ([1954](60523.md), [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup), [1990](157232.md), and [2014](656933.md)), tied with [Italy](362466.md), and only one fewer than the most successful team, [Brazil](149286.md). They have been runners-up at the World Cup four times and have a further four third-place finishes. They are the only national football team to achieve this. Having won three [European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) ([1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1972), [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1980), and [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996)) Germany is second behind [Spain](609825.md), the record holder in that international competition with four. Germany also won the [FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup) in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup).[^cite_note-GermanyFIFA-16] They have been runners-up at European Championship three times, sharing this lead with the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). East Germany won [Olympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics) Gold in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1976_Summer_Olympics). Germany was the first, and is one of only two nations to have won both the FIFA World Cup and the [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) (the other being Spain).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] By combined World Cups, Germany stands as the most successful football nation in history with six World Cups – four for the men's team and two for the women's. At the end of the 2014 World Cup, Germany earned the second highest [Elo rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Football_Elo_Ratings) of any national football team in history, with 2,223 points.[^cite_note-19] Germany is the only European nation that has won a FIFA World Cup in [South America](26769.md). The most notable [German football rivalries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_football_rivalries) on international level are the ones [with the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany%E2%80%93Netherlands_football_rivalry), [with Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany%E2%80%93Italy_football_rivalry) and [with England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England%E2%80%93Germany_football_rivalry).

## History

Main article: [History of the Germany national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Germany_national_football_team)

### Early years (1899–1942)

On 18 April 1897, an early international game on [German soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Germany) was played in [Hamburg](13467.md) when a selection team from the [Danish Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Football_Association) defeated a selection team from the Hamburg-Altona Football Association, 5–0.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

Between 1899 and 1901, prior to the formation of a national team, there were five international matches between Germany and [English](9904.md) selection teams, which are today not recognised as official by either nation's football association (in part because England fielded their [amateur side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_amateur_football_team), which was an overflow or B team). All five matches ended in large defeats for the Germany teams, including a 12–0 loss at [White Hart Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Hart_Lane) in September 1901.[^cite_note-eng-amateurs-22]



Germany national team at its first official international match in 1908

Eight years after the establishment of the [German Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Football_Association) (DFB) in 1900, the first official match of the Germany national football team[^cite_note-23] was played on 5 April 1908, against [Switzerland](681862.md) in [Basel](4911.md), with the Swiss winning 5–3.[^cite_note-First_game-8]

A follow-up to the earlier series between England Amateurs and Germany occurred in March 1909 at [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_City_F.C.)'s White House Ground[^cite_note-24] and resulted in Germany's largest official defeat to date: 9–0 (this time, the match was recognised and recorded as official by the DFB but not by the [FA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association), again due to the amateur side being fielded).[^cite_note-eng-amateurs-22] These early confrontations formed the beginning of the rich [rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England%E2%80%93Germany_football_rivalry) between the two teams: one of the longest and most enduring international rivalries in football.[^cite_note-25]

[Julius Hirsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Hirsch) was the first Jewish player to represent the Germany national football team, which he joined in 1911.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Hirsch scored four goals for Germany against the Netherlands in 1912, becoming the first German to score four goals in a single match.[^cite_note-auto-28][^cite_note-29]



Forward Gottfried Fuchs, key player for Germany at the 1912 Olympic Games

[Gottfried Fuchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Fuchs) scored a world record 10 goals for Germany in a 16–0 win against [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire_national_football_team) at the [1912 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1912_Summer_Olympics) in Stockholm on 1 July, becoming the top scorer of the tournament; his international record was not surpassed until 2001 when [Australia](4792249.md)'s [Archie Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archie_Thompson) scored 13 goals in a [31–0 defeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_31%E2%80%930_American_Samoa) of [American Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Samoa_national_soccer_team).[^cite_note-30] He was Jewish, and the German Football Association erased all references to him from their records between 1933 and 1945.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] As of 2016, he was still the top German scorer for one match.[^cite_note-33]

At that time the players were selected by the DFB, as there was no dedicated coach. The first manager of the Germany national team was [Otto Nerz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Nerz), a school teacher from [Mannheim](99627.md), who served in the role from 1926 to 1936.[^cite_note-34] The German FA could not afford travel to Uruguay for the first World Cup staged in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup) during the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression), but finished third in the [1934 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) in their first appearance in the competition. After a poor showing at the [1936 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics) in Berlin, [Sepp Herberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepp_Herberger) became coach. In 1937 he put together a squad which was soon nicknamed the *Breslau Elf* ([the Breslau Eleven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breslau_Eleven)) in recognition of their 8–0 win over Denmark in the then German city of Breslau, [Lower Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Silesia) (now [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw), Poland).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

After [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_State_of_Austria) became part of [Germany](21212.md) in the *[Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss)* of March 1938, the [Austrian national team](695226.md) – one of Europe's best sides at the time due to professionalism – was disbanded despite having already qualified for the [1938 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup). [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi) politicians ordered five or six ex-Austrian players, from the clubs [Rapid Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Rapid_Wien), [Austria Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Austria_Wien), and [First Vienna FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Vienna_FC), to join the "all-German" team on short notice in a staged show of unity for political reasons. At the 1938 World Cup in France, this "united" Germany national team managed only a 1–1 draw against Switzerland and then lost the replay 2–4 in front of a hostile crowd in Paris. That early exit stands as Germany's worst World Cup result before 2018, and one of just three occasions the team failed to progress from the group stage – the next would not occur until the [2018](13327177.md) tournament, and it would be repeated in [2022](17742072.md).

During [World War II](32927.md), the team played over 30 international games between September 1939 and November 1942. National team games were then suspended, as most players had to join the armed forces. Many of the national team players were gathered together under coach Herberger as *[Rote Jäger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rote_J%C3%A4ger)* through the efforts of a sympathetic [air force officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Graf) trying to protect the footballers from the most dangerous wartime service.

### Three German national teams (1945–1990)

After World War II, Germany was banned from competition in most sports until 1950. The DFB was not a full member of FIFA, and none of the three new German states – [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany), [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany), and [Saarland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saar_\(protectorate\)) – entered the [1950 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup) qualifiers.

The Federal Republic of Germany, which was referred to as West Germany, continued the DFB. With recognition by FIFA and UEFA, the DFB maintained and continued the record of the pre-war team. [Switzerland](681862.md) was the first team that played West Germany in 1950,[^cite_note-37] with the latter qualifying for the [1954 World Cup](60523.md) and the former hosting it.

The [Saarland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland), a French protectorate between 1947 and 1956, did not join French organisations, and was barred from participating in pan-German ones. It sent [their own team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland_national_football_team) to the [1952 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952_Summer_Olympics) and the [1954 World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification). In 1957, Saarland acceded to the Federal Republic of Germany.

In 1949, the [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist) German Democratic Republic (East Germany) was founded. In 1952 the *[Deutscher Fußball-Verband der DDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutscher_Fu%C3%9Fball-Verband_der_DDR)* (DFV) was established and the [East Germany national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany_national_football_team) took to the field. They were the only team to beat the [1974 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup)-winning West Germans in the [only meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany_v_West_Germany_\(1974_FIFA_World_Cup\)) of the two sides of the divided nation. East Germany won the gold medal at the [1976 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1976_Summer_Olympics). After [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) in 1990, the eastern football competition was reintegrated into the DFB.

### 1954 World Cup victory

Main article: [1954 FIFA World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup_final)



[Helmut Rahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Rahn) scored the winning goal in the 1954 FIFA World Cup final.

West Germany, captained by [Fritz Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Walter), met in the 1954 World Cup against [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team), [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) and [Austria](695226.md). When playing favourites [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) in the group stage, West Germany lost 3–8, and faced the Hungarian ["Mighty Magyars"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Team) again in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup_final). Hungary had gone unbeaten for 32 consecutive matches, and West Germany snapped the streak by winning 3–2, with [Helmut Rahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Rahn) scoring the winning goal.[^cite_note-38] The success is called "The Miracle of Bern" (*Das Wunder von Bern*).[^cite_note-39]

### Memorable losses: Wembley goal and game of the century (1958–1970)

After finishing fourth in the [1958 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup) and only reaching the quarter-finals [four years later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup), the DFB made changes. Professionalism was introduced, and the best clubs from the various Regionalligas were assembled into the new [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga). In 1964, [Helmut Schön](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Sch%C3%B6n) took over as coach, replacing Herberger who had been in office for 28 years.

In the [1966 World Cup](61629.md), West Germany reached the final after beating the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_national_football_team) in the semi-final, facing hosts [England](9904.md). In extra time, the first goal by [Geoff Hurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Hurst) was one of the most contentious goals in the history of the World Cup: the linesman signalled the ball had crossed the line for a goal, after bouncing down from the crossbar, when replays showed it did not appear to have fully crossed the line. Hurst then scored another goal giving England a 4–2 win.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

West Germany knocked England out in the [1970 World Cup](157227.md) quarter-finals 3–2, before they suffered a 4–3 extra-time loss in the semi-final against [Italy](362466.md). This match with five goals in extra time is one of the most dramatic in World Cup history, and is called the "[Game of the Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_of_the_Century_\(football\))" in both Italy and Germany.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] West Germany claimed third place by beating [Uruguay](679782.md) 1–0. [Gerd Müller](172313.md) finished as the tournament's top scorer with 10 goals.

### 1974 World Cup title on home soil

Main article: [1974 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup)



The [1974 FIFA World Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) on 7 July, in [Munich](19058.md)'s [Olympiastadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Stadium_\(Munich\))

In 1971, [Franz Beckenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Beckenbauer) became captain of the national team, and he led West Germany to victory at the European Championship at [Euro 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1972), defeating the Soviet Union 3–0 in the final.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

As hosts of the [1974 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup), they won their second World Cup, defeating the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) 2–1 in the final in Munich.[^cite_note-46] Two matches in the 1974 World Cup stood out for West Germany. The first group stage saw a politically charged match as West Germany played a game against [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany_national_football_team). [The East Germans won 1–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany_v_West_Germany_\(1974_FIFA_World_Cup\)) but it made a scant difference to West Germany as the West Germans advanced to the knockout stage.[^cite_note-47] The West Germans advanced to the final against the [Johan Cruyff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Cruyff)-led Dutch team and their brand of "[Total Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Football)". The Dutch took the lead from a [penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_kick_\(football\)). However, West Germany equalised with a penalty by [Paul Breitner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Breitner), and won the match with [Gerd Müller](172313.md)'s fine finish soon after.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

### Late 1970s and early 1980s



[Gerd Müller](172313.md) in 1974

West Germany failed to defend their titles in the next two major international tournaments. They lost to [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team) in the [UEFA Euro 1976 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1976_final) in a [penalty shootout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_shootout_\(football\)) 5–3,[^cite_note-50] their last penalty shootout loss in a major tournament as of 2025.[^cite_note-51]

In the [1978 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup), Germany was eliminated in the second group stage after losing 3–2 to [Austria](695226.md). Schön retired as coach afterward, and the post was taken over by his assistant, [Jupp Derwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupp_Derwall).

West Germany's first tournament under Derwall was successful, as they earned their second European title at [Euro 1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1980) after defeating [Belgium](679716.md) 2–1 in the final.[^cite_note-52] West Germany started the [1982 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup) with a 1–2 upset by newcomers [Algeria](1029528.md) in their first match,[^cite_note-53] but advanced to the second round with a [controversial 1–0 win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany_v_Austria_\(1982\)) over Austria. In the [semi-final against France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany_v_France_\(1982_FIFA_World_Cup\)), they drew 3–3 and won the penalty shootout 5–4.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] In the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup_Final), they were defeated by Italy 1–3.[^cite_note-56]

During this period, West Germany's [Gerd Müller](172313.md) racked up fourteen goals in two World Cups (1970 and 1974). His ten goals in 1970 are the third-most ever in a tournament. Müller's all-time World Cup record of 14 goals was broken by [Ronaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldo_\(Brazilian_footballer\)) in 2006; this was then further broken by [Miroslav Klose](201840.md) in 2014 with 16 goals.[^cite_note-57]

### 1990 World Cup title and Beckenbauer's managing era (1984–1990)



[Franz Beckenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Beckenbauer) in 1990

After West Germany were eliminated in the first round of [Euro 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1984), [Franz Beckenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Beckenbauer) returned to the national team to replace Derwall as manager.[^cite_note-BeckenbauerFIFA-58] At the [1986 World Cup](157231.md) in Mexico, West Germany finished as runners-up for the second consecutive tournament after beating France 2–0 in the semi-finals, but losing to the [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona)-led [Argentina](454699.md) in the final, 2–3.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] In [Euro 1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1988), after drawing Italy 1–1 and beating both Denmark and [Spain](609825.md) 2–0 in the group stage,[^cite_note-61] West Germany's hopes of winning the tournament on home soil were spoiled by the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), as the Dutch beat them 2–1 in the semi-finals.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

At the [1990 World Cup](157232.md) in Italy, West Germany won their third World Cup title, in its unprecedented third consecutive final appearance.[^cite_note-WC1990-Overview-64] Captained by [Lothar Matthäus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothar_Matth%C3%A4us), they defeated [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) (4–1), [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team) (5–1), the Netherlands (2–1), [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team) (1–0), and [England](9904.md) (1–1, 4–3 on penalty kicks) on the way to a final rematch against Argentina in Rome.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] West Germany won 1–0, with the only goal being a penalty scored in the 85th minute by [Andreas Brehme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Brehme).[^cite_note-WC1990-Overview-64] Beckenbauer, who won the World Cup as the national team's captain in 1974, thus became the first person to win the World Cup as both captain and manager,[^cite_note-BeckenbauerFIFA-58] and the second to win as player and manager, after [Mario Zagallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Zagallo) of Brazil.

In [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_football_at_the_1956_Summer_Olympics), [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1960_Summer_Olympics), and [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1964_Summer_Olympics) both states had sent a [United Team of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Team_of_Germany). They went on to win the bronze medal for Germany. As GDR, they won bronze in [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1972_Summer_Olympics) in Munich, gold in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1976_Summer_Olympics) in Montreal, and silver in [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1980_Summer_Olympics) in Moscow.

Prior to 1984, [Olympic football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_football) was an amateur event, meaning that only non-professional players could participate.[^cite_note-67] Due to this, West Germany was never able to achieve the same degree of success at the Olympics as at the World Cup. The first medal coming in the [1988 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1988_Summer_Olympics), when they won the bronze medal after beating [Italy](362466.md) 3–0 in the 3rd place match.[^cite_note-68] West Germany also reached the second round in both [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1972_Summer_Olympics) and [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1984_Summer_Olympics). On the other hand, due to having an ability to field its top-level players who were classified as amateurs on a technicality East Germany did better, winning a gold, a silver and two bronze medals (one representing the United Team of Germany).

### Berti Vogts years (1990–1998)



[Berti Vogts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berti_Vogts)

In February 1990, three months after the fall of the [Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall), [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany_national_football_team) and West Germany were drawn together in [UEFA Euro 1992 qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1992_qualifying_Group_5). In November 1990, the East German association [Deutscher Fußball-Verband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutscher_Fu%C3%9Fball-Verband) integrated into the DFB, by which time the East Germany team had ceased operations, playing its last match on 12 September 1990. The unified Germany national team completed the European Championship qualifying group. The [East German 1990–91 league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990%E2%80%9391_NOFV-Oberliga) continued, with a restructuring of German leagues in [1991–92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_Bundesliga). The first game with a unified Germany national team was against [Switzerland](681862.md) on 19 December 1990.[^cite_note-69]

After the 1990 World Cup, assistant [Berti Vogts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berti_Vogts) took over as the national team coach from the retiring Beckenbauer. In [Euro 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1992), Germany reached the final, but lost 0–2 to underdogs [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team).[^cite_note-70]

In the [1994 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup), they were upset 1–2 in the quarterfinals by [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team).[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] As Germany played its first World Cup as a [reunified nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification), only one former [East German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany_national_football_team) player, [Matthias Sammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Sammer), was included on the roster.[^cite_note-\:0-73] Sammer played a significant role, featuring in four out of Germany's five matches.[^cite_note-\:0-73] The tournament marked a significant shift in East German representation at the World Cup, with few East German-born players being rostered for subsequent tournaments. Some researchers tie this to the excess of restrictions regarding football in the [German Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany).[^cite_note-\:1-74] The East German national team only featured in one World Cup, famously beating hosts West Germany 1-0 during the group stage in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-\:2-75] The unification of the West and East German football leagues had also led to a decline in East German Clubs, as they could no longer rely on government funding to subsidize their expenditures.[^cite_note-\:3-76] In the [1994/95 Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_Bundesliga) season, [Dynamo Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamo_Dresden) was the only East German club in the league, and was relegated at the end of the season.[^cite_note-\:4-77]

Reunified Germany won its first major international title at [Euro 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996), becoming European champions for the third time.[^cite_note-78] They defeated hosts [England](9904.md) in the semi-finals,[^cite_note-79] and the [Czech Republic](729739.md) 2–1 in the final on a [golden goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_goal) in extra time.[^cite_note-80]

However, in the [1998 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), Germany were eliminated in the quarter-finals after a 0–3 defeat to [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team), with all goals being scored after defender [Christian Wörns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_W%C3%B6rns) received a straight red card.[^cite_note-81] Vogts stepped down afterwards and was replaced by [Erich Ribbeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_Ribbeck).[^cite_note-82]

### Erich Ribbeck and Rudi Völler years (2000–2004)

In [Euro 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000), the team went out in the first round, drawing with Romania, then suffering a 1–0 defeat to England and were routed 3–0 by Portugal (which fielded their backup players, having already advanced).[^cite_note-83] Ribbeck resigned, and was replaced by [Rudi Völler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudi_V%C3%B6ller).[^cite_note-84]

Coming into the [2002 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), expectations of Germany were low due to poor results in the [qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)), and not directly qualifying for the finals for the first time. The team advanced from the group, and in the knockout stages they produced three consecutive 1–0 wins against [Paraguay](804025.md),[^cite_note-85] the [United States](582488.md),[^cite_note-86] and co-hosts [South Korea](1018627.md). [Oliver Neuville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Neuville) scored two minutes from time against Paraguay and [Michael Ballack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Ballack) scored both goals in the US and South Korea games, although he picked up a second yellow card against South Korea for a [tactical foul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_foul) and was suspended for the subsequent match.[^cite_note-87] This set up a final against [Brazil](149286.md), the first World Cup meeting between the two. Germany lost 0–2 thanks to two [Ronaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldo_\(Brazilian_footballer\)) goals.[^cite_note-88] Nevertheless, German captain and goalkeeper [Oliver Kahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Kahn) won the [Golden Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_World_Cup_awards#Golden_Ball),[^cite_note-89] the first time in the World Cup that a goalkeeper was named the best player of the tournament.[^cite_note-90]

Germany once again exited in the first round at [Euro 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004), drawing their first two matches and losing the third to the Czech Republic (who had fielded a second-string team).[^cite_note-91] Völler resigned afterwards, and [Jürgen Klinsmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BCrgen_Klinsmann) was appointed head coach.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

### Resurgence under Klinsmann (2004–2006)



Germany vs Portugal in the third place match at the 2006 FIFA World Cup

Klinsmann's main task was to lead the national team to a good showing at the [2006 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) in Germany. He relieved goalkeeper Kahn of the captaincy and announced that Kahn and longtime backup [Jens Lehmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jens_Lehmann) would be competing for the position of starting goaltender, a decision that angered Kahn and Lehmann eventually won that contest.[^cite_note-94] Expectations for the team were low, which was not helped by veteran defender [Christian Wörns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_W%C3%B6rns) being dropped (after Wörns criticised Klinsmann for designating him only as a backup player on the squad), a choice roundly panned in Germany. Italy routed Germany 4–1 in a March 2006 exhibition game, and Klinsmann bore the brunt of the criticism as the team was [ranked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings) only 22nd in the world entering the 2006 World Cup.[^cite_note-95]

As World Cup hosts, Germany won all three group stage matches to finish top of their group. The team defeated [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) 2–0 in the round of 16,[^cite_note-96] and Argentina in the quarter-finals in a penalty shootout.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] The semi-final against Italy was scoreless until near the end of extra time when Germany conceded two goals.[^cite_note-100] In the third place match, Germany defeated Portugal 3–1.[^cite_note-101] [Miroslav Klose](201840.md) was awarded the [Golden Boot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Cup_Golden_Boot) for his tournament-leading five goals.[^cite_note-102]

### Löw era (2006–2021)

#### Euro 2008, 2010 World Cup and Euro 2012

Main articles: [UEFA Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008), [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md), and [UEFA Euro 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012)

Germany's entry into the [Euro 2008 qualifying round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008_qualifying_Group_D) was marked by the promotion of [Joachim Löw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_L%C3%B6w) following the resignation of Klinsmann.[^cite_note-103] At [UEFA Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008), Germany won two out of three matches in group play to advance to the knockout round.[^cite_note-104] They defeated [Portugal](576328.md) 3–2 in the quarter-final,[^cite_note-105] and won their semi-final against [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team).[^cite_note-106] Germany lost the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008_final) against Spain 1–0, finishing as runners-up.[^cite_note-107]

At the 2010 World Cup, Germany won the group and advanced to the knockout stage. In the round of 16, Germany defeated [England](9904.md) 4–1.[^cite_note-108] Miroslav Klose tied [Gerd Müller](172313.md)'s record of 14 World Cup goals,[^cite_note-109] as Germany defeated [Argentina](454699.md) 4–0 in the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-110] In the semi-finals, Germany lost 1–0 to [Spain](609825.md).[^cite_note-111] They defeated Uruguay 3–2 to finish third.[^cite_note-112] [Thomas Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_M%C3%BCller) won the Golden Boot and the [Best Young Player Award](3482503.md#FIFA_Young_Player_Award).[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]



Germany during [Euro 2012 qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_qualification)

At Euro 2012, Germany was placed in [Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_Group_B) along with Portugal, [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), and [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team). Germany won all three group matches, and then defeated Greece in the quarter-finals as they set a record of 15 consecutive wins in all competitive matches.[^cite_note-115] In the semi-finals, Germany lost to Italy, 2–1.

#### 2014 World Cup victory

Main article: [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md)



Germany lifting the World Cup trophy in 2014

Germany were placed in [Group G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_G) of the 2014 World Cup,[^cite_note-116] with [Portugal](576328.md), [Ghana](1029620.md), and the [United States](582488.md). They first faced Portugal in a match billed by some as the "team of all the talents against the team of The Talent ([Cristiano Ronaldo](623737.md))", routing the Portuguese 4–0 thanks to a hat-trick by Thomas Müller.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] In their match with Ghana, they led the game before Ghana fought back to take the lead. When Klose scored to level the match at 2–2, he netted his 15th World Cup goal to join former Brazil striker [Ronaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldo_\(Brazilian_footballer\)) at the pinnacle of World Cup Finals scorers. They then went on to defeat the Klinsmann-led United States 1–0, securing them a spot in the knockout stages.

The round of 16 knockout match against Algeria remained goalless after regulation time, resulting in an extra time period where [André Schürrle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Sch%C3%BCrrle) scored from a Thomas Müller pass after only less than two minutes. [Mesut Özil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesut_%C3%96zil) scored Germany's second goal in the 120th minute with the match ending 2–1. In the [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#France_vs_Germany) against [France](250197.md), [Mats Hummels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mats_Hummels) scored the only goal in the 13th minute, as Germany advanced to a record fourth consecutive semi-final.[^cite_note-119]



Germany posing with a "Champions" banner after the [2014 World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_final)

The [7–1 semi-final win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_v_Germany_\(2014_FIFA_World_Cup\)) against [Brazil](149286.md) was one of the most memorable games in World Cup history; Germany scored four goals in just less than seven minutes and were 5–0 up by the 30th minute with goals from Thomas Müller, Miroslav Klose, Sami Khedira and two from Toni Kroos. Germany's 7–0 lead in the second half was the highest score against Brazil in a single game. It was Brazil's worst ever World Cup defeat,[^cite_note-120] whilst Germany broke multiple World Cup records with the win, including the record broken by Klose, the first team to reach four consecutive World Cup semi-finals, the first team to score seven goals in a World Cup knockout phase game, the fastest five consecutive goals in World Cup history (with four of the goals scored in just 400 seconds), and the first team to score five goals in the first half in a World Cup semi-final.[^cite_note-121]

The [World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_final) was held at the [Maracanã](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracan%C3%A3_Stadium) in Rio de Janeiro on 13 July.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123] [Mario Götze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_G%C3%B6tze)'s 113th-minute goal helped Germany beat Argentina 1–0, becoming the first-ever European team to win a FIFA World Cup in the Americas and the second European team to win the title outside Europe.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125]

#### Euro 2016 to 2017 Confederations Cup

Main articles: [UEFA Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016) and [2017 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup)

After several players retired from the team following the 2014 World Cup, including [Philipp Lahm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_Lahm), [Per Mertesacker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Mertesacker) and Miroslav Klose, the team had a disappointing start in the [UEFA Euro 2016 qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_qualifying). They defeated [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) 2–1 at home, then suffered a 2–0 loss at [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team) (the first in their history), a 1–1 draw against the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team), and a 4–0 win over [Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_national_football_team). Troubles during qualifying for Euro 2016 continued, drawing at home, as well as losing away to Ireland; the team also only narrowly defeated Scotland on two occasions, but handily won the return against Poland and both games against Gibraltar (who competed for the first time). They would eventually win their [group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_qualifying_Group_D) and qualify for the tournament.

On 13 November 2015, Germany played a friendly against [France](250197.md) in Paris when [a series of terrorist attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_2015_Paris_attacks) took place in the city, some in the direct vicinity of the [Stade de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_de_France), where the game was held.[^cite_note-126] For security reasons, the team spent the night inside the stadium, accompanied by the French squad who stayed behind in an act of comradery.[^cite_note-127] Four days later, Germany was scheduled to face [the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) at [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover)'s [HDI-Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niedersachsenstadion), in another friendly. After initial security reservations, the DFB decided to play the match on 15 November.[^cite_note-128] After reports about a concrete threat to the stadium, the match was cancelled 90 minutes before kickoff.[^cite_note-129]

At [UEFA Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016), Germany began their campaign with a 2–0 win against Ukraine. Against Poland, Germany were held to a 0–0 draw but concluded Group C play with a 1–0 win against Northern Ireland. In the round of 16, Germany faced Slovakia and earned a comfortable 3–0 win. Germany then faced off against [rivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany%E2%80%93Italy_football_rivalry) Italy in the quarter-finals. [Mesut Özil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesut_%C3%96zil) opened the scoring in the 65th minute for Germany, before [Leonardo Bonucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_Bonucci) equalised after netting a penalty thirteen minutes later. The score remained 1–1 after extra time, and Germany beat Italy 6–5 in a penalty shootout. It was the first time Germany had overcome Italy in a major tournament.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] The Germans lost to hosts France 2–0 in the semi-finals, their first competitive win against Germany in 58 years.[^cite_note-132]

Germany qualified for the [2017 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) after winning the 2014 World Cup, and won the last version of the Confederations Cup after a 1–0 win against [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup_Final) at the [Krestovsky Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krestovsky_Stadium) in [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md), Russia.[^cite_note-133]

#### Disappointment at the 2018 World Cup, 2018–19 UEFA Nations League and Euro 2020

Main articles: [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md), [2018–19 UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Nations_League), and [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020)



Fans in [Hamburg](13467.md) watching the match between Germany and South Korea

After winning all their qualifying matches and the Confederations Cup the previous year, Germany started their [2018 World Cup](13327177.md) campaign with a defeat to [Mexico](808402.md), their first loss in an opening match since the [1982 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-134] Germany defeated [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) 2–1 in their second game via an injury-time winner from [Toni Kroos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Kroos), but were subsequently eliminated following a 2–0 loss to [South Korea](1018627.md), their first exit in the first round since 1938 and first in the group stage since the format had been reintroduced in 1950. With this result Germany became the fifth defending champions to be eliminated in the group stages of the World Cup, following [Brazil in 1966](61629.md), [France in 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), [Italy in 2010](168079.md), and [Spain in 2014](656933.md).[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

Following the World Cup, Germany's struggles continued into the [inaugural UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Nations_League_A). After a 0–0 draw at home against [France](250197.md), they lost 3–0 against the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team)[^cite_note-137] and 2–1 in the rematch against France three days later; the latter result being their fourth loss in six competitive matches.[^cite_note-138] Due to results elsewhere, the relegation to League B was originally confirmed, but due to the overhaul of the format for the [2020–21 UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_UEFA_Nations_League), Germany were spared from relegation to League B.[^cite_note-139]

In March 2021, the DFB announced that Löw would step down as Germany's manager after [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020).[^cite_note-140] Later that month, Germany lost 2–1 at home to [North Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Macedonia_national_football_team) in the [2022 World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)), their first World Cup qualification defeat since [losing 5–1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Germany_v_England_football_match) to [England](9904.md) in [September 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)) and only their third in history.[^cite_note-141] On 25 May 2021, the DFB announced that former assistant manager [Hansi Flick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansi_Flick) would replace Löw as head coach.[^cite_note-142]

At Euro 2020 (delayed until 2021 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md)), Germany were drawn with World Cup champions France (to whom they lost 1–0) and reigning European champions Portugal (whom they defeated 4–2), with each group having only two guaranteed qualifiers for the next phase, plus a chance for the third-placed team. In the final group match, the Hungarians took the lead twice only to draw. Germany then lost 2–0 to England in the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_knockout_phase#England_vs_Germany), their first round of 16 exit in a major tournament since the 1938 World Cup.[^cite_note-143]

### Recent years (2021–present)

See also: [2022 FIFA World Cup qualification (UEFA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)), [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md), [UEFA Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024), [2026 FIFA World Cup qualification (UEFA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)), and [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md)



Germany vs Curaçao at the 2026 FIFA World Cup

Following Germany's disappointment at Euro 2020, [Hansi Flick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansi_Flick), former [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) manager, took over as coach of the national team. On 11 October 2021, Germany beat North Macedonia 4–0 to become the first team (outside of the hosts) to qualify for the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md) in Qatar.[^cite_note-144] In the [2022–23 Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Nations_League), Germany recorded their first-ever competitive win against Italy as the Germans beat the visitors 5–2. This was Germany's fourth game and first win in the league, however the Germans finished third in the group.[^cite_note-145]

At the 2022 World Cup, Germany were drawn into [Group E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E) with [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_men's_national_football_team), [Japan](993546.md) and [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team). The campaign started with a shock 2–1 defeat to Japan.[^cite_note-146] Germany drew 1–1 with Spain,[^cite_note-147] and then were knocked out of the World Cup in the group stage for the second consecutive tournament, despite a 4–2 win over Costa Rica, missing out on a place in the knockout stages on goal difference.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149]

After several poor performances following Germany's World Cup exit – including a 4–1 home loss to Japan – Flick was dismissed on 10 September 2023.[^cite_note-150] On 22 September 2023, [Julian Nagelsmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Nagelsmann) was named the new head coach.[^cite_note-151] After a rocky end to 2023, only winning one out of four games, Germany found itself back in form in the new year with a second victory against [France](250197.md) and the Netherlands, strengthening hopes for a successful [UEFA Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) campaign.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153]

As hosts of Euro 2024, Germany won the tournament's opening match against Scotland 5–1.[^cite_note-154] Germany went on to defeat [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) 2–0 in their second match to qualify for the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_knockout_stage).[^cite_note-155] After defeating [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) in the round of 16, Germany went on to the quarter-finals, where they were defeated 2–1 after extra time by [Spain](609825.md).[^cite_note-156]

Germany finished the group stage of the [2024-25 UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Nations_League_A#Group_3), further consisting of the Netherlands, [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) and [Bosnia and Herzegovina](1008974.md), undefeated, sealing their participation in the [knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Nations_League_A#Knockout_stage) with a 7–0 home win against Bosnia and Herzegovina.[^cite_note-157] Defeating [Italy](362466.md) on aggregate in the quarter-finals, Germany qualified for the [2025 UEFA Nations League Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Nations_League_Finals), where they lost 1–2 against [Portugal](576328.md) and 0–2 against France, finishing in fourth place.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160]

Having been drawn into Group A of the [2026 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_A) along with [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_national_football_team), [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_national_football_team) and [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_national_football_team), Germany lost the opener 0–2 against Slovakia before embarking on an 11-game winning streak, giving Northern Ireland their first home loss in three years and sealing qualification with a 6–0 win during the rematch with Slovakia.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163]

At the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md), Germany were drawn into [Group E](81660195.md) with [Curaçao](24862626.md), [Ecuador](1016797.md), and [Ivory Coast](1039856.md).[^cite_note-164] Germany beat Curaçao 7–1 and Ivory Coast 2–1 in the group stage, qualifying for the knockout stage for the first time since 2014.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166] The team faced [Paraguay](804025.md) in the round of 32, and after the match ended with a 1–1 draw, Germany were defeated 3–4 via penalty shoot-out, marking their first ever World Cup penalty shoot-out loss.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168] The elimination was regarded as one of the biggest World Cup knockout-stage upsets in terms of FIFA ranking difference, with a 31-place gap as Paraguay were ranked 41st and Germany were ranked 10th.[^cite_note-169] Head coach Julian Nagelsmann stepped down shortly afterwards.[^cite_note-170]

### Kits and crest

The [2006 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_World_Cup) saw a widespread public display of the [German national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Germany).

The national team's home kit has been traditionally a white shirt, black shorts, and white socks. The colours are derived from [the 19th-century flag of the North German State of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Prussia).[^cite_note-171] Since 1988, many of the home kit's designs incorporate details patterned after the modern German flag. For the 2014 World Cup, Germany's kit was white shorts rather than the traditional black due to FIFA's kit clashing rule for the tournament.[^cite_note-172] The away shirt colour has changed several times. Historically, a green shirt with white shorts is the most often used alternative colour combination, derived from the DFB colours – though it is often erroneously reported that the choice is in recognition of the fact that [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team), whose home shirts are green, were the first nation to play Germany in a friendly game after World War II. However, the first team to play Germany after WWII, as stated above, was actually Switzerland.[^cite_note-173] Other colours such as red, grey and black have also been used.

A change from black to red came in 2005 on the request of [Jürgen Klinsmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BCrgen_Klinsmann),[^cite_note-174] but Germany played every game at the 2006 World Cup in its home white colours. In 2010, the away colours then changed back to a black shirt and white shorts, but at the World Cup, the team dressed up in the black shorts from the home kit. The kit used by Germany returned to a green shirt on its away kit, but then changed again to red-and-black striped shirts with white stripes and letters and black shorts.

[Adidas AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) is the longstanding [kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kit_\(football\)) provider to the national team, a sponsorship that began in 1954 and is contracted to continue through 2026.[^cite_note-\:5-175] In the 70s, Germany wore Erima kits (a German brand, formerly a subsidiary of Adidas).[^cite_note-sportmuseum.de-176][^cite_note-auto-28] In March 2024, [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) was announced as the new kit sponsor after 70 years of Adidas, causing widespread criticism and backlash.[^cite_note-177]

As a common practice, three stars were added above the crest in 1996, symbolising Germany's World Cup titles in 1954, 1974, and 1990. In 2014, a fourth star was added after Germany were crowned world champions for the fourth time.

### Home stadium



[Olympic Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Stadium_\(Berlin\)) in Berlin

Germany plays its home matches among various stadiums, in rotation, around the country. They have played home matches in 43 different cities so far, including venues that were German at the time of the match, such as [Vienna](55866.md), Austria, which staged three games between 1938 and 1942.

National team matches have been held most often in [Berlin](3354.md) (46 matches), which was the venue of Germany's first home match (in 1908 against [England](9904.md)). Other common host cities include [Hamburg](13467.md) (34 matches), [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) (32), [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover) (28) and [Dortmund](149349.md). [Munich](19058.md) also hosted noteworthy matches including the [1974 World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup_Final).

### Media coverage

As of January 2025, Germany's broadcasts for friendlies rotate between [RTL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTL_\(German_TV_channel\)), [ARD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARD_\(broadcaster\)) and [ZDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZDF), while UEFA Euro and FIFA World Cup qualifying matches, UEFA Nations League games, and major tournaments are on ARD and ZDF.[^cite_note-187]

## Competitive record

Main article: [Germany national football team records and statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_national_football_team_records_and_statistics)

- 1930–1938 as  →  →  [Germany](250204.md)

- 1950–1990 as  [West Germany](250204.md)

- 1994–present as  [Germany](250204.md)

&#x20; **Champions**    **Runners-up**    **Third place**     Tournament played fully or partially on home soil &#x20;

Germany has won the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) four times, behind only [Brazil](149286.md)'s five.[^cite_note-GermanyWC-204] It has finished as runners-up four times.[^cite_note-GermanyWC-204] In terms of semi-final appearances, Germany leads with 13, two more than Brazil's 11, who have participated in every single tournament.[^cite_note-GermanyWC-204] From 1954 to 2014 (16 tournaments), Germany were one of the last eight teams, before being eliminated in the group stage in 2018.[^cite_note-GermanyWC-204] Germany has also qualified for every one of the 19 World Cups for which it has entered – it did not enter the inaugural competition in Uruguay in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup) for [economic reasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Depression), and were banned from the [1950 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup) as the DFB was reinstated as a FIFA member only two months after this tournament.

Germany has also won the [European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) three times, one less than [Spain](609825.md). [France](250197.md) and [Italy](362466.md), with two titles each, are the only other multiple winners. Germany finished as runners-up three times.[^cite_note-GermanyEC-205] The Germans have qualified for every European Championship tournament except for the first European Championship they entered in [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1968).[^cite_note-GermanyEC-205] For that tournament, Germany was in the only group of three teams and thus only played four qualifying games. The deciding game was a scoreless draw in [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania_national_football_team) which gave Yugoslavia the edge, having won in their neighbour country. The team finished outside the top eight in only three occasions: group stage eliminations in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000)[^cite_note-206] and [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004)[^cite_note-207] alongside a round of 16 exit in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020). In the other editions Germany participated in, they reached at least the semi-finals nine times, an unparalleled record in Europe.

*See also [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany_national_football_team) and [Saarland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saarland_national_football_team) for the results of these separate Germany teams, and [Austria](695226.md) for the team that was merged into the Germany national team from 1938 to 1945.*
