**Operation Urja Suraksha&#x20;**&#x20;([lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Energy protection') is a maritime security operation conducted by [India](14533.md) to protect the nation's energy supplies by escorting Indian-flagged ships and ships destined to reach Indian ports during the [2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis](82550763.md).

## Background

See also: [Prelude to the 2026 Iran war](82272270.md)

Under [Operation Sankalp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sankalp), the Indian Navy had embarked on a policy of constant maritime patrol within the Indian Ocean region as a means to safeguard the nation's shipping interests and trade routes. The [Persian Gulf](24761.md) plays a significant role in India's maritime trade with [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia), from where India imports much of its oil supplies.[^cite_note-2] In recent years, India has been reported to import over 80% of its petroleum and 90% of its Natural Gas via this strategic waterway.[^cite_note-\:16-3] With tensions arising in the middle east following a [United States buildup of ships](82251337.md) between December 2025 and March 2026, multiple Indian naval assets were deployed across the [Persian Gulf](24761.md), the [Red Sea](26191.md) , [Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca) and the central Indian Ocean, aimed at boosting naval diplomacy efforts.[^cite_note-4]

India had reduced purchases of Iranian oil amid sanctions pressure and enforcement actions targeting Tehran's "shadow fleet" from 2019, with Indian authorities in February 2026 seizing multiple vessels linked to illicit Iranian oil shipments as part of broader efforts to curb sanctioned energy trade and align with international sanctions enforcement initiatives. Many of these vessels have been sanctioned by the United States.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] India also remained alert on the potential for a week-long conflict, should such a situation arise.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

When the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md) began, an Indian Navy destroyer and a frigate had been deployed in the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md) and the [Gulf of Aden](58716.md) under Operation Sankalp. The destroyer, [INS *Surat* (D69)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Surat), had visited [Bahrain](18933277.md) to conduct joint training with elements from the [Bahraini Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Bahraini_Naval_Force) and the [US-CENTCOM](338482.md) under Combined Task Force-154 (CTF-154).[^cite_note-10] On 1 March, the [Prime Minister](24452.md)-chaired [Cabinet Committee on Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Committee_on_Security), the country's highest decision-making body on security and strategic affairs, had convened a meeting to review the West Asia conflict and its implications for India. As on 2 March, India was reported to be "closely monitoring" the situation while the warships could be immediately diverted for HADR operations, as per a senior defence official.[^cite_note-\:9-11]

On 10 March, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that the Indian government was evaluating options to escort the Indian-[flagged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagging_\(shipping\)) vessels that were stuck near the crisis-hit [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md). As of then, there were 36–38 such vessels with 1,100 sailors, all of which were safe with the crew members reported to be having sufficient provisions. The ships are complying to "enhanced safety protocols" including reporting to authorities at a higher frequency. Indian ship owners had requested the government for naval escorts. The NYT quoted [Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_\(Indian_Navy\)) P.C. Meena, a senior member of India's apex maritime authority.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

Unlike the passive monitoring protocols seen in previous years, the Navy implemented close-protection escorts for India-flagged liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) and crude oil tankers. Through [back-channel negotiations](3520902.md) led by the [National Security Advisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Advisor_\(India\)), [Ajit Doval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajit_Doval), India may have secured an informal guarantee from [Tehran](57654.md) for the safe passage of Indian vessels, provided they are escorted by Indian naval assets and do not carry coalition materiel.[^cite_note-\:9-11]

On 25 March, Operation Urja Suraksha was reported to have been initiated to secure oil supplies from the [Middle East](19323.md) to India.[^cite_note-\:112-15][^cite_note-\:102-16][^cite_note-17] The Navy provides critical precise instructions to each ship through the Iran-controlled route, closer to the Iran coast than the official shipping lanes.[^cite_note-18] Indian-headed tankers have taken routes that hug the Iranian coast before either dashing into international waters in [Chabahar](1658958.md) or sailing near Pakistani waters to avoid blockade from US Navy ships in the area.The Indian Navy vessels meet commercial shipping at a pre-arranged rendezvous point in the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md), safely away from both US and Iranian operational zones before escorting the ships to Indian ports.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

India was also among the five nations, including [China](5405.md), [Russia](25391.md), [Iraq](7515928.md) and [Pakistan](23235.md), whose ships were allowed to transit the Strait of Hormuz. This was announced by the [Foreign Minister of Iran](1166971.md), [Abbas Araghchi](21951274.md) on 26 March.[^cite_note-21]

## Indian ship transits

### 12–18 March

On 12 March, 28 Indian vessels were struck near the Strait of Hormuz including 24 on the west of the [choke point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choke_point) carrying 668 sailors and three more vessels to the east of the strait with 76 sailors on board.[^cite_note-\:5-22] Before the Indian Navy started the evacuation, the [Islamic Republic of Iran Navy](2160969.md) guided most of the Indian vessels that were allowed to cross the region.[^cite_note-\:4-23] Three warships were deployed in the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md) during 14–15 March. One warship provided escort to each commercial vessel crossing the strait while two more remained on standby. Two task forces were reportedly deployed under [Operation Sankalp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sankalp).[^cite_note-\:28-24][^cite_note-\:6-25][^cite_note-26] Indian Navy [MH-60R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MH-60R) helicopters were also seen engaged for [maritime patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_patrol).[^cite_note-\:27-27]

INS *Surat* also rescued eight Pakistani fishermen on 15 March who were stranded 170 nmi (310 km) east of Oman's [Port of Duqm](29716050.md) after their fishing vessel *Islami* had a fire accident.[^cite_note-28]

### 19–25 March

By 21 March, India was drawing up broader plans to evacuate Indian-flagged vessels from the region. If executed successfully, the operation would salvage 3 days of India's requirement of crude oil, natural gas and cooking gas. Two of the 22 ships are container ships. The remainder are energy carriers, including three LNG carriers, 10 LPG carriers and seven crude tankers. This assessment was compiled by the [Directorate General of Shipping](36399095.md).[^cite_note-39] On 25 March, a report indicated that three crude tankers owned by the Shipping Corporation of India, including a [VLCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VLCC) and an [Aframax](7922808.md), are heading towards India from the west of Hormuz. One of the vessels include *Desh Vaibhav* with a [deadweight tonnage](1346384.md) of 316,200 tonnes. Transponder signals identify the ships as "GOVT. OF INDIA SHIP".[^cite_note-40]

A report by *[The Times of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_India)* on 26 March, citing "sources in the defence establishments", indicated that the Indian Navy deployed over five frontline warships in the region under Operation Urja Suraksha. There were 20 more Indian ships to the west of the Strait of Hormuz. The Indian Navy keeps in contact with the India-bound vessels that are preparing to leave the Persian Gulf. Officials in DG Shipping also said that the Indian [destroyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_destroyer_classes_of_the_Indian_Navy) and [frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_frigates_of_India) would escort and guide an India-bound vessel out of the troubled waters from the Gulf of Oman with a layered security arrangement, after it clears the Strait with Iranian consent. An independent analyst assessed that Iran allows a vessel to leave the region following a "verification" which is conducted during "the ships’ transit inside Iranian waters". Reports also indicate the deployment of large numbers of [sea mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_mines) in the region and that the warships are also guiding the cargo vessels to navigate through the designated safe routes.[^cite_note-\:112-15][^cite_note-\:102-16]

LPG carriers *Jag Vasant* and *Pine Gas* were the first to escape the conflict zone with escorts under the operation. Reports also indicate the deployment of large numbers of [sea mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_mines) in the region and that the warships are also guiding the cargo vessels to navigate through the designated safe routes.[^cite_note-\:112-15][^cite_note-\:102-16] The Iranian Navy had guided the earlier Indian vessels that were allowed to cross the region.[^cite_note-\:19-41]

### 26 March–1 April

On 28 March, two additional LPG carriers, BW *Elm* and BW *Tyr*, reached the eastern part of the Strait according to shipping data from LSEG and Kpler.[^cite_note-\:132-47] The destinations of the ships were [Mumbai Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_Port) and [New Mangalore Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mangalore_Port).[^cite_note-\:14-48] This kept three more LPG tankers — *Jag Vikram*, *Green Asha* and *Green Sanvi* — among the 18 Indian ships in the western part of the Strait.[^cite_note-\:132-47]

### 1–7 April

As per Mukesh Mangal, Additional Secretary at the shipping ministry on 6 April, the 16 Indian vessels in the Persian Gulf included two LPG tankers (*Jag Vikram* and *Green Asha*;[^cite_note-\:132-47] one empty), six crude oil tankers (one empty), one LNG tanker, one chemical products tanker, three container ships, two bulk carriers and a [dredging vessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dredger). Further, Green Sanvi and Green Asha have 25 and 26 sailors, and 46,650 tonnes and 15,405 tonnes of LPG on board, respectively. They will reach Indian ports on 7 and 9 April, respectively.[^cite_note-\:26-54] The Indian Directorate General of Shipping issued a new advisory for Indian seafarers near Iran urging them stay onboard their ships and limit unnecessary shore travel while staying in continuous contact with their company representatives and both Indian and Iranian authorities.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

### 8–15 April

As reported on 11 April the Indian Navy and the multi-ministry task force is preparing to rescue 18 India-bound ships in the region. These include four LPG carriers, three LNG carriers and 11 crude oil tankers. Further, 5 of these are Indian-flagged vessels while the others are leased by Indian companies. There are 15 Indian-linked vessels in the Persian Gulf, three each in the Gulf of Oman and the Gulf of Aden and two in the Red Sea.[^cite_note-69] In a media query the Representative of Iran's Supreme Leader in India, Dr Abdul Majid Hakeem Ilah replied that the situation was not improving and that fewer ships would be allowed to pass through the strait.[^cite_note-70] The Iranian ambassador to India, Mohammad Fathali confirmed that Iran would not be imposing any tariffs on oil being exported to India, citing good ties with the nation.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] [Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_plc) also increased supplies to India, following a fall in ships arriving from [QatarEnergy.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QatarEnergy_LNG)[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

## Incidents

On 18 April 2026, report indicated that several merchant vessels had to turn back before crossing the Strait of Hormuz due to incidents of gunfire from [gunboats](274471.md) of the [IRGC Navy](4676345.md). *Jag Arnav* of the [Great Eastern Shipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Eastern_Shipping), carrying 2 million barrels of Iraqi crude oil and a bulk carrier heading to Al Jubail in Saudi Arabia,[^cite_note-111] was also fired upon while another Indian tanker, *Sanmar Herald*, was in the vicinity which was not targeted. Both the ships return to the Persian Gulf with both the crew and veles reportedly safe.[^cite_note-\:10-112][^cite_note-\:11-113] Amidst the firing incident, the crew from *Sanmar Herald* reported that they had also encountered the firing incident and were turning back.[^cite_note-114] India also summoned the Iranian envoy after the Incident. Meanwhile, the Indian Navy has two destroyers, a frigate and a tanker deployed to the east of the choke point and the force is trying to ascertain details of the incident. *Reuters* reported at least two tankers being targeted including the that of the Indian tanker and a report by the [UK Maritime Trade Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Maritime_Trade_Operations).[^cite_note-\:10-112][^cite_note-\:11-113] The Indian Navy issued an advisory urging seafarers to avoid escalation and steer clear of Iran's [Larak Islands](2832280.md).[^cite_note-115]

On 22 April, the IRGC had seized two vessels and directed them to Iranian ports. One of the ships, the Liberian-flagged *Epaminondas* was fired upon and interned by Iran. ## Analysis

An Informal group of government ministers who coordinated the operation led by [Indian Defence Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_\(India\)), [Rajnath Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajnath_Singh), informed media sources that India had evacuated the highest number of vessels than any other country from the Strait of Hormuz during the course of *Operation Urja Suraksha.* The vessels had collectively managed to bring close to 340 tonnes of fuel, sustaining 11 days of India's import requirements of petrol, diesel and fertilizers. Domestic production was also ramped up by forty-percent to meet the shortfall, enabling continuous food and energy security for India. A large majority of the imports had been prioritised for the pharma, agricultural, polymers, paints, metallurgical, and food processing sectors to prevent supply chain disruptions, though some private sector companies have been reported to have faced challenges for procurement.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

The Indian [Ministry for External Affairs](10302410.md) welcomed the [initial U.S.–Iran ceasefire agreement](82888027.md) and called for an early end to the conflict for “unimpeded” trade flow through the Strait of Hormuz.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] Foreign Ministry Spokesperson [Randhir Jaiswal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randhir_Jaiswal) maintained that no discussions had taken place on the issue or Iran imposing tolls at the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md) and called for free and safe navigation.[^cite_note-137] The Indian [Minister for Petroleum and Natural Gas](10345589.md) [Hardeep Singh Puri](30909065.md) visited [Qatar](8238258.md) and [India's External Affairs Minister](812043.md) [Dr S Jaishankar](40435868.md) visited the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) to negotiate on the future security of oil supplies to India.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139] India has also reacted negatively to Iranian demands to collect tolls after the cease in hostilities in early April 2026.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

In addition, Indian sailors who were imprisoned in Iran prior to the conflict on allegations of Fuel smuggling by the IRGC were released to Armenia on 27 March.[^cite_note-142] Indian sailors in Basra also evacuated themselves from the coflict zone.[^cite_note-143]

Representatives from the Indian Government attended a UK-hosted meeting on International trade and protection in the strait of Hormuz.[^cite_note-144] [Randhir Jaiswal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randhir_Jaiswal), representative of the Indian [Ministry for External Affairs](10302410.md) confirmed that India was also invited to join a Franco-British Initiative to conduct peaceful escort of ships in the Arabian sea and Persian Gulf by the two countries of 17 April 2026.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146] Defence Minister Rajnath Singh also refused to rule out any possibility that India would not participate in the diplomatic efforts.[^cite_note-147] The Indian Navy also designated Hormuz as a primary area of concern after renewed blockade in the area.[^cite_note-148]
