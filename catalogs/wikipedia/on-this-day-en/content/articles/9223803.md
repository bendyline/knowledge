**Kufra Airport** ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **AKF**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **HLKF**) is an airport serving [Al Jawf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jawf,_Libya), capital of the [Kufra District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kufra_District) in southeastern Libya. The airport is just east of the city.

## History

Kufra Airport began as Buma Airfield, built in the 1930s as a minor facility by the Italians. In early [World War II](32927.md), it provided an air link to [Italian East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_East_Africa) ([Ethiopia](187749.md), [Eritrea](17238590.md), and [Italian Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Somaliland)). It was captured by Free French units under General Leclerc on 1 March 1941 along with [Kufra Oasis](3337477.md).

Libyan Airlines operated a twice-weekly service from [Benghazi](204114.md) with [Boeing 727-200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727) for at least ten years prior to its suspension in 2004. For a couple of years leading up to the revolution [Tibesti Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibesti_Airlines?action=edit\&redlink=1) (later renamed [Air Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Libya)) operated a twice-weekly Benghasi – [Kufra](3337477.md) – [Khartum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khartum) service with a leased [British Aerospace 146](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace_146) aircraft. [Air Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Libya) also operated an intermittent weekly direct flight to Tripoli with a Boeing 727-200.

In July 2013, [Libyan Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Airlines) re-launched the Benghazi service that was suspended nine years earlier. During the same month a 2010 contract with Italian company Salini Costruttori to upgrade the airport's runway and taxiways (put on hold due to the [2011 civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Libyan_Civil_War)) was reactivated, with the works slated to take 20 months.[^cite_note-3]

Since the start of the [Sudanese civil war](73556699.md) in 2023, Kufra Airport has served as a major supply hub operated by the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) to support [Rapid Support Forces](43053874.md). In January 2026, the [Libyan National Army](35457665.md) announced a one-month closure of the airport for maintenance. The decision came amid a rift in relations between [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) and the United Arab Emirates.[^cite_note-4]

## Accidents and incidents

In a [tragedy at Kufra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tragedy_at_Kufra), in April 1942 a detachment of Squadron 16 of the [South African Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Air_Force) with three [Bristol Blenheim Mk. IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Blenheim_Mk._IV) aircraft was ordered to [Kufra](3337477.md) under the command of Major [J.L.V. de Wet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.L.V._de_Wet?action=edit\&redlink=1) to strengthen the garrison air defences. On the morning of 4 May 1942 the three aircraft took off on a [familiarization mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Familiarization_mission?action=edit\&redlink=1). They became lost and landed about 150 kilometres northeast of Kufra. A sandstorm thwarted ground and air search parties, and by the time the lost aircraft were located on 11 May only one of the total 12-man crew was alive.[^cite_note-5]

On 26 August 2008, a hijacked Sudanese [Boeing 737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) landed at Kufra Airport after having departed at [Nyala Airport](9265150.md), [Darfur](605837.md), with destination Khartoum. Earlier, Egyptian authorities had refused the plane to land in their national capital, Cairo.
