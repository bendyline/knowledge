A [constitutional crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_crisis) emerged in [Somalia](27358.md) on 30 March 2024, when the [Federal Parliament of Somalia](36817385.md) approved a series of constitutional amendments aimed at establishing a more "stable political system". These changes included a return to [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage), replacing the decades-old clan-based electoral system, and granting the president authority to appoint the prime minister without requiring parliamentary approval. Critics argued that the reforms significantly expanded executive power. In response, the semi-autonomous state of [Puntland Government](43532267.md) announced the following day that it was withdrawing its recognition and confidence in the [Federal Government of Somalia](36817379.md). Puntland called for a "mutually accepted Somali constitution that is subject to a public referendum" and declared that, until such a constitution is in place, it would operate independently.[^cite_note-auto1-10][^cite_note-auto-11]

On 28 November 2024, the semi-autonomous government of [Jubaland](38364812.md) suspended relations and cooperation with the federal government after the latter issued an arrest warrant for Jubaland President [Ahmed Madobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Madobe), accusing him of treason and revealing classified information to foreign entities.[^cite_note-12]

By mid-2025, planned district-level elections had been postponed, with pilot direct elections rescheduled for [Mogadishu](60154.md) in October 2025.[^cite_note-13] State-level elections originally expected in 2025 were also delayed, also national parliamentary and presidential elections term expired without planning upcoming elections for 2026.[^cite_note-14]

Executive President [Hassan Sheikh Mohamud](36973989.md), [both legislative chambers](36817385.md), and regional governments like [Galmudug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galmudug), [Hirshabelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirshabelle), and [South West State](483484.md) were all given unconstitutional extensions of their terms.

## Background

The [Provisional Constitution of the Federal Republic of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Constitution_of_the_Federal_Republic_of_Somalia), adopted in 2012, established a [federal system of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_system_of_government) consisting of two levels: the [Federal Government of Somalia](36817379.md) (FGS) and the semi-autonomous [Federal Member States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Member_States) (FMS). Intended to prevent the reemergence of an authoritarian central government, such as that under former President [Siad Barre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siad_Barre), and to address historical grievances by devolving powers to member states, the provisional constitution provides limited provisions for implementing federalism, forcing the FGS to negotiate with the FMS. Coupled with the ambiguity of the document and disputes over power and resources, has led to competing interpretations of federal governance. Some state governments favor greater autonomy, even asserting control of powers designated for the FGS, while others support a stronger central government. These disagreements have contributed to recurring tensions between the federal and state governments over the past two decades.[^cite_note-\:GGA-15]

### Amendments

On 28 May 2023, following a four-day conference held in Mogadishu, the National Consultative Council (NCC) proposed replacing the decades-old clan-based electoral system with universal suffrage by 2024. The proposal also recommended limiting elections to two main political parties, extending presidential terms from four to five years, and abolishing the position of prime minister in favor of a deputy president. The recommendations faced criticism from several political figures, including former prime ministers and former president [Sharif Sheikh Ahmed](5442210.md), who viewed the plan as a premature and power-driven initiative, calling for broader consultations. Puntland President [Said Abdullahi Deni](41669791.md), who failed to attend the meeting, opposed the electoral change as he considered it to be impossible due to the current situation. According to [The Africa Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Africa_Report), most Somalis expressed support for the shift, welcoming the prospect of gaining the right to vote for the first time in 53 years.[^cite_note-16]

On 19 March 2024, more than 70 members from both chambers of the [Federal Parliament of Somalia](36817385.md) announced their opposition to President [Hassan Sheikh Mohamud](36973989.md)'s constitutional reform plans, following a meeting held the previous evening. They argued that the current process resembled drafting an entirely new constitution rather than amending the existing provisional one.[^cite_note-17]

On 29 March 2024, [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) urged the parliament to reject any constitutional amendments that could weaken protections for [children's rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_rights), including a proposed amendment that would redefine a child as "to a person under the age of 15 years of maturity while the age of responsibility is 18 years, as defined in the law of the Federal Republic of Somalia," warning that it would contravene the [Convention on the Rights of the Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_the_Rights_of_the_Child).[^cite_note-18]

On 27 November 2023, a group of parliamentarians announced the formation of the National Correction Caucus, aimed at safeguarding Somalia's constitutional framework. Complaints included the proposed shift to a two-party system instead of a multiparty system and the potential increase in executive powers.[^cite_note-19]

On 30 March 2024, the federal parliament approved a series of constitutional amendments aimed at establishing a more "stable political system". These changes were a return to [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage), the extension of federal terms from four to five years, the introduction of a three-party limit, and granting the president the authority to appoint a prime minister without parliamentary approval. Critics argued that the reforms significantly expanded executive power. In response, Puntland announced the following day that it was withdrawing its recognition and confidence in the [Federal Government of Somalia](36817379.md). It called for a "mutually accepted Somali constitution that is subject to a public referendum" and declared that, until such a constitution is in place, it would operate independently.[^cite_note-auto1-10][^cite_note-auto-11]

## Events

Amid rising tensions with the FGS, Puntland signed a new cooperation agreement with [Ethiopia](187749.md) on 3 April 2024. The following day, the FGS expelled the Ethiopian ambassador in Mogadishu and ordered the closure of two Ethiopian consulates, the one in Puntland and the one in [Somaliland](51425.md), accusing Ethiopia of "repeatedly violating Somalia's national, territorial independence". In response, Puntland declared that "Mogadishu has failed to eradicate terrorist groups and spread governance across the country and now it is trying to impose its decisions on a peaceful region that does not run with its orders. It cannot close a consulate in Puntland."[^cite_note-20]

### [Puntland](233569.md)



Puntland Press Release

In October, the National Consultative Council (NCC) convened federal and state officials to discuss democratisation and security. During the meeting, the federal government reaffirmed its commitment to advancing electoral reforms. The NCC proposed a one-year extension of the terms of all state presidents until the universal suffrage system could be implemented, which was accepted by the leaders of [Hirshabelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirshabelle), [Galmudug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galmudug), and [South West](483484.md) states. The proposal and constitutional amendments were rejected by Jubaland President [Ahmed Mohamed Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Mohamed_Islam), known as Ahmed Madobe, who believed they would undermine regional autonomy.[^cite_note-\:GGA-15]

### [Jubaland](38364812.md)

Main articles: [2024 Jubaland presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Jubaland_presidential_election) and [Jubaland crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubaland_crisis)

On 10 November, Jubaland suspended cooperation with the federal government, citing constitutional violations.[^cite_note-21] Defying the federal government, Jubaland amended its own constitution to override the two-term limit set by the FGS, extended presidential terms from four to five years, and proceeded with the [presidential election on 25 November 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Jubaland_presidential_election), in which Madobe won a third term.[^cite_note-\:GGA-15] On 27 November, the [Banadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banaadir) Regional Court in [Mogadishu](60154.md) issued an arrest warrant for Madobe, accusing him of treason, violating the constitutional framework, leaking classified information to foreign actors, and undermining national unity.[^cite_note-\:GGA-15][^cite_note-22]

The following day, the government of Jubaland suspended relations with the federal government.[^cite_note-23] Furthermore, a court in [Kismayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kismayo), the regional capital of [Jubaland](38364812.md), announced a \$100,000 [reward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounty_\(reward\)) for the arrest of [Somali President](516101.md) [Hassan Sheikh Mohamud](36973989.md), accusing him of treason, undermining national unity, and conspiring with [Al-Shabaab militia](8726463.md).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

On 11 December 2024, [Somali forces](27366.md) launched a failed attack on [Jubaland regional forces](64948303.md) in the Marnani area near [Ras Kamboni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamboni), having[^cite_note-\:1-26]been deployed by the [federal government](36817379.md) from [Mogadishu](60154.md)'s [Banadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banaadir) region to [Lower Juba](185145.md) region in an attempt to throw out [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_Jubaland) [Ahmed Madobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Mohamed_Islam).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-\:2-28] At the end of the battle, Jubaland captured hundreds of Somali soldiers and took back Ras Kamboni, Lower Juba.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

### South West State crisis

During increased tensions, a rebel group known as the South West Liberation Front claimed to have captured [Qansahdhere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qansahdhere) on 14 March.[^cite_note-SWLF-3] On 17 March 2026, the South West State severed ties with the federal government over issues related to Somalia's ongoing constitutional changes after allegations by [State President](65910790.md) [Abdiaziz Laftagareen](63356863.md) of an attempted coup against him by federal forces.[^cite_note-31] The South West State accused the government of an "invasion" after clashes broke out in several towns between South Western forces and pro-government militias. While government forces captured the *de jure* state capital of [Baraawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baraawe), the South West State retained control over the *de facto* capital [Baidoa](1648752.md).[^cite_note-32]

On 28 March 2026, Abdulaziz Laftagreen was re-elected hours after lawmakers chose Ali Said Fiqi as speaker[^cite_note-Laftagreen_re-elect-33], in a process the Federal Government of Somalia dismissed as an illegal self-appointment that defies constitutional agreements.[^cite_note-34] Tensions stem from South West State's suspension of ties with Mogadishu over disputes on mandate extensions and election methods, with regional states preferring clan-based systems. Laftagareen pledged to focus on development and unity, as opposition leaders offered congratulations and the UN called for dialogue to prevent violence[^cite_note-Laftagreen_re-elect-33]. On 30 March, the [Somali National Army](3972951.md) took control of [Baidoa](1648752.md), with South West President Laftagareen announcing his resignation, and [Ahmed Mohamed Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Mohamed_Hussein?action=edit\&redlink=1) being announced as the head of the new transitional government.[^cite_note-35]

### 2026 term extension

The term of the [Federal Parliament](36817385.md) expired on 14 April, and the federal government has failed to ensure its timely succession. The delay causes a lot of tension because there is no clear plan for the [upcoming elections](67568552.md), which come after a string of problems in the legislature.[^cite_note-36]

On 14 May, heavy fighting erupted in [Baidoa](1648752.md) between [Somali](27358.md) [federal government forces](3972951.md) and troops loyal to former [South West State](483484.md) president [Abdiaziz Laftagareen](63356863.md), following disputes over regional elections  and the end of [president](516101.md) [Hassan Sheikh Mohamud](36973989.md)'s [term](68486735.md) on 15 May. Multiple sources confirm two senior [Somali military](27366.md) officers from the [60th Division](61288579.md) have been killed in a roadside ambush.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] later day [International representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_community), excluding those from the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and the [United States](3434750.md), meet in Mogadishu and deem the [administration of Hassan Sheikh Mohamud](68486735.md) as the [de facto](43594.md) Somali government. Operational Policy 7.30, a framework typically applied to countries that have lost statehood or where a government assumes power outside the country’s constitutional framework, will be utilized.[^cite_note-39] Opposition coalition [Somali Future Council](82855773.md) said the disputed [president](516101.md) Hassan Sheikh Mohamud can no longer perform key presidential functions, including military oversight or dismissing government officials. They propose that [parliamentary speaker](31425297.md) [Aden Madobe](9322839.md) serve as [acting president](352134.md) until elections scheduled for 30 June. The president has lost full executive authority following the expiry of his mandate on 15 May.[^cite_note-40]

On 3 June, at least 13 people were killed and 189 others injured,[^cite_note-41] following [heavy fighting in Mogadishu](83375609.md) near Dabka Junction, which spread to populated neighbourhoods including [Howlwadaag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howlwadaag,_Mogadishu), [Abdiaziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdiaziz,_Mogadishu), [Hodan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodan,_Mogadishu), [Warta Nabadda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warta_Nabada,_Mogadishu), and parts of [Kaaraan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karan,_Mogadishu).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Leaders of the opposition [Somali Future Council](82855773.md) reported being attacked by forces loyal to disputed president Hassan Sheikh Mohamud ahead of planned anti-government protests.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

On 17 August, multiple combatants were killed after Somali federal forces and South West Liberation Front clashed in South West State.[^cite_note-46] Two civilians were also killed.[^cite_note-47] Following militias loyal to former state president Abdiaziz Laftagareen entered the regional capital, [Baidoa](1648752.md), challenging two battalions of the Somali National Army loyal to Somali President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud.[^cite_note-48]

## Reactions

On 20 November 2024, the [international community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_community) expressed concern over the escalating tensions between the FGS and the Jubaland administration,[^cite_note-49] urging both sides to engage in inclusive dialogue to resolve ongoing electoral disputes and calling on Jubaland to restore relations with the central government to prevent further escalation.[^cite_note-50]

The breakaway state of Somaliland distanced itself from the controversy as it does not consider itself a part of Somalia.[^cite_note-garoweonline-51]
