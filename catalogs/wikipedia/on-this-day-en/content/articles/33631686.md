# Copper mining in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

&#x20;**Copper mining in the Democratic Republic of the Congo** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Extraction du cuivre en République démocratique du Congo*) mainly takes place in the [Copper Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_Belt) of the southern [Katanga Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanga_Province) of the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md).[^cite_note-Mbendi-1]

Not all copper deposits contain significant [cobalt](24580536.md), but nearly all cobalt in the country is sourced from copper deposits. The DRC produces about 63% of the world's cobalt, with about 80% from industrial copper mines, and the remaining 20% gathered by [artisanal mining](26285065.md).[^cite_note-2]

## Geology

The Katanga, or Shaba, copperbelt in the DRC is a belt about 70 kilometres (43 mi) wide and 250 kilometres (160 mi) long between [Lubumbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubumbashi) and [Kolwezi](1331550.md) formed in rocks of the [Katanga Supergroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanga_Supergroup). There are at least 72 economic deposits of copper/cobalt (estimated more than 200) and four large mining centers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELaznicka2010441-3] Taken together, the DRC and Zambian copper belts are the second largest global reserve of copper, about 1/3 the size of the [Chilean reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_mining_in_Chile).[^cite_note-MetorexV-4]

The DRC copper belt includes some of the highest-grade [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) deposits in the world. In some reserves the grades are above 5%. The ore also has high grades of [cobalt](24580536.md) and may hold 34% of the world's cobalt reserves. There are large deposits that have yet to be explored using modern technology, so the size of the reserves may be understated. Demand is growing, led by China.[^cite_note-MetorexV-4]

## History

Prehistoric mines in the Katanga belt have been found at Dikuluwe near [Musonoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musonoi), Tenke-Fungurume, [Kambove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kambove_mines), [Luishia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luishia_Mine), Ruashi and Etoile, and at the Naviundo and Luano archaeological sites.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBissonVogel2000116-5] Monseigneur de Hemptinne watched [Yeke people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeke_people) working at Dikuluwe as late as 1924. They worked in the dry season and stopped when the first rains arrived. The mining camp was near a stream where [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet) could be planted. Women and children collected [malachite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malachite) from the surface, while men used iron picks to excavate pits and shafts, using fire to crack the rocks when needed. The mines were between 10 metres (33 ft) and 15 metres (49 ft) deep with galleries up to 20 metres (66 ft) long. The ore would be sorted and then taken to a nearby stream for concentration before being smelted.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBissonVogel200092-6]

Commercial mining began in the 1920s, with a forcibly recruited workforce. The [Union Minière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Mini%C3%A8re_du_Haut_Katanga) constantly struggled to prevent recruits fleeing from the mines, sometimes taking refuge in Angola.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHigginson198953-7] Workers were mistreated and suffered badly from disease brought on by the working conditions. The lowest death rate at the Panda, Lubumbashi, Kisanga and Musonoi work sites in 1928 was four men per month.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHigginson198956-8] Today, mine worker exploitation is still prevalent and instances of slave labor, and especially [child labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_labor), are still observed in the mining industry according to the 2014 [U.S. Department of Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Labor)'s *[List of Goods Produced by Child Labor or Forced Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Goods_Produced_by_Child_Labor_or_Forced_Labor)*.

Between 1970 and 1988 copper metal production was roughly constant at between 400,000 and 500,000 tonnes. Production then dropped steeply to under 50,000 tonnes annually between 1992 and 2001. Since then, production has steadily grown, reaching about 300,000 tonnes in 2008.[^cite_note-MetorexV-4]

In 2005, proceeds from an oil sale to [Glencore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glencore), an Anglo–Swiss multinational commodity trading and mining company, were seized as fraudulent gains as part of an investigation into corruption in the country (Allen-Mills 17 June 2008).[^cite_note-AustralianNews20080617-9] In the course of the Congo events, Nikanor was merged into Katanga in late 2007 in a transaction valued at US\$3.3 billion.[^cite_note-10]

The government of the DRC began to review about 60 mining contracts in 2008, and started to release their findings to the mining companies in February 2008. For example, TEAL, a subsidiary of [African Rainbow Minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Rainbow_Minerals), was asked by letter to submit their feasibility study for their [Kalumines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalumines) property to identify the real contribution of each of the parties "in order to achieve a fair attribution of shares". The letter said the feasibility study should present a planning of "realisation of social actions with a visible impact". It also said that the state-owned [Gécamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9camines) should actively participate in the daily management of the operation.[^cite_note-11]

In August 2009, the DRC government revoked [First Quantum Minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Quantum_Minerals)' (FQM) license to operate the [Kolwezi tailings project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolwezi_tailings_project), alleging "unreasonable behaviour" in negotiations to change the contract.[^cite_note-12] First Quantum had spent \$750 million on acquiring and developing the property. First Quantum took out an action against the DRC government in the [International Chamber of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Chamber_of_Commerce) Court of Arbitration.[^cite_note-13] In May 2010 a Congolese court ruled that FQM's [Lonshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonshi_Mine) and [Frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier_Mine,_Katanga) copper mines had been awarded illegally and that they should revert to state-owned [Sodimico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodimico).[^cite_note-14] According to FQM the ruling was due to FQM's decision to contest the expropriation of their Kolwezi project, which was later sold to the Kazakh mining company [Eurasian Natural Resources Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Natural_Resources_Corporation).[^cite_note-Reuters20110817-15]

## Companies

The largest shareholders in most copper mines in the DRC are foreign corporations, with the Congolese state-owned [Gécamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9camines) holding a minority stake in most projects. In recent years, many Chinese companies have become major holders in various copper mines in the DRC.

### China Nonferrous Metal Mining Group

Main article: [China Nonferrous Metal Mining Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Nonferrous_Metal_Mining_Group)

China Nonferrous Metal Mining Group (CNMC) is the majority owner of the [Deziwa mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deziwa_mine) and Ecaille C mine.

### China Railway

Main article: [China Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Railway)

[China Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Railway), or its subsidiary, [China Overseas Engineering Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Overseas_Engineering_Group), is a partner in the [Luishia mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luishia_mine), the [Kalumbwe Myunga mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalumbwe_Myunga_mine?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the Sicomines venture at [Dikuluwe Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dikuluwe_Mine) and Mashamba West.

### CMOC Group Limited

Main article: [CMOC Group Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMOC_Group_Limited)

[CMOC Group Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMOC_Group_Limited), formerly known as China Molybdenum, entered the DRC in 2016 with the purchase of the [Tenke Fungurume Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenke_Fungurume_Mine). It also purchased the [Kisanfu mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisanfu_mine?action=edit\&redlink=1) which is under construction. Both mines were formerly held by [Freeport-McMoRan](588316.md).

### Gécamines

Main article: [Gécamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9camines)

[Gécamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9camines) (La Générale des Carrières et des Mines), is a state-owned mining company in the DRC. Its principal products are copper (which often accounted for 50% of export earnings), [cobalt](24580536.md) and [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc).[^cite_note-16] Copper mines in which Gécamines has a major interest include [Kambove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kambove_mines), [Kipushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kipushi_mine) and [Kolwezi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolwezi_mine). Gécamines also owns a copper smelter at [Lubumbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubumbashi) and a hydrometallurgical plant at [Shituru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shituru).[^cite_note-17]

The Industrial Development and Mining Corporation of Zaire (Société de développement industriel et minier du Zaire - SODIMIZA) is an operating company for copper and its derivatives in Katanga. The company also operates cobalt and zinc. It was created in 1967 and acquired by [Gécamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9camines) in 1987.

The Kababankola Mining Company (KMC) is a joint-venture between Gécamines (20%) and [Tremalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremalt) (80%) with the objective of exploiting Copper and cobalt.[^cite_note-18]

### Glencore

Main article: [Glencore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glencore)

The Swiss company [Glencore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glencore) owns a number of large copper mines, particularly after acquiring [Katanga Mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanga_Mining) as a wholly owned subsidiary. Currently, Glencore owns majority stakes in *Kamoto Copper Company SARL* (KCC) and *DRC Copper and Cobalt Project SARL* (DCC), which run several copper/cobalt mines, as well as a *Mutanda Mining SARL*, which runs the [Mutanda Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutanda_Mine).

[Katanga Mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanga_Mining) produces copper and cobalt at the [Kamoto Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamoto_Mine), which also includes the Kamoto concentrator, the Luilu metallurgical plant, the Kamoto underground mine and two oxide open pit resources in the Kolwezi district. A joint venture of Katanga Mining (75%) and Gécamines (25%) began mining [Tilwezembe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilwezembe), an open-pit copper and cobalt mine, in 2007.[^cite_note-19] As of 2011 Katanga Mining was 74.8% owned by [Glencore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glencore).[^cite_note-20]

### Jinchuan Group

Main article: [Jinchuan Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinchuan_Group)

[Metorex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metorex), based in [Johannesburg](58893.md), South Africa, has assets in the DRC that include the existing [Kinsenda Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinsenda_Mine), the new [Ruashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruashi_Mine) copper and cobalt mine, and the Dilala East and Lubembe deposit greenfields sites.[^cite_note-21]

Copper Resources Corporation is a subsidiary of Meterox. As of November 2011 it held a 92.5% interest in the Hinoba-an Porphyry Copper project in the Philippines, and a 75% interest in Miniere de Musoshi et Kinsenda (MMK).[^cite_note-BloombergCopperRes-22] MMK in turn owns the flooded Kinsenda and [Musoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musoshi_Mine) copper mines in Katanga. MMK was formerly a subsidiary of [Forrest Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forrest_Group). As of 30 November 2005 it became a subsidiary of Copper Resources Corporation.[^cite_note-23] MMK also holds the Lubembe high-grade deposit.[^cite_note-BloombergCopperRes-22]

[Jinchuan Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinchuan_Group) acquired Metorex in 2012, making it a fully owned subsidiary.

### Zhejiang Huayou Cobalt

Main article: [Zhejiang Huayou Cobalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang_Huayou_Cobalt)

[Zhejiang Huayou Cobalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang_Huayou_Cobalt) holds stakes in the [Luiswishi mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luiswishi_mine) and [Kolwezi copper mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolwezi_copper_mine?action=edit\&redlink=1).

### Other

[Afrimines Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrimines_Resources) SPRL, based in Democratic Republic of the Congo, owns various mining resources.[^cite_note-24] In May 2008 [Tiger Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Resources) announced that it had secured the rights to explore several new and highly prospective tenements in Katanga in partnership with Afrimines Resources and Katanga Minerals Holdings.[^cite_note-25]

The [Eurasian Natural Resources Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Natural_Resources_Corporation) (ENRC) has a significant presence, particularly with the [Mukondo Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukondo_Mine) asset, since its takeover of the [Central African Mining and Exploration Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Mining_and_Exploration_Company) (CAMEX).[^cite_note-26]

## Mines

Copper mines in the Democratic Republic of the Congo are concentrated in the [Copperbelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copperbelt), in the provinces of [Haut-Katanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haut-Katanga_Province) and [Lualaba](4225850.md), both part of the historical province of [Katanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanga_Province) until 2015. The ownership of most mines is structured as a [joint venture](538782.md) split between a foreign company and the DRC state-controlled miners [Gécamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9camines) and [Sodimico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodimico).
