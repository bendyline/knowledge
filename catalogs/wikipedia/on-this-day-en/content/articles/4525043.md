The **Saudi Pro League** (**SPL**), also known as the **[Roshn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roshn) Saudi League** (**RSL**) for sponsorship reasons, is the top-tier professional [association football](10568.md) league in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md). It is the highest level of the [Saudi football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_football_league_system) and is governed by the [Saudi Arabian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian_Football_Federation).

## History

### Early history (1957–2022)

The origins of the Saudi top division date back to the establishment of the His Majesty's League in 1957, which continued until 1974. It was followed by the [1974–75 Saudi Categorization League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_Saudi_Categorization_League), a transitional season before the introduction of the Saudi Premier League. The first official season of the Saudi Premier League was the [1976–77 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976%E2%80%9377_Saudi_Premier_League).

The league used a [round-robin tournament](939742.md) format from its first season until the [1989–90 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_Saudi_Premier_League). Following changes by the [Saudi Arabian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian_Football_Federation), the league was merged with the [King's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Cup_\(Saudi_Arabia\)) and the Golden Box format was introduced. The format included a final knockout stage involving the top four teams from the regular season to decide the champion.

The round-robin format returned in the [2007–08 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_Saudi_Premier_League). The competition was renamed the Saudi Pro League in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Saudi_Pro_League), while records from previous league formats continued to be maintained separately.

The league has used several names throughout its history. For the [2018–19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Saudi_Pro_League) and [2019–20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Saudi_Pro_League) seasons, it was named the Prince Mohammad bin Salman League in honour of [Crown Prince](40373823.md) [Mohammed bin Salman](38689358.md).[^cite_note-1]

In 2019, the General Sports Authority (later transformed into the [Ministry of Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Sport_\(Saudi_Arabia\))) launched the Sports Clubs Support Strategy to improve governance, infrastructure, and fan engagement within Saudi clubs.[^cite_note-2]

In 2022, [Roshn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roshn), owned by the [Public Investment Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Investment_Fund), signed a title sponsorship agreement with the league. The competition was renamed the Roshn Saudi League from the [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Saudi_Pro_League).[^cite_note-3]

### Saudi Pro League expansion (2023–present)

In June 2023, the [Public Investment Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Investment_Fund) acquired 75% stakes in [Al-Nassr](2535118.md), [Al-Hilal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hilal_SFC), [Al-Ittihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ittihad_Club_\(Jeddah\)), and [Al-Ahli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ahli_Saudi_FC) as part of wider development plans linked to [Saudi Vision 2030](50299103.md).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

The league attracted global attention in 2023 after [Cristiano Ronaldo](623737.md) joined Al-Nassr. Other international players, including [Neymar](15326144.md), [Karim Benzema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karim_Benzema), [Sadio Mané](34758489.md), and [N'Golo Kanté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Golo_Kant%C3%A9), later joined Saudi clubs during the same period.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

The privatization process continued with several clubs changing ownership. In 2025, [Al-Kholood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Kholood_Club) became the first Saudi football club to be fully owned by foreign investors after being sold to The Harburg Group, led by American businessman [Ben Harburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Harburg).[^cite_note-9]

The league's development has received international attention and criticism. Some observers have described the investments as [sportswashing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportswashing), while Saudi officials have presented the reforms as part of sports development and economic diversification plans.[^cite_note-10]

## Competition format

### Competition

*As of the [2025–26 Saudi Pro League](80037951.md)*

There are 18 clubs in the Saudi Pro League. During the course of a season (usually from August to May), each club plays every other club twice (a double round-robin system), once at their home stadium and once at their opponent's, for a total of 34 games. Teams receive three points for a win, one point for a draw, and no points for a loss. Teams are ranked on the league table based on points first, then head-to-head record in case of a tie, followed by goal difference.

Each club is allowed a maximum of 25 players, including up to 10 foreign players. Of these 10 foreign players, 8 can be of any age, and 2 must be under 20 years old at the time of signing. The remaining 15 players must be Saudi nationals, with a maximum of 5 players allowed to come from the youth sector if needed or wanted.

For each league game, managers can select up to 8 foreign players to be included in the matchday squad.

### Promotion and relegation

A system of promotion and relegation exists between the Saudi Pro League and [Saudi First Division League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_First_Division_League). The three lowest-placed teams in the Saudi Pro League are relegated to the First Division, in the first division the top two teams are promoted to the Pro League directly, a [play-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playoffs#Association_football) system[^cite_note-15] to determine the third team to be promoted alongside the two. Teams placed between third and sixth position take part in the promotion play-offs. The fifth-placed would face the fourth, while the sixth-placed team would face the third. The final would be single-legged, hosted by the higher-placed team.
