An **extreme heat warning** is a notice issued by the [National Weather Service](286989.md) of the [United States](3434750.md) when extremely dangerous heat is expected or occurring. Usually within 24 to 36 hours. The criteria for this warning varies by location.[^cite_note-nws-1]

Extreme heat can pose a threat to human life and other animals, through conditions such as [heat stroke](24999087.md), [heat exhaustion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_exhaustion), [dehydration](155056.md), and other heat-related illnesses.[^cite_note-nws-1]

On March 10, 2025, the National Weather Service updated the Excessive Heat Warning and Watch, and renamed it to the Extreme Heat Warning and Watch, based on a public survey results in 2018.[^cite_note-2]

## Criteria

Local offices, particularly those where extreme heat is less frequent or in areas with deserts or mountainous terrain, often have their own criteria; which can vary significantly between different parts of the United States.[^cite_note-nws-1]

## Dangers

Due to the extreme illnesses that can occur, athletes should limit their physical activity when an extreme heat warning is issued. According to the National High School Sports Related Injury Surveillance System, High School RIO, an estimated number of 51,943 excessive heat illnesses occurred in nine of the high school sports observed between 2005, 2011, and 2020.
