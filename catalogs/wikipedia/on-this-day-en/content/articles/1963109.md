[Association football](10568.md) is the most popular sport in [France](5843419.md).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] In 2024, 53% of people in France declared an interest in football, with 26% being very interested.[^cite_note-4] The [French Football Federation](682002.md) (FFF, Fédération Française de Football) is the national governing body and is responsible for overseeing all aspects of association football in the country, both professional and amateur.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-nytimes.com-6] The federation organizes the [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France) and is responsible for appointing the management of the [men's](250197.md), [women's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_women's_national_football_team), and [youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_national_youth_football_team) national football teams in [France](5843419.md). The federation gives responsibility of [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) and [Ligue 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_2) to the [Ligue de Football Professionnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_de_Football_Professionnel) (LFP) who oversee, organize, and manage the country's top two leagues. The French Football Federation also supervises the [overseas departments and territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_departments_and_territories_of_France) leagues and hosts football club [AS Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC), a club based in the independent sovereign state of [Monaco](19261.md). In 2022, the FFF had 2.1 million licensees, 1.8 million players and 14,000 registered clubs, the second highest number of registered players in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Europe) after [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Germany).[^cite_note-fffstats-1]

The first [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football) club was introduced to France in 1863 as described in a newspaper article by *[The Scotsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scotsman)*, which stated "A number of English gentlemen living in Paris have lately organised a football club... The football contests take place in the [Bois de Boulogne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bois_de_Boulogne), by permission of the authorities and surprise the French amazingly."[^cite_note-7]

Today, football in France is especially being played successfully by people of non-European origin, in particular [people of Subsaharan origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people_in_France) and [people from North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabs_in_France) who are overrepresented in the [Banlieue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banlieue).[^cite_note-8] The importance of players of non-European origin is also reflected in the composition of the French World Cup winning team where only six of the 23 players were of European descent.[^cite_note-9]

France is a football superpower; by its footballers playing around the world, according to the [CIES Football Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIES_Football_Observatory), in 2023, France is the second country in the world behind [Brazil](633554.md), with the most footballers playing abroad with 1,033.[^cite_note-10] According to [Statista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statista), of the estimated 130,000 professional football players worldwide, 6,368 originated from France, the third highest number in the world after [Brazil](633554.md) (10,694) and [Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Mexico) (9,223).[^cite_note-11]

## League system

See also: [French football league system](3480022.md)

### Ligue de Football Professionnel



The [Stade Vélodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_V%C3%A9lodrome), home of [Olympique de Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille), before a match against [Paris Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_F.C.) in 2015.

The top two divisions of French football, [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) and [Ligue 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_2), are governed by the [Ligue de Football Professionnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_de_Football_Professionnel). The league organizes and manages both leagues and is also responsible for the 36 professional football clubs that contest football in France (18 in Ligue 1 and 18 in Ligue 2).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

Ligue 1 is the French professional league for football clubs. It is the country's primary football competition and serves as the top division of the [French football league system](3480022.md). Contested by 18 clubs, it operates on a system of [promotion and relegation](648049.md) with Ligue 2. Ligue 1 is one of the top national leagues, currently ranked [fifth in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_coefficient) behind the English [Premier League](11250.md), Italian [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A), Spanish [La Liga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Liga) and German [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga). Ligue 1 was inaugurated on 11 September 1932 under the name *National* before switching to *Division 1* after a year of existence. The name lasted until 2002 before switching to its current name. The [current champions of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_French_football_champions) are [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md), who won a record fourteenth title in [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Ligue_1).

Ligue 2 is the second division of French football. Contested by 20 clubs, it operates on a system of [promotion and relegation](648049.md) with the [Championnat National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Championnat_National). The league was created in 1934, a year after Ligue 1 and consisted of 23 clubs that were divided into two groups, Nord and Sud.

### Championnat National

The Championnat National is the third division of French football. Though the league has several clubs that are members of the Ligue de Football Professionnel, it is not governed by the organization primarily because of the LFP's refusal to divide its profits into smaller shares, so they can collaborate with the many amateur clubs in the league to help them become professional. The French Football Federation moderates the league, which was founded in 1993 under the name *National 1*. Contested by 20 clubs, it operates on a system of [promotion and relegation](648049.md) with the [Championnat de France amateur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Championnat_de_France_amateur).

### Championnat National 2

The Championnat National 2 is the fourth division of French football and normally features 72 football clubs. Most clubs that participate in the league are amateur clubs, but a small number of clubs are semi-professional. The CFA consists of 72 clubs spread into 4 parallel groups of 18. It is open to the best reserve teams in France and amateur clubs in France, although only the amateur clubs are eligible for promotion to the Championnat National. The highest-placed amateur team in each pool are promoted, replaced by the 4 lowest-placed in the Championnat National.

### Championnat National 3

The Championnat National 3 is the 5th division in French football and normally consists of 168 teams in 12 groups of 14 organised to align with the [regional leagues](3480022.md#Regional_leagues). The twelve teams (both amateur and reserves of professional teams in higher divisions) that top their league are promoted to Championnat National 2. Relegation from Championnat National 3 is defined by both position in the group and the region the club belongs to. Normally, one club is relegated to each regional league that feeds that group.

### Lower divisions

Some of regional leagues are organised and managed by the *Ligue du Football Amateur*. The LFA, under the watch of the French Football Federation, is responsible for administering and federating the actions of the regional and district leagues.

### Women's football

Main article: [Women's football in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_football_in_France)

### Première Ligue

Main article: [Première Ligue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premi%C3%A8re_Ligue)

The [Première Ligue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premi%C3%A8re_Ligue) is the top league for [women's football](191466.md) clubs in France. The Première Ligue is ranked the best league in Europe according to [UEFA](32332.md). The league was originally created in 1918 by [Alice Milliat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Milliat) and ran for 12 successful seasons until [women's football was banned in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bans_of_women's_association_football#France). The league was refounded in 1974 and officially reinstated in 1975.

It is contested by 12 clubs. The league operates on a system of [promotion and relegation](648049.md) with lower leagues and is governed by the [French Football Federation](682002.md). The league is known as D1 Arkema. [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais_F%C3%A9minin) has won the most league titles, with 18.

### Lower divisions

[Division 2 Féminine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_2_F%C3%A9minine) is the second-tier of French women's football, contested by 12 clubs. Relegation is to [Division 3 Féminine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_3_F%C3%A9minine), contested by 24 fully professional clubs split into two groups. The fourth level is Régional 1 Féminine.

### Overseas leagues

The leagues based in the [overseas departments and territories of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_departments_and_territories_of_France) are run by their respective associations under the watch of the French Football Federation. Under the rules of the FFF, clubs in the leagues are allowed to participate in confederation competitions based on their regional locations. For example, the champion of the [Réunion Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9union_Premier_League) is allowed inclusion into the [CAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_Champions_League).

## Cup competitions



[Coupe de la Ligue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_la_Ligue) trophy.

The most important cup competition in France is the [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France). However, several other national cups are targeted at clubs at different levels.

- The [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France) is the premier [knockout](939680.md) cup competition in French football. It is open to all amateur and professional [football](10568.md) clubs in France, including clubs based in the [overseas departments and territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_departments_and_territories_of_France). The final is played at the [Stade de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_de_France) and, during the [2016–17 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Coupe_de_France), celebrated its 100th anniversary.

- The [Coupe de la Ligue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_la_Ligue) was the second major cup competition in France. It was known outside France as the French League Cup and was a [knockout](939680.md) league cup competition organised by the Ligue de Football Professionnel. Unlike the Coupe de France, it was only open to professional clubs who were members of the LFP. The competition was discontinued in 2020 to prevent fixture congestion.

- The [Trophée des Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troph%C3%A9e_des_Champions) is played each July or January as a one-off match between the Coupe de France winners and the Ligue 1 champions.

- [Regional](58846.md) amateur leagues of France organise their own cup competitions that are run by the French Football Federation. For example, the Coupe Bourgogne only features amateur clubs that are based in the region of [Burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgundy).

- Men's youth cups include the [Coupe Gambardella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_Gambardella), [Coupe Nationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_Nationale), and the Coupe Fédérale. The Coupe Gambardella cup competition held between the under-19s of the French football clubs. The Coupe Nationale holds dual competitions for the under-13 and under-15 teams of football clubs, while the Coupe Fédérale holds a national cup competition for under-16 teams.

- The [Coupe de l'Outre-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_l'Outre-Mer) is a football cup competition that was created in 2008. It was designed to have the national football teams of the overseas territories compete against each other

## National teams



[Zinedine Zidane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinedine_Zidane), member of the national team from 1994 to 2006.

See also: [France national football team](250197.md), [France women's national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_women's_national_football_team), and [France national youth football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_national_youth_football_team)

The [France national football team](250197.md) represents France in international football.[^cite_note-nytimes.com-6] France was one of the four European teams that participated at the [inaugural World Cup in 1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup) and are one of eight national teams to have won the competition, which they did in 1998 when they hosted the Cup, defeating [Brazil](149286.md) 3–0 in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_Final). They won their second world title 20 years later, after defeating [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) 4–2 in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) of the [2018 edition](13327177.md) in Russia. France also won two [European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) in [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1984) and [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000), and hosted the tournament on three occasions, including their victorious 1984 campaign. Following France's [2001 Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) victory, they became the first national team to win the three most important men's titles organised by [FIFA](11049.md): the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), the [FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup), and the [Olympic Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics). This would be followed with [Argentina](454699.md) and [Brazil](149286.md)'s victories at the Summer Olympics in 2004 and 2016. France additionally went on to win a [UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League) title in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Nations_League_Finals).



[Eugénie Le Sommer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eug%C3%A9nie_Le_Sommer), member of the national team since 2009.

The [France women's national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_women's_national_football_team) represents the country in international [women's football](191466.md). France initially struggled on the international stage failing to qualify for three of the first [FIFA Women's World Cups](244862.md) and the six straight [UEFA European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Championship) before reaching the quarter-finals in the [1997 edition of the competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_UEFA_Women's_Championship). However, since the beginning of the new [millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium), France have become a mid-tier national team and one of the most consistent in [Europe](32332.md), having qualified for their first-ever FIFA Women's World Cup in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) and reaching the quarter-finals in the last three consecutive European Championships. They also hosted the [2019 Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup), reaching the quarter-finals.

The [France national youth football teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_national_youth_football_team) consists of age-specific national teams beginning with the [France national under-16 football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_national_under-16_football_team) and ending with the [France national under-21 football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_national_under-21_football_team). Since the coaching tenure of [Aimé Jacquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aim%C3%A9_Jacquet), there is an unwritten rule among [senior national team](250197.md) coaches that players called up to the national team must have had prior international experience with the under-21 team.

[Lilian Thuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilian_Thuram), from [Guadeloupe](12343.md), is the nation's second-most capped male football player.

As an overseas department of the [French Republic](5843419.md), each national team is not a member of [FIFA](11049.md), therefore they are not eligible to enter the [World Cup](11370.md). However, since inhabitants of the overseas departments are French citizens, players are eligible to play for the [France national football team](250197.md). Guadeloupe, Martinique, Saint Martin, and French Guiana are associate members of [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) and are full members of the [Caribbean Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Football_Union), and are thus eligible for all competitions organized by both, while Réunion are associate members of [CAF](715265.md). Indeed, according to the status of the FFF (article 34, paragraph 6): *"\[...]Under the control of related continental confederations, and with the agreement of the FFF, those leagues can organize international sport events at a regional level or set up teams in order to participate to them."*

A special rule of the [CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) only allows players to join the team if they have not played for France during the previous five years. On the other hand, any player joining the team is allowed to join the France national team afterward with no time restrictions.

The use of [overseas department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_departments_and_territories_of_France) players has been extremely beneficial for the France national team. [Lilian Thuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilian_Thuram) and [Bernard Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Lama), who were born in [Guadeloupe](12343.md) and [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique), respectively, were a part of the winning team at the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup). Also on the team were [Thierry Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thierry_Henry) and [Bernard Diomède](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Diom%C3%A8de), who, though born in metropolitan France, were descendants of parents from overseas departments. Currently, [Florent Malouda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florent_Malouda) (French Guiana), [William Gallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gallas), [Mikaël Silvestre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mika%C3%ABl_Silvestre), [Michaël Ciani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micha%C3%ABl_Ciani) (Guadeloupe), [Nicolas Anelka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Anelka) (Martinique), and [Guillaume Hoarau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Hoarau) and [Florent Sinama Pongolle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florent_Sinama_Pongolle) (Réunion) are members of the national team who either hail from or whose families hail from the overseas departments.
