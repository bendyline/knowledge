# Suspicious Russia-related deaths since 2022



[Ravil Maganov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravil_Maganov), chairman of the national oil company [Lukoil](1027881.md), fell from a [Kremlin Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin_Hospital) window under suspicious circumstances, according to reports: CCTV cameras had been "turned off for repairs", President [Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putin) was visiting the hospital the same day, and associates did not believe he was suicidal.



[Roman Starovoyt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Starovoyt), the [Russian Minister of Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transport_\(Russia\)), was dismissed by President [Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putin) on 7 July 2025 and was found dead on the same day.

[^cite_note-transport-minister-1]

Since the beginning of 2022, numerous Russia-related people, particularly businessmen and officials, have died under what some sources suggest were suspicious circumstances.[^cite_note-wave-2][^cite_note-Jesbr-3][^cite_note-dw-4][^cite_note-de_Graaf-5] Incidents include individuals who have suspiciously [fallen out of windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defenestration), killed themselves, and died unexpectedly in accidents. Many businessmen were linked to energy companies such as [Gazprom](320489.md) and [Lukoil](1027881.md). The phenomenon has been called "sudden Russian death syndrome" or "sudden oligarch death syndrome", a play on [sudden arrhythmic death syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudden_arrhythmic_death_syndrome).[^cite_note-atla2912-6][^cite_note-lbc-7]

Many analysts have suggested some deaths are [political assassinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_terrorism) by the Russian state, including the [Federal Security Service](385990.md), related to greater pressure from the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md) and ensuing [international sanctions](70323386.md) against Russia. Others have argued more really are suicides, and some could be killings by competing influential clans to wipe out competitors.

## Analysis

On 3 June 2022, the Dutch [NOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Omroep_Stichting) news network described the phenomenon as "a grim series of Russian billionaires, many from the [oil and gas industries](10450931.md), who have been found dead under unusual circumstances since early this year. The first was on 30 January, when 60-year-old Leonid Shulman, transport chief for Russian energy giant [Gazprom](320489.md), was found dead in the bathroom of his country house in the Leningrad region. Beside his body was a suicide note."[^cite_note-de_Graaf-5] On 6 July 2022, [CNN Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN_Portugal) described the group as "millionaires with direct or indirect links to the Kremlin found dead in a mystery scenario since the beginning of the year".[^cite_note-cnnpt-9-8] It referred to a previous investigation by *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today)*, which concluded that "38 Russian businessmen and oligarchs close to the Kremlin died in mysterious or suspicious circumstances between 2014 and 2017."[^cite_note-cnnpt-9-8] The phenomenon has been called "sudden Russian death syndrome" or "sudden oligarch death syndrome", a play on [sudden arrhythmic death syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudden_arrhythmic_death_syndrome).[^cite_note-atla2912-6][^cite_note-lbc-7] A number of them died by falling from heights, including possibly [being thrown out of windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defenestration).[^cite_note-lbc-7]

Some commentators ruled out suicides or poor health.[^cite_note-voxAnalysis-9] Other commentators, including [Fiona Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiona_Hill_\(presidential_advisor\)) and [Mark Galeotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Galeotti), are skeptical of such conspiracies.[^cite_note-voxAnalysis-9] They point out the deaths are not necessarily all connected, and that it is far more likely some really are suicides, and some could be killings by competing influential clans to wipe out competitors without a centralized Kremlin effort.

The suicide rate in Russia is the third-highest in the world,[^cite_note-voxAnalysis-9] and similar trends were noted in 2020 about doctors who had treated [COVID-19](63030231.md) patients falling from high windows.[^cite_note-voxAnalysis-9] Suicides could be further increased especially in the Russian business community due to substantial pressure from the war in Ukraine and international sanctions.[^cite_note-voxAnalysis-9]

### Calls for further investigations

Friends and families of the deceased Russian businessmen generally found it "unthinkable" that they killed themselves – and in some cases also their wives and children – and have demanded an independent investigation into the mysterious deaths.[^cite_note-de_Graaf-5] [Igor Volobuyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Volobuyev?action=edit\&redlink=1), the Ukrainian-born ex-vice-chairman of [Gazprombank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazprombank), who left Russia during the outbreak of the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) and joined the [Freedom of Russia Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Russia_Legion),[^cite_note-novaya-10][^cite_note-2022jun14Russia_Legion-11] said in an interview with *[The Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Insider_\(website\))* that he thought that his former colleague Vladislav Avayev's alleged murder of his family and subsequent suicide had been staged: "Why? That is difficult to say. Perhaps he knew something and posed some kind of danger."[^cite_note-de_Graaf-5][^cite_note-volobuyev-12] Likewise, Sergey Protosenya's son, who was not in Spain when his parents and sister were found dead in [Lloret de Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloret_de_Mar), stated his father was not the perpetrator ("my father is not a murderer"), but that his parents and sister were murdered by someone else.[^cite_note-de_Graaf-5] Protosenya was the former CEO of gas giant [Novatek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novatek), which published a statement saying he was "a real family man", and called on the Spanish authorities to conduct a thorough and impartial investigation.[^cite_note-de_Graaf-5]

Businessman and critic of the Russian federal government [Bill Browder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Browder) has opined that Putin is personally ordering executions of influential business leaders in critical sectors whom he feels will not be [yes-men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sycophancy) and intimidates their successors with threats of death or violence.[^cite_note-browder-13] In Russian *mokroye delo* or “wet work” is the name given to the business of assassination with people told to commit suicide or they and their families will receive a visitation.[^cite_note-atla2912-6]

According to an investigative report by *[Novaya Gazeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novaya_Gazeta)*, some of the deaths may be connected to large scale accounting fraud by Gazprom executives, who may have funneled money to a network of businesses owned by friends and family members with ties to the [FSB](385990.md) and Russian military.[^cite_note-novaya-2-14]
