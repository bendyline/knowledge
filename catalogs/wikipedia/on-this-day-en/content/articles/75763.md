[Canada](5042916.md) has ten provinces and three territories that are sub-national [administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division) under the jurisdiction of the [Canadian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Canada). In the 1867 [Canadian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation), three provinces of [British North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America)—[New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick), [Nova Scotia](21184.md), and the [Province of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Canada) (which upon Confederation was divided into [Ontario](22218.md) and [Quebec](7954867.md))—united to form a [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation), becoming a fully [independent country](62450.md) over the next century. Over its history, Canada's international borders have changed several times as it has added territories and provinces, making it the [world's second-largest country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area) by area.

The major difference between a Canadian [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province) and a [territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory) is that provinces receive their power and authority from the *[Constitution Act, 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Act,_1867)* (formerly[^cite_note-1] called the *[British North America Act, 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America_Acts)*). Territories are [federal territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_territory) whose territorial governments have powers delegated to them by the [Parliament of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada). Powers are divided between the [Government of Canada](616255.md) (the federal government) and the provincial governments by the *Constitution Act*, either exclusively or concurrently. A change to the division of powers between the federal government and the provinces requires a [constitutional amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amendments_to_the_Constitution_of_Canada). A similar change affecting the territories can be performed unilaterally by the government or [Parliament of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada).

In modern [Canadian constitutional theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_Canada), the provinces are considered to be [co-sovereign](28791.md), based on the division of responsibility between the provincial and federal governments within the *Constitution Act, 1867*; each province thus has its own representative, the [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_governor_\(Canada\)), of the Canadian [Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_in_the_Canadian_provinces). The territories are not sovereign but have their authorities and responsibilities [devolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution#Canada) from the federal level; as a result, each has a [commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioner#Canadian_territories) who represents the federal government.

## Territories

There are three territories in Canada. Unlike the provinces, the territories of Canada have no inherent [sovereignty](28791.md) and have only those powers delegated to them by the [federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] They include all of mainland Canada north of [latitude 60° north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60th_parallel_north) and west of [Hudson Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Bay), and all islands north of the Canadian mainland, from those in [James Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bay) to the [Queen Elizabeth Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_Islands). They cover 40% of Canada's land, but contain only 0.3% of the population.[^cite_note-20]

Another territory, the [District of Keewatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Keewatin), existed from October 7, 1876, to September 1, 1905, when it rejoined the Northwest Territories and became the [Keewatin Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keewatin_Region). It occupied the area that is now the [Kenora District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenora_District) of Ontario, northern Manitoba, and mainland Nunavut.[^cite_note-f365-21] The government of Keewatin was based in Winnipeg, Manitoba. The territory did not have any representation in federal parliament.

The Northwest Territories has varied in size substantially through the years. In 1880, it covered 7.1 million km<sup>2</sup> (2.7 million sq mi), and from 1925 to 1999, it covered 3.4 million km<sup>2</sup> (1.3 million sq mi).[^cite_note-22]

## Population

Main article: [Population of Canada by province and territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_of_Canada_by_province_and_territory)



Canada's population from the 2016 census by province/territory

The vast majority of Canada's population is concentrated in areas close to the [Canada–US border](630260.md). Its four largest provinces by area ([Quebec](7954867.md), [Ontario](22218.md), [British Columbia](3392.md) and [Alberta](717.md)) are (with Quebec and Ontario switched in order) its most populous. Together they account for 86% of Canada's population. The territories (the [Northwest Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territories), [Nunavut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunavut) and [Yukon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon)) account for over a third of Canada's area but are only home to 0.3% of its population, which skews the national [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) value.[^cite_note-\:1-26]

Canada's population grew by 5.0% between [the 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Canadian_census) and [2016 censuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Canadian_census). Except for [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick), all territories and provinces increased in population during this time. In terms of percent change, the fastest-growing province or territory was [Nunavut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunavut) with an increase of 12.7% between 2011 and 2016, followed by [Alberta](717.md) with 11.6% growth, while New Brunswick's population decreased by 0.5%.[^cite_note-27]

Generally, Canadian provinces have steadily grown in population along with Canada. However, some provinces such as Saskatchewan, Prince Edward Island and Newfoundland and Labrador have experienced long periods of stagnation or population decline. Ontario and Quebec have always been the two biggest provinces in Canada, with together over 60% of the population at any given time. The population of [the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada) relative to Canada as a whole has steadily grown over time, while that of [Atlantic Canada](71095.md) has declined.[^cite_note-\:1-26]

## Territorial evolution

Main articles: [Territorial evolution of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_Canada) and [Former colonies and territories in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_colonies_and_territories_in_Canada)

When Canada was formed in 1867 its provinces were a relatively narrow strip in the southeast, with vast territories in the interior. It grew by adding British Columbia in 1871, P.E.I. in 1873, the British Arctic Islands in 1880, and Newfoundland in 1949; meanwhile, its provinces grew both in size and number at the expense of its territories.

The territorial evolution of the borders and the names of Canada's provinces and territories



"[O Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Canada) we stand on guard for thee". Stained glass, Yeo Hall, [Royal Military College of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_College_of_Canada). Featuring arms of the Canadian provinces and territories as of 1965.

Ontario, Quebec, New Brunswick, and Nova Scotia were the original provinces, formed when three [British North American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America) colonies federated on July 1, 1867, into the [Dominion of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_Canada#Kingdom_and_Dominion) and by stages began accruing the indicia of sovereignty from the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-Ajzenstat2003-28] (Ontario and Quebec had been united as the [Province of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Canada) from 1841 to 1867.) Over the next six years, Manitoba (1870), British Columbia (1871), and Prince Edward Island (1873) joined as provinces.[^cite_note-Ajzenstat2003-28]

[The British Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown) had claimed two large areas, known as [Rupert's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert's_Land) and the [North-Western Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-Western_Territory), northwest of the original Dominion and assigned them to the [Hudson's Bay Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_Company). In 1870, the company relinquished its claims for £300,000 (CND\$1.5 million), assigning the vast territory to the government of Canada.[^cite_note-OlsonShadle1996-29] The area entered confederation as the Province of Manitoba and the North-West Territories.[^cite_note-OlsonShadle1996-29]

The North-West Territories encompassed all of current [northern](79987.md) and [western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada) except for the [British Arctic Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Arctic_Territories) in the [Arctic Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Archipelago), the [Colony of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_British_Columbia_\(1866%E2%80%931871\)), and the original Province of Manitoba, a small area in the south of today's province (which expanded to its present size in 1912).[^cite_note-Gough2010-30] It also included the northern two-thirds of Ontario and Quebec.

In 1880, the British claims to the Arctic islands were transferred to Canada, adding to the size of the North-West Territories. In 1898, the Yukon Territory, renamed "Yukon" in 2003, was carved from the area surrounding the [Klondike gold fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klondike_Gold_Rush). In September 1905, a portion of the North-West Territories south of the 60th parallel north became the provinces of Alberta and Saskatchewan.[^cite_note-Gough2010-30] In 1912, the boundaries of Quebec, Ontario, and Manitoba were expanded northward: Manitoba's to the 60° parallel, Ontario's to Hudson Bay and Quebec's to encompass the [District of Ungava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Ungava).[^cite_note-Atlas-31]

In 1869, the people of Newfoundland voted to remain a [British colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) over fears that taxes would increase with Confederation, and that the economic policy of the Canadian government would favour mainland industries.[^cite_note-32] In 1907, Newfoundland acquired dominion status.[^cite_note-Clarke2010-33] In the middle of the [Great Depression in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_Canada), Newfoundland underwent a prolonged [economic crisis](2878852.md), and the legislature turned over political control to the [Newfoundland Commission of Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_Commission_of_Government) in 1933.[^cite_note-FriesenHarrison2010-34] Following [Canada's participation in the Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_in_World_War_II), in a [1948 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Newfoundland_referendums), a narrow majority of Newfoundland citizens voted to enter into Confederation, and on March 31, 1949, Newfoundland became Canada's tenth province.[^cite_note-Blake1994-35] The province was renamed Newfoundland and Labrador in 2001.[^cite_note-Shelley2013-36]

[Bermuda](3460.md), the last British North American colony,[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] which had been somewhat subordinated to Nova Scotia, was one of two [Imperial fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_fortress) colonies in British North America –  the other being Nova Scotia, and more particularly the city of Halifax.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] Halifax and Bermuda were the sites of the [Royal Navy](26061.md)'s [North America and West Indies Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_America_and_West_Indies_Station) main bases, dockyards, and Admiralty Houses (called variously the *River St. Lawrence and Coast of America and North America and West Indies Station*, the *North America and Newfoundland Station*, the *North America and West Indies Station*, and the *America and West Indies Station*). The squadron of the station was based at [Royal Naval Dockyard, Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_Dockyard,_Halifax), during the summers and [Royal Naval Dockyard, Bermuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_Dockyard,_Bermuda), in the winters. In the 1820s, Bermuda, which was better located to control the [Atlantic Seaboard of the United States](89126.md), impossible to attack over land, and almost impregnable against attack over water, became the main base year round.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

A large [British Army garrison in Bermuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_Garrison), which fell under the [commander-in-chief in Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-Chief,_North_America#Commanders-in-Chief,_Maritime_provinces_1783%E2%80%931875), existed to defend the colony as a naval base, and to prevent it becoming as useful a base to the navy of an adversary, and to support amphibious operations throughout the region, such as the [Chesapeake campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake_campaign) during the American [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] Bermuda was consequently the most important British naval and military base in the Americas.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

[Canadian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) resulted in the [Canadian Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Militia) becoming responsible for the defence of [the Maritimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Maritimes), the abolition of the [British Army](4887.md)'s commander-in-chief there, and the reduction of British military forces in the Maritimes to a small garrison for the protection of the Halifax dockyard, which was withdrawn when the dockyard was handed over to the Dominion government in 1905 for use by the new Canadian naval service. Britain retained control of Bermuda as an imperial fortress, with the governor and commander-in-chief of Bermuda (a military officer previously ranking between lieutenant-colonel and major-general) becoming a lieutenant-general termed a *[general officer commanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_officer_commanding)* and the Bermuda garrison becoming a command in its own right.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

Bermuda was consequently left out of Canadian confederation. It retained naval links with Halifax. The [state church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_state) (or *established church*), the [Church of England](5955.md), continued to place Bermuda under the [bishop of Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Newfoundland) until 1919. Bermuda also remained linked to the Maritimes under the Methodist and Roman Catholic churches.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

In 1903, resolution of the [Alaska Panhandle Dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_boundary_dispute) fixed British Columbia's northwestern boundary,[^cite_note-Laxer2010-59] the first of only two provincial reductions in Canada. In 1927, the second reduction occurred when a [boundary dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador#Boundary_dispute) between Canada and the Dominion of Newfoundland saw [Labrador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador) (part of the Dominion of Newfoundland) enlarged at Quebec's expense. (In 1949, this land returned to Canada as part of the province of Newfoundland.)[^cite_note-Cukwurah1967-60] In 1999, Nunavut was created from the northern and eastern portions of the Northwest Territories.[^cite_note-School2013-61][^cite_note-Nuttall2012-62]

The three territories are the most sparsely populated region in Canada, combined covering 3,921,739 km<sup>2</sup> (1,514,192 mi<sup>2</sup>) in land area.[^cite_note-StatsCan-4] They are often referred to as a single region, the North, for organizational and economic purposes.[^cite_note-Development2002-63] For much of the Northwest Territories' early history, it was divided into [several districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_the_Northwest_Territories) for ease of administration.[^cite_note-WaldmanBraun2009-64] The [District of Keewatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_Keewatin) was created as a separate territory from 1876 to 1905, after which, as the [Keewatin Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keewatin_Region), it became an administrative district of the Northwest Territories.[^cite_note-ForsythMuller2001-65] In 1999, it was dissolved when it became part of Nunavut and, under different boundaries, was renamed the [Kivalliq Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kivalliq_Region).

## Government

Main articles: [Federalism in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_Canada), [Monarchy in the Canadian provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_in_the_Canadian_provinces), [Office-holders of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office-holders_of_Canada), and [Legislative assemblies of Canadian provinces and territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_assemblies_of_Canadian_provinces_and_territories)

Theoretically, provinces have a great deal of power relative to the federal government, with jurisdiction over many [public goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_good) such as health care, education, welfare, and intra-provincial transportation.[^cite_note-Mahler1987-66] They receive "[transfer payments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_payment)" from the federal government to pay for these, as well as exacting their own taxes.[^cite_note-Peach2007-67] In practice, however, the federal government can use these transfer payments to influence these provincial areas. For instance, in order to receive healthcare funding under [Medicare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_\(Canada\)), provinces must agree to meet certain federal mandates, such as universal access to required medical treatment.[^cite_note-Peach2007-67]

Provincial and territorial legislatures have no second chamber like the [Canadian Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Canada). Originally, most provinces had such bodies, known as [legislative councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_council), with members titled councillors. These upper houses were abolished one by one, Quebec's being the last in 1968.[^cite_note-Maclure2003wt-68] In most provinces, the single house of the legislature is known as the [legislative assembly](222426.md). The exceptions are Nova Scotia and Newfoundland and Labrador, where the chamber is called the [House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism), and Quebec where it is called the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Quebec).[^cite_note-Tidridge2011nm-69] Ontario has a legislative assembly but its members are called [members of the Provincial Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Provincial_Parliament_\(Canada\)) or MPPs.[^cite_note-Pinto2012-70]

The legislative assemblies use a procedure similar to that of the [House of Commons of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Canada). The head of government of each province, called the [premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_\(Canada\)), is generally the head of the party with the most seats.[^cite_note-Barnhart2004-71] This is also the case in Yukon, but the Northwest Territories and Nunavut have no political parties at the territorial level.[^cite_note-Zellen2009iu-72] The King's representative in each province is the [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_governor_\(Canada\)).[^cite_note-Tidridge2011po-73] In each of the territories there is an analogous [commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioner#Canadian_territories), but they represent the federal government rather than the monarch.[^cite_note-PikeMcCreery2011fr-74]

## Provincial political parties

Most provinces have rough provincial counterparts to major federal parties. However, these provincial parties are not usually formally linked to the federal parties that share the same name.[^cite_note-Cross2011-79] For example, no provincial Conservative or Progressive Conservative Party shares an organizational link to the federal [Conservative Party of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_of_Canada), and neither do provincial Green Parties to the [Green Party of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_Canada).

Provincial New Democratic Parties, on the other hand, are fully integrated with the federal [New Democratic Party](19283982.md)—meaning that provincial parties effectively operate as sections, with common membership, of the federal party.

The [Liberal Party of Canada](149536.md) shares such an organizational integration with [Atlantic Canada](71095.md) provincial Liberals in [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick_Liberal_Association), [Newfoundland and Labrador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Newfoundland_and_Labrador), [Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Scotia_Liberal_Party), and [Prince Edward Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_Island_Liberal_Party). Other provincial Liberal parties are unaffiliated with their federal counterpart.[^cite_note-Cross2011-79]

Some provinces have provincial political parties with no clear federal equivalent, such as the [Saskatchewan Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan_Party).

The provincial political climate of Quebec is different: the main split is between [sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_sovereignty_movement), represented by the [Parti Québécois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parti_Qu%C3%A9b%C3%A9cois) and [Québec solidaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qu%C3%A9bec_solidaire), and [federalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_Quebec), represented primarily by the [Quebec Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Liberal_Party).[^cite_note-Gagnon2000pm-80] The [Coalition Avenir Québec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_Avenir_Qu%C3%A9bec), meanwhile, takes an abstentionist position on the question and does not support or oppose sovereignty.

## Ceremonial territory

The [Canadian National Vimy Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_National_Vimy_Memorial), near [Vimy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vimy), Pas-de-Calais, and the [Beaumont-Hamel Newfoundland Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaumont-Hamel_Newfoundland_Memorial), near [Beaumont-Hamel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaumont-Hamel), both in France, are ceremonially considered Canadian territory.[^cite_note-Wilson2012-83] In 1922, the French government donated the land used for the Vimy Memorial "freely, and for all time, to the Government of Canada the free use of the land exempt from all taxes".[^cite_note-84]

In 1921, the site of the Somme battlefield near Beaumont-Hamel site was purchased by the people of the [Dominion of Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Newfoundland).[^cite_note-Wilson2012-83] These sites do not enjoy [extraterritorial status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterritoriality) and are subject to French law.

## Proposed provinces and territories

Main article: [List of proposed provinces and territories of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_proposed_provinces_and_territories_of_Canada)

Since Confederation in 1867, there have been several proposals for new Canadian provinces and territories. The [Constitution of Canada requires an amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amendments_to_the_Constitution_of_Canada) for the creation of a new province.[^cite_note-amendment-85] The creation of a new territory requires only an [act of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_parliament#Canada), a [legislatively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_procedure) simpler process.[^cite_note-Nicholson1979-86]

In late 2004, Prime Minister [Paul Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Martin) surprised some observers by expressing his personal support for all three territories gaining provincial status "eventually". He cited their importance to Canada as a whole and the ongoing need to assert [sovereignty in the Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_claims_in_the_Arctic), particularly as [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) could make that region more open to exploitation, leading to more complex [international waters disputes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Passage#International_waters_dispute).[^cite_note-87]
