The **2026 Saxony-Anhalt state election** was held on 6 September 2026 to elect the 9th [Landtag of Saxony-Anhalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag_of_Saxony-Anhalt). It took place two weeks before the state elections in [Berlin](74073501.md) and in [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](70944924.md).

This election drew significant national and international attention due to the performance of the [far-right](18526492.md) [Alternative for Germany](38708229.md) (AfD), including the possibility of it getting an absolute majority. The AfD is subject to a [firewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firewall_against_the_far-right_in_Germany) policy as all other major parties refuse to cooperate with it; its state branch is under surveillance by the [Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Office_for_the_Protection_of_the_Constitution), which classified it as a "confirmed right-wing extremist movement" in 2023.

The AfD secured a plurality of the vote, doubling its support to 43.8%, but failed to gain a majority of seats. The governing [Christian Democratic Union](60584.md) (CDU) lost more than half of its previous vote share with 17.2%. The [Social Democratic Party](60585.md) (SPD), [Alliance 90/The Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_90/The_Greens), and [The Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Linke) each got about 9%. The [Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahra_Wagenknecht_Alliance) (BSW) barely passed the 5% [election threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_threshold), while the [Free Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party) (FDP) lost representation.

[Voter turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout) was an all-time high of 77.8%, up from 60% in 2021. The AfD candidates won 38 of the 41 [single-member constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-member_constituencies), with the remaining three won by The Left. The size of the parliament matches the nominal 83 seats for the first time as the AfD's result is large enough that there are no [overhang seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhang_seat). A [tactical voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_voting) campaign supported the SPD and Greens, which were polling at or below the election threshold in the summer, and both ended with small gains compared to 2021.

## Background

In Saxony-Anhalt, the CDU has been the largest political party since the [2002 Saxony-Anhalt state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Saxony-Anhalt_state_election).

In the [2021 Saxony-Anhalt state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Saxony-Anhalt_state_election), the CDU emerged again as the strongest party. The AfD experienced slight losses but remained the second-largest force. Contrary to pre-election predictions of a tight race between the CDU and the AfD, the CDU secured a 16-percentage-point lead over the AfD.[^cite_note-DW-1] The Left also recorded losses, achieving 11% of the vote. The SPD suffered its worst result in Saxony-Anhalt's history. With 6.4% of the vote, the FDP returned to the state parliament after a decade-long absence. The Greens secured 5.9%, retaining parliamentary representation.[^cite_note-DW-1]

The AfD has remained subject to Germany's [firewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firewall_against_the_far-right_in_Germany) policy, under which the country's major political parties exclude cooperation with the party. Its state branch is also monitored by the [Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Office_for_the_Protection_of_the_Constitution) (BfV). The branch was classified as a "confirmed right-wing extremist movement" in 2023, a designation that has continued to be reflected in subsequent assessments by the authorities. The classification followed the branch's designation as a suspected extremist case in 2021 and was based on information collected by the state constitutional protection authority concerning positions it considered incompatible with the principles of human dignity, democracy, and the rule of law.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

Due to the small majority and potential fragility of a [grand coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_coalition_\(Germany\)) between the CDU and the SPD, the FDP was included and formed a black-red-yellow coalition, with the same political colors as the German flag and thus known as the "[Germany coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_coalition)", consisting of all three parties that were represented in the West-Germany-only [Bundestag](3768.md) from 1961 to 1983.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] [Reiner Haseloff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reiner_Haseloff) (CDU) was re-elected as [Minister-President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister-President) only in the second ballot. The coalition government held a clear majority in the state parliament with 56 of the 97 seats.[^cite_note-Invest-6] Haseloff retired in January 2026 and was replaced by [Sven Schulze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sven_Schulze).[^cite_note-7]

## Election date

According to Article 43 of the State Constitution, the state election must take place between the 58th and 62nd month after the start of the legislative period, unless the period is terminated early. The [8th Landtag of Saxony-Anhalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Landtag_of_Saxony-Anhalt) had its first session on 6 July 2021. Consequently, possible election dates initially included all Sundays and public holidays between 10 May and 6 September 2026. In May 2025, the state parliament designated 6 September 2026, thus the last possible day, as the election date.[^cite_note-8]

## Electoral system



In Saxony-Anhalt state elections there used to be more abstentions (*Nichtwähler*) than voters for any party until in 2026 [turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout) rose to 77.8% and AfD won 43.8% of votes and 34% of all eligible voters

The State Parliament of Saxony-Anhalt consists of at least 83 members, although this number may increase due to [overhang and compensatory mandates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhang_seat). Of these, 41 members are directly elected in [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituencies), while the remaining seats are allocated to parties based on their [state lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_list_\(Germany\)). Each voter has two votes: a [first vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_vote) (*Erststimme*) to elect a constituency representative and a [second vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_vote) (*Zweitstimme*) to select a party's state list, which determines the proportional distribution of seats in the [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament). The allocation of seats is governed by the [Hare-Niemeyer method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hare-Niemeyer_method), with only parties receiving at least 5% of the valid second votes considered for proportional distribution. The State Election Committee oversees the process, first determining the total second votes cast for each state list. Seats are then distributed as follows:

1. The committee identifies the number of seats won by independent constituency candidates and by parties that fail to meet the 5% threshold. These are subtracted from the base number of 83 seats to calculate the seats available for proportional allocation.

2. The remaining seats are distributed among qualifying parties based on their second vote totals. Each party's share is calculated by multiplying the total seats by the party's second votes, divided by the total second votes of all qualifying parties. Whole seats are assigned first, and any remaining seats are allocated based on the highest fractional remainders. In case of ties, a lottery conducted by the State Election Officer decides.

3. If a party with over half of the second votes receives less than half of the available seats, it is awarded an additional seat before further distribution.

4. Seats won in constituencies are deducted from a party's total allocated seats, with the remainder filled from the party's state list in the order specified. Candidates already elected in constituencies are excluded from the state list allocation.

5. Overhang mandates occur when a party wins more constituency seats than its proportional share. In such cases, the total number of parliamentary seats increases by twice the number of overhang seats, and the distribution process is repeated. If overhang seats persist, they are retained by the party, and the total number of seats is adjusted accordingly. This process continues until the overhang seats no longer exceed half the number required to form a parliamentary group, calculated as the seats a hypothetical party with 5% of second votes would receive.

6. Non-elected candidates on state lists of parties securing at least one seat are designated as substitutes in the order determined by the State Election Committee, excluding those already elected in constituencies. The election adheres to the provisions of the Saxony-Anhalt Election Law (Landeswahlgesetz, LWG), as published on 18 February 2010, and the state's electoral regulations.

### Lead candidates

#### CDU

Then Minister-President [Reiner Haseloff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reiner_Haseloff) announced on 7 August 2025 that he would not seek re-election in 2026.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-\:1-13] [Sven Schulze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sven_Schulze) was selected to be the CDU's lead candidate.[^cite_note-\:1-13][^cite_note-14] On 28 January 2026, Schulze was elected Minister-President.[^cite_note-15] Previously, he had served as a member of the [European Parliament](9581.md) for seven years. In 2021, he became the CDU's state chairman. Schulze was the State Minister for Economic Affairs in the [Third Haseloff cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Haseloff_cabinet).[^cite_note-\:1-13]

#### AfD

In a state party conference in [Magdeburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdeburg), [Ulrich Siegmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrich_Siegmund) was selected by the AfD to be its lead candidate, getting 98.3% of the votes.[^cite_note-16] Since 2016, Siegmund has been a member of the Landtag.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-\:5-18] In 2022, [Oliver Kirchner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Kirchner), chair of the AfD parliamentary group in Saxony-Anhalt, proposed that both of them could be lead candidate.[^cite_note-\:2-19] Shortly after, Siegmund began co-chairing the parliamentary group with Kirchner.[^cite_note-\:2-19][^cite_note-20] Siegmund was chair of the social affairs committee until February 2024 when the parliament voted to remove him from the role due to his participation in the [2023 Potsdam far-right meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Potsdam_far-right_meeting).[^cite_note-21]

The AfD campaign focused on restrictive immigration and [remigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remigration), including a proposed "deportation and remigration offensive" and tighter immigration controls.[^cite_note-Reuters_2026-22] It proposed separate classes for refugee children as part of its education policy.[^cite_note-23] The party also called for replacing compulsory schooling with a general education requirement and proposed a cash "welcome bonus" for each newborn.[^cite_note-24] It sought to reduce public broadcasting to a "basic service" and to reverse the [energy transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_transition) in favour of fossil fuels and [nuclear power](22153.md).[^cite_note-Reuters_2026-22] Its programme also called for stricter asylum and security policies, including faster deportations and expanded detention capacity for deportees.[^cite_note-Reuters_2026-22] Finally, it advocated a more explicitly patriotic cultural policy, including replacing the state's #moderndenken campaign with #deutschdenken, and replacing [rainbow flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_flag_\(LGBTQ\)) with German flags on public buildings.[^cite_note-25]

On 13 August 2026, German weekly news magazine *[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel)* had a mug-shot style photo of him on its cover,[^cite_note-26] calling him "the most dangerous man in Germany".[^cite_note-27] Siegmund used the magazine cover for his campaign, signing many copies, while *Der Spiegel* had to defend the move.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] During an AfD campaign event in Dessau-Roßlau (Saxony-Anhalt) in July 2026, AfD co-leader [Tino Chrupalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tino_Chrupalla) and [Ulrich Siegmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrich_Siegmund) would sing the former [East German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_German) (GDR) national anthem "[Risen from Ruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risen_from_Ruins)", and afterwards the [German national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_national_anthem) was also sung.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] [Martin Huber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Huber) from the [Christian Social Union in Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Social_Union_in_Bavaria) (CSU) criticised the playing of the former GDR anthem.[^cite_note-32]

#### Left

[Eva von Angern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_von_Angern) was selected to be the Left's lead candidate after getting a large majority of votes at a party conference in [Leuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuna).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-\:3-34] She was nominated by the party's two state chairs: [Janina Böttger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janina_B%C3%B6ttger) and [Hendrik Lange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_Lange).[^cite_note-\:4-35] Von Angern has been a member of the state parliament since 2002 and deputy chair of the Left's state parliamentary group from 2014 until she became the chair in 2020.[^cite_note-\:3-34][^cite_note-36] Additionally, she was the lead candidate for her party in the [2021 Saxony-Anhalt state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Saxony-Anhalt_state_election),[^cite_note-\:3-34][^cite_note-\:4-35] where the Left suffered record losses, although [Dietmar Bartsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietmar_Bartsch), the then-leader of [The Left's national parliamentary group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Left_faction_\(Bundestag\)), argued that this was partly due to [tactical voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_voting).[^cite_note-37]

#### SPD

Getting votes from 97 of the 98 delegates, [Armin Willingmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armin_Willingmann) was elected the lead candidate for the SPD at a party conference in [Quedlinburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quedlinburg).[^cite_note-38] Earlier, the party's state executive committee unanimously voted to nominate him for the position.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] During the [Second Haseloff cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Haseloff_cabinet?action=edit\&redlink=1), Willingmann served as State Secretary in the Ministry of Economic Affairs and Science for 6 months before becoming the Minister. In 2021, he ran to represent the constituency of [Wernigerode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wernigerode) in the state parliament.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-\:6-42] He came in second place, but was still able to become a member of parliament via the party list.[^cite_note-\:5-18][^cite_note-\:6-42] In October 2021, Willingmann resigned from the state parliament and joined the Third Haseloff cabinet as the Minister of Science, Energy, Climate Protection and the Environment.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] After Minister-President Haseloff resigned, Willingmann maintained this position in the newly announced [Schulze cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schulze_cabinet).[^cite_note-\:7-45] In 2022, he was elected deputy chair of his party's state parliamentary group.[^cite_note-46]

#### FDP

On 6 November 2025, the FDP's state delegates' assembly voted to select [Lydia Hüskens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_H%C3%BCskens), Minister of Infrastructure and Digitalization and Second Deputy Minister-President in the [Third Haseloff cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Haseloff_cabinet) and [Schulze cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schulze_cabinet) since 2021, as the party's lead candidate.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

#### Greens

The Greens' state party conference in [Wittenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wittenberg) selected [Susan Sziborra-Seidlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Sziborra-Seidlitz) as the party's lead candidate. 66 out of the 90 delegates voted for her.[^cite_note-\:10-49][^cite_note-50] Since 2016, Sziborra-Seidlitz has been one of her party's state chairs.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-\:11-52] In 2021, she was elected to the state parliament, entering through the party list.[^cite_note-\:5-18][^cite_note-\:10-49][^cite_note-\:11-52]

#### BSW

Due to internal party conflicts at the state level, the BSW selected two lead candidates: [Thomas Schulze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Schulze) and [Claudia Wittig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Wittig). Neither of them have held public office before.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

### Potential coalitions

In 2018, the federal CDU passed a "resolution of incompatibility" which prohibits itself from entering into a "coalition or any similar form of cooperation" with the AfD and Left at any level. With polls in the run-up to the election showing the only possible majority coalition not involving the AfD would be a four-way CDU–SPD–Green–Left coalition (a first in Germany), federal CDU leader and Chancellor [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md) reiterated in an interview with local newspapers that there would be no cooperation with the Left. He noted that a situation similar to the minority [Voigt cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voigt_cabinet) in [Thuringia](31130.md), where The Left positions itself as a "constructive opposition" and supports the government in what amounts to an unwritten [confidence and supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_and_supply) agreement, may be possible. The Left's federal leadership also signaled its openness to this arrangement.[^cite_note-55] On 3 September, members of the CDU district committee in [Harz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harz_\(district\)), the largest in the state, proposed a resolution that would reject any cooperation with The Left, including accepting its tolerance of a minority government. Schulze dismissed it as "the opinion of a single party member", and said that nothing permanent had been decided and he would not discuss the topic before the election.[^cite_note-56]

BSW leading figure [Sahra Wagenknecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahra_Wagenknecht) called on the party to undertake "issue-based" work with all other parties, including the AfD, under an independent Minister-President should it enter the Landtag. This did not extend to entering a formal coalition with the AfD. She also called for the end of "firewall idiocy", referring to the *[cordon sanitaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordon_sanitaire_\(politics\))* around the AfD; lead candidate [Claudia Wittig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Wittig) termed the refusal of cooperation with the AfD "undemocratic" and said it would only lead to deadlock.[^cite_note-57] The issue of potential cooperation with the AfD led to 13 of the 15 BSW MdLs in neighboring Thuringia, as well as its entire state leadership there, leaving the party in the week before this election. The BSW had participated in Germany's only [blackberry coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackberry_coalition) in that state, although throughout the summer of 2026 Wagenknecht repeatedly urged state party leaders to end the coalition and support the same model of an independent Minister-President.[^cite_note-58]

To prevent a possible AfD-led government, an outside political initiative called for [tactical voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_voting) to support the smaller parties, mainly the SPD and Greens; either party failing to enter the Landtag would lower the percentage threshold required for an AfD majority.[^cite_note-59] In a 30 August joint op-ed in the *[Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung](513356.md)*, federal defense minister [Boris Pistorius](69097811.md) (SPD) and [Baden-Württemberg](66401.md) Minister-President [Cem Özdemir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cem_%C3%96zdemir) (Greens) called on the government to swiftly begin proceedings before the [Federal Constitutional Court](34781059.md) to ban the AfD. Per Article 21 of the [Basic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_of_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany), the Federal Constitutional Court has the authority to determine that a party is anti-democratic, and anti-democratic parties are prohibited.[^cite_note-60]

## Aftermath

### Analysis

#### Result

The result was widely regarded as a breakthrough for the AfD,[^cite_note-Murray_&_Knolle_2026-89][^cite_note-Landler_2026-90][^cite_note-91] which won 43.8% of the vote, more than twice its share in the 2021 election,[^cite_note-Landler_2026-90][^cite_note-Roos_2026-92] and higher than its share in the [2024 Thuringian state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Thuringian_state_election), marking the first time that a far-right party has been on the cusp of power in a German state since World War II.[^cite_note-Murray_&_Knolle_2026-89] The composition of the new AfD parliamentary group also attracted scrutiny, with several newly elected [MPs](98066.md) having controversial backgrounds, including a former [Stasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stasi) officer, a member who had participated in pornography films, and MPs linked to [neo-Nazis](54361.md) and other far-right extremist networks.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

Compared to 2021, when the AfD candidates won only one single-member constituency, this time the party won 38 of the 41 constituencies, with no need for [overhang seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhang_seat), while the Left won the remaining three.[^cite_note-Landler_2026-90] By contrast, the CDU suffered a landslide defeat. After having won 40 of 41 constituencies in 2021 with a vote share of 37.1%, which resulted in 40 directly elected CDU members and parliament size enlarged to 97, they did not win a single constituency in 2026 and—as parliament size remained at the regular 83 seats due to AfD winning over 43%—only won 15 seats via the party list, losing 62.5% of their MPs. The CDU's popular vote share fell to 17.2%, its weakest result in the state in decades.[^cite_note-Neu_&_Pokorny_2026-95]

The state's other parties saw mixed results. Compared to 2021, voter share increased for the SPD (8.4% to 9.3%) and the Greens (5.9% to 8.9%). [^cite_note-Frymark_2026-96] Each party won eight seats. This was a slight loss of representation for the SPD but a gain for the Greens, who saw their greatest ever share of the vote in a Saxony-Anhalt election[^cite_note-Frymark_2026-96][^cite_note-Bukow_2026-97] The Left also won eight seats, declining from 11.0% of the vote in 2021 to 8.6% in 2026.[^cite_note-Frymark_2026-96] The BSW, which had split from the left since 2021, won 5.3% of the vote and five seats in its first Saxony-Anhalt state election.[^cite_note-Bukow_2026-97] The FDP's share fell sharply from 6.4% to 2.6%, thus failing to reach the 5% threshold required for representation in the Landtag.[^cite_note-Frymark_2026-96]

The [Konrad Adenauer Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Adenauer_Foundation) identified a generally pessimistic assessment of the economic situation and the future, as well as material and identity-related concerns, as important characteristics of the AfD's support base.[^cite_note-Neu_&_Pokorny_2026-95] The election showed a high degree of electoral volatility.[^cite_note-Neu_&_Pokorny_2026-95] The AfD benefited disproportionately from the sharp increase in turnout to 77.8%, mobilising an estimated 170,000 voters who had not voted in the previous election.[^cite_note-Frymark_2026-96] Voter-transfer estimates indicated that the AfD's gains came from several parties, as well as from the previous non-voter pool, rather than from a single source.[^cite_note-Neu_&_Pokorny_2026-95] The result was also widely interpreted as a protest against the federal government rather than solely as an endorsement of the AfD. In Saxony-Anhalt, 87% of voters said they were dissatisfied with the federal government, while economist [Marcel Fratzscher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Fratzscher) described the election primarily as a vote of no confidence in it, highlighting the role of dissatisfaction with the government alongside support for the AfD.[^cite_note-Murray_&_Knolle_2026-89][^cite_note-Landler_2026-90][^cite_note-Neu_&_Pokorny_2026-95] The election nevertheless had significance beyond the state itself, as AfD candidate [Ulrich Siegmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrich_Siegmund) presented the victory as a first step towards national power in 2029, while the result was welcomed by far-right movements elsewhere in Europe and viewed as part of a broader right-wing nationalist trend.[^cite_note-Murray_&_Knolle_2026-89][^cite_note-Landler_2026-90][^cite_note-Connolly_2026-98] British political commentator and [Reform UK](59757202.md) figure [Gawain Towler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gawain_Towler) also drew attention to Geostrategy's poll for its close prediction of the election result.[^cite_note-99]

#### Tactical voting

Tactical voting played a role in the election result.[^cite_note-Frymark_2026-96] According to the [Centre for Eastern Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Eastern_Studies), some opponents of the AfD, including CDU supporters, voted for the SPD or the Greens in order to help them clear the 5% electoral threshold and thereby reduce the AfD's chances of securing an [absolute majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absolute_majority).[^cite_note-Frymark_2026-96] According to voter-transfer analysis cited by the Centre for Eastern Studies, the SPD and the Greens respectively received an estimated 19,000 and 18,000 votes from CDU voters.[^cite_note-Frymark_2026-96] The [Heinrich Böll Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_B%C3%B6ll_Foundation) likewise assessed that the initiatives promoting tactical voting contributed substantially to the SPD and the Greens remaining in the Landtag, thereby preventing the AfD from obtaining an absolute majority.[^cite_note-Bukow_2026-97]

The tactical-voting campaign was particularly significant because the electoral threshold made the parliamentary arithmetic highly sensitive to the performance of smaller parties.[^cite_note-Frymark_2026-96] The strategy succeeded in preventing an AfD absolute majority,[^cite_note-100] although it did not determine which parties would ultimately be able to form a government.[^cite_note-Bukow_2026-97] The unexpectedly strong result for the Greens was attributed in part to tactical voters, particularly former CDU voters, while the party also benefited from the mobilisation of previous non-voters.[^cite_note-Bukow_2026-97] The Left also experienced tactical vote transfers, with its leadership acknowledging that some of its second votes went to the SPD and the Greens as part of efforts to prevent an AfD majority.[^cite_note-101]

#### Firewall policy

Despite its historic result, the AfD did not obtain an absolute majority,[^cite_note-102] and therefore could not form a government on its own.[^cite_note-Roos_2026-92] The result consequently left the AfD as the largest parliamentary force while the other parties faced the question of whether and how to form a majority without cooperation with it.[^cite_note-Bukow_2026-97] As a result, the outcome was characterised by a combination of unprecedented electoral support for the AfD and continued political barriers to an AfD-led government.[^cite_note-Roos_2026-92] The result intensified debate over Germany's political [firewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firewall_against_the_far-right_in_Germany) against the AfD. The scale of the AfD's victory placed particular pressure on the established refusal of mainstream parties to cooperate with it, while the BSW's entry into the Landtag created a potential route to an AfD-led majority and raised the possibility of cooperation across the traditional political divide.[^cite_note-Roos_2026-92][^cite_note-AP_News_2026-103][^cite_note-Pfeifer_2026-104]

The election sparked a debate over whether maintaining the political firewall remained preferable to allowing the AfD, as the largest parliamentary force, a path into government in Saxony-Anhalt, where governing would expose the party to the practical responsibilities and consequences of implementing its programme.[^cite_note-Roos_2026-92][^cite_note-AP_News_2026-103][^cite_note-Pfeifer_2026-104] The question was not only whether the AfD could enter government, but also whether governing could put its electoral strength to a practical test. Siegmund presented himself as a more approachable figure than some other AfD politicians while maintaining the party's core positions, and political scientist Wolfgang Merkel noted that whether he could govern remained unproven.[^cite_note-Roos_2026-92][^cite_note-Pfeifer_2026-104][^cite_note-Moulson_2026-105] An AfD-led government would therefore expose the party to the practical constraints and consequences of implementing its programme, rather than allowing it to remain primarily in opposition.[^cite_note-AP_News_2026-103][^cite_note-Pfeifer_2026-104]

At the same time, the assumption that excluding the AfD is necessarily more effective than allowing it to govern remained contested as the election raised a broader strategic question: whether continued exclusion could contain the AfD, or whether allowing it to assume governing responsibility would instead expose the limits of its programme and potentially alter its support. Some analysts argued that the political firewall had not prevented the normalisation of far-right positions, while others continued to regard cooperation with the AfD as unacceptable given its extremist classification and political programme.[^cite_note-Murray_&_Knolle_2026-89][^cite_note-Connolly_2026-98][^cite_note-AP_News_2026-103]

The immediate alternatives were consequently more complex than a simple choice between an AfD government and its exclusion. The CDU, the SPD, the Greens, and the Left continued to reject governing with the AfD, while the BSW's five seats gave it a potentially decisive role and opened discussion of forms of cooperation that stopped short of a conventional coalition.[^cite_note-Murray_&_Knolle_2026-89][^cite_note-AP_News_2026-103][^cite_note-106] This left the post-election debate centred not only on whether the firewall should hold, but also on what form of government could replace it, and on whether the AfD should be allowed to assume executive responsibility despite lacking an outright majority.[^cite_note-Roos_2026-92][^cite_note-AP_News_2026-103][^cite_note-Pfeifer_2026-104]

### Government formation

Siegmund had expressed prior to the election that he would only run for Minister-President should the AfD receive an absolute majority in the vote. With this target missed by three seats, neither the AfD nor a hypothetical coalition of the CDU, the SPD, the Greens, and the Left, all of whom have a firewall policy against the far-right, have enough seats for a majority, with both at 39 seats. Furthermore, [Sven Schulze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sven_Schulze) seemingly ruled out a coalition with any party, claiming that the CDU did not have a mandate to govern following its historic defeat and that the party would "sit in the opposition".[^cite_note-107]

Shortly after the election, Siegmund said that he was ready to enter into agreements with other parties or individual MPs in order to form a cabinet, but that he would not compromise on the issues raised in the party manifesto; he has signalled that he will not run for Minister-President unless he can count on a stable enough majority. The BSW had ruled out a formal coalition with the AfD before the election and again on election night; Claudia Wittig repeated on 7 September that her party would not enter one.[^cite_note-108] It nevertheless said it would talk to the AfD about individual projects and changing majorities. BSW founder [Sahra Wagenknecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahra_Wagenknecht) invited the AfD into talks on a [nonpartisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonpartisanship) Minister-President, a proposal that was rejected by the AfD. Siegmund described it as a "messy muddle", while AfD federal leader [Tino Chrupalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tino_Chrupalla) made clear that the party would only support Siegmund as Minister-President.[^cite_note-109]

The election of the Minister-President is governed by article 65 of the state constitution. All parties may nominate a candidate, and any candidate receiving an absolute majority of votes is elected. If no one is elected in the first round, a second round also requiring an absolute majority must take place within seven days. If no one is elected in the second round, the Landtag is given fourteen more days to vote whether to dissolve itself and hold a [snap election](462915.md). If the vote on dissolution fails, a third Minister-President ballot must be held immediately, and the candidate receiving the plurality of votes is elected.[^cite_note-110]

The BSW has announced that, in the absence of an agreement, it will abstain on the third vote to elect the Minister-President. However, since the CDU, SPD, the Greens, and the Left combined have 39 votes just like the AfD, even this may not easily break the impasse. On the other hand, it has been suggested that some of the CDU members could break ranks and vote for Siegmund in spite of the traditional anti-AfD firewall.[^cite_note-Pfeifer_2026-104][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] The AfD invited all other parties to non-binding exploratory talks from 8 September, but all except the BSW quickly declined them.[^cite_note-113] The possibility of defections from the CDU to the AfD became a subject of speculation. On 11 September, Siegmund confirmed that talks with politicians from other parties had taken place, while stating that the party was considering potential defectors. [MDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitteldeutscher_Rundfunk) subsequently surveyed the 15 newly elected CDU members of the Landtag; all of them explicitly ruled out switching to the AfD.[^cite_note-114]

A significant point of discussion between the AfD and the BSW was on whether to allow the proposed establishment of an Israeli-based [Elbit Systems](1341669.md) weapons' factory in [Sangerhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangerhausen). The BSW was opposed to it due to the company's involvement in the [Gaza war](74998519.md), while Siegmund supported it.[^cite_note-115]

The dispute between the two parties (exacerbated by the BSW's continued refusal to support Siegmund as Minister-President) eventually devolved into a public spat. [Oliver Kirchner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Kirchner) (head of the AfD parliamentary group in the Landtag) claimed that it was difficult for the AfD to cooperate with a party led "by a woman named Mohamed Ali and by this half-Italian" (BSW co-leaders [Amira Mohamed Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amira_Mohamed_Ali) and [Fabio De Masi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_De_Masi)).[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] In response, BSW founder Sarah Wagenknecht called out the AfD leadership as being "very weird individuals" unsuited to govern. However, neither party halted negotiations, which proceeded as scheduled.[^cite_note-118]

Parallel to this, on 28 September SPD leadership invited all parties besides the AfD to a meeting to begin exploratory talks toward forming a government under a non-partisan Minister-President. The CDU declined the invitation, with the state executive holding to its post-election decision to go into opposition. The BSW left open whether it would participate and criticized the exclusion of the AfD, but welcomed the proposal of an independent Minister-President.[^cite_note-119] On the same day, Siegmund told the press he would put himself forward as a Minister-President candidate in December; he claimed to be in "constructive talks with another parliamentary group and individual members of other groups" about supporting him.[^cite_note-120]

### Merz government crisis

Main article: [2026 Merz government crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Merz_government_crisis)

The result intensified a national leadership crisis around Chancellor [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md), whose CDU had governed Saxony-Anhalt since 2002 and whose personal unpopularity was widely cited as a factor in the defeat, including by himself.[^cite_note-PoliticoNoAnswers-121]

## Reactions

### National reactions

Chancellor Merz stated that the election result "has changed not only Saxony-Anhalt, but Germany as a whole", and added: "We are all profoundly shocked."[^cite_note-Fleischmann_2026-122] Merz's CDU placed second under the leadership of the outgoing Minister-President [Sven Schulze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sven_Schulze), who stated he would draw lessons from the result.[^cite_note-Fleischmann_2026-122] Former Chancellor [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel) described the election's outcome as "simply shocking" and a "turning point" in German history, stating as a member of the CDU that her "heart bleeds" over the results.[^cite_note-123] Public reactions in Saxony-Anhalt were sharply divided. While some voters expressed shock and concern about the implications for immigrants and the state's future, AfD supporters described the result as an opportunity for the party to implement its programme and "straighten up" the state.[^cite_note-124]

### International reactions

Reaction to the result was relatively split across the ideological spectrum.[^cite_note-Ormiston_2026-125] In France, ministers of the [second Lecornu government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Lecornu_government) expressed concerns.[^cite_note-126] Presidential candidate [Jean-Luc Mélenchon](20298042.md) of [La France Insoumise](52520313.md) stated following the election results that "under these conditions, the German military project to create the first conventional army in Europe is unacceptable to France."[^cite_note-127] Fellow party member [Hadrien Clouet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrien_Clouet) similarly called the German rearmament and AfD win a "dubious combination", and said that the AfD is a neo-Nazi party.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

Polish Prime Minister and former President of the European Council [Donald Tusk](648724.md) said on [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_\(social_media\)) (formerly Twitter) that "only idiots and traitors can rejoice at the triumph of the AfD".[^cite_note-Ormiston_2026-125] Former Polish Prime Minister [Mateusz Morawiecki](48505487.md) said he considered the AfD a "threat" to Poland, adding that he is "not at all pleased when Germany boasts about its growing military spending and the growing strength of the German army", and that he "would prefer the Polish army to be stronger".[^cite_note-130] Similarly, speaking for France's [National Rally](67119.md), which had broken ties with the AfD following comments on the Nazi era by the AfD's lead candidate in the [2024 European Parliament election in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Parliament_election_in_Germany), the [National Assembly](299616.md) member [Jean-Philippe Tanguy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Philippe_Tanguy) said he was "scandalised" regarding the AfD win as voters elected a party that "downplay\[s] Germany's Nazi past".[^cite_note-131] Tanguy added that he left a meeting with AfD politicians in 2019 "terrorised".[^cite_note-132]

Belgian Defence Minister [Theo Francken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theo_Francken) placed the blame of the results on former Chancellor Merkel's "[Wir schaffen das](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wir_schaffen_das)" policy in the [2015 European migrant crisis](46415102.md), calling her leadership "weak" and her legacy "dramatic".[^cite_note-133] Leaders of other European [right-wing populist](8354382.md) parties, such as Hungary's [Viktor Orbán](750582.md) and [László Toroczkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A1szl%C3%B3_Toroczkai),[^cite_note-134] Italy's [Matteo Salvini](1767165.md), and Spain's [Santiago Abascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Abascal), welcomed the result, with the latter stating it represented "a great hope for Germany and Europe".[^cite_note-Ormiston_2026-125] US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) likewise praised the AfD for its electoral victory and political stances,[^cite_note-135] which led Chancellor Merz to cancel a planned phone call with Trump.[^cite_note-136] American businessman [Elon Musk](909036.md), who is the owner of X and CEO of [Tesla](5533631.md), hailed the results, with Siegmund personally thanking him for his support.[^cite_note-137]
