**Videotelephony**, also known as **video chat**, **videoconferencing**, **video calling**, or **telepresence**, is the use of [audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_signal) and [video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video) for simultaneous [two-way communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-way_communication).[^cite_note-mcgraw-1]

**Videophones** were standalone devices for video calling (compare [Telephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone)). As [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphones) and computers have become capable of video calling, the demand for a separate category of videophones has disappeared.&#x20;

*Videoconferencing* implies group communication[^cite_note-Mulbach-1995c-2] and is used in [telepresence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telepresence), the goal of which is to create the illusion that remote participants are in the same room.

The concept of videotelephony was conceived in the late 19th century, and versions were demonstrated to the public starting in the 1930s. In April, 1930, reporters gathered at AT\&T corporate headquarters on Broadway in New York City for the first public demonstration of two-way video telephony. The event linked the headquarters building with a Bell laboratories building on West Street.[^cite_note-3] Early demonstrations were installed at booths in post offices and shown at world expositions. AT\&T demonstrated Picturephone at the 1964 World’s Fair in New York City. In 1970, [AT\&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T) launched Picturephone as the first commercial personal videotelephone system. In addition to videophones, there existed *image phones* which exchanged still images between units every few seconds over [conventional telephone lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_old_telephone_service). The development of advanced [video codecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_codec), more powerful [CPUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPU), and high-bandwidth [Internet service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_service) in the early 2000s allowed the new category of [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone) to provide high-quality low-cost color service between users almost anywhere in the world, eliminating the videophone as a separate product concept.

Applications of videotelephony include [sign language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_language) transmission for [deaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaf) and [speech-impaired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech-impaired) people, [distance education](8997.md), [telemedicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telehealth), and overcoming [mobility issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability). [News media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_media) organizations have used videotelephony for broadcasting.

### Origin

The concept of videotelephony was first conceived in the late 1870s, both in the United States and in Europe, although the basic sciences to permit its very earliest trials would take nearly a half century to be discovered. The prerequisite knowledge arose from intensive research and experimentation in several telecommunication fields, notably [electrical telegraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_telegraphy), [telephony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephony), [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio), and [television](29831.md).

### Early systems

Simple analog videophone communication could be established as early as the invention of the [television](29831.md). Such an antecedent usually consisted of two [closed-circuit television](87179.md) systems connected via [coax cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coax_cable) or [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio). An example of that was the German Reich Postzentralamt (post office) videotelephone network serving Berlin and several German cities via coaxial cables between 1936 and 1940.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]



Gregorio Y. Zara was a Filipino engineer and physicist best remembered for inventing the first two-way video telephone.

[Gregorio Y. Zara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorio_Y._Zara), a Filipino scientist, invented the first videophone in 1954, which was patented in 1955 as a "photo phone signal separator network." He is recognized as the [Father of Videoconferencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Scientist_of_the_Philippines) for his pioneering contribution to the development of videotelephony technology.[^cite_note-6]

The development of videotelephony as a subscription service started in the latter half of the 1920s in the United Kingdom and the United States, spurred notably by [John Logie Baird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Logie_Baird) and [AT\&T's Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs). This occurred in part, at least with AT\&T, to serve as an adjunct supplementing the use of the telephone. A number of organizations believed that videotelephony would be superior to plain voice communications. Attempts at using normal [telephony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephony) networks to transmit slow-scan video, such as the first systems developed by [AT\&T Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Corporation), first researched in the 1950s, failed mostly due to the poor picture quality and the lack of efficient [video compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_compression) techniques.

During the first crewed [space flights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_flight), [NASA](18426568.md) used two radio-frequency ([UHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UHF) or [VHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VHF)) video links, one in each direction. [TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV) channels routinely use this type of videotelephony when reporting from distant locations. The news media were to become regular users of mobile links to [satellites](27683.md) using specially equipped trucks, and much later via special satellite videophones in a briefcase. This technique was very expensive, though, and was not adopted for applications such as [telemedicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemedicine), [distance education](8997.md), and business meetings.

Decades of research and development culminated in the 1970 commercial launch of AT\&T's [Picturephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picturephone) service, available in select cities. However, the system was a commercial failure, chiefly due to consumer apathy, high subscription costs, and lack of [network effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_effect)—with only a few hundred Picturephones in the world, users had extremely few contacts they could actually call, and [interoperability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interoperability) with other videophone systems would not exist for decades.

### Digital

In the 1980s, [digital telephony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_telephony) transmission networks became possible, such as with [ISDN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISDN) networks. During this time, there was also research into other forms of digital video and audio communication. Many of these technologies, such as the [Media space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_space), are not as widely used today as videoconferencing but were still an important area of research.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] The first dedicated systems started to appear as ISDN networks were expanding throughout the world. One of the first commercial videoconferencing systems sold to companies came from [PictureTel Corp.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PictureTel_Corp.), which had an [initial public offering](63879.md) in November, 1984.

In 1984, Concept Communication in the United States created a [circuit board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_board) for standard personal computers that doubled the video [frame rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_rate) of typical digital videotelephone systems from 15 to 30 frames per second, and reduced the cost from \$100,000 to \$12,000.[^cite_note-williamjtobin-9] The company also secured a patent for a [codec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codec) for full-motion videoconferencing, first demonstrated at [AT\&T Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Bell_Labs) in 1986.[^cite_note-williamjtobin-9][^cite_note-rtir-10]



Global Schoolhouse students communicating via [CU-SeeMe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CU-SeeMe), shown here with a video [frame rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_rate) between 0.9 and 3 frames per second (1993)

Very expensive videoconferencing systems continued to rapidly evolve throughout the 1980s and 1990s. Proprietary equipment, software, and network requirements gave way to standards-based technologies that were available for anyone to purchase at a reasonable cost.

While videoconferencing technology was initially used primarily within internal corporate communication networks, one of the first community service uses of the technology started in 1992 through a unique partnership with PictureTel and IBM, which at the time were promoting a jointly developed desktop based videoconferencing product known as the PCS/1. Over the next 15 years, [Project DIANE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_DIANE) (Diversified Information and Assistance Network) grew to use a variety of videoconferencing platforms to create a multi-state cooperative public service and distance education network consisting of several hundred schools, libraries, science museums, zoos and parks, and many other community-oriented organizations.

### Transition to internet and mobile devices

Advances in [video compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_compression) allowed [digital video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_video) streams to be transmitted over the Internet, which was previously difficult due to the impractically high bandwidth requirements of [uncompressed video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncompressed_video). The DCT algorithm was the basis for the first practical [video coding standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_coding_standard) that was useful for online videoconferencing, [H.261](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.261), standardised by the [ITU-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITU-T) in 1988, and subsequent [H.26x](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.26x) [video coding standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_coding_standard).[^cite_note-Huang-11]

In 1992 [CU-SeeMe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CU-SeeMe) was developed at Cornell by Tim Dorcey et al. In 1995 the [first public videoconference between North America and Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Communication_Landmarks#Telecommunication_landmarks) took place, linking a technofair in [San Francisco](49728.md) with a techno-rave and cyberdeli in [Cape Town](6653.md). At the [1998 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Winter_Olympics) opening ceremony in [Nagano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagano,_Nagano), Japan, [Seiji Ozawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiji_Ozawa) conducted the [Ode to Joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ode_to_Joy) from [Beethoven's Ninth Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beethoven's_Ninth_Symphony) simultaneously across five continents in near-real-time.



&#x20;The [Kyocera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyocera) VP-210 Visual Phone was the first commercial mobile videophone (1999).

[Kyocera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyocera) conducted a two-year development campaign from 1997 to 1999 that resulted in the release of the VP-210 Visual Phone, the first mobile colour videophone that also doubled as a [camera phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camera_phone) for still photos.[^cite_note-S\&S-12][^cite_note-CNN-1999.05.18-13] The camera phone was the same size as similar contemporary [mobile phones](19644137.md), but sported a large camera lens and a 5 cm (2 inch) colour [TFT display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TFT_display) capable of displaying 65,000 colors, and was able to process two video frames per second.[^cite_note-CNN-1999.05.18-13][^cite_note-Computerworld-2012.05.11-14]

Videotelephony was popularized in the 2000s via free Internet services such as [Skype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skype) and [iChat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IChat), web plugins supporting [H.26x](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.26x) video standards, and online telecommunication programs that promoted low cost, albeit lower quality, videoconferencing to virtually every location with an Internet connection.

Videotelephony became even more widespread through the deployment of video-enabled [mobile phones](19644137.md) such as 2010's [iPhone 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPhone_4), plus videoconferencing and [computer webcams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webcam) which use [Internet telephony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_telephony). In the upper echelons of government, business, and commerce, [telepresence technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telepresence), an advanced form of videoconferencing, has helped reduce the need to travel.

### Additional history

In May 2005, the first [high definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_video) videoconferencing systems, produced by [Lifesize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifesize), were displayed at the [Interop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interop) trade show in [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley), [Nevada](21216.md), able to provide video at 30 frames per second with a 1280 by 720 [display resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Display_resolution).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] [Polycom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycom) introduced its first high definition videoconferencing system to the market in 2006. As of the 2010s, high-definition resolution for videoconferencing became a popular feature, with most major suppliers in the videoconferencing market offering it.

Technological developments by videoconferencing developers in the 2010s have extended the capabilities of videoconferencing systems beyond the boardroom for use with [hand-held mobile devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_device) that combine the use of video, audio and on-screen drawing capabilities broadcasting in real time over secure networks, independent of location. [Mobile collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_collaboration) systems now allow people in previously unreachable locations, such as workers on an offshore oil rig, the ability to view and discuss issues with colleagues thousands of miles away. Traditional videoconferencing system manufacturers have begun providing mobile applications as well, such as those that allow for live and still image streaming.[^cite_note-17]

The highest ever video call (other than those from aircraft and spacecraft) took place on May 19, 2013, when British adventurer Daniel Hughes used a [smartphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone) with a [BGAN satellite modem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadband_Global_Area_Network) to make a videocall to the [BBC](19344654.md) from the summit of [Mount Everest](42179.md), at 8,848 metres (29,029 ft) above sea level.[^cite_note-18]

[GoToMeeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GoToMeeting) had a 20 percent increase in usage, according to [LogMeIn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LogMeIn).[^cite_note-19] UK based [StarLeaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarLeaf) reported a 600 percent increase in national call volumes.[^cite_note-20] Videoconferencing became so widespread during the pandemic that the term [Zoom fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoom_fatigue) came to prominence, referring to the taxing nature of spending long periods of time on videocalls.[^cite_note-21] This fatigue refers to the psychological and physiological effects participants involved in videoconferencing.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] One experimental study from 2021 revealed a link between camera use in videoconferencing and a prediction of fatigue occurrence an individual.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Furthermore, a 2022 article in the journal "[Computers in Human Behaviour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computers_in_Human_Behavior)" highlighted a study linking negative attitudes with the use of "self-view" when videoconferencing.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

Videotelephony can be categorized by its functionality and intended purpose, and also by its method of transmission.

**Videophones** were the earliest form of videotelephony, dating back to initial tests in 1927 by AT\&T. During the late 1930s, the post offices of several European governments established public videophone services for person-to-person communications using dual cable circuit telephone transmission technology. In the present day, standalone videophones and [UMTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UMTS) video-enabled [mobile phones](19644137.md) are usually used on a person-to-person basis.

**Videoconferencing** saw its earliest use with AT\&T's Picturephone service in the early 1970s. Transmissions were analog over short distances, but converted to digital forms for longer calls, again using telephone transmission technology. Popular corporate video-conferencing systems in the present day have migrated almost exclusively to digital [ISDN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISDN) and [IP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Protocol) transmission modes due to the need to convey the very large amounts of data generated by their cameras and microphones. These systems are often intended for use in conference mode, that is by many people in several different locations, all of whom can be viewed by every participant at each location.

[Telepresence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telepresence) systems are a newer, more advanced subset of videoconferencing systems, meant to allow higher degrees of video and audio fidelity. Such high-end systems are typically deployed in corporate settings.

[Mobile collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_collaboration) systems are another recent development, combining the use of video, audio, and on-screen drawing capabilities using newest generation [hand-held electronic devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_device) broadcasting over secure networks, enabling multi-party conferencing in real time, independent of location. [Proximity chat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proximity_chat) is another alternative mode, focused on the flexibility of small group conversations.

A more recent technology encompassing these functions is TV cams. TV cams enable people to make video calls using video calling services, like Skype on their TV, without using a PC connection. TV cams are specially designed video cameras that feed images in real time to another TV camera or other compatible computing devices like smartphones, tablets and computers.

*[Webcams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webcam)* are popular, relatively low-cost devices that can provide live video and audio streams via [personal computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_computer), and can be used with many [software clients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_video_telecommunication_services_and_product_brands#Software_Client_brands) for both video calls and videoconferencing.[^cite_note-books.google.com-30]

Each of the systems has its own advantages and disadvantages, including [video quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video), [capital cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_cost), degrees of sophistication, [transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_\(telecommunications\)) capacity requirements, and [cost of use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirect_costs).

### By cost and quality of service

From the least to the most expensive systems:

- [Web camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webcam) videophone and videoconferencing systems, either stand-alone or built-in, that serve as complements to personal computers, connected to other participants by [computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_network) and [VoIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VoIP) networks—lowest direct cost, assuming the users already possess computers at their respective locations. Quality of service can range from low to very high, including [high definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_video) video available on the latest model webcams. A related and similar device is a TV camera which is usually small, sits on top of a TV, and can connect to it via its [HDMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDMI) port, similar to how a webcam attaches to a computer via a USB port.

- [Videophones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Videophone)—low to midrange cost. The earliest standalone models operated over either [plain old telephone service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_old_telephone_service) (POTS) lines on the [PSTN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSTN) telephone networks or more expensive [ISDN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISDN) lines, while newer models have largely migrated to [Internet Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Protocol) line service for higher image resolutions and sound quality. Quality of service for standalone videophones can vary from low to high;

- Huddle room or all-in-one systems —low to midrange cost, newer endpoint category based on standard videoconferencing systems, but defined by the camera, microphone(s), speakers, and codec contained in a single piece of hardware. Typically used in small to medium spaces where [beamforming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beamforming) [microphone arrays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microphone_array) located in the system are sufficient, in lieu of table or ceiling microphones in closer proximity to the in-room participants. Quality of service is comparable to standard videoconferencing systems, varying from moderate to high. Some manufacturers' huddle room systems do not include the codec within the soundbar-shaped unit, rather only camera, microphone, and speakers. These systems are usually still classified as huddle room systems, but, like webcams, rely on a USB connection to an external device, usually a PC, to process the video codec responsibilities. Despite its name, video conferencing systems for Huddle Rooms prevent participants from huddling close together to be seen in the camera. All-in-one systems for these types of rooms range from wide angles such as 110° Horizontal field of view (FOV) to as much as 360° FOV that allow a full view of the room.

- [Videoconferencing systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Videoconferencing)—midrange cost, usually using [multipoint control units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multipoint_control_unit) or other bridging services to allow multiple parties on videoconference calls. Quality of service can vary from moderate to high.

- [Telepresence systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telepresence)—highest capabilities and highest cost. Full *high-end* systems can involve specially built teleconference rooms to allow expansive views with very high levels of audio and video fidelity, to permit an 'immersive' videoconference. When the proper type and capacity transmission lines are provided between facilities, the quality of service reaches state-of-the-art levels.

## Security concerns

[Computer security](7398.md) experts have shown that poorly configured or inadequately supervised videoconferencing systems can permit an easy *virtual* entry by [computer hackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_hackers) and criminals into company premises and corporate boardrooms.[^cite_note-NewYorkTimes-2012.01.22-31]

## Adoption

For over a century, [futurists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurist) have envisioned a future where telephone conversations will take place as actual face-to-face encounters with video as well as audio. Sometimes it is simply not possible or practical to have face-to-face meetings with two or more people. Sometimes a telephone conversation or conference call is adequate. Other times, [e-mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mail) exchanges are adequate. However, videoconferencing adds another option and can be considered when:

- A live conversation is needed

- Non-verbal (visual) information is an important component of the conversation

- The parties of the conversation cannot physically come to the same location

- The expense or time of travel is a consideration

[Bill Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Gates) said in 2001 that he used videoconferencing "three or four times a year", because digital scheduling was difficult and "if the overhead is super high, then you might as well just have a face-to-face meeting".[^cite_note-millergates20010904-32] Some observers argue that three outstanding issues have prevented videoconferencing from becoming a widely adopted form of communication, despite the ubiquity of videoconferencing-capable systems.[^cite_note-VanMeggelen-33]

- **Eye contact:** [Eye contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_contact) plays a large role in conversational [turn-taking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn-taking), perceived attention and intent, and other aspects of group communication.

  [^cite_note-34]

  &#x20;While traditional telephone conversations give no eye contact cues, many videoconferencing systems are arguably worse in that they provide an incorrect impression that the remote interlocutor is avoiding eye contact. Some telepresence systems have cameras located in the screens that reduce the amount of [parallax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallax) observed by the users. This issue is also being addressed through research that generates a synthetic image with eye contact using stereo reconstruction.

  [^cite_note-35]

  \
  [Telcordia Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telcordia_Technologies), formerly Bell Communications Research, owns a patent for eye-to-eye videoconferencing using rear projection screens with the video camera behind it, evolved from a 1960s U.S. military system that provided videoconferencing services between the [White House](33057.md) and various other government and military facilities. This technique eliminates the need for special cameras or image processing.

  [^cite_note-36]

- **Appearance consciousness:** A second psychological problem with videoconferencing is being on camera, with the video stream possibly even being recorded. The burden of presenting an acceptable on-screen appearance is not present in audio-only communication. Early studies by Alphonse Chapanis found that the addition of video actually impaired communication, possibly because of the consciousness of being on camera.

  [^cite_note-37]

- **Signal latency**: The information transport of digital signals in many steps need time. In a telecommunicated conversation, an increased [latency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latency_\(engineering\)) (time lag) larger than about 150–300 ms becomes noticeable and is soon observed as unnatural and distracting. Therefore, next to a stable large bandwidth, a small [total round-trip time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Control_Message_Protocol) is another major technical requirement for the communication channel for interactive videoconferencing.

  [^cite_note-Percy-38]

- **Bandwidth and quality of service**: In some countries, it is difficult or expensive to get a high-quality connection that is fast enough for good-quality videoconferencing. Technologies such as [ADSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADSL) are usually provided as two separate lines (for uplink/downlink) because each has limited upload speeds and cannot upload and download simultaneously at full speed. As Internet speeds increase, higher quality and high-definition videoconferencing will become more readily available.

- **Complexity of systems**: Most users are not technically experienced and want a simple interface. In hardware systems, an unplugged cord or an unresponsive remote control is seen as a failure, contributing to a perceived unreliability. Successful systems are backed by support teams who can provide fast assistance when required.

- **Perceived lack of interoperability**: Not all systems can readily interconnect; for example, ISDN and IP systems require a gateway. Popular software solutions cannot easily connect to hardware systems. Some systems use different standards, features, and qualities which can require additional configuration when connecting to dissimilar systems. Free software systems circumvent this limitation by making it relatively easy for a single user to communicate over multiple incompatible platforms.

- **Expense of commercial systems**: Well-designed [telepresence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telepresence) systems require specially designed rooms which can cost hundreds of thousands of dollars to fit out their rooms with codecs, integration equipment (such as [Multipoint Control Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multipoint_Control_Unit)), high fidelity sound systems, and furniture. Monthly charges may also be required for bridging services and high-capacity broadband service.

These are some of the reasons many organizations only use the systems internally, where there is less risk of loss of customers. An alternative for those lacking dedicated facilities is the [rental of videoconferencing-equipped meeting rooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_video_telecommunication_services_and_product_brands#Public_videoconferencing_facilities) in cities around the world. Clients can book rooms and turn up for the meeting, with all technical aspects being prearranged and support being readily available if needed. The issue of eye contact may be solved with advancing technology, including smartphones which have the screen and camera in essentially the same place. In developed countries, the near-ubiquity of [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone), [tablet computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tablet_computer), and computers with built-in audio and webcams removes the need for expensive dedicated hardware.

### Components and types



**Dual display**: A mid-2000s [Polycom VSX 7000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycom_VSX_7000) system and camera used for videoconferencing, with two displays for simultaneous broadcast from separate locations



A videoconference meeting facilitated by [Google Hangouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Hangouts)

The core technology used in a videotelephony system is digital compression of audio and video streams in real time. The hardware or [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software) that performs compression is called a [codec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codec) (coder/decoder). Compression rates of up to 1\:500 can be achieved. The resulting digital stream of 1s and 0s is subdivided into labeled [packets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packet_\(information_technology\)), which are then transmitted through a [digital network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_network) of some kind (usually [ISDN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISDN) or [IP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Protocol)).

The other components required for a videoconferencing system include:

- **Video input**: ([PTZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan%E2%80%93tilt%E2%80%93zoom_camera) / [360°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/360-degree_video) / [Fisheye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisheye_lens)) [video camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_camera), or [webcam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webcam)

- **Video output**: [computer monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_monitor), [television](29831.md), or [projector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_projector)

- **Audio input**: [microphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microphone), CD/DVD player, cassette player, or any other source of PreAmp audio outlet.

- **Audio output**: usually [loudspeakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loudspeaker) associated with the display device or telephone

- **Data transfer**: analog or digital telephone network, [LAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAN), or [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet)

- **Computer**: a data processing unit that ties together the other components, does the compressing and decompressing, and initiates and maintains the data linkage via the network.

There are basically three kinds of videoconferencing and videophone systems:

1. **Dedicated systems** have all required components packaged into a single piece of equipment, usually a console with a high quality [remote controlled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_control) video camera. These cameras can be controlled at a distance to pan left and right, tilt up and down, and zoom. They became known as PTZ cameras. The console contains all electrical interfaces, the control computer, and the software or hardware-based codec. Omnidirectional microphones are connected to the console, as well as a TV monitor with loudspeakers and/or a [video projector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_projector). There are several types of dedicated videoconferencing devices:&#x20;

   1. Large group videoconferencing are built-in, large, expensive devices used for large rooms such as conference rooms and auditoriums.

   2. Small group videoconferencing are either non-portable or portable, smaller, less expensive devices used for small meeting rooms.

   3. Individual videoconferencing are usually portable devices, meant for single users, and have fixed cameras, microphones, and loudspeakers integrated into the console.

2. **Desktop systems** are add-ons (hardware boards or software codec) to normal PCs and laptops, transforming them into videoconferencing devices. A range of different cameras and microphones can be used with the codec, which contains the necessary codec and transmission interfaces.

3. **[WebRTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WebRTC) platforms** use a web browser instead of dedicated [native application](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_application) software. Solutions such as [Adobe Connect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe_Connect) and [Cisco WebEx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisco_WebEx) can be accessed using a URL sent by the meeting organizer, and various degrees of security can be attached to the virtual room. Often the user must download and install a [browser extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browser_extension) to enable access to the local camera and microphone and establish a connection to the meeting. But WebRTC does not require any special software, instead a WebRTC-compliant web browser itself provides the facilities for 1-to-1 and 1-to-many videoconferencing calls. Several enhancements to WebRTC are provided by independent vendors.

### Videoconferencing modes

Videoconferencing systems use several methods to determine which video feed or feeds to display.[^cite_note-V\&VCF-39]<sup>: 11–16 </sup>

*Continuous Presence* simply displays all participants at the same time, usually with the exception that the viewer either does not see their own feed, or sees their own feed in miniature.

*Voice-Activated Switch* selectively chooses a feed to display at each endpoint, with the goal of showing the person who is currently speaking. This is done by choosing the feed (other than the viewer) which has the loudest audio input (perhaps with some filtering to avoid switching for very short-lived volume spikes). Often, if no remote parties are currently speaking, the feed with the last speaker remains on the screen.

[Acoustic echo cancellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustic_echo_cancellation) (AEC) is a processing [algorithm](775.md) that uses the knowledge of audio output to monitor audio input and filter from it noises that echo back after some time delay. If unattended, these echoes can be re-amplified several times, leading to problems including:

- The remote party hearing their own voice coming back at them (usually significantly delayed)

- Strong [reverberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverberation), which makes the voice channel useless

- Howling created by feedback

Echo cancellation is a processor-intensive task that usually works over a narrow range of sound delays.

### Bandwidth requirements



**[Deutsche Telekom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Telekom) T-View 100** [ISDN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISDN)-type videophone meant for home offices and small businesses, with a lens cover which can be rotated upward for privacy

See also: [Broadband Internet access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadband_Internet_access)

Videophones have historically employed a variety of transmission and reception bandwidths, which can be understood as data transmission speeds. The lower the transmission/reception bandwidth, the lower the data transfer rate, resulting in a progressively limited and poorer image quality (i.e. lower resolution and/or frame rate). Data transfer rates and live video image quality are related but are also subject to other factors such as [data compression techniques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_compression). Some early videophones employed very low data transmission rates with a resulting poor video quality.

*Broadband bandwidth* is often called *high-speed*, because it usually has a high rate of data transmission. In general, any connection of 256 kbit/s (0.256 Mbit/s) or greater is more concisely considered broadband Internet. The [International Telecommunication Union Telecommunication Standardization Sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Telecommunication_Union_Telecommunication_Standardization_Sector) (ITU-T) recommendation [I.113](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I.113?action=edit\&redlink=1) has defined broadband as a transmission capacity at 1.5 to 2 Mbit/s. The [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) (United States) definition of broadband is 25 Mbit/s.[^cite_note-40]

Currently, adequate video for some purposes becomes possible at data rates lower than the ITU-T broadband definition, with rates of 768 kbit/s and 384 kbit/s used for some videoconferencing applications, and rates as low as 100 kbit/s used for videophones using [H.264/MPEG-4 AVC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.264/MPEG-4_AVC) compression protocols. The newer [MPEG-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MPEG-4) [video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_compression) and [audio compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_data_compression) format can deliver high-quality video at 2 Mbit/s, which is at the low end of [cable modem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_modem) and [ADSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ADSL) broadband performance.

### Standards

Such devices need to route calls through a Video Communication Server to be able to reach [H.323](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.323) systems, a process known as "interworking" (2009).

- ITU [H.320](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.320) is known as the standard for [public switched telephone networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_switched_telephone_network) (PSTN) or videoconferencing over integrated services digital networks. While still prevalent in Europe, [ISDN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISDN) was never widely adopted in the United States and Canada.

- ITU [H.264](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.264) [Scalable Video Coding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalable_Video_Coding) (SVC) is a compression standard that enables videoconferencing systems to achieve highly error resilient [Internet Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_Protocol) (IP) video transmissions over the public Internet without quality-of-service enhanced lines.

  [^cite_note-41]

  &#x20;This standard has enabled wide scale deployment of high definition desktop videoconferencing and made possible new architectures,

  [^cite_note-42]

  &#x20;which reduces latency between the transmitting sources and receivers, resulting in more fluid communication without pauses. In addition, an attractive factor for IP videoconferencing is that it is easier to set up for use along with [web conferencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_conferencing) and [data collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_collaboration). These combined technologies enable users to have a richer [multimedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimedia) environment for live meetings, collaboration and presentations.

- [ITU-T V.80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITU-T_V.80): videoconferencing is generally compatibilized with [H.324](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.324) standard point-to-point videotelephony over regular (POTS) phone lines.

The [Unified Communications Interoperability Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Communications_Interoperability_Forum) (UCIF), a non-profit alliance between communications vendors, launched in May 2010. ### Call setup

Videoconferencing in the late 20th century was limited to the [H.323](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.323) protocol (notably Cisco's [SCCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skinny_Client_Control_Protocol) implementation was an exception), but newer videophones often use [SIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Session_Initiation_Protocol), which is often easier to set up in home networking environments.[^cite_note-45] It is a text-based protocol, incorporating many elements of the [Hypertext Transfer Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertext_Transfer_Protocol) (HTTP) and the [Simple Mail Transfer Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_Mail_Transfer_Protocol) (SMTP).[^cite_note-46] H.323 is still used, but more commonly for business videoconferencing, while SIP is more commonly used in personal consumer videophones. A number of call-setup methods based on [instant messaging](55951.md) protocols such as [Skype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skype) also now provide video.

Another protocol used by videophones is [H.324](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.324), which mixes call setup and video compression. Videophones that work on regular phone lines typically use H.324, but the bandwidth is limited by the modem to around 33 kbit/s, limiting the video quality and frame rate. A slightly modified version of H.324 called [3G-324M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G-324M) defined by [3GPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3GPP) is also used by some cellphones that allow video calls, typically for use only in [UMTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UMTS) networks.[^cite_note-IMTC-47][^cite_note-48]

There is also [H.320](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.320) standard, which specified technical requirements for narrow-band visual telephone systems and terminal equipment, typically for videoconferencing and videophone services. It applied mostly to dedicated circuit-based switched network (point-to-point) connections of moderate or high bandwidth, such as through the medium-bandwidth [ISDN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISDN) digital phone protocol or a fractionated high bandwidth T1 lines. Modern products based on H.320 standard usually support also H.323 standard.[^cite_note-49]

The [IAX2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAX2) protocol also supports videophone calls natively, using the protocol's own capabilities to transport alternate media streams. A few hobbyists obtained the Nortel 1535 Color SIP Videophone cheaply in 2010 as surplus after [Nortel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nortel)'s bankruptcy and deployed the sets on the [Asterisk (PBX)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asterisk_\(PBX\)) platform. While additional software is required to patch together multiple video feeds for conference calls or convert between dissimilar video standards, SIP calls between two identical handsets within the same PBX were relatively straightforward.[^cite_note-50]

Further information: [Video codec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_codec)

### Conferencing layers

The components within a videoconferencing system can be divided up into several different layers: User Interface, Conference Control, Control or Signaling Plane, and Media Plane.

Videoconferencing User Interfaces (VUI) can be either graphical or voice-responsive. Many in the industry have encountered both types of interface, and normally a graphical interface is encountered on a computer. User interfaces for conferencing have a number of different uses; they can be used for scheduling, setup, and making a video call. Through the user interface, the administrator is able to control the other three layers of the system.

Conference Control performs resource allocation, management, and routing. This layer along with the User Interface creates meetings (scheduled or unscheduled) or adds and removes participants from a conference.

Control (Signaling) Plane contains the stacks that signal different endpoints to create a call and/or a conference. Signals can be, but are not limited to, [H.323](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.323) and [Session Initiation Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Session_Initiation_Protocol) (SIP) Protocols. These signals control incoming and outgoing connections as well as session parameters.

The Media Plane controls the audio and video mixing and streaming. This layer manages Real-Time Transport Protocols, User Datagram Packets (UDP) and [Real-Time Transport Control Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTP_Control_Protocol) (RTCP). The RTP and UDP normally carry information such the payload type which is the type of codec, frame rate, video size, and many others. RTCP on the other hand acts as a quality control Protocol for detecting errors during streaming.[^cite_note-V\&VCF-39]

### Multipoint control

Main article: [Multipoint Control Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multipoint_Control_Unit)

Simultaneous videoconferencing among three or more remote points is possible in a hardware-based system by means of a [Multipoint Control Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multipoint_Control_Unit) (MCU). This is a bridge that interconnects calls from several sources (in a similar way to the audio conference call). All parties call the MCU, or the MCU can also call the parties which are going to participate, in sequence. There are MCU bridges for IP and ISDN-based videoconferencing. There are MCUs which are pure software and others that are a combination of hardware and software. An MCU is characterized according to the number of simultaneous calls it can handle, its ability to conduct transposing of data rates and protocols, and features such as Continuous Presence, in which multiple parties can be seen on-screen at once. MCUs can be stand-alone hardware devices, or they can be embedded into dedicated videoconferencing units.

The MCU consists of two logical components:

1. A single multipoint controller (MC), and

2. Multipoint Processors (MP), sometimes referred to as the mixer.

The MC controls the conferencing while it is active on the signaling plane, which is simply where the system manages conferencing creation, endpoint signaling and in-conferencing controls. This component negotiates parameters with every endpoint in the network and controls conferencing resources. While the MC controls resources and signaling negotiations, the MP operates on the media plane and receives media from each endpoint. The MP generates output streams from each endpoint and redirects the information to other endpoints in the conference.

Some systems are capable of multipoint conferencing with no MCU, stand-alone, embedded or otherwise. These use a standards-based H.323 technique known as *decentralized multipoint*, where each station in a multipoint call exchanges video and audio directly with the other stations with no central *manager* or other bottleneck. The advantages of this technique are that the video and audio will generally be of higher quality because they do not have to be relayed through a central point. Also, users can make ad hoc multipoint calls without any concern for the availability or control of an MCU. This added convenience and quality comes at the expense of some increased network bandwidth, because every station must transmit to every other station directly.[^cite_note-V\&VCF-39]

### Cloud storage

Cloud-based videoconferencing can be used without the hardware generally required by other videoconferencing systems, and can be designed for use by [SMEs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_and_medium_enterprises),[^cite_note-51] or larger international or multinational corporations like [Facebook](7529378.md).[^cite_note-Stair-52][^cite_note-53] Cloud-based systems can handle either 2D or 3D video broadcasting.[^cite_note-54] Cloud-based systems can also implement mobile calls, VOIP, and other forms of video calling. They can also come with a video recording function to archive past meetings.[^cite_note-55]

High speed Internet connectivity has become more widely available and affordable, as has good-quality video capture and display hardware. Mobile phones can also use broadband wireless Internet, whether through the cell phone network or over a local [Wi-Fi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi) connection, along with software-based videophone apps to make calls to any video-capable Internet user, whether mobile or fixed.

A higher level of videoconferencing that employs advanced telecommunication technologies and high-resolution displays is called *[telepresence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telepresence)*.

Today the principles, if not the precise mechanisms, of a videophone are employed by many users worldwide in the form of [webcam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webcam) videocalls using personal computers, with inexpensive webcams, microphones, and free video calling [Web client](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_client) programs. Thus an activity that was disappointing as a separate service has found a niche as a minor feature in software products intended for other purposes.

### Education



Indonesian and U.S. students participate in an educational videoconference (2010)

See also: [Distance education](8997.md)

Videoconferencing has gained widespread popularity within education in recent years, particularly so following the COVID-19 Pandemic of early 2020, when much education provision moved online. It provides students with the chance to learn by participating in two-way communication forums. Because it is live, videotelephony allows teachers to access remote or otherwise isolated learners. Students from diverse communities and backgrounds can come together to learn about one another through practices known as [telecollaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecollaboration)[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] (in foreign language education) and [virtual exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_exchange), although [language barriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_barrier) will continue to be present. Such students are able to explore, communicate, analyze, and share information and ideas with one another.

Educational institutions have promoted videoconferencing as a way to reduce costs and increase student numbers, with lectures and seminars now often being provided online through videoconferencing technology. Videoconferencing offers educational institutes the possibility to provide courses and education to greater numbers of students, dispersed over large geographical areas than can be provided from a single bricks-and-mortar location[^cite_note-67]

Through videoconferencing, students can visit other parts of the world, including museums and other cultural and educational sites. Such [virtual field trips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_virtual_learning_environments) can provide enriched learning opportunities to students, especially those who are geographically isolated or economically disadvantaged. Small schools can use these technologies to pool resources and provide courses, such as in foreign languages, which could not otherwise be offered.

Other benefits that videoconferencing can provide to education include:

- faculty members keeping in touch with classes while attending conferences;

- faculty members attending conferences 'virtually'

  [^cite_note-68]

  [^cite_note-69]

- guest lecturers brought in classes from other institutions;

  [^cite_note-70]

- researchers collaborating with colleagues at other institutions on a regular basis without loss of time due to travel;

- schools with multiple campuses collaborating and sharing professors;

  [^cite_note-71]

- schools from two separate nations engaging in [cross-cultural exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtual_exchange);

  [^cite_note-72]

- faculty members participating in thesis defenses at other institutions;

- administrators on tight schedules collaborating on budget preparation from different parts of campus;

- faculty committee auditioning scholarship candidates;

- researchers answering questions about grant proposals from agencies or review committees;

- alternative enrollment structures to purely in-person attendance;

- student interviews with employers in other cities, and

- [teleseminars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teleseminar).

### Medicine and health

Main article: [Telemedicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemedicine)

Videoconferencing is a highly useful technology for real time [telemedicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemedicine) and [telenursing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telenursing) applications, such as [diagnosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_diagnostics), consulting, prevention, treatment, and transmission of [medical images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging).[^cite_note-73] With videoconferencing, patients may contact [nurses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurse) and [physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician) in [emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_emergency) or routine situations; physicians and other [paramedical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramedical) professionals can discuss cases across large distances. Rural areas can use this technology for diagnostic purposes, thus saving lives and making more efficient use of health care money. For example, a rural medical center in [Ohio](22199.md) used videoconferencing to successfully cut the number of transfers of sick infants to a [hospital](18994221.md) 70 miles (110 km) away. This had previously cost nearly \$10,000 per transfer.[^cite_note-74]

Special peripherals such as [microscopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscope) fitted with [digital cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_camera), [videoendoscopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoscopy), [medical ultrasound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_ultrasound) imaging devices, [otoscopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otoscope), etc., can be used in conjunction with videoconferencing equipment to transmit data about a patient. Recent developments in [mobile collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_collaboration) on hand-held mobile devices have also extended video-conferencing capabilities to locations previously unreachable, such as a remote community, long-term care facility, or a patient's home.[^cite_note-75]

[Mayo Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayo_Clinic) uses videoconferencing to enable collaboration among multidisciplinary teams of specialists developing treatment plans for complex cases. The technology links Mayo locations with doctors at hospitals that require Mayo’s expertise and input.[^cite_note-76]

### Business

Videoconferencing can enable individuals in distant locations to participate in meetings on short notice, with time and money savings. Technology such as [VoIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VoIP) can be used in conjunction with desktop videoconferencing to enable low-cost face-to-face business meetings without leaving the desk, especially for businesses with widespread offices. The technology is also used for [remote work](158555.md). One research report based on a sampling of 1,800 corporate employees showed that, as of June 2010, 54% of the respondents with access to videoconferencing used it "all of the time" or "frequently".[^cite_note-InformationWeek-2010.09.30-77][^cite_note-Plantronics-78]

Aside from traditional meetings, videoconferencing enables collaborative group sessions in which people collaborate to produce products and services. [Industrial Light & Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Light_&_Magic) uses videoconferencing as part of a 24-hour global video effects production environment for the film industry.[^cite_note-79]

Intel Corporation have used videoconferencing to reduce both costs and environmental impacts of its business operations.[^cite_note-MISQuarterly-2012.V11.No2-80]

Videoconferencing is also currently being introduced on online networking websites, in order to help businesses form profitable relationships quickly and efficiently without leaving their place of work. This has been leveraged by banks to connect busy banking professionals with customers in various locations using [video banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_banking) technology.

Videoconferencing on hand-held mobile devices ([mobile collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_collaboration) technology) is being used in industries such as manufacturing, energy, healthcare, insurance, government, and public safety. Live, visual interaction removes traditional restrictions of distance and time, often in locations previously unreachable, such as a manufacturing plant floor thousands of miles away.[^cite_note-81]

In the increasingly [globalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalized) [film industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_industry), videoconferencing has become useful as a method by which creative talent in many different locations can collaborate closely on the complex details of film production. For example, for the 2013 award-winning animated film *[Frozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frozen_\(2013_film\))*, [Burbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burbank,_California)-based [Walt Disney Animation Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_Animation_Studios) hired the [New York City](645042.md)-based husband-and-wife songwriting team of [Robert Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lopez) and [Kristen Anderson-Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristen_Anderson-Lopez) to write the songs, which required two-hour-long transcontinental videoconferences nearly every weekday for about 14 months.[^cite_note-Hogg-82][^cite_note-Truax-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

With the development of lower-cost endpoints, the integration of video cameras into personal computers and mobile devices, and software applications such as [FaceTime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FaceTime), Skype, [Teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Teams), [BlueJeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlueJeans) and Zoom, videoconferencing has changed from just a business-to-business offering to include business-to-consumer (and consumer-to-consumer) use.

Although videoconferencing has frequently proven its value, research has shown that some non-managerial employees prefer not to use it due to several factors, including anxiety.[^cite_note-86] Some such anxieties can often be avoided if managers use the technology as part of the normal course of business. Remote workers can also adopt certain behaviors and best practices to stay connected with their co-workers and company.[^cite_note-87]

Researchers also find that attendees of business and medical videoconferences must work harder to interpret information delivered during a conference than they would if they attended face-to-face.[^cite_note-88] They recommend that those coordinating videoconferences make adjustments to their conferencing procedures and equipment.

### Press

Main article: [Press videoconferencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_videoconferencing)

The concept of [press videoconferencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_videoconferencing) was developed in October 2007 by the PanAfrican Press Association (APPA), a Paris France-based [non-governmental organization](46539.md), to allow African journalists to participate in international [press conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_conferences) on developmental and [good governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_governance) issues.

Press videoconferencing permits international press conferences via videoconferencing over the Internet. Journalists can participate on an international press conference from any location, without leaving their offices or countries. They need only be seated by a computer connected to the Internet in order to ask their questions.

In 2004, the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) introduced the Online Media Briefing Center, a password-protected site available only to professional journalists. The site enables the IMF to present press briefings globally and facilitates direct questions to briefers from the press. The site has been copied by other international organizations since its inception. More than 4,000 journalists worldwide are currently registered with the IMF.

### Sign language



Video Interpreter sign used at VRS/VRI service locations

Main articles: [Video Relay Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_Relay_Service) and [Video Remote Interpreting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_Remote_Interpreting)

One of the first demonstrations of the ability for [telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunication) to help sign language users communicate with each other occurred when [AT\&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T)'s [videophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Videophone) (trademarked as the *Picturephone*) was introduced to the public at the [1964 New York World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_New_York_World's_Fair)—two deaf users were able to communicate freely with each other between the fair and another city.[^cite_note-BellLabs-89] Various universities and other organizations, including [British Telecom's Martlesham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BT_Research#History) facility, have also conducted extensive research on [signing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_language) via video telephony.[^cite_note-NS-1989.08.19-90][^cite_note-Sperling-91][^cite_note-BritishTelecomLaboratories-92]

The use of sign language via videotelephony was hampered for many years due to the difficulty of its use over slow [analog copper phone lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_old_telephone_service),[^cite_note-Sperling-91] coupled with the high cost of better quality [ISDN (data) phone lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_Services_Digital_Network).[^cite_note-NS-1989.08.19-90] Those factors largely disappeared with the introduction of more efficient and powerful [video codecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_codec) and the advent of lower-cost high-speed ISDN data and IP (Internet) services in the 1990s.

#### 21st-century improvements

Significant improvements in video call quality of service for the deaf occurred in the United States in 2003 when [Sorenson Media Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorenson_Media_Inc.?action=edit\&redlink=1) (formerly Sorenson Vision Inc.), a video compression software coding company, developed its VP-100 model stand-alone [videophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Videophone) specifically for the deaf community. It was designed to output its video to the user's television in order to lower the cost of acquisition and to offer remote control and a powerful [video compression codec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorenson_codec) for unequaled video quality and ease of use with video relay services. Favorable reviews quickly led to its popular usage at educational facilities for the deaf, and from there to the greater deaf community.[^cite_note-Fitzgerald-93]

Coupled with similar high-quality videophones introduced by other electronics manufacturers, the [availability of high-speed Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadband_Internet_access), and [sponsored video relay services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_Relay_Service#United_States_VRS_regulation) authorized by the U.S. [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) in 2002, VRS services for the deaf underwent rapid growth in that country.[^cite_note-Fitzgerald-93]



A deaf or hard-of-hearing person uses a Video Relay Service at his workplace to communicate with a hearing person in London (2007).

Using such video equipment in the present day, the deaf, hard-of-hearing, and speech-impaired can communicate between themselves and with hearing individuals using [sign language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_language). The United States and several other countries compensate companies to provide video relay services (VRS). Telecommunication equipment can be used to talk to others via a sign language interpreter, who uses a conventional telephone at the same time to communicate with the deaf person's party. Video equipment is also used to do on-site sign language translation via [Video Remote Interpreting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_Remote_Interpreting) (VRI). The relatively low cost and widespread availability of [3G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G) [mobile phone](19644137.md) technology with video calling capabilities have given deaf and speech-impaired users a greater ability to communicate with the same ease as others. Some wireless operators have even started free sign language gateways.

Sign language interpretation services via VRS or by VRI are useful in the present day where one of the parties is [deaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaf), [hard-of-hearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard-of-hearing), or [speech-impaired (mute)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_disorder). In such cases the interpretation flow is normally within the same principal language, such as [French Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Sign_Language) (LSF) to spoken French, [Spanish Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Sign_Language) (LSE) to spoken Spanish, [British Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Sign_Language) (BSL) to spoken English, and [American Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Sign_Language) (ASL) also to spoken English (since BSL and ASL are completely distinct from each other), [German Sign Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Sign_Language) (DGS) to spoken German, and so on.

[Multilingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilingual) [sign language interpreters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpreters#Sign_language_interpreting), who can also translate as well across principal languages (such as a multilingual interpreter interpreting a call from a deaf person using ASL to reserve a hotel room at a hotel in the [Dominican Republic](8060.md) whose staff speaks Spanish only, therefore the interpreter has to use ASL, spoken Spanish, and spoken English to facilitate the call for the deaf person), are also available, albeit less frequently. Such activities involve considerable mental processing efforts on the part of the translator, since sign languages are distinct [natural languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language) with their own [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics), [semantics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semantics) and [syntax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syntax), different from the aural version of the same principal language.

With video interpreting, [sign language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sign_language) interpreters work remotely with live [video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video) and audio feeds, so that the interpreter can see the deaf or mute party, and converse with the hearing party, and vice versa. Much like [telephone interpreting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_interpreting), video interpreting can be used for situations in which no on-site [interpreters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpreters#Sign_language_interpreting) are available. However, video interpreting cannot be used for situations in which all parties are speaking via telephone alone. VRS and VRI interpretation requires all parties to have the [necessary equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_video_telecommunication_services_and_product_brands). Some advanced equipment enables interpreters to control the video camera remotely, in order to [zoom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoom_lens) in and out or to point the camera toward the party that is signing.

## Descriptive names and terminology

See also: [List of video telecommunication services and product brands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_video_telecommunication_services_and_product_brands)

The name *videophone* never became as standardized as its earlier counterpart *telephone*, resulting in a variety of names and terms being used worldwide, and even within the same region or country. Videophones are also known as *video phones*, *videotelephones* (or *video telephones*) and often by an early trademarked name *Picturephone*, which was the world's first commercial videophone produced in volume. The compound name *videophone* slowly entered into general use after 1950,[^cite_note-MW-96] although *video telephone* likely entered the lexicon earlier after *video* was coined in 1935.[^cite_note-ED-97]

*Videophone calls* (also: *videocalls*, *video chat*)[^cite_note-98] as well as [*Skype* and *Skyping*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skype) in verb form[^cite_note-Toronto_Star-2014-10-01-99] differ from videoconferencing in that they expect to serve individuals, not groups.[^cite_note-Mulbach-1995c-2] However that distinction has become increasingly blurred with technology improvements such as [increased bandwidth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadband_Internet_access) and sophisticated [software clients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_client) that can allow for multiple parties on a call. In general everyday usage the term *videoconferencing* is now frequently used instead of *videocall* for point-to-point calls between two units. Both videophone calls and videoconferencing are also now commonly referred to as a *video link*.

*[Webcams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webcam)* are popular, relatively low-cost devices that can provide live video and audio streams via personal computers, and can be used with many [software clients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_video_telecommunication_services_and_product_brands#Software_Client_brands) for both video calls and videoconferencing.[^cite_note-books.google.com-30]

A *[videoconference system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_video_telecommunication_services_and_product_brands#Videoconferencing_systems_meant_for_multiple_participants)* is generally higher cost than a videophone and deploys greater capabilities. A *videoconference* (also known as a *videoteleconference*) allows two or more locations to communicate via live, simultaneous two-way video and audio transmissions. This is often accomplished by the use of a [multipoint control unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multipoint_control_unit) (a centralized distribution and call management system) or by a similar non-centralized multipoint capability embedded in each videoconferencing unit. Again, technology improvements have circumvented traditional definitions by allowing multiple-party videoconferencing via web-based applications.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

A *[telepresence system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telepresence)* is a high-end videoconferencing system and service usually employed by [enterprise-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company) corporate offices. Telepresence conference rooms use state-of-the-art room designs, video cameras, displays, sound systems and processors, coupled with high-to-very-high capacity bandwidth transmissions.

Typical uses of the various technologies described above include calling one-to-one or conferencing one-to-many or many-to-many for personal, business, educational, [deaf Video Relay Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_Relay_Service) and [tele-medical, diagnostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemedicine) and [rehabilitative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telerehabilitation) purposes.[^cite_note-USAToday-2010.03.31-102] personal videocalls to [inmates incarcerated in penitentiaries](19008450.md), and videoconferencing to resolve [airline engineering issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_maintenance_checks) at [maintenance facilities](1011776.md), are being created or evolving on an ongoing basis.

Other names for *videophone* that have been used in English are: *Viewphone* (the [British Telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Telecom) equivalent to [AT\&T's Picturephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_videotelephony#AT\&T_Picturephone_Mod_I:_1964%E2%80%931970)),[^cite_note-New_Scientist-1966.11.24-103] and *visiophone*, a common French translation that has also crept into limited English usage, as well as over twenty less common names and expressions. Latin-based translations of *videophone* in other languages include *vidéophone* (French), *Bildtelefon* (German), *videotelefono* (Italian), both *videófono* and *videoteléfono* (Spanish), both *beeldtelefoon* and *videofoon* (Dutch), and *videofonía* (Catalan).

A *telepresence robot* (also [telerobotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telerobotics)) is a robotically controlled and motorized videoconferencing display to help give a better sense of remote physical presence for communication and collaboration in an office, home, school, etc. when one cannot be there in person. The robotic avatar device can move about and look around at the command of the remote person it represents.[^cite_note-104]

## Popular culture



Dr. Heywood Floyd in the 1968 film [*2001: A Space Odyssey*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001:_A_Space_Odyssey_\(film\)) calls his daughter on Earth.

In [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction) literature, names commonly associated with videophones include *telephonoscope*, *telephote*, *viewphone*, *vidphone*, *vidfone*, and *visiphone*. The first example was probably the cartoon "Edison's Telephonoscope" by George du Maurier in *Punch* 1878.[^cite_note-105] In «In the year 2889», published 1889, the French author Jules Verne predicts that «*The transmission of speech is an old story; the transmission of images by means of sensitive mirrors connected by wires is a thing but of yesterday.*»[^cite_note-106] Early examples in Anglophone literature, using the word videotelephone, includes *[The World of Null-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_of_Null-A)* [Harl Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harl_Vincent) from late 1920s.[^cite_note-107] In many [science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_fiction) [movies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movies) and TV programs that are set in the future, videophones were used as a primary method of communication. One of the first movies where a videophone was used was [Fritz Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Lang)'s *[Metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_\(1927_film\))* (1927).[^cite_note-108]

Other notable examples of videophones in popular culture include an iconic scene from the 1968 film [*2001: A Space Odyssey*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001:_A_Space_Odyssey_\(film\)) set on [Space Station V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_spacecraft_from_the_Space_Odyssey_series?action=edit\&redlink=1#Space_Station_V). The movie was released shortly before AT\&T began its efforts to commercialize its Picturephone Mod II service in several cities and depicts a video call to Earth using an advanced AT\&T videophone—which it predicts will cost \$1.70 for a two-minute call in 2001 (a fraction of the company's real rates on Earth in 1968). Film director [Stanley Kubrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Kubrick) strove for scientific accuracy, relying on interviews with scientists and engineers at [Bell Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Labs) in the United States. Dr. [Larry Rabiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Rabiner) of Bell Labs, discussing videophone research in the documentary *2001: The Making of a Myth*, stated that in the mid-to late-1960s videophones "... captured the imagination of the public and ... of Mr. Kubrick and the people who reported to him". In one *2001* movie scene a central character, Dr. Heywood Floyd, calls home to contact his family, a social feature noted in the *Making of a Myth*. Floyd talks with and views his daughter from a [space station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_station) in [orbit above the Earth](568972.md), discussing what type of present he should bring home for her.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111]

Other earlier examples of videophones in popular culture included a videophone that was featured in the [Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros.) cartoon, *[Plane Daffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plane_Daffy)*, in which the female spy [Hatta Mari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatta_Mari) used a videophone to communicate with [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md) (1944), as well as a device with the same functionality has been used by the comic strip character [*Dick Tracy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Tracy#Evolution_of_the_strip), who often used his "2-way wrist TV" to communicate with police headquarters.[^cite_note-112] (1964–1977).

By the early 2010s videotelephony and videophones had become commonplace and unremarkable in various forms of media, in part due to their real and ubiquitous presence in common electronic devices and laptop computers. Additionally, TV programming increasingly used videophones to interview subjects of interest and to present live coverage by news correspondents, via the Internet or by satellite links. In the mass market media, the popular U.S. [TV talk show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_talk_show) hostess [Oprah Winfrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oprah_Winfrey) incorporated videotelephony into her TV program on a regular basis from May 21, 2009, with an initial episode called *Where the Skype Are You?*, as part of a marketing agreement with the Internet telecommunication company [Skype](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skype).[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]
