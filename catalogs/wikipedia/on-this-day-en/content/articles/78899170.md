The **Zhelyazkov Government** was the 105th cabinet of Bulgaria. It was approved by the parliament of Bulgaria on 16 January 2025.[^cite_note-3] The cabinet is a minority coalition government with ministers nominated from [GERB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GERB), [BSP–OL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BSP_%E2%80%93_United_Left) and [ITN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/There_is_Such_a_People).[^cite_note-4] The cabinet resigned on 11 December 2025 after weeks of anti-government protests in the whole country and abroad over its economic policies and its perceived failure to tackle corruption.[^cite_note-5] The government resignation was approved by the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Bulgaria) on 12 December. On February 19, 2026, the cabinet handed over power to the caretaker government of Andrey Gyurov.&#x20;

The cabinet was initially elected with the support of [APS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_Rights_and_Freedoms). However, on 16 April 2025, following disagreements with the government and the passing of legislation with [DPS–NN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DPS_%E2%80%93_A_New_Beginning), the party announced it would no longer support the government.[^cite_note-6] The government's primary commitment has been the country's entry into the eurozone in 2026.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

Though the government is not committed to creating a formal deal, it has been widely reported on and accepted that the government's majority depends on the support of DPS–NN.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Prior to [the approval of Bulgaria's accession to the Eurozone in July 2025](5516984.md), [PP–DB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Continue_the_Change_%E2%80%93_Democratic_Bulgaria), the largest parliamentary opposition, had pledged to abstain in no-confidence votes.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]
