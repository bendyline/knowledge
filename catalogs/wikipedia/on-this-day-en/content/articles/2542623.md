**Khánh Hòa** (Vietnamese pronunciation: [\[xaɲ˧˥ hʷa˨˩\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Vietnamese), [Cham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cham_language): ꨇꩆꨨꨩ ꨨꨯꨀꨩ) is a coastal [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Vietnam) in the south region of [Vietnam](202354.md). It borders [Đắk Lắk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90%E1%BA%AFk_L%E1%BA%AFk) to the northwest, [Lâm Đồng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A2m_%C4%90%E1%BB%93ng) to the southwest and the [South China Sea](74209.md) to the east.

In June 2025, it was merged with [Ninh Thuận province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninh_Thu%E1%BA%ADn_province), forming the new Khánh Hòa Province with an area of 8,555.86 km<sup>2</sup> (3,303.44 sq mi) and a population of 2,243,554 people.[^cite_note-Quốc_hội-2] When [Jean-Louis Taberd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Louis_Taberd) published the map of Annam in 1838, the province was named "Bình Hòa Trấn," with "Trấn" meaning "Department" or province.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMeinheit2016-5]

## Geography



Ba Hồ stream in Khánh Hòa province

The highest peak in the province is Vọng Phu Mountain (2,051 m (6,729 ft)) at the border of [Đắk Lắk province](1461530.md).[^cite_note-Atlat-6] The largest lowland area is located around [Ninh Hòa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninh_H%C3%B2a) in the north of the province.[^cite_note-Atlat-6] 87,100 ha (215,230 acres) or 16.7% of Khánh Hòa's total area are used for farming.[^cite_note-GSO09-7][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStatistical_data-8] Forests cover more than half of the province's area.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStatistical_data-8]

## Demographics

Khánh Hòa had an urban population of 466,500 people or 40.7% of the total in 2007, making it the most urbanized province of the [South Central Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_Coast). The average population growth per year between 2000 and 2007 was 1.26%, close to the average of the region. Growth was strongest in urban areas (2.24%).[^cite_note-GSO09calc-9]

## Economy

Khánh Hòa has a GDP per capita of 16.1 million [VND](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VND) (2007).[^cite_note-GSO09calc-9] Its agricultural sector is smaller than industry and services. Khánh Hòa has had a trade surplus in years, with exports in 2007 of US\$503.3 million and imports of US\$222.5 million.[^cite_note-GSO09-7]

Rice harvests are 188,500t in 2007. Its output of sugar cane (738,200t in 2007, 4.25% of the national figure) and cashew nuts (5,238t, 1.74%) are more significant.[^cite_note-GSO09calc-9] Khánh Hòa is one of the provinces with a higher [gross output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_output) in fishing than in agriculture.[^cite_note-GSO09-7]

[Nha Trang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang) is the [South Central Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_Coast)'s second largest industrial center[^cite_note-Atlat-6] and the province in general accounts for more than one fifth of the region's industrial GDP.[^cite_note-GSO09calc-9] Food processing industries include those processing local fishing products and food for shrimp farms. Other industrial sectors produce beverages, fabric, textiles, paper, and construction materials.[^cite_note-GSO09-7] The province has investment related to the former Russian naval base at [Cam Ranh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cam_Ranh), to which around 30 factories were attached.[^cite_note-11]

## Infrastructure

Khánh Hòa is located along Vietnam's north–south transport corridors. [National Road 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Route_1_\(Vietnam\)) and the [North–South Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_Railway_\(Vietnam\)) run through the province.[^cite_note-Road_Atlas_2004-12]

[Cam Ranh International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cam_Ranh_International_Airport) is located in the south of the province and is the fourth busiest airport in Vietnam, with an international terminal serving as a gateway for international tourists.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

## Tourist attraction

Nha Trang is the capital and largest city of Khanh Hoa Province, located on Vietnam's south-central coast. The city is known for its beaches. Other attractions include Doc Let Beach and Tu Van Pagoda, a Buddhist temple established in 1968.[^cite_note-15]
