**Nagasaki Prefecture** (長崎県, *Nagasaki-ken[^cite_note-3]*) is a [prefecture](74517.md) of [Japan](15573.md), mainly located on the island of [Kyūshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ky%C5%ABsh%C5%AB), although it also includes a number of islands off Kyūshū's northwest coast - including Tsushima and Iki. Nagasaki Prefecture has a population of 1,246,481 (1 February 2025) and has a geographic area of 4,130 [km<sup>2</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_kilometre) (1,594 [sq mi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sq_mi)). Nagasaki Prefecture borders [Saga Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saga_Prefecture) to the northeast.

[Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki) is the capital and largest city of Nagasaki Prefecture, with other major cities including [Sasebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasebo), [Isahaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isahaya,_Nagasaki), and [Ōmura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cmura,_Nagasaki). Nagasaki Prefecture is located in western Kyūshū with a territory consisting of many mainland [peninsulas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula) centered around [Ōmura Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cmura_Bay), as well as islands and [archipelagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) including [Tsushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsushima_Island) and [Iki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iki_Island) in the [Korea Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Strait) and the [Gotō Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Got%C5%8D_Islands) in the [East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Sea). Nagasaki Prefecture is known for its century-long [trading history with the Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanban_trade) and as the sole place of direct trade and exchange between Japan and the outside world during the *[Sakoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakoku)* period. Nagasaki Prefecture is home to several of the [Hidden Christian Sites in the Nagasaki Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Christian_Sites_in_the_Nagasaki_Region) which have been declared a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md).

## History

See also: [Historic Sites of Nagasaki Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Sites_of_Nagasaki_Prefecture)

Nagasaki Prefecture was created by merging of the western half of the former province of [Hizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hizen_Province) with the island provinces of [Tsushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsushima_Province) and [Iki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iki_Province).[^cite_note-4] Facing China and Korea, the region around [Hirado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirado,_Nagasaki) was a traditional center for traders and pirates.



[Kuichi Uchida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuichi_Uchida)'s image of Nagasaki in 1872

During the 16th century, [Catholic missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_missions) and traders from [Portugal](23033.md) arrived and became active in Hirado and Nagasaki, which became a major center for foreign trade. After being given free rein in [Oda Nobunaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oda_Nobunaga)'s period, the missionaries were forced out little by little, until finally, in the [Tokugawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period) era, Christianity was banned under the *[Sakoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakoku)* national isolation policy: Japanese foreign trade was restricted to Chinese and Dutch traders based at [Dejima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dejima) in Nagasaki. However, *[Kirishitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishitan)* (Japanese Christian) worship continued underground. These *[Kakure Kirishitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakure_Kirishitan)* (hidden Christians) were tried at every step, forced to step on [*fumi-e*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumie) ("trample pictures", images of the [Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Mary) and saints) to prove that they were non-Christian. With the banishment of all Catholic [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionaries), traders from Catholic countries were also forced out of the country. Along with them, their children, half Japanese and half European, were forced to leave. The majority was sent to Jagatara ([Jakarta](16275.md)) and are still remembered by the locals as the people who wrote the poignant letters which were smuggled across the sea to their homeland.

Today, Nagasaki has prominent Catholic churches, and the [Hidden Christian Sites in the Nagasaki Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Christian_Sites_in_the_Nagasaki_Region), have been included on the [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Sites_in_Japan).



Nagasaki Prefect Office, Meiji Period

During the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration), Nagasaki and [Sasebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasebo,_Nagasaki) became major ports for foreign trade, and eventually major military bases and shipbuilding centers for the [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy) and the [Mitsubishi Heavy Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_Heavy_Industries) up to [World War II](32927.md). [On August 9, 1945, the United States dropped an atomic bomb on Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki), which destroyed all buildings in a 1.6-kilometre (1.0 mi) radius from the point of impact and extensively damaged other parts of the city. Roughly 39,000 people were killed, including 27,778 Japanese munitions workers, 2,000 Korean forced workers, and 150 Japanese soldiers. About 68–80% of the industrial production was destroyed to the point it would not recover for months or at least a year.



An overview of 1957 Isahaya floods

Nagasaki Prefecture contains many areas prone to heavy rain and subsequent landslide damage. In July 1957, mainly in the Isahaya area, damage from heavy rains, flooding and landslides lead to a death toll of 586, with 136 people missing and 3,860 injured. In July 1982, typhoon damage in the Nagasaki area lead to 299 fatalities, according to a report by the Japanese government.

## Geography

Nagasaki borders [Saga Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saga_Prefecture) on the east, and is otherwise surrounded by water, including [Ariake Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariake_Bay), the [Tsushima Straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsushima_Straits) (far from [Busan](68115.md) and [South Gyeongsang Province](319775.md), [South Korea](27019.md)), and the [East China Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Sea). It also includes a large number of islands such as [Tsushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsushima_Island), [Iki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iki_Island) and [Goto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goto_Islands). Most of the prefecture is near the coast and there are a number of ports such as [Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki,_Nagasaki) and a [United States Navy](20518076.md) base at [Sasebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasebo,_Nagasaki).

As of 1 April 2014, 18% of the total land area of the prefecture was designated as [Natural Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan#History), namely the [Saikai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saikai_National_Park) and [Unzen-Amakusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unzen-Amakusa_National_Park) National Parks; [Genkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genkai_Quasi-National_Park) and [Iki-Tsushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iki-Tsushima_Quasi-National_Park) Quasi-National Parks; and [Hokushō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokush%C5%8D_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Nishi Sonogi Hantō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishi_Sonogi_Hant%C5%8D_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Nomo Hantō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomo_Hant%C5%8D_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Ōmurawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cmurawan_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Shimabara Hantō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimabara_Hant%C5%8D_Prefectural_Natural_Park), and [Taradake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taradake_Prefectural_Natural_Park_\(Nagasaki\)) Prefectural Natural Parks.[^cite_note-5]

## Culture

### Religion

Religious denominations in the Nagasaki Prefecture (1996)

[^cite_note-1996statistics-6]

1. Pure Land Buddhism (19.5%)

2. Zen Buddhism (3.60%)

3. Tendai or Shingon Buddhism (4.90%)

4. Soka Gakkai (3.00%)

5. Nichiren Buddhism (5.10%)

6. Other Buddhist schools (3.00%)

7. Christianity (5.10%)

8. Shinto sects (2.00%)

9. Folk Shinto or no religion (53.8%)

Nagasaki is the most [Christianized area in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Japan) with Roman Catholic missions having been established there as early as the 16th century. [Shusaku Endo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusaku_Endo)'s novel *[Silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silence_\(End%C5%8D_novel\))* draws from the oral history of the local Christian ([Kirishitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirishitan)) communities, both [Kakure Kirishitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakure_Kirishitan) and [Hanare Kirishitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanare_Kirishitan).

As of 2002, there are 68,617 [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) in Nagasaki Prefecture, accounting for 4.52 percent of the population of the prefecture.

## Politics

Main article: [Politics of Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Nagasaki)

The current governor of Nagasaki is [Ken Hirata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Hirata) who defeated incumbent [Kengo Oishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kengo_Oishi) in 2026.[^cite_note-7] Oishi, a doctor, was 39 years old when he took office, and the youngest sitting prefectural governor in Japan.[^cite_note-8] [Hōdō Nakamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C5%8Dd%C5%8D_Nakamura), a three-term governor, was first elected in 2010 to succeed [Genjirō Kaneko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genjir%C5%8D_Kaneko) and was previously a vice-governor.

The [Nagasaki Prefectural Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki_Prefectural_Assembly?action=edit\&redlink=1) has a regular membership of 46, elected in 16 electoral districts in unified regional elections (last round: [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Japanese_unified_local_elections)). As of April 2014, the [LDP](164231.md)-led caucus has 23 members, the [DPJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Japan,_1998\))-[SDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Japan\))-led caucus 17.

In the National Diet, Nagasaki is represented by four directly elected members of the House of Representatives and two (one per ordinary election) of the House of Councillors. After the most recent national elections of 2010, 2012 and 2013, Nagasaki sends an all-LDP delegation to the Diet (excluding members who lost election in Nagasaki districts, but were elected to the proportional representation segment of the House of Representatives in the [Kyūshū block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ky%C5%ABsh%C5%AB_PR_block?action=edit\&redlink=1)).
