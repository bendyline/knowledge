**Leipzig/Halle Airport** ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **LEJ**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **EDDP**) is an [international airport](1519705.md) located in [Schkeuditz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schkeuditz), [Saxony](28395.md), [Germany](11867.md), and serves both [Leipzig](17955.md), Saxony, and [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_\(Saale\)), [Saxony-Anhalt](38138.md). It is a [state-owned enterprise](204768.md) and allows 24 hour take off and landing for cargo flights. It is notable for having the highest number of freight-only movements (arrivals and departures) in the entirety of the [European Union](9317.md).

As of 2024, the airport was the second-busiest cargo airport in Germany after [Frankfurt Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_Airport), and the fourth-busiest in Europe, having handled 1,383,319 metric tonnes of cargo. It is [Germany's 12th largest airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Germany) by passengers and handled more than 2.19 million passengers in 2024, mainly on flights to European leisure destinations. The airport is [DHL Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DHL_Aviation)'s main European hub and [AeroLogic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AeroLogic)'s main hub. Military installations have also been built at the airport for [NATO](21133.md) and EU military aircraft.[^cite_note-nato-7]

## History

The airport's restaurant in 1931

### Early years

The airport was built new from the ground up at a location between Halle and Leipzig from 1926 and opened in 1927 as **Schkeuditz Airfield** (*Flugplatz Schkeuditz*). In 1928, the airport was renamed **Halle/Leipzig Airport** (*Flughafen Halle/Leipzig*), which would later change order of the city names to its current name.[^cite_note-Buchsbaum-8]

The airport structures were expanded in the 1930s, including a restaurant. A new terminal was added in 1936/1937, built in the then-widespread [Nazi style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_architecture). At the beginning of [World War II](32927.md), the airport was the fourth [in the country](21212.md) for commercial traffic, with more than 40 takeoffs per day. This ended competely during the war, as the airport was entirely taken by the [Luftwaffe](17885.md) and used only for military purposes. On April 16, 1944, it was bombed and mostly destroyed by Allied planes.[^cite_note-Buchsbaum-8]

During the early years of the [German Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Democratic_Republic), the airport was rebuilt though used exclusively as an industrial airport. In 1957-1960 a new, 2500 m-long runway was added as part of plans of commercial air traffic in the country, but traffic in Leipzig remained scarce. Starting from 1963, twice a year the airport was used as the seat of the [Leipzig Trade Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_Trade_Fair), with regular service from the state's flag carrier [Interflug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interflug); the structures, however, were dismantled after the fair's end, and only in 1968 a more modern terminal was built. In 1972, for the first time, the airport operated for the whole year.[^cite_note-Buchsbaum-8]

On 18 March 1986, [Air France](14998012.md) flew a [Concorde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concorde) to Leipzig to coincide with the [Leipziger Buchmesse (Leipzig book fair)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig_Book_Fair) as part of the [special Concorde flights to East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concorde_operational_history#Special_flights_to_East_Germany). Two days later, [British Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Airways) flew a Concorde to Leipzig/Halle only once and never again.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] In the following years, Air France operated Concorde flights from Paris when the trade fair was held in Leipzig. Leipzig remains the only airport in Communist Bloc to be served by Concorde and only in Germany to have frequent Concorde flights (about ten or so times).

By 1988, traffic had doubled from 1972, reaching some 550,000 passengers per year. The terminal was enlarged including a new immigration hall, completed in 1984. The following year the departure hall was also upgraded.[^cite_note-Buchsbaum-8]

In 1991, after the [German Reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification), the current Terminal A was modernized. In 1992 for the first time annual passenger numbers reached 1,000,000. Construction of Terminal B was completed in 1996,[^cite_note-11] and a new 3,500 m-long runway was added starting from 1998, being completed by 2003. A new control tower was inaugurated in 2000.[^cite_note-Buchsbaum-8]

### Development since 2000

Despite its name, the airport is located in the town of Schkeuditz, [Nordsachsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordsachsen). A deal between the city of Leipzig and the [Landkreis Delitzsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delitzsch_\(district\)) led to a land exchange. In 2007, Leipzig received land outside the airport while ownership of the airport land was transferred to Delitzsch. The District of Delitzsch, which by now has merged to become part of Landkreis Nordsachsen, owns and claims taxes from the grounds and commercial interest from the airport.

In early 2008, [DHL Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DHL_Aviation) moved its European hub from [Brussels Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels_Airport) to Leipzig/Halle, leading to a significant increase in cargo traffic at the airport. Leipzig bid to host the 2012 [Olympic Games](22576.md) and the airport was modernised as a result, even though London was eventually awarded the games.

In spring 2013, [Ryanair](262878.md) announced the start of operating six routes from Leipzig/Halle. One year later all of them, except the flights to [London–Stansted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Stansted_Airport), were terminated.[^cite_note-12] In February 2015, [Etihad Regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etihad_Regional) announced the immediate termination of all their Leipzig/Halle operations which had started only two years earlier. All three routes were shut down while two newly announced ones did not start.[^cite_note-13]

In September 2016, [Pakistan International Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_International_Airlines) announced plans to replace Manchester on their [Islamabad](51153.md) - [Manchester](20206.md) - [New York City](645042.md) route with Leipzig/Halle in 2017 to lower operating costs and save time. As the airline holds [Fifth Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedoms_of_the_air#Fifth_freedom) rights, this would have been Leipzig/Halle's first passenger service to the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-14] In October 2017, the airline announced it would end all routes to the [United States](3434750.md), so plans to use Leipzig as a layover to [New York-JFK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York-JFK) never materialised.[^cite_note-15]

In October 2016, [DHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DHL) started major expansion of its freight facilities at the airport. Two new [cargo terminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_terminal) increased handling capacity by 50 percent.[^cite_note-16]

In April 2018, the largest passenger airline at Leipzig/Halle Airport measured by weekly departures was [Condor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condor_Flugdienst) with 55 outgoing flights per week. The airline had stationed three [Airbus A321](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A321) aircraft at the airport. The second-largest airline was [Small Planet Airlines (Germany)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Planet_Airlines_\(Germany\)) with 18 outgoing flights per week. The airline stationed an [Airbus A320](164933.md) aircraft to serve several leisure destinations for [TUI Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TUI_Group).[^cite_note-17] It was followed by [SunExpress Deutschland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SunExpress_Deutschland) which operated 15 weekly departures and stationed for this a [Boeing 737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) aircraft at the airport.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

In August 2018, [DHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DHL) announced further expansion of its facilities at the airport. In addition to enlarging the apron, a new pilot training center was also planned. The number of employees was to increase from 5700 to 6000.[^cite_note-20] At the same time, [EAT Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EAT_Leipzig) announced that they would add five used [Airbus A330](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A330-200F) freighters to their fleet, for a total of 36 aircraft.[^cite_note-21] Also in August 2018, the Russian [Volga-Dnepr Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga-Dnepr_Group) announced plans to launch a new German freight subsidiary based at the airport.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] As of 2021, there were up to 170 nighttime take offs and landings for cargo operations.[^cite_note-LZ-24]

In 2018, the airport and [DHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DHL) extended an agreement for the latter to maintain a freight hub in Leipzig/Halle until at least 2019.[^cite_note-25]

In October 2024, [Ryanair](262878.md) announced the termination of all routes at three German airports including Leipzig/Halle, citing high operating costs.[^cite_note-26] In October 2025, [Condor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condor_\(airline\)) announced the closure of its base at the airport with immediate effect, handing over a downsized network to sister carrier [Marabu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marabu_\(airline\)).[^cite_note-condorhandover-27][^cite_note-ch-avmarabu-28]

## Facilities

### Terminal

The modern airport terminal structure extends over the adjacent motorway and railway. It integrates the main car park as well as the check-in-facilities and is connected to a pier equipped with six [jet bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_bridge) as well as several apron stands. Due to its compact design, walking distances at the airport are short. The airport terminal has immigration facilities for international flights but no international transfer area.

### Runways

The airport has two runways. Terminal access is south of the railway. Runway 08L/26R runs parallel to the road north of the railway, requiring aircraft to taxi on a bridge over the tracks and roads.

### Military

Some US airlines have flown to Leipzig/Halle on behalf of the [US Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Department_of_Defense), to bring [US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army) troops and US Marines to [Afghanistan](737.md) and [Iraq](7515928.md). Leipzig/Halle is used as a [technical stop](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/technical%20stop) for refueling on these flights.[^cite_note-62] They do not appear at any official timetable. Marines and soldiers flown via Leipzig/Halle are listed as transit passengers in its traffic statistics. Military charter flights are also operated via Leipzig/Halle, for example on behalf of [NATO](21133.md).[^cite_note-nato-7]

## Ground transportation

### Train



Leipzig/Halle Airport railway station

[Leipzig/Halle Airport railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leipzig/Halle_Airport_station) is located directly under the passenger terminal and has [Intercity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_\(Deutsche_Bahn\)) connections on the [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden)-[Magdeburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdeburg)-[Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover)-[Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) route. Two lines of the [S-Bahn Mitteldeutschland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-Bahn_Mitteldeutschland) connect directly to Leipzig and Halle, and onwards to [Altenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altenburg) and [Zwickau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zwickau) with connections to most parts of [Central Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_German_Metropolitan_Region).

### Car

The airport is connected to two motorways: the [A14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_14) connecting to [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden) (130 km), [Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_\(Saale\)) (20 km) and [Magdeburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdeburg) (130 km), and the [A9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesautobahn_9) connecting to [Munich](19058.md) (430 km), [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg) (280 km), and [Berlin](3354.md) (180 km).

### Coach

*[Flixbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flixbus)* connects in both directions twice per day to [Dresden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dresden), [Göttingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6ttingen), and [Kassel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassel) and once per day to [Dortmund](149349.md) and [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne).

The airport is owned by the Mitteldeutsche Flughafen Holding AG, a [state-owned enterprise](204768.md). According to public records for 2005 to 2014, its [operating losses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_losses) totalled almost 570 million € and from 2000 to 2020 they were 800 million €.[^cite_note-LZ-24] The main reason for the losses have been take-off and [landing fees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_fee), which do not cover [operating costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_costs). DHL has been profiting from these low prices; its particular subsidy DHL-Hub Leipzig GmbH does not publish its own figures, but as part of the [DHL Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DHL_Express) division, it has been making profits for many years, with 2.04 billion in 2019 alone.[^cite_note-LZ-24]

Residents in the [Saale district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saale_district) have been complaining about the [noise pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_pollution) for years and have been asking to adapt start and landing fees, pricing up night flights.[^cite_note-julke-77][^cite_note-mdr-78]
