# Palestinian political violence

This article is about political violence committed by Palestinians. For political violence in Palestine committed by Zionists, see [Zionist political violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionist_political_violence).

For the historical label to describe some of its perpetrators, see [Palestinian fedayeen](14068131.md).



Palestinian insurgents fighting against [British forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) during the [1936–1939 Arab revolt in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%931939_Arab_revolt_in_Palestine)



Palestinian *[fedayeen](14068131.md)* at a [Fatah](11104.md) rally in [Beirut, Lebanon](37428.md) in 1979



[Hamas](13913.md)' military wing, the [Al-Qassam Brigades](1703333.md), on parade in 2011

As [Palestinians](23267.md) have not had a fully [recognized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_Palestine) state with a [regular army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_army), much of **Palestinian political violence** in the context of the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](46216.md) has taken the form of [insurgency](589841.md). Common objectives of political violence by Palestinian groups include [self-determination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_self-determination) in or sovereignty over the [region of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_of_Palestine), seeking a [one-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-state_solution),[^cite_note-Waartp2232-1][^cite_note-Dewaalp292-2] or the recognition of a [Palestinian state](241405.md). This includes the objective of ending the [Israeli occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupation). Goals also include the release of [Palestinian prisoners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_prisoners_in_Israel) held by [Israel](9282173.md) and recognition of the [Palestinian right of return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_right_of_return).[^cite_note-Palestine19682-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

Palestinian groups that have been involved in politically motivated violence include the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO), [Fatah](11104.md), the [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine) (PFLP), the [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine – General Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine_%E2%80%93_General_Command) (PFLP-GC), the [Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine), the [Abu Nidal Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Nidal_Organization), the [Palestinian Islamic Jihad](13093253.md), and [Hamas](13913.md).[^cite_note-PIJCFR-8] Several of these groups are considered [terrorist organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_organizations) by the governments of the [United States](3434750.md),[^cite_note-State.gov-9] [Canada](5042916.md),[^cite_note-Currently_listed_entities-10] the [United Kingdom](31717.md),[^cite_note-11] [Japan](15573.md),[^cite_note-12] [New Zealand](4913064.md)[^cite_note-NZ-r1373-terrorlist-13] and the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

Attacks have taken place both within Israel and [Palestine](241405.md) as well as internationally. They have been directed at both military targets and civilians of many countries. Tactics have included hostage taking, plane hijacking, boat hijacking, [stone throwing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_stone-throwing), [improvised explosive device](265112.md), knife attacks, shooting sprees, [attacks with vehicles](44318569.md), car bombs and assassinations. In the 1990s, groups seeking to stop Israeli-Palestinian negotiations began adopting [suicide bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_suicide_attacks), predominantly targeting civilians, which later peaked during the [Second Intifada](71717.md). In recent decades, violence has also included [rocket attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_rocket_attacks_on_Israel) on Israeli urban centers. The [October 7 attacks](74998317.md) resulted in massacres and [hostage-taking](75025451.md).

Suicide bombings constituted 0.5% of Palestinian attacks against Israelis in the first two years of the [Second Intifada](71717.md), though this percentage accounted for half of the Israelis killed in that period.[^cite_note-Margalit-16] As of 2022, a majority of Palestinians, 59%, believe armed attacks against Israelis inside Israel are an effective measure to end the occupation, with 56% supporting them.[^cite_note-17]

### Overview and context



A Jewish bus equipped with wire screens to protect against rock, glass, and grenade throwing, late 1930s



A demolished farmhouse in Tel Mond, Israel, after a fedayun attack

In protest against the 1917 [Balfour Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration), which proposed a national home for the Jewish people in Palestine, both Muslim and Christian Palestinians began to organize in opposition to [Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism). By the end of [Ottoman rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria), the Jewish population of Palestine was 56,000[^cite_note-18] or one-sixth of the total population.[^cite_note-19] Hostility to Jewish immigration led to numerous incidents such as the [1920 Nebi Musa riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Nebi_Musa_riots), the 1921 [Jaffa riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa_riots_\(May_1921\)), the [1929 Palestine riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_Palestine_riots) and the [1936–39 Arab revolt in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%9339_Arab_revolt_in_Palestine). The Arab revolt was suppressed by British security forces and led to the deaths of approximately 5,000 Palestinians. After the passing of the [United Nations Partition Plan for Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine) in 1947 which called for the establishment of independent Arab and [Jewish States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_state), the [1947–48 Civil War in Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947%E2%80%9348_Civil_War_in_Mandatory_Palestine) broke out. Following the [Israeli Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Declaration_of_Independence) on May 15, 1948, the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War) began, involving intervention by neighboring Arab states. Casualties included 6,000 Israelis and, according to the 1958 survey by [Arif al-Arif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arif_al-Arif), 13,000 Palestinians.[^cite_note-20] Additionally some 750,000 Palestinians were expelled or fled during the [Nakba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestinian_exodus) subsequently becoming [refugees](74681.md).[^cite_note-21]

In the [Six-Day War](29328.md), a further [280,000–360,000 Palestinians became refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Palestinian_exodus), the [West Bank](33209.md) including [Jerusalem](16043.md) was [ captured and occupied from Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank) and [Gaza](12047.md) was [occupied from Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Gaza_Strip_by_Egypt). These [occupied Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories) later began to be settled by [Jewish and Israeli settlers](15123.md), while the Palestinians were placed under [Israeli military administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Civil_Administration). Historically, Palestinian militancy was fragmented into several groups. The [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) led, and eventually united, most factions, while conducting military campaigns, varying from airplane hijackings, militant operations, and civil protest. In 1987, the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada), a revolt of predominantly civil resistance, broke out. It led to the [Madrid Conference of 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Conference_of_1991), and subsequently to the [Oslo I Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_I_Accord). Oslo I produced an interim understanding allowing the new [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) to exercise [limited autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_Areas_in_the_Oslo_II_Accord) in 3%, later 17%, of the [West Bank](33209.md), and parts of the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), which were not used or designated for Israeli settlement. Unsatisfied with concessions, Islamist organizations such as Hamas and [Palestinian Islamic Jihad](13093253.md) adopted the usage of [suicide bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_suicide_attacks),**[^cite_note-Siniver_2023_et_al-22][^cite_note-Gupta_2005_et_al-23]** predominantly against Israeli civilians.[^cite_note-EC-24] Frustration over the perceived failure of the peace talks to yield a [Palestinian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_state)[^cite_note-25] led to the outbreak of the [Second Intifada](71717.md) from September 2000 until 2005, coincident with [Israel's unilateral disengagement plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel's_unilateral_disengagement_plan). The rise of [Hamas](13913.md), the use of [Palestinian rocketry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_rocket_attacks_on_Israel) and [Israel's control of Gaza's borders](19224310.md), has led to further chronic violence, culminating in a further two conflicts, the [Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_\(2008%E2%80%9309\)) of 2008–09 and [Operation Pillar of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pillar_of_Defense) in 2012.

Since 1967, some reports  estimate that some 40% of the male population of the [West Bank](33209.md) and [Gaza Strip](12047.md) have been arrested or detained in Israeli prisons for political or military reasons.[^cite_note-26]

### British Mandate for Palestine (1917–1947)

Following the [Balfour Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration) in November 1917 which encouraged Jewish migrants to settle in Palestine, violence against the Jews increased in the region. At this time Arabs were the majority, both geographically and demographically compared to the Jewish population. The majority of Arab Palestinians were distributed throughout the highlands of [Judea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea), [Samaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaria) and [Galilee](12639.md) whereas the Jewish population was scattered in small towns and rural communities. Arabs hostile to the Jewish population adopted a "[war of attrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attrition_warfare)" tactic which was advantageous to the more numerous Arab community.[^cite_note-Alon_1980-27]

Many of the deaths were inflicted during short time spans and in a few locations. On a day in April 1920, about 216 Jews were wounded or killed in [Jerusalem](16043.md). By May 1921, around 40 [Jews](25955086.md) were killed or wounded per day. In August 1929 that number had risen to 80 per day. During the 1929 riots, one percent of the Jewish population of Jerusalem were wounded or killed, in Safed 2 percent, and in [Hebron](38577.md) 12 percent.[^cite_note-Alon_1980-27] During the 1920–1929 attacks on [Jews](25955086.md) were organized by local groups and encouraged by local religious leaders. As the Jewish community did not count on the British authorities to protect them, they formed the [Haganah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haganah) which were predominantly defensive in the 1920s.[^cite_note-Alon_1980-27] During the [Arab Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Revolt) in the 1936–1939 period, violence was coordinated and organized by the [Grand Mufti of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Mufti_of_Jerusalem) and was directed against both Jews and the British. Due to the rising level of Arab violence, the [Haganah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haganah) started to pursue an offensive strategy.[^cite_note-Alon_1980-27]

### Independence of Israel to establishment of PLO (1949–1964)

See also: [List of attacks against Israeli civilians before 1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_attacks_against_Israeli_civilians_before_1967)

Throughout the period 1949–56 the Egyptian government opposed the movement of refugees from the Gaza strip into Israel, but following the IDF's Gaza Raid on February 28, 1955, the Egyptian authorities facilitated militant infiltration but still continued to oppose civilian infiltration.[^cite_note-Ref_f-28]

Around 400 [Palestinian insurgents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Fedayeen_insurgency) were killed by Israeli Security Forces each year in 1951, 1952 and 1953; a similar number and probably far more were killed in 1950. In 1949, 1,000 or more Palestinians were killed. At least 100 Palestinians were killed during 1954–1956. In total upward of 2,700 and possibly as many as 5,000 were killed by the IDF, police, and civilians along Israel's borders between 1949 and 1956. Most of the people in question were refugees attempting to return to their homes, take back possessions that had been left behind during the war and to gather crops from their former fields and orchards inside the new Israeli state.[^cite_note-benvenisti-29] Meron Benivasti states that the fact that the "infiltrators" were for the most part former inhabitants of the land returning for personal, economic and sentimental reasons was suppressed in Israel as it was feared that this may lead to an understanding of their motives and to the justification of their actions.[^cite_note-benvenisti-29]

After Israel's [Operation Black Arrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Black_Arrow) in 1955, in response to massacres in the city of [Rehovot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehovot), the [Palestinian fedayeen](14068131.md) were incorporated into an Egyptian unit.[^cite_note-Ref_g-30] [John Bagot Glubb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bagot_Glubb), a British general who commanded the [Arab Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Legion), claimed in his 1957 autobiography *A Soldier with the Arabs* that he convinced the Legion to arm and train the fedayeen for free.[^cite_note-Ref_h-31] Between 1951 and 1956, 400 Israelis were killed and 900 wounded by fedayeen attacks.[^cite_note-Ref_k-32][^cite_note-Ref_l-33]

The [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) was founded in 1964. At its first convention in [Cairo](6293.md), hundreds of Palestinians met to "call for the right of self-determination and the upholding of the rights of the Palestinian nation".[^cite_note-Miltonp132-34] To achieve these goals, a Palestinian army of liberation was thought to be essential; thus, the [Palestinian Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Liberation_Army) was established with the support of the Arab states.[^cite_note-Miltonp132-34] [Fatah](11104.md), a Palestinian group founded in the late 1950s to organize the armed resistance against Israel, and headed by [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat), soon rose to prominence within the PLO. The PLO charter called for "an end to the State of Israel, a return of Palestinians to their homeland, and the establishment of a single democratic state throughout Palestine".[^cite_note-Kapitanp30-35]

### Six-Day War and aftermath

> Our basic aim is to liberate the land from the Mediterranean Seas to the Jordan River. We are not concerned with what took place in June 1967 or in eliminating the consequences of the June War. The Palestinian revolution's basic concern is the uprooting of the Zionist entity from our land and liberating it.

— Yasser Arafat, 1970[^cite_note-Ref_n-36]

Due to Israel's defeat of Arab armies in the [Six-Day War](29328.md), the Palestinian leadership came to the conclusion that the Arab world was unable to challenge Israel militarily in open warfare. Simultaneously, the Palestinians drew lessons from movements and uprisings in Latin America, North Africa and Southeast Asia which led them to move away from guerilla warfare in rural areas and towards terrorist attacks in urban environments with an international reach. This led to a series of aircraft hijackings, bombings and kidnappings which culminated in the [killings of Israeli athletes during the 1972 Munich Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_massacre). The military superiority of Israel led Palestinian fighters to employ guerrilla tactics from bases in Jordan and Lebanon.[^cite_note-Kapitanp30-35]



[George Habash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Habash), founder of the [PFLP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine), masterminded the hijackings of four Western airliners to Jordan, which led to the [Black September](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September) conflict.

[^cite_note-h20080127-37]

In the wake of the [Six-Day War](29328.md), confrontations between Palestinian guerrillas in Jordan and government forces became a major problem within the kingdom. By early 1970, at least seven Palestinian guerrilla organizations were active in Jordan, one of the most important being the [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine) (PFLP) led by [George Habash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Habash). Based in the Jordanian refugee camps, the fedayeen developed a virtual state within a state, receiving funds and arms from both the Arab states and Eastern Europe and openly flouting the law of the country. The guerrillas initially focused on attacking Israel, but by late 1968, the main fedayeen activities in Jordan appeared to shift to attempts to overthrow the Jordanian monarchy.[^cite_note-loc-38]

### Black September

Main article: [Black September in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September_in_Jordan)

Various clashes between the fedayeen and the army occurred between the years 1968–1970. The situation climaxed in September 1970, when several attempts to assassinate [King Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Hussein) failed. On September 7, 1970, in the series of [Dawson's Field hijackings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawson's_Field_hijackings), three planes were hijacked by [PFLP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine): a [SwissAir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SwissAir) and a [TWA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans_World_Airlines) that were landed in [Azraq](4600231.md) area and a [Pan Am](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_American_World_Airways) that was landed in [Cairo](6293.md). Then on September 9, a [BOAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BOAC) flight from Bahrain was also hijacked to [Zarqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarqa). The PFLP announced that the hijackings were intended "to pay special attention to the Palestinian problem". After all hostages were removed, the planes were dramatically blown up in front of TV cameras.

A bitterly fought 10-day civil war known as [Black September](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September_in_Jordan) ensued, drawing involvement by [Syria](7515849.md) and [Iraq](7515928.md), and sparking troop movements by Israel and the [United States Navy](20518076.md). The number of people killed on all sides were estimated as high as 3,500,[^cite_note-loc-38] other sources claiming it to be as high as 20,000.

Battles between Palestinian guerrilla forces and the Jordanian army continued during the closing months of 1970 and the first six months of 1971. In November 1971, members of the Palestinian [Black September group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September_\(group\)), who took their name from the civil war, assassinated Jordanian Prime Minister [Wasfi al-Tal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasfi_al-Tal) in [Cairo](6293.md). In December the group made an unsuccessful attempt to assassinate the Jordanian ambassador in Britain.[^cite_note-loc-38]

### Relocation to Lebanon and Lebanese Civil War

In the aftermath of Black September in Jordan, many Palestinians arrived in [Lebanon](17771.md), among them Yasser Arafat and the Palestinian Liberation Organization (PLO). In the early 1970s their presence exacerbated an already tense situation in Lebanon, and in 1975 the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) broke out. Beginning with street fighting in [Beirut](37428.md) between Christian [Phalangists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalangists) and Palestinian militiamen, the war quickly deteriorated into a conflict between two loosely defined factions: the side wishing to preserve the status quo, consisting primarily of [Maronite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronite) militias, and the side seeking change, which included a variety of militias from leftist organizations and guerrillas from rejectionist Palestinian (nonmainstream PLO) organizations. The Lebanese civil war lasted until 1990 and resulted in an estimated 130,000 to 250,000 civilian fatalities and one million wounded.



Charred remains of the bus hijacked and burnt by Palestinian militants in 1978 in the [Coastal Road massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Road_massacre)

After Black September, the PLO and its offshoots waged an international campaign against Israelis. Notable events were the [Munich Olympics massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Olympics_massacre) (1972), the hijacking of several civilian airliners (some were thwarted, see for example: [Entebbe Operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entebbe_Operation)), the [Savoy Hotel attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy_Operation), the [Zion Square explosive refrigerator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Yehuda_Street_Bombing) and the [Coastal Road massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Road_massacre). During the 1970s and the early 1980s, Israel suffered attacks from PLO bases in Lebanon, such as the [Avivim school bus massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avivim_school_bus_massacre) in 1970, the [Maalot massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maalot_massacre) in 1974 (where Palestinian militants massacred 21 school children) and the [Nahariya attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Nahariya_attack) led by [Samir Kuntar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samir_Kuntar) in 1979, as well as a terrorist bombing by Ziad Abu Ein that killed two Israeli 16-year-olds and left 36 other youths wounded during the [Lag BaOmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lag_BaOmer) celebration in Tiberias.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-WhoIsAffraidOfAbuEin-40] Following the [1982 Israeli invasion of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Invasion_of_Lebanon), called "[Operation Peace for Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Peace_for_Galilee)" by the IDF, and the exile of the PLO to [Tunis](57659.md), Israel had a relatively quiet decade.

### First Intifada (1987–1993)



Palestinians in [Qalandiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalandiya) throw rocks from behind an ambulance during a riot as part of the [Nakba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakba) protests.

The [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada) was characterized more by grassroots and non-violent political actions from among the population in the [Israeli occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupation) [Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories).[^cite_note-Crottyp87-41] A total of 160 Israelis and 2,162 Palestinians were killed, including 1,000 Palestinians killed by other Palestinians under the accusation of being collaborators.[^cite_note-42] The Intifada lasted five years and ended with the signing of the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords).[^cite_note-Maozp264-43] The strategy of non-violence, though widespread among Palestinians, was not always adhered to, and there were youth who threw [molotov cocktails](20922.md) and stones, with such violence generally directed against Israeli soldiers and settlers.[^cite_note-Boerp190-44]

There were two attacks that represented new developments in terms of political violence inside Israel in this period. The first [Palestinian suicide attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Palestinian_suicide_attacks) took place on July 6, 1989, when a member of the [Palestinian Islamic Jihad](13093253.md) boarded the [Tel Aviv Jerusalem bus 405](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Aviv_Jerusalem_bus_405_attack). He walked up to the driver and pulled the wheel to the right, driving the vehicle into a ravine, killing 16 people.[^cite_note-Ref_p-45] The end of the intifada also saw the first use of [suicide bombing](18835454.md) as a tactic by Palestinian militants. On April 16, 1993, [Hamas](13913.md) carried out the [Mehola Junction bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehola_Junction_bombing), in which operative Saher Tamam al-Nabulsi detonated his explosives-laden car between two buses. One person, a Palestinian, other than the attacker was killed, and 21 were wounded.[^cite_note-Ref_q-46]

During this period, the [Abu Nidal Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Nidal_Organization) became subsumed by infighting and [mass executed hundreds of its members and their families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Nidal_Organization_internal_executions) during 1987–1988. The number of executed is estimated at 600 people, mostly Palestinians, across several separate locations in Syria, Lebanon and Libya.[^cite_note-47]

### Oslo Accords to Camp David Summit (1993–2000)

Further information: [Palestinian suicide bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_suicide_bombings)



Bus after 1996 terror bombing in [Jerusalem](16043.md)

The years between the intifadas were marked by intense diplomatic activity between Israel and Palestinians, who were represented by the PLO. This led to the signing of the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords) and the creation of the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority). In response, Islamist organizations such as [Hamas](13913.md) and the [Palestinian Islamic Jihad](13093253.md) (PIJ) adopted the tactic of suicide bombings, influenced by Lebanese groups, to derail the peace process, weaken the PLO and polarize Israeli politics.**[^cite_note-Siniver_2023_et_al-22][^cite_note-Gupta_2005_et_al-23]**

In this period, suicide bombings of Israeli buses and crowded spaces became a regular tactic, particularly by Hamas and Islamic Jihad.  Attacks during this period include the [Beit Lid massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Lid_massacre), a double-suicide bombing at a crowded junction that killed 21 people and the [Dizengoff Center massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizengoff_Center_massacre), a suicide bombing outside a Tel Aviv shopping mall that killed 13 people.

### Second Intifada (2000–2005)

Main article: [Second Intifada](71717.md)

Further information: [Palestinian suicide bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_suicide_bombings)



Bus after 2003 terror bombing in [Haifa](55606.md)

The Second Intifada (2000–2005) witnessed a significant increase in Palestinian political violence, including many [suicide bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_suicide_attacks), which predominantly targeted Israeli civilians.[^cite_note-EC-24] According to [B'Tselem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B'Tselem), as of July 10, 2005, over 400 members of the Israeli Security forces, and 821 Israeli civilians have been killed by Palestinians since the signing of the Oslo Accords in 1993, 553 of whom were killed within the [1949 Armistice lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_lines), mainly by suicide bombings. Targets of attacks included buses, [Israeli checkpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_checkpoint), restaurants, discothèques, shopping malls, a university, and civilian homes.[^cite_note-casualties-48][^cite_note-Ref_r-49][^cite_note-Harrison-50]

#### Ramallah lynching

Main article: [2000 Ramallah lynching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Ramallah_lynching)

In October 2000, a [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) [mob](928595.md) lynched two non-combatant [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) [reservists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservist), Vadim Nurzhitz (sometimes spelled as Norzhich) and Yossi Avrahami (or Yosef Avrahami),[^cite_note-Ref_u-51] who had accidentally entered the [Palestinian Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority)-controlled city of [Ramallah](44132.md) in the [West Bank](33209.md). The brutality of [the event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Ramallah_lynching), captured in a photo of a Palestinian rioter proudly waving his blood-stained hands to the crowd below, sparked international outrage and further intensified the ongoing conflict between Israeli and Palestinian forces.[^cite_note-revenge-52][^cite_note-brutal-53][^cite_note-voice-54]

#### Suicide bombings and attacks on civilians

A spate of suicide bombings and attacks, aimed mostly at civilians (such as the [Dolphinarium discotheque suicide bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolphinarium_discotheque_suicide_bombing)), was launched against Israel and elicited a military response. A suicide bombing dubbed the [Passover Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netanya_suicide_attack) (30 Israeli civilians were killed at Park hotel, [Netanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netanya)) climaxed a bloody month of March 2002, in which more than 130 Israelis, mostly civilians, were killed in attacks. Israel launched [Operation Defensive Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Defensive_Shield). The operation led to the apprehension of many members of militant groups, as well as their weaponry and equipment. 497 Palestinians and 30 Israelis were killed during Operation Defensive Shield.[^cite_note-55]

Main article: [2004 Sinai bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Sinai_bombings)

In 2004, 31 people were killed and 159 others were wounded in a simultaneous attack against multiple tourist destinations in Egypt.[^cite_note-Ref_v-56] Of the dead, 15 were [Egyptians](31912046.md), 12 were from Israel, two from Italy, one from Russia, and one was an Israeli-American. According to the Egyptian government, the bombers were [Palestinians](23267.md) led by Iyad Saleh, who had tried to enter Israel to carry out attacks there but were unsuccessful.[^cite_note-sinai-57]

### 2005–2013

See also: [List of Palestinian suicide attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Palestinian_suicide_attacks) and [Palestinian domestic weapons production](6486832.md)



A kindergarten classroom in the Israeli city of [Beer Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_Sheva) after being hit by a [Grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BM-21_Grad) rocket fired from the [Gaza Strip](12047.md)

In the mid-2000s, [Hamas](13913.md) started putting greater emphasis on its political characteristics and strengthened its popularity amongst Palestinians. In [2006 Palestinian legislative elections](38945340.md) Hamas won a majority in the [Palestinian Legislative Council](40067464.md), prompting the United States and many European countries to cut off all funds to Hamas and the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md),[^cite_note-Ref_w-58] insisting that Hamas must recognize Israel, renounce violence and accept previous peace pacts.[^cite_note-wsvn-59]

After the [Israel's unilateral disengagement plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel's_unilateral_disengagement_plan) in 2005 and the [2006 Palestinian legislative elections](38945340.md), Hamas [took control over all the Gaza Strip in June 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatah%E2%80%93Hamas_conflict) in a bloody coup. Palestinian militant groups in the Gaza strip increased the firing of [Qassam rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qassam_rocket), [mortars](324499.md) and [Grad missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BM-21_Grad) on southern Israel. Attacks continued outside the Gaza Strip perimeter, including the attack that resulted in the Israeli soldier [Gilad Shalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilad_Shalit) being captured and held in the Gaza Strip for over five years.

Hamas has made use of guerrilla tactics in the Gaza Strip and to a lesser degree the West Bank.[^cite_note-ynetnews_weight-60] Hamas has adapted these techniques over the years since its inception. According to a 2006 report by rival Fatah party, Hamas had smuggled "between several hundred and 1,300 tons" of advanced rockets, along with other weaponry, into Gaza. Some Israelis and some Gazans both noted similarities in Hamas's military buildup to that of [Hezbollah](13919.md) in the 2006 Israel-Hezbollah war.[^cite_note-ynetnews_weight-60]

Hamas has used [IEDs](265112.md) and [anti-tank rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_rocket) against the [IDF](39237.md) in Gaza. The latter include standard [RPG-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RPG-7) warheads and home-made rockets such as the [Al-Bana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bana), Al-[Batar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batar) and Al-[Yasin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasin_\(RPG\)). The IDF has a difficult, if not impossible time trying to find hidden weapons caches in Palestinian areas – this is due to the high local support base Hamas enjoys.[^cite_note-Issam2006-61]

During the [Gaza War (2008–09)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_\(2008%E2%80%9309\)), Palestinian militant groups fired rockets aimed at civilian targets which struck the cities of Ashdod, Beersheba and Gedera. The military wing of Hamas said that after a week from the start, it had managed to fire 302 rockets, at an average of 44 rockets daily. 102 rockets and 35 mortars were fired by Fatah at Israel. Over 750 rockets and mortars were fired from Gaza into Israel during the conflict wounded 182 civilians, killing 3 people, and causing minor suffering to another 584 people suffering from shock and anxiety. Several rockets landed in schools and one fell close to a kindergarten, all located in residential areas. The UN fact finding mission stated that this constituted a deliberate attack against the civilian population and was unjustifiable in international law.[^cite_note-BBC_Monitoring_International_Reports_January_3,_2009-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-jpost_total_rockets-64]

In 2012, terror attacks against Israelis in the West Bank increased compared to 2011. The number of terror attacks in the West Bank increased from 320 in 2011 to 578 in 2012.[^cite_note-Terror_attacks_increased_in_2013-65] The attacks mainly involved rock throwing, [Molotov cocktails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov_cocktails), firearms and explosives.[^cite_note-Terror_attacks_increased_in_2013-65]

In 2013, Hamas stated that the "kidnapping of IDF soldiers to trade for Palestinian prisoners is at the heart of Palestinian culture".[^cite_note-66]

### Israel-Gaza war

Main articles: [Gaza war](74998519.md) and [October 7 attacks](74998317.md)

See also: [Sexual and gender-based violence in the October 7 attacks](75436869.md), [War crimes in the Gaza war](75113830.md), and [Gaza war hostage crisis](75025451.md)



A blood-stained home floor in the aftermath of the [Nahal Oz massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahal_Oz_massacre)

On October 7, 2023, Hamas and [other Palestinian factions](75192468.md) launched [an attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Hamas-led_attack_on_Israel), breaching the [Gaza–Israel barrier](478337.md). For months prior to the attack, Hamas had been leading Israeli intelligence to believe that they were not seeking conflict.[^cite_note-67] Hamas fighters proceeded to massacre hundreds of civilians at [a music festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Re'im_music_festival_massacre) and in [kibbutz Be'eri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be'eri_massacre) and take [hostages in Southern Israel back to the Gaza Strip](75025451.md). In total, [1,139 civilians, IDF soldiers and foreign nationals were killed in Israel](75130785.md), making this the deadliest attack by Hamas militants since the foundation of Israel in 1948.[^cite_note-reuters1-68] The Hamas-led attack marked the beginning of the ongoing [Gaza war](74998519.md).

## Government involvement

In 2011, Israeli PM Benyamin Netanyahu stated that the incitement promulgated by the Palestinian Authority was destroying Israel's confidence, and he condemned what he regarded as the glorification of the murderers of the [Fogel family in Itamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itamar_massacre) on PA television. The perpetrator of the murders had been described as a "hero" and a "legend" by members of his family, during a weekly program.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

[Isi Leibler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isi_Leibler) wrote in the Jerusalem Post that Mahmoud Abbas and his chief negotiator Saeb Erekat deny Israel's right to exist and promote vicious hatred against Jews, in statements made in Arabic. He claimed that the state-controlled Palestinian media praised the murders committed by Palestinians. Abbas al-Sayed who perpetrated the Passover suicide attack at the Park Hotel in Netanya which killed 30 civilians was described by Abbas as a "hero" and "symbol of the Palestinian Authority".[^cite_note-71]

Following the [Itamar massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itamar_massacre) and a bombing in Jerusalem, 27 US senators sent a letter requesting the US Secretary of State to identify the administration's steps to end Palestinian incitement to violence against Jews and Israel that they said was occurring within the "Palestinian media, mosques and schools, and even by individuals or institutions affiliated with the Palestinian Authority".[^cite_note-72]

The United Nations body [UNESCO](21786641.md) stopped funding a children's magazine sponsored by the Palestinian Authority that commended Hitler's killing of Jews. It deplored this publication as contrary to its principles of building tolerance and respect for human rights and human dignity.[^cite_note-73]

Palestinian Media Watch reported that the Palestinian Authority spent more than \$5 million a month paying salaries to Palestinians and Israeli Arabs imprisoned in Israel for terror crimes. They also stated that groups in a summer camp for children sponsored by PA Prime Minister [Salam Fayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salam_Fayyad) were named after militants: Dalal Mughrabi, who led the [Coastal Road Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Road_Massacre); Salah Khalaf, head of Black September that carried out the [Munich massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_massacre); and Abu Ali Mustafa, the general secretary of the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine who perpetrated many attacks. [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein), the leader of [Iraq](7515928.md), donated \$25,000 to the families of suicide bombers, and \$10,000 to the families of Palestinian civilians killed by the Israeli military.[^cite_note-Ref_x-74][^cite_note-75]

After Israel agreed to hand over the bodies of dead Palestinian suicide bombers and other militants as part of what the Israeli Government described as 'a humanitarian gesture' to PA chairman Mahmoud Abbas to help the peace process, the Palestinian Authority planned a national rally to honour them and to provide full military funerals. The bodies included the suicide bombers that perpetrated the [bus bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shmuel_HaNavi_bus_bombing) in Jerusalem's Shmuel Hanavi neighborhood which killed twenty-three people, many of them children, and the attacker in the [Cafe Hillel bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cafe_Hillel_bombing). Israel will also return the remains of the bombers that committed the bombings on [two buses in Beersheba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beersheba_bus_bombings) in 2004 killing 16 people, the [Stage night club bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_Club_bombing), the attack on the open-air [Hadera market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadera_Market_bombing) as well as the attackers of the [Savoy Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy_Hotel_Attack) in Tel Aviv who killed eight hostages. The Palestinian Authority and [Hamas](13913.md) both planned official ceremonies and PA president Abbas attended a ceremony at his Muqataa compound. Prisoners Affairs Minister Qaraqi called on Palestinians for a day of celebration. The rally in honor of the dead will be attended by PA President Mahmoud Abbas, PLO leaders, and families of the dead militants. The dead are considered martyrs by Palestinians, but viewed as terrorists by Israelis.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

Palestinian President [Mahmoud Abbas](232595.md) has been accused of incitement to violence, on the basis of a statement he made concerning youths injured in defending the [Haram al Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haram_al_Sharif)/[Temple Mount](65719.md) from what Palestinians have seen as attempts to alter the status quo. He declared in September 2015: "Every drop of blood spilled in Jerusalem is pure, every [shahid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahid) will reach paradise, and every injured person will be rewarded by God."[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

## Involvement of women and children



Bloody child's shoe after Palestinian attack on an Israeli shopping mall

In the 1930s, the emergence of organized youth cadres was rooted in the desire to form a youth paramilitary. It was believed that armed youth might bring an end to [British hegemony in the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire). Youth were cajoled into violence by Palestinian political figures and newspapers that glorified violence and death. The [Palestinian Arab Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Arab_Party) sponsored the development of storm troops consisting solely of children and youth. A British report from the period stated that "the growing youth and scout movements must be regarded as the most probable factors for the disturbance of the peace".[^cite_note-Ref_y-81]

As a youngster, [Yasir Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasir_Arafat) led neighborhood children in marching and drills, beating those who did not obey. In the 1940s, Arafat's father organized a group of militants in Gaza which included Yasir Arafat and his brothers. The leader, Abu Khalid, a mathematics teacher in Gaza, gave Arafat the name Yasir in honor of the militant Yasir al-Bireh.[^cite_note-Ref_z-82]

### Child suicide bombers

Main article: [Child suicide bombers in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_suicide_bombers_in_the_Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict)

As part of the [Arab–Israeli conflict](7960202.md), especially during the [Second Intifada](71717.md) from 2000 to 2005, [Palestinian](23267.md) militant groups used children for [suicide bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_bombings). Minors were recruited to attack [Israeli](9282173.md) targets, both military and civilian. This deliberate involvement of children in armed conflict was condemned by international human rights organizations.[^cite_note-HRW_Stop_Use_of_Children-83][^cite_note-84]

According to [Amnesty International](18947898.md): "Palestinian armed groups have repeatedly shown total disregard for the most fundamental human rights, notably the right to life, by deliberately targeting Israeli civilians and by using Palestinian children in armed attacks. Children are susceptible to recruitment by manipulation or may be driven to join armed groups for a variety of reasons, including a desire to avenge relatives or friends killed by the Israeli army."[^cite_note-85]

### Human shields

Main articles: [Palestinian rocket attacks on Israel § United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_rocket_attacks_on_Israel#United_Nations), and [Human shield § Israeli–Palestinian conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_shield#Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict)

According to the [United Nations Secretary-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretary-General) [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon) in 2015,[^cite_note-86] Hamas launched rockets from inside schools to use the retaliatory child deaths for propaganda and deter Israel from attacking Gaza.[^cite_note-87] This tactic is called the [human shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_shield).

### Involvement of women

Women in particular have increasingly associated political violence with expanded citizenship rights due to the perceived failure of nonmilitaristic tactics to achieve political goals, primary amongst these, the achievement of Palestinian autonomy.[^cite_note-Cunnighamp76-88]

The profile of the female Palestinian suicide bombers has been the subject of study by Katherine VanderKaay, who presented her profiling of the subjects at the [American Psychological Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Psychological_Association)'s annual meeting. While the first suicide bombing undertaken by a Palestinian took place in 1994, the first [female suicide bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_suicide_bomber) from among Palestinian society did not emerge until January 2002. The bomber was [Wafa Idris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wafa_Idris), a 28-year-old paramedic and a supporter of secularist parties.[^cite_note-Ref_ae-89][^cite_note-Ref_af-90]

## Violence against civilians



Qassam rockets fired at [Sderot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sderot)



Timeline of Israelis and Palestinians killed in the occupied Palestinian territory and Israel from 2008 to October 6, 2023

According to [B'Tselem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B'Tselem), the Israeli Information Center for Human Rights in the Occupied Territories, 500 Israeli civilians were killed by Palestinians from September 29, 2000, to March 31, 2012, in Israel, and another 254 Israeli civilians were killed in the Gaza Strip and the West Bank.[^cite_note-Ref_ag-91]

B'tselem reported that the main argument used to justify violence against civilians is that "all means are legitimate in fighting for independence against a foreign occupation". B'Tselem criticized this argument, saying it is completely baseless, and contradicts the fundamental principle of [international humanitarian law](1093604.md).&#x20;

> "According to this principle, civilians are to be protected from the consequences of warfare, and any attack must discriminate between civilians and military targets. This principle is part of international customary law; as such, it applies to every state, organization, and person, even those who are not party to any relevant convention."[^cite_note-btselem1-92]

&#x20;B'Tselem further noted that Palestinian spokespersons distinguish between attacks inside Israel proper and attacks directed at settlers in the [Occupied Territories](2781576.md), stating that since the [settlements](15123.md) are illegal and many settlers belong to Israel's security forces, settlers are not entitled to the international law protections granted to civilians. Human rights group B'tselem rejected this argument, and stated:&#x20;

> "The illegality of the settlements has no effect at all on the status of their civilian residents. The settlers constitute a distinctly civilian population, which is entitled to all the protections granted civilians by international law. The Israeli security forces' use of land in the settlements or the membership of some settlers in the Israeli security forces does not affect the status of the other residents living among them, and certainly does not make them proper targets of attack. B'Tselem strongly opposes the attempts to justify attacks against Israeli civilians by using distorted interpretations of international law. Furthermore, B'Tselem demands that the Palestinian Authority do everything within its power to prevent future attacks and to prosecute the individuals involved in past attacks."[^cite_note-btselem1-92]

### Rocket attacks on Israel

See also: [Palestinian rocket attacks on Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_rocket_attacks_on_Israel)



Rocket attacks fired at Israel from the Gaza Strip, 2001–2021

[^cite_note-pinfold-93]

Palestinian rocket and mortar attacks on Israel from the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) have occurred since 2001. Between 2001 and January 2009, over 8,600 rockets had been launched, leading to 28 deaths and several hundred injuries,[^cite_note-BBC_Q\&A-94][^cite_note-95] as well as widespread psychological trauma and disruption of daily life.[^cite_note-BBC_catandmouse-96]

The weapons, often generically referred to as [Qassams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qassam_rocket), were initially crude and short-range, mainly affecting the Israeli city of [Sderot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sderot) and other communities bordering the Gaza Strip. In 2006, more sophisticated rockets began to be deployed, reaching the larger coastal city of [Ashkelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkelon), and by early 2009 major cities [Ashdod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashdod) and [Beersheba](312755.md) had been hit by [Katyusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katyusha_rocket_launcher) and [Grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BM-21_Grad) rockets.

Attacks have been carried out by all Palestinian armed groups,[^cite_note-ai-97] and, prior to the 2008–2009 [Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_\(2008%E2%80%9309\)), were consistently supported by most Palestinians,[^cite_note-support-98][^cite_note-pcpsr2004-99][^cite_note-pcpsr2006-100][^cite_note-pcpsr2008-101] although the stated goals have been mixed. The attacks, widely condemned for targeting civilians, have been described as terrorism by United Nations, [European Union](9317.md) and Israeli officials, and are defined as [war crimes](48626.md) by human rights groups [Amnesty International](18947898.md) and [Human Rights Watch](46530.md).



Osher Twito, an Israeli boy crippled by Palestinian rocket fire

Defenses constructed specifically to deal with the weapons include fortifications for schools and bus stops as well as an alarm system named [Red Color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Color). [Iron Dome](18342427.md), a system to intercept short-range rockets, was developed by Israel and first deployed in the spring of 2011 to protect Beersheba and Ashkelon, but officials and experts warned that it would not be completely effective. Shortly thereafter, it intercepted a Palestinian Grad rocket for the first time.[^cite_note-102]

The attacks were a stated cause of the [Gaza blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_blockade), the Gaza War (December 27, 2008 – January 21, 2009) and other [Israeli military](39237.md) operations in the Gaza Strip, including [Operation Rainbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Rainbow) (May 2004), [Operation Days of Penitence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Days_of_Penitence) (2004), the [2006 Israel-Gaza conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Israel-Gaza_conflict), [Operation Autumn Clouds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Autumn_Clouds) (2006), and [Operation Hot Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Hot_Winter) (2008). Attacks began in 2001. Since then, nearly 4,800 rockets have hit southern Israel, just over 4,000 of them since [Israel withdrew from the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel's_unilateral_disengagement_plan) in August 2005. The range of the rockets has increased over time. The original Qassam rocket has a range of about 10 km (6.2 mi) but more advanced rockets, including versions of the old Soviet Grad or Katyusha have hit Israeli targets 40 km (25 mi) from Gaza.[^cite_note-BBC_Q\&A-94]

Some analysts see the attacks as a shift away from reliance on [suicide bombing](18835454.md), which was previously Hamas's main method of attacking Israel, and an adoption of the rocket tactics used by Lebanese militant group [Hezbollah](13919.md).[^cite_note-103]

### Stone-throwing

Main article: [Palestinian stone-throwing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_stone-throwing)

Palestinian stone-throwing is a violent political statement which encompasses the practice of throwing stones by hand and using powerful slings variously aimed at Israel security personnel, Israeli civilians, and at both civilian and military vehicles.

## Palestinian attitudes

### 1980s

The PLO officially "declared its rejection and condemnation of terrorism in all its forms" in 1988.[^cite_note-108]

### 1995–2000

A study conducted by Mkhaimer Abusada of [Al-Azhar University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Azhar_University) explored attitudes towards the use of political violence. Four questions were posed on the subject of political violence to over a thousand respondents randomly selected from localities in the West Bank and Gaza Strip. The first question was: "Do you support the continuing resort of some Palestinian factions to armed operations against Israeli targets in Gaza and Jericho?" Overall, 56% of respondents responded negatively. Those affiliated with leftist groups showed the highest levels of support for armed attacks against Israelis (74%), while those affiliated with parties supporting the peace process showed the lowest levels (24%). The Islamic opposition was split, with slightly over half in favor, and slightly less than half opposed.[^cite_note-Abusada-109]

In September 1995, survey participants were asked whether they supported, opposed or had no opinion with regard to "armed attacks against Israeli army targets", "armed attacks against Israeli settlers", and "armed attacks against Israeli civilian targets". The majority supported the use of armed attacks against Israeli military targets and settlers in the West Bank and Gaza Strip. Support crossed all party lines and groups, and was highest among the Islamic opposition (91% and 84%) and the leftists (90% and 89%), though a significant majority of those who supported the peace process also supported armed attacks on military targets and settlers (69% and 73%). To explain the apparent paradox in the latter position, Abusada quotes Shikaki (1996) who "contends that Palestinian support for the use of armed attacks against Israeli military targets and settlers does not indicate 'opposition to the peace process but Palestinian insistence that the process entails an end to occupation and settlements.'"[^cite_note-Abusada-109] Palestinian support for armed attacks against Israeli civilian targets in Israel was 20% overall, with support being highest among those affiliated with the Islamic opposition (42%) and the leftists (32%), and lowest among supporters of the peace process (12%) and the National Independents (10%).[^cite_note-Abusada-109]

### 2000–04

A July 2001 poll conducted by the Palestinian Center for Policy & Survey Research (PSR) found that 58 percent of Palestinians supported armed attacks against Israeli civilians inside Israel and 92 percent supported armed confrontations against the Israeli army in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-Ref_aq-110] A May 2002 poll by the center found that support for bombings of civilians inside Israel dropped to 52%, but support for armed attacks against Israeli settlers remained "very high" at 89 percent. Support for armed attacks against soldiers stood at 92 percent.[^cite_note-Ref_ar-111] A poll after the 2003 [Maxim restaurant suicide bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_restaurant_suicide_bombing), in which 20 Israelis were killed, concluded that 75 percent of Palestinians supported the attack, with support higher "in the Gaza Strip (82%) compared to the West Bank (70%), in refugee camps (84%) compared to towns and villages (69%), among women (79%) compared to men (71%), among the young (78%) compared to the old (66%), among students (81%) compared to professionals (33%), and among supporters of Hamas (92%) compared to supporters of Fateh (69%)".[^cite_note-Ref_as-112]

The [firing of rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_rocket_attacks_on_Israel) from [Beit Hanoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Hanoun) into Israel was acceptable to about three-quarters of the Palestinian public in the occupied territories, and was higher in the West Bank (78%) compared to the Gaza Strip (71%), among students (83%) compared to merchants (63%), and among supporters of Hamas (86%) compared to supporters of Fatah (73%). While firing rockets from Beit Hanoun was supported by a majority of Palestinians (75%), 59% of the residents of Beit Hanoun rejected this practice. 83% of Palestinians favored a mutual cessation of violence.[^cite_note-Ref_at-113]

A report by the [Jerusalem Media and Communication Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Media_and_Communication_Center), a Palestinian organization, showing trends based on polls conducted since 1997, indicated that Palestinian support for military operations against Israeli targets stood at 34–40 percent in 1997–1999, climbed to 65–85 percent in 2000–2004, and dropped back to 41 percent at the end of 2004. "Military operations" were defined as including shootings, [car bombs](155282.md) and [mortar rocket attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_rocket_attacks_on_Israel), but not [suicide bombings](18835454.md).[^cite_note-Ref_au-114] A 2005 poll by the center indicated that 53 percent of Palestinians supported "the continuation of \[the] [Al-Aqsa Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Intifada), 50 percent supported "suicide bombings against Israeli civilians", and 36 percent supported "the resumption of military operations against Israeli targets".[^cite_note-Ref_av-115]

A 2004 study by Victoroff et al. was conducted on a group of 52 boys, all 14 years old, from the [al-Shati camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shati_\(camp\)) in Gaza. Forty-three percent of the boys reported that a family member had been wounded or killed by the IDF, and half lived in households where the father's employment was lost following the outbreak of the Second Intifada. "Sympathy for terrorism" was found to be correlated with depression and anxiety scores, as well as with the level of "perceived oppression", and "emotional distress". Of those who felt subject to unjust treatment, 77 percent expressed sympathy for political violence.[^cite_note-Victoroffp230-116]

### 2005–2012



Jerusalem, July 2, 2008. A Palestinian man drives a front-end loader into several vehicles in Jerusalem, killing three before being shot dead.

A March 2008 report by Palestinian Center for Policy & Survey Research (PSR) noted that the level of support for armed attack against Israeli civilians inside Israel increased significantly with 67% supporting and 31% opposed, compared to support by 40% in 2005 and 55% in 2006. A February 2008 suicide bombing that killed one Israeli woman in [Dimona](9103.md) was supported by 77% and opposed by 19%. An overwhelming majority of 84 percent supported the March 2008 [Mercaz HaRav massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercaz_HaRav_massacre), in which a Palestinian gunman killed eight students and wounded eleven in a Jerusalem school. Support for the attack was 91 percent in the Gaza Strip compared to 79 percent in the West Bank. Similar suicide attacks in 2005 had been less widely supported, with 29% support for a suicide attack that took place in Tel Aviv, and 37% support for another one in Beersheba.[^cite_note-PSR27-117]

The [2009 Hamas political violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Hamas_political_violence?action=edit\&redlink=1) took place in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) during and after the 2009 [Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_\(2008%E2%80%9309\)). A series of violent acts, ranging from physical assaults, [torture](47702.md), and executions of [Palestinians](23267.md) suspected of [collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaboration) with the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md), as well as members of the [Fatah](11104.md) political party, occurred. According to [Human Rights Watch](46530.md), at least 32 people were killed by these attacks: 18 during the conflict and 14 afterward, and several dozen more were maimed, many by shots to the legs.[^cite_note-JP_HRW-118][^cite_note-HRW_report-119]

In 2012, the number of militant attacks in the [West Bank](33209.md) rose from 320 in 2011 to 578 in 2012, including 282 in [Jerusalem](16043.md) alone compared to 191 in 2011. According to an annual [Shin Bet](311752.md) report, the increase was due in part to a 68% rise of attacks using [molotov cocktails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov_cocktails). The number of attacks involving [firearms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firearms) and [explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosives) grew by 42%, from 26 to 37.[^cite_note-120]

  - Armed wing is the [Izz ad-Din al-Qassam Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izz_ad-Din_al-Qassam_Brigades); the sole target of its suicide bombings and missile attacks is Israel.

### PLO

- [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine) (PFLP) (founded 1967)&#x20;

  - Left-wing

  - Joined the PLO in 1968 and became the second-largest PLO faction, after Arafat's al-Fatah, but withdrew in 1974, accusing the group of moving away from the goal of abolishing the State of Israel. It was led by [Abu Ali Mustapha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Ali_Mustapha) until his assassination in 2001.

    [^cite_note-126]

    [^cite_note-127]

    [^cite_note-128]

  - Armed wing is the [Abu Ali Mustapha Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Ali_Mustapha_Brigades) and [Jihad Jibril Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihad_Jibril_Brigades)

    [^cite_note-129]

  - Currently led by [Ahmad Sa'adat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Sa'adat)

- [Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine) (DFLP) (founded 1969)&#x20;

  - [Marxist-Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist-Leninist) group that believes Palestinian national goals can be achieved only through revolution of the masses. Split into two factions in 1991; [Nayif Hawatmah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayif_Hawatmah) leads the majority and more hard-line faction, which continue to dominate the group. Joined with other rejectionist groups to form the [Alliance of Palestinian Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_of_Palestinian_Forces) (APF) to oppose the [Declaration of Principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Principles) signed in 1993. Broke from the APF – along with the PFLP – over ideological differences. Has made limited moves towards merging with the PFLP since the mid-1990s.

- Abu Nidal organization (ANO), also known as [Fatah - the Revolutionary Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatah_-_the_Revolutionary_Council) (FRC), (founded 1974)&#x20;

  - Split from PLO; part of the so-called rejectionist front, the ANO is a secular, nationalist group. Was led by [Abu Nidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Nidal), widely regarded as the most ruthless of the Palestinian leaders, until his death in August 2002. According to Kameel Nasr, *Arab and Israeli Terrorism*, The group was infiltrated and influenced by Israeli security.

- [Palestinian Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Liberation_Front) (PLF)&#x20;

  - Minor Left wing faction

  - Founded in 196 by [Ahmed Jibril](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Jibril) and [Shafiq al-Hout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafiq_al-Hout), re established in 1977 by [Abu Abbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Abbas)

- [Arab Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Liberation_Front) (ALF)&#x20;

  - Minor faction tied to the [Iraqi Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'ath_Party#The_Iraq-based_Ba'ath_Party)

  - Founded in 1969, first leader was [Zeid Heidar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeid_Heidar?action=edit\&redlink=1)

  - Currently led by [Rakad Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakad_Salem)

- [As-Sa'iqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As-Sa'iqa) (VPLW)&#x20;

  - Palestinian branch of the [Syrian Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'ath_Party#Syria)

  - Founded in 1966 as alternative to Fatah, organisation boycotts [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) and is opposed to Oslo Accords

  - Organisation was not active during the Second Intifada

  - Currently led by Farhan Abu Al-Hayja.

- [Palestinian Popular Struggle Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Popular_Struggle_Front) (PPSF)&#x20;

  - Minor Socialist faction formerly led by [Samir Ghawshah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samir_Ghawshah)

- [Palestinian Arab Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Arab_Front) (PAF)&#x20;

  \* Minor [Arab Nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Nationalist) faction

  - Originally part of the ALF, split from the ALF in 1993

  - Supports the Palestinian right of return and creation of Palestinian state within 1967 borders

  - Currently led by Jameel Shihadeh.

- [Fatah](11104.md) (founded early 1960s)

  [^cite_note-Ref_az-130]

  - [Palestinian nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_nationalist) political party

  - Reverse acronym for "Harekat at-Tahrir al-Wataniyyeh al-Falastiniyyeh" ("Palestinian National Liberation Movement" in Arabic)

  - Also known as the Movement for the National Liberation of Palestine

  - Founded by [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat) in 1959. Took control of the PLO in 1968, with Arafat as chairman.

  - Currently led by [Mahmoud Abbas](232595.md)

#### Fatah associates

- [Tanzim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzim) (founded 1995)&#x20;

  - Means "organization" in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic)

  - Loosely organized Fatah militia

  - Led by [Marwan Barghouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwan_Barghouti) until his arrest in 2002.

- [Force 17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_17) (early 1970s–2007)&#x20;

  - Elite unit of the PLO once under Yasser Arafat's direct guidance.

  - Acts as a versatile unit for combat and [intelligence](146678.md)-gathering.

  - Dismantled in 2007 and incorporated into the Palestinian Presidential Guard.

- [Fatah Special Operations Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatah_Special_Operations_Group) (Fatah-SOG)&#x20;

  - Founded in the early 1970s by Col. Abdullah Abd al-Hamid Labib

  - Also known as the Martyrs of Tel Al Za'atar, Hawari, and Amn Araissi.

  - Recently inactive (as of 2004)

- [Ahmed Abu Reish Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Abu_Reish_Brigade?action=edit\&redlink=1)&#x20;

  - Extremist offshoot of Fatah.

  - Was involved in the July 17, 2004, kidnappings in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md).

  - Possibly linked to the Popular Resistance Committees

  - Led by Ahmed Abu Reish

- [Al Aqsa Martyrs Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Aqsa_Martyrs_Brigade)&#x20;

  - Responsible for many [suicide bombings](18835454.md) and shootings of Israeli civilians

  - Responsible for executing suspected conspirators and leaders of opposition against Arafat

  - Funded by Fatah and the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md)

  - Offshoot of this group, [Fatah Hawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatah_Hawks), has carried out guerrilla attacks against Israeli military personnel in the Gaza Strip.

- [Black September Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September_Organization) (1970–1973)&#x20;

  - Began as a small cell of Fatah men determined to take revenge upon King Hussein and the Jordanian army for [Black September in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September_in_Jordan). Recruits from the PFLP, as-Sa'iqa, and other groups also joined.

  - Carried out [Munich massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_massacre).

  - Carried out [Attack on the Saudi embassy in Khartoum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_the_Saudi_embassy_in_Khartoum)

  - In 1973 two members of the Black September attacked, with sub-machine guns and grenades, at the passenger lounge at [Ellinikon International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellinikon_International_Airport) in Athens, Greece. Three civilians have been killed and 55 have been wounded. After the attack the gunmen took hostages, for more than two hours, before surrendering to the Greek police. Most of the casualties and injured were Greeks and Americans.

    [^cite_note-131]

  - The group was established in 2006 by [Shaker al-Abssi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaker_al-Abssi) who led the group until killed by Lebanese forces in 2007.

  - [Abu Mohamad Awad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Mohamad_Awad?action=edit\&redlink=1) succeeded al-Abbsi as the group's leader.
