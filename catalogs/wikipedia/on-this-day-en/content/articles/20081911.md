[Hungary](13275.md) and [Serbia](29265.md) maintain diplomatic relations established between Hungary and the [Kingdom of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia) in 1921.[^cite_note-1] From 1921 to 2006, Hungary [maintained relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary%E2%80%93Yugoslavia_relations) with the Kingdom of Yugoslavia, the [Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) (SFRY), and the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (FRY) (later [Serbia and Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro)), of which Serbia is considered [shared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreement_on_Succession_Issues_of_the_Former_Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) (SFRY) or sole (FRY) legal [successor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succession_of_states).[^cite_note-2]

## History

Hungary and Serbia share deep historical ties that have been characterized by periods of cooperation and conflict. Contacts begin with the immigration of [Hungarian tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magyar_tribes) to the [Carpathian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Basin) around the 10th century. The first serious ties between [Serbs](59512.md) and [Hungarians](17106358.md) came with the formation of the [medieval Kingdom of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia_\(medieval\)) and the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary). Daughter of Serbian ruler [Uroš I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uro%C5%A1_I,_Grand_Prince_of_Serbia) Jelena married the son and heir of [Stephen II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_II_of_Hungary) around 1130 while marking the first dynastic marriage between the ruling families of the two countries.[^cite_note-Djokić-3]

Later, the Hungarian princess [Catherine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_of_Hungary,_Queen_of_Serbia) became the wife of King [Stefan Dragutin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Dragutin), while his brother, the later King [Stefan Milutin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Milutin), married the Hungarian princess [Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_of_Hungary,_Queen_of_Serbia). Serbian Prince [Stefan Lazarević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Lazarevi%C4%87) had a great alliance with King [Sigismund of Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismund,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) and formed the knightly [Order of the Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Dragon). He also received Belgrade as a gift in 1403, which then became the Serbian capital for the first time. During the defense against Turkish attacks, especially during the [siege of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Belgrade_\(1456\)) in 1456, the Hungarian hero [John Hunyadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hunyadi) stood out, who is mentioned in many [Serbian epic poems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_epic_poetry) as *Sibinjanin Janko*. King [Matthias Corvinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Corvinus) was a great friend of the Serbs and helped the Serbian states in their defense against the Turkish invasion. After the [fall of Smederevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Smederevo_\(1459\)) in 1459, he accepted thousands of Serbian refugees into Hungary and allowed the descendants of [Đurađ Branković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90ura%C4%91_Brankovi%C4%87) to bear the title of [despot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Despot_\(court_title\)) while fighting under the Hungarian flag.[^cite_note-4] One of them was the famous [Black Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Army_of_Hungary) commander [Vuk Grgurević-Branković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuk_Grgurevi%C4%87), known as Vuk the Fiery Dragon, a friend of Matthias Corvinus who conquered [Sarajevo](26786.md) and brought thousands of Serbian refugees from [Bosnia](3463.md) to [Syrmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrmia).[^cite_note-5] Hungarian and Serbian refugees fled from Ottoman occupations, and used to be part of the famous Winged [Hussars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussar), a military regiment of the previous [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth).&#x20;



Foreign Ministers Aleksandar Cincar-Marković and László Bárdossy signing the Treaty of Eternal Friendship between Yugoslavia and Hungary, 1940



Memorial plaque at the Embassy of Serbia in Budapest, in memory of [Imre Nagy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imre_Nagy) who found a sanctuary there during the [Hungarian Revolution of 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956)

However, relationship between two states turned tense in the later half of the 19th century, with the establishment of the [Kingdom of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia) and Serbian interests toward [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md) and [Vojvodina](176801.md), both having significant Serbian population. As part of [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), the Hungarian army took a significant part of the [invading army on Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_campaign_of_World_War_I) at the [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-6] After the war, Hungary lost Vojvodina, a former territory of the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), to Serbia.[^cite_note-7]

Hungary signed a Non-aggression Treaty and Treaty of Eternal Friendship with Yugoslavia in 1940. However, Hungary participated in the Axis invasion on Yugoslavia which prompted the then Prime Minister of Hungary [Pál Teleki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1l_Teleki) to commit suicide.[^cite_note-8] In the communist era, [Yugoslavia](34244.md)'s [Josip Broz Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito) was against the [Hungarian Revolution of 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1956) and supportive of Soviet military uses against Hungarians;[^cite_note-9] but the suppression of Hungarian protesters by Soviet army might have influenced Tito's decision to change against the Soviets at the [Prague Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Spring) a decade later.

The relationship between Serbia and Hungary went downhill in the 1990s, when Yugoslavia started to disintegrate. Hungary was one of the earliest supporters of newly independent [Croatia](5573.md) and allowed Croats to supply weapons through its territory.[^cite_note-10] However, during the [NATO](21133.md)-led [bombing of Serbia](322473.md), Hungary under [Prime Minister](378359.md) [Viktor Orbán](750582.md) refused the requests of the [United States](3434750.md) and [Great Britain](31717.md) to invade the [north of Serbia](176801.md) in order to hinder the intervention of Serbian forces in [Kosovo](17391.md) but expressed concern about the situation of the Hungarian minority in Serbia and had to cede airspace to NATO forces because it got membership in NATO before the war.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Several protests against the bombing were also organized in Budapest.[^cite_note-13] Therefore, relations between the two countries slowly improved in the post-war period. During the premiership of [Ferenc Gyurcsány](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferenc_Gyurcs%C3%A1ny), the relations continued to worsen, with Hungary being one of the earliest nations in the world to recognize Kosovo as an independent state.[^cite_note-14]

In 2013, a historical reconciliation was marked after decades of tensions between two countries.[^cite_note-15] The [President of Hungary](380393.md), [János Áder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A1nos_%C3%81der) gave a speech in front of the [Serbian National Assembly](619753.md) where he issued an official apology for the [Hungarian role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_in_World_War_II) in the [World War II persecution of Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_persecution_of_Serbs) such as [Novi Sad raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Sad_raid).[^cite_note-16] In turn, in 2014 the Serbian National Assembly symbolically voided Yugoslav laws on collective guilt of Hungarian people for crimes committed during World War II.[^cite_note-17]

## Political relations

Hungary (along with Greece, Slovakia and Romania) is considered one of the closest allies of Serbia in the [European Union](9317.md), while Serbia is one of Hungary's closest allies in foreign policies.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

Although it recognizes [Kosovo's independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Kosovo_declaration_of_independence), in many cases Hungary was on the Serbian side when issue of Kosovo was discussed, e.g. it voted against Kosovo's membership in the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) in 2023.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

In 2024, Hungary was the only EU member state to vote against a UN resolution recognizing the 11 July 1995 [Srebrenica massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica_massacre) as a genocide, effectively siding with Serbia.[^cite_note-23]

Neither Hungary nor Serbia have joined the sanctions against Russia following the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), with the exception of those Hungary had to adopt as a member of the European Union. Both countries have been vocally against restricting Russian gas imports, and as a consequence have also suffered a deterioration in relations with Ukraine.

In recent years Hungary has been viewed positively in Serbia. According to recent polls, Serbs consider Hungary to be their closest ally in the European Union. Among the neighboring countries, Hungary is viewed the most positive by Serbs.[^cite_note-24]

## Economic relations

Trade between two countries amounted to [\$](18717338.md)3.3 billion in 2023; Hungary's merchandise export to Serbia was over \$1.6 billion; Serbian exports were roughly the same standing at \$1.6 billion.[^cite_note-25]

Hungarian companies have been active on Serbian market. [OTP Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OTP_Bank_\(Serbia\)) is the second-largest bank in Serbia. [MOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOL_Group) has the retail network of 71 filling stations in Serbia. Hungarian manufacturing companies present in Serbia include Masterplast (building materials plant in [Subotica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subotica)), Tisza Automotive (automotive parts plant in [Senta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senta) and [Kikinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikinda)), UBM (animal feedingstuffs plant in [Šid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0id)).

Recently, there were several infrastructure projects connecting two countries: high-speed [Budapest–Belgrade railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest%E2%80%93Belgrade_railway) (opened in 2025), extension of the [TurkStream](45206475.md) natural gas pipeline (completed in 2021); currently ongoing projects include crude oil pipeline between [Algyő](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algy%C5%91_oil_field) and [Novi Sad](113288.md) with capacity of 5 million TOE (due to be completed by 2028) as well as additional high-voltage electricity transmission line (due to be completed by late 2020s).[^cite_note-26]

## Hungarians in Serbia

Main article: [Hungarians in Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarians_in_Serbia)



[Csárdás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cs%C3%A1rd%C3%A1s) folk dance in [Doroslovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doroslovo)

Hungarians in Serbia are a recognized [ethnic minority group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_group). They number 184,442, constituting 2.8% of the total population, which makes them the largest ethnic minority group in the country. The vast majority of Hungarians in Serbia live in the northern part of [Vojvodina](176801.md) province, where they make up 10.5% of provincial population. [Hungarians of Roman Catholic faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_Hungary) originated mostly from [Dunántúl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dun%C3%A1nt%C3%BAl), while [Hungarians of Protestant faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical-Lutheran_Church_in_Hungary) originated mostly from [Alföld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf%C3%B6ld).

Parts of today's Vojvodina were included into the medieval Kingdom of Hungary in the 10th century, and Hungarians then began to settle in the region. Until the 16th century, Hungarians formed the largest part of population in today's Vojvodina. After the [Ottoman](22278.md) conquest of Hungary, the region was devastated by the conquerors, the population were killed, or fled. Hungarians started to settle back to the territory after the Ottomans were driven out in the late 17th and early 18th centuries. Hungarian colonists settled in [Bačka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C4%8Dka) throughout the 18th century and by the 19th century, their colonization increased. The first Hungarian settlers in [Syrmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrmia) moved there during the 1860s, mainly from [Bačka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C4%8Dka).

## Serbs in Hungary

Main article: [Serbs of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_of_Hungary)

Serbs are a recognized ethnic minority in Hungary. According to data from the 2022 census, the population of ethnic Serbs in Hungary is 11,622, constituting 0.1% of the total population.[^cite_note-27]

Small Serb communities are scattered mostly in the southern part of the country (near the border with Serbia) where they usually make up from 2 to 5% of the population (up to around 240 ethnic Serbs at most): [Battonya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battonya) (241 ethnic Serbs or 4.8% of total population), [Deszk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deszk) (115 or 2.9%), [Hercegszántó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercegsz%C3%A1nt%C3%B3) (61 or 3.7%). There are couple of settlements with Serb communities in wider Budapest region ([Pest County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pest_County)), on the [Csepel Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Csepel_Island): [Lórév](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%B3r%C3%A9v) ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): Ловра / *Lovra*) - the only settlement with an ethnic Serb majority in Hungary (154 or 51%), and [Szigetcsép](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szigetcs%C3%A9p) (67 or 2.4%).[^cite_note-28] Most ethnic Serbs nowadays are to be found in cities and towns. In [Budapest](36787.md), ethnic Serbs form 7th largest ethnic group with 2,818 people or 0.1% of city's population.[^cite_note-29] In [Szeged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szeged), third-largest city in Hungary, Serbs form 4th largest ethnic group with 758 people or 0.4% of city's population. There are also small Serb communities in towns of [Baja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baja,_Hungary) (131 or 0.4%) and [Szentendre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szentendre) (114 or 0.4%).&#x20;



Grabovac Monastery

The presence of Serbs in the territory of present-day Hungary dates from the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages). Since the 15th century, and particularly during the late 17th-early 18th century, escaping from the [Ottoman](22278.md) threat, a large number of Serbs migrated to the Kingdom of Hungary. The South Slavic peoples (Serbs, Bosnian Muslims) expanded northward in the wake of the Ottoman conquest, while the Hungarian population that had survived it fled the area over the course of the 17th century. Throughout the 17th century, the newly settled Orthodox South Slavic population provided the Ottoman army in this region with military garrisons, logistical support, and food supplies. Consequently, the Hungarians derisively referred to the region of Ottoman conquest as "Rascia" (Serbia) thereafter.[^cite_note-30] The most prominent example of Serb architectural heritage in Hungary is the old town of [Szentendre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szentendre) (Serbian: *Sentandreja*), with seven Serbian Orthodox churches, brightly coloured merchant houses and the Museum of Serbian Orthodox Heritage. In Budapest, there is an old Serbian Orthodox church in Serb Street, [Pest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pest_\(city\)), and the famous Serbian college, Thökölyanum (Serbian: *Tekelijanum*). The number of Serbian Orthodox churches is far higher than would be expected by the small size of contemporary Serb population in Hungary. These [baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) churches were mostly built in the 18 and 19th centuries when Serb merchants formed rich and influential communities in Hungarian towns. Village churches show the historical presence of Serbs even in many places from where they absolutely disappeared by now.
