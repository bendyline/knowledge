The **politics of the [Central African Republic](5478.md)** formally take place in a framework of a [semi-presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system) [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic). In this system, the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heads_of_state_of_the_Central_African_Republic_\(and_Central_African_Empire\)) is the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state), with a [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heads_of_government_of_the_Central_African_Republic_\(and_Central_African_Empire\)) as [head of government](37570.md). [Executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power) is exercised by the government. [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power) is vested in both the government and parliament.

Changes in government have occurred in recent years by three methods: violence, negotiations, and elections. Both [François Bozizé](213625.md) and [Michel Djotodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Djotodia) assumed the Presidency through takeover by violent means; however, elections were held in March 2005 and promised in 2013. A ceasefire agreement in January 2013 called for a multipartisan unity government.

The government was deposed on 13 March 2003 by forces under the rebel leader Bozizé, who promised elections in 18 to 30 months. A new cabinet was set up on 1 April 2003. Elections were held on 13 March 2005.

On 11 January 2013, a ceasefire was signed by the [Séléka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9l%C3%A9ka) rebel coalition, which had aimed to bring down the government of President Bozizé. According to this agreement, a new unity government would be formed. The President would appoint a new prime minister from the opposition parties, and the [National Assembly of the Central African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_the_Central_African_Republic) would be dissolved and new legislative elections would be held within twelve months.[^cite_note-1]

However, two months later, the Séléka rebels felt their terms were not being met, and at the culmination of the [Central African Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Civil_War), they attacked and took the capital, [Bangui](56617.md). The president, Bozizé, fled to neighboring [Cameroon](5447.md) via the [Democratic Republic of Congo](76762.md) on 24 March 2013.[^cite_note-cnn20130324-2][^cite_note-nyt20130326-3]

On 14 December 2015, a new [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Central_African_Republic) by referendum and ratified on 27 March 2016. Since 30 March 2016, [Faustin-Archange Touadéra](15370659.md) is the president of the [Central African Republic](5478.md).

The president is elected by popular vote for a five-year term, the prime minister is appointed by the president. The president also appoints and presides over the [Council of Ministers](16885611.md), which initiates laws and oversees government operations.

## Legislative branch

Since 27 March 2016, the [Parliament of the Central African Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_the_Central_African_Republic) is composed of two bodies: the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_the_Central_African_Republic) and the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Central_African_Republic).

The [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_the_Central_African_Republic) (*Assemblée Nationale*) has 105 members, elected for a five-year term using the two-round (or [Run-off](25932.md)) system.

The [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Central_African_Republic) (*Sénat*) will have members, elected for a five-year term using an indirect vote.

## Judicial branch

The Supreme Court, or *Cour Supreme*, is made up of judges appointed by the president. There is also a Constitutional Court, and its judges are also appointed by the president.

## Administrative divisions

The Central African Republic is divided in 14 prefectures (prefectures), 2 economic prefectures\* (prefectures economiques), and 1 commune\*\*; Bamingui-Bangoran, Bangui\*\*, Basse-Kotto, Gribingui\*, Haute-Kotto, Haute-Sangha, Haut-Mbomou, [Kemo-Gribingui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemo-Gribingui), Lobaye, Mbomou, Nana-Mambere, Ombella-Mpoko, Ouaka, Ouham, Ouham-Pende, Sangha\*, Vakaga.
