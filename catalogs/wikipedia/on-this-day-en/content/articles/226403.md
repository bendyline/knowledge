# Gelignite



Gelignites&#x20;

**Gelignite** ([/ˈdʒɛlɪɡnaɪt/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)), also known as **blasting gelatin** or simply "**jelly**", is an [explosive material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_material) consisting of [collodion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collodion)-[cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) (a type of [nitrocellulose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrocellulose) or guncotton) dissolved in either [nitroglycerine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitroglycerine) or [nitroglycol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitroglycol) and mixed with [wood pulp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_pulp) and [saltpetre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_nitrate) ([sodium nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_nitrate) or [potassium nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_nitrate)).

It was invented in 1875 by Swedish chemist [Alfred Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Nobel), who also invented [dynamite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamite). It is more stable than dynamite, but can still suffer from "sweating" or leaching out nitroglycerine.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] Its composition makes it [easily moldable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_explosive) and safe to handle without protection, as long as it is not near anything capable of detonating it.&#x20;

Considered one of the cheapest explosives, it burns slowly and cannot explode without a [detonator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detonator). It can be stored safely as long as it does not sweat.[^cite_note-3]

In the United Kingdom, an explosives certificate issued by the local Chief Officer of Police is required for possession of gelignite.[^cite_note-4] Due to its widespread civilian use in [quarries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarry) and [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining), it has historically been used by rebel groups such as [Mudiad Amddiffyn Cymru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudiad_Amddiffyn_Cymru),[^cite_note-5] the [Provisional Irish Republican Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Irish_Republican_Army)[^cite_note-6] and the [Ulster Volunteer Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Volunteer_Force),[^cite_note-7] who often used gelignite as a [booster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booster_charge).
