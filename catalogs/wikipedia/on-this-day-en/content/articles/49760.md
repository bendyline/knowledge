A **letter bomb**[^cite_note-1] is an [explosive device](445224.md) sent via the [postal service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mail), and designed with the intention to injure or kill the recipient when opened. They have been used in [terrorist](30636.md) attacks such as those of the [Unabomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Kaczynski). Some countries have agencies whose duties include the interdiction of letter bombs and the investigation of letter bombings.[^cite_note-2] The letter bomb may have been in use for nearly as long as the common postal service has been in existence, as far back as 1764 (*see [Examples](#Examples)*).

## Description

Letter bombs are usually designed to explode immediately on opening, with the intention of seriously injuring or killing the recipient (who may or may not be the person to whom the bomb was addressed). A related threat is mail containing unidentified powders or chemicals, as in the [2001 anthrax attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_anthrax_attacks).

## Use by suffragettes

See also: [Suffragette bombing and arson campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragette_bombing_and_arson_campaign)

One of, if not the first, groups to consistently use letter bombs on a wide scale were the British [suffragettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragettes) of the [Women's Social and Political Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Social_and_Political_Union) in the years before the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War).[^cite_note-Sky-3] The group were the original inventors of a form of letter bomb designed to maim or kill politicians or opponents.[^cite_note-Sky-3] In 1913, numerous letter bombs were sent to politicians such as the [Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Exchequer) [David Lloyd George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lloyd_George) and [Prime Minister](24150.md) [H.H. Asquith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.H._Asquith), but they invariably all exploded in post offices, post boxes or in mailbags while in transit across the country.[^cite_note-Fern-4] Suffragettes also once attempted to [assassinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassinate) an anti-[women's suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage) judge, Sir [Henry Curtis-Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Curtis-Bennett_\(Chief_Metropolitan_Magistrate\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), with a letter-bomb made partly out of bullets, but the bomb was intercepted by London postal workers before it could reach him.[^cite_note-5]

## Patentability

Letter-bombs, along with [anti-personnel mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-personnel_mine), are typical examples of [subject-matter excluded from patentability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patentable_subject_matter) under the [European Patent Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Patent_Convention), because the publication or exploitation of such [inventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invention) are contrary to the "*[ordre public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_public)*" and/or [morality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morality) ([Article 53(a)](https://new.epo.org/en/legal/epc/2020/a53.html) [EPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Patent_Convention)).[^cite_note-6]

- What might be the first recorded case of a device broadly similar to a modern parcel bomb featured in the 18th century affair known as the [Bandbox Plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandbox_Plot). On November 4, 1712, a bandbox (i.e. a lightweight hat-box) was sent to [Robert Harley, Earl of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Harley,_Earl_of_Oxford), the British [Lord Treasurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Treasurer). It contained a number of loaded and cocked [pistols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistols), to whose triggers was attached a thread which would have made the pistols fire the moment the box was opened. The plot was foiled by the perspicacity of [Jonathan Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Swift), who happened to be visiting the Earl of Oxford. Swift, perceiving the thread, seized the package and cut the thread, thus disarming the device. The attack was laid at the door of the opposition [Whig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Whig_Party) party and threw enormous popular sympathy behind Harley. The precise perpetrators were never identified or apprehended.

- One of the world's first mail bombs is mentioned in the 18th century diary of Danish official and historian [Bolle Willum Luxdorph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolle_Willum_Luxdorph). His diary mainly consists of concise references to news from Denmark and abroad. In the entry for January 19, 1764, he writes the following: *Colonel Poulsen residing at [Børglum Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B8rglum_Abbey) was sent by mail a box. When he opens it, therein is to be found gunpowder and a firelock which sets fire unto it, so he became very injured.* The entry for February 15 same year says: *Colonel Poulsen receives a letter in German, \[saying] that soon the dose will be increased.* It is referring to the dose of gunpowder in the box. The perpetrator was never found.
