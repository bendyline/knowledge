# Stun grenade

"Flashbang" redirects here. For other uses, see [Flashbang (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashbang_\(disambiguation\)).

"Thunderflash" redirects here. For the RF-84F Thunderflash reconnaissance aircraft, see [Republic F-84F Thunderstreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_F-84F_Thunderstreak).



[M84 stun grenade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M84_stun_grenade)

A **stun grenade**, also known as a **flash grenade**, **flashbang**, **thunderflash**, or **sound bomb**,[^cite_note-1] is a [non-lethal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-lethal_weapon) [explosive device](445224.md) used to temporarily disorient an enemy's senses. Upon detonation, a stun grenade produces [a blinding flash of light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_blindness) and an extremely loud "bang". They are often used in [close-quarters combat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close-quarters_combat), [door breaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Door_breaching), and [riot control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riot_control), typically to stun enemies or distract them.[^cite_note-2]

Originally developed to simulate explosions during military training, stun grenades were first used by the [British Army](4887.md) [Special Air Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Air_Service)'s [counterterrorist](83392.md) wing in the late 1970s,[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] and have been used by [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police) and [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) forces worldwide since.[^cite_note-5]

Despite their less-lethal nature, stun grenades are still capable of causing harm, and can injure or kill when detonating in close proximity. They are also capable of sparking fires.[^cite_note-6]

## Effects



A stun grenade, thrown by U.S. Marines during a training exercise, detonating in a small room

Stun grenades are designed to produce a blinding flash of light of around 7 [megacandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megacandela) (Mcd) and an intensely loud "bang" of greater than 170 [decibels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decibel) (dB).[^cite_note-7]

## Construction

Unlike a [fragmentation grenade](20353360.md), stun grenades are constructed with a casing designed to remain intact during detonation and avoid [fragmentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragmentation_\(weaponry\)) injuries, while having large circular cutouts to allow the light and sound of the explosion through. The filler comprises a [pyrotechnic](187584.md) metal-oxidant mix of [magnesium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesium) or [aluminium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium) and an [oxidizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidizing_agent) such as [potassium perchlorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_perchlorate) or [potassium nitrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_nitrate).[^cite_note-8]

## Hazards

While stun grenades are designed to limit injury, permanent hearing loss has been reported.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] The concussive blast has the ability to cause injuries, and the heat generated may ignite flammable materials. The fires that occurred during the [1980 Iranian Embassy siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Embassy_siege) in London were caused by stun grenades.[^cite_note-embassy-11]
