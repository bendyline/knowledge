**Crimea**[^cite_note-2] ([/kraɪˈmiːə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/15/Crimea_pronunciation.mp3?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org\&utm_campaign=index\&utm_content=original](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/15/Crimea_pronunciation.mp3?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org\&utm_campaign=index\&utm_content=original)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*kry-MEE-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a [peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsula) in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe), on the northern coast of the [Black Sea](3386.md), almost entirely surrounded by the Black Sea and the smaller [Sea of Azov](78019.md). The [Isthmus of Perekop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Perekop) connects the peninsula to [Kherson Oblast](1273370.md) in mainland [Ukraine](31750.md). To the east, the [Crimean Bridge](22563317.md), constructed in 2018, spans the [Strait of Kerch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Kerch), linking the peninsula with [Krasnodar Krai](474125.md) in [Russia](25391.md). The [Arabat Spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabat_Spit), located to the northeast, is a narrow strip of land that separates the [Syvash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syvash) lagoons from the Sea of Azov. Across the Black Sea to the west lies Romania and to the south is Turkey. The population is 2.4 million,[^cite_note-pop-1] and the largest city is [Sevastopol](51587.md). The region, internationally recognised as part of Ukraine, has been under [Russian occupation since 2014](71944314.md).

Called the **Tauric Peninsula** until the [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period), Crimea has historically been at the boundary between the [classical world](252905.md) and the [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic%E2%80%93Caspian_steppe). [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_in_pre-Roman_Crimea) colonised its [southern fringe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Coast_\(Crimea\)) and were absorbed by the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Crimea) and [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherson_\(theme\)) Empires and [successor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Trebizond) [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodoro) while remaining culturally Greek. Some cities became trading colonies of [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa), until conquered by the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). Throughout this time the interior was occupied by a [changing cast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crimean_Steppe) of [steppe nomads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe_nomads), coming under the control of the [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde) in the 13th century from which the [Crimean Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate) emerged as a successor state. In the 15th century, the Khanate became a dependency of the Ottoman Empire. Lands controlled by Russia[^cite_note-3] and [Poland-Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_union) were often the target of [slave raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean%E2%80%93Nogai_slave_raids_in_Eastern_Europe) during this period.

In 1783, after the Russo-Turkish War (1768–1774), the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) [annexed Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Empire). Crimea's strategic position led to the 1854 [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) and [many short lived regimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimea_during_the_Russian_Civil_War) following the 1917 [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution). When the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik) secured Crimea, it became an [autonomous soviet republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Autonomous_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) within the [Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic). It was [occupied by Germany during World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_occupation_of_Crimea_during_World_War_II). When the Soviets retook it in 1944, [Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars) were [ethnically cleansed and deported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_of_the_Crimean_Tatars) under the orders of [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin), in what has been described as a cultural genocide. Crimea was downgraded to [an oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Oblast) in 1945. In 1954, the USSR [transferred the oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_Crimea_in_the_Soviet_Union) to the [Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) on the 300th anniversary of the [Pereyaslav Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Pereyaslav) in 1654.

After Ukrainian independence in 1991, most of the peninsula was reorganised as the [Autonomous Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Republic_of_Crimea). The [Soviet fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet) in Crimea was [in contention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet_dispute), but a [1997 treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_Treaty_on_the_Status_and_Conditions_of_the_Black_Sea_Fleet) allowed Russia to continue basing its fleet in Sevastopol. In 2014, the peninsula was [occupied](71944314.md) by [Russian forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_green_men_\(Russo-Ukrainian_War\)) and [annexed by Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), but most countries [recognise Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_reactions_to_the_annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) as Ukrainian territory.[^cite_note-INTLCOM-4]

## Name

The spelling "Crimea" is from the Italian form, *la Crimea*, since at least the 17th century[^cite_note-5] and the "Crimean peninsula" becomes current during the 18th century, gradually replacing the classical name of *Tauric Peninsula* in the course of the 19th century.[^cite_note-6] In English usage since the [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period) the Crimean Khanate is referred to as *Crim Tartary*.[^cite_note-7] Today, the Crimean Tatar name of the peninsula is *Qırım*, while the Russian is Крым (*Krym*), and the Ukrainian is Крим (*Krym*).[^cite_note-8]

The city *[Staryi Krym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stary_Krym)* ('Old Crimea'),[^cite_note-9] served as a capital of the Crimean province of the [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde). Between 1315 and 1329 CE, the Arab writer [Abū al-Fidā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu'l-Fida) recounted a political fight in 1300–1301 CE which resulted in a rival's decapitation and his head being sent "to the Crimea",[^cite_note-10] apparently in reference to the peninsula,[^cite_note-11] although some sources hold that the name of the capital was extended to the entire peninsula at some point during [Ottoman suzerainty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate) (1441–1783).[^cite_note-12]

The word *Qırım* is derived from the [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages) term *qirum* ("fosse, trench"), from *qori-* ("to fence, protect").[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] Another classical name for Crimea, *[Tauris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Crimea#Antiquity)* or *Taurica*, is from the Greek Ταυρική (*Taurikḗ*), after the peninsula's Scytho-Cimmerian inhabitants, the [Tauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauri). The name was revived by the Russian Empire during the mass hellenisation of [Crimean Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatar_language) place names after the [annexation of the Crimean Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_the_Crimean_Khanate_by_the_Russian_Empire), including both the peninsula and mainland territories now in Ukraine's Kherson and Zaporizhzhia oblasts.[^cite_note-16]

In 1764 imperial authorities established the [Taurida Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurida_Oblast) (*Tavricheskaia oblast*), and reorganised it as the [Taurida Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurida_Governorate) in 1802. While the Soviets replaced it with *Krym* ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Крим; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Крым) depriving it of official status since 1921, it is still used by some institutions in Crimea, such as the [Taurida National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurida_National_University) established by the [Crimean Regional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Regional_Government) in 1918, the [Tavriya Simferopol football club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Tavriya_Simferopol) so named in 1963, and the [Tavrida federal highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavrida_Highway) being built under [Russian occupation](71944314.md) from 2017.

Other suggestions either unsupported or contradicted by sources, apparently based on similarity in sound, include:

## History

Main article: [History of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Crimea)



Ruins of the ancient Greek colony of [Chersonesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chersonesus)

### Ancient history

Further information: [Bosporan Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosporan_Kingdom), [Greek Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Crimea), and [Crimea in the Roman era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimea_in_the_Roman_era)

The recorded history of Crimea begins around 5th century BCE when several [Greek colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_in_pre-Roman_Crimea) were established on its [south coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Coast_\(Crimea\)), the most important of which was [Chersonesos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chersonesos) near modern-day [Sevastopol](51587.md), with [Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians) and [Tauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tauri) in the hinterland to the north. The Tauri gave the name the Tauric Peninsula, which Crimea was called into the [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period). The southern coast gradually consolidated into the [Bosporan Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosporan_Kingdom) which was annexed by [Pontus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Pontus) in Asia Minor and later became a [client kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Client_kingdom) of [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Crimea) from 63 BCE to 341 CE.

### Medieval history



[Genoese fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese_fortress,_Sudak) in [Sudak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudak), 13th century, [Republic of Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa), originally a fortified [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) town, seventh century

The south coast remained Greek in culture for almost two thousand years including under Roman successor states, the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherson_\(theme\)) (341–1204 CE), the [Empire of Trebizond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Trebizond) (1204–1461 CE), and the independent [Principality of Theodoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Theodoro) (ended 1475 CE). In the 13th century, some Crimean port cities were controlled by the [Venetians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) and by the [Genovese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa), but the interior was much less stable, enduring a [long series of conquests and invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crimean_Steppe). In the medieval period, it was partially conquered by [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') whose [prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_the_Great) was baptised at [Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chersonesus_Cathedral) starting the [Christianisation of Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianisation_of_Kievan_Rus').[^cite_note-Norwich2013-23]

### Mongol Conquest (1238–1449)

The north and centre of Crimea fell to the [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol) [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde), although the south coast was still controlled by the Christian [Principality of Theodoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Theodoro) and [Genoese colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazaria_\(Genoese_colonies\)). The [Genoese–Mongol Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese%E2%80%93Mongol_Wars) were fought between the 13th and 15th centuries for control of south Crimea.[^cite_note-24]

### Crimean Khanate (1443–1783)

Main article: [Crimean Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate)



The Crimean Khanate after the destruction of the [Great Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Horde) in 1502

In the 1440s the [Crimean Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate) formed out of the collapse of the horde[^cite_note-25] but quite rapidly itself became subject to the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), which also conquered the coastal areas which had kept independent of the Khanate. A major source of prosperity in these times was [frequent raids into Eastern Europe for slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean%E2%80%93Nogai_slave_raids_in_Eastern_Europe).[^cite_note-26]

### Russian Empire (1783–1917)

See also: [1783 Russian annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1783_Russian_annexation_of_Crimea), [Novorossiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiya), and [Taurida Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurida_Governorate)



The 11-month [siege of Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sevastopol_\(1854%E2%80%9355\)) during the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War)

In 1774, the Ottoman Empire was [defeated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1768%E2%80%931774\)) by [Catherine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great) with the [Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_K%C3%BC%C3%A7%C3%BCk_Kaynarca) making the Tatars of the Crimea politically independent. Catherine the Great's [incorporation of the Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Empire) in 1783 into the Russian Empire increased Russia's power in the Black Sea area.[^cite_note-GP223-27]

From 1853 to 1856, the strategic position of the peninsula in controlling the Black Sea meant that it was the site of the principal engagements of the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War), where Russia lost to a French-led alliance.[^cite_note-28]

### Russian Civil War (1917–1921)

Main article: [Crimea during the Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimea_during_the_Russian_Civil_War)

During the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), Crimea [changed hands many times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimea_during_the_Russian_Civil_War) and was where [Wrangel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Nikolayevich_Wrangel)'s anti-Bolshevik [White Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement) made their last stand. Many anti-Communist fighters and civilians escaped to [Istanbul](3391396.md) but up to 150,000 were killed in Crimea.

### Soviet Union (1921–1991)

See also: [Crimea in the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimea_in_the_Soviet_Union) and [Transfer of Crimea to Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_Crimea_to_Ukraine)



The "[Big Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II)" at the [Yalta Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalta_Conference) in Crimea: [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill), [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt), and [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)

In 1921 the [Crimean Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Autonomous_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) was created as part of the [Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic).[^cite_note-blacksea-crimea/hist-29] It was [occupied by Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurida_Subdistrict) from 1942 to 1944 during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_campaign). After the Soviets regained control in 1944, they [deported the Crimean Tartars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_of_the_Crimean_Tatars) and several other nationalities to elsewhere in the USSR. The autonomous republic was dissolved in 1945, and Crimea became [an oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Oblast) of the Russian SFSR. [It was transferred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_Crimea_in_the_Soviet_Union) to the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR) in 1954, on the 300th anniversary of the [Treaty of Pereyaslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Pereyaslav).

### Independent Ukraine (since 1991)

Main article: [History of Crimea (1991–2014)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Crimea_\(1991%E2%80%932014\))

With the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) and Ukrainian independence in 1991 most of the peninsula was reorganised as the [Autonomous Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Republic_of_Crimea).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-szporluk-31][^cite_note-5_May_1992_in_Crimea-32][^cite_note-33] A [1997 treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_Treaty_on_the_Status_and_Conditions_of_the_Black_Sea_Fleet) partitioned the [Soviet Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet), allowing Russia to continue basing its fleet in Sevastopol, with the [lease extended](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_Pact) in 2010.

#### Russian occupation (from 2014)

Main articles: [Russian occupation of Crimea](71944314.md) and [Republic of Crimea (Russia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Crimea_\(Russia\))

Further information: [2014 Russian annexation of Crimea](42228673.md) and [Crimea attacks (2022–present)](71954377.md)



Unmarked Russian soldiers ("[Little Green Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_green_men_\(Russo-Ukrainian_War\))") outside the occupied [parliament of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkhovna_Rada_of_Crimea)

In 2014, Crimea saw demonstrations against the removal of the Russia-leaning [Ukrainian president](20688479.md) [Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych) [in Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity) and protests in support of [Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan).[^cite_note-EN25214-34][^cite_note-guardian226-35] Ukrainian historian Volodymyr Holovko estimates 26 February protest in support of the integrity of Ukraine in Simferopol at 12,000 people, opposed by several thousand pro-Russian protesters.[^cite_note-36] On 27 February, Russian forces occupied parliament and government buildings[^cite_note-37] and other strategic points in Crimea[^cite_note-38] and the Russian-organised [Republic of Crimea declared independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_the_Republic_of_Crimea) from Ukraine following an illegal and internationally unrecognised [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Crimean_status_referendum).[^cite_note-39] Russia then annexed Crimea, although most countries (100 votes in favour, 11 against, 58 abstentions) continued to recognise Crimea as part of Ukraine.[^cite_note-\:0-40][^cite_note-UNGA-41][^cite_note-INTLCOM-4][^cite_note-UKRMFA-42]

Geography of Crimea

Further information: [East European Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_European_Plain) and [Black Sea Lowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Lowland)

Covering an area of 27,000 km<sup>2</sup> (10,425 sq mi), Crimea is located on the northern coast of the Black Sea and on the western coast of the [Sea of Azov](78019.md); the only land border is shared with Ukraine's [Kherson Oblast](1273370.md) on the north. Crimea is almost an island and only connected to the continent by the [Isthmus of Perekop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmus_of_Perekop), a strip of land about 5–7 kilometres (3.1–4.3 mi) wide.

Much of the natural border between the Crimean Peninsula and the Ukrainian mainland comprises the [Syvash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syvash) or "Rotten Sea", a large system of shallow lagoons stretching along the western shore of the Sea of Azov. Besides the isthmus of Perekop, the peninsula is connected to the Kherson Oblast's [Henichesk Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henichesk_Raion) by bridges over the narrow [Chonhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chonhar_Strait) and [Henichesk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henichesk_Strait) straits and over Kerch Strait to the [Krasnodar Krai](474125.md). The northern part of [Arabat Spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabat_Spit) is administratively part of Henichesk Raion in Kherson Oblast, including its two rural communities of [Shchaslyvtseve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shchaslyvtseve) and [Strilkove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strilkove). The eastern tip of the Crimean peninsula comprises the [Kerch Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_Peninsula), separated from [Taman Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taman_Peninsula) on the Russian mainland by the [Kerch Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_Strait), which connects the Black Sea with the Sea of Azov, at a width of between 3–13 kilometres (1.9–8.1 mi).

Geographers generally divide the peninsula into three zones: the [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic_steppe), the [Crimean Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Mountains), and the [Southern Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Coast_\(Crimea\)).

### Crimean Mountains

Main article: [Crimean Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Mountains)



Eclizee-Burun Mountain

The southeast coast is flanked at a distance of 8–12 kilometres (5.0–7.5 mi) from the sea by a parallel range of mountains: the Crimean Mountains.[^cite_note-43] These mountains are backed by [secondary parallel ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuesta).

The main range of these mountains rises with extraordinary abruptness from the deep floor of the Black Sea to an altitude of 600–1,545 metres (1,969–5,069 ft), beginning at the southwest point of the peninsula, called [Cape Fiolent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fiolent). Some Greek myths state that this cape was supposedly crowned with the temple of [Artemis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis) where [Iphigeneia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iphigeneia) officiated as priestess.[^cite_note-EB1911-44] [Uchan-su](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uchan-su_\(waterfall\)), on the south slope of the mountains, is the highest waterfall in Crimea.[^cite_note-45]

### Hydrography

"Crimea river" redirects here; not to be confused with [Cry Me a River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cry_Me_a_River_\(disambiguation\)).

There are 257 rivers and major streams on the Crimean peninsula; they are primarily fed by rainwater, with snowmelt playing a very minor role. This makes for significant seasonal fluctuation in water flow, with many streams drying up completely during the summer.[^cite_note-46] The largest rivers are the [Salhyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salhyr) (Salğır, Салгир), the Kacha (Кача), the [Alma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma_\(Crimea\)) (Альма), and the Belbek (Бельбек). Also important are the Kokozka (Kökköz or Коккозка), the Indole (Indol or Индо́л), the [Chorna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chyornaya_\(Crimea\)) (Çorğun, Chernaya or Чёрная), the Derekoika (Dereköy or Дерекойка),[^cite_note-47] the Karasu-Bashi (Biyuk-Karasu or Биюк-Карасу) (a tributary of the Salhyr river), the Burulcha (Бурульча) (also a tributary of the Salhyr), the [Uchan-su](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uchan-su_\(river\)), and the Ulu-Uzen'. The longest river of Crimea is the Salhyr at 204 km (127 mi). The Belbek has the greatest average discharge at 2.16 cubic metres per second (76 cu ft/s).[^cite_note-48] The Alma and the Kacha are the second- and third-longest rivers.[^cite_note-BSE-49]



Following Russia's annexation of Crimea, Ukraine blocked the [North Crimean Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Crimean_Canal), which provided 85% of Crimea's drinking and agriculture water.

[^cite_note-canal-50]

There are more than fifty salt lakes and [salt pans](390410.md) on the peninsula. The largest of them is Lake Sasyk (Сасык) on the southwest coast; others include [Aqtas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqtas_Lake), Koyashskoye, Kiyatskoe, Kirleutskoe, Kizil-Yar, Bakalskoe, and [Donuzlav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donuzlav).[^cite_note-51] The general trend is for the former lakes to become salt pans.[^cite_note-52] [Lake Syvash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Syvash) (Sıvaş or Сива́ш) is a system of interconnected shallow [lagoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagoon) on the north-eastern coast, covering an area of around 2,560 km<sup>2</sup> (988 sq mi). A number of dams have created reservoirs; among the largest are the Simferopolskoye, Alminskoye,[^cite_note-53] the Taygansky and the Belogorsky just south of [Bilohirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilohirsk) in [Bilohirsk Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilohirsk_Raion).[^cite_note-54] The [North Crimea Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Crimean_Canal), which transports water from the [Dnieper](380138.md), is the largest of the man-made irrigation channels on the peninsula.[^cite_note-construction-55] Crimea was facing an unprecedented [water shortage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_shortage) crisis following the blocking of the canal by Ukraine in 2014.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-canal-50] After the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), the flow of water was restored however the [destruction of the Kakhovka Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_the_Kakhovka_Dam) could lead to problems with water supply again.

### Steppe

Main article: [Pontic–Caspian steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic%E2%80%93Caspian_steppe)

Seventy-five percent of the remaining area of Crimea consists of semiarid [prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie) lands, a southward continuation of the Pontic–Caspian steppe, which slope gently to the northwest from the foothills of the Crimean Mountains. Numerous [kurgans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurgan), or [burial mounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burial_mound), of the ancient [Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythia) are scattered across the Crimean steppes.

### Southern Coast

Main article: [Southern Coast (Crimea)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Coast_\(Crimea\))



The [Crimean Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Mountains) in the background and [Yalta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalta) as seen from the [Tsar's Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar's_Path).

The terrain that lies south of the sheltering Crimean Mountain range is of an altogether different character. Here, the narrow strip of coast and the slopes of the mountains are covered with greenery. This "riviera" stretches along the southeast coast from capes [Fiolent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fiolent) and [Aya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Aya), in the south, to Feodosia. There are many summer sea-bathing resorts such as [Alupka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alupka), [Yalta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalta), [Gurzuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurzuf), [Alushta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alushta), [Sudak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudak), and [Feodosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feodosia). During the years of Soviet rule, the resorts and [dachas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacha) of this coast were used by leading politicians[^cite_note-58] and served as prime perquisites of the politically loyal. In addition, vineyards and fruit orchards are located in the region. Fishing, mining, and the production of [essential oils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_oil) are also important. Numerous Crimean Tatar villages, mosques, [monasteries](45856.md), and palaces of the Russian imperial family and nobles are found here, as well as picturesque ancient Greek and medieval castles.

The Crimean Mountains and the southern coast are part of the [Crimean Submediterranean forest complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Submediterranean_forest_complex) ecoregion. The natural vegetation consists of scrublands, woodlands, and forests, with a climate and vegetation similar to the [Mediterranean Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Basin).

### Climate



Crimea's Southern Coast has a subtropical climate

Crimea is located between the [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate) and [subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_climate) belts and is characterised by warm and sunny weather.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] It is characterised by diversity and the presence of microclimates.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] The northern parts of Crimea have a moderate [continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate) with short but cold winters and moderately hot dry summers.[^cite_note-crimeageography-60] In the central and mountainous areas the climate is transitional between the continental climate to the north and the [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) to the south.[^cite_note-crimeageography-60] Winters are mild at lower altitudes (in the foothills) and colder at higher altitudes.[^cite_note-crimeageography-60] Summers are hot at lower altitudes and warm in the mountains.[^cite_note-crimeageography-60] A subtropical, Mediterranean climate dominates the southern coastal regions, is characterised by mild winters and moderately hot, dry summers.[^cite_note-crimeageography-60]

The climate of Crimea is influenced by its geographic location, relief, and influences from the [Black Sea](3386.md).[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] The Southern Coast is shielded from cold air masses coming from the north and, as a result, has milder winters.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] Maritime influences from the Black Sea are restricted to coastal areas; in the interior of the peninsula the maritime influence is weak and does not play an important role.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] Because a high-pressure system is located north of Crimea in both summer and winter, winds predominantly come from the north and northeast year-round.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] In winter these winds bring in cold, dry continental air, while in summer they bring in dry and hot weather.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] Winds from the northwest bring warm and wet air from the Atlantic Ocean, causing precipitation during spring and summer.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] As well, winds from the southwest bring very warm and wet air from the subtropical latitudes of the Atlantic Ocean and the Mediterranean sea and cause precipitation during fall and winter.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59]

Mean annual temperatures range from 10 °C (50.0 °F) in the far north ([Armiansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armiansk)) to 13 °C (55.4 °F) in the far south ([Yalta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalta)).[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] In the mountains, the mean annual temperature is around 5.7 °C (42.3 °F).[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] For every 100 m (330 ft) increase in altitude, temperatures decrease by 0.65 °C (1.17 °F) while precipitation increases.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] In January mean temperatures range from −3 °C (26.6 °F) in Armiansk to 4.4 °C (39.9 °F) in [Myskhor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myskhor?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] Cool-season temperatures average around 7 °C (44.6 °F) and it is rare for the weather to drop below freezing except in the mountains, where there is usually snow.[^cite_note-61] In July mean temperatures range from 15.4 °C (59.7 °F) in [Ai-Petri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ai-Petri) to 23.4 °C (74.1 °F) in the central parts of Crimea to 24.4 °C (75.9 °F) in Myskhor.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] The frost-free period ranges from 160 to 200 days in the steppe and mountain regions to 240–260 days on the south coast.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59]

Precipitation in Crimea varies significantly based on location; it ranges from 310 millimetres (12.2 in) in [Chornomorske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chornomorske) to 1,220 millimetres (48.0 in) at the highest altitudes in the Crimean mountains.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] The Crimean mountains greatly influence the amount of precipitation present in the peninsula.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] However, most of Crimea (88.5%) receives 300 to 500 millimetres (11.8 to 19.7 in) of precipitation per year.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] The plains usually receive 300 to 400 millimetres (11.8 to 15.7 in) of precipitation per year, increasing to 560 millimetres (22.0 in) in the southern coast at sea level.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] The western parts of the Crimean mountains receive more than 1,000 millimetres (39.4 in) of precipitation per year.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] Snowfall is common in the mountains during winter.[^cite_note-crimeageography-60]

Most of the peninsula receives more than 2,000 sunshine hours per year; it reaches up to 2,505 sunshine hours in [Qarabiy yayla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qarabiy_yayla) in the Crimean Mountains.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] As a result, the climate favours recreation and tourism.[^cite_note-crimeaclimate-59] Because of its climate and subsidised travel-packages from Russian state-run companies, the southern coast has remained a popular resort for Russian tourists.[^cite_note-62]

### Strategic value

Further information: [Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet)



Map of the [historical trade route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route_from_the_Varangians_to_the_Greeks) (shown in purple) connecting [Uppsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppsala) with [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople) via [Cherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chersonesus_\(Crimea\)). The major centres of [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') – [Kyiv](585629.md) itself, [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod) and [Ladoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staraya_Ladoga) – arose along this route.

&#x20;The Black Sea ports of Crimea provide quick access to the [Eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean), [Balkans](4829.md) and Middle East. [Historically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Crimea), possession of the southern coast of Crimea was sought after by most empires of the greater region since antiquity ([Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Crimea), [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherson_\(theme\)), [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gedik_Ahmed_Pasha), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurida_Governorate), [British and French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War), [Nazi German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sevastopol_\(1941%E2%80%931942\)), [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet#Soviet_Navy)).[^cite_note-63]

The nearby [Dnieper River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_River) is a major waterway and transportation route that crosses the European continent from north to south and ultimately links the Black Sea with the [Baltic Sea](3335.md), of strategic importance since the historical trade route [from the Varangians to the Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_from_the_Varangians_to_the_Greeks). The Black Sea serves as an economic thoroughfare connecting the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) region and the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) to central and Eastern Europe.[^cite_note-Crimea_Annexation_'Robbery_on_International_Scale'-64]

According to the [International Transport Workers' Federation](1521533.md), as of 2013 there were at least 12 operating merchant seaports in Crimea.[^cite_note-Черное_море_признано-65]

## Economy



Tourism is an important sector of Crimea's economy



[Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simferopol)'s city centre

In 2016 Crimea had Nominal GDP of [US\$](18717338.md)7 billion and US\$3,000 per capita.[^cite_note-66]

The main branches of the modern Crimean economy are agriculture and fishing oysters pearls, industry and manufacturing, tourism, and ports. Industrial plants are situated for the most part in the southern coast (Yevpatoria, Sevastopol, Feodosia, Kerch) regions of the republic, few northern (Armiansk, Krasnoperekopsk, Dzhankoi), aside from the central area, mainly Simferopol okrug and eastern region in Nizhnegorsk (few plants, same for Dzhankoj) city. Important industrial cities include [Dzhankoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzhankoi), housing a major railway connection, [Krasnoperekopsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnoperekopsk) and [Armiansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armiansk), among others.

After the Russian annexation of Crimea in early 2014 and subsequent sanctions targeting Crimea, the tourist industry suffered major losses for two years. The flow of holidaymakers dropped 35 percent in the first half of 2014 over the same period of 2013.[^cite_note-67] The number of tourist arrivals reached a record in 2012 at 6.1 million.[^cite_note-68] According to the Russian administration of Crimea, they dropped to 3.8 million in 2014,[^cite_note-69] and rebounded to 5.6 million by 2016.[^cite_note-70]

The most important industries in Crimea include food production, chemical fields, mechanical engineering, and metalworking, and fuel production industries.[^cite_note-CMU-71] Sixty percent of the industry market belongs to food production. There are a total of 291 large industrial enterprises and 1002 small business enterprises.[^cite_note-CMU-71]

In 2014, the republic's annual GDP was \$4.3 billion (500 times smaller than the size of Russia's economy). The average salary was \$290 per month. The [budget deficit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_budget_balance) was \$1.5 billion.[^cite_note-72]

### Agriculture

Agriculture in the region includes cereals, vegetable-growing, gardening, and [wine-making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winemaking_in_Crimea), particularly in the Yalta and [Massandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massandra) regions. Livestock production includes cattle breeding, poultry keeping, and sheep breeding.[^cite_note-CMU-71] Other products produced on the Crimean Peninsula include salt, [porphyry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porphyry_\(geology\)), [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), and [ironstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironstone) (found around [Kerch](47638.md)) since ancient times.[^cite_note-EB1911-44]

The vine mealybug (*[Planococcus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planococcus_\(bug\)) [ficus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planococcus_ficus)*) was first discovered here in 1868. First discovered on [grape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape), it has also been found as a [pest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pest_\(organism\)) of some other [crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop) and has since spread worldwide.[^cite_note-Daane-et-al-2008-73] [Sunn pests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunn_pest)—especially *[Eurygaster integriceps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurygaster_integriceps)*[^cite_note-Critchley-1998-74] and *[E. maura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurygaster_maura)*[^cite_note-Eurygaster-maura-AgroAtlas-75]—are significant [grain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain) pests.[^cite_note-Critchley-1998-74] [Scelioninae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scelioninae) and [Tachinidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachinidae) are important [parasitoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasitoid) of sunn pests.[^cite_note-Critchley-1998-74] [Bark beetles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bark_beetle) are pests of [tree crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_fruit), and are themselves hosts for *[Elattoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elattoma?action=edit\&redlink=1)* mites and various [entomopathogenic fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entomopathogenic_fungus) transmitted by those *Elattomae*.[^cite_note-Khaustov-2000-76][^cite_note-Hofstetter-Moser-2014-77]

Electricity

Crimea has 540 MW of its own electricity generation capacity, including the 100 MW Simferopol Thermal Power Plant, the 22 MW Sevastopol Thermal Power Plant and the 19 MW Kamish-Burunskaya Thermal Power Plant.[^cite_note-82] This local electricity generation has proven insufficient for local consumption, and since annexation by Russia, Crimea has been reliant on an underwater power cable to mainland Russia.[^cite_note-83]

Power generation is set to be increased by two combined-cycle gas steam turbo thermal plants PGU, each {*or should this be both combined?*} providing 470 MW (116 167 MW GT, 235 MW block), built by TPE (among others) with turbines provided by Power Machines; NPO Saturn with Perm PMZ; either modified GTD-110M/GTE-160/GTE-180 units or UTZ KTZ, or a V94.2 supplied by MAPNA, modified in Russia by PGU Thermal.

Solar photovoltaic SES plants are plentiful on the peninsula, including a small facility north of Sevastopol. There also is the Saky gas thermal plant near the Jodobrom chemical plant, featuring SaKhZ(SaChP) boosted production with Perm GTE GTU25P (PS90GP25 25 MW aeroderivative GP) PGU turbogenerators. Older plants in operation include the Sevastopol TEC (close to Inkerman) which uses AEG and Ganz Elektro turbines and turbogenerators generating about 25 MW each, Simferopol TEC, Yevpatoria, Kamysh Burun TEC (Kerch south – Zaliv) and a few others.

Main article: [Crimean Bridge](22563317.md)

In May 2015, work began on a multibillion-dollar road-rail link (a pair of parallel bridges) across the Kerch Strait.[^cite_note-84] The road bridge opened in May 2018, and the rail bridge in December 2019. With a length of 19 km, it is the longest bridge in Europe, surpassing [Vasco da Gama Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama_Bridge) in [Lisbon](18091.md). The Crimean Bridge was damaged by [an attack](71948884.md)[^cite_note-85] on 8 October 2022, and [another](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2023_Crimean_Bridge_explosion) on 17 July 2023.[^cite_note-86]

Public transportation

Almost every settlement in Crimea is connected with another settlement by bus lines. Crimea contains the longest (96 km or 59 mi) [trolleybus route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Trolleybus) in the world, founded in 1959, stretching from Simferopol to Yalta.[^cite_note-Crimea-87] The trolleybus line starts near [Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simferopol)'s Railway Station (in Soviet times it started near [Simferopol International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simferopol_International_Airport)) through the mountains to [Alushta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alushta) and on to Yalta. The length of line is about 90 km and passengers are assigned a seat. Simferopol, Yalta and Alushta also have an urban and suburban trolleybus network. Trolleybuses also operate in [Sevastopol](51587.md) and [Kerch](47638.md).

A tram system operates in the city of Yevpatoria. In the nearby [townlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban-type_settlement) village of Molochnoye, a 1.6 km-long tram line provides the only connection between the sea shore and a holiday resort, but its operation is halted since 2015.

Railway traffic

There are two railroad lines running through Crimea: the non-electrified [Armiansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armyansk)–Kerch (with a link to Feodosia), and the electrified [Melitopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melitopol)–Simferopol–Sevastopol (with a link to Yevpatoria), connecting Crimea to the Ukrainian mainland.

Until 2014 the network was part of the Cisdneper Directorate of the [Ukrainian Railways](3149341.md). Long-distance trains provided connection to all major Ukrainian cities, to many towns of Russia, [Belarus](3457.md) and, until the end of the 2000s, even to [Vilnius](32597.md), [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), [Warsaw](32908.md) and [Berlin](3354.md).

Since 2014 the railways are operated by the [Crimea Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimea_Railway). Local trains belong to the *Yuzhnaya Prigorodnaya Passazhirskaya Kompaniya* (Southern Suburban Passenger Company), serving the entire network of the peninsula and via the Crimean Bridge three trains daily to [Anapa](1194038.md). Long-distance trains under the name *Tavriya* – operated by the company *Grand Servis Ekspress* – connect Sevastopol and Simferopol daily with Moscow and Saint Petersburg; in the summer season Yevpatoria and Feodosia are also directly connected by them. Several times a week Simferopol is also linked with [Volgograd](32585.md), [Sochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sochi), [Yekaterinburg](66524.md), [Omsk](62593.md) and even [Murmansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk) by train.

Further development plans consist of a bypass line between Simferopol and Kerch, and a complete electrification of the network with changing the voltage of the already electrified lines from 3 kV DC to 25 kV 50 Hz AC.

The development of Crimea as a holiday destination began in the second half of the 19th century. The development of the transport networks brought masses of tourists from central parts of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). At the beginning of the 20th century, a major development of palaces, villas, and dachas began—most of which remain. These are some of the main attractions of Crimea as a tourist destination. There are many [Crimean legends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_legends) about famous touristic places, which attract the attention of tourists.

A new phase of tourist development began when the Soviet government started promoting the healing quality of the local air, lakes and therapeutic muds. It became a "health" destination for Soviet workers, and hundreds of thousands of Soviet tourists visited Crimea.

[Artek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artek_\(camp\)) is a former [Young Pioneer camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Pioneer_camp) on the [Black Sea](3386.md) in the town of [Hurzuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurzuf), near [Ayu-Dag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayu-Dag), established in 1925.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-tub-89] By 1969 it had an area of 3.2 km<sup>2</sup> (1.2 sq mi), and consisted of 150 buildings. Unlike most of the young pioneer camps, Artek was an all-year camp, due to the warm climate. Artek was considered to be a privilege for Soviet children during its existence, as well as for children from other communist countries. During its heyday, 27,000 children a year vacationed at Artek. Between 1925 and 1969 the camp hosted 300,000 children.[^cite_note-90] After the breaking up of the [Young Pioneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Pioneer_organization_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1991 its prestige declined, though it remained a popular vacation destination.[^cite_note-tub-89]

In the 1990s, Crimea became more of a get-away destination than a "health-improvement" destination. The most visited areas are the south shore of Crimea with cities of Yalta and Alushta, the western shore – Yevpatoria and Saky, and the south-eastern shore – Feodosia and Sudak. According to [National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_Society), Crimea was among the top 20 travel destinations in 2013.[^cite_note-91]

### Sanctions

Main article: [International sanctions during the Russo-Ukrainian war](43449324.md)

Following Russia's largely unrecognised annexation of Crimea, the [European Union](9317.md), the United States, Canada, Australia, Japan, and several other countries (including Ukraine) imposed [economic sanctions](411315.md) against Russia, including some specifically targeting Crimea. Many of these sanctions were directed at individuals—both Russian and Crimean.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] In general they prohibit the sale, supply, transfer, or export of [goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_\(economics\)) and technology in several sectors, including services directly related to tourism and infrastructure. They list seven ports where [cruise ships](314855.md) cannot dock.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] Sanctions against individuals include travel bans and asset freezes. [Visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_Inc.) and [MasterCard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MasterCard) temporarily stopped service in Crimea in December 2014.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] The Russian national payment card system allows Visa and MasterCard cards issued by Russian banks to work in Crimea.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] The [Mir payment system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_\(payment_system\)) operated by the [Central Bank of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Russia) operates in Crimea[^cite_note-102] as well as MasterCard[^cite_note-103] and Visa.[^cite_note-104] However, there are no major international banks in Crimea.[^cite_note-105]

## Politics

Main articles: [Politics of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Crimea) and [Political status of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_status_of_Crimea)

See also: [State Council of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_of_Crimea) and [Legislative Assembly of Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Sevastopol)

Crimea is Ukrainian territory that was occupied and [annexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) by Russia in 2014; Ukraine has not relinquished title and it remains internationally recognised as part of Ukraine.[^cite_note-\:0-40][^cite_note-UNGA-41][^cite_note-INTLCOM-4][^cite_note-UKRMFA-42] Ukrainian president [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) drew attention to this in August 2022 when he stated that it was "necessary to liberate Crimea" from Russian occupation and to re-establish "world law and order".[^cite_note-abcky-106]

Within the context of the largely unrecognised annexation, the politics of Crimea today is, *de facto*, that of two Federal subjects of Russia: the [Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Crimea_\(Russia\)) and the [federal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_cities_of_Russia) of [Sevastopol](51587.md). The [2024 elections to the State Council of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Crimean_parliamentary_election), the legislature of the Republic of Crimea, resulted in [United Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Russia) winning 68 of the 75 seats.[^cite_note-107] The State Council then re-elected [Sergey Aksyonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Aksyonov) to the position of [Head of the Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_Republic_of_Crimea), a position he had held since 2014.[^cite_note-108]

Crimea has four Deputies in the Russian [State Duma](1010187.md); three representing [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituencies_of_Russia) in the Republic of Crimea ([Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simferopol_constituency), [Kerch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerch_constituency) and [Yevpatoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevpatoria_constituency)) and one representing [Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevastopol_constituency).

As of 2014, the total population of the Republic of Crimea and Sevastopol was 2,248,400 people (Republic of Crimea: 1,889,485; Sevastopol: 395,000).[^cite_note-109] This was down from the [2001 Ukrainian Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Census_\(2001\)) figure of 2,376,000 (Autonomous Republic of Crimea: 2,033,700; Sevastopol: 342,451).[^cite_note-110]

According to the 2014 Russian census, 84% of Crimean inhabitants named Russian as their native language; 7.9% Crimean Tatar; 3.7% [Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatar_language); and 3.3% Ukrainian.[^cite_note-2014_Russian_Census-111] It was the first official census in Crimea since a Ukrainian-held census in 2001.[^cite_note-dt-112]

According to the 2001 census, 77% of Crimean inhabitants named [Russian as their native language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language_in_Ukraine); 11.4% Crimean Tatar; and 10.1% Ukrainian.[^cite_note-113] In 2013, however, the Crimean Tatar language was estimated to be on the brink of extinction, being taught in Crimea in only about 15 schools at that time. Turkey provided the greatest support to Tatars in Ukraine, which had been unable to resolve the problem of education in their mother tongue in Crimea, by modernising the schools.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]



Catholic Church of Santa Maria Assunta in [Kerch](47638.md), reference for the [Italians of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians_of_Crimea)

At the beginning of the 19th century, [Italian emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_diaspora) to the Crimea came from various Italian regions ([Liguria](71864.md), [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campania), [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia)), with immigrants settling mainly in the coastal cities of the [Black Sea](3386.md) and the [Sea of Azov](78019.md), as well as in [Odesa](55545.md), [Mykolaiv](901495.md), [Sevastopol](51587.md), [Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariupol), [Berdiansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berdiansk) and [Taganrog](1288065.md). With the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution) of 1917, with which the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) became the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), a bitter period began for minorities in Russia. [Italians of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians_of_Crimea) therefore faced much repression. Between 1936 and 1938, during [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)'s [Great Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Purge), many Italians were accused of [espionage](10501.md) and were arrested, tortured, deported or executed. The few survivors were allowed to return to Kerch in the 1950s and 1960s during [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev)'s administration. Crimean Tatars, a predominantly [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) ethnic minority who in 2001 made up 12.1% of the population,[^cite_note-2001CensusUKRCR-127] formed in Crimea in the early modern era, after the Crimean Khanate had come into existence. The Crimean Tatars were [forcibly expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_of_the_Crimean_Tatars) to Central Asia by [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)'s government as a form of collective punishment, on the grounds that some had joined the invading [Waffen-SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waffen-SS), forming [Tatar Legions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatar_Legion), during World War II. After the fall of the Soviet Union, Crimean Tatars began to return to the region.[^cite_note-128] According to the [2001 Ukrainian population census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Census_\(2001\)), 60% of the population of Crimea are ethnic Russians and 24% are ethnic Ukrainians.[^cite_note-2001CensusUKRCR-127]

[Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Russia) in Crimea were historically [Krymchaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krymchaks) and [Karaites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Karaites) (the latter a small group centred at [Yevpatoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevpatoria)). The 1879 census for the Taurida Governorate reported a Jewish population of 4.20%, not including a Karaite population of 0.43%. The Krymchaks (but not the Karaites) were [targeted for annihilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_Russia) during [Nazi occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurida_Subdistrict). The Nazis [murdered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_Ukraine) around 40,000 Crimean Jews.[^cite_note-129]

The number of [Crimea Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimea_Germans) was 60,000 in 1939. During WWII, they were forcibly deported on the orders of Stalin, as they were regarded as a potential "fifth column".[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] This was part of the 800,000 [Germans in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Germans_in_Russia,_Ukraine_and_the_Soviet_Union) who were relocated within the Soviet Union during Stalinist times.[^cite_note-133] The 2001 Ukrainian census reports just 2,500 ethnic Germans (0.1% of population) in Crimea.

Besides the Crimean Germans, Stalin in 1944 also deported 70,000 [Greeks](42056.md), 14,000 [Crimean Bulgarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Bulgarians)[^cite_note-134] and 3,000 [Italians of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians_of_Crimea).

## Culture

See also: [Crimean legends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_legends) and [Crimean Tatar cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatar_cuisine)



[Alexander Pushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pushkin) in Bakhchisaray Palace. Painting by [Grigory Chernetsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Chernetsov)

What is thought to be the first work of literature in the [Crimean Tatar language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatar_language), a version of *[Yusuf and Zulaykha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_and_Zulaykha)*, was composed around the early thirteenth century, apparently by [Mahmud Qırımlı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_Q%C4%B1r%C4%B1ml%C4%B1).[^cite_note-138] [Alexander Pushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pushkin) visited [Bakhchysarai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhchysarai) in 1820 and later wrote the poem [The Fountain of Bakhchisaray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fountain_of_Bakhchisaray). Crimea was the background for [Adam Mickiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Mickiewicz)'s seminal work, [The Crimean Sonnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crimean_Sonnets) inspired by his 1825 travel. A series of 18 [sonnets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonnets) constitute an artistic telling of a journey to and through the Crimea, they feature romantic descriptions of the oriental nature and culture of the East which show the despair of an exile longing for the homeland, driven from his home by a violent enemy.

[Ivan Aivazovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Aivazovsky), the 19th-century marine painter of Armenian origin, who is considered one of the major artists of his era was born in [Feodosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feodosia) and lived there for the most part of his life. Many of his paintings depict the Black Sea. He also created battle paintings during the Crimean War.[^cite_note-139]



Crimean tatar singer [Jamala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamala) dedicated her 2016 [Eurovision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision) winning song "[1944](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_\(song\))" to the deported Crimean Tatars

Crimean Tatar singer [Jamala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamala) won the [Eurovision Song Contest 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2016) representing Ukraine with her song "[1944](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_\(song\))", about the historic deportation of Crimean Tatars in that year by Soviet authorities.[^cite_note-140]

### Sport

Following [Crimea's vote to join Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Crimean_status_referendum) and subsequent [annexation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) in March 2014, the top football clubs withdrew from the [Ukrainian leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_football_league_system). Some clubs registered to join the Russian leagues but the [Ukrainian Association of Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Association_of_Football) objected. [UEFA](32332.md) ruled that Crimean clubs could not join the Russian leagues but should instead be part of a Crimean league system. The [Crimean Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Premier_League) is now the top football league in Crimea.[^cite_note-141] On 16 July 2023, [FC Sevastopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Sevastopol_\(2014\)) and [FC Rubin Yalta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Rubin_Yalta) played their first matches in the [Russian Second League Division B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Second_League), the fourth tier: the Ukrainian Association of Football asked UEFA and [FIFA](11049.md) to consider excluding the [Russian Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Football_Union) in response.[^cite_note-142]

Since 2014, a number of Crimean-born athletes have switched from representing Ukraine to representing Russia including [Vera Rebrik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Rebrik) (javelin), [Artem Ivanov (weightlifter)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artem_Ivanov_\(weightlifter\)), [Aleksey Sokirskiy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksey_Sokirskiy) (hammer thrower), and [Maksim Oberemko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maksim_Oberemko) (wind surfer).[^cite_note-143] While Russia was [suspended from all international athletic competitions](55980489.md), Rebrik participated in tournaments as a "neutral" athlete.[^cite_note-144]
