**Palau**,[^cite_note-9] officially the **Republic of Palau**,[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] is an [island country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_country) located in the [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia) subregion of [Oceania](22621.md) in the western [Pacific Ocean](23070.md). The Republic of Palau consists of approximately 340 islands and is the western part of the [Caroline Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Islands), while the eastern and central parts make up the [Federated States of Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_States_of_Micronesia).

It has a total area of 466 square kilometers (180 sq mi), making it the sixteenth [smallest country in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area).[^cite_note-12] The most populous island is [Koror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koror), home to the country's most populous city of [the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koror_\(city\)). The capital, [Ngerulmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngerulmud), is located on the largest island of [Babeldaob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babeldaob), in [Melekeok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melekeok) State. Palau shares [maritime boundaries](29613061.md) with [international waters](394357.md) to the north, the [Federated States of Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_States_of_Micronesia) to the east, [Indonesia](14579.md) to the south, and the [Philippines](23440.md) to the northwest.

The country was originally settled approximately 1,000 BCE by migrants from [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-SmithWMP-14] Palau was first drawn on a European map by the Bohemian missionary [Paul Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Clain)[^cite_note-Francis_X._Hezel,_SJ-15] based on a description given by a group of [Palauans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palauans) shipwrecked on the Philippine coast on [Samar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samar). The Palau islands were made part of the [Spanish East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_East_Indies) in 1885. Following Spain's defeat in the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War) in 1898, the islands were sold to [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) in 1899 under the terms of the [German–Spanish Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German%E2%80%93Spanish_Treaty_\(1899\)), where they were administered as part of [German New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_New_Guinea).

After [World War I](4764461.md), the islands were made a part of the Japanese-ruled [South Seas Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Seas_Mandate) by the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations). During [World War II](32927.md), skirmishes including the major [Battle of Peleliu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Peleliu) were fought between American and Japanese troops as part of the [Mariana and Palau Islands campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_and_Palau_Islands_campaign). Along with other [Pacific Islands](341111.md), Palau was made a part of the United States–governed [Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_Territory_of_the_Pacific_Islands) in 1947. Having voted in [a referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Trust_Territory_of_the_Pacific_Islands_constitutional_referendum) against joining the Federated States of Micronesia in 1978,[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] the islands gained full [sovereignty](28791.md) in 1994 under a [Compact of Free Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_of_Free_Association) with the United States.

Politically, Palau is a [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) republic in [free association](346414.md) with the [United States](3434750.md), which provides defense, funding, and access to [social services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_services). Legislative power is concentrated in the [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) [Palau National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_National_Congress). [Palau's economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Palau) is based mainly on tourism, [subsistence agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_agriculture), and fishing, with a significant portion of gross national product (GNP) derived from foreign aid. The country uses the United States dollar as its official currency. The islands' culture mixes Micronesian, [Melanesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesia), Asian, and Western elements. Ethnic Palauans, the majority of the population, are of mixed [Micronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesians), [Melanesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesians), and [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) descent. A smaller proportion of the population is of Japanese descent. The country's two [official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) are [Palauan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palauan_language) (a member of the [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_languages) language family) and English. The minor Micronesian languages [Sonsorolese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonsorolese) and [Tobian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobian_language), as well as [Japanese](15606.md), are recognized at the state level.

## Etymology

The name for the islands in the [Palauan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palauan_language), *Belau*, derives from the Palauan word for "village", *beluu* (thus ultimately from [Proto-Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Austronesian_language) \**banua*),[^cite_note-18] or from *aibebelau* ("indirect replies"), relating to a [creation myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_myth).[^cite_note-19] The name "Palau" originated in the Spanish *Los Palaos*, eventually entering English via the German *Palau*. An archaic name for the islands in English was the "Pelew Islands".[^cite_note-20] The name of the country "Palau" is most likely not derived from the Malay word *Pulau* (island), despite the similarity in its word form.

### Early history

Palau was originally settled between the 3rd and 2nd millennia BCE by [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples), most likely from what is now the Philippines or Indonesia.[^cite_note-21] [Sonsorol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonsorol) was sighted by the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) as early as 1521, when the [Spanish mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_missions_in_the_Carolinas) of the *[Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad_\(ship\))*, the flagship of [Ferdinand Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan)'s voyage of circumnavigation, sighted two small islands around the [5th parallel north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_parallel_north), naming them "San Juan".[^cite_note-22]

In December 1696, a group of sailors from the [Caroline Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Islands) were stranded on [Samar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samar), near [Guiuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guiuan), when they met European missionary [Paul Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Clain). Using pebbles, the sailors attempted to show Klein the approximate location and size of the islands. He used this information to produce the first European map of the Palau area. Klein sent the map to [Jesuit Superior General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_General_of_the_Society_of_Jesus), along with a letter detailing the names of the islands, the culture of the people, and his experiences with them.[^cite_note-23]

### Spanish era

Koror chiefs in 1915

The Klein map and letter caused a vast interest in the new islands. Another letter written by Fr. Andrés Serrano was sent to Europe in 1705, essentially copying the information given by Klein. The letters resulted in three unsuccessful Jesuit attempts to travel to Palau from [Spanish Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_the_Philippines) in 1700, 1708, and 1709. The islands were first visited by the [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) expedition led by Francisco Padilla on 30 November 1710. The expedition ended with the stranding of the two priests, Jacques Du Beron and Joseph Cortyl, on the coast of Sonsorol, because the mother ship *[Santísima Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity)* was driven to [Mindanao](20546.md) by a storm. Another ship was sent from [Guam](11974.md) in 1711 to save them only to capsize, causing the death of three more Jesuit priests. The failure of these missions gave Palau the original Spanish name *Islas Encantadas* (Enchanted Islands).[^cite_note-24]

### Transitions era



Palau in Japanese mandate

British traders became regular visitors to Palau in the 18th century (the British East India Company [packet ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packet_boat) [*Antelope*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antelope_\(1781_EIC_packet_ship\)) shipwrecked off [Ulong Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulong_Island) in 1783, leading to [Prince Lee Boo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Lee_Boo)'s visit to London), followed by expanding Spanish influence in the 19th century. Palau, under the name *Palaos*, was included in the [Malolos Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malolos_Congress) in 1898, the first revolutionary congress in the Philippines, which wanted full independence from colonialists. Palau was part of the [Spanish East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_East_Indies) headquartered in the Spanish Philippines. Palau had one appointed member to the Congress. The Malolos Congress also supported the right of Palau to self-determination if ever it wished to pursue such a path.[^cite_note-PhilStar2006-25] The term used in the Congress, *Palaos*, referred to the Pacific islands that were part of Philippine jurisdiction at the time. These islands include the [Marshall Islands](19147.md), [Mariana Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_Islands), and the [Caroline Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Islands) including Palau. At the time, Palau was the main center for trade in the area, thus, the entire area also became known as *Palaos*.[^cite_note-spotphquezon-26]

Later in 1899 as part of the Caroline Islands, Palau was sold by the Spanish Empire to the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) as part of [German New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_New_Guinea) in the [German–Spanish Treaty (1899)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German%E2%80%93Spanish_Treaty_\(1899\)). During [World War I](4764461.md), the [Japanese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) annexed the islands after seizing them from Germany in 1914. Following World War I, the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) formally placed the islands under Japanese administration as part of the [South Seas Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Seas_Mandate). In World War II, Palau was used by Japan to support its [1941 invasion of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_campaign_\(1941%E2%80%931942\)), which succeeded in 1942. The invasion overthrew the American-installed [Commonwealth government in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines) and installed the Japanese-backed [Second Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Philippine_Republic) in 1943.[^cite_note-27]

### United States era

During World War II, the United States captured Palau from Japan in 1944 after the costly [Battle of Peleliu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Peleliu), when more than 2,000 Americans and 10,000 Japanese were killed, and later the [Battle of Angaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Angaur). In 1945–1946, the United States re-established control of the Philippines and managed Palau through the Philippine capital of Manila. By the latter half of 1946, however, the Philippines was granted full independence with the formation of the [Third Republic of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1946%E2%80%931965\)), shifting the U.S. Far West Pacific capital to [Guam](11974.md). Palau was passed formally to the United States under [United Nations](31769.md) auspices in 1947 as part of the [Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_Territory_of_the_Pacific_Islands) established pursuant to [Security Council Resolution 21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_21).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

### Independence



TTPI High Commissioner and staff, 1960s

Four of the Trust Territory districts joined and formed the Federated States of Micronesia in 1979, but the districts of Palau and the [Marshall Islands](19147.md) voted against the proposed constitution. Palau, the westernmost cluster of the Carolines, instead opted for independent status in 1978, which was widely supported by the Philippines, [Taiwan](25734.md), and Japan. It approved a new constitution and became the Republic of Palau on 1 January 1981.[^cite_note-30] It signed a [Compact of Free Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compact_of_Free_Association) with the United States in 1982. In the same year, Palau became one of the founding members of the [Nauru Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauru_Agreement). The compact entered into force on 1 October 1994,[^cite_note-31] concluding Palau's transition from trusteeship to independence[^cite_note-gao-08-732_p7-32] as the last portion of the [Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trust_Territory_of_the_Pacific_Islands) to secure its independence pursuant to [Security Council Resolution 956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_956). Palau also became a member of the [Pacific Islands Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islands_Forum) but withdrew in February 2021 after a dispute regarding [Henry Puna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Puna)'s election as the forum's secretary-general.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] Legislation making Palau an "offshore" financial center was passed by the [U.S. Senate](24909346.md) in 1998.[^cite_note-35]

In 2005, Palau led the [Micronesia Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia_Challenge), which would conserve 30% of near-shore coastal waters and 20% of forest land of participating countries by 2020. In 2009, Palau created the world's first [shark sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark_sanctuary), banning commercial shark fishing within its waters. In 2012, the [Rock Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Islands) of Palau was declared as a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-36]

In 2015, Palau became a member of the [Climate Vulnerable Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Vulnerable_Forum) under the chairmanship of the Philippines, and at the same time, the country officially protected 80% of its water resources, becoming the first country to do so.[^cite_note-37] The protection of its water resources made significant increases in the country's economy in less than two years.[^cite_note-38] In 2017, it became the first state in the world to establish an eco-promise, known as the *Palau Pledge*, which is stamped on local and foreign passports.[^cite_note-39] In 2018, Palau and the Philippines began re-connecting their economic and diplomatic relations. The Philippines supported Palau to become an observer state in [ASEAN](106539.md).

## Politics and government

Main article: [Politics of Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Palau)



[Capitol of Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_of_Palau), the seat of government.

Palau is a [democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic). The [President of Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Palau) is both [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and [head of government](37570.md). Executive power is exercised by the government, while legislative power is vested in both the government and the [Palau National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_National_Congress). The [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Palau) is independent of the executive and the legislature. Palau adopted a constitution in 1981.

The Compact of Free Association between the United States and Palau[^cite_note-40] sets forth the free and voluntary association of their governments. It primarily focuses on the issues of government, economic, security, and defense relations.[^cite_note-41] Palau has no independent military, relying on the United States for its defense. Under the compact, the [American military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_military) was granted access to the islands for 50 years. The [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) role is minimal, limited to a handful of Navy [Seabees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabee_\(US_Navy\)) (construction engineers). The [U.S. Coast Guard](32223.md) patrols in national waters. The government has agreed to host a large [United States Air Force](32090.md) high-frequency radar station in Palau, an [over-the-horizon-radar](1694703.md) system costing well over \$100 million, which is expected to be operational in 2026.[^cite_note-id-20220505-42][^cite_note-td-20221230-43]

### Foreign relations

As a sovereign state, Palau conducts [its own foreign relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Palau).[^cite_note-gao-08-732_p7-32] Since independence, Palau has established diplomatic relations with numerous countries, including many of its Pacific neighbors, like Micronesia and the Philippines. On 29 November 1994, the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) passed [Resolution 963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_963) recommending Palau's admission to the United Nations. The [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) approved admission for Palau pursuant to Resolution 49/63 on 15 December 1994.[^cite_note-44] Palau has since joined several other international organizations. In September 2006, Palau hosted the first Taiwan-Pacific Allies Summit. Its president has made official visits to other Pacific countries, including Japan.[^cite_note-45] Taiwan has provided financial support for Palau to maintain a presence at international fora, including the [United Nations](31769.md) and [United Nations Climate Change Conference](38755144.md).[^cite_note-46]



Flags of countries who have foreign relations with Palau, [Palasia Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palasia_Hotel)

The United States maintains a diplomatic delegation and an embassy in Palau, but most aspects of the countries' relationship have to do with compact-funded projects, which are the responsibility of the U.S. [Department of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_the_Interior)'s [Office of Insular Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Insular_Affairs).[^cite_note-47] For example, as part of this compact, Palau was granted [ZIP Codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZIP_Code) 96939 and 96940, along with regular [U.S. Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service) delivery. In international politics, Palau often votes in tandem with the United States on [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) resolutions.[^cite_note-48]

Palau has maintained close ties with Japan, which has funded infrastructure projects, including the [Koror–Babeldaob Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koror%E2%80%93Babeldaob_Bridge). In 2015, Emperor [Akihito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akihito) and [Empress Michiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Michiko) visited [Peleliu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peleliu) to honor the 70th anniversary of World War II.[^cite_note-49]

In 1981, Palau voted for the world's first nuclear-free constitution. This constitution banned the use, storage, and disposal of [nuclear](21785.md), [toxic chemical](27179600.md), gas, and [biological weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_weapon) without first being approved by a 75 percent majority in a referendum.[^cite_note-50] This ban delayed Palau's transition to independence because while negotiating the compact, the U.S. insisted on the option to operate [nuclear-propelled vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_marine_propulsion) and store nuclear weapons within the territory,[^cite_note-51] prompting campaigns for independence.[^cite_note-52] In 2017, Palau signed the United Nations [Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons).[^cite_note-53]

Palau is a member of the [Nauru Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauru_Agreement) for the Management of Fisheries.[^cite_note-radioaustralia-54] The Philippines, a neighboring ally of Palau to the west has expressed its intent to back Palau if ever it wishes to join [ASEAN](106539.md).[^cite_note-mb.com.ph-55]

In June 2009, Palau announced that it would [accept up to seventeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_detainees_at_Guantanamo_Bay) [Uyghurs](31783.md) who had previously been detained by the American military at [Guantanamo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_detention_camp),[^cite_note-Ctv2009-06-10-56] with some American compensation for the cost of their upkeep.[^cite_note-Abc2009-06-10-57] Only one of the Uyghurs initially agreed to resettlement,[^cite_note-Rnzi2009-06-30-58] but by the end of October, six of the seventeen had been transferred to Palau.[^cite_note-Afp2009-10-31-59] An aid agreement with the United States, finalized in January 2010, was reported to be unrelated to the Uyghur agreement.[^cite_note-60]

### Political economy

In the view of [Islands Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islands_Society) president Michael Walsh, Palau is a key example of the successes of modern state-building in the Indo-Pacific region. It has successfully transitioned peacefully from colonial rule to full admission to the United Nations. Palau has maintained strong foreign relations with its neighbors in its region of Oceania. There have also been pushes for Palau to have observer status to the ASEAN as a demonstration of its growing influence in the region.[^cite_note-66] However, Palau's peaceful transition to fully autonomous sovereign state is not without debate.

Palau is hugely reliant on international aid, as demonstrated by President Surangel Whipps Jr address to the UN General Assembly in 2021.[^cite_note-67] American influence has led some to contest that there are challenges to its sovereignty with its reliance on the American military under the Compact of Free Association, although not officially designated a de facto [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) or otherwise. American influence has resulted in huge changes to Palau's society, economy, and political processes, and as such Palau may not yet be seen as a fully independent state or a fully realized success of modern state-building.[^cite_note-68]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Palau)



Share of forest area in total land area, top countries (2021). Palau has the fifth highest percentage of forest cover in the world.

Palau's territory consists of an archipelago located in the Pacific Ocean. Its most populous islands are [Angaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angaur), [Babeldaob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babeldaob), [Koror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koror) and [Peleliu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peleliu). The latter three lie together within the same [barrier reef](87410.md), while Angaur is an oceanic island several kilometers to the south. About two-thirds of the population lives on Koror. The coral [atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atoll) of [Kayangel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayangel) is north of these islands, while the uninhabited [Rock Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Islands) (about 200) are west of the main island group. A remote group of six islands, known as the [Southwest Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Islands_\(Palau\)), some 604 kilometers (375 miles) from the main islands, make up the states of Hatohobei and Sonsorol.

### Climate

&#x20;Palau has a [tropical rainforest climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate) with an annual mean temperature of 28 °C (82 °F). Rainfall is heavy throughout the year, averaging 3,800 mm (150 in). The average humidity is 82% and, although rain falls more frequently between June and October, there is still much sunshine. Palau lies on the edge of the [typhoon belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_belt). Tropical disturbances frequently develop near Palau every year, but significant [tropical cyclones](8282374.md) are quite rare. [Mike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Mike), [Bopha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Bopha) and [Haiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Haiyan) are the only systems that struck Palau as typhoons on record.[^cite_note-69]

### Environment



Aerial view of [Ngerukewid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngerukewid)



Aerial view of [Rock Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Islands_\(Palau\))



[Rock Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Islands_\(Palau\)) in Palau



An aerial view of limestone islands

Palau has a history of strong environmental conservation. For example, [Ngerukewid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngerukewid) and the surrounding area are protected under the Ngerukewid Islands Wildlife Preserve, which was established in 1956.[^cite_note-71] While much of Palau remains free of [environmental degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_degradation), areas of concern include illegal [dynamite fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_fishing), inadequate [solid waste disposal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_management) facilities in Koror, and extensive sand and coral [dredging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dredging) in the Palau lagoon.

As with other Pacific island states, [rising sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea-level_rise) presents a major environmental threat. However, according to the [Emissions Database for Global Atmospheric Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emissions_Database_for_Global_Atmospheric_Research) average [carbon dioxide emissions per person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_carbon_dioxide_emissions_per_capita) were 60 tonnes in 2019, the highest in the world, and mostly from transport.[^cite_note-72] Inundation of low-lying areas threatens coastal vegetation, agriculture, and an already insufficient [water supply](684928.md). [Wastewater treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wastewater_treatment) is a problem, along with the handling of [toxic waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxic_waste) from [fertilizers](37401.md) and [biocides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biocide).

One species of [saltwater crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltwater_crocodile), *Crocodylus porosus*, is native to Palau, occurring in varying numbers throughout the [mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) and in parts of the Rock Islands. Although this species is generally considered extremely dangerous, there has only been one fatal human attack, on 28 December 1965, in Palau in modern history.[^cite_note-73] This attack led to a crocodile eradication program and trade in crocodile hides that ran into the 1980s. A management and conservation program running since the 1990s has led to a stabilization of the Palauan crocodile population.[^cite_note-74]

On 5 November 2005, [President Tommy E. Remengesau Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Remengesau_Jr.) took the lead on a regional environmental initiative called the Micronesia Challenge, which would conserve 30% of near-shore coastal waters and 20% of [forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest) land by 2020. Following Palau, the initiative was joined by the Federated States of Micronesia, the Marshall Islands, and the US territories of Guam and [Northern Mariana Islands](21402.md). Together, this combined region represents nearly 5% of the marine area of the Pacific Ocean and 7% of its coastline.

Palau contains the [Palau tropical moist forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_tropical_moist_forests) terrestrial ecoregion.[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-75] It had a 2019 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index) mean score of 8.09/10, ranking it 27th globally out of 172 countries.[^cite_note-FLII-Supplementary-76] The country is vulnerable to earthquakes, [volcanic activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano), and [tropical storms](8282374.md).

### Shark sanctuary

Further information: [Palau National Marine Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_National_Marine_Sanctuary)

On 25 September 2009, Palau announced that it would create the world's first [shark sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shark_sanctuary).[^cite_note-Palau_creates_world's_first_shark_haven-77] Palau banned all commercial shark fishing within the waters of its [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone). The sanctuary protects about 600,000 square kilometers (230,000 sq mi) of ocean,[^cite_note-78] a similar size to France.[^cite_note-Palau's_EEZ_becomes_shark_sanctuary-79][^cite_note-World's_first_shark_sanctuary_created_by_Pacific_island_of_Palau-80][^cite_note-France-Size_Shark_Sanctuary_Created_--_A_First-81] President [Johnson Toribiong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_Toribiong) announced the sanctuary at a meeting of the United Nations.[^cite_note-Palau's_EEZ_becomes_shark_sanctuary-79][^cite_note-Palau_creates_shark_sanctuary_to_protect_tourism_and_prevent_overfishing-82][^cite_note-Palau_to_Ban_Shark_Fishing-83] Toribiong proposed a worldwide ban on fishing for sharks.[^cite_note-Palau's_EEZ_becomes_shark_sanctuary-79] In 2012, Palau received the Future Policy Award from [World Future Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Future_Council), because "Palau is a global leader in protecting marine ecosystems".[^cite_note-84]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Palau)



The artificially made [German Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Channel) is one of the most popular [dive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_diving) [sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_dive_sites). It is also a major transport route for boats that connects the lagoon to the Pacific Ocean in the south-west.



Aerial view of [Koror–Babeldaob Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koror%E2%80%93Babeldaob_Bridge) in 2016

Palau's economy consists primarily of tourism, subsistence agriculture, and fishing. Tourist activity focuses on scuba diving and snorkeling in the islands' rich marine environment, including its [barrier reefs](87410.md)' walls and World War II wrecks. In April 2022, Palau launched *Ol'au Palau*, a responsible tourism program aimed to preserve the country's natural environment and traditional culture.[^cite_note-85] The government is the largest employer, relying heavily on US financial assistance. Business and tourist arrivals numbered 65,558 in fiscal year 2025.[^cite_note-86]

Although Palau's ship registry represents less than 0.001% of the world fleet of commercial ships, it contains almost 60% of last-voyage flags in 2019. It suggests that the registry is used by shipping companies to evade end-of-life responsibilities. These responsibilities entail the decommissioning of a ship in such a way that the environmental impact and labor conditions are in order.[^cite_note-87]

The population enjoys a per capita income twice that of [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia) as a whole. Long-term prospects for the key tourist sector have been greatly bolstered by the expansion of air travel in the [Pacific Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Rim), the rising prosperity of leading East Asian countries and the willingness of foreigners to finance infrastructure development.[^cite_note-88]

In November 2006, [Pacific Savings Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Savings_Bank?action=edit\&redlink=1) officially announced bankruptcy. On 13 December 2006, the *[Palau Horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_Horizon)* reported that 641 [depositors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deposit_account) had been affected. Among them, 398 held less than [US\$](18717338.md)5,000, with the remainder ranging from US\$5,000 to US\$2 million. On 12 December, 79 affected people received compensation. Toribiong said, "The fund for the payout came from the balance of the Palau government's loan from [Taiwan](25734.md)." From a total of US\$1 million, which originally was for assisting Palau's development, US\$955,000 was left at the time of bankruptcy. Toribiong requested the Taiwanese government use the balance to repay its loans. Taiwan agreed to the request. The compensation would include those who held less than US\$4,000 in an account.[^cite_note-89]

The income tax has three brackets with [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_tax) rates of 9.3 percent, 15 percent, and 19.6 percent respectively. [Corporate tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_tax) is four percent, and the Palau Goods and Services Tax was introduced on 1 January 2023. It is a broad-based tax of 10%, applied to most goods and services and other items sold or consumed in Palau.[^cite_note-90] There are no [property taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax).

Major tourist draws in Palau include Rock Islands Southern Lagoon, a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md),[^cite_note-91] and four tentative UNESCO sites, namely, [Ouballang ra Ngebedech (Ngebedech Terraces)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngebedech_Terraces), [Imeong Conservation Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imeong_Conservation_Area), Yapease Quarry Sites, and [Tet el Bad (Stone Coffin)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tet_el_Bad_Stone_Coffin).[^cite_note-92]

## Healthcare

Palau is served by an 80-bed hospital, Belau National Hospital. With some medical specialties, there is no such specialty care in Palau, necessitating medical care in Taiwan, the Philippines, or Hawaii.[^cite_note-93] There are no dermatologists or ophthalmologists in Palau.[^cite_note-auto-94] VEGF drugs for diabetic eye diseases cannot be given for eye conditions so laser surgery is done by visiting American ophthalmologists.[^cite_note-auto-94] Belau National Hospital cannot treat certain brain hemorrhages necessitating emergency airlift to Taiwan.[^cite_note-95]

## Transportation



[Palau International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_International_Airport)

[Palau International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_International_Airport) provides scheduled direct flights with [Guam](11974.md) ([Antonio B. Won Pat International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_B._Won_Pat_International_Airport)), [Manila](184334.md) ([Ninoy Aquino International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino_International_Airport)), [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane) ([Brisbane Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane_Airport)) and [Taipei](57648.md) ([Taoyuan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoyuan_International_Airport)). In addition, the states of Angaur and Peleliu have regular service to domestic destinations. Air service has at times been spotty. [Palau Micronesia Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_Micronesia_Air), [Asian Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Spirit) and [Pacific Flier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Flier?action=edit\&redlink=1) provided service to the Philippines and other destinations at various times during the 2000s, but all suspended service.[^cite_note-96] [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines) provides near-daily service to and from Guam, and once-weekly service to [Yap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yap). [Korean Air](315411.md) also runs a direct service three times per week to [Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon). Two to four times per week, China Airlines flies between Koror and Taipei.

In May 2024, [Nauru Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauru_Airlines) started direct weekly flights to [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane_Airport), Australia. This service was taken over by [Qantas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qantas) in December 2024 operating the “Palau Paradise Express” once a week from Brisbane with a [Boeing 737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-800).[^cite_note-97] The route aims to enhance connectivity in the Pacific and boost tourism, trade, and people-to-people links, with support from the Australian and Palau governments.

Freight, military, and cruise ships often call at Malaehaka Harbor, on [Malakal Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malakal_Island) outside Koror. The country has no railways, and of the 61 km or 38 mi of highways, only 36 km or 22 mi are paved. Driving is on the right, and the speed limit is 40 km/h (25 mph). Taxis are available in Koror. They are not metered, and fares are negotiable. Transportation between islands mostly relies on private boats and domestic air services. However, there are some state-run boats[^cite_note-98] between islands as a cheaper alternative.

The population of Palau is approximately 21,947 (2025),[^cite_note-99] 73% of whom are native Palauans of mixed [Melanesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesia) and [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) descent. There are many Asian communities within Palau. [Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos_in_Palau) form the largest Asian group and second largest ethnic group in the country, dating back to the Spanish colonial period. There are significant numbers of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_in_Palau) and [Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans_in_Micronesia). There are also smaller numbers of Palauans of mixed or full [Japanese ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_settlement_in_Palau). Most Palauans of Asian origin came during the late 20th century with many Chinese, Bangladeshis, and Nepalese coming to Palau as unskilled workers and professionals.[^cite_note-100] There are also small numbers of Europeans and Americans.

### Languages

The official languages of Palau are [Palauan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palauan_language) and English. The indigenous languages (which are Sonsorolese and Tobian) are recognized as national languages.[^cite_note-101] Because of the inheritance of the education system from the Republic's time as a trust territory, English is a core subject within the Palauan Education System, with a majority of its population utilizing it as a second language. [A local dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palauan_English) influenced by [Philippine English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_English) is developing.

[Sonsorolese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonsorolese) and [Tobian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobian_language) are official within their respective states, [Sonsorol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonsorol) and [Hatohobei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatohobei);[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] the [Angaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angaur) dialect of Palauan is "the language" of that state. Japanese was made an additional official language of Angaur in the 1982 constitution,[^cite_note-const-104] when some elders still knew the language, but it is no longer spoken.

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Palau)

According to the 2020 census, 46.9% of the population is [Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church), 25.9% [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) (primarily [evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Christianity)), 5% [Seventh-day Adventist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh-day_Adventism), 5.1% [Modekngei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modekngei), 4.9% [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim), 0.9% [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints), and 11.4% other religions.[^cite_note-2020Census-2]

The German and Japanese occupations of Palau both subsidized missionaries to follow the Spanish. Germans sent Roman Catholics and Protestants, the Japanese sent Shinto and Buddhist, and the Spaniards sent Roman Catholic missionaries as they controlled Palau. Three-quarters of the population are Christians (mainly Roman Catholics and Protestants), while [Modekngei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modekngei) (a combination of Christianity, traditional Palauan religion and fortune telling) and the ancient Palauan religion are commonly observed. Japanese rule brought [Mahayana Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahayana_Buddhism) and [Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto) to Palau, which was the majority religion among Japanese settlers. However, following Japan's World War II defeat, the remaining Japanese largely converted to Christianity, while some continued to observe Buddhism but stopped practicing Shinto rites.[^cite_note-105] There are approximately 400 [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis) Muslims.

## Culture

See also: [Music of Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Palau)



A traditional Palauan [*bai*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_\(house\)) in 2012

Palauan society follows a very strict [matrilineal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrilineality) system. Matrilineal practices are seen in nearly every aspect of Palauan traditions, especially in funerals, marriages, inheritance, and the passing of traditional titles.

### Cuisine

The cuisine includes local foods such as [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), [taro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taro), [yam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yam_\(vegetable\)), potato, fish and pork. It is also heavily influenced by Japanese, American, and [Filipino cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_cuisine), because of the significant presence of Filipino migrant workers. [Fruit bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabat) soup is a Palauan delicacy.[^cite_note-106] Some local drinks include an alcoholic drink made from a coconut on the tree; a drink made from the roots of the [kava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kava); and the chewing of [betel nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areca_nut). A dessert called *tama* was developed in Palau.[^cite_note-107]

### Sports

[Baseball has been popular in Palau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_in_Palau) since its introduction to the country in the 1920s by the Japanese. The [Palau national baseball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_national_baseball_team) won the gold medal at the 1990, 1998 and [2010 Micronesian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Micronesian_Games), as well as at the [2007 Pacific Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Pacific_Games). On 20 June 2022, left fielder [Bligh Madris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bligh_Madris) played his first game for the [Pittsburgh Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Pirates) against the [Chicago Cubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Cubs), thus becoming the first person of Palauan descent to play in the MLB. Palau has a [national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_national_football_team), organized by the [Palau Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_Football_Association), but is not a member of [FIFA](11049.md). The association organized the [Palau Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_Soccer_League) until the outbreak of COVID-19.[^cite_note-110]

The [Belau Omal Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belau_Omal_Marathon) began in 2023 as a partnership between Palau and [Taiwan](25734.md).

## Education

Primary education is required until age 16. Schools include both public (including [Palau High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_High_School)) and private institutions as well as some fields of study available at [Palau Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palau_Community_College). For further undergraduate, graduate, and professional programs, students travel abroad to attend tertiary institutions, primarily in the United States. Palau offers distance learning through [San Diego State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_State_University) and the [University of the South Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_South_Pacific).[^cite_note-111]
