**Matthew Langford Perry** (August 19, 1969 – October 28, 2023) was an American and Canadian actor. Known primarily for his work in comedy, he gained international fame for starring as [Chandler Bing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandler_Bing) on the [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) television sitcom *[Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends)* (1994–2004), receiving his first [Primetime Emmy Award](6579025.md) nomination in 2002 for the role. Perry also appeared on *[Ally McBeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ally_McBeal)* (2002), *[The West Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_West_Wing)* (2003) and *[The Ron Clark Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ron_Clark_Story)* (2006) receiving [Primetime Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award) nominations for his performances in the latter two. He played a leading role in the NBC series *[Studio 60 on the Sunset Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_60_on_the_Sunset_Strip)* (2006–2007), and also became known for his leading film roles in *[Fools Rush In](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fools_Rush_In_\(1997_film\))* (1997), *[Almost Heroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_Heroes)* (1998), *[Three to Tango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_to_Tango)* (1999), *[The Whole Nine Yards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Whole_Nine_Yards_\(film\))* (2000), *[Serving Sara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serving_Sara)* (2002), *[The Whole Ten Yards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Whole_Ten_Yards)* (2004), and *[17 Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17_Again_\(film\))* (2009).

Perry was the co-creator, co-writer, executive producer and star of the [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) sitcom *[Mr. Sunshine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Sunshine_\(2011_TV_series\))*, which ran from February to April 2011.[^cite_note-1] In August 2012, he starred as sportscaster Ryan King on the NBC sitcom *[Go On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_On_\(TV_series\))*. He co-developed and starred in a revival of the [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) sitcom *[The Odd Couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Odd_Couple_\(2015_TV_series\))* portraying Oscar Madison from 2015 to 2017. He had recurring roles in the legal dramas *[The Good Wife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Wife)* (2012–2013), and *[The Good Fight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Fight)* (2017). Perry portrayed [Ted Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Kennedy) in *[The Kennedys: After Camelot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kennedys:_After_Camelot)* (2017) and appeared as himself in his final television appearance, *[Friends: The Reunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends:_The_Reunion)* (2021).[^cite_note-2] He voiced Benny in the video game *[Fallout: New Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallout:_New_Vegas)* (2010).

For most of his life, Perry suffered from [severe addictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_use_disorder) to drugs and alcohol. Through his recovery, he became an advocate for rehabilitation and a spokesperson for the [National Association of Drug Court Professionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Drug_Court_Professionals). In 2013, Perry received the Champion of Recovery Award from the [White House Office of National Drug Control Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Office_of_National_Drug_Control_Policy). In 2022, he released his memoir, *[Friends, Lovers, and the Big Terrible Thing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends,_Lovers,_and_the_Big_Terrible_Thing)*. He died on October 28, 2023, at age 54, from the acute effects of [ketamine](16948.md) use, exacerbated by accidental drowning. Five people were charged in connection with helping him acquire lethal doses of the drug; all five pleaded guilty and were sentenced to house arrest or prison.

Perry's portrayal of Chandler Bing was regarded as one of television's most recognizable comic performances. Critics noted his precision timing and a distinctive cadence that was widely imitated and quoted, and that he brought a rare strain of vulnerability to the sitcom archetype.[^cite_note-\:1-3][^cite_note-\:2-4]

## Early life and education

Matthew Langford Perry was born on August 19, 1969, in [Williamstown, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamstown,_Massachusetts).[^cite_note-guardian_obit-5] His mother, Suzanne Marie Morrison ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names) Langford, born 1943),[^cite_note-6] is a Canadian journalist who was press secretary to Canadian prime minister [Pierre Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Trudeau). His father, [John Bennett Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bennett_Perry) (born 1941), is an American actor and former model.[^cite_note-nytfame-7][^cite_note-8]

Perry's parents separated when he was a year old and his mother married Canadian broadcast journalist [Keith Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Morrison). Perry was mostly raised by his mother in Ottawa, Ontario, but he also lived briefly in Toronto and Montreal.[^cite_note-latobit-9] He attended [Rockcliffe Park Public School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockcliffe_Park_Public_School) and [Ashbury College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashbury_College), a boarding school in Ottawa.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-rematch-11] He had four younger maternal half-siblings—Caitlin, Emily, Will, and Madeline—as well as a younger paternal half-sister named Maria. His siblings "would stand and applaud" him for early performances.[^cite_note-Siblings-12]

By the age of 10, Perry started misbehaving. He stole money, smoked, let his grades slip and beat up fellow student and future Canadian prime minister [Justin Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Trudeau).[^cite_note-rematch-11][^cite_note-bbcobit-13] Perry later attributed his behavior to his feeling like a family outsider who no longer belonged once his mother began having children with Morrison. He wrote, "I was so often on the outside looking in, still that kid up in the clouds on a flight to somewhere else, unaccompanied."[^cite_note-Siblings-12] At age 14, Perry began consuming alcohol, and was drinking every day by his 18th birthday.[^cite_note-14] He practiced tennis, often for 10 hours per day,[^cite_note-nytfame-7] and became a top-ranked junior player in Canada with the possibility of a tennis career. However, his prospects diminished when he moved from Ottawa, at age 15, to live with his father in Los Angeles, where competition was much tougher.[^cite_note-nytfame-7][^cite_note-bbcobit-13][^cite_note-15]

At 15, Perry began studying acting at the [Buckley School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckley_School_\(California\)), a [college-preparatory school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College-preparatory_school) in [Sherman Oaks, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_Oaks,_Los_Angeles), from which he graduated in 1987.[^cite_note-16] While in high school, he took [improvisational comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvisational_comedy) classes at L.A. Connection in Sherman Oaks.[^cite_note-Snarky-17]

## Career

### 1979–1993: Early roles



Perry in 1987

Perry's first credited role was a small part in *[240-Robert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/240-Robert)* in 1979 as a child actor.[^cite_note-deadlineobit-18][^cite_note-19] Shortly after moving to Los Angeles, Perry started auditioning for roles.[^cite_note-nytfame-7] Perry made guest appearances on *[Not Necessarily the News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Not_Necessarily_the_News)* in 1983, *[Charles in Charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_in_Charge)* in 1985, and *[Silver Spoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Spoons)* in 1986.[^cite_note-deadlineobit-18][^cite_note-Snarky-17] In 1987 and 1988, he played Chazz Russell in the television series *[Second Chance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Chance_\(1987_TV_series\))* (later called *Boys Will Be Boys*). Perry made his film debut in 1988 with *[A Night in the Life of Jimmy Reardon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Night_in_the_Life_of_Jimmy_Reardon)*.[^cite_note-20] In 1989, he had a three-episode arc on *[Growing Pains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growing_Pains)*, portraying Carol Seaver's boyfriend Sandy, who dies in a [drunk driving](29981150.md) incident.[^cite_note-21]

Perry was cast as a regular on the 1990 CBS sitcom *[Sydney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_\(TV_series\))*, playing the younger brother of [Valerie Bertinelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valerie_Bertinelli)'s character.[^cite_note-Snarky-17] In 1991, he made a guest appearance on *[Beverly Hills, 90210](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hills,_90210)* as Roger Azarian.[^cite_note-22] Perry played the starring role in the [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) sitcom *[Home Free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Free_\(1993_TV_series\))*, which aired in 1993.[^cite_note-23]

### 1994–2004: Breakthrough with *Friends*



Perry departing from rehearsal for the [1995 Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/47th_Primetime_Emmy_Awards)

Perry's commitment to a pilot for a sitcom called *LAX 2194*, set in the baggage handling department of Los Angeles Airport 200 years in the future,[^cite_note-24] initially made him unavailable for a role in another pilot, *Six of One*, later called *[Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends)*. After the *LAX 2194* pilot fell through, he had the opportunity to read for a part in *Six of One* and was cast as [Chandler Bing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandler_Bing). At age 24, he was the youngest member of the main cast.[^cite_note-25] After making the pilot and while waiting for the show to air, Perry spent the summer of 1993 performing at the [Williamstown Theater Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamstown_Theater_Festival) alongside [Gwyneth Paltrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwyneth_Paltrow).[^cite_note-26]

*Friends* was hugely successful, and it made Perry an international celebrity.[^cite_note-nytfame-7] By 2002, he and his co-stars [Jennifer Aniston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Aniston), [Courteney Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courteney_Cox), [Lisa Kudrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Kudrow), [Matt LeBlanc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_LeBlanc), and [David Schwimmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Schwimmer) were making \$1 million per episode.[^cite_note-27] The program earned him an [Emmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Awards) nomination in 2002 for the [Outstanding Lead Actor in a Comedy Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Lead_Actor_-_Comedy_Series) award.[^cite_note-emmys-28] Perry appeared in films such as *[Fools Rush In](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fools_Rush_In_\(1997_film\))*, *[Almost Heroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almost_Heroes)*, *[Three to Tango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_to_Tango)*, *[The Whole Nine Yards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Whole_Nine_Yards_\(film\))* and its sequel *[The Whole Ten Yards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Whole_Ten_Yards)*, and *[Serving Sara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serving_Sara)*.

For his performance as Joe Quincy in *[The West Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_West_Wing)*, Perry received two [Emmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award) nominations for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Drama Series in 2003 and 2004.[^cite_note-emmys-28] He appeared as attorney Todd Merrick in two episodes of *[Ally McBeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ally_McBeal).*[^cite_note-saveally-29] In 2004, he made his directorial debut and acted in an episode of the fourth season of the comedy-drama *[Scrubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrubs_\(2001_TV_series\))*, an episode which included his father.[^cite_note-starscrubs-30]

### 2005–2022: Later work

Perry starred in the [TNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_\(U.S._TV_network\)) movie *[The Ron Clark Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ron_Clark_Story),* which premiered August 13, 2006,[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] and received a [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe) and [Emmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy) nomination for his performance.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-emmys-28] From 2006 to 2007, he appeared in [Aaron Sorkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Sorkin)'s drama *[Studio 60 on the Sunset Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_60_on_the_Sunset_Strip)*. Perry played Matt Albie alongside [Bradley Whitford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradley_Whitford)'s Danny Tripp, a writer-director duo brought in to help save a failing [sketch show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sketch_comedy).[^cite_note-34]

In 2006, Perry began filming *[Numb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numb_\(2007_film\))*, a film based on a man suffering from [depersonalization disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depersonalization-derealization_disorder). The release was postponed several times, but it was finally released on DVD on May 13, 2008.[^cite_note-Numb-35] Perry also appeared on stage in London in [David Mamet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Mamet)'s *[Sexual Perversity in Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_Perversity_in_Chicago)*.[^cite_note-36] In 2008, Perry starred in the independent film *[Birds of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birds_of_America_\(film\))*.[^cite_note-37] [Showtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showtime_\(TV_network\)) passed on a pilot called *The End of Steve*, a [dark comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_comedy) starring, written, and produced by Perry and [Peter Tolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Tolan).[^cite_note-38]

In 2009, Perry starred in the film *[17 Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17_Again_\(film\))* playing a 37-year-old man who transforms into his 17-year-old self ([Zac Efron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zac_Efron)) after an accident.[^cite_note-39] The film received mixed reviews and was a box-office success.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] A review on [WRC-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WRC-TV) found Perry miscast in his role, emphasizing the disbelief in Efron growing up to resemble Perry, both physically and behaviorally — a sentiment echoed by other critics.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

In 2009, Perry was a guest on *[The Ellen DeGeneres Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ellen_DeGeneres_Show)*, when he presented [Ellen DeGeneres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_DeGeneres) with an [Xbox 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_360) video game console and a copy of the game *[Fallout 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallout_3)*. The gesture led to game studio [Obsidian Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsidian_Entertainment) casting him in *[Fallout: New Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallout:_New_Vegas)* as the voice of Benny.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]



Perry at the 62nd Primetime Emmy Awards in 2010

Perry's new comedy pilot, *[Mr. Sunshine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Sunshine_\(2011_TV_series\))*, based on his original idea for the show, was bought by [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_\(television_network\)).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] He played the lead role as a middle-aged man with an identity crisis.[^cite_note-49] ABC canceled the series after nine episodes in 2011.[^cite_note-50]



Perry in 2012

In 2012, Perry starred in the NBC comedy series *[Go On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_On_\(TV_series\))*, written and produced by former *Friends* writer/producer [Scott Silveri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Silveri). Perry portrayed Ryan King, a sportscaster who tries to move on after the death of his wife through the help of mandatory therapy sessions.[^cite_note-51] In the same year, he guest-starred on the CBS drama *[The Good Wife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Wife)* as attorney Mike Kresteva. He reprised his role in the fourth season in 2013.[^cite_note-52]

In 2014, Perry made his British television debut in the one-off comedy program *The Dog Thrower*, which aired on May 1 as part of [Sky Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Arts)' *[Playhouse Presents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playhouse_Presents)*. He portrayed "a charismatic man" who enchanted onlookers by throwing his dog in the air.[^cite_note-BBC_20140304-53] From 2015 to 2017, Perry starred in, co-wrote, and served as executive producer of a reboot of the sitcom *[The Odd Couple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Odd_Couple_\(2015_TV_series\))* on CBS. He played Oscar Madison opposite [Thomas Lennon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Lennon) as Felix Unger.[^cite_note-54]

Perry played the lead role in the world premiere production of his play *[The End of Longing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_End_of_Longing)*, which opened on February 11, 2016 at the [Playhouse Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playhouse_Theatre) in London.[^cite_note-55] Its limited run proved successful despite mixed reviews.[^cite_note-56] Perry restructured the play and appeared alongside [Jennifer Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Morrison) in its second [off-Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-Broadway) production, which opened at the [Lucille Lortel Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucille_Lortel_Theatre) on June 5, 2017. It closed on July 1 after receiving poor reviews.[^cite_note-57] Years later Perry described the play as "a personal message to the world, an exaggerated form of me as a drunk. I had something important to say to people like me, and to people who love people like me."[^cite_note-58]

In March 2017, Perry again reprised his role as attorney Mike Kresteva in *[The Good Fight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Fight)*, a sequel show to the CBS drama *[The Good Wife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Wife)*.[^cite_note-59] Later that year, he starred as [Ted Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Kennedy) in the mini-series *[The Kennedys: After Camelot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kennedys:_After_Camelot)*.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

In May 2021, he participated in the special episode *[Friends: The Reunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends:_The_Reunion)*.[^cite_note-62] He was meant to have a role in *[Don't Look Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Look_Up)*, but withdrew in 2020 because of CPR-induced broken ribs.[^cite_note-63] Perry published a memoir, *[Friends, Lovers, and the Big Terrible Thing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends,_Lovers,_and_the_Big_Terrible_Thing)*, in October 2022. It became a bestseller on both Amazon and *[The New York Times](30680.md)* charts.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

## Personal life

Perry held American citizenship by birth and Canadian citizenship through his mother. He dated [Yasmine Bleeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasmine_Bleeth) in 1995, [Julia Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Roberts) from 1995 to 1996, and [Lizzy Caplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizzy_Caplan) from 2006 to 2012.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] In November 2020, Perry became engaged to literary manager Molly Hurwitz. Their engagement ended in 2021.[^cite_note-68] Perry never married or had any children.

Residences owned at some point by Perry included a condo in [Sierra Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Towers) purchased from [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John), a house in [Hollywood Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Hills), a house in [Malibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malibu,_California), and a cottage in [Pacific Palisades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Palisades,_Los_Angeles).[^cite_note-69] In 2017, Perry purchased a condo occupying the top floor of [The Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Century_\(Los_Angeles\)) in Los Angeles for \$20 million,[^cite_note-70] selling it to [Nick Molnar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Molnar) for \$21.6 million in 2021.[^cite_note-71] In June 2023, Perry purchased a [mid-century modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-century_modern) house in [Hollywood Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Hills).[^cite_note-72]

Perry had a [perfectionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfectionism_\(psychology\)) and [obsessive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixation_\(psychology\)) personality, spending many hours perfecting his [answering machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Answering_machine) message.[^cite_note-nytfame-7] He also believed in God, with whom he had "a very close relationship",[^cite_note-73] calling himself "a seeker".[^cite_note-74]

### Health and addiction

In his memoirs, Perry wrote that by age 14, he had become an alcoholic.[^cite_note-guardian-75] He later described his first drink as “For the first time in my life, nothing bothered me. The world made sense; it wasn’t bent and crazy. I was complete, at peace.… I thought; this is what I’ve been missing. This must be how normal people feel all the time.” [^cite_note-76] He became addicted to [Vicodin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocodone/paracetamol) after a [jet ski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_watercraft) accident in 1997 and completed a 28-day rehab program at the [Hazelden Betty Ford Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazelden_Betty_Ford_Foundation) that year.[^cite_note-struggle-77] His weight dropped to 128 pounds (58 kg) as he took as many as 55 Vicodin pills per day.[^cite_note-colon-78][^cite_note-79] In May 2000, he was admitted to [Cedars-Sinai Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedars-Sinai_Medical_Center) with alcohol-induced [pancreatitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancreatitis).[^cite_note-struggle-77][^cite_note-80]

Perry said in 2002 that he made an effort not to drink on the set of *Friends* but arrived with extreme [hangovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangover) and sometimes would shake or sweat excessively.[^cite_note-nytfame-7][^cite_note-guardian-75] During later seasons, he was frequently drunk or high on set. His castmates made efforts to help him, and staged an [intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intervention_\(counseling\)),[^cite_note-guardian-75] but were unsuccessful.[^cite_note-nytfame-7]

In February 2001, Perry paused productions of *Friends* and *Serving Sara* for two months[^cite_note-nytfame-7] to enter in-patient rehabilitation for his addictions to Vicodin, [methadone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methadone), [amphetamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substituted_amphetamine) and alcohol.[^cite_note-81] He said later that, due to his [substance use disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_use_disorder), he had no memory of three years of his work on *Friends*.[^cite_note-82]

In 2018, Perry spent five months in a hospital for a [gastrointestinal perforation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrointestinal_perforation). During the hospital stay, Perry nearly died after his [colon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_intestine) burst from [opioid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opioid) abuse. He spent two weeks in a coma and used a [colostomy bag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostomy_system) for nine months. Doctors told his family that Perry had a 2% chance of survival when he was admitted. He was connected to an [extracorporeal membrane oxygenation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extracorporeal_membrane_oxygenation) (ECMO) machine.[^cite_note-colon-78]

Perry faked pain to get a prescription for 1,800 milligrams of [hydrocodone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocodone) per day and was having daily [ketamine](16948.md) infusions. He was given [propofol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propofol) in conjunction with a surgery, which stopped his heart for five minutes. The resulting CPR resulted in eight broken ribs. He paid \$175,000 for a private jet to take him to Los Angeles to get more drugs. When doctors there refused, he booked another \$175,000 private flight to fly back to Switzerland that night.[^cite_note-83] In 2022, he estimated that he had spent \$9 million on his addiction, including 14 stomach surgeries, 15 stays in rehab and therapy twice a week for 30 years and had attended approximately 6,000 [Alcoholics Anonymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholics_Anonymous) meetings.[^cite_note-bbcobit-13][^cite_note-84]

### Philanthropy and advocacy

In July 2011, Perry lobbied the [United States Congress](31756.md) as a celebrity spokesperson for the [National Association of Drug Court Professionals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Drug_Court_Professionals) in support of funding for [drug courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_courts_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-85] He received a Champion of Recovery award in May 2013 from the White House [Office of National Drug Control Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_National_Drug_Control_Policy) for opening Perry House, a rehab center in his former mansion in Malibu.[^cite_note-86] In 2015, Perry sold the mansion and relocated its services.[^cite_note-87] During the [COVID-19 pandemic](63136490.md), he launched an apparel line inspired by *Friends*, with proceeds donated to the [World Health Organization](33583.md)'s [COVID-19](63030231.md) relief fund.[^cite_note-88]

## Death and funeral

On October 28, 2023, Perry was found unresponsive in a [jacuzzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_tub) at his home in [Pacific Palisades, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Palisades,_Los_Angeles). He was pronounced dead at 4\:17 pm the same day. He was 54 years old.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

On November 3, 2023, Perry's funeral was held at [Forest Lawn Memorial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Lawn_Memorial_Park_\(Hollywood_Hills\)) in Los Angeles where he was buried.[^cite_note-91] His father, mother and stepfather attended, as did his five *Friends* co-stars.[^cite_note-92] The [Peter Gabriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gabriel) and [Kate Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Bush) song "[Don't Give Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Give_Up_\(Peter_Gabriel_and_Kate_Bush_song\))" was played; Perry was enamored of the song and referenced it in signed copies of his autobiography, released in part to help people suffering from depression or addiction issues.[^cite_note-93] Following Perry's death, the [National Philanthropic Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Philanthropic_Trust) established the Matthew Perry Foundation to support people suffering from addiction.[^cite_note-94]

On December 15, 2023, Perry's death was revealed to have occurred due to acute effects of [ketamine](16948.md).[^cite_note-death-cause-95][^cite_note-bbc_2023-12-15-96] Other circumstances that contributed to his death included the effects of [buprenorphine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buprenorphine), drowning, and [coronary artery disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_artery_disease).[^cite_note-death-cause-95] The [Los Angeles County Department of Medical Examiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Department_of_Medical_Examiner) said in a statement that

> ...at the high levels of ketamine found in his [post-mortem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-mortem) blood specimens, the main lethal effects would be from both cardiovascular overstimulation and respiratory depression... ...drowning contributes due to the likelihood of submersion into the pool as he lapsed into unconsciousness; coronary artery disease contributes due to exacerbation of ketamine induced myocardial effects on the heart.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

Perry had been receiving [ketamine-assisted psychotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketamine-assisted_psychotherapy) sessions to treat anxiety. His last known session occurred the week before his death. However, the report stated that the ketamine in his system at death could not be from that infusion, since ketamine's [half-life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_half-life) is 3 to 4 hours or less.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-bbc_2023-12-15-96]

### Investigation and guilty pleas

In May 2024, the Los Angeles Police Department opened an investigation to determine how Perry obtained the high dose of ketamine that caused his death.[^cite_note-100] On August 15, 2024, indictments and criminal charges were filed against five individuals: Perry's personal assistant, Kenneth Iwamasa; two physicians, Mark Chavez and Salvador Plasencia; and two alleged drug dealers (television director [Erik Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Fleming_\(director\)) and [Jasveen Sangha](81785708.md), known as the "Ketamine Queen").[^cite_note-flemingguiltyplea-101] Prosecutors alleged that the defendants were involved in the distribution of ketamine that resulted in Perry's death, as well as the death of at least one other individual.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-\:0-104]

Three of the accused – Fleming, Iwamasa, and Chavez, agreed to plead guilty.[^cite_note-\:0-104][^cite_note-pleasigned-105][^cite_note-chavezplea-106] Chavez's medical license was initially reported as suspended, but was later confirmed to have been voluntarily surrendered the following month.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-medicallicenseoct2024report-108][^cite_note-109] Following her arrest in August 2024, Sangha had her bail revoked; since then, she has been held at a federal prison.[^cite_note-sanghasentencedalreadydetained-110][^cite_note-111]

According to U.S. Attorney Martin Estrada, Perry paid the two doctors approximately \$55,000 in cash for ketamine during the two months preceding his death.[^cite_note-112] Iwamasa admitted to obtaining ketamine for Perry and administering injections, while Fleming admitted to obtaining ketamine from suppliers and delivering it to Iwamasa for Perry's use.[^cite_note-pleasigned-105] Text messages entered into evidence showed that Plasencia purchased ketamine supplied to Perry from Chavez, including one message in which Plasencia asking Chavez, "I wonder how much this moron will pay."[^cite_note-december3peoplecomplasenciasangha-113][^cite_note-114]

However, prosecutors stated that the primary supplier of the ketamine Perry used at the time of his death was Jasveen Sangha, described as a major drug dealer serving "high-end and celebrities." Sangha's [North Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Hollywood) residence, which was raided by the U.S. [Drug Enforcement Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEA) in March 2024, was characterised as a "drug-selling emporium."[^cite_note-fiveguiltypleas-115][^cite_note-116] In his guilty plea, Fleming admitted that he obtained 71 vials of ketamine from Sangha, which were intended for sale to Perry.[^cite_note-pleasigned-105][^cite_note-pleadeal-117] Plasencia and Sangha were identified by prosecutors as the primary targets of the investigation, with Sangha becoming Perry's main supplier after Plasencia reduced his distribution of ketamine.[^cite_note-118]

On August 18, 2025, Sangha, the final defendant in the case, had her guilty plea, which was signed on August 14, 2025, officially filed in court.[^cite_note-guiltypleas-119] Sangha pled guilty to one count of maintaining a drug-involved premises, three counts of distribution of ketamine, and one count of distribution of ketamine resulting in death or serious injury.[^cite_note-guiltypleas-119] She agreed to the plea shortly before her trial was scheduled to begin in September 2025.[^cite_note-120] At the time, Sangha also admitted to selling ketamine that contributed to at least one prior fatal overdose.[^cite_note-fiveguiltypleas-115][^cite_note-pleadeal-117] Sangha formally entered guilty pleas to all five counts while appearing before the U.S. District Court for the Central District of California on September 3, 2025.[^cite_note-jasveenofficialguiltyplea-121]

Plasencia, who remained free on bond until sentencing,[^cite_note-plasenciapleabondandsentence-122] faced up to 10 years' imprisonment on each of the four charges to which he pled guilty, for a combined statutory maximum of 40 years.[^cite_note-plasecniaguiltyplea-123][^cite_note-pleadeal-117] In September 2025, Plasencia surrendered his California medical license.[^cite_note-plasenciaofficiallysentencedandlicensesurrender-124][^cite_note-plasenciamedicallicensegoneandsentence-125] On December 3, 2025, Plasencia, the first defendant in the case to be sentenced, received a sentence of 30 months' imprisonment followed by two years of supervised release; he was also ordered to pay a \$5,600 fine.[^cite_note-126] Following sentencing, Plasencia surrendered and was taken into federal custody.[^cite_note-plasenciajailed-127][^cite_note-plasenciaofficiallysentencedandlicensesurrender-124][^cite_note-plasenciamedicallicensegoneandsentence-125]

It was later acknowledged that Chavez had relinquished his medical license, which had initially been reported as merely "suspended,"[^cite_note-medicallicenseoct2024report-108] after entering his guilty plea.[^cite_note-chavezmedicallicensegoneandsentenced-128] On December 16, 2025, Chavez was sentenced to eight months of house arrest, three years of supervised release, and 300 hours of community service.[^cite_note-chavezmedicallicensegoneandsentenced-128] Chavez would begin his sentence on December 17, 2025.[^cite_note-129]

Fleming, who pled guilty to one count of conspiracy to distribute ketamine and one count of distribution of ketamine resulting in death, faced a maximum sentence of 25 years' imprisonment.[^cite_note-flemingguiltyplea-101][^cite_note-pleadeal-117] Fleming was scheduled to be sentenced on January 7, 2026.[^cite_note-plasenciaofficiallysentencedandlicensesurrender-124][^cite_note-futuresentencings-130]

Iwamasa, who pled guilty to a single count of conspiracy to distribute ketamine resulting in death, faced a maximum sentence of 15 years' imprisonment.[^cite_note-pleadeal-117] Iwamasa was scheduled to be sentenced on January 14, 2026.[^cite_note-plasenciaofficiallysentencedandlicensesurrender-124][^cite_note-futuresentencings-130]

Sangha faced a statutory maximum of 45 years' imprisonment.[^cite_note-fiveguiltypleas-115] In December 2025, the office of the [U.S. Attorney for the Central District of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Attorney_for_the_Central_District_of_California) confirmed that Sangha was scheduled to be sentenced on February 25, 2026.[^cite_note-plasenciaofficiallysentencedandlicensesurrender-124][^cite_note-futuresentencings-130][^cite_note-131] On April 8, 2026, Sangha received a 15-year prison sentence to be served in a federal prison.[^cite_note-132] On May 13, 2026, Fleming was sentenced to two years in prison.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134] On May 27, 2026, Iwamasa was sentenced to 41 months in prison, ordered to pay fines of \$10,000 and \$100, and be on supervised released for two years.[^cite_note-iwamasasentence-135][^cite_note-iwamasaguiltyplea-136] Iwamasa began his sentence on July 20, 2026.[^cite_note-137]

## Legacy

Perry's portrayal of Chandler Bing on *Friends* (1994–2004) became one of television's most recognizable comic performances. Critics noted his precision timing and a distinctive "could I be any more…?" cadence that was widely imitated and quoted, and that he brought a rare strain of vulnerability to the sitcom archetype.[^cite_note-\:1-3][^cite_note-\:2-4]

Beyond acting, Perry advocated for addiction recovery. In 2013 he met with the White House Office of National Drug Control Policy to support drug courts, and that year opened Perry House, a men's sober-living facility in his former Malibu home, which operated from 2013 to 2015.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-guardian_obit-5] He said he hoped to be remembered more for helping people achieve sobriety than for his acting career, a mission continued by the Matthew Perry Foundation, launched days after his death.[^cite_note-139] Tributes reflected that impact: the main *Friends* cast issued a joint statement, and Max added an on-screen memorial card to the series. On August 19, 2025, Perry's *Fools Rush In* co-star [Salma Hayek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salma_Hayek) marked what would have been his 56th birthday with an Instagram tribute.[^cite_note-140]

In May 2026, [Heritage Auctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_Auctions) partnered with the Matthew Perry Foundation to sell his collection of art, *Friends* memorabilia, and accessories.[^cite_note-141]
