# Mudflow



Mailboxes caught in a mudflow following the May 1980 [Mount St. Helens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_St._Helens) volcanic eruption.

A **mudflow**, also known as **mudslide** or **mud flow**, is a form of [mass wasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_wasting) involving fast-moving flow of debris and [dirt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil) that has become liquified by the addition of water.[^cite_note-1] Such flows can move at speeds ranging from three to five metres per second (9.8 to 16.4 ft/s).[^cite_note-2] Mudflows contain a significant proportion of clay, which makes them more fluid than [debris flows](4415356.md), allowing them to travel farther and across lower slope angles. Both types of flow are generally mixtures of particles with a wide range of sizes, which typically become sorted by size upon deposition.[^cite_note-3]&#x20;

Mudflows are often called **mudslips**, a term applied indiscriminately by the mass media to a variety of mass wasting events.[^cite_note-4] Mudflows often start as slides, becoming flows as water is entrained along the flow path; such events are often called **mud failures**.[^cite_note-5]&#x20;

Other types of mudflows include [lahars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahar) (involving fine-grained [pyroclastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroclastic_rock) deposits on the flanks of volcanoes) and [jökulhlaups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B6kulhlaup) (outbursts from under glaciers or icecaps).[^cite_note-6]&#x20;

## Triggering of mudflows



The Mameyes mudflow disaster, in barrio [Tibes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibes), Ponce, [Puerto Rico](23041.md), was caused by heavy rainfall from [Tropical Storm Isabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugu%C3%A9s_Urbano#Mameyes_landslide) in 1985. The mudflow destroyed more than 100 homes and claimed an estimated 300 lives.

&#x20;Heavy rainfall, [snowmelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowmelt), or high levels of [groundwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_water) flowing through cracked bedrock may trigger a movement of soil or sediments in [landslides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslides) that continue as mudflows. [Floods](50482.md) and [debris flows](4415356.md) may also occur when strong rains on hill or mountain slopes cause extensive [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) and/or mobilize loose sediment that is located in steep mountain channels. The 2006 [Sidoarjo mud flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidoarjo_mud_flow) may have been caused by rogue drilling.&#x20;

The point where a muddy material begins to flow depends on its [grain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain) size, the water content, and the slope of the topography. Fine grained material like mud or sand can be mobilized by shallower flows than a coarse [sediment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sediment) or a [debris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debris) flow. Higher water content (higher precipitation/overland flow) also increases the potential to initiate a mudflow.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIversonReidLaHusen1997-7]&#x20;

After a mudflow forms, coarser sediment may be picked up by the flow. Coarser sediment picked up by the flow often forms the front of a mudflow surge and is pushed by finer sediment and water that pools up behind the coarse-grained moving mudflow-front.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFletcherHungrEvans2002-8] Mudflows may contain multiple surges of material as the flow scours channels and destabilizes adjacent hillslopes (potentially nucleating new mudflows).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeanMcCoyTuckerStaley2013-9] Mudflows have mobilized boulders 1–10 metres (3 ft 3 in – 32 ft 10 in) across in mountain settings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStockDietrich2006-10]&#x20;

Some broad mudflows are rather [viscous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscous) and therefore slow; others begin very quickly and continue like an [avalanche](73321.md). They are composed of at least 50% silt and clay-sized materials and up to 30% water. Because mudflows mobilize a significant amount of sediment, mudflows have higher flow heights than a clear water flood for the same water discharge. Also, sediment within the mudflow increases granular friction within the flow structure of the flow relative to clear water floods, which raises the flow depth for the same water discharge.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeanStaleyCannon2011-11]&#x20;

## Mudflows and landslides

"Mudslide" redirects here. For other uses, see [Mudslide (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudslide_\(disambiguation\)).

*[Landslide](37208.md)* is a more general term than mudflow. It refers to the gravity-driven failure and more subsequent movement downslope of any types of surface movement of soil, rock, or other debris. The term incorporates earth slides, rock falls, flows, and mudslides, amongst [other categories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landslide_classification) of hillslope [mass movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_wasting).[^cite_note-12]&#x20;

## Largest recorded mudflow

The world's largest historic subaerial (on land) landslide occurred during the [1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_eruption_of_Mount_St._Helens), a volcano in the Cascade Mountain Range in the US state of [Washington](13015878.md).[^cite_note-13] The volume of material displaced was 2.8 cubic kilometres (0.67 cu mi).[^cite_note-14]&#x20;

The largest known of all prehistoric landslides was an enormous [submarine landslide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine_landslide) that disintegrated 60,000 years ago and produced the longest flow of sand and mud yet documented on Earth. The massive submarine flow travelled 1,500 kilometres (930 mi) – the distance from London to Rome.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-FOOTNOTETallingWynnMassonFrenz2007-16]&#x20;

By volume, the [largest submarine landslide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_landslides) (the [Agulhas slide](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Agulhas_slide\&action=edit\&redlink=1) off South Africa) occurred approximately 2.6 million years ago. The volume of the slide was 20,000 cubic kilometres (4,800 cu mi).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDingle1977-17]&#x20;
