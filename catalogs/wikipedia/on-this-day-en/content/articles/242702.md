**Mpox** ([/ˈɛmpɒks/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [*EM-poks*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); originally known as **monkeypox**)[^cite_note-w315-7] is an infectious [viral disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_disease) that can occur in humans and other animals. Symptoms include a [rash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rash) that forms [blisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blister) and then crusts over, as well as fever and [swollen lymph nodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphadenopathy). The illness is usually mild, and most infected individuals recover within a few weeks without treatment. The time from [exposure to the onset of symptoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incubation_period) ranges from three to seventeen days, and symptoms typically last from two to four weeks. However, cases may be severe, especially in children, pregnant women, or people with [suppressed immune systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunodeficiency).[^cite_note-WHO_Q\&A-8][^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1][^cite_note-CDC_Sym-2]

The disease is caused by the [monkeypox virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monkeypox_virus), a [zoonotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoonotic) virus in the genus *[Orthopoxvirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthopoxvirus)*. The [variola virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variola_virus), which causes [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox), is also in this genus.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1] Human-to-human transmission can occur through direct contact with infected skin or body fluids, including sexual contact.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1] People remain infectious from the onset of symptoms until all the lesions have scabbed and healed.[^cite_note-WHO_Q\&A-8] The virus may spread from infected animals through handling infected meat or via bites or scratches.[^cite_note-WHO_Q\&A-8] Diagnosis can be confirmed by [polymerase chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymerase_chain_reaction) (PCR) testing a lesion for the virus's [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA).[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1]

Vaccination is recommended for those at high risk of infection.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1] No vaccine has been developed specifically against mpox, but smallpox vaccines have been found to be effective.[^cite_note-9] There is no specific treatment for the disease, so the aim of treatment is to manage the symptoms and prevent complications.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1][^cite_note-NIH_Mpox-10] Antiviral drugs such as [tecovirimat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecovirimat) can be used to treat mpox,[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1] although their effectiveness has not been proven.[^cite_note-CSC_TPOXX-11]

Mpox is [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_\(epidemiology\)) in [Central](539606.md) and [Western Africa](67393.md), where several species of mammals (specifically the [fire-footed rope squirrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-footed_rope_squirrel)) are suspected to act as a [natural reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_reservoir) of the virus.[^cite_note-\:7-12] The first human cases were diagnosed in 1970 in [Basankusu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basankusu), [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md).[^cite_note-Bunge2022-13] Since then, the frequency and severity of outbreaks have significantly increased, possibly as a result of waning [immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herd_immunity) since the cessation of routine smallpox vaccination.[^cite_note-Bunge2022-13] A global [outbreak of clade II in 2022–2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%932023_mpox_outbreak) marked the first incidence of widespread [community transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_transmission) outside of Africa. In July 2022, the [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO) declared the outbreak a [public health emergency of international concern](42694003.md) (PHEIC). The WHO reverted this status in May 2023,[^cite_note-WHO_PR_20240814-14] as the outbreak came under control, citing a combination of vaccination and public health information as successful control measures.[^cite_note-WHO2023-05-15]

An [outbreak of a new variant of clade I mpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_mpox_epidemic) (known as clade Ib) was detected in the Democratic Republic of the Congo during 2023.[^cite_note-16] As of August 2024, it had spread to several African countries, raising concerns that it may have adapted to more sustained human transmission.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-\:1-18] In August 2024, the WHO declared the outbreak a [public health emergency of international concern](42694003.md).[^cite_note-MSN_20240814-19][^cite_note-WHO_PR_20240814-14]

## Nomenclature

In 1958, the pathogen that caused mpox was discovered following two incidents where monkey colonies were infected with a pox-like illness for research purposes. It was originally dubbed "monkeypox virus", and the disease "monkeypox", as it was first detected in laboratory monkeys, but the exact origin of the disease is not known. While it is believed that transmission from African rodents and non-human primates, including monkeys, is possible, there is little evidence that any monkeys are a reservoir species.[^cite_note-CDC_About-20]

Beginning during the [2022 outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%932023_mpox_outbreak), public health experts and researchers, particularly in Africa, urged the [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO) to rename the disease, in order to decrease association with Africa.[^cite_note-21] There had been racist comments on social media associating the disease's name with African populations. Stigmatizing remarks had also wrongly identified mpox as a "gay disease", as gay men, bisexuals, and men who have sex with men are among the most affected globally. This stigma is thought to deter individuals from seeking diagnosis, vaccination, and treatment, reminiscent of the early days of the [HIV/AIDS pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemiology_of_HIV/AIDS) in the 1980s. Additionally, misinformation has incited violence against monkeys in certain regions, wrongly held accountable for transmitting *monkeypox*.[^cite_note-w315-7][^cite_note-w318-22][^cite_note-Berger_2025-23][^cite_note-Guardian_2022-24]

The [World Health Organization](33583.md) (the authority on disease names) announced the new name "mpox" in November 2022,[^cite_note-WHO1-25] this was gradually adopted as the preferred term in the [International Classification of Diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Classification_of_Diseases) (ICD) after December 2023.[^cite_note-w318-22] The name change retains a connection to [poxviruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poxviridae) while making it easier to spell in various languages.[^cite_note-w315-7] The subtypes of monkeypox virus were also renamed; the [clade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade) formerly known as "Congo Basin (Central African)" was renamed **clade I**, and the clade formerly known as "West African" was renamed **clade II.**[^cite_note-26] For the purpose of preserving access to historical records to facilitate research, the term *monkeypox* and old subtypes names will remain in the ICD database as searchable terms.[^cite_note-WHO1-25]

Virus naming is the responsibility of the [International Committee on the Taxonomy of Viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Committee_on_the_Taxonomy_of_Viruses) (ICTV). In June 2024, the official species name of the virus was updated from *monkeypox virus* to *Orthopoxvirus monkeypox* as part of a review of this [taxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxon).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-Who291122-28]

In September 2025, the United States [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](6811.md) (CDC) returned to using the name *monkeypox*, citing the *monkeypox virus* causes the disease.[^cite_note-p437-29][^cite_note-30]

[Initial symptoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prodrome) of mpox infection are fever, [muscle pains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle_pains), and sore throat, followed by an itchy or painful rash, headache, swollen lymph nodes, and [fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatigue). Not everyone will exhibit the complete range of symptoms.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1][^cite_note-CDC_Sym-2]

People with mpox usually become symptomatic about a week after infection. However the [incubation period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incubation_period) can vary in a range between one day and four weeks.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1][^cite_note-McFarland2023-32]

The rash comprises numerous small lesions, which may appear on the palms, soles, face, mouth, throat, genitals, or anus.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1] They begin as [small flat spots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macule), before developing into [small bumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papule), which then fill [with fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesicle_\(dermatology\)), eventually bursting and scabbing over, typically lasting around ten days.[^cite_note-CDC_Sym-2] In rare cases, lesions may become necrotic, requiring [debridement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debridement) and taking longer to heal.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-\:4-31]

Some people may manifest only a single sore from the disease, while others may have hundreds.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1] An individual can be infected with *Orthopoxvirus monkeypox* without showing [any symptoms](602790.md).[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1] Symptoms typically last for two to four weeks but may persist longer in people with weakened immune systems.[^cite_note-CDC_Sym-2][^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1]

### Complications

Complications include [secondary infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infection#Secondary_infection), [pneumonia](52135.md), [sepsis](158400.md), [encephalitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encephalitis), and loss of vision following [corneal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornea) infection.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1] Persons with weakened immune systems, whether due to medication, medical conditions, or HIV, are more likely to develop severe cases of the disease.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1] If infection occurs during pregnancy, this may lead to [stillbirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stillbirth) or other complications.[^cite_note-WHO_Q\&A-8]

### Outcome

Provided there are no complications, [sequelae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequela) are rare; after healing, the scabs may leave [pale marks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypopigmentation) before becoming [darker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperpigmentation) scars.[^cite_note-Goldman20202-34]

### Deaths

Historically, the [case fatality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_fatality_rate) (CFR) of past outbreaks was estimated at between 1% and 10%, with clade I considered to be more severe than clade II.[^cite_note-Death_Rates-35][^cite_note-Gessain_2022-36]

The case fatality rate of the [2022–2023 global outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%932023_mpox_outbreak) caused by clade IIb was very low, estimated at 0.16%, with the majority of deaths in individuals who were already [immunocompromised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunodeficiency).[^cite_note-37] In contrast, as of April 2024, the outbreak of clade I in Democratic Republic of the Congo has a CFR of 4.9%.[^cite_note-pmid_38753567-38]

The difference between these estimates is attributed to:

- differences in the [virulence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virulence) of clade I versus clade II.

  [^cite_note-pmid_38753567-38]

- under-reporting of mild or [asymptomatic](602790.md) cases in the endemic areas of Africa, which generally have poor healthcare infrastructure.

  [^cite_note-Death_Rates-35]

- [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimal_virulence) of the virus to cause milder disease in humans.

  [^cite_note-39]

- better general health, and better health care, in the populations most affected by the 2022–2023 global outbreak.

  [^cite_note-Death_Rates-35]

### In other animals

It is thought that small mammals provide a reservoir for the virus in endemic areas.[^cite_note-\:7-12] Spread among animals occurs via the [fecal–oral route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fecal%E2%80%93oral_route) and through the nose, through wounds and eating infected meat.[^cite_note-Wingfield20092-40] The disease has also been reported in a wide range of other animals, including monkeys, anteaters, hedgehogs, prairie dogs, squirrels, and shrews. Signs and symptoms in animals are not well researched and further studies are in progress.[^cite_note-CDC_Animals2-41]

There have been instances of animal infection outside of endemic Africa; during the 2003 US outbreak, [prairie dogs (*Cynomys ludovicianus*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_dog) became infected and presented with fever, cough, [sore eyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjunctivitis), poor feeding and rash.[^cite_note-CDCanimals2-42] There has also been an instance of a domestic [dog (*Canis familiaris*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog) which became infected displaying lesions and ulceration.[^cite_note-43]

## Cause

Mpox in both humans and animals is caused by infection with *Orthopoxvirus monkeypox* – a [double-stranded DNA virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_virus#Double-stranded_DNA_viruses) in the genus *[Orthopoxvirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthopoxvirus)*, family *[Poxviridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poxviridae),* which is closely related to the smallpox, [cowpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowpox), and [vaccinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccinia) viruses.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1]

The two major [subtypes of virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade#viruses) are clade I and clade II. In April 2024, after detection of a new variant, clade I was split into subclades designated Ia and Ib. Clade II is similarly divided into subclades: clade IIa and clade IIb.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1][^cite_note-UKHSA-4][^cite_note-44]

Clade I is estimated to cause more severe disease and higher mortality than clade II.[^cite_note-NIH_Mpox-10]



Monkeypox viral structure and scale

The virus is considered to be [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_\(epidemiology\)) in [tropical rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest) regions of Central and West Africa.[^cite_note-NIH_mpox-45] In addition to monkeys, the virus has been identified in [Gambian pouched rats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambian_pouched_rat) (*Cricetomys gambianus*), [dormice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormice) (*[Graphiurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphiurus)* spp.) and African squirrels (*[Heliosciurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliosciurus)*, and *[Funisciurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funisciurus)*). The use of these animals as food may be an important source of transmission to humans.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1]

## Transmission

The [natural reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_reservoir) of *Orthopoxvirus monkeypox* is thought to be small mammals in tropical Africa.[^cite_note-\:7-12] The first definite evidence of this was revealed in February 2026, when [squirrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-footed_rope_squirrel) meat was confirmed as a source of infection for a group of [sooty mangabeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sooty_mangabeys).[^cite_note-Nature651-46] The virus can be transmitted from animal to animal or to human from bites or scratches, through hunting or skinning infected animals, or through eating raw or poorly cooked meat.[^cite_note-CDC_Transmission-47][^cite_note-48] The virus enters the body through broken skin, or mucosal surfaces such as the mouth, respiratory tract, or genitals.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1]

Mpox can be transmitted from one person to another through contact with infectious lesion material or fluid on the skin, in the mouth or on the genitals; this includes touching, close contact, and during sex.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1][^cite_note-CDC_Transmission-47][^cite_note-49] There is also a risk of infection from [fomites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fomite) (objects which can become infectious after being touched by an infected person) such as clothing or bedding which has been contaminated with lesion material.[^cite_note-CDC_Transmission-47]

### Zoonotic spillover

Sustained human-human transmission leaves a signature in MPXV genomes, which makes it possible to determine for each lineage how much mutation has happened in humans. Genomic research based on this has shown that "MPXV diversity mostly reflects multiple independent" spillover events, of increased frequency.is thought to be small mammals in tropical Africa.[^cite_note-Nature651-46]

### Non-human hosts

While no animal has been shown to be a [natural reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_reservoir) responsible for specific spillover events, a significant number of wild hosts have been identified, in addition to the captive primates which lead to the virus's name. In addition, in the [2003 Mpox outbreak in the US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Midwest_mpox_outbreak), captive [black-tailed prairie dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-tailed_prairie_dogs) (*Cynomys ludovicianus*) which were the main vector, had been kept with rodents imported from Ghana ([giant pouched rats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_pouched_rats) *Cricetomys* spp., [rope squirrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rope_squirrels) *Funisciurus* sp. and [dormice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormice) *Graphiurus lorraineus*.[^cite_note-50] Further species collected from the distribution chain also tested positive (using various tests), some of them are only identified to genus level, for example all tests of at least seven *Graphiurus* spp. from one distributor were positive, but the species are not identified, while another sample is of one species (*Graphiurus lorraineus*) only.

## Diagnosis

Clinical differential diagnosis distinguishes between rash illnesses, such as chickenpox, measles, bacterial skin infections, [scabies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scabies), [poison ivy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poison_ivy), [syphilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syphilis), and medication-associated allergies.[^cite_note-o104-51]

[Polymerase chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymerase_chain_reaction) (PCR) testing of samples from skin lesions is the preferred diagnostic test, although it has the disadvantage of being relatively slow to deliver a result.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1] In October 2024, the WHO approved the first diagnostic test under the Emergency Use Listing (EUL) procedure. The [Alinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbott_Laboratories) m MPXV assay enables the detection of the virus by laboratory testing swabs of skin lesions, giving a result in less than two hours.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

## Prevention

### Vaccine

See also: [Smallpox vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox_vaccine)

Historically, [smallpox vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox_vaccine) had been reported to reduce the risk of mpox among previously vaccinated people in Africa. The decrease in immunity to poxviruses in exposed populations is a factor in the increasing prevalence of human mpox. It is attributed to waning cross-protective immunity among those vaccinated before 1980, when mass smallpox vaccinations were discontinued, and to the gradually increasing unvaccinated proportion of the population.[^cite_note-Kantele,_A._2016-54]

As of August 2024, there are four vaccines in use to prevent mpox, all originally developed against smallpox.[^cite_note-55]

- [MVA-BN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox_vaccine#MVA-BN) (marketed as Jynneos, Imvamune or Imvanex) manufactured by [Bavarian Nordic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Nordic). Licensed for use against mpox in Europe, United States and Canada.

  [^cite_note-56]

- [LC16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox_vaccine#LC16m8) from [KMB Biologics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KMB_Biologics?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Japan) – licensed for use in Japan.

  [^cite_note-57]

- OrthopoxVac, licensed for use in Russia and manufactured by the [State Research Center of Virology and Biotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Research_Center_of_Virology_and_Biotechnology_VECTOR) (VECTOR) in Russia

  [^cite_note-58]

- [ACAM2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACAM2000), manufactured by [Emergent BioSolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergent_BioSolutions). Approved for use against mpox in the United States as of August 2024.

  [^cite_note-FDA_PR_20240830-59]

  [^cite_note-60]

The MVA-BN and ACAM 2000 vaccines both contain [vaccinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccinia) virus. Vaccinia is a less virulent [orthopoxvirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthopoxvirus) than either monkeypox virus or variola virus (the causative agent of smallpox), and owing to a high level of protein identity among orthopoxviruses, the two vaccinia virus vaccines elicit antibodies that provide cross-protection against other orthopoxviruses such as monkeypox virus. Because the vaccinia virus in the MVA-BN vaccine cannot replicate, it is recommended over ACAM2000 in the United States for use by people who are either considered at high risk of exposure to mpox, or who may have recently been exposed to it.[^cite_note-\:2-61]

The United States [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](6811.md) (CDC) recommends that people investigating mpox outbreaks, those caring for infected individuals or animals, and those exposed by close or intimate contact with infected individuals or animals should receive a vaccination.[^cite_note-CDC_About-20] The CDC and the [EMA](1427054.md) recommend the use of a 2-dose MVA-BN vaccination series for adults who are considered at risk for becoming infected with mpox through sexual activity.[^cite_note-\:2-61][^cite_note-Imvanex-62]

### Other measures

The CDC has made detailed recommendations in addition to the [standard precautions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission-based_precautions#Standard_precautions) for infection control. These include that healthcare providers wear a gown, mask, goggles, and a disposable filtering respirator (such as an [N95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N95_respirator)), and that an infected person should be isolated a private room to keep others from possible contact.[^cite_note-63]

Those living in countries where mpox is endemic should avoid contact with sick mammals such as rodents, marsupials, non-human primates (dead or alive) that could harbour *Orthopoxvirus monkeypox* and should refrain from eating or handling wild game ([bush meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushmeat)).[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-WHO_Q\&A-8]

During the 2022–2023 outbreak, several public health authorities launched public awareness campaigns in order to reduce spread of the disease.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

## Treatment

Most cases of mpox present with mild symptoms and there is complete recovery within 2 to 4 weeks.[^cite_note-Gov.UK2022-6][^cite_note-WHO_Q\&A-8] There is no specific treatment for the disease, although antivirals such as [tecovirimat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecovirimat) have been approved for the treatment of severe mpox.[^cite_note-CSC_TPOXX-11][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-NIH_Mpox-10] A 2023 [Cochrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochrane_\(organisation\)) review found no completed [randomized controlled trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_controlled_trial) studying therapeutics for the treatment of mpox.[^cite_note-\:0-69] The review identified non-randomized controlled trials which evaluated the safety of therapeutics for mpox, finding no significant risks from [tecovirimat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecovirimat) and low certainty evidence that suggests [brincidofovir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brincidofovir) may cause mild liver injury.[^cite_note-\:0-69] Pain is common and may be severe; [supportive care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symptomatic_treatment) such as [pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pain_management) or [fever control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipyretic) may be administered.[^cite_note-WHO_Q\&A-8][^cite_note-BMJ_Best-70] People with mild disease should [isolate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isolation_\(health_care\)) at home, stay hydrated, eat well, and take steps to maintain their mental health.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1]

People who are at high risk from the disease include children, pregnant women, the elderly and those who are [immunocompromized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunodeficiency).[^cite_note-71] For these people, or those who have severe disease, hospital admission and careful monitoring of symptoms is recommended.[^cite_note-BMJ_Best-70] Symptomatic treatment is recommended for complications such as [proctitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proctitis) and [pruritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itch).[^cite_note-BMJ_Best-70]

A trial in the Democratic Republic of the Congo found that the antiviral drug [tecovirimat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecovirimat) did not shorten the duration of mpox lesions in people with clade I mpox.[^cite_note-72] Despite this, the trial's overall mortality rate of 1.7% was notably lower than the 3.6% or higher mortality rate seen in the Democratic Republic of the Congo's general mpox cases.[^cite_note-73] This suggests that hospitalization and high-quality supportive care significantly improve outcomes for mpox people.[^cite_note-74] The trial was sponsored by the [NIH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health) and co-led by the Democratic Republic of the Congo's Institut National de Recherche Biomédicale.

An additional 2024 study on [Siga Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIGA_Technologies)' antiviral drug, [tecovirimat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tecovirimat), found it ineffective in reducing lesion healing time or pain in adults with the clade II strain of mpox.[^cite_note-75] Based on interim results, a safety board recommended halting further patient enrollment. The trial, launched in September 2022 by the U.S. [National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases](1013155.md), involved patients from several countries, including the U.S., Argentina, and Japan, who had mpox symptoms for less than 14 days.[^cite_note-76] An interim analysis revealed no significant differences in lesion resolution or pain reduction between tecovirimat and a placebo.[^cite_note-77]

### Diagnostics in resource limited settings

With the August 2024 outbreak in the [DRC](76762.md), the [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO) urged manufacturers to submit their products for emergency review.[^cite_note-78] This initiative is part of the WHO's effort to ensure effective diagnostics, particularly for low-income populations. The agency has called for manufacturers to submit their tests for Emergency Use Listing, which would allow the WHO to approve these medical products more quickly.[^cite_note-79] This process is designed to help countries procure essential products through UN agencies and other partners. The urgency comes as a new, easily transmissible form of the 2024 outbreak has raised global concerns, leading the WHO to declare mpox a global public health emergency.[^cite_note-80]

### History

Mpox was first identified as a distinct illness in 1958 among laboratory monkeys in [Copenhagen](5166.md), Denmark, however the source of the disease remains unknown.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-CDC_About-20] The first documented human cases occurred in 1970, involving six unvaccinated children during the smallpox eradication efforts, with the first being a 9-month-old boy in the Democratic Republic of the Congo.[^cite_note-Bunge2022-13][^cite_note-Cho1973-82] From 1981 to 1986, over 300 human cases of mpox were reported in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (then known as Zaire), primarily due to contact with animals.[^cite_note-Meyer2002-83] The virus has been detected in [Gambian pouched rats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambian_pouched_rat), [dormice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormice), and African squirrels, which are often used as food.[^cite_note-\:3-84][^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1]

Many more mpox cases have been reported in Central and West Africa, particularly in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, where 2,000 cases per year were recorded between 2011 and 2014. However, the collected data is often incomplete and unconfirmed, hindering accurate estimations of the number of mpox cases over time.[^cite_note-Skle-85] Originally thought to be uncommon in humans, cases have increased since the 1980s,[^cite_note-Andrew2020-86][^cite_note-Bunge2022-13] possibly as a result of waning immunity following the cessation of routine smallpox vaccination.[^cite_note-Mc2014-5]

### Future threat

The natural reservoir of *Orthopoxvirus monkeypox* has not been conclusively determined. Small rodents are considered the most likely candidate.[^cite_note-\:7-12] Without a major vaccination campaign, mpox outbreaks in humans will continue indefinitely in the endemic areas, with an ongoing risk that disease outbreaks will spread to non-endemic areas. Other evidence – that the virus is evolving to be more transmissible among humans, that it can infect a wide range of host species, and that human-to-animal transmission can occur – led to concerns that mpox may either become established in new natural reservoirs outside of Africa, or cause future global epidemics.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

Following the 2022–2023 outbreak, mpox (clade IIb) remains present in the human population outside Africa at very low levels.[^cite_note-91] In November 2023, the WHO reported increasing numbers of cases of mpox (clade I) in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, with 12,569 cases year-to-date and 651 fatalities; there was also the first evidence of sexual transmission of clade I.[^cite_note-92]

There has been a rise in monkeypox virus clade I infections in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) since November 2023, with more cases now reported in other African countries that previously had no mpox cases. Two imported cases were also found in Sweden and Thailand. As of 23 August 2024, over 20,000 mpox cases have been reported in 13 African Union Member States, with 3,311 confirmed cases and 582 deaths. Most cases are found in the DRC, where subclade Ia and Ib are prevalent.[^cite_note-v952-93]

Clade Ib was linked to a reported mpox case in Sweden on 15 August 2024, which was related to traveling to an African country where the virus is found. Despite the low incidence, cases associated with clade II have been reported in EU/EEA countries since. In 2024, the WHO added the monkeypox virus to its list of "priority pathogens" that could cause a pandemic.[^cite_note-94]

### Nigeria

Main article: [Mpox in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpox_in_Nigeria)

Two cases of human mpox infections were identified in Nigeria in 1971. In September 2017, *Orthopoxvirus monkeypox* was reported in Nigeria. The subsequent outbreak was, at that time, the largest ever outbreak of clade II of the virus, with 118 confirmed cases. Unlike previous outbreaks of this clade, infection was predominantly among young male adults and human-to-human transmission appears to have readily occurred. Seven deaths (5 male, 2 female, case fatality rate of 6%) were reported, including a baby and four people who had [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS). Additionally, a pregnant woman in her second trimester had a spontaneous miscarriage attributed to *Orthopoxvirus monkeypox* infection.[^cite_note-104]

In May 2022, the Nigerian government released a report stating that between 2017 and 2022, 558 cases were confirmed across 32 states and the Federal Capital Territory. There were 8 deaths reported, making for a 1.4% [Case Fatality Ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_fatality_rate). In 2022, NCDC implemented a National Technical Working Group for reporting and monitoring infections, strengthening response capacity.[^cite_note-105]

### Singapore

In May 2019, a 38-year-old man who traveled from Nigeria was hospitalized in an isolation ward at the [National Centre for Infectious Diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Centre_for_Infectious_Diseases) in Singapore, after being confirmed as the country's first case of mpox. As a result, 22 people were quarantined.[^cite_note-CIDRAPMay2019-106] The case may have been linked to a simultaneous outbreak in Nigeria.[^cite_note-WHO_May_2019-107]

### Sudan

During 2022, an outbreak of clade I mpox was reported in refugee camps in Sudan.[^cite_note-WHOfact2023-1] The first case in the country was recorded in August, and in September, six additional cases were discovered in [Khartoum](17320.md).[^cite_note-Dafallah2023-108] In October, more than 100 cases were reported among Ethiopian refugee camps.[^cite_note-Dafallah2023-108]

### United Kingdom

In September 2018, the United Kingdom's first case of mpox was recorded. The person, a Nigerian national, is believed to have contracted mpox in [Nigeria](21383.md) before travelling to the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-109] A second case was confirmed in the town of [Blackpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackpool), with a further case that of a medical worker who cared for the infected person from Blackpool.[^cite_note-110]

In December 2019, mpox was diagnosed in a person in South West England who had traveled to the UK from Nigeria.[^cite_note-PHE4Dec2019-111]

In May 2021, two cases of mpox from a single household were identified by [Public Health Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Health_Wales) in the UK. The [index case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_case) had traveled from Nigeria. Covid guidance to isolate after travel helped detection of the outbreak and to prevent further transmission.[^cite_note-112]

### United States

Main article: [2003 Midwest mpox outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Midwest_mpox_outbreak)

In May 2003, a young child became ill with fever and rash after being bitten by a prairie dog purchased at a local swap meet near [Milwaukee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee), [Wisconsin](33127.md).[^cite_note-113] In total, 71 cases of mpox were reported through 20 June 2003. All cases were traced to Gambian pouched rats imported from [Accra](56824.md), [Ghana](12067.md), in April 2003 by a Texas exotic animal distributor. No deaths resulted.[^cite_note-medscape-114] [Electron microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscope) and serologic studies were used to confirm that the disease was human mpox.[^cite_note-115] Everyone affected reported direct or close contact with prairie dogs, later found to be infected with the *Orthopoxvirus monkeypox*.[^cite_note-pmid12855947-116]

In July 2021, in the US, an American returning from a trip in Nigeria was diagnosed with mpox. Subsequent testing identified the virus as belonging to clade II. The patient was hospitalized and treated with tecovirimat and was discharged after 32 days.[^cite_note-117]

The first case of clade I mpox in the United States was identified in November 2024; the California Department of Public Health reported that an unidentified person outside San Francisco had tested positive following travel to and from [East Africa](260987.md).[^cite_note-118] Clade II mpox continues to circulate at low levels;[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] from 1 January to 1 September 2025, the weekly average was between 1 and 9 cases reported per day.[^cite_note-121]

### 2022–2023 global outbreak

Main article: [2022–2023 mpox outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%932023_mpox_outbreak)

An outbreak of mpox caused by clade IIb of the virus was first identified in May 2022.[^cite_note-WHO13-122] The first case was detected in London, United Kingdom, on 6 May, in a patient with a recent travel history from [Nigeria](21383.md), where the disease is [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_\(epidemiology\)).[^cite_note-gov.uk-123][^cite_note-WHO12-124] Subsequent cases were reported in an increasing number of countries and regions.[^cite_note-131] In July 2022, the WHO declared the outbreak a public health emergency of international concern. This status was terminated in May 2023 due to steady progress in controlling the spread of the disease, attributed to a combination of vaccination and public health information.[^cite_note-kimball511-132] As of August 2024, clade IIb mpox cases outside of endemic regions in Africa continued to be reported at a low level.[^cite_note-\:6-101]

### 2023–2026 mpox outbreak

Main article: [2023–2026 mpox epidemic](77612845.md)

During 2023, a clade I outbreak of mpox disease in the Democratic Republic of the Congo resulted in 14,626 suspected cases being reported, with 654 associated deaths, making for a case-fatality rate of 4.5%. The outbreak continued into 2024, with 3,576 suspected mpox cases and 265 deaths reported in the Democratic Republic of the Congo through the first nine weeks of the year, making for an estimated CFR of 7.4%.[^cite_note-drcmpox-133]

Transmission of the virus in the outbreak appears to be primarily through sexual and close familial contact, with cases occurring in areas without a history of mpox, such as [South Kivu](446959.md) and [Kinshasa](45773.md). An estimated 64% of the cases and 85% of fatalities have occurred in children. The outbreak consists of two separate sub-variants of clade I, with one of the sub-variants having a novel mutation, making detection with standard assays unreliable.[^cite_note-drcmpox-133][^cite_note-134]

The outbreak spread to the neighbouring country of the [Republic of the Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Congo), with 43 cases reported in March 2024.[^cite_note-drcmpox-133] By August 2024, the outbreak spread further into central and southern Africa with cases of clade I and clade II strains reported in [Burundi](21490998.md), [Rwanda](25645.md), [Uganda](31816.md), [Kenya](188171.md), [Côte d'Ivoire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4te_d'Ivoire), and [South Africa](17416221.md).[^cite_note-cohen-102][^cite_note-\:1-18]

The WHO declared a global health emergency in August 2024.[^cite_note-MSN_20240814-19][^cite_note-WHO_PR_20240814-14][^cite_note-135] [Sweden](5058739.md) became the first non-African country to report a case of clade I mpox.[^cite_note-\:5-136] A case of mpox was confirmed in Pakistan.[^cite_note-137]

### 2025–2026

Beginning in October 2025, local circulation of mpox clade Ib was reported in Europe (Spain, Portugal, Netherlands, Italy) by the ECDC.[^cite_note-138] Subsequently, this resulted in several outbreaks, among others in the Netherlands[^cite_note-139] and Germany.[^cite_note-140]

In February 2026, the United Nations reported that the World Health Organization (WHO) had identified a new recombinant strain of mpox in both the United Kingdom and India, combining genetic material from two known monkeypox virus clades (Ib and IIb), though no severe illness or onward transmission was detected in the confirmed cases and the global public health risk assessment stayed the same.[^cite_note-141]
