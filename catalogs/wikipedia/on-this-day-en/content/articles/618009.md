A **stabbing** is [penetration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penetrating_trauma) or rough contact with a sharp or pointed object at close range. *Stab* connotes purposeful action, as by an [assassin](2963.md) or [murderer](20171.md), but it is also possible to accidentally stab oneself or others. Stabbing differs from slashing or [cutting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting) in that the motion of the object used in a stabbing generally moves perpendicular to and directly into the victim's body, rather than being drawn across it.

[Stab wounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stab_wound) is the specific form of penetrating trauma to the skin that results from stabbing. Stabbings have been common among [gangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangs) and in [prisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisons) because knives are cheap, easy to acquire (or manufacture), easily concealable and relatively effective. Stabbing has also been used [in terrorism](53684322.md).

## Epidemiology

In 2013, about 8 million stabbings occurred worldwide.[^cite_note-1]

In the US in 2020, 9% of the 22,429 [homicides](123451.md) involved a sharp instrument; of these a larger proportion of females used a sharp instrument (13%) versus males (8.2%).[^cite_note-mmwr-2]

## History



After being attacked and stabbed in September 1898, [Austro-Hungarian Empress Elisabeth of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Elisabeth_of_Austria) boarded a ship, unaware of the severity of her condition as consequence of an [acute stress reaction](1585862.md). Bleeding to death from a puncture wound to the heart, Elisabeth's last words were, "What happened to me?"



Murder weapon, evidence and photos from a murder case in [Trondheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trondheim), Norway 1928. The broken blade of the knife was stuck in the back of the stabbed victim. Exhibits in the Norwegian National Museum of Criminal Justice.

Stabbings have been common throughout human history and were the means used to [assassinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassinate) a number of distinguished historical figures, such as Second [Caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph) [Umar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umar) and [Roman dictator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_dictator) [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar) and [emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_emperor) [Caligula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caligula).

In Japan, the historical practice of stabbing oneself deliberately in [ritual suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritual_suicide) is known as *[seppuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seppuku)* (more colloquially *hara-kiri*, literally "belly-cutting" since it involves cutting open the abdomen). The ritual is highly codified, and the person committing suicide is assisted by a "second" who is entrusted to decapitate him cleanly (and thus expedite death and prevent an undignified spectacle) once he has made the abdominal wound.

A notable stabbing was the [13 November 1893 stabbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13_November_1893_stabbing) by [anarchist](12.md) [Léon Léauthier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9on_L%C3%A9authier).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFerragu201921-31-3] This attack is considered a foundational event of [modern terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_terrorism) because Léauthier targeted a complete stranger; he was thus acting within the emerging logic of indiscriminate and mass terrorism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFerragu201921-31-3]

## Mechanism

The human [skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin) has a somewhat elastic property as a self-defense; when the human body is stabbed by a thin object such as a small [kitchen knife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitchen_knife), the skin often closes tightly around the object and closes again if the object is removed, which can trap some blood within the body. It has thus been speculated that the [fuller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuller_\(weapon\)), an elongated concave depression in a metal blade, functions to let blood out of the body in order to cause more damage. This misconception has led to fullers becoming widely known as "blood grooves". The fuller is actually a structural reinforcement of the blade similar in design to a metal I-beam used in construction. However, [internal bleeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_bleeding) is just as dangerous as external bleeding; if enough blood vessels are severed to cause serious injury, the skin's elasticity will do nothing to prevent blood from exiting the circulatory system and accumulating uselessly in other parts of the body.

Death from stabbing is caused by [shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_\(circulatory\)), severe [blood loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_loss), infection, or loss of function of an essential organ such as the [heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart) and/or [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung).

## Medical treatment

Further information: [Stab wound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stab_wound)

Although previously a victim of abdominal stabbing would be subject to exploratory surgery [laparotomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laparotomy), it is now considered safe not to operate if the patient is stable. In that case, they should be observed for signs of decompensation indicating a serious injury. If the patient initially presents stabbing injuries and is unstable, then laparotomy should be initiated to discover and rectify any internal injury.

## Autopsy examination

When someone who has sustained a stab wound dies, the body is autopsied and the wound is inspected by a [forensic pathologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forensic_pathologist). Such examination can yield valuable information about the weapon used to produce the injury. From the external appearance and internal findings, the pathologist will usually be able to offer opinion about the dimensions of the weapon including the width and minimum possible length of the blade. It is possible to determine whether the weapon was single edged or double edged.[^cite_note-Biju-4]

Sometimes factors like the taper of the blade and movement of knife in the wound can also be determined. [Bruises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruise) or [abrasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abrasion_\(medical\)) may give information about the [guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knife#Parts).[^cite_note-Biju-4]
