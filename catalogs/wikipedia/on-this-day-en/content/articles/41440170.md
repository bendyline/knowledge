**Bruno Lafont** (born 8 June 1956) is a French businessman. He served as the chief executive officer of [Lafarge](41902084.md) from 2006 to 2015, when it merged with [Holcim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holcim) to become [LafargeHolcim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LafargeHolcim). He served as the co-chairman of LafargeHolcim from 2015 to April 2017.

## Early life

Lafont was born in 1956.[^cite_note-arcelormittalboard-1] He graduated from [HEC Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HEC_Paris) in 1977 as well as the [École nationale d'administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_nationale_d'administration) in [Paris](22989.md).[^cite_note-arcelormittalboard-1][^cite_note-lefigbrunolafontlhommequi-2]

## Career

Lafont joined [Lafarge](41902084.md) as an auditor in the finance department in 1983,[^cite_note-arcelormittalboard-1][^cite_note-lefigbrunolafontlhommequi-2] He subsequently worked in Germany and Turkey.[^cite_note-lefigbrunolafontlhommequi-2] He was appointed as its CEO on 1 January 2006.[^cite_note-lafargebrunolafontappointedchief-3] Under his tenure, he oversaw the international expansion of Lafarge to 70 countries, including the acquisition of minority shareholders in Lafarge North America.[^cite_note-lefigbrunolafontlhommequi-2] Additionally, he cut costs by 60% within the first year, notably by divesting from its roof-manufacturing subsidiary.[^cite_note-lefigbrunolafontlhommequi-2] By December 2008, he acquired Orascom Cement, a subsidiary of [Orascom Construction Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orascom_Construction_Industries), for €8.8 billion, and he brought billionaires [Albert Frère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Fr%C3%A8re) and [Nassef Sawiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassef_Sawiris) to Lafarge's board.[^cite_note-lesechoslafargevaracheter-4] He stepped down as CEO in 2015, shortly after its merger with [Holcim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holcim).[^cite_note-lefigbrunolafontquittelafarge-5] He was awarded a €2.5 million [bonus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance-related_pay) for it.[^cite_note-fttheriffbrunolafontsearlybonus-6] He served as the co-chairman of LafargeHolcim until April 2017.[^cite_note-lafonttostepdownrowland-7][^cite_note-lefiglepatrondelafargeholcim-8]

Lafont serves on the boards of directors of [Électricité de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lectricit%C3%A9_de_France) and [ArcelorMittal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArcelorMittal).[^cite_note-arcelormittalboard-1][^cite_note-edfboard-9] He is an advisor to the mayor of [Chongqing](171752.md) in China.[^cite_note-arcelormittalboard-1] He is also the chairman of the [Fondation nationale pour l'enseignement de la gestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fondation_nationale_pour_l'enseignement_de_la_gestion).[^cite_note-fondationconseildaministration-10]



The Union Minister for Commerce & Industry, [Anand Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anand_Sharma), with the Chairman & CEO, Lafarge Group, Mr. Bruno Lafont, on the sidelines of the 6th India-France CEOs Forum, in [Paris](22989.md) on July 08, 2013.

In April 2026, he was sentenced to six years in prison by a French court for paying millions in 'protection money' to Islamic State in Syria.[^cite_note-11]

## Personal life

Lafont is married to Marie-Constance de Maistre, a violinist and a descendant of [Joseph de Maistre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_de_Maistre).[^cite_note-lesechosbrunolafontapril8-12]
