The **Portugal national football team** ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Seleção Portuguesa de Futebol*)  has represented [Portugal](23033.md) in men's international [football](10568.md) competition since 1921. The national team is controlled by the [Portuguese Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Football_Federation) (FPF), the governing body for [football in Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Portugal). Portugal's home stadium is the [Estádio Nacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Nacional) in [Oeiras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oeiras,_Portugal), located next to its primary [training ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_ground_\(association_football\)) and the FPF headquarters (*[Cidade do Futebol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cidade_do_Futebol)*), but the team usually plays its home matches in more [modern stadiums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_football_stadiums_in_Portugal) throughout the country, such as the [Estádio da Luz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_da_Luz), [Estádio José Alvalade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Jos%C3%A9_Alvalade) and the [Estádio do Dragão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_do_Drag%C3%A3o). The head coach is [Jorge Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Jesus), and the captain is [Cristiano Ronaldo](623737.md), who holds the team records for most caps and most goals.

Portugal's first participation in a major tournament finals was at the [1966 FIFA World Cup](61629.md), which saw a team featuring [Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballon_d'Or) winner [Eusébio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eus%C3%A9bio) finish in third place. Since the 2000s, Portugal started an uninterrupted streak of qualification for every European Championship and World Cup finals, including a runners-up finish at [Euro 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004) on home soil. They experienced much of their success in the late 2010s to mid 2020s, during the captaincy of Ballon d'Or winner Cristiano Ronaldo, where they won their first-ever major trophy [Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016) and the [UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League) in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_UEFA_Nations_League), hosting the [first finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_UEFA_Nations_League_Finals), and a record second time in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Nations_League_final).

Portugal is colloquially referred to as the *Seleção das [Quinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Portugal)* (a synecdoche based on the [flag of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Portugal)) and has several notable rivalries with other national teams. These include [Spain](609825.md), due to their historical rivalry as Iberian neighbours;[^cite_note-Fanatics-3] as well as [France](250197.md) due to several high-stake meetings against them at the Euro and World Cup.[^cite_note-rivalidade-4]

## History

Main article: [History of the Portugal national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Portugal_national_football_team)

### Early World Cup attempts

Portugal were not invited to the [1930 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), which only featured a finals stage and no [qualification round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification). The team took part in the [1934 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), but failed to eliminate their Spanish opponents, aggregating two defeats in the two-legged round, with a 9–0 loss in [Madrid](41188263.md) and 2–1 loss in [Lisbon](18091.md) for an aggregate score of 11–1.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

In the [1938 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), the *Seleção* played one game against Switzerland held in neutral ground in [Milan](36511.md). They lost 2–1 and failed to qualify for the finals.[^cite_note-7] The [Second World War](32927.md) delayed the World Cup until [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup) and subsequently, the national team rarely played.[^cite_note-8] A 10–0 home friendly loss against [England](9904.md), two years after the war, still stands as their biggest ever defeat.[^cite_note-9]

### 1950s and early 1960s

Similar to 1934, Portugal [were to play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification#Group_6) a two-legged round against [Spain](609825.md). After a 5–1 defeat in Madrid, they managed to draw the second game 2–2. With a 7–3 aggregate score, they did not qualify on the pitch, however they would later be invited to replace [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team), which had withdrawn from participating. Portugal refused to participate.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

In [1954 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), the team would play [Austria](695226.md); the Austrians won the first game with a 9–1 result.[^cite_note-12] The best the Portuguese could do was hold the Austrians to a goalless draw in Lisbon, resulting in a 9–1 aggregate defeat.[^cite_note-13] [Four years later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_8), Portugal won a qualifying match for the first time, a 3–0 home victory over [Italy](362466.md). Nevertheless, they finished last in a group that also featured [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_national_football_team); only the first-placed team, Northern Ireland, would qualify.[^cite_note-14]

1960 was the year that UEFA created the [European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship). The [first edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_European_Nations'_Cup) was a knock-out tournament with the last four teams participating in the finals stage that only featured one leg while the earlier stages had two legs. In the first round, the *Seleção das Quinas* won 2–0 at [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany_national_football_team) and then 3–2 in [Porto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto), advancing with a 5–2 two-legged win.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Portugal faced Yugoslavia in the quarter-finals, losing 6–3 on aggregate.[^cite_note-17]

Portugal faced England and [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_national_football_team) in [1962 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_6) and ended up second in the group, behind England, who would be the only team in Group 6 to qualify.[^cite_note-18] In the [1964 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_European_Nations'_Cup), Portugal played against [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team) in the [qualifying rounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_European_Nations'_Cup_qualifying). The Portuguese lost in [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia) and won in Lisbon. With the round tied 4–4, a replay was needed in a neutral country.[^cite_note-auto1-19] In the [Stadio Olimpico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Olimpico) in Rome, Portugal lost 1–0 thanks to a late strike from [Georgi Asparuhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgi_Asparuhov).[^cite_note-auto1-19]

### Third place at the 1966 World Cup

Portugal were drawn with [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_football_team) and Turkey for [1966 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_4).[^cite_note-1966WC-20] They topped the group with only one draw and one defeat in six games and finally qualified for a FIFA World Cup, with a 1–0 away win against Czechoslovakia and Turkey and a 5–1 home win against the Turks being notable results.[^cite_note-1966WC-20]

At the World Cup, the team started out with three wins in the group stage after they beat [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) 3–1,[^cite_note-21] Bulgaria 3–0, and two-time defending champions [Brazil](149286.md) 3–1.[^cite_note-22] Secondly, they beat quarter-finalists [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team) 5–3, with [Eusébio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eus%C3%A9bio) getting four markers to overturn a 3–0 deficit.[^cite_note-23] Later, they reached the semi-finals where they were beaten by hosts England 2–1; in this game, Portugal would have played in [Liverpool](18081.md), but as England were the hosts, FIFA decided that the game would be played in London.[^cite_note-24] Portugal then defeated the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team) 2–1 in the third place match for their best World Cup finish to date.[^cite_note-25] Eusébio was the top scorer of the World Cup with nine goals. Portugal would not qualify for another World Cup for 20 years.

### 1980s

Portugal won their [Euro 1984 qualifying group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1984_qualifying#Group_2) that contained [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland_national_football_team), Poland and the Soviet Union with a win over the latter,[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] allowing them to qualify and be placed in Group B alongside Spain, [West Germany](250204.md) and Romania in the finals.[^cite_note-1984EC-28] In the first two matches, they drew 0–0 and 1–1 against West Germany and Spain, respectively.[^cite_note-1984EC-28] A 1–0 win over Romania resulted in a second-place finish in group play. Portugal were paired against hosts [France](250197.md) in the semi-finals.[^cite_note-1984EC-28] After a draw in regular time, Portugal initially led 2–1 in extra time, but the hosts scored in the 114th and 119th minutes to eliminate the Portuguese 3–2 and go through to the final.[^cite_note-1984EC-28]

[For 1986 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)#Group_2), the *Seleção* played against Czechoslovakia, [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_national_football_team), Sweden and West Germany for the two spots that would guarantee them a ticket to Mexico.[^cite_note-29] Needing a win in the last game against West Germany in [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), Portugal won the game to become the first team to beat West Germany at their home ground in an official match. The team exited early in the group stages after a win and two losses.[^cite_note-30] They started with a 1–0 win against England,[^cite_note-31] but later were beaten by Poland and [Morocco](1024865.md) 1–0 and 3–1 respectively.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] Their staying in Mexico was marked by the [Saltillo Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltillo_Affair), where players refused to train in order to win more prizes from the [Portuguese Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Football_Federation). Mexico marked their last World Cup appearance until 2002.

### 1995–2006: The golden generation

At [UEFA Euro 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996), Portugal finished first in Group D, and in the [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996_knockout_stage#Quarter-finals), they lost 1–0 to the [Czech Republic](729739.md).



Portugal lost the [Euro 2004 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004_final) 0–1 to Greece with a header from [Angelos Charisteas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelos_Charisteas) (pictured)

Portugal failed to [qualify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)#Group_9) for the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup). In [Euro 2000 qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000_qualifying#Group_7), Portugal finished second in their group, one point short of first-placed Romania. However, after finishing as the top runner-up nation in qualifying, Portugal nonetheless secured a spot in the finals. They then defeated England 3–2, Romania 1–0 and Germany 3–0 to finish first in [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000_Group_A), then defeated Turkey in the quarter-finals. In the semi-finals against France, Portugal were eliminated in extra time when [Zinedine Zidane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinedine_Zidane) converted a [penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_kick_\(association_football\)). Referee [Günter Benkö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCnter_Benk%C3%B6) awarded the spot kick for a handball after [Abel Xavier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Xavier) blocked a shot. Xavier, [Nuno Gomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuno_Gomes) and [Paulo Bento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Bento) were all given lengthy suspensions for subsequently shoving the referee.[^cite_note-34] The final eventually finished 2–1.

During [2002 FIFA World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_2), Portugal won the group.[^cite_note-FIFA2WC-35] Several problems and poor judgement decisions occurred during the preparation and tournament itself and were widely reported in the Portuguese press,[^cite_note-FIFA2WC-35] including questionable managing choices and some amateurism, as well as lack of agreement on prizes.[^cite_note-FIFA2WC-35] Portugal entered the tournament as favourites to win Group D.[^cite_note-FIFA2WC-35] However, they were upset 3–2 by the [United States](582488.md).[^cite_note-FIFA2WC-35] They then rebounded with a 4–0 victory over Poland.[^cite_note-FIFA2WC-35] Needing a draw to advance, they lost the last group game to hosts [South Korea](1018627.md).[^cite_note-36] Portugal underachieved and ended third in its group stage, subsequently eliminated. Manager [António Oliveira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_Oliveira_\(footballer,_born_1952\)) was fired after the World Cup.



Ronaldo, pictured playing against [Germany](250204.md) at [Euro 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012), assumed the captaincy in the wake of Euro 2008

The next major competition, the [UEFA Euro 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004), was held in Portugal. For preparation, the Football Federation made a contract with [Luiz Felipe Scolari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luiz_Felipe_Scolari) to manage the team until the tournament ended.[^cite_note-37] The host nation lost the first game against Greece 1–2.[^cite_note-38] They achieved their first win against [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team) 2–0 and also beat Spain 1–0.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] They went on to play against England, in a 2–2 draw that went into penalties, with Portugal winning.[^cite_note-41] Portugal beat the Netherlands 2–1 in the semi-finals,[^cite_note-42] and suffered a second defeat from Greece, 1–0, in the final.[^cite_note-Szreter-43]

After the tournament ended, many players belonging to the *Geração de Ouro* (Golden Generation), abandoned their international footballing careers, with only [Luís Figo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_Figo) remaining in the team, despite a temporary retirement.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] The silver lining for Portugal was the emergence of Cristiano Ronaldo, who was selected in the UEFA Euro All-Star team.[^cite_note-46] While Portugal were playing in the competition, Scolari agreed in a new two-year deal with the Federation.

Portugal finished first in the [qualifying round for the 2006 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_3),[^cite_note-Ronaldo_profile-47] and topped Group D in the World Cup, with victories over [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_national_football_team) (1–0), [Iran](272865.md) (2–0) and [Mexico](808402.md) (2–1).[^cite_note-Por._Vs_Iran-48][^cite_note-49] Portugal defeated the Netherlands 1–0 in the Round of 16 in an infamous matched that has come to be known as the [Battle of Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nuremberg_\(2006_FIFA_World_Cup\)), marked by violent fouls and many players [cautioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_card) or [sent off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ejection_\(sports\)) by referee [Valentin Ivanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentin_Ivanov_\(footballer,_born_1961\)).[^cite_note-50] Portugal drew 0–0 after extra time with England in the quarter-final, but won 3–1 on [penalties](511302.md) to reach their first World Cup semi-final since 1966.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] Portugal lost the semi-final 1–0 against France,[^cite_note-53] and were then defeated 3–1 by the tournament hosts, Germany, in the third-place play-off match.[^cite_note-54]

### 2006–2014: Post-golden generation and mixed results

For [Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008) Portugal finished second in [qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008_qualifying_Group_A) behind Poland,[^cite_note-55] and won their first two group games against Turkey and the Czech Republic, although a loss to co-hosts Switzerland set up a quarter-final matchup with Germany which the team lost 3–2.[^cite_note-56] After the tournament, Scolari left to take over at [Chelsea](7473.md).[^cite_note-57] Afterwards, [Carlos Queiroz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Queiroz) was appointed as the head coach of the Portugal national team.[^cite_note-CQ_Guardian-58][^cite_note-CQ_Reuters_UK-59][^cite_note-CQ_BBC_Sport-60][^cite_note-CQ_AP-61]

Portugal [came second in the qualifying stages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_1) for the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md) under Queiroz, then beat [Bosnia and Herzegovina](1008974.md) in a [play-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round), thereby reaching every tournament in the decade.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] A 19-match undefeated streak, in which the team conceded only three goals, ended with a loss to eventual champions Spain in the round of 16, 1–0.[^cite_note-65] Queiroz was later criticised for setting up his team in an overly cautious way.[^cite_note-66] After the World Cup, squad regulars [Simão Sabrosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%A3o_Sabrosa), [Paulo Ferreira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Ferreira), [Miguel Monteiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Monteiro) and [Tiago Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiago_Mendes) all retired from international football.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] Queiroz was banned from coaching the national team for one month after he tried to block a doping test to the team while preparing for the World Cup, as well as directing insulting words to the testers.[^cite_note-70] In consequence, he received a further six-month suspension. Several media outbursts from Queiroz[^cite_note-71] against the heads of the Portuguese Football Federation followed, which partly prompted his dismissal. Paulo Bento was appointed as his replacement at head coach.[^cite_note-72]

Bento's team qualified for Euro 2012; they were drawn with Germany, Denmark, and the Netherlands in a widely speculated "[group of death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_death)".[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] They lost their first game 0–1 to Germany, then beat Denmark 3–2.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] The final group stage match was against the Netherlands. After [Van der Vaart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_van_der_Vaart) had given the Dutch a 1–0 lead, Ronaldo netted twice to ensure a 2–1 victory.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] Portugal finished second in the group and qualified for the knockout phase. Portugal defeated the Czech Republic 1–0 in the quarter-finals with a header from Ronaldo.[^cite_note-82] The semi-final match was against Spain, who defeated Portugal 4–2 on penalties after a goalless draw.[^cite_note-83]

In [2014 FIFA World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)), Portugal won 4–2 on aggregate in a play-off against Sweden with all four goals being scored by Ronaldo, and were drawn into [Group G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_G) with the United States, Germany and [Ghana](1029620.md). Their first match against the Germans was their worst-ever defeat in a World Cup, a 4–0 loss.[^cite_note-84] They went on to draw 2–2 against the United States and won 2–1 against Ghana.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-BBC14-86] However, the team were eliminated due to inferior goal difference to the Americans.[^cite_note-87]

Portugal's starting lineup for the [UEFA Euro 2016 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_final)

In [UEFA Euro 2016 qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_qualifying), Bento was dismissed following a defeat to Albania and was replaced by [Fernando Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Santos_\(footballer,_born_1954\)) in September 2014.[^cite_note-88] After qualifying for the finals, Portugal finished third in Group F but advanced to the knockout stages as the third-best third place team following three straight draws. Portugal beat Croatia 1–0 after extra time in the round of 16[^cite_note-89] and then defeated Poland 5–3 on penalties to reach the semi-finals,[^cite_note-90] where they defeated [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_football_team) 2–0.[^cite_note-91] In the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_final) against the hosts, France, Ronaldo went off injured. In extra time, substitute [Eder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eder_\(footballer,_born_1987\)) scored the winning goal for Portugal in the 109th minute with a strike from 25 yards past [Hugo Lloris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Lloris).[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

Following their [Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016) victory, Portugal participated in the [2017 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup). They finished top of their group,[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] but lost to [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) on penalties after a goalless draw in the [semi-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup#Semi-finals),[^cite_note-96] but rebounded in the [third place game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_FIFA_Confederations_Cup#Third_place_play-off), defeating Mexico 2–1 after extra time.[^cite_note-fifa2017-97]



Portugal lining up before a match at the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md)

At the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md), Portugal opened their campaign with a 3–3 draw with Spain, with Cristiano Ronaldo scoring a [hat-trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_World_Cup_hat-tricks).[^cite_note-cbssports.com-98] After a 1–0 victory against Morocco,[^cite_note-99] Portugal drew 1–1 with Iran to progress to the knockout round as group runners-up.[^cite_note-100] Portugal were eliminated following a 2–1 defeat to [Uruguay](679782.md) in the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage).[^cite_note-101]

Following the World Cup, Portugal won the inaugural [UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Nations_League) beating the Netherlands at the [Estádio do Dragão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_do_Drag%C3%A3o) in Porto, with the only goal scored by [Gonçalo Guedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gon%C3%A7alo_Guedes) in the 60th minute.[^cite_note-Sports_Mole-102][^cite_note-103]

At [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020), Portugal were drawn into a group containing [France](250197.md), [Germany](250204.md) and [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) which was widely speculated as being the "group of death". Portugal advanced to the [next round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_knockout_stage) by defeating Hungary, drawing with France and losing to Germany. There, they faced [Belgium](679716.md) but lost 1–0.

For the [2022 FIFA World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_A), Portugal were required to qualify for the finals via the [play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round) after finishing second in their group.[^cite_note-104] Nevertheless, Portugal managed to beat [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team)[^cite_note-105] and [North Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Macedonia_national_football_team) to qualify for the final tournament.[^cite_note-106] At the 2022 World Cup, Portugal defeated [Ghana](1029620.md) 3–2 in their first group game[^cite_note-107] and then beat Uruguay 2–0.[^cite_note-108] to qualify for the knockout stages.[^cite_note-109] The Portuguese would demolish [Switzerland](681862.md) 6–1 in the next round, their highest tally in a World Cup knockout game since the [1966 World Cup](61629.md), with [Gonçalo Ramos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gon%C3%A7alo_Ramos) scoring a hat-trick.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111] However, they were eliminated by tournament in the quarter-finals by [Morocco](1024865.md), 1–0.[^cite_note-112] Following a disappointing World Cup campaign, Fernando Santos was dismissed on 15 December.[^cite_note-113]

### 2023–present: Further international titles

On 9 January 2023, [Roberto Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Mart%C3%ADnez) was announced as the new head coach of Portugal.[^cite_note-114] On 11 September, Martinez led Portugal to their biggest victory in international history by defeating [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_national_football_team) 9–0 at home.[^cite_note-115] Martinez's Portuguese side were one of the first European sides to advance from qualifying into the [UEFA Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) after their 3–2 win over [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_national_football_team), making it their quickest qualification to a major tournament in their history.[^cite_note-116] Following a 2–0 home over Iceland, Martinez led Portugal to a perfect qualification campaign, overtaking the record for most scored and least scored against in a qualifying campaign in the country's history, with a record of 36 goals scored and only two goals conceded, keeping nine clean sheets in the process. Portugal also became one of the few European national teams to win all of their matches for the qualifiers of the [UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship), with ten wins from ten matches, the first time in Portugal's international history.[^cite_note-117]

In their European Championship group, Portugal were paired with [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_national_football_team), [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) and [Czechia](729739.md).[^cite_note-118] With wins against Czechia and Turkey and a loss to Georgia, Portugal qualified for the Round of 16 as group winners, where they played against [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia_national_football_team). The game finished 0–0 and was decided on penalties, with Portuguese goalkeeper [Diogo Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogo_Costa) saving each of Slovenia's penalties, while Portugal scored all of theirs.[^cite_note-119] Facing [France](250197.md) in the Quarter finals, the game again finished 0–0 and was decided on penalties; a miss by [João Felix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_F%C3%A9lix) meant France won 5–3, and Portugal were eliminated.[^cite_note-120]

On 9 June 2025, Roberto Martinez guided Portugal to their second [UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League) title after defeating the reigning European champions Spain 5–3 on penalties.[^cite_note-121]

In the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md), Portugal were drawn into [Group K](81660204.md) with [Colombia](680175.md), [DR Congo](1030243.md), and [Uzbekistan](1032413.md). On June 17, they tied their opener against DR Congo 1–1 despite completing 724 passes, their most in a World Cup match.[^cite_note-122] After a disappointing group stage where they finished second place in their group, Martinez let Portugal to an underwhelming [round of 16](81667005.md#Portugal_vs_Spain) finish, after a 1–0 defeat to Spain. Following the match, he announced his resignation with immediate effect.[^cite_note-123] The team was heavily criticized for their performance, including former Euro 2016 winner Ricardo Quaresma, who harshly criticized Portugal's exit, questioning the narrative of Portugal's the best generation in national history, their passive playing style, and expressed deep frustration with the team's overall lack of ambition.[^cite_note-124]

On 10 July, [Jorge Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Jesus) was appointed as head coach of the Portugal national team.[^cite_note-125] He was chosen to become the new manager to inject a relentless, club-style urgency and intense tactical discipline into a squad that underperformed relative to its immense talent pool at the World Cup.[^cite_note-126]

### Kits

Wikimedia Commons logo

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [Portugal national football team kits](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Portugal%20national%20football%20team%20kits).

Portugal's traditional [home kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kit_\(association_football\)) is mainly red with a green trim, reflecting the colours of the nation's [flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Portugal). Over the years, the particular shade of red has alternated between a darker [burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgundy_\(color\)) and a lighter [scarlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlet_\(color\)), the latter matching the flag.[^cite_note-127] Both green and red shorts have been used to complete the strip.

The team's away kits, on the other hand, have varied more considerably. White has typically been preferred as a dominant colour, either with blue shorts, or red and green highlights. In recent times, all-black has been utilised, as has a turquoise-teal colour, the latter of which was prominently featured during the title winning [Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016) campaign.
