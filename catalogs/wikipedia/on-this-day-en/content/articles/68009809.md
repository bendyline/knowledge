**Belgium–Rwanda relations** refer to the international and diplomatic relations between [Belgium](3343.md) and [Rwanda](25645.md).&#x20;

Belgian relations with Rwanda started under the League of Nations mandate when the modern-day countries of Rwanda and Burundi were governed as Ruanda-Urundi.&#x20;

As the colonial power, Rwanda's relationship with Belgium has been significant throughout the country's history, even after independence.

## Historical relations

### Initial contact

[Ruanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Rwanda) and [Burundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Burundi) were independent kingdoms in the [Great Lakes region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Great_Lakes) before the [Scramble for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa). In 1894, they were annexed by the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) and eventually became two districts of [German East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_East_Africa).[^cite_note-\:0-1]

As part of the Allied [East African Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Campaign_\(World_War_I\)), Ruanda and Burundi were invaded by a Belgian force in 1916. The German forces in the region were small and hugely outnumbered. Ruanda was occupied from April–May and Burundi in June 1916.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

### Belgian rule

Main article: [Ruanda-Urundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruanda-Urundi)

The [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles) after World War I divided the [German colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_colonial_empire) among the Allied nations. German East Africa was partitioned, and Belgium was allocated to Ruanda-Urundi, even though this represented only a fraction of the territories already occupied by Belgian forces in East Africa.&#x20;

Later, the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) officially awarded Ruanda-Urundi to Belgium as a [B-Class Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate) on 20 July 1922. The mandatory regime was also controversial in Belgium and was not approved by Belgium's parliament until 1924.[^cite_note-2]

Unlike [colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony) which belonged to its colonial powers, a mandate was theoretically subject to international oversight through the League's [Permanent Mandates Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Mandates_Commission) (PMC) in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), [Switzerland](26748.md).

After a period of inertia, the Belgian administration became actively involved in Ruanda-Urundi between 1926 and 1931 under the governorship of [Charles Voisin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Voisin_\(governor\)). The Belgians extended and consolidated a power structure based on indigenous institutions.&#x20;

In practice, they developed a [Tutsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutsi) ruling class to formally control a mostly [Hutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutu) population through the system of chiefs and sub-chiefs under the overall rule of the two *Mwami*. Contemporary racial science and [eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics) led Belgian administrators to believe that the Tutsi were genetically more closely related to Europeans than the Hutu superior and deserved power.&#x20;

Some scholars circulated, including [John Hanning Speke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hanning_Speke), propagated the [Hamitic hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamitic_hypothesis) which held that the Tutsi were descended from "black Caucasians" who invaded Europe and were the ancestors of all the more "civilised" African peoples.[^cite_note-3]

The League of Nations was [formally dissolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations#Demise_and_legacy) in April 1946 following its failure to prevent World War II.&#x20;

For practical purposes, it was succeeded by the new [United Nations](31769.md) (UN). In December 1946, the new body voted to end the mandate over Ruanda-Urundi and replace it with the new "[Trust Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_trust_territories)" status.[^cite_note-4]

## Post-independence relations

Following anti-colonial independence movements throughout Africa and unrest in Rwanda including the overthrow of the monarchy during the [Rwandan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Revolution), Ruanda-Urundi became independent after a rushed transition on 1 July 1962. It was broken up on traditional lines, becoming the [Republic of Rwanda](25645.md) and the [Kingdom of Burundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Burundi).[^cite_note-5]

### Rwandan genocide

The [Arusha Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arusha_Accords_\(Rwanda\)) of 1993, the peace treaty which initially ended the [Rwandan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Civil_War), led to the United Nations approving the mandate of [UNAMIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Assistance_Mission_for_Rwanda) as a peacekeeping force. Belgium and Bangladesh were the first nations to contribute troops.[^cite_note-6]

The Belgian contingent was made up of around 440 troops.[^cite_note-7]

After the [assassination of Juvénal Habyarimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Juv%C3%A9nal_Habyarimana_and_Cyprien_Ntaryamira) on 6 April 1994, rumours that Belgian troops were responsible spread on [Hutu Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutu_Power) propaganda radio, including the [Radio Television Libre de Milles Collines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_T%C3%A9l%C3%A9vision_Libre_des_Mille_Collines).[^cite_note-\:1-8]

En route to protecting then-Prime Minister [Agathe Uwilingiyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agathe_Uwilingiyimana), fifteen Belgian UNAMIR peacekeepers were taken prisoner by the Rwandan Army. Ten of them, from a Paracommando Brigade, were killed - hacked to death with machetes and dismembered.[^cite_note-\:1-8][^cite_note-9]

This was a strategic move by the Hutu Power extremists.&#x20;

As [Human Rights Watch](46530.md)'s [Allison Des Forges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Des_Forges) points out, these murders were "the first step in the plan revealed in the January 11 cable for getting rid of an effective UNAMIR force".[^cite_note-\:1-8]

On 12 April, the Belgian government announced that it was withdrawing its troops and began lobbying for a complete withdrawal of UNAMIR.[^cite_note-10]

## Relations after the Rwandan genocide

Belgium re-established diplomatic relations with Rwanda after the Genocide. It is one of Rwanda's largest bilateral aid donors, donating \$41.84 million in 2019.[^cite_note-11]

On March 17, 2025, Rwanda severed diplomatic ties with Belgium, accusing it of siding in the regional conflict in eastern [Democratic Republic of Congo](76762.md) (DRC). The Rwandan Foreign Ministry criticized Belgium's "neocolonial delusions" and its "destructive historical role" in fueling ethnic extremism leading to the 1994 genocide against the [Tutsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutsi). Consequently, all Belgian diplomats were given 48 hours to leave Rwanda. In response, Belgium declared Rwandan diplomats *persona non grata*, escalating tensions between the two nations.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

## Resident diplomatic missions

- Belgium has an embassy in [Kigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali).

- Rwanda has an embassy in [Brussels](3708.md).

## Education

[École Belge de Kigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_Belge_de_Kigali) is a Belgian international school in Kigali.
