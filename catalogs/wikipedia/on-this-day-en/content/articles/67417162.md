The **ambassador of Russia to Romania** is the official representative of the [president](239646.md) and the [government of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Russian_Federation) to the [president](273659.md) and the [government of Romania](6046282.md).

The ambassador and his staff work at large in the [Russian embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_Russia_in_Bucharest) in [Bucharest](36877.md).[^cite_note-1] There is a [consulate general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulate_general) in [Constanța](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constan%C8%9Ba). The current Russian ambassador to Romania is [Vladimir Lipayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lipayev) since June 2024.

## History of diplomatic relations

Main article: [Romania-Russia relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania-Russia_relations)

Diplomatic relations between the regions that now constitute Romania and the Russian Federation date back to the [medieval period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_period). The [Tsardom of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia) offered support for the [Danubian Principalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danubian_Principalities) during their struggle for independence from the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). [Moldavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavia) ruler [Stephen the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_the_Great) concluded a military-political alliance with Russia in the fifteenth century, sealed by the marriage of his daughter [Elena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_of_Moldavia) to [Ivan the Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_the_Young), the son and heir of [Ivan III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_III_of_Russia). [Wallachian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallachia) ruler [Michael the Brave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_the_Brave) also advocated an alliance with Russia towards the end of the sixteenth century.[^cite_note-embassy-2] Relations continued to be strengthened after the establishment of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), with the 1711 [Treaty of Lutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lutsk) between [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) and Moldovan leader [Dimitrie Cantemir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimitrie_Cantemir); and the establishment of permanent diplomatic relations between Wallachia and Russia during the 1688-1714 reign of [Constantin Brâncoveanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Br%C3%A2ncoveanu).[^cite_note-embassy-2]

In the nineteenth century, following the [1828-1829 Russo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1828%E2%80%9329\)), Russia established a protectorate over the Danube principalities, with Count [Pavel Kiselyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Kiselyov) serving as plenipotentiary representative of the councils of Moldova and Wallachia between 1829 and 1834. Kiselyov oversaw various reforms to public administration, and the adoption of the *[Regulamentul Organic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulamentul_Organic)*.[^cite_note-embassy-2] Following the [1877–1878 Russo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1877%E2%80%931878\)), which included the [Romanian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_War_of_Independence) and the defeat and expulsion of the Ottoman Empire, Russia recognised Romanian independence, and on 15 October 1878, Baron [Dmitry Stuart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Stuart?action=edit\&redlink=1) [presented his credentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letters_of_credence) to the Romanian prince, and later king, [Carol I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_I_of_Romania).[^cite_note-embassy-2] 15 October 1878 is now considered the official date for the establishment of Russian-Romanian diplomatic relations.[^cite_note-embassy-2]

Diplomatic relations continued into the twentieth century, interrupted by the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution) in 1917. The [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and Romania maintained a bilateral dialogue, with [Maxim Litvinov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_Litvinov) of the Soviet Union and [Nicolae Titulescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Titulescu) of Romania co-operating on the signing of the 1933 [London Convention on the Definition of Aggression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Convention_on_the_Definition_of_Aggression).[^cite_note-embassy-2] This was followed with the re-establishment of diplomatic relations on 9 June 1934.[^cite_note-embassy-2][^cite_note-history1-3] Relations were once more suspended on 22 June 1941 after the [Axis invasion of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa), and remained so during most of the Second World War.[^cite_note-history1-3] They were re-established on 6 August 1945, after the defeat of the Axis powers, and between 20 and 24 August the mission was upgraded to the level of an embassy.[^cite_note-embassy-2][^cite_note-history2-4] During the [Cold War](325329.md) Romania was a member of the [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact), and with the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) at the end of 1991, the Russian Federation emerged as the Soviet Union's legal successor. The incumbent ambassador of the Soviet Union to Romania, [Feliks Bogdanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feliks_Bogdanov?action=edit\&redlink=1), continued as representative of Russia until 1992.[^cite_note-history2-4]
