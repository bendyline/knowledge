# Business jet

"Bizjet" redirects here. For other uses, see [Bizjet (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bizjet_\(disambiguation\)).



Over 2,000 [Cessna CitationJets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_CitationJet) have been delivered, making it the most popular line of business jets.

[^cite_note-8jun2017PR-1]



View of the cabin, inside a business jet

A **business jet**, **private jet**, or **bizjet** is a [jet aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_aircraft) designed for transporting small groups of people, typically [business executives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_executive) and high-ranking [associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coworker). Business jets are generally designed for faster [air travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_travel) and more personal comfort than [commercial aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_aircraft), and may be adapted for other roles, such as [casualty evacuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casualty_evacuation) or express [parcel deliveries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Package_delivery), and some are used by [public bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_bodies), [government officials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_official), [VIPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_important_person), or even the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military).

## History

### Early developments

The first small, jet-powered civilian aircraft was the [Morane-Saulnier MS.760 Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morane-Saulnier_MS.760_Paris), developed privately in the early 1950s from the [MS.755 Fleuret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morane-Saulnier_MS.755_Fleuret) two-seat [jet trainer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_trainer). First flown in 1954, the MS.760 Paris differs from subsequent business jets in having only four seats arranged in two rows without a center aisle, similar to a [light aircraft](2643987.md), under a large sliding [canopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canopy_\(aircraft\)) similar to that of a [fighter](10929.md). A U.S. [type certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate) was awarded in July 1958, but commercial sales were limited, with most examples going to the military; an improved civilian version similar to a modern [very light jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_light_jet), with a six-seat enclosed cabin and a conventional door, never proceeded past the prototype stage.[^cite_note-GA_2010-2] The commercial failure of the MS.760 Paris prompted the cancellation of projects by [Cessna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna) and [Douglas Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Aircraft) to market similar jets.[^cite_note-Flying1987-3]

The development of center-aisle cabin business jets was accelerated by an August 1956 [United States Air Force](32090.md) (USAF) letter of the requirement for two "[off-the-shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_off-the-shelf)" aircraft, the larger UCX (cargo) and smaller UTX (trainer). These requirements differed from standard USAF procurement contracts in that no formal competitions would occur, and manufacturers were expected to develop the aircraft without government funds; despite this, a substantial USAF purchase would offset the large investment necessary to develop prototypes. Both [Lockheed Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Corporation) and [McDonnell Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Aircraft) began the development of UCX aircraft, while [North American Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aviation) pursued the UTX requirement.[^cite_note-Flying1987-3][^cite_note-4]

### 1950s first flight

The [Morane-Saulnier MS.760 Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morane-Saulnier_MS.760_Paris) had a [gross weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_gross_weight) of 7,650 lb (3.47 t), initially powered by two [Turboméca Marboré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbom%C3%A9ca_Marbor%C3%A9) [turbojets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbojet) of 880 lbf (3.9 kN) thrust, although most aircraft were later upgraded to 1,058 lbf (4.71 kN) units. The aircraft seated a single pilot and up to three passengers under a sliding canopy, and was first flown on 29 July 1954; 219 were built.



The [Lockheed JetStar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_JetStar) is the earliest business jet with a center aisle.

The [Lockheed JetStar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_JetStar), designed to meet USAF UCX requirements and seating 10 passengers and 2 crew, first flew on 4 September 1957. In total, 204 aircraft were produced from 1957 to 1978 powered by several different engines; four 3,300 lbf (15 kN) [Pratt & Whitney JT12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_JT12) turbojets, then [Garrett TFE731](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrett_TFE731) turbofans for a 44,500 lb (20.2 t) [maximum take-off weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_take-off_weight) (MTOW), then two [General Electric CF700](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_CF700) turbofans.

The smaller, 17,760-pound (8.06 t) MTOW [North American Sabreliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Sabreliner), tailored to the USAF UTX requirement, first flew on 16 September 1958. Powered by two [Pratt & Whitney JT12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_JT12) turbojet engines then [Garrett TFE731s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrett_TFE731), more than 800 were produced from 1959 to 1982.

Designed in 1957 for the UCX requirement, the [McDonnell 119](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_119) was delayed by the cancellation of the [Fairchild J83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_J83) engine program, and first flew on 11 February 1959 powered by four [Westinghouse J34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_J34) turbojets. The 119 was certified for a 45,328 lb (20.6 t) MTOW with four Pratt & Whitney JT12 or General Electric CF700 engines, but no firm orders were received, and only the single prototype was completed.[^cite_note-Francillon1990-5]

### 1960s first flight



The 1963 [Learjet 23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learjet_23) was the first light jet.



The first large, long-range jet was the [Grumman Gulfstream II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_Gulfstream_II) in 1966.

The 25,000 lb (11 t) MTOW [British Aerospace 125](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace_125) first flew on 13 August 1962 as the de Havilland DH.125, powered by two 3,000 lbf (13 kN) [Armstrong Siddeley Viper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armstrong_Siddeley_Viper) turbojets. Its engines were replaced by Garrett TFE731s, then [Pratt & Whitney Canada PW300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada_PW300) turbofans. Almost 1,700 aircraft of all variants, including the [Hawker 800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_800), were produced between 1962 and 2013.

The Aero Commander 1121 Jet Commander, which later became the [IAI Westwind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAI_Westwind), first flew on 27 January 1963, powered by two [General Electric CJ610](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_CJ610) turbojets, then [Garrett TFE731s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrett_TFE731). Production of Jet Commanders and Westwinds from 1965 to 1987 came to 442 aircraft, and it was developed as the [IAI Astra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAI_Astra), later rebranded as the [Gulfstream G100](3493147.md).

The 29,000 lb (13 t) MTOW [Dassault Falcon 20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon_20) first flew on 4 May 1963, powered by two [General Electric CF700s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_CF700), then [Garrett ATF3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrett_ATF3) turbofans and Garrett TFE731s. In total, 508 were built from 1963 to 1988, and it is the basis of the [Dassault Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon) family.

The first light jet first flew on 7 October 1963 - the [Learjet 23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learjet_23). Powered by two 2,850 lbf (12.7 kN) [General Electric CJ610s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_CJ610), its 12,500 lb (5.7 t) MTOW complies with [FAR Part 23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAR_Part_23) regulations. The first member of the [Learjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learjet) family, 104 were built between 1962 and 1966.

The forward wing-sweep, 20,280 lb (9.20 t) MTOW [Hamburger Flugzeugbau HFB 320 Hansa Jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger_Flugzeugbau_HFB_320_Hansa_Jet) first flew on 21 April 1964, powered by two General Electric CJ610s; 47 were built between 1965 and 1973. The joint Piaggo-Douglas, 18,000 lb (8.2 t) MTOW [Piaggio PD.808](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio_PD.808) first flew on 29 August 1964, powered by two [Armstrong Siddeley Vipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armstrong_Siddeley_Viper); 24 were built for the Italian Air Force.

On 2 October 1966 the first large business jet first flew, the 65,500 lb (29.7 t) MTOW [Grumman Gulfstream II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_Gulfstream_II), powered by two 11,400 lbf (51 kN) [Rolls-Royce Spey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Spey) turbofans. From 1967 to the late 1970s, 258 were built, and it led to the ongoing [Gulfstream Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_Aerospace) long-range family.

The 11,850 lb (5.38 t) MTOW [Cessna Citation I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_Citation_I) first flew on 15 September 1969, powered by two 2,200 lbf (9.8 kN) [Pratt & Whitney Canada JT15D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada_JT15D) turbofans. Produced between 1969 and 1985, for a total of 689 examples, it is the first of the [Cessna Citation family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_Citation_family).

### 1970s first flight

The trijet [Dassault Falcon 50](3098320.md) made its first flight on 7 November 1976. The 40,000 lb (18 t) MTOW airplane is powered by three 3,700 lbf (16 kN) TFE731 engines. With the cross-section of the Falcon 20, it is the basis of the larger [Falcon 900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_900).

On 8 November 1978, the prototype [Canadair Challenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadair_Challenger) took off. The 43,000–48,000 lb (20–22 t) MTOW craft, usually powered by two 9,200 lbf (41 kN) [General Electric CF34s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_CF34), formed the basis of the long range [Bombardier Global Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Global_Express) family and of the [Bombardier CRJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_CRJ) regional airliners. The 1000th Challenger entered service in 2015.

On 30 May 1979 the all-new 22,000 lb (10.0 t) MTOW [Cessna Citation III](9374265.md) took off for the first time, powered by two 3,650 lbf (16.2 kN) TFE731s.

The [Mitsubishi MU-300 Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_MU-300_Diamond) made its first flight on 29 August 1978. The 16,100 lb (7.3 t) MTOW jet was powered by two 2,900 lbf (13 kN) [JT15D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JT15D). The design was later sold and was renamed Beechjet 400 then [Hawker 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_400), with a total of 950 produced of all variants.

### 1980s first flight

The 1980s only saw the introduction of derivatives and no major new designs. The late 1980s saw the advent of fractional ownership for business jets.[^cite_note-6] For much of the 1980s, sales of new aircraft slumped.[^cite_note-resmocj-7]

### 1990s first flight

On 29 April 1991, the [Cessna CitationJet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_CitationJet) was first flown. Powered by two 1,900 lbf (8.5 kN) [Williams FJ44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_FJ44) engines, the 10,500 lb (4.8 t) light jet used a modified [Citation II](3878365.md) fuselage with a new wing and tail, and was subsequently developed into the CJ series and M2, ultimately replacing the Citation I, Citation II and [Citation V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citation_V) series. The 2,000th CitationJet was delivered in 2017.

The first flight of the all-new [Learjet 45](7075948.md) was on 7 October 1995. All of the 642 aircraft built since then have been powered by two 3,500 lbf (16 kN) TFE731 engines.

Powered by two 2,300 lbf (10 kN) Williams FJ44s, the 12,500 pounds (5.7 t) [Beechcraft Premier I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_Premier_I) light jet made its first flight on 22 December 1998. Nearly 300 had been made before production stopped in 2013.

### 2000s first flight

In the opposite way compared to Bombardier, which developed airliners from a business jet, Embraer derived the [Legacy 600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legacy_600) from the [Embraer ERJ family](273890.md) of regional jet airliners. Powered by two 8,800 lbf (39.2 kN) [Rolls-Royce AE 3007s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_AE_3007), the first flight of the 50,000-pound (22.5 t) aircraft was on 31 March 2001. M

On 14 August 2001, the [Bombardier Challenger 300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Challenger_300) made its first flight. The 38,850-pound (17.62 t) aircraft is powered by two 6,825 lbf (30.36 kN) [HTF7000s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTF7000). The 500th example was delivered in 2015.

The first very light jet, the 5,950-pound (2.70 t) MTOW [Eclipse 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclipse_500), took off for the first time on 26 August 2002, powered by two 900 lbf (4.0 kN) [Pratt & Whitney Canada PW600s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada_PW600). Between then and the end of production in 2008, 260 were produced.

Another new small jet, the 10,701-pound (4.854 t) [Honda HA-420 HondaJet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honda_HA-420_HondaJet), first flew on 3 December 2003 powered by two 2,050 lbf (9.1 kN) [GE Honda HF120](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Honda_HF120) engines mounted above the wing in a configuration unique amongst business jets. As of March 2020, 150 had been delivered.

It was followed by the 8,645-pound (3.921 t) MTOW [Cessna Citation Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_Citation_Mustang) on 23 April 2005, powered by two 1,460 lbf (6.5 kN) Pratt & Whitney Canada PW600s and with more than 450 produced.

The [Embraer Phenom 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_Phenom_100) made its maiden flight on 26 July 2007. The 10,500-pound (4.75 t) MTOW airplane is powered by two 1,600 lbf (7.2 kN) Pratt & Whitney Canada PW600s. With its [Phenom 300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenom_300) development, nearly 600 have been built.

### 2010s first flight

The first flight of the midsize, [fly-by-wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fly-by-wire), 7,000 lbf (31 kN) [Honeywell HTF7000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeywell_HTF7000)-powered [Embraer Legacy 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_Legacy_450/500_and_Praetor_500/600) was on 27 November 2012. It was followed by the shorter [Legacy 450](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_Legacy_450/500_and_Praetor_500/600) on 28 December 2013.

### New models



A [Global 7500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_7500) prototype in 2018

After peaking in 2008, deliveries slowed due to [political instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_instability) but the industry hopes to revive demand by introducing more attractive and competitive new models, four in 2018:[^cite_note-AvWeek22dec2017-8]

- the 7,400 nmi (13,700 km) range [Bombardier Global 7500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Global_7500) large-cabin jet, before the 7,900 nmi (14,600 km) Global 8000 in 2019;

- the 5,200 nmi (9,600 km) large-cabin [Gulfstream G500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_G500), replacing the [Gulfstream G450](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_G450), then the longer, 6,500 nmi (12,000 km) G600 eventually superseding the [G550](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G550);

- the 3,500 nmi (6,500 km) [Cessna Citation Longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_Citation_Longitude) super midsize jet;

- the superlight [Pilatus PC-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilatus_PC-24).

In October 2018, consultant Jetcraft expected 20 variants or new designs to enter service before 2023 (seven large, seven midsize and six small): in 2019 the [Global 5500/6500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Global_Express#Global_5500/6500), [Gulfstream G600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_G600), [Citation XLS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citation_XLS)++ and a [CitationJet CJ4+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_CitationJet/M2)/, while the [Embraer Praetor 500/600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_Legacy_500) to be introduced in 2019 were predicted for 2021/2022; in 2020 a [Gulfstream G750](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_G750?action=edit\&redlink=1); in 2021 the [Dassault Falcon 6X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon_6X), [Learjet 70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learjet_70)XR/75XR and [Global 7500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_7500)XR; in 2022 the [Bombardier Challenger 350XRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Challenger_350); in 2023 the [Citation Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citation_Hemisphere), an [Embraer Legacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_Legacy) 700, [Phenom 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenom_100)V+, [Dassault Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon) 9X, [Bombardier Challenger 750](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Challenger_750?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Gulfstream G400NG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_G400NG?action=edit\&redlink=1); in 2025 a [Citation Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citation_Mustang) 2+.[^cite_note-AvWeek15oct2018-9]

## Configuration

Most production business jets use two [jet engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_engine) as a compromise between the operating economy of fewer engines and the ability to safely continue flight after an engine failure. Exceptions include the early [Lockheed JetStar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_JetStar) with four engines; the [Dassault Falcon 50](3098320.md) and derivatives with three; and the [Cirrus Vision SF50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirrus_Vision_SF50) with one, a configuration also used in several similar [very light jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_light_jet) design concepts. Most business jets use [podded engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podded_engine) mounted on the rear fuselage with a [cruciform tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruciform_tail) or [T-tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-tail) to reduce [interference drag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interference_drag) and increase exhaust clearance. Practical limits on the ground clearance of these smaller aircraft have prompted designers to avoid the common [jetliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetliner) configuration of a [low wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_wing) with underslung podded engines. The sole business jet to use this layout, the early McDonnell 119, was rejected by the USAF due to [foreign object damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_object_damage) concerns, leading to the failure of the program.[^cite_note-10] The recent HondaJet uses wing-mounted engines but mitigates this problem with its unique over-the-wing engine pods.

As with jetliners, [swept wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swept_wing) are often used to increase cruise speed, but [straight wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straight_wing) are also commonplace; notably, Cessna deliberately prioritized docile low-speed handling in choosing straight wings for many models in its popular Citation family, envisioning that owners transitioning from slower [piston engined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piston_engine) or [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop) aircraft would want to maintain the ability to use relatively short runways, and that lower approach speeds would ease single-pilot operations, particularly by relatively inexperienced owner-pilots.[^cite_note-11]

[Rolls-Royce plc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_plc) powers over 3,000 business jets, 42% of the fleet:[^cite_note-12] all the [Gulfstreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_Aerospace) and [Bombardier Globals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Global_Express), the [Cessna Citation X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_Citation_X) and [Embraer Legacy 600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_Legacy_600), early [Hawkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_HS-125), and many small jets with the [Williams-Rolls FJ44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams-Rolls_FJ44).[^cite_note-13]

## Market



The apron at [Anguilla Wallblake Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anguilla_Wallblake_Airport) clogged with business jets

### Fleet

About 70% of the fleet was in North America at the end of 2011, the European market is the next largest, with growing activity in the Middle East, Asia, and Central America.[^cite_note-cji-14]

On 1 April 2017, there were 22,368 business jets in the worldwide fleet, of which 11.2% were for sale.[^cite_note-15]

By October 2018, the entire private jet fleet was dominated by [Textron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textron_Aviation) ([Beechcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft), [Cessna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna) and [Hawker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Beechcraft) branded aircraft) with 43.9%, then Bombardier with 22.4%, Gulfstream with 13.0%, Dassault with 9.6% and Embraer with 5.8%, mostly in North America (64.6%), followed by Europe (13.0%) South America (12.1%) and Asia-Pacific (5.9%).[^cite_note-16]

As on March 31, 2019, there are 22,125 business jets in the worldwide fleet and top 20 country markets account for 89% of this total fleet.[^cite_note-17]

### Market shares

In 2015 the total airplane billing amounted to US\$21.9 billion, and 718 business jets were delivered to customers across the globe: 199 (27.7%) by [Bombardier Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Aviation), 166 (23.1%) by [Textron Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textron_Aviation), 154 (21.4%) by [Gulfstream Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_Aerospace), 120 (16.7%) by [Embraer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer) and 55 (7.7%) by [Dassault Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Aviation).[^cite_note-GAMA-18]

In 2017, 676 business jets were shipped, led by Gulfstream with \$6.56 billion for 120 aircraft, Bombardier with \$5.2 billion for 140, Textron with \$2.87 billion (including propeller aircraft and 180 jets), Dassault with \$2.42 billion for 49 and Embraer with \$1.35 billion for 109.[^cite_note-19]

In 2022, 712 business jets were shipped, led by Gulfstream with \$6.60 billion for 120 aircraft, Bombardier with \$6,04 billion for 123, Textron Aviation with \$3,62 billion, Dassault Aviation with \$1,76 billion for 32, Embraer with \$1,36 for 102 and Pilatus with \$900 million for 123 aircraft.[^cite_note-20]

### Second hand

The residual value level for a five-year old aircraft is at 56% of the list price.[^cite_note-2017JetcraftForecast-21] A new [business aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_aircraft) typically depreciates by 50% in five years before [depreciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depreciation) flattens between years 10 and 15, and the owner of a 15-to-20 year old aircraft is often the last, matching [luxury cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxury_car).[^cite_note-22]

Business jets have varying [value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_value) retention, between the leading [Embraer Phenom 300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_Phenom_300)E, sold for \$9.45 million in 2018 and expected to retain 68% of its value 15 years later for \$6.46 million in 2033, and the trailing \$24.5 million [Gulfstream G280](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_G280), predicted to retain 42% of its value for \$10.25 million.[^cite_note-23]

### Forecasts

In October 2017 Jetcraft forecast 8,349 unit deliveries in the next decade for \$252 billion, a \$30.2 million average. Cessna should lead the numbers with 27.3% of the deliveries ahead of Bombardier with 20.9%, while Gulfstream would almost lead the revenue market share with 27.8% trailing Bombardier with 29.2%.[^cite_note-2017JetcraftForecast-21] For 2016–2025, Jetcraft forecast [Pratt & Whitney Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Canada) should be the first engine supplier with 30% of the \$24 billion revenue, in front of the current leader [Rolls-Royce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_Holdings) at 25%. Honeywell will hold 45% of the \$16 billion in [avionics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avionics) revenue ahead of [Rockwell Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockwell_Collins) with 37% and [Garmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garmin).[^cite_note-2016JetcraftForecast-24]

There are three basic types of operators that own, manage and operate private jets.

### Flight departments



A [Gulfstream G550](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_G550) owned by [Luxottica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxottica)

Flight departments are corporate-owned operators that manage the aircraft of a specific company. [Ford Motor Company](30433662.md), [Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler), and [Altria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altria) are examples of companies that own, maintain and operate their own fleet of private aircraft for their employees. Flight departments handle all aspects of aircraft operation and maintenance. In the United States, flight-department aircraft operate under [FAR 91](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Aviation_Regulations#Organization) operating rules.

A 2010 study by the United States [National Business Aviation Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Business_Aviation_Association) found that small and midsize companies that use private jets produce a 219% higher earnings growth rate than those that strictly use airlines.[^cite_note-29]

### Charter companies



Interior of a [NetJets](2299551.md) [Gulfstream G450](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_G450)

Charter operators own or manage private jets for multiple clients. Like traditional flight departments, charter companies handle all aspects of aircraft operation and maintenance. However, they are not aligned with just one corporation. They manage aircraft for a private owner or corporation and also handle the sales of available flight time on the aircraft they own or manage. Maintenance services can also be provided.

In the United States, business aircraft may be operated under either FAR 91 as private operations for the business purposes of the owner, or under [FAR 135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Aviation_Regulations#Organization) of the Federal Aviation Regulations as commercial operations for the business purposes of a third party. One common arrangement for operational flexibility purposes is for the aircraft's owner to operate the aircraft under FAR 91 when needed for its own purposes, and to allow a third-party charter-manager to operate it under FAR 135 when the aircraft is needed for the business purposes of third parties (such as for other entities within the corporate group of the aircraft's owner).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

Aircraft charter brokers have entered the marketplace through the ease of setting up a website and business online. Aircraft charter operators are legally responsible for the safe operation of aircraft and charter brokers require no economic authority and are largely unregulated. The [Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Transportation) requires that air charter brokers disclose to the consumer that they do not operate aircraft and cannot use terms like "our fleet of aircraft", "we operate", "our charter service" and others.[^cite_note-32]

### Fractional ownership

Since 1996 the term "fractional jet" has been used in connection with business aircraft owned by a consortium of companies. Under such arrangements, overhead costs such as [flight crew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircrew_member), hangarage and maintenance are split among the users.

[Fractional ownership of aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_ownership_of_aircraft) involves an individual or corporation that pays an upfront equity share for the cost of an aircraft. If four parties are involved, a partner would pay one-fourth of the aircraft price (a "quarter share"). That partner is now an equity owner in that aircraft and can sell the equity position if necessary. This also entitles the new owner to a certain number of hours of flight time on that aircraft, or any comparable aircraft in the fleet. Additional fees include monthly management fees and incidentals such as catering and ground transportation. In the United States, fractional-ownership operations may be regulated by either FAA [part 91 or part 135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Part_91_operator).[^cite_note-33]

Buying blocks of time in predetermined increments involves buying blocks of time in hours. This model usually depends on how many flight hours a client will be needing. Pay as you fly resembles the business model used by [commercial airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_airline) where a client only pays for a flight they use. The downside to this model is that clients find it difficult to secure flights because most private flights are usually booked in advance.

Paying a flat rate for unlimited usage as the name implies, uses a business model where clients pay a flat rate, and then the jet is made available to them for the duration that payment lasts. This model makes it possible for a client to pay a certain amount for unlimited access and usage to a jet, or a fleet of jets for the duration. This model has been discontinued by most private jet hire companies because clients have exploited the usage in the past. This model now employs a [fair usage policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_usage_policy).

## Environmental impact

Further information: [Environmental impact of aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_aviation)



Demonstration against business jets ([Switzerland](26748.md), 2023)

Calculations suggest that people traveling in their own private jets leave a disproportionate [carbon footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_footprint) relative to those who use commercial jetliners or other means of travel. Some individuals travel over 350,000 km (220,000 mi) annually, generating thousands of tons of CO<sub>2</sub> emissions.[^cite_note-59]

### Tracking projects

As a reaction to the disproportionate emission of [greenhouse gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gases) by private jets, projects dedicated to tracking the movement of these airplanes in general, as well as the jets owned by specific celebrities, politicians, entrepreneurs and elite groups, have gained widespread attention by the internet public and the media. The programmer [Jack Sweeney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sweeney) became known for his various social media accounts that track jets owned by individuals such as [Taylor Swift](5422144.md), [Elon Musk](909036.md), [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos), [Mark Zuckerberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Zuckerberg), [Bill Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Gates), [Donald Trump](4848272.md),[^cite_note-\:0-60] and [Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_\(musician\)).[^cite_note-\:4-61]
