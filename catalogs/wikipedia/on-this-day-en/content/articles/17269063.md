# Chess World Cup

Not to be confused with [World Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship).

The **FIDE World Cup** is a major [chess](5134.md) event organized by [FIDE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE), the international governing body of chess.&#x20;

## History

Three different formats have been used:

- In 2000 and 2002, it was a [multi-stage tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-stage_tournament), with a [group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_stage) consisting of 24 players in four groups, followed by a [knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knockout_stage).

- From 2005 to 2019, it was a biennial 128-player [single-elimination](939680.md) tournament, forming part of the qualification for the [World Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship).

- From the [2021 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_World_Cup_2021) to the [2025 edition](78998807.md), the number of players has increased to 206. A system of [byes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bye_\(tournament\)) is used in the first round; from the second round onwards the format is identical to the previous system.

- Beginning in 2027, the number of players will be increased to 224. Additionally, there will be two stages. The first one will be a swiss qualification, featuring four Swiss pools and nine rounds across five days. The second one will be a knockout finals, where 16 players qualified from the Swiss pools will play in a single-elimination tournament.

### Chess World Cup (1979)

Before FIDE introduced the Chess World Cup, a tournament was organized in [Montreal](7954681.md) in 1979 under the name "World Cup". It was later renamed the "Man and his World Chess Challenge Cup" after the venue, [Man and his World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_67).[^cite_note-\:0-1] The tournament was held as a [double round-robin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_round-robin) between ten leading grandmasters.[^cite_note-2] [Anatoly Karpov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Karpov) and [Mikhail Tal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Tal) shared first place after drawing each other in the final round.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

### Chess World Cup (1988–1989)

Main article: [Chess World Cup 1988–1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_World_Cup_1988%E2%80%931989)

In 1988–1989, the Grandmasters Association (GMA), which had six players from the 1979 tournament as directors, organized six tournaments as the "[GMA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_World_Cup_1988%E2%80%931989)".[^cite_note-\:0-1] Participants were high-ranking [grandmasters](12304.md); each round was a large [round robin](939742.md) termed a "Grand Prix". They were considered the flagship tournaments of the GMA but were abandoned as the association gradually collapsed in the early 1990s.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

## FIDE World Cup (2000–2002)

In 2000 and 2002 [FIDE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE), the International Chess Federation, staged their "First Chess World Cup" and "Second Chess World Cup" respectively. These were major tournaments, but not directly linked to the [World Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship). Both the [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_World_Cup_2000)[^cite_note-7] and [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_World_Cup_2002)[^cite_note-8] events were won by [Viswanathan Anand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viswanathan_Anand) of [India](14533.md).

## FIDE World Cup (2005–present)

See also: [FIDE World Chess Championships (1998–2004)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_World_Chess_Championships_\(1998%E2%80%932004\))

Since 2005, a different event of the same name has been part of the World Chess Championship cycle. This event is being held every two years. It is a 128-player knockout tournament, in the same style as the [Tilburg tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilburg_chess_tournament) between 1992 and 1994, or the [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_World_Chess_Championship_1998), [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_World_Chess_Championship_1999), [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_World_Chess_Championship_2000), [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_World_Chess_Championship_2002) and [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_World_Chess_Championship_2004) [FIDE World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_World_Championship).

The event was held in 2005, 2007, 2009, and 2011 in Khanty-Mansiysk, and subsequently FIDE has given preference to bids for the Olympiad that also contain a bid for the preceding World Cup.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] During the 2015 finals of the World Cup, the main organizer commented "We received the right to host the Olympiad and then we were given an additional event – the World Cup."[^cite_note-11]

The [Chess World Cup 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_World_Cup_2005) qualified ten players for the [Candidates Tournament](1368341.md) for the [World Chess Championship 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_2007). Since then, every World Cup has qualified between one and three players for the Candidates Tournament.

Two World Cup qualifiers ([Boris Gelfand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Gelfand) in 2009 and [Sergey Karjakin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Karjakin) in 2015) won the subsequent Candidates tournament and played in the World Championship match, in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_2012) and [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_2016) respectively.

### Format

From 2005 to 2019, the format was 128 players with 7 single-elimination rounds of "mini-matches", which are 2 games each followed by a series of rapid then blitz tiebreaks if necessary. Since 2021, the World Cup has been expanded to 206 players playing 8 single-elimination rounds, with 50 players receiving a bye to the 2nd round. The final usually has 4 games before the tiebreaks start. Since 2015, an extra rest day has recently been added before the semi-finals, in addition to before the final.[^cite_note-12]

Some criticism has been leveled at the scheduling effects, with the event being rather long (26 days), particularly with almost all of the players having left long before the end.[^cite_note-13] Fatigue thus plays a critical role, and while some players seek to conserve energy by avoiding tiebreaks, others "agree" (either explicitly or implicitly) to make short draws in the 2 long games and decide the winner in tiebreaks. It is often remarked that the system is mostly a lottery of who survives, though better players have more chances on the whole.[^cite_note-14] The anticlimax of the 4-round final, with both players now already qualified for the [Candidates](1368341.md), has also been criticized.[^cite_note-15]

### Winners

"Qual" refers to the number of players who qualify for the [Candidates Tournament](1368341.md) (marked with green background). For example, in 2015, the top 2 finishers qualified for the [2016 Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Candidates_Tournament). In 2021, [Sergey Karjakin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Karjakin) qualified for the [2022 Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Candidates_Tournament) via the World Cup, but was subsequently disqualified for making statements in support of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine). In 2023, only the top three players were meant to qualify, but [Magnus Carlsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Carlsen) declined to participate in the Candidates; thus the fourth place, [Nijat Abasov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nijat_Abasov), qualified as well.
