A **draft** is a process used in some countries (especially in [North America](21139.md)) and sports (especially in [closed leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed_league)) to allocate certain players to teams. In a draft, teams take turns selecting from a pool of eligible players. When a team selects a player, the team receives exclusive rights to sign that player to a contract, and no other team in the league may sign the player. The process is similar to [round-robin item allocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round-robin_item_allocation).

The best-known type of draft is the entry draft, which is used to allocate players who have recently become eligible to play in a league. Depending on the sport, the players may come from [college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_sports), high school or junior teams, or teams in other countries. An entry draft is intended to prevent expensive bidding wars for young talent and to ensure that no team can sign contracts with all of the best young players and make the league uncompetitive. To encourage [parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parity_\(sports\)), teams that do poorly in the previous season usually get to choose first in the postseason draft, sometimes with a "lottery" factor in an attempt to discourage teams from [tanking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanking_\(sports\)).

Other types of drafts include the [expansion draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_draft), in which a new team selects players from other teams in the league, and the dispersal draft, in which a league's surviving teams select players from the roster of a newly defunct franchise. Major professional sports leagues also have special contingency plans for rebuilding a team via a [disaster draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disaster_draft), should an accident or other disaster kill or disable many players.[^cite_note-nyt1992-1]

Drafts are usually permitted under [antitrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antitrust_law) or [restraint of trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restraint_of_trade) laws because they are included in [collective bargaining agreements](12728750.md) between leagues and labor unions representing players. These agreements generally stipulate that after a certain number of seasons, a player whose contract has expired becomes a [free agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_agent) and can sign with any team. They also require minimum and sometimes maximum salaries for newly drafted players. Leagues may also allow teams to [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_\(sports\)) draft picks among each other in exchange for other draft picks or in exchange for players.

In 1935, [National Football League](21211.md) president [Joseph Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Carr) instituted the [NFL draft](710502.md) as a way to restrain teams' payrolls and reduce the dominance of the league's perennial contenders.[^cite_note-2] It was adopted by the precursor of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md) in 1947; by the [National Hockey League](21809.md) in 1963; and by [Major League Baseball](38776.md) in 1965, although draft systems had been used in baseball since the 19th century.[^cite_note-3]

[Major Indoor Lacrosse League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_Indoor_Lacrosse_League) ([National Lacrosse League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lacrosse_League)) adopted a collegiate draft in 1988, [Major League Soccer](71802.md) in 2000, [Major League Lacrosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Lacrosse) in 2001, and [Major League Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Rugby) in 2020.[^cite_note-urlQuestions_And_Concerns_About_MLR_Draft_|_Goff_Rugby_Report-4]

Sports drafts are uncommon outside [the U.S. and Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada). Most professional football clubs and those in other sports obtain young players through [transfers](2643292.md) from smaller clubs or by developing youth players through their own academies. The [youth system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_system) is operated directly by the teams themselves, who develop their players from childhood. Parity in these leagues is instead maintained through [promotion and relegation](648049.md), which automatically expels the weakest teams from a league in exchange for the strongest teams in the next lower league.

## Australia

### AFL draft

Main article: [Australian Football League draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Football_League_draft)

In [Australian rules football](2403.md)'s premier competition, the [Australian Football League](4732785.md) (AFL), a [draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Football_League_draft) was introduced in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_VFL_season) when the competition was then known as the [Victorian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_Football_League) (VFL). This was in response to the increasing transfer fees and player salaries at the time, which in combination with declining attendances, threatened to derail the league. It was also a result of the failure of [country zoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoning_\(Australian_rules_football\)), introduced in the late 1960s, which had led to a systematic inequality whereby the clubs with the best zones, like [Carlton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlton_Football_Club) and [Hawthorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorn_Football_Club), could dominate over clubs with poorer zones like [Melbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_Football_Club).

In the AFL draft, clubs receive picks based on the position in which they finish on the ladder. Therefore, the teams that finish at the bottom of the AFL ladder will get the first draft picks. Also, any team that finishes in a low ladder position for consecutive seasons will receive priority picks.

The AFL's national draft is held in November, with a pre-season draft and a rookie draft held in December.

### NRL draft

The [1991 NSWRL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_NSWRL_season) featured the introduction of [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) football's first draft system. The draft allowed teams to recruit players on a roster system based on where the club finished the previous year. It ran in reverse order with the [wooden spooners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_rugby_league_wooden_spooners) getting first choice and the premiers last. The draft lasted just the one season before being defeated in the courts by players and coaches opposed to its limitations.[^cite_note-5]

## Africa

### SA20 Auction

Like the [IPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPL) the [SA20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SA20) also has an auction where teams bid on players and subject a salary cap and restrictions on roster size. After the main auction takes place there is then a rookie draft where each team has two minutes to pick one rookie player that is 18–22 years of age. The team that finished last place in the last season is the first to pick and the team that finished first in the last season is the last to pick.

## North America

### CFL draft

Main article: [CFL draft](6237354.md)

The [Canadian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League) (CFL) holds its annual player drafts before the start of the season, either in the last days of April or the start of May. The league's drafts consists of two that occur on the same day: a larger main draft for Canadian players preceded by a smaller draft for "global" players.

The main draft was formerly held as part of the annual league meetings in [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario), but is now typically held by conference call with the first two rounds being broadcast live on [TSN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sports_Network). Since [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_CFL_draft), the main draft has consisted of eight rounds, with teams drafting in inverse order of their records in the previous season, with the [Grey Cup](230107.md) runner-up selecting second-to-last and the Grey Cup champion selecting last. As with the [NFL](21211.md) Draft, trading of picks is very common, meaning that a team will not necessarily have eight picks in a given draft. The draft main is restricted to Canadian citizens, plus non-citizens who were raised in Canada since childhood (see [the relevant section of the main CFL article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League#Players_and_compensation)). Eligible players can be drafted both from [U Sports football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Sports_football) programs in Canada and [U.S. college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football) programs.

Since 2019, players who are neither American nor Canadian, designated as "global players", are eligible for and subject to a smaller separate draft that occurs earlier in the morning before the main draft for Canadian players. This draft is structured like the main draft, but is not broadcast on television.

American players, which can compose up to half of a CFL team's roster, are not subject to a draft and enter the league by way of the [negotiation list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negotiation_list), a process that allows a [CFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League) team to unilaterally stake claim to any international player on a first-come, first-served basis without the player's consent and bar them from negotiating with any other CFL team; this includes almost all of the league's [quarterbacks](25277.md).

### NFL draft

Main article: [NFL draft](710502.md)

Draft order in the NFL is determined in a reverse-record order (the previous season's worst team picking first, the [Super Bowl](27718.md) winner picking last). There are 7 rounds of the draft (a maximum of 224 picks), so each team can have 7 selections, plus whatever compensatory selections a team receives as a result of [free agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_agency_\(sports\)) (up to 32 compensatory selections are given each year), and any picks awarded for developing minority candidates for NFL head coaching or general manager positions (starting with the 2021 draft in accordance with NFL Resolution JC-2A). Teams are allowed to trade draft picks (including compensation picks since 2017) among each other in exchange for other draft picks or in exchange for players.

Because the NFL requires that players be three years removed from high school, and of the lack of an effective junior development system outside the college and university programs, players are chosen almost exclusively from [National Collegiate Athletic Association](60706.md) [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football) programs.

The NFL draft has become one of the key events on the American football calendar, airing live on television each April. In recent years it had been held at New York's [Radio City Music Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_City_Music_Hall), but in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_NFL_draft) and [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NFL_draft) it was held at Chicago's [Auditorium Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auditorium_Theatre),[^cite_note-Chicago/Los_Angeles-6] and in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_NFL_draft) it was held on the [Rocky Steps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Steps) at the [Philadelphia Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Museum_of_Art). The [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NFL_draft) draft was the first ever to be held at an NFL stadium, namely the [Dallas Cowboys](8121.md)' [AT\&T Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Stadium).

### NBA draft

Main article: [NBA draft](835946.md)

See also: [Eligibility for the NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eligibility_for_the_NBA_draft)

The [NBA draft](835946.md), held historically in an NBA city, but in recent drafts in either a theater in the New York area or at the [Barclays Center](1658814.md) each summer, is only two rounds long (60 picks). Instead of automatically granting the top pick to the worst team from the year before, the NBA holds a [draft lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_draft_lottery) to determine who chooses first. The top four picks are allocated by chance among the 14 teams that did not make the [playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playoffs) the year before. This discourages a team from losing on purpose to get a better draft pick, but also causes other controversies such as allegations and [conspiracy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_theory) suggesting that the draft lottery is rigged.

NBA teams choose players from the [NCAA](60706.md) and from teams overseas. It was formerly common for players to be chosen directly from high school, but in 2006, the NBA required that players wait a year after high school before playing in the NBA. Almost all top U.S. players thus play at least one year in college.

### NHL entry draft

Main article: [NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_entry_draft)

The NHL operates a seven-round off-season draft (224 picks). Like the NBA, the NHL uses a [lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lottery) system to determine which team gets the top pick. All 16 teams, up from 15 between 2017 and 2021, that failed to qualify for the playoffs take part in the weighted lottery with the winner moving up to select first overall. Any North American player aged 18–20, and any overseas player aged 18–21 is eligible to be selected. Players are generally chosen from [junior hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_hockey) teams, high schools, the NCAA and overseas clubs.

The NHL rotates the draft's location among cities with teams in the league. Like baseball, players drafted in the entry draft usually have to wait a few years in development, either in junior hockey or the minor leagues, before cracking an NHL roster; usually, only one or two draft picks, generally, those that are widely predicted to be blue-chip superstars, jump directly from the draft to the NHL (e.g. [Sidney Crosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Crosby) or [Jaromír Jágr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarom%C3%ADr_J%C3%A1gr)).

The league has incrementally expanded the draft lottery process over the course of the early 2010s to discourage "tanking"—the act of deliberately losing to get a better draft pick. Historically, the league only subjected the first overall pick to the draft lottery among the five worst teams in the league, meaning that if a draft had more than one marquee prospect (a generally rare occurrence), it would still be a viable strategy to tank, as a second overall pick was still guaranteed. This was made evident in the [2015 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_NHL_entry_draft), where marquee prospects [Connor McDavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_McDavid) and [Jack Eichel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Eichel) were both seen as NHL-ready and likely to be stars; much speculation surrounded the struggling [Buffalo Sabres](69918.md), who allegedly tanked to secure the rights to at least the second overall pick (a charge that, although the Sabres' fans openly encouraged it, the team officially denied). From the [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NHL_entry_draft) to [2020 drafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_NHL_entry_draft), the top *three* overall picks were subject to lottery among all fourteen teams that did not make the playoffs, meaning the last-place team will only be assured of the fourth overall pick, at which point virtually any marquee prospect will have already been selected. Since the [2021 draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NHL_entry_draft), only two draws are held for the first two selections. Starting with the [2022 lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NHL_entry_draft), the team winning one of the two lotteries is only allowed to move up a maximum of ten spots in the draft order and teams will only be allowed to win the lottery twice in a five-year period.[^cite_note-7]

The three major junior leagues that make up the [Canadian Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Hockey_League) also hold drafts of teenage players in their territories.

### MLB draft

Main article: [Major League Baseball draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_draft)

Major League Baseball holds two drafts each year. In June, the first-year player draft, MLB's entry draft, takes place. Only players from Canada, the U.S. or a U.S. territory may be drafted; players from elsewhere are free agents and can be signed by any team. Draftees are high-school graduates who have opted not to go to college, [college baseball](1370306.md) players at four-year institutions who have played three years or turned 21, or [junior college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_college) baseball players. As of 2021, the draft lasts 20 rounds (down from 40 in 2019 with the exception of a 5-round 2020 draft), but earlier drafts have lasted up to 100 rounds. The MLB draft generally receives less attention than the drafts in other American sports, since drafted players usually spend several years in the [minor leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_leagues) before they crack the Major League team's roster. Also, unlike the MLS, NFL, NBA and the NHL Drafts, the MLB draft takes place during the season instead of in the offseason.

In December, MLB holds the much shorter [Rule 5 draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_5_draft). If an organization keeps a player in the minor leagues for a certain number of years, other teams can draft him in the Rule 5 draft. The drafting team must keep the player on its major league roster; it cannot put the player in its own [minor leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_league) system.

### MLS Draft

Main article: [MLS SuperDraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_SuperDraft)

Major League Soccer has two types of draft that occur each year, plus a third that is held intermittently when the league [adds one or more teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_of_Major_League_Soccer). The two annual drafts are the three-round long [MLS SuperDraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_SuperDraft) (a maximum of 81 picks since 2021, down from four rounds and 104 picks in 2020), which was held for the first time in 2001, and [MLS Supplemental Draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Supplemental_Draft), with the MLS Expansion Draft held in the offseason immediately before the league expands.

The MLS SuperDraft happens every January. During the draft the teams from the league will pick up to three players each from colleges across the United States. Only players from the American college sports system (e.g., the [NCAA](60706.md) and the [NAIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Intercollegiate_Athletics)) are eligible to be drafted. Canadian [U Sports men's soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Sports_men's_soccer) players are not included.

The draft is divided into three rounds in which each club has one selection.

In addition to drafted players, MLS teams also acquire new players from their [youth academies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_system). These players are called homegrown players. Homegrown players are academy players who are given a first-team contract. They are required to have spent at least one year in a club's academy, and to have lived in the club's territory (for example, in the case of [Orlando City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando,_Florida), northern Florida).

The draft gives to the teams opportunity to acquire players from other regions of the country, from outside their territory.

The teams can also acquire new players by signing players from other leagues (usually foreigner leagues), generally young promising players from Latin America and veterans from Europe. MLS has two [transfer windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_windows)—the primary pre-season transfer window lasts three months from mid February until mid May, and the secondary mid season transfer window runs one month from early July to early August.

### NWSL drafts

Main article: [List of drafts held by the NWSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_drafts_held_by_the_NWSL)

The [National Women's Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women's_Soccer_League) used drafts from its [inaugural 2013 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_National_Women's_Soccer_League_season) until the [2024 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_National_Women's_Soccer_League_season). The first was a [draft of college players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_NWSL_College_Draft), followed by a [supplemental draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_NWSL_Supplemental_Draft). The final draft of 2013 was a preseason waiver draft in which teams could select players who had been waived by their first squads, but only one player was selected at that time.

The league held a college draft, referred to after 2021 simply as the [NWSL Draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL_Draft) after the inclusion of non-collegiate selections,[^cite_note-11] prior to each league season. The waiver draft, now called the "re-entry wire", has also become a permanent part of the league. The league also created other draft mechanisms to address other types of eligibility, such as the unsubsidized individual distribution ranking order introduced for college draft-ineligible [NWSL federation players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NWSL_federation_players) in 2016,[^cite_note-12] and the under-18 entry list for domestic minors introduced in 2023.[^cite_note-cbs-draft-2023-13] With league expansion and contraction since its establishment, the league has also irregularly held both [expansion drafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NWSL_Expansion_Draft) to help stock the rosters of new teams,[^cite_note-nwsl-draft-2022-14] and a [dispersal draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NWSL_Dispersal_Draft) in which teams could select players from a [defunct team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Breakers).[^cite_note-15]

On August 22, 2024, the NWSL announced the elimination of its draft and discovery systems as part of its renegotiated [collective bargaining agreement](12728750.md) with the [National Women's Soccer League Players Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women's_Soccer_League_Players_Association). NWSL teams could pursue and sign any prospective collegiate and international players as free agents, transfers between NWSL teams would require player consent, and all contracts would become guaranteed, bringing the league closer in line with global standards within the sport.[^cite_note-draft-end-16][^cite_note-17]

### WNBA draft

Main article: [WNBA draft](849824.md)

The [WNBA draft](849824.md) is held every spring. It has had several locations during its history; the most recent draft in 2022 took place in New York City, following two drafts held virtually due to [COVID-19](63136490.md) concerns. The draft is currently three rounds long with each of the 12 teams in the league (trades aside) making three picks each. Draft order for teams that made the playoffs the previous year are based on team records. The team with the highest previous record will pick last. Since eight teams qualify for [playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBA_Playoffs), the bottom eight picks are determined by this method. For the remaining top four picks, a selection process similar to the NBA Draft Lottery is conducted for the four teams that did not qualify for the playoffs.

### WWE Draft

Main article: [WWE Draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWE_Draft)

The WWE Draft is used to refresh [WWE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWE)'s [Raw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raw_\(WWE_brand\)) and [SmackDown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SmackDown_\(WWE_brand\)) rosters, as well as shuffle talent between the two.

## South Asia

### IPL auction

[Cricket](25675557.md)'s [Indian Premier League](13260340.md) instead holds a yearly [auction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auction) before the start of each season in which teams bid on players, subject to a [salary cap](364837.md) and restrictions on roster size.

### ISL draft

[Indian Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Super_League) is responsible for holding drafts prior to the commencement of each season. Teams can buy foreign players with international experience while they still can purchase Indian players with little or no experience.

### PSL draft

The [Pakistan Super League](38199013.md) uses a draft system for player recruitment before the start of every season to fairly distribute the league's new players. Teams are allotted slots in every round of the draft and slots can be exchanged with other teams.

## Southeast Asia

### PBA draft

Main article: [Philippine Basketball Association draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Basketball_Association_draft)

The [Philippine Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Basketball_Association) (PBA) draft is an annual event in the PBA calendar in which teams can acquire new players outside the league which are not free agents. Only [natural-born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_nationality_law) [Filipinos](2298105.md) and [foreign nationals with Filipino ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_diaspora), whether or not they acquired Philippine citizenship, are eligible to be drafted. The draft began in 1985; prior to that teams directly hired [rookies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rookie).

Until 2014, there was a draft lottery between the two worst team of the preceding season. This was scrapped after the events of the [2014 draft lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_PBA_draft#Controversy).

The [PBA Developmental League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBA_Developmental_League) also has their own draft.

## Other terminology

### Draft bust

A *draft bust* is a highly touted or highly selected draftee who does not meet expectations. This can be for a variety of reasons such as being unable to adjust to the professional level or if their career was derailed by injuries or personal issues. A player is also regarded as a bust if more successful players are drafted after them.

#### NFL

First-, second-, and third-round picks are considered high draft picks drafted to be long-term starters for the team that selected them. NFL players who are drafted in this range and fail to develop into starters for their original team are thus considered busts. This label particularly applies for 1st-round picks, who are considered to be premium talents. One of the most frequently cited examples of a draft bust in the NFL is [Ryan Leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Leaf), who was selected second overall in the [1998 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_NFL_draft) by the [San Diego Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Chargers), after the [Indianapolis Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Colts) had selected future Hall of Famer [Peyton Manning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton_Manning). The Chargers had chosen Leaf over future Hall of Famers [Charles Woodson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Woodson), [Randy Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Moss), and [Alan Faneca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Faneca), as well as six future [Pro Bowlers](274117.md).

Despite being described as being equal in talent to Manning prior to the draft, Leaf only managed to play two seasons with the Chargers and start 18 games (winning only four) for them before being released in 2000. Leaf played only three further games for the [Dallas Cowboys](8121.md), and was out of the league by 2002.[^cite_note-ArgumentESPN-18][^cite_note-BiggestBust-19][^cite_note-20]

[Tony Mandarich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Mandarich) and [JaMarcus Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JaMarcus_Russell), two other frequently cited draft busts, are examples of players whose status as busts are amplified by their placements in the draft:

- Mandarich is the only top-five pick from the [1989 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_NFL_draft) who is not in the Hall of Fame, having been selected second overall by the [Green Bay Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers) after the [Dallas Cowboys](8121.md) selected [Troy Aikman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_Aikman), with the subsequent three picks being [Barry Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Sanders), [Derrick Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrick_Thomas), and [Deion Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deion_Sanders). The Packers also passed on Hall of Famer [Steve Atwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Atwater) as well as six future Pro Bowlers to select Mandarich.

- Russell was selected first overall in the [2007 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NFL_draft) by the [Oakland Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Raiders), with the next two picks being Hall of Famers [Calvin Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Johnson) and [Joe Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Thomas_\(offensive_tackle\)). The Raiders also passed on Hall of Famers [Darrelle Revis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darrelle_Revis) and [Patrick Willis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Willis) as well as Pro Bowlers [Adrian Peterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Peterson), [Marshawn Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshawn_Lynch), [Eric Weddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Weddle), [Ryan Kalil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Kalil), [Greg Olsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Olsen_\(American_football\)), [Joe Staley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Staley), and [Marshal Yanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshal_Yanda) to select Russell.

  [^cite_note-CBS_Sports-21]

Like Leaf, Russell was released by the Raiders after three seasons and was out of the league by 2010. Mandarich later managed to become a serviceable offensive lineman for the [Indianapolis Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianapolis_Colts) after being released by the Packers in 1992, but ultimately never lived up to his draft position.

#### NBA

In the NBA, one of the most well-known examples of a draft bust is [LaRue Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaRue_Martin), who was selected first overall in the [1972 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_NBA_draft) by the [Portland Trail Blazers](72897.md): the Trail Blazers passed on [Hall of Famers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naismith_Memorial_Basketball_Hall_of_Fame) [Bob McAdoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_McAdoo), [Paul Westphal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Westphal), and [Julius Erving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Erving)[^cite_note-46] to select Martin. #### MLB

Notable players widely considered draft busts include [Steve Chilcott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Chilcott) (1966), [Brien Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brien_Taylor) (1991), and [Brady Aiken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brady_Aiken) (2014), all of whom were 1st overall draft picks who never reached the majors.

A well-known example is pitcher [David Clyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Clyde), the first overall pick in the [1973 draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Major_League_Baseball_draft) by the [Texas Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Rangers_\(baseball\)). Despite being only 18 years old at the time of his selection, the Rangers called-up Clyde directly to the majors instead of developing him in the minors. After 2 mediocre seasons with the Rangers, Clyde was sent down to the minors and would not return to the majors until 1978 after he was traded to the [Cleveland Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Guardians). After 2 seasons with the Indians, Clyde retired at only 26 years old due to shoulder injuries. Clyde's career made him the "poster-boy" for bringing up young players prematurely. The Rangers passed on future Hall of Famers [Robin Yount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Yount), [Dave Winfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Winfield), and [Eddie Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Murray) to take Clyde.

[Matt Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Bush_\(baseball\)) (2004), another 1st overall pick, dealt with legal troubles culminating in incarceration and waited nearly 12 years to make the major leagues, eventually doing so as a middle relief pitcher in 2016.

Pitcher [Mark Appel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Appel), the first overall pick in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Major_League_Baseball_draft), stepped away from baseball after 2018 due to repeated struggles on the mound. He returned to the game in 2021, but did not make his major league debut until the [2022 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Major_League_Baseball_season) for the [Philadelphia Phillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies), appearing in only six games and has not pitched in the majors since.[^cite_note-66]

### Draft steal

Conversely, a player who was drafted at a low spot or in later rounds and is expected to have little success yet goes on to have a stellar and productive career is known as a *draft steal*.

#### MLB

[Mike Piazza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Piazza), who went on to become one of the best catchers of the 1990s, a 12-time [MLB](38776.md) [All-Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_All-Star_Game) selection and a [Hall of Famer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Baseball_Hall_of_Fame_and_Museum), was chosen in the 62nd round (1390th overall)[^cite_note-90] of the [1988 MLB draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Major_League_Baseball_draft) and was selected only as a favor to [Tommy Lasorda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Lasorda) (whose team, the Los Angeles Dodgers, drafted Piazza): to further put the pick in historic perspective, the MLB draft is now much shorter, having most recently been reduced from 40 rounds to 20 in 2021.

[José Bautista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Bautista) was selected in the 20th round, 599th overall by the [Pittsburgh Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Pirates) in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Major_League_Baseball_Draft). Although his major league career started off slowly in 2004, Bautista, after being traded to the [Toronto Blue Jays](30847.md) in 2008, experienced a breakthrough season in 2010. He led the majors in [home runs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_run) in 2010 and 2011 and would make the American League All-Star Team six consecutive seasons from 2010-2015.

#### NFL

One of the most notable examples is [Tom Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Brady), who was drafted late in the sixth round (at 199th overall pick) of the [2000 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_NFL_draft) by the then-mediocre [New England Patriots](21719.md) only as a fourth-string backup [quarterback](25277.md), but went on to have a two-decade career winning seven [Super Bowls](27718.md) (six with the Patriots and one with the [Tampa Bay Buccaneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Buccaneers)), five [Super Bowl MVPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_Most_Valuable_Player_Award), and three [NFL MVPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_MVP), setting the all-time records for wins, passing yards, completions, and touchdowns in the process. Similarly, two-time [Super Bowl](27718.md) champion [Roger Staubach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Staubach) was a tenth-round pick in both the AFL and NFL drafts, largely due to having a four-year commitment to serve in the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md).

[Pro Football Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame) quarterbacks that were draft steals include [Johnny Unitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Unitas), who was drafted in the ninth round of the [1955 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_NFL_draft) by the Pittsburgh Steelers, who cut him at the end of the team's training camp, while [Bart Starr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bart_Starr), a two-time Super Bowl MVP and five-time championship winner, was the 200th overall pick [in 1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_NFL_draft) due to concerns about back injury problems.[^cite_note-91] [Joe Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Montana) and [Dan Fouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Fouts) were third-round selections, with Montana slipping due to an average combine performance.

Wide receiver [Terrell Owens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrell_Owens) was selected in the third round (89th overall) by the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers) in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_NFL_draft). Owens went on to play 17 seasons in the NFL and became a six-time [Pro Bowler](274117.md) and a five-time [All-Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Pro) and led the NFL in touchdown receptions three times. Owens was inducted into the [Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame) in 2018 and is widely considered one of the greatest wide receivers in NFL history.&#x20;

Also in the 1996 draft, [linebacker](492475.md) [Zach Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Thomas), who was considered too "small", "short", and "unathletic" to play the linebacker position at the pro level, was selected in the fifth round with the 154th overall pick by the [Miami Dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Dolphins). He would go on to be one of the best and most consistent linebackers of his era, anchoring a Top 10 ranked Miami defense in eight out of his 12 years in Miami, while being selected to seven Pro Bowls, five first team [All-Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Pro) selections, and the [NFL 2000s All-Decade Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_2000s_All-Decade_Team). Thomas was inducted to the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 2023.

The [2017 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_NFL_draft) has seen two draft steals emerge. Wide receiver [Cooper Kupp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Kupp), who was selected in the third round (69th overall) by the [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams), has the most receptions, touchdown catches, and receiving yards of any wide receiver or player drafted in 2017 (as of the conclusion of the 2022 NFL season), including the six wide receivers that were drafted ahead of him.[^cite_note-92] Furthermore, in 2021, Kupp won the receiving triple crown, AP Offensive Player of the Year Award, and [Super Bowl LVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVI) MVP in what is regarded as one of the greatest seasons by a wide receiver in NFL history. [Tight end](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tight_end) [George Kittle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Kittle) was selected in the fifth round (146th overall) by the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers). As of the end of the [2024 NFL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NFL_season), Kittle has been selected to six [Pro Bowl](274117.md) teams and five [All-Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Pro) teams and was a key piece in the 49ers reaching Super Bowls [LIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LIV) and [LVIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVIII).

Wide receiver [Tyreek Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyreek_Hill), was selected in the fifth round (165th overall) of the [2016 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NFL_draft) by the [Kansas City Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs), has been named to eight Pro Bowls, six with Chiefs two with the Dolphins, was included in the [NFL 2010s All-Decade Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_2010s_All-Decade_Team), was a 4 time [All-Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Pro) receiver from 2018 to 2023 and also an [All-Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Pro) first-team punt returner in his rookie year in 2016, led the Chiefs to their first Super Bowl win in 50 years [Super Bowl LIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LIV) and was the key member to the Chief's dynasty until he was traded in 2022 to the [Miami Dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Dolphins).

[Antonio Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Brown) was drafted 195th overall by the [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers) in 2010 but was an [All-Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Pro) receiver from 2013 to 2018, before being traded to the [Oakland Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Raiders) in 2019 (later getting released by them and the [New England Patriots](21719.md) over accusations of sexual assault). Brown would eventually help the [Tampa Bay Buccaneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Buccaneers) win [Super Bowl LV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LV).

[Cornerback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornerback) [Richard Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Sherman_\(American_football\)) was selected in the 5th round, 154th overall by the [Seattle Seahawks](28390.md) in the [2011 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NFL_draft). In his eleven year career, Sherman was selected to five [All-Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Pro) and five [Pro-Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-Bowl) teams. Sherman also helped the Seahawks reach Super Bowls [XLVIII](4057023.md) and [XLIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLIX) with the Seahawks winning the former. After signing with the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers) in 2018, Sherman helped them reach [Super Bowl LIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LIV).

Also taken in 2011 was [center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_\(gridiron_football\)) [Jason Kelce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Kelce) who was selected in the sixth round, 191st overall by the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles). Kelce went on to play thirteen seasons with the Eagles, being selected to seven [Pro Bowl](274117.md) teams and six [All-Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Pro) teams, and is widely considered one of the greatest centers in NFL history. Kelce also helped the Eagles reach two [Super Bowls](27718.md) and won [Super Bowl LII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LII). Similarly, Jason's brother [Travis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Kelce) was a third-round selection by the [Kansas City Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs), but went on to become a seven-time All-Pro while setting multiple receiving records for the tight end position and helping the Chiefs win Super Bowls [LIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LIV), [LVII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVII), and [LVIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVIII).

Wide receiver [Amon-Ra St. Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amon-Ra_St._Brown) was drafted in the fourth round at 112th overall of the [2021 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NFL_draft) by the [Detroit Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Lions). St. Brown played a key part in the rebuilding of the Lions organization, led by head coach [Dan Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Campbell), that turned them from a struggling team into a [Super Bowl](27718.md) contender.[^cite_note-93]

Quarterback [Brock Purdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brock_Purdy) was selected [last overall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Irrelevant) in the [2022 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NFL_draft) by the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers). Initially a third-string backup, he eventually was moved to starter after injuries to [Trey Lance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trey_Lance) and [Jimmy Garoppolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Garoppolo). Purdy has since led the 49ers to an NFC Championship Game in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NFL_season) and an appearance in [Super Bowl LVIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LVIII) in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NFL_season), leading the NFL in [passer rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passer_rating), garnering a Pro Bowl spot, and being an MVP finalist in the latter season.

#### NBA

[Manu Ginóbili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manu_Gin%C3%B3bili), a key contributor to four [San Antonio Spurs](72889.md) championships in the 21st century, was the next-to-last pick (57th) in the [1999 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NBA_draft). He was also the centerpiece of [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_national_basketball_team)'s national team in the [2004 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_at_the_2004_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament), where he led his country to a gold medal and was named the [MVP of the tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Men's_Olympics_Most_Valuable_Player).

Another notable draft steal is [Isaiah Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Thomas_\(basketball\)), who was selected as the 60th and last pick in the [2011 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NBA_draft), but emerged as an All-Star in 2017 and led the Boston Celtics to the first seed in the Eastern Conference that same year. He also finished 5th in MVP voting in 2017 with an average of 28.9 points and 5.9 assists.

[Draymond Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draymond_Green), who was selected as the 35th pick in the [2012 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_NBA_draft), was a key contributor to the [Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors) winning four championships. Green has been named to multiple All-Star selections and also won the [NBA Defensive Player of the Year Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Defensive_Player_of_the_Year_Award) in 2017.

[Nikola Jokić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_Joki%C4%87), a Serbian who was selected by the [Denver Nuggets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Nuggets) as the 41st pick in the [2014 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_NBA_draft), remained in Europe for the 2014–15 season to develop his skills before arriving in the NBA in 2015. In his second season, he quickly became a solid post playmaker, averaging about 16.7 ppg, 4.9 apg, and 9.8 rpg. He was also named to the All-NBA Team in both 2019 (first team) and 2020 (second team), and won [league MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) in 2021, 2022 and 2024 while leading the Nuggets to their first championship in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NBA_Finals). He was the first 2nd round pick to win MVP since [Willis Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willis_Reed) in 1970; the latter was the 8th pick in the [1964 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_NBA_draft). Jokić was also a crucial member of [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_men's_national_basketball_team)'s national team in the [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_at_the_2016_Summer_Olympics_-_Men's_tournament) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_-_Men's_tournament) Summer Olympics, where he won silver and bronze medals respectively. Jokic's selection was notably preempted by a [Taco Bell](37563.md) ad during ESPN's coverage of the draft.[^cite_note-94]

[DeAndre Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeAndre_Jordan), who was selected as the 35th pick in the [2008 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_NBA_draft), was named All-NBA three times in his career, with three straight selections from 2015 to 2017, including a first team appearance in 2016, was selected to back-to-back All-Defense teams in 2014-15 and 2015-16, became an All-Star in 2017 and led the league in rebounding two years in a row (2014, 2015). He became a significant contributor of the "[Lob city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lob_city) Clippers" era for the [Los Angeles Clippers](72892.md), along with [Blake Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_Griffin) and [Chris Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Paul). Jordan would win alongside Jokić and the Denver Nuggets in the 2023 NBA Finals.

[Kyle Korver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Korver), who was selected as the 51st pick in the [2003 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_NBA_draft), went to have a successful 17-year career in the NBA. He was an All-Star in 2015 and is one of the most prolific 3-point shooters in league history, [ranking eighth all-time in 3-point field goals made](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Basketball_Association_career_3-point_scoring_leaders) with 2,450.

[Jordan Clarkson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Clarkson), who was selected as the 46th pick in the [2014 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_NBA_draft), won the [NBA Sixth Man of the Year Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Sixth_Man_of_the_Year_Award) with the [Utah Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Jazz) in 2021, and helped the [New York Knicks](72855.md) end their 53-year championship drought in the [2026 NBA Finals](82427430.md).

[Malcolm Brogdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Brogdon), who was the 36th pick in the [2016 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NBA_draft), won the [Rookie of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Rookie_of_the_Year_Award) award in 2017 and became a member of the exclusive [50–40–90 club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50%E2%80%9340%E2%80%9390_club) in 2019. He also won the [NBA Sixth Man of the Year Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Sixth_Man_of_the_Year_Award) in 2023.

[Jalen Brunson](43132078.md), who was selected as the 33rd pick in the [2018 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NBA_draft), went on to have a breakout year in 2024, making his first All-Star and All-NBA team during the 2023-24 season, and led the Knicks to their first championship since [1973](4875070.md) in the 2026 NBA Finals.

[Marc Gasol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Gasol) was selected as the 48th pick by the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) in the [2007 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NBA_draft), but was traded to the [Memphis Grizzlies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis_Grizzlies) in a package that sent his older brother [Pau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pau_Gasol) to the Lakers. Like his brother Pau, he became an elite rim protector who could pass, shoot, and score efficiently in the post, and later in his career became an example of a "stretch five", a center capable of effective scoring from [three-point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-point_field_goal) range. He was also the 2013 [NBA Defensive Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Defensive_Player_of_the_Year_Award), and he helped the [Toronto Raptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Raptors) win their first championship in 2019.

#### NHL

The [1984 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_NHL_entry_draft) is noted for the unusually high number of future Hall of Famers picked, particularly in lower rounds: [Patrick Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Roy), the only player with three [Conn Smythe trophies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conn_Smythe_Trophy), was drafted 51st in the third round; [Brett Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Hull), the fifth highest goal scorer of all time, was drafted 117th in the sixth round; and [Luc Robitaille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc_Robitaille), who retired as the highest-scoring left winger in the NHL, was drafted 171st in the ninth round.

Hall of Famer [Doug Gilmour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Gilmour) was drafted by the [St. Louis Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Blues) in the seventh round, 134th overall at the [1982 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_NHL_entry_draft); during his career he scored over 1,400 points, won a [Stanley Cup](66968.md) and [Frank J. Selke Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_J._Selke_Trophy).

[Pavel Datsyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Datsyuk), drafted 171st in the [1998 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_NHL_entry_draft), won two Stanley Cups and is considered one of the most talented NHL players of all-time.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-\:1-96][^cite_note-\:2-97]

The [Detroit Red Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Red_Wings) have drafted [Nicklas Lidström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicklas_Lidstr%C3%B6m) (53rd in [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_NHL_entry_draft)), [Sergei Fedorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Fedorov) (74th in 1989), [Vladimir Konstantinov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Konstantinov) (221st in 1989), [Tomas Holmström](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomas_Holmstr%C3%B6m) (257th in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_NHL_entry_draft)) and [Henrik Zetterberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Zetterberg) (210th in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NHL_entry_draft)) in later rounds, all of whom contributed to one or more of the four Stanley Cup wins, between 1997 and 2008, by the Red Wings.[^cite_note-\:1-96][^cite_note-\:2-97]

A similar Stanley Cup legacy was achieved by the [Tampa Bay Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Lightning), drafting [Alex Killorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Killorn) (77th in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NHL_entry_draft)), [Nikita Kucherov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Kucherov) (58th in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NHL_entry_draft)), [Ondřej Palát](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ond%C5%99ej_Pal%C3%A1t) (208th in 2011), [Cédric Paquette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9dric_Paquette) (101st in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_NHL_entry_draft)), [Brayden Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brayden_Point) (79th in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_NHL_entry_draft)), [Anthony Cirelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Cirelli) (72nd in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_NHL_entry_draft)), [Mathieu Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathieu_Joseph) (120th in 2015) and [Ross Colton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Colton) (118th in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NHL_entry_draft)), all of whom contributed to back-to-back Stanley Cups wins by the Lightning in 2020 and 2021.[^cite_note-98]

The [Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Oilers) [dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_dynasty) that won 5 Stanley Cups between 1984 to 1990 had several notable draft steals, drafting [Mark Messier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Messier) (48th in [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_NHL_entry_draft)), [Glenn Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Anderson) (69th in 1979), [Jari Kurri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jari_Kurri) (69th in [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_NHL_entry_draft)), [Andy Moog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Moog) (132nd in 1980), [Steve Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Smith_\(ice_hockey,_born_April_30,_1963\)) (111th in [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_NHL_entry_draft)), and [Esa Tikkanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esa_Tikkanen) (80th in [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_NHL_entry_draft)). All of whom contributed to 1 of the 5 Stanley Cup wins between 1984 to 1990.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

[Vezina Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vezina_Trophy) winners [Dominik Hašek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominik_Ha%C5%A1ek) (drafted 199th in [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_NHL_entry_draft)), [Tim Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Thomas_\(ice_hockey,_born_1974\)) (drafted 217th in 1994), [Miikka Kiprusoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miikka_Kiprusoff) (drafted 116th in [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_NHL_entry_draft)), [Ryan Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Miller) (drafted 138th in 1999), [Henrik Lundqvist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Lundqvist) (drafted 205th in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_NHL_entry_draft)), [Pekka Rinne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pekka_Rinne) (drafted 258th in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_NHL_entry_draft)), [Braden Holtby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braden_Holtby) (drafted 93rd in 2008), [Connor Hellebuyck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_Hellebuyck) (drafted 130th in 2012), [Linus Ullmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Ullmark) (drafted 163rd in 2012), and [Igor Shesterkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Shesterkin) (drafted 118th in 2014) were considered some of the best goalie draft steals, with Thomas winning the [Conn Smythe Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conn_Smythe_Trophy) as the most valuable player of the [2011 Stanley Cup playoffs](26981832.md) while being the lowest-drafted player to win the award and setting a league record for the most saves by a goaltender in a single postseason.

[Pavel Bure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Bure) was drafted by the [Vancouver Canucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks) in the 6th round, 113th overall, at the [1989 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_NHL_entry_draft). Due to international transfer disputes which prompted the Canucks to draft Bure later, he had his rookie season two seasons later in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_NHL_season), where he would win the [Calder Memorial Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calder_Memorial_Trophy) for being the most outstanding rookie of that season. Later in his career, Bure won the [Maurice "Rocket" Richard Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_%22Rocket%22_Richard_Trophy) for being the leading goal scorer in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_NHL_season) and [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000-01_NHL_season).

[Dave Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Taylor_\(ice_hockey\)) was drafted by the [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings) in the 15th round, 210th overall, at the [1975 NHL amateur draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_NHL_amateur_draft). Taylor would have a productive 17 season career in the NHL as part of the [Triple Crown Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Crown_Line), and still holds the record for being the lowest drafted player to score more than 1,000 points in the NHL.

[Theoren Fleury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoren_Fleury) was drafted by the [Calgary Flames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Flames) in the 8th round, 166th overall, at the [1987 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_NHL_entry_draft), then scored over 1,000 points, while playing 1,000 games in the NHL between 1989 and 2003.

[Daniel Alfredsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Alfredsson) was drafted by the [Ottawa Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Senators) in the 6th round, 133rd overall, at the [1994 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_NHL_entry_draft), in Alfredsson near 20 year career he recorded over 1,000 points and played in over 1,000 games, and help lead the Senators to their first Stanley Cup Final appearance in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Stanley_Cup_Final).[^cite_note-\:1-96]

[Joe Pavelski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Pavelski) was selected by the [San Jose Sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Sharks) in the seventh round, 205th overall, of the [2003 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_NHL_entry_draft), has become one of the best American-born players of his generation. He recorded 1,068 points in 1,332 regular-season NHL games.

[Jamie Benn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Benn) was selected by the [Dallas Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Stars) in the 5th round, 129th overall in 2007, became the franchise's sixth captain in 2013, and won the [Art Ross Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Ross_Trophy) in 2015.

[Patric Hörnqvist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patric_H%C3%B6rnqvist) was drafted last by the [Nashville Predators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Predators) at the [2005 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_NHL_entry_draft) (230th in the 7th round), then won two Stanley Cups with the [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins).

[Mark Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Stone) was drafted in the 6th round, 178th overall by the [Ottawa Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Senators), in the [2010 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_NHL_entry_draft), and has been nominated for the [Frank J. Selke Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_J._Selke_Trophy) (awarded to the league's best defensive forward) as a member and now captain of the [Vegas Golden Knights](47712000.md), Stone lead the Golden Knights to their first Stanley Cup win in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Stanley_Cup_Final).

[Kirill Kaprizov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirill_Kaprizov) was drafted in the 5th round, 135th overall by the [Minnesota Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Wild). Kaprizov remained in his native [Russia](25391.md) to play in the [Kontinental Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kontinental_Hockey_League) for four seasons to develop his skills before making his NHL debut in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_NHL_season) where he won the [Calder Memorial Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calder_Memorial_Trophy) as rookie of the year. Since then, Kaprizov has been selected as an all-star game player three times in his career.

Late [Calgary Flames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Flames) and [Columbus Blue Jackets](73128.md) forward [Johnny Gaudreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Gaudreau), who was selected in the 4th round, 104th overall by the Flames in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NHL_entry_draft), opted to play [college ice hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_ice_hockey) in the [National Collegiate Athletic Association](60706.md) for three years where he won many accolades in the process, including one [Hobey Baker Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hobey_Baker_Award) for being the most outstanding player in college hockey. Gaudreau had his first full NHL season in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_NHL_season), where he was named to the [All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_National_Hockey_League_All-Star_Game) and was a finalist of the Calder Memorial Trophy. Later in his career, Gaudreau would be named to six more all-star games, win the [Lady Byng Memorial Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Byng_Memorial_Trophy) for being the most sportsmanlike player of the [2016–17 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_NHL_season), and be named to the [First All-Star Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_All-Star_team) in the [2021–22 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_NHL_season), where he finished second in league scoring with 115 points (40 goals and 75 assists).

#### Australian Football League

An example of a draft steal is [Hawthorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawthorn_Football_Club) midfielder [Sam Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Mitchell_\(footballer\))[^cite_note-afl.com.au-87][^cite_note-foxsports.com.au-102] who was drafted with pick 36 in the [2001 AFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_AFL_draft). Mitchell went on to become a four-time premiership player, a premiership captain with Hawthorn in 2008, a [2012 Brownlow Medalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Brownlow_Medal), a three-time [All-Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Australian_team), a five-time [Peter Crimmins Medallist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Crimmins_Medal) and the 2003 [AFL Rising Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Rising_Star).

Another example of a draft steal in the AFL is former [Essendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essendon_Football_Club) midfielder [James Hird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hird),[^cite_note-afl.com.au-87][^cite_note-foxsports.com.au-102][^cite_note-ReferenceA-103] who was drafted with pick 79 in the [1990 AFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_AFL_draft). Hird went on to become a two time premiership player, a premiership captain in 2000, the [1996 Brownlow Medalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Brownlow_Medal), the 2000 [Norm Smith Medallist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norm_Smith_Medal), a five-time [All-Australian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Australian_team), a five-time [W. S. Crichton Medalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._S._Crichton_Medal), and a member of the Essendon Team of the Century and the [Australian Football Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Football_Hall_of_Fame).

Another notable AFL draft steal is [Collingwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collingwood_Football_Club) midfielder [Dane Swan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dane_Swan),[^cite_note-foxsports.com.au-102][^cite_note-ReferenceA-103] who was drafted with pick 58 in the 2001 AFL draft. Swan became a premiership player with Collingwood, a three-time [Copeland Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copeland_Trophy) winner, the [2011 Brownlow Medalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Brownlow_Medal) and a five-time All-Australian.

Another notable AFL draft steal was [West Coast Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_Eagles) midfielder [Matt Priddis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Priddis). Priddis was drafted with the 31st Pick in the [2005 Rookie Draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_AFL_Draft) after being overlooked in the previous three national drafts. Priddis would go on to be the winner of the 2014 [Brownlow Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownlow_Medal), the first player to do this who was originally drafted in the Rookie draft. In his final season (2017), Priddis became the all-time league leader in tackles and became the first person to reach 1,500 tackles. Priddis also became just the second player to win both the Brownlow Medal and [Sandover Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandover_Medal).

### Other

[Mr. Irrelevant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Irrelevant) is a title given to the last player selected in each year's NFL Draft. The phrase pokes fun at the typically poor chances such a player has of ultimately making an impact in the league, although several went on to productive or notable NFL careers, such as [Ryan Succop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Succop) and [Brock Purdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brock_Purdy).

Some unusual draft picks in professional sports history have included [Taro Tsujimoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taro_Tsujimoto), a fictional Japanese ice hockey forward who was drafted in the [1974 NHL amateur draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_NHL_amateur_draft) by the [Buffalo Sabres](69918.md) (a move made in protest of the league's decision to hold the draft by phone, in an attempt to discourage the rival [WHA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Hockey_Association)); actor [John Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wayne), who at age 64 was legally drafted by the [Atlanta Falcons](2103.md) in the [1971 NFL draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_NFL_draft);[^cite_note-merron20050418-104] and [Derrell Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derrell_Robertson), a man who was mistakenly drafted by the [Ottawa Rough Riders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Rough_Riders) in the 1995 CFL Dispersal Draft for the [Las Vegas Posse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Posse) after his death in the previous year.
