The **Merz cabinet** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Kabinett Merz* [\[kabiˈnɛt ˈmɛʁts\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German)) is the 25th and current [Government](1327803.md) of the [Federal Republic of Germany](11867.md) during the [21st legislative session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Bundestag) of the [Bundestag](3768.md). It succeeded the [previous cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholz_cabinet) led by [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz). The cabinet is led by [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md).

The cabinet is composed of Merz's [Christian Democratic Union](60584.md) (CDU), its Bavarian sister-party [Christian Social Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Social_Union_in_Bavaria) (CSU) (which form the [CDU/CSU](10796483.md) alliance; the so called *Union*) and the [Social Democratic Party](60585.md) (SPD). It is the fifth time a [governing coalition between Union and SPD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_coalition_\(Germany\)) has been formed in post-war German history and the first since the [Fourth Merkel cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Merkel_cabinet) led by then-Chancellor [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel) in 2018.[^cite_note-1]

## Coalition formation

See also: [List of participants in the Merz cabinet coalition negotiations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_participants_in_the_Merz_cabinet_coalition_negotiations)



Composition of the [21st Bundestag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Bundestag)

The Union (CDU/CSU) with its Chancellor candidate Friedrich Merz emerged from [2025 German federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_German_federal_election) as the strongest faction in the new Bundestag. However, the CDU/CSU needed coalition partners (as is usual in German politics) in order to form a majority government. The only possibility of forming a coalition with just one other party was a coalition with the SPD. Mathematically, a two-party coalition with the [AfD](38708229.md), which is generally considered to be on the far right, would also have been possible; however, the CDU/CSU had ruled this out before the election.[^cite_note-2]

For the coalition negotiations the CDU team included Friedrich Merz and other high-ranking officials such as CSU leader [Markus Söder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markus_S%C3%B6der), CDU General Secretary [Carsten Linnemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carsten_Linnemann) and CDU parliamentary group leader [Thorsten Frei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorsten_Frei), CSU parliamentary group leader [Alexander Dobrindt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dobrindt), the [Minister President of Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_minister-presidents_of_Saxony) [Michael Kretschmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kretschmer), the deputy chairwoman of the CDU [Karin Prien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karin_Prien) and CSU politician [Dorothee Bär](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothee_B%C3%A4r). The SPD delegation was made up of important personalities such as [Defence Minister](2563857.md) [Boris Pistorius](69097811.md) and [Labour Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_of_Labour_and_Social_Affairs) [Hubertus Heil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubertus_Heil), as well as the Secretary General [Matthias Miersch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Miersch), [Bundestag President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Bundestag) [Bärbel Bas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A4rbel_Bas), the two Minister Presidents [Manuela Schwesig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuela_Schwesig) and Anke Rehlinger, and the head of the [SPD in North Rhine-Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPD_North_Rhine-Westphalia) [Achim Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achim_Post).[^cite_note-\:36-3][^cite_note-\:37-4]

Both sides had originally wanted the talks not to commence until 5 March, in view of the [2025 Hamburg state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Hamburg_state_election) on 2 March, as well as [Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_in_Germany,_Switzerland_and_Austria) (peaking 3 March), which is very popular in some parts of Germany. Subsequently, however, Klingbeil and Merz agreed to an early start in their talks, so exploratory talks between the two parties began in [Berlin](3354.md) on 28 February 2025, five days after the election. Merz had set himself the target of forming a coalition by [Easter](9325.md).[^cite_note-5]

From the outset, there were differences of opinion between the CDU and SPD on the reform of the [debt brake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_balanced_budget_amendment) (which limited the German budget deficit to 0.35% of GDP per year), a possible tax reform, the minimum tax reform, the minimum wage, the citizen's income, [immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Germany), and a new right to vote. On 9 April, both SPD and the CDU/CSU parliamentary groups had reached a coalition agreement to form a new cabinet under [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md)’s chancellorship. The coalition government would consist of 10 ministers from the CDU/CSU,[^cite_note-“CDSU”-6] and 7 from SPD as per the agreement. Additionally, the [Vice Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-Chancellor_of_Germany) would be nominated by the SPD among 7 of its ministers.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

### Chancellor election

The Constitution (Basic Law) requires the [President of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Germany) to propose a Chancellor to the [President of the Bundestag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Bundestag) for approval by an absolute majority of [Bundestag](3768.md) members. Accordingly, after the party leaders of the [CDU](60584.md), [CSU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Social_Union_in_Bavaria) and [SPD](60585.md) signed their coalition agreement on 5 May, [Frank-Walter Steinmeier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank-Walter_Steinmeier) officially proposed [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md) to Bundestag President [Julia Klöckner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Kl%C3%B6ckner) for election as Chancellor, which was put to the vote the next day by [secret ballot](479210.md).[^cite_note-\:3-9] Although the [CDU/CSU](10796483.md) and [SPD](60585.md) had [328 of 630 members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Bundestag#List_of_current_members) of the Bundestag, in the first round of voting Merz received only 310 votes, below the required 316. This is the first time this has happened in the history of the [Federal Republic of Germany](11867.md). After the election result was announced, the session was interrupted.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-\:1-11][^cite_note-\:2-12][^cite_note-13]

According to [Article 63 (3) of the Basic Law](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_gg/englisch_gg.html#p0296), the election of the Chancellor was now in the second phase: within a period of 14 days, i.e. until 20 May, a quarter of the [members of the Bundestag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Bundestag#List_of_current_members) could nominate a candidate for [election as Federal Chancellor](3768.md#Electoral_term_and_principle_of_discontinuation). The number of [ballots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballot) is not predetermined and is theoretically unlimited. If a candidate is elected in one of these ballots, the President must appoint him or her. If no new Federal Chancellor is elected within this period, a final election must be held "without delay" (*unverzüglich*) in accordance with [Article 63 (4) of the Basic Law.](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_gg/englisch_gg.html#p0296) If a candidate achieves the [Chancellor majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_majority?action=edit\&redlink=1), he or she must be appointed; if no candidate achieves this majority, the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Germany) must decide whether to appoint the candidate with the most votes ([relative majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_\(voting\))) or dissolve the Bundestag.[^cite_note-\:2-12]

Surprised by his failure to win enough votes, Merz's coalition asked the parliament's legal department whether a second vote could be held the same day.[^cite_note-benoit20250506-14] During the adjournment of the session, the [factions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Bundestag#Parliamentary_groups) of the Bundestag discussed a second round of voting. All parliamentary groups agreed that the second round of voting should take place on 6 May. The CDU/CSU and SPD parliamentary groups once again put forward [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md) as their candidate. However, a resolution of the Bundestag was required for this second ballot, as the motion for the election proposal was distributed as a printed matter, which, according to the Bundestag's rules of procedure, may be dealt with no earlier than three days after its submission ([Section 78 (5) GOBT](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/btgo_1980/__78.html)). The [CDU/CSU](10796483.md), [SPD](60585.md), [The Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_90/The_Greens) and [The Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Left_\(German_Party\)) parliamentary groups then tabled a motion that the deadline be shortened, which is permitted under [Section 126](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/btgo_1980/__126.html) of the Standing Rules and needs a two-thirds majority to pass. The shortening of the deadline was approved unanimously. Friedrich Merz was elected in the second round of voting. He was then appointed Chancellor by Federal President Frank-Walter Steinmeier and sworn in to the Bundestag. The federal ministers were then appointed by Federal President Steinmeier and then sworn in by the Bundestag.[^cite_note-\:3-9][^cite_note-\:1-11][^cite_note-\:2-12][^cite_note-15]

In addition to the chancellor, the cabinet consists of 17 ministers, 16 of whom have their own portfolio and one is [Minister for Special Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Minister_for_Special_Affairs_of_Germany) (in order to give cabinet rank to the [head of the chancellery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_Federal_Chancellery_of_Germany)). The total number of ministers has thus risen by one in comparison to the Scholz cabinet. The [Federal Ministry for Digital and State Modernisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Ministry_for_Digital_and_State_Modernisation) has been newly created as a split from the [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport) ministry and parts of the portfolio of the ministry for the interior and of the ministry for economic affairs. Other portfolio [reshuffles](845103.md) includes the re-integration of [climate action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_action) back into the ministry for the environment; it had been merged with economic affairs under outgoing minister [Robert Habeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Habeck). The responsibility for international climate policy and climate negotiations was also transferred to the ministry for the environment from the foreign office.[^cite_note-16] The responsibility for [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education) is being merged with the portfolio of family, seniors, women and youth affairs, while the remaining ministry for research has been given the added designations of technology and [astronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_space_programme).

## 2026 crisis

Main article: [2026 Merz government crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Merz_government_crisis)

In July 2026 a personnel scandal involving Union parliamentary leader [Jens Spahn](37152181.md) and a poorly handled cabinet reshuffle produced the first open talk of replacing Merz only 14 months into his chancellorship.[^cite_note-PoliticoJuly-19]

After the [2026 Saxony-Anhalt state election](70945225.md) on 6 September, in which the far-right [Alternative for Germany](38708229.md) (AfD) won 43.8 percent of the party-list vote, while the CDU fell to 17.2 percent, Merz, his party and cabinet were again placed under scrutiny. Merz admitted his personal unpopularity was as a factor in the historic defeat.[^cite_note-PoliticoNoAnswers-20]

An [INSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INSA) survey from 12 September 2026 found that 14 percent of respondents in Germany regarded Friedrich Merz as the right chancellor and 78 percent did not, including a majority of Union voters.[^cite_note-ZeitINSA-21][^cite_note-ReutersMunicipal-22]

By mid-September 2026, German media spoke of a *Kanzlerkrise* (chancellor crisis), *Kanzlerdämmerung* (chancellor twilight) or a *Kanzlertausch* (chancellor swap) in favour of [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md) minister-president [Hendrik Wüst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrik_W%C3%BCst) or Bavarian minister-president [Markus Söder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markus_S%C3%B6der).[^cite_note-Tagesspiegel-23][^cite_note-Handelsblatt-24]
