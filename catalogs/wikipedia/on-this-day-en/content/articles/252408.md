**Bulacan**, officially the **Province of Bulacan** ([Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Lalawigan ng Bulacan*), is a [province](165100.md) in the [Philippines](23440.md) located in the [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) [region](216786.md). Its capital is the city of [Malolos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malolos). Bulacan was established on August 15, 1578, and part of the [Metro Luzon Urban Beltway Super Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_regions_of_the_Philippines). This province is a part of the [Greater Manila Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manila_Area).

It has 572 barangays in 20 municipalities and four component cities ([Baliwag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baliwag), [Malolos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malolos) the provincial capital, [Meycauayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meycauayan), and [San Jose del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_del_Monte) the largest city). Bulacan is located immediately north of [Metro Manila](72340.md). Bordering Bulacan are the provinces of [Pampanga](255602.md) to the west, [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija) to the north, [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_\(province\)) and [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon) to the east, and Metro Manila and [Rizal](232458.md) to the south. Bulacan also lies on the north-eastern shore of [Manila Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bay).

In the 2024 census, Bulacan had a population of 3,876,806 people, the most populous in [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) and the third most populous in the Philippines, after [Cebu](160278.md) and [Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite).[^cite_note-PSA20–03-6] Bulacan's most populated city is [San Jose del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_del_Monte), the most populated municipality is [Santa Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria,_Bulacan), while the least populated is [Doña Remedios Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do%C3%B1a_Remedios_Trinidad) yet the largest municipality in terms of area.

In 1899, the historic [Barasoain Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barasoain_Church) in Malolos was the birthplace of the [First Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic), sometimes described as the first constitutional democracy in Asia.

On November 7, 2018, the Provincial Government of Bulacan bagged its fourth Seal of Good Local Governance award. The SGLG award is a progressive assessment system that gives distinction to remarkable governance performance.[^cite_note-7]

## Etymology

The name *Bulacan* was named after the town *Bulakan* which is derived from the [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) word *bulak*, which means *cotton* in the [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_English) language. It is due to the abundance of [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) plant growing in the region.

### Spanish colonial period

The conquest of the area comprising present-day Bulacan traces to the first years of the Spanish in the Philippines. Upon the defeat of the [Macabebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macabebe) and [Hagonoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagonoy,_Bulacan) forces led by Bambalito in the [Battle of Bangkusay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bangkusay) on June 3, 1571, [Martín de Goiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mart%C3%ADn_de_Goiti) to proceeded north, first to [Lubao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubao) in September 1571.

Two months later, on November 14, 1571, Goiti reached Malolos and Calumpit, respectively, and it was reported to Adelantado [Miguel Lopez de Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Lopez_de_Legazpi), the first [Governor-General of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_the_Philippines). Adelantado established Calumpit and Malolos as an [encomienda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encomienda) entrusted to Sargento Juan Moron (Morones in other documents) and Don Marcos de Herrera.[^cite_note-8] These two conquistadores were one of the first group of conquerors accompanied by Legaspi who have arrived in the Islands in 1565.

On April 5, 1572, the encomiendas of Calumpit and Malolos were unified and co-administered by Moron and Herrera. Also in that year, Alcaldia de Calumpit was formed in which the areas of Macabebe, Candaba, Apalit in Pampanga, and the settlements of Meyto, Panducot, Meysulao, and Malolos. On December 28, 1575, Governor-General Francisco Sande ordered to include Hagonoy in Calumpit.

In 1575, Bulakan was established as a visita of Tondo, and it is not part of Calumpit as the boundary between Tondo and Calumpit was marked in Mambog River and placed the statue of Our Lady of Visitacion (patroness of Calumpit) was erected. It was gone and recreated in 1997 upon the re-establishment of the Roman Catholic Parish of Our Lady of Presentacion in Malolos.

On April 30, 1578, the town of Bulakan was officially established by the Augustinians, with Fray Diego Vivar as its first prior, and the convent was dedicated to San Agustin; when this was changed to Our Lady of Assumption is uncertain. It was reported that the western part of present-day Bulacan was to be very well-populated and rich. There is no documentation of the exact year and date when the Alcaldia de Calumpit was dissolved nor of the exact foundation year of the province of Bulacan. It has only been documented that Malolos (then part of Calumpit in 1572) was first to appear as part of Alcaldia de Bulacan in 1582. It may be assumed that the reorganization of encomiendas occurred between 1580 and 1582 at the time of Governor-General [Gonzalo Ronquillo de Peñalosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzalo_Ronquillo_de_Pe%C3%B1alosa).

The same document, also from the 1582 *Relacion de las Islas Filipinas* by Miguel de Loarca, reports that Alcaldia de Calumpit had jurisdiction in the areas of Calumpit (the capital), Capalangan, Cabangbangan and Hagonoy, which made up the Alcaldia's [villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villages). Then Loarca mentioned that Alcaldia de Bulacan had Bulakan (its capital), Malolos, Caluya, Guguinto, Binto and Catanghalan (instead of Meycauayan) as its *encomiendas*, which formerly had one *alcalde* (mayor), though Loarca wrote that Alcaldia de Bulacan was formed in 1580 at the time of Peñalosa.

According to the document of [Governor-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_the_Philippines) [Luis Pérez Dasmariñas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_P%C3%A9rez_Dasmari%C3%B1as) in the *Account of the Encomiendas* for the [King of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Spain) furnished on June 21, 1591, the Alcaldia of Bulacan was part of La Pampanga with the Encomiendas subject to it such as the Encomiendas of Malolos (3,600 persons), Binto (2,000 persons), Guiguinto (2,000 persons), Caluya (2,800 persons), Mecabayan (2,800 persons) and Bulacan identified as " capital" and residence of "alcalde mayor" with 4,800 persons. In the same 1591 document, it was mentioned that "Calumpit y Hagonoy" belonged to Juan Moron with 12,800 persons, 2 Augustinian Convents, and 1 Alcalde Mayor of its own.

However, the establishment and development of the southern part of present-day Bulacan were not simultaneous and identified with the West. In 1578, the Order of Friars Minor, headed by Juan de Plasencia and Diego Oropesa, arrived in the area called Toril (now part of Meycauayan) and their headquarters. Also in 1578, Plasencia established the Town of Meycauayan. Its pueblos were first only settlements of the Old [Meycauayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meycauayan), founded by [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan)[^cite_note-9]

The province of Bulacan is on the island of Luzon and is one of the most important *Alcadias de Termino*. Civilly and politically it corresponds to the *Audiencia y capitanía general de Filipinas* and spiritually belongs to the Archbishop of Manila.[^cite_note-10] [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan) friars [Juan Plasencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Plasencia) and Fray Diego de Oropesa founded [Meycauayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meycauayan) in 1578, and for a time it was the capital of the province of Meycauayan (which differs from Western Bulacan, administered by Augustinian Order since 1572). The Meycauayan people were able to flourish and became so rich that their sons were six of the best in the then-Province of Meycauayan. These were the towns of Bocaue, Polo, San Jose del Monte, Santa Maria de Pandi, Obando and Marilao).[^cite_note-11]



The Casa Real de Malolos. Served as the office and residency of the Governor of Malolos.

During the General Visitation of October 5, 1762, by Don [Simón de Anda y Salazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_de_Anda_y_Salazar), the province was headed by Capitan Don Jose Pasarin, *alcalde mayor* of the province.[^cite_note-12] 1795–96, Don Manuel Piñon was the alcalde mayor.[^cite_note-13]

In the same year, [British occupation of Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_occupation_of_Manila) happened, and many Tagalog refugees from Manila & north areas of Cavite escaped to Bulacan and to neighboring Nueva Ecija, where the original Kapampangan settlers welcomed them. Bulacan, along with Nueva Ecija, was natively Kapampangan when Spaniards arrived. Majority of Kapampangans sold their lands to the newly-arrived Tagalog settlers and others intermarried with and assimilated to the Tagalog, which made Bulacan dominantly Tagalog.[^cite_note-Indúng_Kapampangan-14][^cite_note-Tantingco:_The_Kapampangan_in_Us-15][^cite_note-mega-Pampanga-16]

In 1774, authorities from Bulacan, Tondo, Laguna Bay, and other areas surrounding Manila reported with consternation that discharged soldiers and deserters (from Mexico, Spain and Peru) were providing Indios military training for the weapons that had been disseminated all over the territory during the British war.[^cite_note-17]

By the end of the 1700s, Bulacan had 16,586 native families and 2,007 [Spanish Filipino families](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipinos) who were mestizos.[^cite_note-Estadismo1-18]<sup>: 539 </sup>[^cite_note-Estadismo2-19]<sup>: 31, 54, 113 </sup> By 1818, the census noted an increase of tributes or families totalling to around 50,441 tributes or families,[^cite_note-Dic-20]<sup>: 414 </sup> of which there were 6 Spanish families who were settlers from Spain,[^cite_note-Dic-20]<sup>: 414 </sup> and 164 were new Spanish-Filipino Mestizo families[^cite_note-Dic-20]<sup>: 414 </sup> (with the old 2,007 Spanish-Filipino mestizo families having been absorbed into the native category),[^cite_note-DanielDoeppers-21] 3 converted Negroes (Blacks) families,[^cite_note-Dic-20]<sup>: 414 </sup> and 7 newly Christianized Chinese-Filipino families.[^cite_note-Dic-20]<sup>: 414 </sup>

Of the new Spanish-Filipino families, these were their number and places of residency: 11 at [Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo,_Bulacan), 16 at [Meycauayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meycauayan,_Bulacan), 28 at [Marilao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marilao,_Bulacan), 88 at [Bocaue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocaue), 17 at [Santa Maria de Pandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria,_Bulacan), and 10 at [San Rafael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Rafael,_Bulacan).[^cite_note-Dic-20]<sup>: 414 </sup>

According to the "Guia de 1839", Bulacan province on the island of Luzon, Philippines, was governed by a mayor and consisted of 19 pueblos, 36,394 tributes and 181,970 souls.[^cite_note-22] D. Felipe Gobantes, *Alcalde* of the province of Bulacan erected a stone column in the plaza of Bulacan in Memory of Fr. [Manuel Blanco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Manuel_Blanco) O.S.A. who died on April 1, 1845.[^cite_note-23]

In 1848, when the boundaries of [Pampanga](255602.md) were changed, the region, which includes the important town of *San Miguel de Mayumo* and neighboring places that were formerly part of Pampanga, was [adjudicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjudicate) to Bulacan.[^cite_note-24] Bulacan also had claims on the present-day eastern portion of [Rizal](232458.md), northern portion of [Santa Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria,_Laguna), [Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_\(province\)), and western portion of [General Nakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Nakar) and [Real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real,_Quezon) in [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon) (formerly Tayabas).[^cite_note-25]

#### The Philippine Revolution



Opening of the [Malolos Congress (1898)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malolos_Congress_\(1898\))

In an earlier period during 1890, Malolos was a hot spot of Liberal *[Ilustrados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilustrado)*, notably the "20 Women of Malolos", who exerted pressure for education under Filipino professors. However, the first phase of the [revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution) ceased in 1897 with the signing of the [Pact of Biak-na-Bato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pact_of_Biak-na-Bato) in [San Miguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel,_Bulacan). Under its terms, the leaders of the revolution were to go to [Hong Kong](13404.md) and reside there. Under the illusory peace created by the pact, the end of 1897 saw greater determination on the part of the Filipinos to carry on the revolutionary struggle.

In early 1898, the provinces of [Zambales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambales), [Ilocos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos), Pampanga, Bulacan, [Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_\(province\)), [Pangasinan](1691800.md), [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija), [Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarlac), and [Camarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambos_Camarines) rose again. In [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon), a revolutionary government was organized under General [Francisco Macabulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Macabulos), a [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_people) revolutionary leader of [La Paz, Tarlac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Paz,_Tarlac).

Pandi was, in 1896-97, with the "Kakarong Republic", the early epicenter of revolutionary fevor. Despite its defeat in the [Battle of Kakarong de Sili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kakarong_de_Sili), the memory of the Kakarong defenders still remain as part of the history and heritage of Bulacan as the first organized revolutionary government established in the era of the Revolution.

### American colonial period

The Americans established a local Philippine government in the Philippines when they held the first municipal election in the country in [Baliwag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baliwag) on May 6, 1899. At the beginning of the [American rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1898%E2%80%931946\)), 1899–1900, Malolos became the headquarters of the Military Governor of the Philippines at Casa Real. On February 27, 1901, the [Philippine Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commission) officially transferred the seat of government to Malolos, and the *Casa Real de Malolos* was the seat of the Provincial Governor from 1900 to 1930 until the completion of the capitol building at Guinhawa, Malolos.

### Japanese occupation

In 1942, at the height of [World War II](32927.md), the [Japanese Imperial Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Imperial_Army) occupied Bulacan and made *Casa Real de Malolos* its headquarters. In 1945, combined Filipino and American forces and local guerrillas attacked the Japanese Imperial Forces and liberated Bulacan.

After the war, Bulacan was embroiled in the [Hukbalahap Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hukbalahap_Rebellion).[^cite_note-26] In February 1945, Huk Squadron 77 composed of 109 veteran guerillas was surrounded, shot, and buried in a mass grave in Malolos.[^cite_note-27]

### Martial law era

The beginning months of the 1970s marked a period of turmoil and change in the Philippines, as well as in Bulacan.[^cite_note-Robles2016-28] During his [bid to be the first Philippine president to be re-elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos%E2%80%99_1969_reelection_campaign) for a second term, Ferdinand Marcos launched an unprecedented number of foreign debt-funded public works projects. This caused[^cite_note-Balbosas1992-29][^cite_note-Balisacan\&Hill2003-30] the Philippine economy took a sudden downwards turn known as the [1969 Philippine balance of payments crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Philippine_balance_of_payments_crisis), which in turn led to a period of economic difficulty and a significant rise of social unrest.[^cite_note-Cororaton1997-31][^cite_note-Celoza1997-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-Kessler1989-34]<sup>: "43" </sup>

With only a year left in his last constitutionally allowed term as president, Ferdinand Marcos placed the Philippines under [Martial Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_Law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos) in September 1972 and thus retained the position for fourteen more years.[^cite_note-Kasaysayan9ch10-35] This period in Philippine history is remembered for the Marcos administration's record of [human rights abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship),[^cite_note-McCoy199909202-36][^cite_note-Abinales\&Amoroso20052-37] particularly targeting political opponents, student activists, journalists, religious workers, farmers, and others who fought against the Marcos dictatorship.[^cite_note-Rappler-38]

By this time, the social unrest of the pre-martial law period and the Marcos' violent responses to the protests of the time led many of the Philippines' youth, who previously held moderate positions calling for political reform, to be radicalized. Some were convinced to joined the newly-formed [New People's Army](1262096.md) as a last desperate way to resist Marcos' authoritarianism.[^cite_note-Rodis-39][^cite_note-Lacaba_1982_11–45,_157–178-40] On June 21, 1982, a group of young activists opposing the Marcos dictatorship tried to help farmers form a local chapter of Alyansa ng Magbubukid sa Gitnang Luzon (AMLG). They were arrested by armed soldiers from the 175th [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) Company and were found dead the following day in San Rafael, Bulacan.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-\:0-42][^cite_note-\:1-43] The name of the so-called [Bulacan Martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulacan_Martyrs) was added to the Bantayog ng mga Bayani's 'Wall of Remembrance' in 2012.[^cite_note-\:0-42][^cite_note-\:1-43]

### Move of Valenzuela to Metro Manila

Through *Presidential Decree № 824*, Bulacan was [partitioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_\(politics\)) on November 7, 1975, to form the [National Capital Region](72340.md). The municipality of [Valenzuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valenzuela,_Metro_Manila) (formerly Polo) was excised to form the new region, while the other 25 towns remained in Bulacan.

#### Issues concerning the foundation date

To determine the tentative date of Bulacan's foundation and to trace its roots from colonial period, efforts and research conducted by Jaime Veneracion, Reynaldo Naguit of the Center for Bulacan Studies, and Isagani Giron of the *Samahang Pangkasaysayan ng Bulacan* (Sampaka) shows that Bulacan was identified as a visita of Tondo in 1578.

With regards to exact date of foundation of Bulacan as a province, Veneracion correlated it with the Spanish practice of dedicating the founding of a *pueblo* to the feast of a [patron saint](68055.md). In the case of Bulacan it is the *Nuestra Señora de la Asuncion*, which is also the patron saint of [Bulakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulakan) town, the first capital of the province.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-2] Officially, the province of Bulacan was created under *Act 2711* on March 10, 1917.[^cite_note-Bulakeño_Values-44]

## Geography

Bulacan covers a total area of 2,796.10 square kilometers (1,079.58 sq mi)[^cite_note-nscb-45] occupying the southeastern section of the [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) region. The province is bounded by [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija) ([San Isidro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Isidro,_Nueva_Ecija), [Gapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gapan), [General Tinio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Tinio)) on the north, [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_\(province\)) ([Dingalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dingalan,_Aurora)) on the northeast, [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon) ([General Nakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Nakar,_Quezon)) on the east, [Rizal](232458.md) ([Rodriguez](335181.md)) on the southeast, [Metro Manila](72340.md) ([Valenzuela City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valenzuela_City), [Malabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabon), [Navotas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navotas), [Caloocan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caloocan) and [Quezon City](284223.md)) on the south, [Manila Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bay) on the southwest, and [Pampanga](255602.md) ([Candaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candaba), [San Luis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis,_Pampanga), [Apalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apalit), [Macabebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macabebe), [Masantol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masantol)) on the west.

Several rivers irrigate the province of Bulacan; the largest one is that of Angat. Angat River passes through the towns of Norzagaray, Angat, Bustos, San Rafael, Baliwag, Plaridel, Pulilan, and Calumpit. It flows thence into the [Pampanga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampanga_River), goes out again, washes Hagonoy, and loses itself in the mangroves. The banks of these rivers are very fertile and are covered with trees.

### Terrain

Bulacan lies in the southern portion of the fertile plains of Central Luzon. The area is drained by the [Angat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angat_River) and [Pampanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampanga_River) rivers. The [Sierra Madre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_\(Philippines\)) mountain range forms the highlands of Bulacan in the east and is a protected area known as the [Angat Watershed Forest Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angat_Watershed_Forest_Reserve). Angat Lake, which was formed by the [Angat Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angat_Dam) is located in that area. The highest point in the province at 1,206 meters (3,957 ft) is Mount Oriod, part of the Sierra Madre.[^cite_note-46]

### Climate

November to April is generally dry while wet for the rest of the year. The northeast [monsoon](57630.md) (*[amihan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amihan)*) prevails from October to January bringing in moderated and light rains. From February to April, the east trade winds predominate but the [Sierra Madre (Philippines)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_\(Philippines\)) mountain range to the east disrupts the winds resulting to a dry period. From May to September, the southwest monsoon (*[habagat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habagat)*).

The hottest month is May with an average temperature of 29.7 °C (85.5 °F) while the coldest is February with an average temperature of 25.1 °C (77.2 °F).

## Demographics

The population of Bulacan in the 2024 census was 3,876,806 people,[^cite_note-PSA20–03-6] making it the second most populous province in the country, only behind from [Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite), which is also located in Luzon. It had a density of 1,400 inhabitants per square kilometer or 3,600 inhabitants per square mile, the country's 4th highest for a province.

On May 1, 2010, the province had 2,924,433 inhabitants with an annual population growth rate of 2.73 from the year 2000 to 2010,[^cite_note-NSO10–03-50] There were 588,693 households in the province with an average size of 4.8 persons. Bulacan had a median age of 23 years in 2007.[^cite_note-51]

### Languages and ethnicities

As it is part of the Tagalog cultural sphere (*Katagalugan*), [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language) is the predominant language of Bulacan. The Tagalog dialect spoken in Bulacan resembles a poetic form of speech. Some inhabitants also speak [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language), especially in areas close to the border of Pampanga. Bulacan Tagalog itself contains many loanwords of Kapampangan origin. Like mentioned aboved, Bulacan was natively Kapampangan when Spaniards arrived, but when [British invaded Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_occupation_of_Manila), the province became dominantly Tagalog after many Tagalog refugees from Manila and northern areas of Cavite escaped to Bulacan, where the original Kapampangan settlers welcomed them and assimilated to them.[^cite_note-Indúng_Kapampangan-14][^cite_note-Tantingco:_The_Kapampangan_in_Us-15] Many place names of Kapampangan origin are evident in the province, like Malolos, Baliwag, Guiguinto, Pandi, Quingwa (former name of Plaridel), & San Miguel de Mayumo.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-Tantingco:_The_Kapampangan_in_Us-15][^cite_note-mega-Pampanga-16] Three municipalities ([San Miguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel,_Bulacan), [Doña Remedios Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do%C3%B1a_Remedios_Trinidad), and [Norzagaray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norzagaray)) and one city ([San Jose del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_del_Monte)) are the homelands of the Alta Kabulowan (also known as Dumagat people), the first inhabitants of Bulacan, [whose language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Alta_language) is also called Alta Kabulowan. Their language is currently endangered due to a present influx of Tagalog speakers. Being bordered by Metro Manila from its south, people from farther provinces settled in Bulacan, resulting in minor but significant usage of non-native languages in the province such as [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language), [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language), [Waray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waray_language), [Maranao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao_language), [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_language), [Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tausug_language), [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language), [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language) and [Bicolano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bikol_language).[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

San Jose del Monte, [Santa Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria,_Bulacan), [Malolos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malolos), [Marilao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marilao), San Miguel and Norzagaray bear the highest numbers of English speakers in the province.

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_the_Philippines)

#### Catholicism

[Roman Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_the_Philippines) (89.44%) is the predominant religion in the province. Malolos City is the seat of the Roman Catholic [Diocese of Malolos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Malolos), with its mother church, the [Minor Basilica and Cathedral of the Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malolos_Cathedral). On March 21, 2021, [Sta. Maria Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Conception_Parish_Church_\(Santa_Maria\)) was also granted the status of [Minor Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_basilica) by Pope Francis. It became Bulacan's second Minor Basilica and the 18th in the Philippines.

#### Islam

Here, most Muslims are converts, especially from Roman Catholicism, who are called *[Balik Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balik_Islam?action=edit\&redlink=1)*. In mid-2015, nearly a hundred people from a village in [Hagonoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagonoy,_Bulacan) municipality declared their [conversion to Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_to_Islam). They were guided by Ustadz Mohammed Yousef Pamintuan, from an Balik Islam organization there.[^cite_note-55]

#### Others

Other [Christian](8276776.md) groups include the second largest religion [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md) (4.01%),[MCGI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCGI), [Evangelicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicals) (1.53%), [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) (0.37%), Bible Baptist Church (0.30%), [Muslims](19541.md) (0.21%), [Seventh-day Adventists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh-day_Adventists) (0.21%), and [Aglipayans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aglipayans) (0.17%), and other small numbers of Christians and non-Christian groups are also present.

### Industries

The province of Bulacan is steadily becoming [industrialized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialization) due to its proximity to [Metro Manila](72340.md). Many corporations put up industrial plants and site in Bulacan. Some of the businesses and industries include [agribusiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agribusiness); [aquaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture); banking; [business process outsourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process_outsourcing_in_the_Philippines); cement bag making; ceramics; construction; courier; electronics; education; food/food processing; furniture; garments; gifts, houseware & decors; home appliance assembly; hospitals; hotels, resorts and restaurants; [information and communications technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_and_communications_technology); insurance; jewelry; leather and leather tanning; [manpower](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/manpower); marble; pharmaceutical manufacturing; printing press; [pyrotechnics](187584.md) and fireworks manufacturing; realty/real property development; school & office supply manufacturing; shoe manufacturing; textile; trade; transport services; travel and tours.

### Agribusiness and aquaculture

The rural areas still mostly depend on agriculture and aquaculture as a source of income. Some of the major crops are [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), vegetables, and fruits such as [mangoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango). An orchid farm is operating at Golden Bloom Orchids at Barangay Maguinao in San Rafael, Bulacan. The fisheries of Bulacan, aside from fishponds and rivers, include [Bustos Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bustos_Dam) and waterlogged areas. Major species cultured include [bangus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangus), [tilapia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilapia), [prawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prawn), and [catfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catfish). This made Bulacan a leading province in bangus production based on reports of the Bureau of Agricultural Statistics (BAS).[^cite_note-65]

### Banking and finance

Bulacan is served by all major banks with more than 200 banks doing business in the province. The entrepreneurial culture is supported by the strong cooperative movement with total assets of over [₱](227672.md)2 billion.

## Transportation



[Bocaue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bocaue) Toll Plaza of the North Luzon Expressway

Bulacan is dubbed as "The Gateway to the Northern Philippines". The province is linked with [Metro Manila](72340.md) primarily through the [North Luzon Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Luzon_Expressway) and [Manila North Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_North_Road) (better known as the [MacArthur Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacArthur_Highway)) which crosses the province into [Pampanga](255602.md) and western part of Northern Luzon (western [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon), [Ilocos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos) and [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region)). While taking the [Cagayan Valley Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Philippine_Highway) in [Guiguinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guiguinto), the road leads to [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija) and to the eastern part of Northern Luzon (eastern [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) and [Cagayan Valley Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_Valley_Region)). Bulacan will be accessed by the future C-6 Road connecting the provinces of [Rizal](232458.md) and [Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite) and the cities of [Taguig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taguig), [Parañaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para%C3%B1aque) and [Muntinlupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muntinlupa) in [Metro Manila](72340.md).

The proposed [North Luzon East Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Luzon_East_Expressway) (NLEE) is the future expressway link between [Metro Manila](72340.md) and the provinces of Bulacan and [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija). It will also serve as a new alternate route of motorists coming from Manila going to [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_\(province\)) and [Cagayan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_Valley) region.

The MacArthur Highway traverses the province from north to south. Most major towns can be reached through the North Luzon Expressway. A good number of motor vehicles owned largely by private individuals provide mobility to Bulacan's populace. Aside from five main highways that traverse the province, all roads are widely dispersed throughout Bulacan.

Bus terminals of [Baliwag Transit Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baliwag_Transit), Golden Bee Transport and Logistics Corp., California Bus Line, Sampaguita Liner, and Royal Eagle are in Baliwag, Balagtas and Hagonoy. The main bus lines of Philippine Rabbit, [Victory Liner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Liner), Aladdin Transit that originates from their main terminals in Manila, Pasay and Quezon City and travels northward to cities and towns in Pampanga, Tarlac, and Zambales, pass through Bulacan via the Tabang exit. Other bus companies that travel to Bulacan include ES Transport Corp. (Earth Star Transportation), [Baliwag Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baliwag_Transit), [First North Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Star_Bus_Company), [Five Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Star_Bus_Company), Agila Bus Transport, Sta Monica Transport Corp TSC, NSDC Buenasher Lines (Del Carmen), Shannen And Pauline Bus Co., [Phil. Corinthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Kings_Transport?action=edit\&redlink=1), Marsan, Mayamy, RJ Express. Bulacan is the home of its pride, one of the biggest bus lines in Luzon, the [Baliwag Transit Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baliwag_Transit) which headquarters in [Baliwag, Bulacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baliwag,_Bulacan) hence it's named.

Public transportation within the province, like in most of the urban areas in the Philippines, is facilitated mostly using inexpensive [jeepneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeepneys) and buses. Tricycles are used for short distances.

A construction of [Philippine National Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_National_Railways) (PNR) [North-South Commuter Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-South_Commuter_Railway) (NSCR) system is on track, and officials say the remnants of old PNR stations built in Bulacan province in 1892 will be preserved. The ruins of the station in Guiguinto town, Bulacan province, will be among the structures that will be preserved. The structures in the city and in the towns of Balagtas, Guiguinto, Malolos, and Calumpit would also be renovated to complement the design of the new railway stations.[^cite_note-78]

Junn Magno, PNR general manager, said 10 structures left standing from the old stations would be restored to give a glimpse of the PNR's history.

The [San Miguel Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_Corporation)'s proposed Bulacan Airport, dubbed as [New Manila International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Manila_International_Airport), involves the construction of a brand-new international airport and is being positioned as an alternative to the congested [NAIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino_International_Airport) in Manila.[^cite_note-79] It has also been seen that the four million tourists that visit the country yearly will be tripled once the airport project proposal pushes through.[^cite_note-80]

### Private schools

There are numerous privately owned (by individual or group) and church-operated schools located in the province.

The Immaculate Conception School for Boys and the Immaculate Conception School of Malolos are both under the [Diocese of Malolos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Malolos), with the incumbent Bishop of Malolos as president. Also under the Diocese is the Immaculate Conception Seminary. Others are under the direction of religious orders and congregations such as the [La Consolacion University Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Consolacion_University_Philippines) (Augustinian Sisters of Our Lady of Consolation), St. Paul College of Bocaue and St. Paul University at San Miguel ([Congregation of the Sisters of St. Paul of Chartres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisters_of_Saint_Paul_of_Chartres)) and the Holy Spirit Academy of Malolos (Sister Servants of the Holy Spirit). The [Immaculate Heart of Mary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Heart_of_Mary_School,_Bulacan) was established in 1992 with student enrollment less than 200 (as of 2016–17). The Colegio de San Pascual Baylon (formerly St. Pascual Institution) which is established in 1913 and currently managed by the Dominican Sisters of St. Joseph.

There are also schools under other denominations such as the Bulacan Ecumenical School and Bulacan Ecumenical Kindergarten (United Methodist Church).

Private schools in the province are members of the Bulacan Private Schools Association (BULPRISA) While in the City of San Jose del Monte private schools are organized by City of San Jose del Monte Private Schools Association (CSanPRISA). In Malolos, private schools are organized as Malolos City Private Schools Association (MACIPRISA). In Meycauayan, private schools are organized as Meycauayan City Private Schools Association (MEYCIPRISA). In Marilao, private schools are organized as Association of Private Schools in Marilao (APRISM).
