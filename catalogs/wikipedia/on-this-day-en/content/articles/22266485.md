# Separatism in the United Kingdom



Major and minor separatist movements in the United Kingdom.&#x20;

&#x20;  Scotland (incl. Orkney and Shetland) for Scottish independence

&#x20;  Orkney and Shetland, for independence for either or both of them.

&#x20;  Northern Ireland

&#x20;  Irish reunification (with Republic of Ireland)

&#x20;  Ulster independence

&#x20;  Wales, for Welsh independence

&#x20;  Cornwall (in England), for Cornish independence

&#x20;  England, for either English independence, or localised movements

&#x20; Northern England, Yorkshire, Northumbria, Midlands, Mercia, London, and Wessex, in various lighter overlapping shades of red.

**Separatism in the United Kingdom** may refer to the [secession](144732.md) of any of the [countries of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countries_of_the_United_Kingdom) ([England](9316.md), [Scotland](26994.md), [Northern Ireland](21265.md) and [Wales](69894.md)) from [the union](31717.md). Less significant movements for separatism exist such as [Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall) within England.

The main separatist movements of the countries of the United Kingdom may be described collectively to possibly lead to the **breakup of the United Kingdom**, in relation to the impact a successful movement may have on the other movements.

Since May 2026, the [governments of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Government), [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Executive) and [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Government) are led by separatist parties, with the main separatist party being the largest party in each of the three [devolved legislatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution_in_the_United_Kingdom).[^cite_note-\:1-1]

## Major movements

### England

Main articles: [English separatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_separatism) and [English independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_independence)



England (dark red) within the United Kingdom

On the political level, some [English nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_nationalists) have advocated [self-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-government) for England. This could take the form either of a [devolved English Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolved_English_parliament) within the United Kingdom or the re-establishment of an [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_independence) sovereign state of England outside the UK.

The [English Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Democrats) are an English nationalist political party that call for the creation of a devolved English Parliament within a federal UK.

### Scotland

Main article: [Scottish independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_independence)



Scotland (dark blue) within the United Kingdom

Scottish independence is supported most prominently by the [Scottish National Party](28887.md), but other parties also support independence. Other pro-independence parties which have held representation in the Scottish Parliament include the [Scottish Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Green_Party),[^cite_note-2] the [Scottish Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Socialist_Party) and [Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_\(Scotland\)). At the [2016 Scottish Parliament election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Scottish_Parliament_election), 69 of the 129 seats available were won by pro-independence parties (63 SNP and 6 Greens). The independence movement consists of many factions with varying political views. The SNP wants Scotland to keep the monarchy (see [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union)) and become an independent [Commonwealth realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm), similar to Canada or Australia. Others—such as the SSP and Solidarity—want Scotland to become an independent republic. The SSP has led republican protests and authored the [Declaration of Calton Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Calton_Hill), calling for an independent republic.[^cite_note-3]

Since the [2007 Scottish Parliament election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Scottish_Parliament_election),[^cite_note-4] (being continuously re-elected, including as recently as [2026](65934215.md)) the Scottish National Party have held the most seats in the [Scottish Parliament](61188.md) and have formed governments.[^cite_note-5]

### Northern Ireland with Ireland

Main article: [United Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Ireland)

See also: [Irish reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_reunification), [Partition of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Ireland), [repartition of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repartition_of_Ireland), and [Brexit and the Irish border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit_and_the_Irish_border)



Present day political map of Ireland

[Northern Ireland](21265.md) is part of the United Kingdom but has a substantial nationalist population who would prefer to be part of a [united Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Ireland). In Northern Ireland, the term "nationalist" is used to refer to those who seek Irish reunion by constitutional means. A more militant strand of nationalism, traditionally associated with the [Sinn Féin](28175.md) party, is generally described as "republican" and was regarded as somewhat distinct, although the modern-day party declares itself to be a constitutional party committed to exclusively peaceful and democratic means.[^cite_note-6] The opposite of Irish nationalism is [Ulster unionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_unionism), which seeks to maintain the union with the United Kingdom, again by constitutional means. The more militant strand of Unionism is called [Loyalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Loyalism).

The majority of Northern Ireland, like Scotland, voted to Remain in the [EU Referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum). A factor in this vote was the fear of a disruptive [hard border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_border) between Northern Ireland and the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md), as well as fears that such a border might lead to the collapse of the [Good Friday Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Friday_Agreement) (Belfast Agreement). Unionist (and particularly Loyalist) discontent with the [Northern Ireland Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Protocol) (a [protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_\(politics\)) to the [Brexit withdrawal agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brexit_withdrawal_agreement)) and the consequent [Irish Sea border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Sea_border) it has entailed, has also threatened the stability of the Agreement.

Sinn Féin have held the most seats in the [Northern Ireland Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Assembly) since the [2022 Northern Ireland Assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Northern_Ireland_Assembly_election), and their leader, [Michelle O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_O'Neill), has co-lead the [Northern Ireland Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Executive) as its [first minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_and_Deputy_First_Minister_of_Northern_Ireland).[^cite_note-7] Since the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_general_election), Sinn Féin have also held more Westminster constituencies than any other Northern Irish party.

### Wales

Main article: [Welsh independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_independence)



Wales (dark green) within the United Kingdom

Welsh independence ([Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language): *Annibyniaeth i Gymru*) is a political ideal advocated by some political parties, advocacy groups, and people in [Wales](69894.md) that would see Wales [secede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secede) from the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and become an independent [sovereign state](23604120.md). This ideology is promoted mainly by the [Welsh nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_nationalist) party, [Plaid Cymru](53343.md), and the non-partisan [YesCymru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YesCymru) campaign.

Since the [2026 Senedd election](68245236.md), Plaid Cymru have held the most seats in the [Senedd](238480.md), with their leader being [first minister](602076.md) who leads the [Welsh Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Government).[^cite_note-\:1-1][^cite_note-8]

The [Wales Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_Green_Party) also supports Welsh independence and gained representation in the Senedd for the first time in the 2026 Senedd election, gaining 2 seats.

## Minor movements

### Cornwall

Main articles: [Cornish nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornish_nationalism), [Cornish devolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornish_devolution), and [Constitutional status of Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_status_of_Cornwall)



Cornwall (orange) within England and the United Kingdom (both lighter shades of orange)

The [Celtic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_League) and [Celtic Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_Congress) have a Cornish branch and recognise Cornwall as a Celtic Nation alongside the [Isle of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man), [Ireland](147575.md), [Scotland](26994.md), [Wales](69894.md) and [Brittany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittany). The league is a political pressure group that campaigns for independence and Celtic cooperation.[^cite_note-9] [Mebyon Kernow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mebyon_Kernow) is a regional party in Cornwall that promotes Cornish nationalism.[^cite_note-10]

### Independent Northern Ireland

Main article: [Ulster separatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_separatism)



Map of Northern Ireland (dark yellow) within the United Kingdom

Independence (separate from the Republic of Ireland) is a fringe view in Northern Ireland, but has been supported by groups such as [Ulster Third Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Third_Way) and some factions of the [Ulster Defence Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Defence_Association). However, it has not been supported by any of the political parties represented in the [Northern Ireland Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Assembly), by the [government of the United Kingdom](25318118.md), or by the [government of the Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Republic_of_Ireland).

### London

Main article: [London independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_independence)



Map of Greater London within England

London independence, sometimes shortened to Londependence,[^cite_note-11] refers to a belief favouring full-fledged independence for [London](45367389.md) as a [city-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-state), separate from the [United Kingdom](31717.md).

### North of England

See also: [Devolution to the North of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution_to_the_North_of_England)



Map of Northern England (red) within England

"Northern Independence" refers to a belief favouring independence for the [North of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_of_England). The [North–South divide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_divide_in_England) is of significant political and cultural importance in [England](9316.md).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-\:0-13]

### Orkney and Shetland

Further information: [Constitutional status of Orkney, Shetland and the Western Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_status_of_Orkney,_Shetland_and_the_Western_Isles)



Map of the Orkney Islands within Scotland

In July 2023, [Orkney Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orkney_Council) announced they were to look in alternative constitutional arrangements including changing its status within the UK or leaving the UK to become associated with [Norway](21241.md).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

A movement called [Wir Shetland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wir_Shetland) was launched in October 2015[^cite_note-16] to secede from the rest of Scotland in favour of becoming either a [Crown Dependency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Dependency) or a [British Overseas Territory](207114.md), as a means of achieving greater autonomy for the Shetland Isles.[^cite_note-17]

In September 2020 the Shetland Islands Council voted in favour of exploring options for "financial and political self-determination", stating that the islands' reliance on Scotland was "seriously threatening the prosperity, and even basic sustainability, of Shetland as a community."[^cite_note-18]

### Other parts of England

See also: [Acting Witan of Mercia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acting_Witan_of_Mercia) and [Wessex Regionalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wessex_Regionalists)

[Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire), [Mercia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercia) (the [Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midlands)), [Wessex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wessex) (southernmost England) and [Northumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northumbria_\(modern\)) (centred on [North East England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_England)) are other areas considered in media as potential areas for separation prior to the [2014 Scottish independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Scottish_independence_referendum).[^cite_note-19]

The village of [Piddington, Oxfordshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piddington,_Oxfordshire) in a referendum, voted in favour of leaving the United Kingdom in September 2026.[^cite_note-Vote-20]

## Collective context

The separatist movements of the countries of the United Kingdom (excluding England), have been said to have increased as a result of [Brexit](41688778.md). Media outlets and individuals have described this as leading to "break-up of the United Kingdom", "End of the UK", or a "Disunited Kingdom".[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

Media outlets such as NBC News have said that Scotland is the most likely to leave, followed by a United Ireland, then Wales (the latter two being catalysed by the independence of others).[^cite_note-\:5-25] In March 2021, a poll found that 44% of people in Northern Ireland thought Scottish independence would make a referendum on a United Ireland more likely.[^cite_note-26]

Following the success of Plaid Cymru in the [2026 Senedd election](68245236.md), Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland were all under the control of pro-independence parties simultaneously, for the first time. Following this, in September 2026, the leaders of the Scottish Nationalist Party, Plaid Cymru, and Sinn Fein signed a [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) regarding independence referendums. The goal of [the memorandum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Cardiff_Agreement) was to force [Andy Burnham](414818.md) to allow each country to hold a referendum. The memorandum stated that "No Westminster government has the right to block democracy or undermine the principle that our people will decide their own future." [John Swinney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Swinney) and [Michelle O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_O'Neill) both called for immediate independence referendums in Scotland and Northern Ireland. However, a Welsh independence referendum before the 2030 Senedd election has been ruled out.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]
