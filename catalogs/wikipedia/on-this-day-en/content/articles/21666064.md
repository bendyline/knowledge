The **Algeria–Holy See relations** are the diplomatic relations between [Algeria](358.md) and the [Holy See](13393.md), which is sovereign over the [Vatican](32408.md). The Holy See has an [Apostolic Nunciature to Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Nunciature_to_Algeria).

## Algerian independence

Prior to independence, Algeria was home to a million [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) settlers (10%).[^cite_note-Decolonization-1] During the [Algerian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War) of 1954-1962 the Holy See accepted the occupation of Algeria by the [French colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire), and did not speak out in favor of Algerian independence[^cite_note-2] despite pleas from the Algerian rebels to mediate.[^cite_note-3] After Algeria became independent most of the French settlers left the country. However, Algeria generally retained close relationships with France, maintained diplomatic ties with the Holy See and allowed [Roman Catholic](606848.md) priests to continue ministering to the remaining [Catholics in Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Algeria).[^cite_note-4]

## Official relationship

Although Islam is the state religion of Algeria, the Holy See has maintained a comparatively good relationship with the Algerian authorities, with [Léon-Étienne Duval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9on-%C3%89tienne_Duval) being famously recognized as the *bishop of Muslims* after the departure of French colonists.[^cite_note-5]

The Algerian state has sought to clearly distinguish between the Catholic religion, which is licit in Algeria with limitations to proselytism, and between Evangelical sects, which are officially forbidden and legally repressed by state powers.

## Recent events



Holy See embassy in [Algiers](1644.md).

In 1996, [seven Cistercian monks were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_the_monks_of_Tibhirine) by [Islamist](15012.md) extremists, followed by the death of Roman Catholic bishop [Pierre Claverie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Claverie) when a bomb exploded at his residence.[^cite_note-6] The [19 martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19_martyrs_of_Algeria) were beatified in the city of [Oran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oran) on 8 December 2018, the first such ceremony in a Muslim nation.[^cite_note-beatification-7]

On the death of Pope [John Paul II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Paul_II) in April 2005, the president of Algeria [Abdelaziz Bouteflika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdelaziz_Bouteflika) attended the funeral.[^cite_note-8]

Following [remarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI_Islam_controversy) by Pope [Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_XVI) in [Regensberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regensberg) on 12 September 2006 which were widely interpreted as being offensive to the Muslim religion, the Foreign Affairs minister asked the Vatican's ambassador to provide explanations of these statements.[^cite_note-9] The Algerian Association of Koranic Doctors called on Muslim countries to withdraw their ambassadors from Vatican City in protest.[^cite_note-10]

In March 2017, [Pope Francis](1687680.md) appointed [White Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Fathers) missionary M. Afr. [John MacWilliam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_MacWilliam) as bishop of the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Laghouat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Laghouat), covering the largely Saharan inland of Algeria. MacWilliaam had risen to the rank of major in the British army through 18 years of service, was ordained a priest in 1991,[^cite_note-11] and joined the White Fathers after the assassination of four of its members in [Tizi Ouzou, Algeria](1462858.md). He had become known for his efforts at building bridges with the Muslim community.[^cite_note-12]

On April 13, 2026, [Pope Leo XIV](54520372.md) arrived in [Algiers](1644.md), marking the first [papal visit](26660117.md) to Algeria.[^cite_note-13]
