**Aydarus Qassem Abdulaziz al-Zubaydi**[^cite_note-5] (born 1967) is a Yemeni politician who served as the president of the [Southern Transitional Council](54251566.md) from 2017 until [its dissolution](82075309.md) in 2026. He was Vice President of the [Presidential Leadership Council](70488867.md) (PLC) from 2022 to 2026.[^cite_note-6] He previously served as the governor of [Aden Governorate](3604348.md) from December 2015 to April 2017.

Following the dissolution of the STC in 2026, al-Zubaydi fled into exile in the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) via [Somaliland](51425.md).[^cite_note-7]

## Biography

### Early life and career

Zubaydi was born on 23 July 1967 in Zubayd, a village in the mountains of Dhale Governorate in [South Yemen](718749.md). His family is of the Zubayd Himyar tribe.[^cite_note-\:3-8] In Zubayd, he received primary and secondary education before joining the South Yemen military.[^cite_note-\:1-9][^cite_note-\:2-10] He moved to Aden where studied at the air force academy, graduating in 1988 with the rank of second lieutenant.[^cite_note-\:0-2] He served as an officer in the Air Defense Forces from then on until late 1989. After the [unification of Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Yemen) in 1990, he was transferred to the [Central Security Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Security_Organization) of the [Ministry of Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_\(Yemen\)),[^cite_note-\:3-8] in which he served as chief of staff for a diplomatic protection battalion in the capital of [Sanaa](18691665.md), before later serving in the special forces.[^cite_note-\:2-10]

During the [Yemeni civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_civil_war_\(1994\)) of 1994, Zubaydi sided with the breakaway [Democratic Republic of Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Yemen) and fought alongside secessionist forces against the northerners. Upon the defeat of the southerners, he fled to [Djibouti](17207794.md) in July 1994.[^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-\:2-10] He returned to Yemen in 1996 and founded the Hatham Movement, an insurgent group based in Dhale which advocated for the self-determination of the south.[^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-\:1-9] From 1997 to 1998, the group waged an assassination campaign against northern military officials, for which he was [tried in absentia](582826.md) and sentenced to death.[^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-\:2-10]

The death sentence imposed in Zubaydi was pardoned by President [Ali Abdullah Saleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Abdullah_Saleh) in 2000. From thereon, he entered the political sphere of Yemen through working with the [Joint Meeting Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Meeting_Parties), and by 2002 the Hatham Movement had become inactive.[^cite_note-\:2-10] The group was revived by Zubaydi in 2011 amidst the [Yemeni revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_revolution), during which it claimed responsibility for several attacks on the Yemeni military forces in Dhale.[^cite_note-\:0-2] Clashes with his forces in the south were again reported in 2013.[^cite_note-\:4-11] In early 2014, Zubaydi announced the establishment of the Southern Resistance, a militant group aligned with the [Southern Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Movement). His statement pledged to continue armed resistance against the Yemeni government until the achievement of an autonomous southern state.[^cite_note-\:2-10]

### Yemeni civil war and Aden governorship

Upon the outbreak of the [Yemeni civil war](46215853.md), Zubaydi aligned himself with the internationally-recognized government led by President [Abdrabbuh Mansour Hadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdrabbuh_Mansour_Hadi) to fight against the [Houthis](17240332.md).[^cite_note-\:1-9] His Southern Resistance praised the commencement of [Operation Decisive Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Decisive_Storm) by the [Saudi Arabia](349303.md)-led coalition in March 2015. Zubaydi led his forces in the face of a Houthi offensive in Dhale, forcing their complete withdrawal from the governorate by 25 May, the first in the south to be liberated from Houthi presence.[^cite_note-\:3-8][^cite_note-12] His forces later advanced upon the al-Anad Air Base in Lahij, and participated in the [Battle of Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Aden_\(2015\)) alongside coalition and government units. On 4 August, he declared all areas in the south as liberated from the Houthis.[^cite_note-\:3-8]

Acknowledging his leverage in the south, Hadi issued a presidential decree appointing Zubaydi as the governor of Aden on 7 December 2015.[^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-\:2-10] He succeeded [Jaafar Mohammed Saad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaafar_Mohammed_Saad), who was [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Aden_car_bombing) in a car bombing claimed by the [Islamic State – Yemen Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_%E2%80%93_Yemen_Province) (IS–YP). Zubaydi came into office during a time of extensive [instability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden_unrest_\(2015%E2%80%932019\)) in Aden, with groups such as IS–YP and [al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Arabian_Peninsula) (AQAP) occupying territory in multiple districts. Four assassination attempts against him were claimed by IS–YP during his tenure, three of which were car bombs. In response, Zubaydi ordered a large-scale operation in early March 2016 to liberate all areas in Aden from the militant groups, which was successfully completed later in the month.[^cite_note-\:3-8] Adam Baron of the [European Council on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council_on_Foreign_Relations) described these operations as only "tenuous progress", with Aden remaining rife with armed factions by the end of the year.[^cite_note-\:5-13]

As Zubaydi's tenure continued, his influence increased greatly in the region, but his relationship with the central government frayed.[^cite_note-\:4-11][^cite_note-\:0-2] He and his security director, Shalal Ali Shaye, acted largely autonomously from the government's authority, cooperating with foreign actors such as the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) at their own will while refusing to do so sometimes with the northerner-dominated Hadi administration.[^cite_note-\:5-13] On 10 September 2016, he publicly proposed the establishment of an independent political and security body in the south, and expressed hope for recognition from the government and Saudi-led coalition.[^cite_note-\:2-10][^cite_note-\:4-11]

### Southern Transitional Council

He was fired on 27 April 2017 by President Hadi.[^cite_note-14] On May 3, major rallies were held in Aden to protest the decision of Hadi.

One week later, the [Southern Transitional Council](54251566.md) (STC) was formed; some of the members were the governors of [Dhale Governorate](3604352.md), [Shabwah Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabwah_Governorate), [Hadramaut Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadramaut_Governorate), [Lahij Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahij_Governorate), [Socotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socotra), and [Al Mahrah Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Mahrah_Governorate). Al-Zubaydi became a member of the [Southern Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Movement) and President of the Southern Transitional Council.[^cite_note-15]

On 29 January 2018, in the [Battle of Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Aden_\(2018\)), al-Zubaydi announced a state of emergency in Aden and that "the STC has begun the process of overthrowing Hadi's rule over the South".[^cite_note-Jonkers-16] In April 2020, the STC announced self-rule in [southern Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Yemen).[^cite_note-Al-Jazeera-1-17]

On 7 January 2026, [Rashad al-Alimi](70502008.md) announced that al-Zubaydi was dismissed from the Presidential Leadership Council.[^cite_note-18] He stated that al-Zubaydi had committed treason and ordered an investigation. Simultaneously, al-Zubaydi was reported to have been absent from peace negotiations in [Riyadh](51554.md) concerning the Yemeni civil war.[^cite_note-19] The PLC accused al-Zubaydi of fleeing instead to [Dhale Governorate](3604352.md) with a "large number" of STC forces, although the STC said al-Zubaydi was still in Aden.[^cite_note-20] The [Saudi-led coalition in Yemen](46230181.md) later said that al-Zubaydi travelled to [Berbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbera), [Somaliland](51425.md) by boat and boarded a plane before he flew to [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md) via [Mogadishu](60154.md), [Somalia](27358.md).[^cite_note-21]
