**Israel–Netherlands relations** are foreign relations between [Israel](9282173.md) and the [Netherlands](21148.md). The Netherlands has an embassy in [Ramat Gan](312761.md), an information office in [Jerusalem](16043.md) and two honorary consulates in [Eilat](66162.md) and [Haifa](55606.md).[^cite_note-1] Israel has an embassy in [The Hague](30269.md).[^cite_note-2] Both countries are full members of the [Union for the Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_the_Mediterranean).

Relations have historically been friendly, but have degraded somewhat since October 2023. The Netherlands' role as home to various [EU institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutions_of_the_European_Union) is a factor in the two nations' relationship.&#x20;

## History



Dutch Prime Minister [Mark Rutte](4321056.md) and Israeli Prime Minister [Benyamin Netanyahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benyamin_Netanyahu), 2013

### Yishuv

King [Willem-Alexander of the Netherlands](100384.md) commemorated the long-lasting bonding between the Netherlands and the re-establishment of a Jewish homeland in Israel (President Shimon Peres' dinner 2013): “It was a Dutch banker from The Hague, [Jacobus Kann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobus_Kann), who made possible the purchase of the land on which the first modern districts of Tel Aviv were built, more than 100 years ago.”[^cite_note-3] Jacobus Henricus Kann (1872–1944) was a Jewish Dutch banker and owner of the private bank NV Bankierskantoor van Lissa & Kann ([ABN AMRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABN_AMRO) Bank), among his clientele were members of the Dutch royal family, a founder of Bank [Otzar Hityashvut Hayehudim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otzar_Hityashvut_Hayehudim?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Bank Anglo Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Anglo_Palestine?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([Bank Leumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Leumi)), and an affluent investor in companies as the Palestine Potash Limited ([Dead Sea Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Sea_Works)) and [Frutarom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frutarom). Kann sponsored many of [Hertzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hertzel) activities and the [zionist conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Zionist_Congress) and purchased the land for [Achuzat Bait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achuzat_Bait?action=edit\&redlink=1)- the seed of Tel-Aviv. Kann arrived to Jerusalem for a third time in 1924 with his family and was nominated as the first Consul of the Netherlands in Jerusalem (1924–27). Kann died on October 7, 1944, in the [Theresienstadt concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresienstadt_concentration_camp).

In 1947, the Netherlands voted in favor of the [United Nations Partition Plan for Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine). Both countries established diplomatic relations in 1949.[^cite_note-4]

### 1948–1999

The Netherlands was initially among the most supportive countries of Israel in Europe. It was one of the few countries to establish an embassy in [Jerusalem](16043.md), whereas most countries preferred placing their embassies in the [Tel Aviv](31453.md) area until the final status of Jerusalem was resolved. Following the [Six-Day War](29328.md), the Dutch people raised about \$4.2 million for Israel in donations.[^cite_note-5] After the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) cut off diplomatic relations with Israel due to the Six-Day War, the Dutch Embassy in [Moscow](19004.md) established an Israel interests' section which represented Israel in the Soviet Union until diplomatic relations were reestablished in January 1991.[^cite_note-6] In 1973, during the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War), the Netherlands was one of only two European countries (the other being [Portugal](23033.md)) that allowed American aircraft ferrying military equipment to Israel as part of [Operation Nickel Grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Nickel_Grass) to use its airbases. The Netherlands also supplied Israel with military equipment during the war, primarily engines, spare parts, and shells for its British-built [Centurion tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centurion_tank) after the British government had imposed an [arms embargo on Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_embargo_on_Israel) and thus cut off Israel's original source for this material, but also parts and munitions for light AMX tanks, artillery ammunition, and aircraft bombs.[^cite_note-7] A number of Dutch truck drivers worked in Israel during the war to replace Israeli truck drivers who had been called up for military service.[^cite_note-8]

In 1980, following the [Jerusalem Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Law) and a UN Resolution asking member states to withdraw their Jerusalem embassies in response, the Dutch government moved its embassy from Jerusalem.[^cite_note-9] Dutch forces were deployed as part of the [UNIFIL](787766.md) peacekeeping force in [Lebanon](17771.md), and during the [1982 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War), a unit of Dutch peacekeepers was one of the few UNIFIL units that attempted to obstruct the Israeli advance. The Dutch soldiers put up obstacles that damaged two Israeli tanks, but the Israeli armored column ultimately forced its way through their position.[^cite_note-10]



The Embassy of Israel in the Netherlands is located in this building in [The Hague](30269.md). (May 2014)

During the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) in 1991, [Iraq](7515928.md) launched [Scud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud) missile attacks against Israel in the hopes of provoking an Israeli counterattack and thus rupturing the [Allied Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_of_the_Gulf_War), which included Arab countries. Israel agreed not to attack Iraq in exchange for protection, and Coalition [Patriot missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_missile) batteries were deployed in Israel to counter the Scud attacks. The [Royal Netherlands Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Air_Force) deployed a patriot missile squadron to Israel and [Turkey](11125639.md). The Dutch Defense Ministry later confirmed that the Patriot missiles had been largely ineffective, but the psychological value was high.[^cite_note-11]

### 21st century

After the [Israeli invasion of the Gaza Strip](74998519.md), a court in the Netherlands ordered the government to freeze all exports of F-35 fighter jets that Israel uses to bomb the Gaza Strip. This ruling is the result of a complaint against the government by Dutch humanitarian organizations Oxfam Novib, PAX Netherlands Peace Movement Foundation and The Rights Forum.[^cite_note-12]

On June 15, 2025, an estimated 100,000 to 150,000 took part in a protest in [The Hague](30269.md) that called on the Dutch government to take firmer steps against Israel's actions in the war; that protest was the largest in the Netherlands in two decades.[^cite_note-\:0-13][^cite_note-14]

On June 19, 2025, during the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War) the Dutch embassy was hit causing damage but no injuries.[^cite_note-15]

In July 2025, for the first time, the Netherlands listed Israel as a foreign threat to the country's national security, citing alleged [disinformation](142690.md) campaigns threatening the lives of Dutch citizens during the [riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_2024_Amsterdam_riots) by [Maccabi Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maccabi_Tel_Aviv_F.C.) fans in November 2024. According to a report by the [National Coordinator for Security and Counterterrorism (NCTV)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coordinator_for_Security_and_Counterterrorism), it says that Israel attempted to influence public opinion and politics in the Netherlands by circulating documents, which contained "unusual and unwanted personal details" about Dutch citizens, to Dutch journalists and politicians. NCTV also voiced concerns that Israel and the United States constantly threatened the [International Criminal Court](14880.md), which could disrupt the court's work.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] That same month, the Netherlands banned [Finance Minister](7088294.md), [Bezalel Smotrich](46196834.md) and [Minister of National Security](28572000.md), [Itamar Ben-Gvir](54504115.md) from entering the country, labelling them *[persona non grata](521241.md)* for their calls for ethnic cleansing in Gaza.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] The government also recalled the Dutch ambassador to Israel, [Marriët Schuurman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marri%C3%ABt_Schuurman).[^cite_note-20]

On August 22, 2025, Dutch foreign minister [Caspar Veldkamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspar_Veldkamp) resigned from the caretaker [Schoof cabinet](77133656.md) and his [New Social Contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Social_Contract) left the governing coalition after failing to secure sanctions against Israel.[^cite_note-\:0-13]

On August 25, 2026, Israeli Foreign Minister [Gideon Sa'ar](485357.md) announced that two Dutch officials would be expelled from the US-backed [Gaza Ceasefire Coordination Center](81409826.md) in response to the Dutch government's anti-Israeli actions, including a ban on imports of goods from illegal Israeli settlements.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] The ban on goods included all the [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze_in_Israel) towns and the Alawite town of [Ghajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghajar) in the Golan Heights, as well as the Catholic [Latrun Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latrun#Trappist_monastery).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] On September 27, Israel notified Dutch diplomats serving in [Ramallah](44132.md) that the diplomatic privileges granted by Israel would be terminated in seven days, in retaliation for the bans that took effect a few days prior. Minister Sa'ar said "if the Netherlands wishes to continue representing its interests with the Palestinians, it may do so from Palestinian Authority territory, rather than from the territory of the State of Israel". He also accused the Dutch government of failing to address the rise of antisemitism in the country.[^cite_note-26]

## Public opinion

According to a 2025 [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center) survey, 19% of people in the Netherlands had a favorable view of Israel, while 78% had an unfavorable view; 19% had confidence in Israeli Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md), while 78% did not.[^cite_note-27]
