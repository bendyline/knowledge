**Aichi Prefecture** (愛知県, *Aichi-ken*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[aꜜi.tɕi, ai.tɕi̥ꜜ.keɴ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-2]) is a [prefecture](74517.md) of [Japan](15573.md) located in the [Chūbu region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%ABbu_region) of [Honshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honsh%C5%AB).[^cite_note-Frédéric_2002-3]<sup>: 11, 126 </sup> Aichi Prefecture has a population of 7,461,111 (as of 1 January 2025) and a geographic area of 5,172.92 [square kilometres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_kilometre) (1,997.28 [sq mi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_mile)) with a population density of 1,442 inhabitants per square kilometre (3,730/sq mi). Aichi Prefecture borders [Mie Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mie_Prefecture) to the west, [Gifu Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gifu_Prefecture) and [Nagano Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagano_Prefecture) to the north, and [Shizuoka Prefecture](180999.md) to the east. [Nagoya](61720.md) is the capital and largest city of the prefecture.

## Overview

[Nagoya](61720.md) is the capital and largest city of Aichi Prefecture, and the [fourth-largest city in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_cities_in_Japan_by_population_by_decade). Other major cities include [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota,_Aichi), [Okazaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okazaki,_Aichi), and [Ichinomiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichinomiya,_Aichi). Aichi Prefecture and Nagoya form the core of the [Chūkyō metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%ABky%C5%8D_metropolitan_area), the [third-largest metropolitan area in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_Japan) and one of the largest [metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) in the world.[^cite_note-Frédéric_2002-3]<sup>: 685 </sup> Aichi Prefecture is located on Japan's [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) coast and forms part of the [Tōkai region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dkai_region), a [subregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subregion) of the Chūbu region and [Kansai region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_region). Aichi Prefecture is home to the [Toyota Motor Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota). Aichi Prefecture had many locations with the [Higashiyama Zoo and Botanical Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higashiyama_Zoo_and_Botanical_Gardens), the [Chubu Centrair International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chubu_Centrair_International_Airport), and the [Legoland Japan Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legoland_Japan_Resort).

Located near the centre of the Japanese main island of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu), Aichi Prefecture faces the [Ise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ise_Bay) and [Mikawa Bays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikawa_Bay) to the south and borders [Shizuoka Prefecture](180999.md) to the east, [Nagano Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagano_Prefecture) to the northeast, [Gifu Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gifu_Prefecture) to the north, and [Mie Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mie_Prefecture) to the west. It measures 106 km (66 mi) east to west and 94 km (58 mi) south to north and forms a major portion of the [Nōbi Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C5%8Dbi_Plain). With an area of 5,172.48 square kilometres (1,997.11 sq mi) it accounts for approximately 1.36% of the total surface area of Japan. The highest spot is [Chausuyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chausuyama_\(Aichi\)) at 1,415 m (4,642 ft) above sea level.

The western part of the prefecture is dominated by [Nagoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya,_Aichi), Japan's third largest city, and its suburbs, while the eastern part is less densely populated but still contains several major industrial centres. Due to its robust economy, for the period from October 2005 to October 2006, Aichi was the fastest growing prefecture in terms of population, beating [Tokyo](30057.md), at 7.4% and around with after [Saitama Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_Prefecture).

As of 1 April 2012, 23% of the total land area of the prefecture was designated as [Natural Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan#History), namely the [Aichi Kōgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aichi_K%C5%8Dgen_Quasi-National_Park), [Hida-Kisogawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hida-Kisogawa_Quasi-National_Park), [Mikawa Wan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikawa_Wan_Quasi-National_Park), and [Tenryū-Okumikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenry%C5%AB-Okumikawa_Quasi-National_Park) Quasi-National Parks along with seven Prefectural Natural Parks.[^cite_note-NationalPark-4]

## History

See also: [Historic Sites of Aichi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Sites_of_Aichi_Prefecture)

Originally, the region was divided into two provinces of [Owari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owari_Province) and [Mikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikawa_Province).[^cite_note-Frédéric_2002-3]<sup>: 780 </sup> After the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration), Owari and Mikawa were united into a single entity. In 1871, after the [abolition of the han system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_the_han_system), Owari, with the exception of the [Chita Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chita_Peninsula), was established as Nagoya Prefecture, while Mikawa combined with the Chita Peninsula and formed [Nukata Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nukata_Prefecture). Nagoya Prefecture was renamed to Aichi Prefecture in April 1872 and was united with Nukata Prefecture on 27 November of the same year.

The government of Aichi Prefecture is located in the [Aichi Prefectural Government Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aichi_Prefectural_Government_Office) in Nagoya, which is the old capital of Owari. The [Aichi Prefectural Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aichi_Prefectural_Police) and its predecessor organisations have been responsible for law enforcement in the prefecture since 1871.

The [Expo 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2005) [World Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_fair) was held in [Seto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seto,_Aichi) and [Nagakute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagakute,_Aichi).

### Etymology

In the third volume of the *[Man'yōshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man'y%C5%8Dsh%C5%AB)* there is a poem by Takechi Kurohito that reads: "The cry of the crane, calling to Sakurada; it sounds like the tide, draining from Ayuchi flats, hearing the crane cry". Ayuchi is the original form of the name *Aichi*, and the [Fujimae tidal flat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujimae-higata) is all that remains of the earlier Ayuchi-gata. It is now a protected area.[^cite_note-pref.aichi.jp-6][^cite_note-Kato_2000-7]

For a time, an Aichi Station existed on the [Kansai Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_Line) (at the time the Kansai Railway) between [Nagoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_Station) and [Hatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatta_Station) stations, but its role was overtaken by [Sasashima-raibu Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasashima-raibu_Station) on the [Aonami Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aonami_Line) and [Komeno Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komeno_Station) on the [Kintetsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintetsu_Railway) [Nagoya Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_Line_\(Kintetsu\)).

Notable sites in Aichi include the [Meiji Mura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Mura) open-air architectural museum in [Inuyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuyama,_Aichi), which preserves historic buildings from Japan's [Meiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_period) and [Taishō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taish%C5%8D_period) periods, including the reconstructed lobby of [Frank Lloyd Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lloyd_Wright)'s old [Imperial Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Hotel,_Tokyo) (which originally stood in Tokyo from 1923 to 1967).

Other popular sites in Aichi include the tour of [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota) car factory in the city by the same name, the monkey park in Inuyama, and the castles in [Nagoya](61720.md), [Okazaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okazaki,_Aichi), [Toyohashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyohashi), and Inuyama.

Aichi Prefecture has many beaches, including [Himakajima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himakajima) Beach, Shinojima Beach, Akabane Beach and Utsumi Beach.
