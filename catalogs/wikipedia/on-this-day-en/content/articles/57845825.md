The **Raoul Wallenberg Centre for Human Rights (RWCHR)** is a Canadian [non-governmental organization](46539.md) and lobbying group focused on issues of [human rights](13831.md). The RWCHR's name is inspired by [Raoul Wallenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Wallenberg).

Based in [Montreal](7954681.md), the RWCHR has advised policies in Canada and Europe. Their advocacy aligns closely with the [foreign policy of the United States](7564.md) and [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_Israel). A large part of their work is in lobbying for "[Magnitsky-style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitsky_Act)" [economic sanctions](411315.md) on adversary nations such as [Russia](25391.md), [China](5405.md) and [Iran](14653.md), and training groups in other countries to advocate for them.[^cite_note-rwchr-sanctions-1] Starting in 2026, RWCHR has also made public statements in support of [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s [regime change](154074.md) efforts against [Venezuela](32374.md) and [Iran](14653.md), corresponding with the [2026 United States intervention in Venezuela](82021123.md) and the [2026 Iran War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_War)[^cite_note-ir-rc-2].

The RWCHR was founded by human rights lawyer and former [Canadian Minister of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Justice_\(Canada\)) [Irwin Cotler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irwin_Cotler) in 2015. The first Raoul Wallenberg All-Party Parliamentary Caucus for Human Rights was launched in Canada in 2017.[^cite_note-\:3-3]

## Themes

The RWCHR's mission is to mobilize a "unique international consortium" of academics, activists, lawyers, jurists, Parliamentarians, NGOs, citizens and students.[^cite_note-\:3-3] The RWCHR's mission statement is organized around five themes, based on [Raoul Wallenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Wallenberg)'s humanitarian legacy. Each theme is co-chaired by a leading jurist, parliamentarian, advocate or citizen in the country of [Wallenberg's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Wallenberg) Honorary Citizenship. The honorary co-chairs are [Elie Wiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie_Wiesel) (US), [Rosalie Abella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalie_Abella) (Canada), [Meir Shamgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meir_Shamgar) (Israel) and [Göran Persson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6ran_Persson) (Sweden).[^cite_note-4] All of the RWCHR's activity relates to one or more of the below themes.

### Heroes of humanity

The RWCHR believes in a "moral imperative and historic responsibility" to pay tribute to those they call "heroes of humanity" who put their lives at risk for human rights.[^cite_note-\:11-5] Notable "heroes of humanity" that the RWCHR has made efforts to commemorate are [Raoul Wallenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Wallenberg), [Boris Nemtsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Nemtsov) and [Elie Wiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie_Wiesel).

**Raoul Wallenberg**

According to the RWCHR, [Raoul Wallenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Wallenberg), a Swedish diplomat who has been alleged by some to have saved 100,000 [Hungarian Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Jews) during [The Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust), represents how one man with the 'compassion to care and the courage to act' can save more lives than the entire international bystander community.[^cite_note-\:8-6] Other historical analyses have suggested that the figures of Jews saved by Wallenberg were far lower. Notably in a 2004 paper, Hungarian historian and Holocaust survivor [Randolph L. Braham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph_L._Braham) alleged that 7,000 to 9,000 Jews were saved by Wallenberg,[^cite_note-7] while Israeli historian [Yehuda Bauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yehuda_Bauer) has offered a figure of 4,500.[^cite_note-8] Wallenberg can also be said, according to the RWCHR, to have foreshadowed the foundational principles of [international human rights](219877.md) and [humanitarian law](1093604.md).[^cite_note-\:8-6] Further, Wallenberg serves to highlight the plight of [political prisoners](37307.md) because once the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) arrived to liberate [Hungary](13275.md), Wallenberg, a liberator himself, was imprisoned never to be heard from again. Wallenberg's fate remains unknown due to [Russia](25391.md)'s unwillingness to reveal [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB) archives.[^cite_note-9]

RWCHR's chair, [Irwin Cotler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irwin_Cotler), served as the chair of the International Commission on the Fate and Whereabouts of Raoul Wallenberg writing a 1,200-page report which presented "incontrovertible" evidence that Wallenberg did not die in 1947 (as the Soviets claimed), "compelling" evidence that he was alive in the 1950s and 1960s, and "credible" evidence that he lived until the 1970s and 1980s.[^cite_note-\:11-5] More recently, the RWCHR joined a legal action to appeal a [Russian court](1838621.md) ruling that denied Wallenberg's family access to documents that may reveal his fate.[^cite_note-10] The RWCHR's team has also authored recent [Op-Eds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-ed), like the ones in *[The Times of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_Israel)* and *[The Jerusalem Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jerusalem_Post)*, on the need to remember this Wallenberg.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

According to Canadian Senator [Sheila Finestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheila_Finestone) and [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Canada) proceedings, the RWCHR's chair was consequential in getting the [Canadian Government](616255.md) to adopt a Raoul Wallenberg Commemorative Day, which is held annually on 17 January, the day Wallenberg was captured by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The RWCHR also co-sponsors the annual International Raoul Wallenberg Roundtable.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-\:6-17]

**Boris Nemtsov**

[Boris Nemtsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Nemtsov), a Russian liberal politician that opposed [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), was assassinated on 27 February 2015 in central [Moscow](19004.md) weeks after expressing concern that Putin would try to assassinate him.[^cite_note-18] Nemtsov was a harsh critic of Putin's [corruption](27352290.md) and his [annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) and a strong advocate for [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_in_Russia) and the adoption of the [Magnitsky Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitsky_Act) sanctions against Russia.

The RWCHR has taken steps to commemorate Nemtsov. On 8 February 2018, the RWCHR co-hosted a screening of the documentary "Nemtsov" at [Queen's Park, Toronto, Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_Park_\(Toronto\)), preceding a discussion with the RWCHR's chair, [Vladimir Kara-Murza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Vladimirovich_Kara-Murza) and [Peter MacKay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_MacKay).[^cite_note-19] On 9 March 2016, the RWCHR co-sponsored the first annual Boris Nemtsov Memorial Russia Freedom Forum.[^cite_note-20] The RWCHR has also co-authored an [Op-Ed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-ed) in the *[Ottawa Citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Citizen)* urging the [Canadian Government](616255.md) to follow the [United States](3434750.md) and [Lithuania](17675.md)'s lead in adopting Nemtsov commemorations by doing such things as renaming a park or bridge.[^cite_note-21]

**Elie Wiesel**

[Elie Wiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elie_Wiesel) is a [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust) survivor and [Nobel Peace Prize](26230922.md) winner who dedicated his life to giving a voice to victims of the Holocaust and speaking out against recent genocides in [Rwanda](676659.md), [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_genocide), [Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_genocide) and [Darfur](55472698.md). Wiesel said, "there may be times when we are powerless to prevent injustice, but there must never be a time when we fail to protest".[^cite_note-22] The RWCHR's chair worked closely with Wiesel for over 50 years helping to, after Wiesel's death on 2 July 2016, create 'Elie Wiesel Park' at the busiest intersection in [Côte-St-Luc, Québec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4te-St-Luc).[^cite_note-23] The RWCHR has also written numerous [Op-Eds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-ed) and participated in events and galas dedicated to commemorating Wiesel.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

### Holocaust and genocide

The RWCHR seeks to prevent atrocities similar to the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust) by remembering and acting upon the lessons related to it. On 4 May 2016, The RWCHR and the [International March of the Living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_the_Living) co-chaired the International Nuremberg Symposium at [Jagiellonian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University) in [Kraków, Poland](16815.md) on the "Double Entendre of Nuremberg: the Nuremberg of Hate and the Nuremberg of Justice".[^cite_note-26] The "Never Again" Declaration, which highlights some of the following universal lessons, was unanimously adopted at the Symposium: the danger of forgetting and the responsibility to remember, the danger of genocide denial and the responsibility to speak truth to power and repudiate false witness, the danger of impunity and the responsibility to bring war criminals to justice, the danger of the betrayal of the elites, the danger of assaults on the vulnerable, the danger of state-sanctioned incitement to hate, the danger of the bystander community and the danger of silence and indifference in the face of evil, the responsibility to act, prevent, protect and intervene, the responsibility to pay tribute to the rescuers, and the responsibility to respect the legacy of survivors.[^cite_note-\:0-27]

The RWHCR has mounted an advocacy campaign to highlight these universal lessons. Accordingly, numerous [Op-Eds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-ed) have been written by RWCHR staff members relating these universal lessons to past and contemporary atrocities. Examples include articles in the *[National Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Post)* and [*Huffington Post*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HuffPost) about the lessons of the [Rwandan genocide](676659.md),[^cite_note-28] implementing the lessons of the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust) to combat [anti-semitism](1078.md),[^cite_note-29] and the international community's failure to invoke the [Responsibility to Protect (R2P)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsibility_to_protect) in [Syria](30741795.md).[^cite_note-30]

The RWCHR hosts, organizes and participates in many events that pertain to the Holocaust and genocides more generally. For example, on 1 March 2018, the RWCHR co-organized an event with the [Montreal Holocaust Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Holocaust_Memorial_Centre), the [McGill Centre for Human Rights and Legal Pluralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University_Faculty_of_Law) and the [Montreal Institute for Genocide and Human Rights Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Institute_for_Genocide_and_Human_Rights_Studies) featuring a discussion with [Payam Akhavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payam_Akhavan) and [Bob Rae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Rae) to raise awareness and discuss the responsibility to protect the [Rohingya](1918632.md), victims of mass persecution in [Myanmar](19457.md).[^cite_note-\:4-31] On the celebration of the 150th anniversary of Canada's [confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation), the RWCHR endorsed and participated in an event which saw the Armenian, Ukrainian, Jewish, Rwandan and Greek communities of [Canada](5042916.md) come together at the Armenian Community Centres in [Montréal](7954681.md), [Toronto](64646.md) and [Vancouver](32706.md) to discuss the role [Canada](5042916.md) should play in [human rights](13831.md) today.[^cite_note-32] Other examples include the RWCHR partnering with [Vanier College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanier_College) to host the 25thand 26th Annual Vanier Conference on the Holocaust and Genocide.[^cite_note-\:5-33]

As of June 2025 the RWCHR denies the [Gaza genocide](75668427.md),[^cite_note-34] instead blaming the humanitarian crisis and civilian casualties on [Hamas](13913.md), putting it at odds with the United Nations, [Amnesty International](18947898.md), [Doctors Without Borders](20498.md) and [The Lemkin Institute for Study of Genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemkin_Institute).

### Democracy

The RWCHR advocates for fighting against [authoritarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) and [populism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism), which is seen as a threat to [liberal democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracy). On 26 May 2017, the RWCHR became a signatory to the Prague Appeal for Democratic Renewal, a declaration issued by the International Coalition for Democratic Renewal which calls for the mobilization of a coalition of conscience in defence of democracy.[^cite_note-35] The RWCHR is also a sponsor of the [Magnitsky Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitsky_Act).

The RWCHR hosts, organizes and participates in many events that pertain to the protection of democracy. Since 2016, the RWCHR has been a co-sponsor of the [Geneva Summit for Human Rights and Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Summit_for_Human_Rights_and_Democracy).[^cite_note-36] At the [10th Annual Geneva Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Summit_for_Human_Rights_and_Democracy), the RWCHR's chair gave the summit's closing remarks and presented [Vladimir Kara-Murza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Vladimirovich_Kara-Murza) with the Geneva Summit 2018 Courage Award.[^cite_note-\:11-5] The RWCHR also participated in the 2017 [Oslo Freedom Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Freedom_Forum) discussing "Democracy and the Rule of Law".[^cite_note-37] In May 2017, the RWCHR joined forces with the [Montreal Institute for Genocide and Human Rights Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Institute_for_Genocide_and_Human_Rights_Studies), [Amnesty International Canada](18947898.md) and the United Committee of Armenian Organizations of Quebec to host *Rights City/Montréal, ville des droits humains*, which comprises three major events to celebrate [Montreal](7954681.md)'s role in advancing [human rights](13831.md) and democracy.[^cite_note-38] To discuss the threat illiberal populism and authoritarianism pose to academic freedom, the RWCHR and its chair participated in the Canadian Science Policy Conference in [Ottawa](22219.md) alongside [Home Hoodfar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homa_Hoodfar) and Viviana Fernandez.[^cite_note-39] In August 2017, the RWCHR helped host *Vietnamplify*, a conference that highlights the fight for [human rights](13831.md) in [Vietnam](202354.md).[^cite_note-40] On 7 October 2017, the RWCHR played a role in highlighting the humanitarian crisis caused by the turn to [authoritarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism) in [Burundi](21490998.md) by inviting [Pierre-Claver Mbonimpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Claver_Mbonimpa) to [Ottawa](22219.md) for a press conference and meeting with Parliamentarians.[^cite_note-41]

### Political prisoners

The RWCHR mobilizes international advocacy in concert with other international human rights bodies, governments and NGOs to defend [political prisoners](37307.md) around the world. According to the RWCHR's chair, the "plight of a political prisoner serves as a looking glass into the human rights violator country that imprisons them".[^cite_note-42] The RWCHR serves as international legal counsel to the following political prisoners: [Raif Badawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raif_Badawi), a Saudi blogger imprisoned for exercising his right to freedom of expression;[^cite_note-The_Raif_Badawi_Foundation-43] [Leopoldo Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldo_L%C3%B3pez), a leader of the Venezuelan opposition currently in exile;[^cite_note-44] [Dr. Wang Bingzhang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Bingzhang_\(dissident\)), a Chinese pro-democracy [dissident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissident) currently in Chinese prison;[^cite_note-\:10-45] Sun Qian, a [Falun Gong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falun_Gong) practitioner,[^cite_note-en.tuidang.org-46] [Saeed Malekpour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saeed_Malekpour), a [Canadian permanent resident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_residency_in_Canada) who was imprisoned in [Evin Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evin_Prison) in Iran for developing and hosting a [pornographic website](5710507.md) while in Iran until 2019;[^cite_note-47] [Ayatollah Hossein Kazemeyni Boroujerdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossein_Kazemeyni_Boroujerdi), imprisoned in [Evin Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evin_Prison) for advocating for [separation of religion and government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_church_and_state);[^cite_note-48] the [Baháʼí 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_7), imprisoned in [Evin Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evin_Prison) for practicing their [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith) and promulgating [religious freedom](46006.md).[^cite_note-\:7-49] In 2017, the RWCHR used [Human Rights Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Day) to co-host a press conference with the Raoul Wallenberg All-Party Parliamentary Caucus for Human Rights to highlight the cases of political prisoners.[^cite_note-50]

### Women's rights

The RWCHR is a supporter of women's rights. The RWCHR has begun to host events and engage in public advocacy intended to help women exercise [self-determination](29269.md). Examples include an Op-Ed authored by the RWCHR and published in the 'Huffington Post on [International Women's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Women's_Day) on the need to continue advancing women's rights. On 20 March 2018, the RWCHR co-sponsored an event with [UN Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Watch) parallel to the [United Nations Commission on the Status of Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Commission_on_the_Status_of_Women) with the goal of highlighting on the role of women in "defending democracy and pursuing justice". Participants of the event included [Wai Wai Nu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wai_Wai_Nu), [Mahnaz Afkhami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahnaz_Afkhami), [Maria Corina Machado](5791389.md) and Ketty Niviyabandi.[^cite_note-51]

## Activity

### Magnitsky legislation

The [Magnitsky legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitsky_legislation) is a series of laws that give governments the power to levy sanctions on officials deemed to have violated internationally recognized [human rights](13831.md). The legislation is named after [Sergei Magnitsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Magnitsky), a Russian tax accountant who was imprisoned, and later killed, after uncovering and reporting the theft of \$230-million by Russian state officials. The U.S. [Magnitsky Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitsky_Act) was originally intended to sanction the Russian officials involved in the wrongful imprisonment and killing of [Sergei Magnitsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Magnitsky), it began a global push to pass similar sanction laws targeting the [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation).

The RWCHR and its chair played a relatively significant role in helping create the global movement for Magnitsky legislation. The RWCHR's chair started a parliamentary process in 2016 to pass Canadian Magnitsky legislation inviting [Bill Browder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Browder), [Vladimir Kara-Murza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Vladimirovich_Kara-Murza), and [Garry Kasparov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov) to testify before [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada).[^cite_note-52] This, coupled with a campaign engaged in by the RWCHR to get the Canadian Foreign Affairs Committee to submit a report calling for a framework for sanctions created enough momentum for the Canadian Magnitsky legislation, officially titled the [Justice for Victims of Corrupt Foreign Officials Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_for_Victims_of_Corrupt_Foreign_Officials_Act), to unanimously pass in the [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Canada).[^cite_note-53] Following this, the Canadian Foreign Minister [Chrystia Freeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrystia_Freeland) and [Bill Browder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Browder) credited the RWCHR and its chair with the passing of the Canadian Magnitsky legislation.[^cite_note-\:0-27][^cite_note-54] On 1 November 2017, the RWCHR, along with [Bill Browder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Browder) and other [Parliamentarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada) held a press conference with Nikita and Natalia Magnitsky, the son and wife of the late [Sergei Magnitsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Magnitsky), to celebrate the passing of Magnitsky legislation in Canada.

On 14 December 2017, [Bill Browder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Browder), [Garry Kasparov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov) and the RWCHR's chair testified in Washington before the [Commission on Security and Cooperation in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Security_and_Cooperation_in_Europe), also known as the [U.S. Helsinki Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Helsinki_Commission), to examine the implementation of the [Magnitsky Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitsky_Act) and its impact on the Russian government and members of [Putin's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_under_Vladimir_Putin) inner circle.[^cite_note-\:0-27]

The RWCHR, with the help of their Raoul Wallenberg All-Party Parliamentary Caucus for Human Rights, have urged the [Canadian government](616255.md) to support the global Magnitsky legislation movement by persuading other countries to adopt similar laws.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] The RWCHR has also taken matters in their own hands attempting to use their influence to sway other countries to adopt Magnitsky legislation. To this end, two RWCHR lawyers, [Irwin Cotler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irwin_Cotler) and Brandon Silver, visited the Netherlands in February to meet with [Martijn Van Helvert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martijn_van_Helvert) and other Members of the Foreign Affairs Committee to encourage them to pass Dutch Magnitsky legislation.[^cite_note-57]

### Russia

Much of the RWCHR's work regarding combating human rights violations in [Putin's Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_under_Vladimir_Putin) involves promoting the [Magnitsky Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitsky_Act), both in [Canada](5042916.md) and abroad. The RWCHR also devote significant time to honouring [Boris Nemtsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Nemtsov), a liberal opposition politician who was assassinated in 2015. In both of these regards, the RWCHR has partnered with Russian dissident [Vladimir Kara-Murza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Vladimirovich_Kara-Murza), who has been *[de facto](43594.md)* exiled from [Russia](25391.md) for his promotion of the [Magnitsky Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitsky_Act).[^cite_note-58] The RWCHR has also been engaged with the case of [Alexey Pichugin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Pichugin), evidenced by his mention in an [Op-Ed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-ed) in *[The Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hill_\(newspaper\))* authored by the RWCHR's chair.[^cite_note-59]

### Iran

RWCHR is critical of the [Government of Iran](50913538.md), frequently calling for increased sanctions and regime change against the Islamic Republic. In February 2026, in the lead-up to the [2026 Iran War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_War), RWCHR lobbied the Canadian parliament for "immediate action" against Iran.[^cite_note-ir-rc-2]

In 2013, the RWCHR's chair launched Iran Accountability Week, an advocacy initiative held annually in the first week of May by the [Canadian Subcommittee on International Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_House_of_Commons_Standing_Committee_on_Foreign_Affairs_and_International_Development) to highlight the domestic repression and rights-abuses perpetrated by [Khamenei's](385653.md) Iran.[^cite_note-\:9-60] A centrepiece of Iran Accountability Week is the Global Iranian Political Prisoner Advocacy Project whereby [Parliamentarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada) and [Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Canada) "adopt" political prisoners and engage in sustained public [advocacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advocacy), partly by co-authoring an [Op-Ed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-ed), on their behalf.[^cite_note-\:7-49] The cases of [Saeed Malekpour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saeed_Malekpour), [Ayatollah Hossein Kazemeyni Boroujerdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossein_Kazemeyni_Boroujerdi) and the [Baháʼí 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_7) have been advocated for in this regard. The cases of the [political prisoners](37307.md) held in Iran's notorious [Evin Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evin_Prison) were also highlighted by the RWCHR in a demonstration conducted on [Parliament Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_Hill) the day before 1 September 2017, a day of solidarity with political prisoners held in Iran officially remembered due to the [1988 massacre of political prisoners in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_executions_of_Iranian_political_prisoners), an event that [Canada](5042916.md) was the first to recognize as a [crime against humanity](61099.md) thanks to the leadership of [Irwin Cotler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irwin_Cotler), amongst other individuals.[^cite_note-61] At the gathering, RWCHR's chair reminded everyone that there is no statute of limitations for [crimes against humanity](61099.md) and that, as a result, Canada should push to establish an international tribunal, such as the ones created in response to the [Rwandan](676659.md) and [Bosnian genocides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_genocide), to bring perpetrators to justice.[^cite_note-62] The case of [Saeed Malekpour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saeed_Malekpour) was further highlighted by the RWCHR in 2017 when staff members spoke alongside [Homa Hoodfar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homa_Hoodfar) and [Alex Neve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Neve) at an event held at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto).[^cite_note-\:142-63] The RWCHR also uses the media to mount their public advocacy campaign authoring numerous [Op-Eds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Op-ed) intended to bring attention to the particular cases of political prisoners. For example, op-eds written by the RWCHR in the [*Huffington Post*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HuffPost) and *[The Jerusalem Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jerusalem_Post)* discuss the imprisonment of [Nasrin Sotoudeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasrin_Sotoudeh), [Ayatollah Hossein Kazemeyni Boroujerdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossein_Kazemeyni_Boroujerdi) and the Baháʼí 7 respectively.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

Iran Accountability Week also pays particular attention to the state sanctioned [persecution of Baháʼís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%ADs) in Iran. During the 5th annual Iran Accountability Week, the RWCHR's chair personally testified before the [Subcommittee on International Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_House_of_Commons_Standing_Committee_on_Foreign_Affairs_and_International_Development) calling the persecution of the Baháʼís in Iran a "litmus test" of the country's human rights violations.[^cite_note-\:102-66] During the 6th annual Iran Accountability Week, the RWCHR and RWCHR All-Party Parliamentary Caucus on Human Rights hosted a public panel in [Canadian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada) discussing many of the [Islamic Republic of Iran's](14653.md) human rights abuses, with particular focus on the Baha'is.[^cite_note-\:112-67] Participants and attendees at the 6th annual Iran Accountability Week included [Payam Akhavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payam_Akhavan), [Maziar Bahari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maziar_Bahari), [Mark Dubowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Dubowitz), Corinne Box, [Michael Levitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Levitt_\(politician\)), [Judy Sgro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judy_Sgro) and [Marilou McPhedran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marilou_McPhedran). The RWCHR also participated with [Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Canada) and [Parliamentarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada) in panel discussions hosted by the Canadian Friends of a Democratic Iran to discuss, among other things, the persecution of the [Baháʼí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith).[^cite_note-\:132-68] On 22 November 2017, the RWCHR, the Raoul Wallenberg All-Party Parliamentary Caucus for Human Rights, the [National Spiritual Assembly of the Baháʼís in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritual_Assembly) and the [University of Ottawa's Human Rights Research and Education Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ottawa_Faculty_of_Social_Sciences) co-hosted a screening of "The Cost of Discrimination", a documentary which draws parallels between life under [Apartheid in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) and life for the Baháʼís in today's [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-\:32-69] An op-ed highlighting the plight of the Baháʼí was also written in the *[National Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Post)* by RWCHR staff members.[^cite_note-70]

In attempt to sanction the Iranian officials that have allegedly perpetrated human rights abuses, in 2016 and 2017 the RWCHR participated in an event for the Canadian Coalition Against Torture with RWCHR's chair testifying before the [Canadian Senate Standing Committee on Foreign Affairs and International Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Senate_Standing_Committee_on_Foreign_Affairs_and_International_Trade) regarding the need to pass Bill S-219, an Act to deter Iran-sponsored terrorism and human rights violations through the levying of sanctions.[^cite_note-\:122-71] In addition to Parliamentary testimony, the RWCHR has used the media to lobby the [Canadian](616255.md) and [U.S. governments](195149.md) to use the power vested in the [Magnitsky Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitsky_Act) to sanction Iranian officials allegedly complicit in rights abuses.[^cite_note-72]

### Saudi Arabia

Main article: [Walid Fitaihi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walid_Fitaihi)

Much of the RWCHR's work regarding human rights in Saudi Arabia relate to [Raif Badawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raif_Badawi), a [prisoner of conscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_conscience) sentenced to 10,000 lashes and 10 years in prison for exercising his right to [freedom of expression](21401843.md). The RWCHR serves as Raif Badawi's international legal counsel.[^cite_note-The_Raif_Badawi_Foundation-43] To secure his freedom, the RWCHR has mounted a public [advocacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advocacy) campaign which has entailed pushing for Badawi to receive honorary Montreal citizenship, leading a rally on the eve of the 5th anniversary of Badawi's imprisonment, and writing op-eds in the [*Huffington Post*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HuffPost), [*Time*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\)) and *[The Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hill_\(newspaper\))*, which were used to point out that Badawi's imprisonment is a violation of [Saudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_system_of_Saudi_Arabia) and [international law](8195726.md).[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] The RWCHR also organized a 'Tweetstorm' on the occasion of the International Day to End Impunity for Crimes against Journalists in 2018, with participants including the likes of [J.K Rowling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._K._Rowling), and [Hillel Neuer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillel_Neuer).[^cite_note-75] The RWCHR has also engaged in many behind-the-scenes [diplomatic efforts](28165911.md). In March 2018, when [Saudi Crown Prince Mohammad bin Salman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_bin_Salman) was in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), the RWCHR's chair handed him a [legal brief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brief_\(law\)) on Badawi's case based on [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Law) and [Saudi Arabian law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_system_of_Saudi_Arabia). The [Crown Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_bin_Salman) said he would consider the brief.[^cite_note-76]

The RWCHR has also heavily criticized the Canadian government's decision to sell billions of dollars of militarized armoured vehicles to [Saudi Arabia](349303.md). The RWCHR's chair authored an op-ed in the *[Montreal Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Gazette)* criticizing the Canadian government for saying that the sale was "consistent with Canada's foreign and defence policies, including human rights" given the "appalling state of human rights in Saudi Arabia".[^cite_note-77] The RWCHR was adamant in saying that the arms deal, which the [Saudi government](27272.md) said was awarded to Canada to cement the friendship between the two countries, should have been leveraged to secure the release of [Raif Badawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raif_Badawi) who has a strong connection to Canada given that his family resides in [Sherbrooke, Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherbrooke).[^cite_note-78]

### Venezuela

RWCHR has made frequent calls to place economic sanctions on Venezuela[^cite_note-79]. In September 2017, the [Secretary General of the Organization of American States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_General_of_the_Organization_of_American_States) appointed the RWCHR's chair to an expert panel intended to investigate whether [Venezuela](32374.md) should be referred to the International Criminal Court for alleged crimes against humanity.[^cite_note-80] In December, the RWCHR co-hosted a civil society roundtable with several [NGOs](46539.md), academics and Parliamentarians which, according to the [Government of Canada](616255.md), helped inform Canada's strategy in dealing with the crisis, especially regarding the government's approach to the [Lima Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_Group).[^cite_note-\:6-17] In April 2018, Brandon Silver, a member of RWCHR's legal team, attended a roundtable discussion with [Canadian Parliamentarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada) and government officials submitting constructive proposals for dealing with the [Venezuelan crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_in_Venezuela_\(2012%E2%80%93present\)). On 30 May 2018, the RWCHR held a press conference in Ottawa with an all-party group of [Parliamentarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Canada) and [Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Canada) to discuss the report,[^cite_note-81] which found [Maduro regime](7161476.md) to have committed crimes against humanity since 2014.[^cite_note-82] To intensify the advocacy against the [Maduro regime](7161476.md), on 2 April 2018, Brandon Silver, published an article in *[The Hill Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hill_Times)* alleging human rights abuses in Venezuela.[^cite_note-83] On 21 June 2018, the RWCHR's chair testified before the European Parliament to discuss the need to refer Venezuela to the International Criminal Court.[^cite_note-84] Finally, on 25 September 2018, several state parties to the [Organization of American States](22788.md) – Argentina, Canada, Chile, Colombia, Paraguay and Peru – jointly referred the situation in Venezuela to the International Criminal Court, marking the first time state parties to the International Criminal Court referred another state party for investigation.[^cite_note-85] As of January 2026, the International Criminal Court has not taken any action on the Venezuela referral.

The RWCHR also serves as legal counsel to [Leopoldo Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldo_L%C3%B3pez), the leader of Venezuela's opposition.[^cite_note-86] In May 2016, RWCHR's chair received a Special Award by the Standing Committee on Foreign Policy, Sovereignty and Integration of the [Venezuelan National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Venezuela\)) for his work on this matter.[^cite_note-87]

In January 2026, RWCHR issued a statement in support of the [2026 United States strikes on Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_strikes_on_Venezuela)[^cite_note-88] and kidnapping of President [Nicolás Maduro](7161476.md).

### Israel

The RWCHR is outspoken in its support for [Israel](9282173.md) and fighting alleged [anti-Semitism](1078.md) by [critics of Israel](30074296.md). As of June 2025 the RWCHR denies the [Gaza genocide](75668427.md)[^cite_note-89], instead blaming the humanitarian crisis and civilian casualties on [Hamas](13913.md), putting it at odds with the United Nations, [Amnesty International](18947898.md), [Doctors Without Borders](20498.md) and [The Lemkin Institute for Study of Genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemkin_Institute). The RWCHR opposes the [Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boycott,_Divestment_and_Sanctions) movement, calling BDS a "new, global, sophisticated, virulent and even lethal form of anti-Semitism" masked "with universal values in attempt to make Israel the enemy of all that is good and the repository of all that is evil".[^cite_note-90] [BDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boycott,_Divestment_and_Sanctions) is, according to the RWCHR, an example of "three Ds": demonization, delegitimization, and double standard applied towards Israel.[^cite_note-91] The RWCHR represents the family of Lt. Hadar Goldin pro bono.[^cite_note-jta.org-92] Lt. Hadar Goldin was a soldier in the [Israeli Defence Forces](39237.md) who was killed by Hamas soldiers during the [2014 Gaza War](43243199.md), hours after a UN-mandated ceasefire took effect.[^cite_note-93] Goldin's body was taken by Hamas who, in violation of international humanitarian law, has refused to repatriate their remains.[^cite_note-94] On 22 December 2017, the RWCHR's Chair appeared before a special session of the UN Security Council alongside Leah Goldin, Hadar's mother, to claim the Palestinian Authority was in standing violation of the [Rome Statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_Statute_of_the_International_Criminal_Court) and [International Criminal Court](14880.md), which the [Palestinian Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) bound themselves to by becoming members of the [International Criminal Court](14880.md) in 2015.[^cite_note-95] The RWCHR's Chair claimed that a state "cannot be a violator of international humanitarian law and also be a member in good standing of the organizations \[that uphold this law]".[^cite_note-jta.org-92] Representatives of the RWCHR were also invited to a meeting of the Committee on Foreign Affairs of the [European Union](9317.md) held under the title "The Humanitarian Rights of Hadar Goldin", which they attended on 20 June 2018.[^cite_note-\:1-96] At this meeting, the RWCHR's Chair gave recommendations for the European Union to take leading a resolution to be adopted by the European Union which declares that Hamas and the Palestinian Authority's holding of Goldin's remains to be in standing violation of international humanitarian law.[^cite_note-\:2-97] The representatives of [Romania](25445.md), [Hungary](13275.md) and [the Netherlands](21148.md) joined RWCHR's Chair in arguing that all [humanitarian aid](434679.md) to Gaza should be conditioned on the return of remains of IDF soldiers and Israeli civilians. On 4 July 2018, the RWCHR's Chair addressed the [Knesset](74656.md) Lobby for Returning Soldiers Home claiming that the Goldin case is not only an Israeli case, but a case anchored in international human rights and humanitarian law and necessitates the involvement of the entire international community.[^cite_note-98] The RWCHR's Chair re-iterated the recommendations he made to the European Parliament at the Knesset arguing that states that have "leverage" over Hamas and the Palestinian Authority have a 'responsibility' to make humanitarian aid conditioned on the Palestinian Authority's compliance with international law.[^cite_note-99]

### China

During his time as a [Member of Parliament](98066.md), the RWCHR's chair spent significant energy on alleged [organ harvesting from Falun Gong practitioners in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_harvesting_from_Falun_Gong_practitioners_in_China). In 2013, he tabled a [private member's bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_member's_bill) (Bill C-561) to this effect.

The RWCHR acts pro bono as international legal counsel to Sun Qian, a Canadian citizen and [Falun Gong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falun_Gong) practitioner who has been detained in [China](5405.md) since February, 2018 being subject to alleged mental and physical torture.[^cite_note-en.tuidang.org-46] The RWCHR also acts as international legal counsel to [Dr. Wang Bingzhang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Bingzhang_\(dissident\)), a Chinese dissident sentenced to life in prison for his role in organizing the [1989 Tiananmen Square Protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Tiananmen_Square_Protests).[^cite_note-\:10-45] To highlight his case, the RWCHR sponsored the screening of "Inside These Walls", a documentary about the plight of Wang Bingzhang, at [Concordia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia_University)'s Cinema Politica. The RWCHR has called on the [Canadian government](616255.md) to use the Magnitsky Act to impose sanctions on Chinese officials involved in Wang's imprisonment.[^cite_note-100]

In 2021, the Centre released a report[^cite_note-101] in partnership with the Washington, D.C.-based [Newlines Institute for Strategy and Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newlines_Institute_for_Strategy_and_Policy) regarding the activities of the Chinese state in Xinjiang, alleging that the Chinese government is responsible for an ongoing [genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Uyghurs_in_China) against the [Uyghur people](31783.md). The report details evidence that a genocide is occurring, including arbitrary detention, forced abortions and sterilization, the forcible transfer of children to other non-Uyghur groups, killings, and creating conditions inconducive to the free expression and practice of minority culture.[^cite_note-102]

### Mauritania

In the words of the RWCHR's chair and executive director, [Irwin Cotler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irwin_Cotler) and Judith Abitan respectively, "Although Mauritania claims that it has abolished slavery, it actually remains one of the world's only bastions of slavery." Accordingly, the RWCHR acts [pro bono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_bono) as international legal counsel to [Biram Dah Abeid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biram_Dah_Abeid), a leader of the international anti-slavery movement and founder and president of the Initiative for the Resurgence of the Abolitionist Movement in Mauritania (IRA-Mauritania).[^cite_note-103] In December 2018, a few months after the RWCHR announced that they had taken up Biram's case, he was released from prison. [Biram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biram_Dah_Abeid) has since declared himself a presidential candidate in the June 2019 elections.

### Cameroon

In 2019, the RWCHR worked together with the [Centre for Human Rights and Democracy in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Human_Rights_and_Democracy_in_Africa) (CHRDA) in documenting human rights violations during the [Anglophone crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglophone_crisis), a civil war involving the [Southern Cameroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cameroons) region of Cameroon that started in 2017. The CHRDA had earlier, in August 2018, published a list of 106 villages that had been raided and burnt down by Cameroonian government forces since October 2017 in the context of the Anglophone crisis. Citing eyewitness accounts, videos and photos as evidence, the CHRDA claimed that the 106 villages had been "attacked, burned down partially or completely and deserted either completely or partially".[^cite_note-JCam_burnt_villages-104]

In May 2019, the RWCHR and CHRDA together claimed that human rights violations in the Anglophone crisis constituted [crimes against humanity](61099.md).[^cite_note-opencanada_RWallenberg-105]
