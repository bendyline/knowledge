**Adamawa** ([Pronunciation](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d7/Ha-Adamawa_state.ogg/Ha-Adamawa_state.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [state](226734.md) in the [North-East geopolitical zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_\(Nigeria\)) of [Nigeria](21383.md), bordered by [Borno](2227628.md) to the northwest, [Gombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombe_State) to the west, and [Taraba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taraba_State) to the southwest while its eastern border forms part of the national [border with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon-Nigeria_border) [Cameroon](5447.md). It takes its name from the historic [emirate of Adamawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamawa_Emirate), with the emirate's old capital of [Yola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yola,_Adamawa) serving as the capital city of Adamawa State. The state was formed in 1991 when the former [Gongola State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongola_State) was broken up into Adamawa and [Taraba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taraba_State) states.[^cite_note-4] The state is one of the most heterogeneous in Nigeria, having over 100 indigenous ethnic groups.

Of the [36 states](226734.md), Adamawa is the [eighth largest in the area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_area), but the [thirteenth least populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_population) with an estimated population of about 4.25 million as of 2016.[^cite_note-5] Geographically, the state is mainly composed of highlands and mountains (the [Atlantika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantika_Mountains), [Mandara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandara_Mountains), and the [Shebshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shebshi_Mountains) ranges) and the [Adamawa Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamawa_Plateau) crossed by valleys and rivers, most notably the [Benue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benue_River) and [Gongola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongola_River) rivers. The lowlands of Adamawa are all part of the [West Sudanian savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Sudanian_savanna) in the north and the wetter [Guinean forest-savanna mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinean_forest-savanna_mosaic) in parts of the south, while elevated areas are parts of the [Mandara Plateau mosaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandara_Plateau_mosaic) and [Cameroonian Highlands forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroonian_Highlands_forests) [ecoregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ecoregions_in_Nigeria). In the extreme south of the state is part of the [Gashaka Gumti National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gashaka_Gumti_National_Park), a large wildlife park that contains large populations of [bushbuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_bushbuck), [African buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_buffalo), [patas monkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patas_monkey), [black-and-white colobus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-and-white_colobus), [giant pangolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_pangolin), and [hippopotamus](37764.md) along with some of Nigeria's last remaining [Nigeria-Cameroon chimpanzee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria-Cameroon_chimpanzee), [African leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_leopard), and [African golden cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_golden_cat) populations.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

What is now known as Adamawa state has been inhabited for years by various ethnic groups, including the Verre, Tsobo, [Bwatiye (Bachama)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bwatiye_languages), [Bali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bali_language_\(Adamawa\)), Bata (Gbwata), [Gudu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudu), [Mbula-Bwazza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbula-Bwazza_language), and Nungurab (Lunguda) in the central region; the [Kamwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamwe_language#People) in the north and central region; the [Jibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jibu_language) in the far south; the [Kilba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilba_people), [Mafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matakam_people), [Marghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marghi_people), and Waga in the north, and the Kwah \[Bah], [Mumuye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumuye_people) in the south while the [Fulani](102383.md) live throughout the state, often as [nomadic herders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulani_herdsmen). Adamawa is also religiously diverse as about 50% of the population is [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) Muslim and 40% is Christian (mainly [Lutheran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Church_of_Christ_in_Nigeria), [EYN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Brethren_in_Nigeria), [ECWA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Church_Winning_All), and [Pentecostal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism)), while the remaining 10% are adherents of traditional ethnic religions.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

As an agriculturally-based state, the Adamawa State economy mainly relies on [livestock](25160767.md) and crops, such as [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), [groundnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut), [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet), [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), [guinea corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_corn), and [yams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yam_\(vegetable\)). Due to the [Boko Haram insurgency](27868253.md) affecting development in the state,[^cite_note-\:3-10] Adamawa has the [eleventh lowest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_Human_Development_Index#2019) [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) in the country but as the [insurgency](27868253.md) has abated since 2016 due to terrorist infighting,[^cite_note-Province_vs_Shekau-11] development has been renewed.[^cite_note-HDI-3][^cite_note-12]

## History

Further information: [Adamawa Emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamawa_Emirate)



Illustration by Edmund D. Morel titled "A Hausa from Yola" (1902)

Before it became a state in Nigeria, Adamawa was a subordinate kingdom of the [Sultanate of Sokoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sokoto), which also included much of northern [Cameroon](5447.md). The rulers bear the title of [emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) ("*lamido*" in the local language, [Fulfulde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulfulde)).[^cite_note-13]

The name "Adamawa" came from the founder of the kingdom, [Modibo Adama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modibo_Adama), a regional leader of the [Fulani Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_jihads) organized by [Usman dan Fodio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usman_dan_Fodio) of Sokoto in 1804. Modibo Adama came from the region of Gurin (now just a small village) and in 1806, received a green flag for leading the jihad in his native country. In the following years, Adama conquered many lands and tribes. In 1838, he moved his capital to Ribadu, and in 1839, to Joboliwo. In 1841, he founded [Yola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yola,_Nigeria), where he died in 1848. After the [European colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_colonialism) (first by [Germany](11867.md) and then by [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland)), the rulers remained as emirs and the line of succession has continued to the present day.



Dancers of Adamawa state in their cultural adornment

In the early 1800s, the [Fulani jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulani_jihad) seized much of modern-day Adamawa State and formed the [Adamawa Emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamawa_Emirate) under the [Sokoto Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokoto_Caliphate). About 90 years later, forces from [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Kamerun) and the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) defeated the Emirate in the [Adamawa Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamawa_Wars) and split the area. The British-controlled area (much of the west of modern-day Adamawa) was incorporated into the [Northern Nigeria Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Nigeria_Protectorate) which later merged into [British Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Nigeria) before becoming independent as [Nigeria](21383.md) in 1960. The German-controlled area formed a part of [German Kamerun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Kamerun) until [allied forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) invaded and occupied Kamerun during the [Kamerun campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamerun_campaign) of [World War I](4764461.md). After the war, what is now eastern Adamawa State became most of the [Northern Cameroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Cameroons) within the [British Cameroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Cameroons) until 1961, when a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_British_Cameroons_referendum) led to a merger with [Nigeria](21383.md).

Originally, modern-day Adamawa State was a part of the post-independence [Northern Region](2292852.md) until 1967 when the area became part of the [North-Eastern State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-Eastern_State). After the North-Eastern State was split in 1976, [Gongola State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongola_State) was formed on 3 February 1976 alongside ten other states. Fifteen years after statehood, Gongola was split with the state's south becoming [Taraba State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taraba_State) while its north became Adamawa State.

A [measles](58911.md) outbreak was reported in an [internally displaced persons](427408.md) camp, in January 2015.[^cite_note-14]

### Boko Haram insurgency

Adamawa State has been badly impacted by the Boko Haram insurgency. In January 2012, Boko Haram [attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2012_Northern_Nigeria_attacks) [Gombi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombi), [Mubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubi_\(town\)) and [Yola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yola,_Nigeria). By 2014, the state became home to camps housing an estimated 35,000 internally displaced people, fleeing violence from Boko Haram in locations including Mubi, [Madagali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagali), [Askira Uba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Askira_uba), [Bama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bama,_Nigeria) and [Gwoza](24822782.md) in the states of Adamawa, Borno, and Yobe. In 2014, an estimate placed the number of IDPs around Yola at 400,000.[^cite_note-Deliver_Relief-15] [An attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2014_Northern_Nigeria_attacks) occurred in Chakawa in 2014. A [suicide](18835454.md) [bombing](265112.md) in Yola [in 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_2015_Yola_bombing) killed over 30 people.[^cite_note-16] A [double suicide bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagali_suicide_bombings) in [Madagali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagali) in 2016 killed over 50 people. Mubi is the worst affected place in Adamawa State, suffering major attacks [in 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Polytechnic,_Mubi_attack), [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Mubi_bombing), [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Mubi_bombing) [and 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Mubi_suicide_bombings).

Organizations serving the community include the Adamawa Peace Initiative (API) - a group of business, religious, and community leaders - and the Adamawa Muslim Council. The [United States Agency for International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Agency_for_International_Development) has pledged to provide continuing humanitarian assistance.[^cite_note-Deliver_Relief-15]

On 21–22 February 2020, Boko Haram [terrorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) launched an attack on homes and churches in Garkida, killing three soldiers and wounding [civilians](145804.md).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Geography

Adamawa is one of the largest states of [Nigeria](21383.md) and occupies about 36,917 square kilometres (14,254 mi<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-19] It is bordered by the states of [Borno](2227628.md) to the northwest, [Gombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombe_State) to the west and [Taraba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taraba_State) to the southwest. Its eastern border forms the national eastern [border with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon-Nigeria_border) [Cameroon](5447.md).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-Mamman_Oyebanji_Petters-21]

Topographically, it is a mountainous land crossed by large river valleys – [Benue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benue_River), [Gongola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongola_River), and [Yedsarem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yedsarem?action=edit\&redlink=1). The valleys of the [Mount Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Cameroon), [Mandara Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandara_Mountains),[^cite_note-22] and [Adamawa Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamawa_Plateau) form part of the landscape.

### Climate change



Global warming has had a negative impact on climate and weather patterns as is visible here with the River Gongola flooding some households and farmlands in Yola.

A study conducted on climate in 2012 in Nigeria's Adamawa state revealed monthly mean temperature rise in Gyawana, Yola, and Mubi, while annual rainfall declined. Delays in rainfall and reduced length of fall were observed in these areas.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

Contrarily, in recent times, the [humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humidity) and temperature of Adamawa state, especially during the dry season, begin to rise in November, which is usually very hot. The [harmattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmattan) period is experienced between December and February every year.[^cite_note-Mamman_Oyebanji_Petters-21]

In recent years, the effect of [climate change](5042951.md) has begun to be more evident. [Tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest) wet and dry weather prevails in Adamawa State. The wet season lasts from April to October, whereas the dry season lasts for at least five months (November to March) yearly. An upsurge in rainfall in September in recent years is usually accompanied by floods.[^cite_note-25] According to [Dr. Sulieman Muhammad,](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Sulieman_Muhammad,?action=edit\&redlink=1) the Executive Secretary of the [Adamawa State Emergency Management Agency](https://adspc.ad.gov.ng/adamawa-state-emergency-management-agency-adsema/) ( ADSEMA), 25 people died in September 2022 due to floods brought on by both the overflow of water from the [Lagdo Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagdo_Reservoir) in [Cameroon](5447.md) due to severe rainfall.[^cite_note-26]

In October 2022, an additional flood claimed 37 lives and submerged 89,000 thousand hectares of farmlands with 58 others sustaining various degrees of injuries.[^cite_note-\:0-27]

Flooding in September 2023 killed 5 people.[^cite_note-28] Flooding the next month across 14 out of 21 [LGAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_areas_of_Nigeria) in Adamawa caused 33 deaths and displaced 51,043 people. 11 temporary settlements across the LGAs of [Yola South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yola_South), [Yola North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yola_North), Lamude, [Madagali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagali), and [Demsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demsa) were set up to house the [internally displaced](427408.md).[^cite_note-29]

## Economy



Mandara Mountains from Yola

Adamawa has vast arable land for agriculture, waterfalls and landscape, and solid minerals like [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), [tantalite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantalite) and [kaoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaolinite). The [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) of the state is estimated to be ₦2.66 trillion. It has the 18th largest economy among the 36 [states of Nigeria](226734.md) and the largest in the [North-East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_\(Nigeria\)) region. The total [revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue) of the state experienced a growth of 9.93% from around ₦700.602 billion in 2020 to ₦777.62 billion in 2021. However, the state heavily depends on federally distributed revenues, with 77.52% of its total revenue being [Gross FAAC](https://rmafc.gov.ng/allocation/) in 2021.[^cite_note-\:1-30] Adamawa had an IGR of ₦8.65 billion in 2021. The state imported \$25.02 million worth of goods between 2019 and 2021. It has a debt stock of ₦132.68 billion as of December 2021, ranking the 19th most indebted state in Nigeria and 2nd most indebted in the [North-East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_\(Nigeria\)).[^cite_note-\:1-30]

The [Dangote group](22094689.md) operates a sugar production factory in [Numan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numan,_Nigeria) which has a sugar refining capacity of 3,000 tonnes of cane per day. The company announced plans to expand to factory to a capacity of 6,000tcd, 9,800tcd and to 15,000 tcd. They also plan to increase the size of the factory from 8,700 hectares in 2022 to about 24,200 hectares within seven years.[^cite_note-31]

The markets found in the state are incredibly important to its economic activity and to its inhabitants. These markets, especially its cattle markets, enhanced the development of economic activities in the state, [cash crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_crop) like [groundnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut) and [beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bean) are grown and crops like [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) and [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum) are grown as food crops. Around [River Yedseram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yedseram_River_valley) and its tributaries, vegetable gardening in the dry season is practised. Fishing is also very popular in the riverine areas. The breeds of cattle found in Adamawa are; [Adamawa Gudali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamawa_cattle), Sokoto Gudali, [White Fulani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Fulani), Ambala, Red Sokoto and [Red Fulani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Fulani). The state has around 1.5 million cattle and 64 grazing reserves of which 30 are gazetted.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

Adamawa state has a thriving [livestock](25160767.md) industry especially cattle-rearing. It has 2.5 million heads of cattle. Traders come from all over the country and West Africa to buy and sell cattle in its markets, such as the Mubi International cattle market in [Mubi South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubi_South). Despite the state's high level of cattle production, the markets are poorly developed without essential services like meat processing. Meaning, live animals have to be transported in trucks for 4 days before they reach cities with high demand to regions like the [South-West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_\(Nigeria\)), [South-South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_South) or [South-East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_\(Nigeria\)) Nigeria.

The development of many communities in the state can be traced to the colonial era when the Germans ruled a swath of territory known as the Northern and Southern [Kameruns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamerun) from [Dikwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dikwa) in the North to Victoria ([Limbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbe,_Cameroon)) on the Atlantic coast in the 19th century. These were, however, handed over as [United Nations Trust Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Trust_Territories) to the British at the end of the [World War I](4764461.md) with the signing of the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles). After a series of referendums, the Northern Kameruns joined Nigeria to form the then Sardauna Province and the Southern Kameruns formed a Confederation with French-speaking Cameroon.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

## Religion

Adamawa is a Muslim-majority state in Nigeria, with a substantial Christian population.[^cite_note-37] Historically, Adamawa is home to the major happenings of the Islamic Jihad, led by the [Sokoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokoto_Caliphate) [Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate) in the early 1800s.

Adamawa is also home to the [headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headquarters) of two [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) churches, the [Church of the Brethren in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Brethren_in_Nigeria) (EYN Church) with its headquarters in mararaba Mubi in the northern zone of the state, and the [Lutheran Church of Christ in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Church_of_Christ_in_Nigeria) (LCCN Church) with headquarters in Numan in the southern zone of the state.[^cite_note-38] The Church of the Brethren in Nigeria (EYN church) was founded in Garkida, Gombi Local Government of the state, in March 1923 by American missionaries.[^cite_note-39] [The Lutheran Church](23371382.md) of Christ in Nigeria (LCCN Church) was founded in Numan by Dutch missionaries in 1913.[^cite_note-40] 4.5% Catholic with 192,767 followers (2020) in the Diocese of Yola (1950) with 39 parishes under Bishop Stephen Dami Mamza (2011) \[<https://www.catholic-hierarchy.org/diocese/dyola.html> Yola (Diocese) Catholic-Hierarchy. The Anglican Diocese of Yola (1990) with 39 parishes is led by Bishop Markus Ibrahim (2020), also the Archbishop of the Province of Jos [Anglican Diocese of Yola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Yola).

### Electoral system

The electoral system of each state is selected using a modified two-round system. To be elected in the first round, a candidate must receive the plurality of the vote and over 25% of the vote in at least two-thirds of the State local government Areas. If no candidate passes the threshold, a second round will be held between the top candidate and the next candidate to have received a plurality of votes in the highest number of local government areas.[^cite_note-56]
