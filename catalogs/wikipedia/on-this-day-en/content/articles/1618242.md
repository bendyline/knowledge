The **Lebanese Parliament** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مجلس النواب, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Majlis an-Nuwwab*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Chamber of Deputies') is the *de facto* [unicameral](204304.md) national legislature of the [Lebanese Republic](17771.md). According to Article 22 of the [Constitution of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Lebanon), amended after the [Taif Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taif_Agreement), this chamber, known as the Chamber of Deputies, is the *de jure* lower house of the Parliament which elects members on a non-sectarian basis while the Senate is the *de jure* upper house that elects its religious representatives on a sectarian basis as written in the [National Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Pact).

There are 128 members elected to a four-year term in [multi-member constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district), apportioned among Lebanon's diverse [Christian](4796145.md) and [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Lebanon) denominations but with half of the seats reserved for Christians and half for Muslims per Constitutional Article 24.[^cite_note-17] Lebanon has [universal adult suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage). The parliament's major functions are to elect the [President](2032981.md) of the republic, to approve the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_Lebanon) (although appointed by the President, the [Prime Minister](508949.md), along with the Cabinet, must retain the confidence of a majority in the Parliament), and to approve laws and expenditure.&#x20;

The Parliament was most recently [elected on 15 May 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Lebanese_general_election). While terms are four years long, parliaments can extend their own terms: the parliament [elected in June 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Lebanese_general_election) did so on three occasions, delaying the next election until [May 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Lebanese_general_election) while a new electoral law was prepared. According to the Lebanese constitution[^cite_note-18] and the electoral law of 2017,[^cite_note-19] elections are held on a Sunday during the 60 days preceding the end of the sitting parliament's mandate, with the next one due in May 2028.

## Allocation of seats

A unique feature of the Lebanese system is the principle of "confessional distribution": each religious community has an allotted number of deputies in the Parliament in a form of [consociationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consociationalism).

In elections held between 1932 and [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Lebanese_general_election), seats were apportioned between Christians and Muslims in a 6\:5 ratio, with various denominations of the two faiths allocated representation roughly proportional to their size. By the 1960s, Muslims had become openly dissatisfied with this system, aware that their own higher birthrate and the higher emigration rate among Christians had by this time almost certainly produced a Muslim majority, which the parliamentary distribution did not reflect. Christian politicians were unwilling to abolish or alter the system, however, and it was one of the factors in the [1975–1990 civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War). The [Taif Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taif_Agreement) of 1989, which ended the civil war, reapportioned the Parliament to provide for equal representation of Christians and Muslims, with each electing 64 of the 128 deputies.

Although distributed confessionally, all members, regardless of their religious faith, are elected by universal suffrage, forcing politicians to seek support from outside of their own religious communities, unless their co-religionists overwhelmingly dominate their particular constituency.

## Political parties

Main article: [List of political parties in Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Lebanon)

Numerous [political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Lebanon) exist in Lebanon. Many parties are little more than *ad hoc* electoral lists, formed by negotiation among influential local figures representing the various confessional communities; these lists usually function only for the election, and do not form identifiable groupings in the parliament subsequently. Other parties are personality-based, often comprising followers of a present or past political leader or warlord. Few parties are based, in practice, on any particular ideology, although in theory most claim to be.

No single party has ever won more than 12.5 percent of the total number of seats in the Parliament, and until 2005, no coalition ever won more than a third of the total. The [general election held in 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Lebanese_general_election), however, resulted in a clear majority (72 seats out of 128) being won by the alliance led by [Saad Hariri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saad_Hariri) (son of murdered former Prime Minister [Rafik Hariri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafic_Hariri)); half of these were held by Hariri's own [Future Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Movement).

## Speaker

Main article: [List of Speakers of the Parliament of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Speakers_of_the_Parliament_of_Lebanon)



[Nabih Berri](592775.md), incumbent speaker



[Elias Bou Saab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elias_Bou_Saab), incumbent deputy speaker

The Speaker of the Parliament, who by custom must be a [Shi'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi'a_Islam_in_Lebanon) Muslim, is now elected to a four-year term, and is the highest office in the parliament.[^cite_note-20] Prior to the [Taif Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taif_Agreement), they were elected to a two-year term. They form part of a "troika", together with the President (required to be a [Maronite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronite_Christianity_in_Lebanon) Christian) and the Prime Minister (a [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam_in_Lebanon) Muslim). The privileges of the Speaker are unusually powerful, relative to other democratic systems. The current speaker is the leader of the [Amal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amal_Movement) Party, [Nabih Berri](592775.md).[^cite_note-21]

### Deputy Speaker

The [Deputy Speaker of the Parliament of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Speaker_of_the_Parliament_of_Lebanon) is the second-highest-ranking official of the [Lebanese Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Parliament). The office is always attributed to a [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Greek_Orthodox_Christians) practitioner.[^cite_note-22]

## Parliamentary committees

The Lebanese parliament has sixteen committees to facilitate the legislative process and provide oversight on specific areas of government policy.[^cite_note-23] The Finance and Budget Committee is considered the most important, as it reviews the government's budget and spending plans. Other influential committees include Administration and Justice, Health and Labor, and Economy and Industry.

## Electoral system

Main article: [Elections in Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Lebanon)

According to the Lebanese constitution[^cite_note-25] and the electoral law of 2017,[^cite_note-26] elections are held on a Sunday during the 60 days preceding the end of the sitting parliament's mandate. In June 2017, a new electoral law was passed. While in the previous system, the 128 members of parliament were elected from 26 multi-member constituencies under [plurality block voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_block_voting), and the candidates with the highest number of votes within each religious community were elected,[^cite_note-IPU-27] the new electoral law instituted [proportional representation](23298.md) in 15 multi-member constituencies while still maintaining the confessional distribution.[^cite_note-28] However, the 7 out of the 15 of the electoral districts are divided into 2 or more 'minor districts' (largely corresponding to the smaller electoral districts from the old electoral law).[^cite_note-el-29] Where applicable, [preference vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_list) is counted on the 'minor district' level.[^cite_note-30]

## Parliament building



Lebanese Parliament Building 1947

The Parliament building was designed by [Mardiros Altounian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardiros_Altounian), who was also the architect of the [Étoile clock tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nejmeh_Square). The building was completed in 1934 during the [French Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Mandate) period. Advised to build in the spirit of [Lebanese](17771.md) tradition, the architect visited the Emirs' palaces in the [Chouf Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chouf_Mountains). He also drew inspiration from the [Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental) styles developed in Paris, [Istanbul](3391396.md), and [Cairo](6293.md) at the turn of the 20th century.
