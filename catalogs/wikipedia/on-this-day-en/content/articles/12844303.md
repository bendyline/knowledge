The **Constitution of Bosnia and Herzegovina** ([Serbo-Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbo-Croatian_language): *Ustav Bosne i Hercegovine*, Устав Босне и Херцеговине) is the supreme law of [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md). The current text is **Annex 4** to the [General Framework Agreement for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement), initiated on 21 November 1995 and signed on 14 December 1995.[^cite_note-OHR-Annex4-1][^cite_note-OSCE-GFAP-2] The Constitution affirms state continuity, establishes institutions, allocates competences between the State and the Entities, and incorporates extensive human-rights guarantees with explicit reference to the [European Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights).[^cite_note-OHR-Text-3]

## Text and structure

The Constitution consists of a [Preamble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preamble) and twelve Articles (I–XII), with annexes cross-referencing human-rights treaties.[^cite_note-OHR-Text-3] Article I defines the State and citizenship; Article II incorporates the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights) (ECHR) and enumerates rights; Article III distributes competences between the State and the [Entities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entities_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) (with provision for “additional responsibilities” by agreement); Article IV establishes the bicameral [Parliamentary Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina); Article V defines the three-member [Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina); Article VI establishes the [Constitutional Court](11273863.md); Article VII provides for the [Central Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina); Articles VIII–XII cover finances, general and transitional provisions, and amendments.[^cite_note-OHR-Annex4-1]

## Human rights

Under Article II(2), the ECHR has direct applicability and priority over other law in Bosnia and Herzegovina; Annex I lists additional human-rights instruments binding on the State.[^cite_note-OHR-Annex4-1] The Constitution prohibits discrimination and guarantees return of refugees and displaced persons (Article II(4)–(5)).[^cite_note-OHR-Text-3]

## Institutions and competences

The [Parliamentary Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) comprises the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Bosnia_and_Herzegovina\)) and the [House of Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Peoples_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina).[^cite_note-OHR-Annex4-1] The collective [Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) has three members (a Bosniak and a Croat from the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina, and a Serb from Republika Srpska).[^cite_note-OHR-Annex4-1] The [Constitutional Court](11273863.md) (Article VI) “shall uphold this Constitution,” with jurisdiction to resolve disputes under the Constitution and to review the constitutionality of laws.[^cite_note-OHR-Text-3] The [Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) (Article VII) maintains monetary stability.[^cite_note-OHR-Annex4-1]

## Amendment procedure

Article X provides that the Constitution may be amended by decision of the Parliamentary Assembly, including a two-thirds majority of those present and voting in the House of Representatives. No amendment may eliminate or diminish the rights and freedoms in Article II.[^cite_note-OHR-Annex4-1] The Constitution has been amended once (**Amendment I**, 2009) to integrate the [Brčko District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%8Dko_District) into the constitutional text.[^cite_note-Amendment-I-4]

## Judicial interpretation

In the landmark **U-5/98 (“Constituent Peoples”)** case (2000), the Constitutional Court interpreted key provisions concerning equality and representation of the three constituent peoples across both entities.[^cite_note-CC-U598-5] Subsequent analysis by the [Venice Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission_for_Democracy_through_Law) addressed implementation and broader constitutional implications.[^cite_note-VC-2005-004-6]

## Compliance with the European Convention on Human Rights

The [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights) has delivered several judgments concerning constitutional arrangements:

- ***Sejdić and Finci v. Bosnia and Herzegovina*** (Grand Chamber, 22 December 2009) held that ineligibility of persons not belonging to the constituent peoples to stand for the Presidency and House of Peoples was discriminatory.

  [^cite_note-ECtHR-SejdicFinci-7]

- ***Zornić v. Bosnia and Herzegovina*** (15 July 2014) found a violation where a citizen refusing to declare affiliation with a constituent people was ineligible.

  [^cite_note-ECtHR-Zornic-8]

- ***Pilav v. Bosnia and Herzegovina*** (9 June 2016) found a violation regarding a Bosniak resident of Republika Srpska ineligible to stand for the Presidency.

  [^cite_note-ECtHR-Pilav-9]

- ***Pudarić v. Bosnia and Herzegovina*** (8 December 2020) found a violation where a politician residing in the Federation could not run for the Serb seat in the Presidency.

  [^cite_note-ECtHR-Pudaric-10]

- ***Kovačević v. Bosnia and Herzegovina***—Chamber judgment of 29 August 2023 found a violation of Article 1 of Protocol No. 12 in respect of representation in the House of Peoples and the Presidency; however, on 25 June 2025 the Grand Chamber held the application inadmissible (abuse of process; lack of victim status).

  [^cite_note-ECtHR-Kovacevic-2023-11]

  [^cite_note-ECtHR-Kovacevic-GC-Operative-12]

  [^cite_note-ECtHR-Kovacevic-GC-PR-13]

## Reform proposals

In 2006, a U.S.-facilitated compromise—popularly known as the **“April Package”**—sought to modernize institutions (streamlining the Presidency, strengthening the Council of Ministers and State competences) but failed in the House of Representatives by two votes.[^cite_note-US-DoS-2006-14] The [Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Council_of_Europe) took note of the outcome in Resolution 1513 (2006).[^cite_note-PACE-1513-15] The [Venice Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commission_for_Democracy_through_Law) issued an opinion on the 2006 draft amendments.[^cite_note-VC-2006-019-16] Subsequent reform discussions have continued in light of the ECtHR case-law.
