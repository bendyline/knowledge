**Cristian Mungiu** (Romanian: [\[kristiˈan munˈdʒi.u\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Romanian)) is a Romanian filmmaker. A figure in the [Romanian New Wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_New_Wave), his films usually explore Romania's contemporary society and politics. Mungiu is best known for his [Palme d'Or](612946.md)–winning films *[4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_Months,_3_Weeks_and_2_Days)* (2007) and *[Fjord](82004708.md)* (2026). For *[Beyond the Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_the_Hills)* (2012) and *[Graduation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduation_\(2016_film\))* (2016), he won [Best Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival_Award_for_Best_Screenplay) and [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival_Award_for_Best_Director) prizes at the [65th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Cannes_Film_Festival) and [69th Cannes Film Festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Cannes_Film_Festival), respectively.

## Early life and education

Cristian Mungiu was born in [Iași](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ia%C8%99i) on April 27, 1968. His sister is political analyst [Alina Mungiu-Pippidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alina_Mungiu-Pippidi).

After studying [English literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_literature) at the [Alexandru Ioan Cuza University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_Ioan_Cuza_University), he worked for a few years as a teacher and journalist. He subsequently enrolled at the [I. L. Caragiale National University of Theatre and Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._L._Caragiale_National_University_of_Theatre_and_Film) in [Bucharest](36877.md) to study film directing, graduating in 1998.

## Career

Mungiu made several short films after graduating. In 2002, he made his feature debut with *[Occident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occident_\(film\))*, which was selected for the [Directors' Fortnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directors'_Fortnight) of the [55th Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Cannes_Film_Festival). The film received critical acclaim, winning prizes at several film festivals.

In 2007, Mungiu wrote and directed his second feature, *[4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_Months,_3_Weeks_and_2_Days)*. The film premiered in Competition at the [60th Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Cannes_Film_Festival), where it received critical acclaim and won the [Palme d'Or](612946.md), marking the first time the prize was awarded to a Romanian filmmaker.[^cite_note-1] The film was selected as the [Romanian entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Romanian_submissions_for_the_Academy_Award_for_Best_International_Feature_Film) for [Best Foreign Language Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_International_Feature_Film) at the [80th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/80th_Academy_Awards).[^cite_note-2]



Mungiu in 2012

His 2012 film *[Beyond the Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_the_Hills)* was screened in Competition at the [65th Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Cannes_Film_Festival),[^cite_note-3] where Mungiu won the award for [Best Screenplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival_Award_for_Best_Screenplay), with its two lead actresses [Cristina Flutur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristina_Flutur) and [Cosmina Stratan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmina_Stratan) sharing the award for [Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Actress_Award_\(Cannes_Film_Festival\)).[^cite_note-4] The film was also selected as the [Romanian entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Romanian_submissions_for_the_Academy_Award_for_Best_International_Feature_Film) for [Best Foreign Language Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_International_Feature_Film) at the [85th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/85th_Academy_Awards),[^cite_note-5] making the December shortlist.[^cite_note-6] In May 2013, he served as a jury member at the [66th Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Cannes_Film_Festival).[^cite_note-7]

In 2016, Mungiu produced [Nae Caranfil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nae_Caranfil)'s *[6.9 on the Richter Scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6.9_on_the_Richter_Scale)*.[^cite_note-8] He also directed the tragedy [*Graduation*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduation_\(2016_film\)), which premiered at the [69th Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Cannes_Film_Festival), where he shared the [Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival_Award_for_Best_Director) prize with France’s [Olivier Assayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Assayas) for *[Personal Shopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_Shopper)*.[^cite_note-Cannes69-9] In June 2016, Mungiu was invited to join the [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Motion_Picture_Arts_and_Sciences).[^cite_note-10]

In 2021, Mungiu presided over the [Critics' Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics'_Week) jury of the [74th Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Cannes_Film_Festival).[^cite_note-11] In 2022, Mungiu's feature *[R.M.N.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.M.N.)* was selected in Competition at the [75th Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Cannes_Film_Festival). The film's co-producers include the [Dardenne brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dardenne_brothers)' Les Films du Fleuve.[^cite_note-12]

His 2026 drama film *[Fjord](82004708.md)*, starring [Sebastian Stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Stan) and [Renate Reinsve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Reinsve),[^cite_note-13] won the [Palme d'Or](612946.md) at the [79th Cannes Film Festival](82368000.md), making him the tenth filmmaker to win the award twice.[^cite_note-Cannes79-14]

## Influences and style

Mungiu has said that [Miloš Forman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo%C5%A1_Forman) and [Robert Altman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Altman) are important filmmakers who influenced him. He also respects the realism of *[Bicycle Thieves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_Thieves)*, among other famous realistic films.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]
