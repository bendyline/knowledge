# Maternity hospital



The [Rotunda Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotunda_Hospital) in [Ireland](147575.md), the oldest continuously-operating maternity hospital in the world



*Kätilöopisto*, a former maternity hospital in [Helsinki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki), [Finland](10577.md) in 2019



In a maternity hospital in [Yakutsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakutsk), [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) in 1981

A **maternity hospital** specializes in caring for women during [pregnancy](1771587.md) and [childbirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childbirth). It also provides care for newborn [infants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant), and may act as a centre for clinical training in [midwifery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwifery) and [obstetrics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstetrics). Formerly known as [lying-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lying-in) hospitals, most of them, like [cottage hospitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottage_hospital), have been absorbed into larger [general hospitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_hospital), where they operate as the maternity department.

## History



The premises of the former [General Lying-In Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Lying-In_Hospital), now a hotel

Maternity hospitals in the United Kingdom can be traced back to a number of 18th century establishments in [London](17867.md) and [Dublin](8504.md). Prior to these foundations, childbirth was a domestic occasion. The term coined for these establishments, but now [archaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaism), is "a lying-in hospital", referring to the custom of [lying-in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lying-in), prolonged [bedrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedrest) after childbirth, better known now as [postpartum confinement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postpartum_confinement).

The first noted lying-in hospital appears to be one founded by Sir [Richard Manningham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Manningham) in [Jermyn Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jermyn_Street), London, in 1739 and which evolved into the [Queen Charlotte's Maternity Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Charlotte's_and_Chelsea_Hospital). A better documented foundation is that of the [Dublin Lying-In Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotunda_Hospital), established in 1745 by [Bartholomew Mosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartholomew_Mosse), and which served as a model for three subsequent London foundations: the [British Lying-In Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Lying-In_Hospital), a 1749 establishment in [Holborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holborn); the 1750 [City of London Lying-In Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London_Maternity_Hospital), in [the City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London); and the [General Lying-In Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Lying-In_Hospital) on [Westminster Bridge Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Bridge_Road), established in 1767.[^cite_note-AIM25-1][^cite_note-LMA-2][^cite_note-Ryan-3] A number of other such hospitals were formed in the mid-18th century. All of these were run by male physicians, women being blocked from completing training as doctors until the 1870s.

The first maternity hospital founded and run by a woman was [Elizabeth Garrett Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Garrett_Anderson)'s [New Hospital for Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hospital_for_Women), which evolved from an existing dispensary in the 1770s, and was renamed in 1918 the Elizabeth Garrett Anderson Hospital.[^cite_note-UCLH-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Its work continues in the modern Elizabeth Garrett Anderson maternity wing of [University College Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Hospital), part of UCLH NHS Foundation Trust.

### Today

The [Portland Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Hospital) in central London was created in 1983 as a [private hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_hospital), i.e. not part of the [National Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Service). Also in 1983, the [Rosie Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosie_Hospital) opened in Cambridge, next to [Addenbrooke's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addenbrooke's_Hospital).

The [National Maternity Hospital, Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Maternity_Hospital,_Dublin) is the largest mother-and-baby hospital in Ireland.
