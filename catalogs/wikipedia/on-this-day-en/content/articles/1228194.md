The **South African Police Service** (**SAPS**) is the national police force of the [Republic of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_South_Africa). Its 1,165 police stations in South Africa are divided according to the [provincial borders](53215.md), and a Provincial Commissioner is appointed in each province.[^cite_note-AR25-3][^cite_note-4] The nine Provincial Commissioners report directly to the **National Commissioner**. The head office is in the Wachthuis Building in [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria).[^cite_note-5]

The [Constitution of South Africa](441610.md) lays down that the South African Police Service has a responsibility to prevent, combat and investigate crime, maintain public order, protect and secure the inhabitants of the Republic and their property, uphold and enforce the law, create a safe and secure environment for all people in South Africa, prevent anything that may threaten the safety or security of any community, investigate any crimes that threaten the safety or security of any community, ensure criminals are brought to justice and participate in efforts to address the causes of crime.[^cite_note-6]

In 2013, [Amnesty International](18947898.md) expressed concern over South African [police brutality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality), including torture and extrajudicial killings.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## History

### Colonial years

The South African Police Service traces its origin to the Dutch Watch, a paramilitary organisation formed by settlers in the [Cape Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Province) in 1655 to protect civilians and to maintain law and order. In 1795, British officials assumed control over the Dutch Watch, and in 1825 established the Cape Constabulary (which became the [Cape Town](6653.md) Police Force in 1840).[^cite_note-Irish_Times_-_The_Irish_in_South_Africa-10]

In 1854, a police force was established in [Durban](313650.md) which would become the Durban Borough Police, and in 1935 the Durban City Police (DCP).[^cite_note-csvr.org.za_-_Metropolitan_Police_Services-11] Act 3 of 1855 established the Frontier Armed and Mounted Police Force in the [Eastern Cape](313645.md), restyled as the Cape Mounted Riflemen in 1878.[^cite_note-Irish_Times_-_The_Irish_in_South_Africa-10]

### 1913-1994

Main article: [South African Police](3281856.md)

The [South African Police](3281856.md) (SAP) was created after the establishment of the [Union of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa) in 1910. Four years later, the Mounted Riflemen's Association relinquished its civilian responsibilities to the SAP as most of its riflemen left to serve in the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War). The SAP and the military maintained a close relationship even after the SAP assumed permanent responsibility for domestic law and order in 1926. Police officials often called on the army for support in case of emergencies. During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), one SAP brigade served with the [2nd Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_2nd_Infantry_Division) of the [South African Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Army) in North Africa.

When the [National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_\(South_Africa\)) (NP) edged out its more liberal opponents in nationwide elections in 1948, the new government enacted legislation that strengthened the relationship between the police and the military. Police subsequently became heavily armed, especially when facing unruly or hostile crowds.

The Police Act (No. 7) of 1958 broadened the mission of the SAP beyond conventional police functions, and allowed police to quell civil unrest and conduct counterinsurgency operations. The Police Amendment Act (No. 70) of 1965 allowed police to detain any person, receptacle, vehicle, aircraft, or premises within one mile of any national border, and to seize anything found without a warrant. This search-and-seize zone was extended to within eight miles of any border in 1979 and to the entire country in 1983.

### Post-apartheid (1994–present)

The new Minister of Safety and Security, [Sydney Mufamadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Mufamadi), obtained police training assistance from [Zimbabwe](34399.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md), and [Canada](5042916.md) and proclaimed that racial tolerance and human rights would be central to police training in the future. By the end of 1995, the SAPS had incorporated the ten police agencies of the former [homelands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantustan), and had reorganised at both national and provincial level.

The Investigative Psychology Unit (IPU), also referred to as Investigative Psychology Section (IPS), was founded by forensic psychologist [Micki Pistorius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micki_Pistorius)[^cite_note-Labuschagne-12] in 1996. Elmarie Myburgh was a founding member. It was then a division of the Serious and Violent Crimes Unit, later being moved to the Detective Service, and from June 2008 to the Criminal Record and Forensic Science Service (CRFSS). At that time, it comprised only three members at national head office level, led by Gerard Labuschagne.[^cite_note-13]

### Rank structure

A new ranking system of the South African Police Service was adopted in April 2010.[^cite_note-15] The change caused some controversy as new ranks like "general" and "colonel" have a military connotation. Furthermore, the new rank system mirrors the system used by the [South African Police](3281856.md) during the [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) era. In 2009, Deputy Minister of Police [Fikile Mbalula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fikile_Mbalula) spoke of making the police a paramilitary force by changing the SAPS ranking system so that it would closely mirror the military ranking system. This created a significant amount of controversy from people critical of what they called the "militarisation" of the police.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

The ranking system was amended in 2016. The role of regional police commissioner was introduced, with the rank of lieutenant general. The major and lieutenant ranks were eliminated, with lieutenants assuming the rank of captain and majors assuming the rank of lieutenant colonel.[^cite_note-rank_reform_iol-19][^cite_note-rank_reform_ewn-20]

### Firearms



Officers with [Vektor R5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R4_assault_rifle) rifles in [Johannesburg](58893.md), 2010

South African Police Service officers generally carry a Vektor Z88 9mm [pistol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistol) and [pepper spray](157302.md). Each patrol car is usually also equipped a [R5 rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R4_assault_rifle). To quell disturbances a variety of firearms are used, including [BXP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milkor_9mm_BXP_Sub-Machine_Gun) sub-machine gun, Musler 12 gauge shotgun (capable of firing anti-riot rubber bullets contained in standard 12 bore shotgun cartridges), as well as [tear gas](1202646.md) and pencil flares.

The R1 rifle has been withdrawn from all front-line police armories since the mid-1990s, but is still used by elements of the [Special Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Police_Service_Special_Task_Force).

## Criticism and controversies

Public trust in the South African Police Service between 1998 and 2024.[^cite_note-HSRC1-30] View [chart definition](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Data\:Template%20line.chart).

### Administration

Since the departure of democratic South Africa's first National commissioner George Fivaz in January 2000, a number of successive commissioners have been unable to complete a single term in office, most implicated in and charged with misconduct.[^cite_note-khaas-31][^cite_note-edit-32]

The distribution of personnel has been controversial, with local legislators questioning why areas most in need of policing resources are being neglected.[^cite_note-33]

The department was criticised by the Western Cape Government for providing the lowest number personnel (adjusted for population) with a shortage of 2,392 officers,[^cite_note-34] despite having the highest murder rate.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] This was the subject of the [Khayelitsha Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khayelitsha_Commission) in 2012.

### Brutality and repression

Main article: [Political repression in post-apartheid South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_repression_in_post-apartheid_South_Africa)

[Amnesty International](18947898.md) has expressed concerns about police brutality, including torture and extrajudicial killings, in South Africa.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] There has also been concern about brutal training methods for the police.[^cite_note-43] According to Peter Jordi from the Wits Law Clinic "\[Police] Torture is spiralling out of control. It is happening everywhere."[^cite_note-44] Brandon Edmonds argues that "The cops prey on the poor in this country."[^cite_note-45] Independent studies have confirmed that the SAPS has been used to repress peaceful marches.[^cite_note-46]

In April 2009, SAPS attempted to ban [unFreedom Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UnFreedom_Day)[^cite_note-47] and was implicated in support for September 2009 ANC mob that attacked the elected leadership of the shack settlement at [Kennedy Road, Durban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Road,_Durban).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] Police officers have also been accused of excessive policing in [Blikkiesdorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blikkiesdorp) in [Delft, Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delft,_Cape_Town), by suppression of freedom and ordering illegal curfews.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

In 2011, 630 police officers from [Gauteng Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauteng_Province) were arrested for fraud and corruption, rape and murder.[^cite_note-54] An April 2012 editorial in *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TimesLIVE)* opined: "It seems torture and outright violation of human rights is becoming the order of the day for some of our police officers and experts warn that the line between criminals and our law enforcement officers is "blurred"."[^cite_note-55]

In February 2013, police in [Daveyton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daveyton), Gauteng were caught on video brutalising [Mido Macia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Mido_Macia), a [Mozambican](19301.md) taxi driver accused of parking illegally. Macia was handcuffed to a police van and dragged through the streets, later succumbing to his injuries.[^cite_note-56] Eight police officers were arrested and later convicted of murder.[^cite_note-57]

Three police officers were arrested for the controversial [shooting of Nathaniel Julies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_of_Nathaniel_Julies), a 16 year-old boy with [down syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_syndrome), in [Eldorado Park.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eldorado_Park,_Gauteng)[^cite_note-58]

#### Marikana massacre

Main article: [Marikana miner strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marikana_miner_strike)

The [Marikana Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marikana_Massacre),[^cite_note-59] was a mass shooting that occurred when police broke up a gathering by striking [Lonmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonmin) workers on a 'koppie' (hilltop) near the Nkaneng shack settlement in [Marikana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marikana) on 16 August 2012. 34 miners were killed and 78 miners injured, causing anger and public outcry, fueled by reports that most of the victims were shot from behind[^cite_note-60] and many shot far from police lines.[^cite_note-61]

It later emerged that the violence had actually started on 11 August when leaders from the National Union of Mineworkers opened fire on striking NUM members killing two.[^cite_note-62] It is alleged that police did nothing in the aftermath thereby creating a situation in which workers felt that they would have to use other means to protect themselves.[^cite_note-63]

Between 12 and 14 August, approximately 8 more people were killed including two policemen and two security guards.[^cite_note-64] It is the country's deadliest incident between police and the civilian population since the [Sharpeville Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharpeville_Massacre) of 1960, and has been referred to as a turning-point in post-1994 South Africa.[^cite_note-thinkafricapress.com-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

## Corruption

On 10 September 2007 an arrest warrant was issued by the National Prosecuting Authority (NPA) for National Police Commissioner [Jackie Selebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Selebi) ([Interpol](14338129.md) president from 2004 to 2008). On 23 September 2007, President Mbeki suspended NPA Head Vusi Pikoli, allegedly because of "an irretrievable breakdown" in the relationship between Pikoli and Justice Minister Brigitte Mabandla.

However, [journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalist) at the *[Mail and Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mail_and_Guardian)*, a major South African news publication, claim to have solid information supporting the widespread suspicion that President Mbeki suspended Pikoli as part of a bid to shield Police Commissioner Selebi.[^cite_note-mail-68] According to the *Mail and Guardian* on 5 October 2007 the NPA was investigating Selebi for corruption, fraud, racketeering and defeating the ends of justice.[^cite_note-mail-68] Selebi was found guilty of corruption in July 2010, but not guilty of further charges of perverting the course of justice.[^cite_note-guilty-69]

In February 2011 [Bheki Cele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bheki_Cele) was implicated in unlawful conduct and [maladministration](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/maladministration) with a R500m lease agreement for the new police headquarters in Pretoria.[^cite_note-70] In October 2011, President [Jacob Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma) announced that Cele had been suspended pending an investigation into the agreement.[^cite_note-71] After recommendation from a board of inquiry, Zuma dismissed Cele and announced that [Riah Phiyega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riah_Phiyega), the first female commissioner, would replace him.[^cite_note-72]

In February 2018, SAPS Lieutenant-General Khomotso Phahlane, who was also former acting SAPS Commissioner, and his wife appeared in court on charges of fraud and corruption.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] On 30 July 2020, Phahlane was dismissed from the police after three years on suspension.[^cite_note-convictedandis-76] The same day, he was found guilty of dishonest conduct.[^cite_note-convictedandis-76]

On 12 July 2019, it was announced the five North West Province police officers were arrested during the week in three separate corruption cases.[^cite_note-77] On 4 June 2020, six senior [Gauteng](309661.md) police officers where among 14 people arrested on corruption charges.[^cite_note-78] Two other senior officers, now retired, were arrested as well. Among the Guateng-based SAPS officers charged with corruption included three brigadiers and a retired SAPS Lieutenant General,[^cite_note-gauteng-79]

On 12 October 2020, Lieutenant-General [Bonang Mgwenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonang_Mgwenya?action=edit\&redlink=1), the country's second-most senior police official, was arrested on charges of corruption, fraud, theft and money laundering involving about R200-million and afterwards appeared in Ridge Magistrates court. At the time of Mgwenya's arrest, she and Phahlane were among 14 fellow officers who were charged with corruption.[^cite_note-sapsarrested-80] Mgwenya was suspended on 15 October 2020 and was dismissed from SAPS on 13 November 2020.[^cite_note-81]

On 23 December 2020, four [Cape Town](6653.md) police officers attached to the national border control unit at Cape Town International Airport were arrested for extorting money from Chinese businesses.[^cite_note-82]

On 23 December 2020, Peter Ntsime, Acting Deputy General Secretary of the [South African Policing Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Policing_Union) (SAPU), reported that Colonel Kamelash Dalip Singh, a senior SAPU policeman from the KwaZulu-Natal Provincial Anti-Corruption Unit, was arrested, and then released on bail, on a bribe charge. Ntsime criticised the arrest, stating Singh was at the forefront of arresting crooked police officers and was onto a big syndicate. [Directorate for Priority Crime Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawks_\(South_Africa\)) (Hawks) spokesperson, Captain Simphiwe Mhlongo, said that undercover Hawks officers caught Singh red-handed accepting a R5,000 bribe.[^cite_note-arrestcrook-83]

On 28 December 2020, three law enforcement officers who were employed by the [Emalahleni Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emalahleni_Local_Municipality,_Mpumalanga) were arrested in Mpumalanga on corruption and bribery charges which involved allegations of not issuing standard fines to motorists who committed traffic violations, but instead extorting them for bribes.[^cite_note-84]

In 2022, two criminal charges were lodged by the [Independent Police Investigative Directorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Police_Investigative_Directorate) against the National Police Commissioner [Khehla Sitole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khehla_Sitole?action=edit\&redlink=1) for refusing to cooperate with its investigation into the [murder of Charl Kinnear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Charl_Kinnear). Kinnear was a police intelligence officer investigating organised crime within the SAPS.[^cite_note-85]

On 6 July 2025, KwaZulu Natal Provincial Police [Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_commissioner), [Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nhlanhla_Mkhwanazi) became a whistleblower against high-ranking police officials and politicians, including Police Minister, [Senzo Mchunu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senzo_Mchunu), and members of the judiciary. He accused them of aiding criminal syndicates, involving themselves in investigations and obstructing justice.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88]

On 13 July 2025, South African president [Cyril Ramaphosa](1089194.md) placed Senzo Mchunu on special leave.[^cite_note-89] Deputy national commissioner is challenging the "unlawful" suspension against him.[^cite_note-90]
