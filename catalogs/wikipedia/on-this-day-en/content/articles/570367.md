The **Royal Saudi Air Force (RSAF)**[^cite_note-4] is the [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_warfare) of [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) and one of the principal branches of the [Saudi Arabian Armed Forces](27276.md). Its origins date to 1916, when the [Hejaz Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz_Air_Force) was established during [World War I](4764461.md). It operates alongside the [Royal Saudi Air Defense Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Saudi_Air_Defense_Forces), a separate service responsible for [air defense](146640.md).

The Royal Saudi Air Force currently has [wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_\(military_unit\)), [squadrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squadron_\(aviation\)), and a [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces) unit dedicated to [combat search and rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_search_and_rescue). It has developed from a largely defensive military force into one with an advanced offensive capability, beginning in the 1990s with the acquisition of [laser-guided bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser-guided_bomb) and [unguided bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unguided_bomb).

The backbone of the RSAF is the [F-15](11715.md) family, including the advanced F-15SA variant; it maintains the second largest fleet of [F-15 Eagles](11715.md) after the [U.S. Air Force](32090.md). The RSAF also operates the [Eurofighter Typhoon](167667.md), acquired under the Al-Salam agreement with [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems). Many earlier aircraft were delivered under the [Al-Yamamah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Yamamah_arms_deal) contracts with [British Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Aerospace) (now [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems)).

## History



"The Saudi pilots training in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy) 1935"—a scene from 'Our Eagles', one of four video wall shows made for the [Royal Saudi Air Force Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqr_Al_Jazeera_Aviation_Museum).

&#x20;The Royal Saudi Air Force was formed in the mid-1920s with British assistance from the remains of the [Hejaz Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz_Air_Force).[^cite_note-5] It was initially equipped with [Westland Wapiti IIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Wapiti) general purpose aircraft flown by pilots who had served [Ali of Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_bin_Hussein,_King_of_Hejaz) but had been pardoned by the [King Abdulaziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Saud).[^cite_note-6]

Following a contract with the British government, which was concluded in 1937, a [military airstrip at Jeddah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeddah_International_Airport_\(closed_1981\)) was established in 1939. The military airstrip was where the Air Force was stationed. In 1949, a group of aviation students were sent to study in England, after completing training at Al-Houba. By 1950, a second group of students were sent. The Air Force was re-organized in 1950 and began to receive American assistance from 1952 including the use of [King Abdulaziz Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdulaziz_Air_Base) by the [United States Air Force](32090.md). In 1951, the group of aviation students returned to form the Royal Saudi Air Force. On November 5, 1952, the Air Force's flag was raised at a military hangar in Jeddah Airport, which was attended by [Prince Mishaal bin Abdulaziz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mishaal_bin_Abdulaziz_Al_Saud), Minister of Defense and Aviation at the time. Shortly afterwards, Air Force schools begun opening around the kingdom. Early aircraft used by the RSAF included the [Caproni Ca.100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caproni_Ca.100), [Albatros D.III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albatros_D.III), [Armstrong Whitworth F.K.8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armstrong_Whitworth_F.K.8), [Farman MF.11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farman_MF.11) [Airco DH.9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airco_DH.9), [dH 82 Tiger Moth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Tiger_Moth), [Westland Wapiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Wapiti), [Avro Anson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_Anson), [Douglas C-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_C-47_Skytrain), and the [Douglas A-26 Invader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_A-26_Invader). . In 1957, the Royal Saudi Air Force began major developments, including the establishments of 5 Squadrons, all based at Jeddah Airport. The transportation squadron was equipped with Douglas C-54 Skymasters and Fokker S-13s. The No.2 Squadron was equipped with Douglas B-26 Invaders and the No.5 Squadron was equipped with 20 [De Havilland Vampires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Vampire).[^cite_note-\:3-7]

As part of the *Magic Carpet* arms deal between the United Kingdom and Saudi Arabia, four single-seat [Hawker Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Hunter) F.6s and two Hunter T.7s were gifted by Britain to Saudi Arabia. The aircraft were delivered to No. 6 Squadron at Khamis Mushayt Airbase in May 1966. The Hunters were operational, but Saudi Arabia knew that against the Egyptian Air Force the Hunters would not be successful as interceptors as they lacked any warning radar. They were used for ground attack primarily against Yemeni rebel forces. One single-seat aircraft was lost in 1967 and the remaining aircraft were gifted to Jordan in 1968.[^cite_note-8]

The Saudi forces are equipped with mainly western equipment. Main suppliers to the RSAF are companies based in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and the United States. Both the UK and the US are involved in training programs conducted in Saudi Arabia.

During the 1980s and 1990s, the armed forces of Saudi Arabia were relatively small by Middle Eastern standards. Its strength however was derived from advanced technology. The backbone of the strike / ground attack force is formed by ca 70 [Tornados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panavia_Tornado) (a second batch of 48 Tornado IDS were ordered in 1993 under the [Al-Yamamah II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Yamamah_arms_deal) program), and 72 F-15S aircraft delivered from the mid-1990s that operate beside the remnants of more than 120 F-15C/D aircraft delivered starting in 1981. Pilot training is executed on the [Pilatus PC-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilatus_PC-21) and [BAE Systems Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Hawk). The C-130 Hercules is the mainstay of the transport fleet and is assisted by [CN-235s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA/IPTN_CN-235) and [Beechcraft Super King Air](9023483.md) light transports. Reconnaissance is performed by Tornadoes and F-15s equipped with the DJRP electro-optical reconnaissance pod. The Boeing E-3A is the Airborne Early Warning platform operated by [No. 18 Squadron RSAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._18_Squadron_RSAF).

The VIP support fleet consists of a wide variety of civil registered aircraft such as the [Airbus A330](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A330), [Airbus A320 family](164933.md), [737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) and [747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747), [Lockheed Tri-Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_L-1011_TriStar), [MD11s](382371.md) and G1159A as well as Lockheed L-100-30. The HZ- prefix used in the civilian registrations of these aircraft derived from the former name of the territory ([Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz)).

From 1989 to 1991 three [Lockheed C-130 Hercules](7697.md) of the RSAF were [destroyed in accidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_accidents_and_incidents_involving_the_Lockheed_C-130_Hercules#Saudi_Arabia).

The September 1991 issue of *[Air Forces Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Forces_Monthly)* lists Nos 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 (Tornado IDS), 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 21, 22, 29 Squadron (Dhahran with Tornado ADVs); 34, 37, 42, and 66 Squadrons.[^cite_note-9]

### Purchases and other activities in the 21st century



SAF Roundel on the side of a Lightning Aircraft

The [Al-Yamamah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Yamamah_arms_deal) contract was controversial because of the alleged bribes associated with its award. Nonetheless, the RSAF announced its intention to purchase the [Typhoon](167667.md) from [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems) in December 2005. On 18 August 2006, a [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) was signed for 72 aircraft in a [GB£](270673.md)6–10 billion deal.[^cite_note-10] The [Eurofighter Typhoon](167667.md) was part of a multinational collaborative program having included a consortium of several companies from Spain, Germany and France, with the latter having left the program and later created the Rafale through Dassault Aviation. Following this order, the investigation of the Al-Yamamah contract was suppressed by the British prime minister [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair) in December 2006, citing "strategic interests" of the UK. On 17 September 2007 Saudi Arabia announced it had signed a £4.4bn deal with BAE Systems for 72 Typhoons.[^cite_note-11]

On 29 December 2011, the United States signed a \$29.4 billion deal to sell 84 F-15s in the SA (Saudi Advanced) configuration. The sale includes upgrades for the older F-15s up to the SA standard and related equipment and services.[^cite_note-12]

On 23 May 2012, the British defence firm BAE Systems agreed to sell 22 [BAE Systems Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems_Hawk) advanced jet trainer aircraft to the Royal Saudi Air Force for a total of £1.9 billion (\$3 billion). The deal also included simulators, ground and training equipment and spares.[^cite_note-13] In April 2013, BAE Systems delivered the first two new Typhoons of 24 to Saudi Arabia.

In 2013, the USAF tendered an offer for security services to protect the Saudi air force from cyberwarfare attacks.[^cite_note-14]

In March 2021, RSAF started a joint military exercise, that will last until April 10, with the US and Pakistani Air Forces that will help in exchanging experiences and expertise.[^cite_note-15]

### Drones

Saudi Arabia owns unmanned aerial vehicles (Drones), including for attack, surveillance, and reconnaissance. In 2012, Saudi Arabia purchased 50 Italian [Selex Galileo Falco drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selex_ES_Falco). In 2014, Saudi Arabia signed a contract with China to purchase [Wing Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAIG_Wing_Loong) drones, and Saudi Arabia has more to receive so far.

In April 2013, Saudi Arabia announced its desire to buy 6 Turkish [TAI Anka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAI_Anka) drones, however these efforts fell through. Saudi Arabia has pursued projects to manufacture national drones, the first of which was in 2012, when Saudi Arabia announced a program to manufacture drones in the [King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdulaziz_City_for_Science_and_Technology). The project was called [Saqr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saqr_\(drone\)), and 3 new models of the drone have been introduced. Saudi Arabia also announced a new drone called Samoom, the Saudi crown prince showcased the new drone to the Egyptian President Abdul-Fattah Al-Sisi during which he showed significant interest in it.

Saudi Arabia also announced in 2021 that it will start producing a high capability drone called SkyGuard. It also established a laboratory for robotic vehicle research at the [Prince Sultan Advanced Technology Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Sultan_Advanced_Technology_Research_Institute) at [King Saud University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Saud_University). The laboratory aims to build and transfer technology in the field of smart vehicles of all kinds, such as unmanned aircraft, autonomous land vehicles, and others. The laboratory has manufactured many unmanned aircraft, and the aircraft are still undergoing research and development.

Saudi Arabia has started technology transfer projects and joint ventures with countries to manufacture drones. The General organization for Military Industries obtained a license to manufacture the German drone project [Luna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMT_Luna_X-2000), manufacturing hundreds of them for the Saudi armed forces. Saudi Arabia also entered a joint venture with South Africa to manufacture the [Seeker 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denel_Dynamics_Seeker) [UCAV](337755.md).

In 2023, Saudi Arabia and Baykar has signed a contract for [AKINCI UCAV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baykar_Bayraktar_Ak%C4%B1nc%C4%B1) export. The amount was not declared but hinted that it is the biggest export at once. SSB President Görgün, who made a statement about the contract in question, said, “The largest Turkish defence industry export, made at once, is over three billion dollars.”[^cite_note-30]
