The **Ortler Alps** ([Italian](14708.md): *Alpi dell'Ortles*; [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Ortler-Alpen*; [Romansh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romansh_language): *Alps da l'Ortler*[^cite_note-1]) are a [mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_range) of the [Southern Limestone Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Southern_Limestone_Alps) mountain group in the [Central Eastern Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Eastern_Alps), in [Italy](14532.md) and [Switzerland](26748.md).

## Geography

The Ortler Alps are separated from:

1. the [Sesvenna Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesvenna_Alps) in the north by the [Ofen Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofen_Pass) and the [Val Müstair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_M%C3%BCstair)

2. the [Livigno Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livigno_Alps) in the southwest by the Passo di Fraéle and the [Adda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adda_\(river\)) valley ([Valtellina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valtellina))

3. the [Adamello-Presanella Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamello-Presanella_Alps) in the south by the [Tonale Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonale_Pass)

4. the [Ötztal Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96tztal_Alps) in the north-east by the upper [Adige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adige) valley ([Vinschgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinschgau)).

The part west of the [Gavia Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavia_Pass) is also called **Sobretta-Gavia Group**.

The Ortler Alps are drained by the rivers [Adda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adda_River,_Italy), [Oglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oglio), [Adige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adige) and its tributary [Noce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noce_\(river\)?action=edit\&redlink=1).

## History



Highest [trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench_warfare) in history near the Ortler's peak, 1917

The Ortler Alps were part of the [Italian front during World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Campaign_\(World_War_I\)). In this area, the Austro-Hungarians and the Italians dug in during a trench war fought at altitudes above 3,000 m (10,000 ft) for most of the war. Some trenches are still visible today, and war relics continue to be found in the area.
