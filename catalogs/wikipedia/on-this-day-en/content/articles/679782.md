The **Uruguay national football team** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Selección de fútbol de Uruguay*), nicknamed ***la Celeste*** ("the Sky Blue") and ***los Charrúas*** ("the [Charrúas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charr%C3%BAa)"), have represented [Uruguay](31843.md) in international men's [football](10568.md) since their first international match in 1902 and is administered by the [Asociación Uruguaya de Fútbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguayan_Football_Association) (English: Uruguayan Football Association), the governing body of [football in Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Uruguay), which is a founding member of [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) since 1916 and a member of [FIFA](11049.md) since 1923. It was also a member of [PFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panamerican_Championship#Panamerican_Football_Confederation), which was the attempt at a unified confederation of the Americas from 1946 to 1961. Uruguay's home stadium is the [Estadio Centenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Centenario).

Considered one of the most successful national teams in international competitions and by FIFA as "football's first global powerhouse,"[^cite_note-11] Uruguay has won four world FIFA–organized championships, two [FIFA Olympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics) titles and two [FIFA World Cups](11370.md).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-\:0-13] Their first two senior world titles came at the Olympic tournaments of [France 1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1924_Summer_Olympics) and [Netherlands 1928](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1928_Summer_Olympics),[^cite_note-espn24-14] two events that were directly organized by FIFA as open tournaments that included professionals.[^cite_note-\:1-15] In the former, Uruguay beat [Switzerland](681862.md) 3–0 in the final, whereas in the latter, Uruguay defeated Argentina 2–1 in the replay match.[^cite_note-16] They then secured a third consecutive title at the inaugural [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) in [Montevideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), where they beat Argentina 4–2 in the decisive match.[^cite_note-17] Uruguay's fourth title came in [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup) after beating hosts [Brazil](149286.md) in the [final match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay_v_Brazil_\(1950_FIFA_World_Cup\)) 2–1, a match that still holds the record for the highest official attendance for a football match ever (173,850 people at the gate). On account of these achievements, the Uruguayan national team displays [four five-pointed stars approved by FIFA on its football crest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_stars_above_Uruguay's_football_crest). Uruguay were unbeaten in world championship matches from the 1924 Olympics until their semi-final loss in the [1954 World Cup](60523.md) marking a 30-year unbeaten streak and winning four consecutive world titles in tournaments they participated in, having chosen to opt out of the [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1938 World Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup). FIFA officially recognizes Uruguay as *Champions of World Champions*, following their victory in the [1980 World Champions' Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_World_Champions'_Gold_Cup) (also known as the 1980 Mundialito). This unique designation makes Uruguay the only national team in football history to be formally acknowledged by FIFA with that title.[^cite_note-18] In regional competitions, Uruguay has won the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) 15 times, having also won the inaugural edition in [1916](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1916_South_American_Championship), second only to [Argentina](454699.md) for the most titles in the tournament's history, with their most recent title in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica). As of 2025, based on international senior official titles, Uruguay have won the second most major trophies, behind Argentina.[^cite_note-19]

Uruguay has consistently performed well in global football rankings throughout their history, achieving their highest [FIFA World Ranking](719095.md) of 2nd place in June 2012.[^cite_note-20] In the [World Football Elo Ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Football_Elo_Ratings), they have also reached the top spot on multiple occasions.[^cite_note-21] Uruguay has also developed many rivalries through the years, with the most notable ones being with neighboring countries [Argentina](454699.md), known as the *[Clásico del Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Uruguay_football_rivalry)* based on their [geographical location](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata) and [cultural similarities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Uruguay_relations), and [Brazil](149286.md), known as the [*Clásico del Río Negro*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil%E2%80%93Uruguay_football_rivalry) in reference to the 1950 FIFA World Cup final known to football fans as the [*Maracanazo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay_v_Brazil_\(1950_FIFA_World_Cup\)).[^cite_note-22]

## History

### Beginnings (1900–1916)

Historically, football has been a fundamental element in the consolidation of Uruguayan nationality and in projecting Uruguay's image internationally at the beginning of the 20th century.

Uruguay is one of the most successful teams in the world, having won nineteen official competitions recognized by [FIFA](11049.md), a world record at senior national team level. These include two editions of the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), two editions of the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics) when they were contested by senior national teams and organized by FIFA (at that time, the only global-level football competition), and fifteen editions of the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica).

Uruguay was the first national team, along with [Argentina](454699.md), to play an international match outside the British Isles, on 16 May 1901 in Montevideo, with Argentina winning 3–2. However, because the sides were combined teams rather than national selections, the match is considered unofficial. In that game, Frank Chevallier Boutell, president of the Argentine Association Football League, served as a linesman.

A precedent suggests there was an even earlier match, though not between the two countries' national teams, but between representative teams of their capitals, [Buenos Aires](255919.md) and [Montevideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montevideo), on 15 August 1889. Two representative sides from the two capitals faced each other in commemoration of the seventieth anniversary of [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria) of the United Kingdom. Supposedly, the match took place at the so-called *New Ground* in [La Blanqueada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Blanqueada) and ended with a 3–0 victory for the Buenos Aires team over the Montevideo team.

The first official match between the national teams of Uruguay and Argentina was played on 20 July 1902 at the Albion ground, with Argentina winning 6–0 (to this day the heaviest away win in the derby). Of Uruguay's starting eleven, 8 were from [Club Nacional de Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Nacional_de_Football). Uruguay's lineup was: Enrique Sardeson (Albion), Carlos Carve Urioste (Nacional), Germán Arímalo (Nacional), Miguel Nebel (capt.) (Nacional), Alberto Peixoto (Albion), Luis Carbone (Nacional), Bolívar Céspedes (Nacional), Gonzalo Rincón (Nacional), Juan Sardeson (Albion), Ernesto Boutón Reyes (Nacional), and Carlos Céspedes (Nacional). Goalscorers were [Bolívar Céspedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bol%C3%ADvar_C%C3%A9spedes?action=edit\&redlink=1) and William Poole.

On 13 September 1903, Uruguay won its first match, defeating Argentina in Buenos Aires 3–2 with a team made up entirely of Nacional players.

Their nickname, La Celeste ("the Sky Blue"), dates back to its first international victory in Montevideo on 15 August 1910, when it beat Argentina 3–1 in the first match in which it wore its sky-blue jersey. This kit was adopted as a national emblem in recognition of the triumph shortly before by [River Plate Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Plate_F.C.) over the powerful [Alumni Athletic Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alumni_Athletic_Club) of Buenos Aires, 2–1, using those same colors.

### First international successes (1916–1924)

Uruguay won the first two editions of the [South American Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_Championship): the first held in [Buenos Aires](255919.md) in 1916 and the second in 1917, in Montevideo.

The Uruguayan national team became the first continental champion, finishing ahead of [Argentina](454699.md) in the [1916 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1916_South_American_Championship), played in Buenos Aires. The tournament had a round-robin format: a 0–0 draw against Argentina on the final match-day gave the title to *La Celeste*.

Uruguay also won the following [1917 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917_South_American_Championship), this time as hosts. Once again, the title was decided in the last round against Argentina, and Uruguay prevailed 1–0 to become champions of South America for the second consecutive time.



In 1917, Uruguay won back-to-back South American titles – this time as hosts.

In the [1919 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1919_South_American_Championship) held in Brazil, Uruguay again reached the decisive match. After drawing 2–2 against the hosts, both teams finished level on points, forcing a playoff. The playoff was won 1–0 by [Brazil](149286.md), who claimed their first continental title, with Uruguay finishing as runner-up.

In the [1920 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_South_American_Championship) held in Chile, Uruguay once again arrived at the final match on top of the standings, and defeated [the hosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) 2–1 to claim their third championship. Argentina's win over Brazil on the same day proved insufficient, leaving the Argentines in second place.

In the tournaments of [1921](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1921_South_American_Championship) (Argentina) and [1922](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_South_American_Championship) (Brazil), Uruguay could not secure the title, finishing third in both editions: first behind champions Argentina and then behind hosts Brazil.



Uruguay's 1923 Copa America had a significant impact on global football, as they decided to enter the 1924 FIFA organized Olympics due to this result.

The Copa América returned to Uruguay in [1923](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_South_American_Championship) and [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_South_American_Championship). In the 1923 edition, Uruguay and Argentina were level at the top entering the final round. Uruguay defeated their rivals 2–0 at the [Gran Parque Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Gran_Parque_Central) to take the title. In 1924, a 0–0 draw with Argentina on the last match-day was enough for Uruguay to be crowned champions once again, thanks to having more wins in the tournament.

### Golden Era: Olympic Games and the first World Cup (1924–1930)



1924 – Uruguay performed football's first [Lap of Honour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lap_of_honour) in France after beating Switzerland 3–0 to become the sport's first world champions.



1928 – Uruguay beat Argentina in Amsterdam to claim back-to-back FIFA world championships.

Uruguay was the first South American country to participate in the [Olympic football tournaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics). Their debut was at the [1924 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1924_Summer_Olympics) in Paris, where they defeated Switzerland 3–0 in the final to claim [FIFA](11049.md)'s first recognized non-amateur world championship. Uruguay returned in 1928, this time alongside Argentina (winners of the [1927 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1927_South_American_Championship)). Both reached the final, with Uruguay prevailing 2–1 in a replay after drawing the initial match 1–1. The expectation for this final was so high that reportedly over 500,000 tickets were demanded across Europe.

The Uruguayan national team dazzled Europe with its Olympic performances, earning admiration and respect worldwide. These triumphs elevated South American football to global prominence at a time when the continent was still largely ignored in international sport. The Olympic tournaments of 1924 and 1928 were the only editions officially recognized by FIFA as world championships. This is why Uruguay is allowed to display four stars on its football crest, despite having won the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) twice (in 1930 and 1950).

In terms of continental success during this period, Uruguay won the [1926 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926_South_American_Championship) in Chile. They did not participate in the [1925 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1925_South_American_Championship) in Argentina (won by the hosts), finished as runners-up behind Argentina in 1927, and placed third in 1929.

### Inaugural 1930 World Cup

Uruguay proposed hosting the first [FIFA World Cup in 1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), and FIFA granted the country the right to organize it in commemoration of the centenary of the [Jura de la Constitución](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Uruguay) (Uruguayan Constitution), in addition to its world titles at the Olympics. In Europe, this decision was met with disapproval, with arguments that the tournament should be held there, particularly in [England](9316.md), the birthplace of football and site of its subsequent development. There were also complaints about the long journey and high costs involved in traveling to South America.

However, although the Uruguayan government offered to cover all expenses, many European nations still declined to participate. FIFA ignored these complaints, reasoning that the continent was still reeling from [World War I](4764461.md), while Uruguay enjoyed peace and economic stability. Thus, the first World Cup was held in Uruguay.

The opening matches of the tournament were [France](250197.md) vs. [Mexico](808402.md) and [United States](582488.md) vs. [Belgium](679716.md), both played on July 13 at 15\:00. In the France–Mexico game, [Lucien Laurent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucien_Laurent) scored after 19 minutes, recording the first goal in World Cup history.



The Estadio Centenario in Montevideo – declared by FIFA as the first Historical Monument of World Football, to this day the only building to achieve this recognition worldwide.

Uruguay, considered the strongest team for its Olympic titles in 1924 and 1928, made its debut on July 18 at the new [Estadio Centenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Centenario) before about 50,000 spectators against [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_national_football_team). In a lackluster performance, Uruguay won 1–0 with a goal from [Héctor Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ctor_Castro), drawing some criticism from the press.

In its next match on July 21, Uruguay defeated [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_football_team) 4–0, with goals from [Pablo Dorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Dorado), [Héctor Scarone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ctor_Scarone), [Pedro Cea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Cea), and [Santos Iriarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_Iriarte), advancing to the semi-finals.

There, the Celeste faced [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) and won convincingly 6–1, with a hat-trick from Cea, a brace from Santos Anselmo, and one from Iriarte, securing their place in the final.



1930 – Uruguay became the first and only nation in history to win three consecutive FIFA world titles after winning the inaugural World Cup

In the decisive match, played on July 30 before 70,000 spectators, Uruguay once again met their great rivals, Argentina, who were also considered favourites. Leading up to the match, tensions were high, with reports that Uruguayans disrupted Argentina's training sessions throughout the tournament.

In the final itself, Dorado gave Uruguay the lead after 12 minutes, but Argentina responded with goals from [Carlos Peucelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Peucelle) (20’) and [Guillermo Stábile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_St%C3%A1bile) (37’), taking a 2–1 advantage into halftime. In the second half, Uruguay came out determined: Cea equalized at 57’, Iriarte put them ahead at 68’, and Castro sealed the 4–2 victory at 89’.

With that result, Uruguay became the first ever World Cup champion in history and claimed their third consecutive title.

### 1940s–1960s



The team that beat Brazil in the decisive match to win the [1950 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup)

In the 1940s, Uruguay achieved early success in a decade largely dominated by Argentina by winning their eighth South American championship in 1942. As the World Cup was not being played, this championship became the priority.



1950 World Cup winning captain, Obdulio Varela

In 1950, Uruguay re-entered the World Cup for the first time since 1930. This time, La Celeste would enter a final-group where they would draw [Spain](609825.md) 2–2 and beat [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) 3–2 en route to the final match against hosts [Brazil](149286.md). On 16 July 1950, Uruguay claimed their second FIFA World Cup and fourth senior world title when they beat Brazil 2–1 from behind in an iconic match known as the *[Maracanazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay_v_Brazil_\(1950_FIFA_World_Cup\))* in front of a record crowd of 173,850. Historical estimates indicate that the crowd reached well over 200,000.[^cite_note-23] This feat became an integral part of Uruguay's football identity and a source of inspiration for upcoming footballers with each passing generation.



1954 – Uruguay vs. Hungary was later dubbed "Match of the Century" by English magazine Four-Four-Two, and is often referenced as one of the greatest World Cup matches of all-time.

In the [1954 World Cup](60523.md), Uruguay reached the semi-finals for the fourth time after defeating [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) and [England](9904.md). The semi-final against [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) is considered one of the best matches in World Cup history, as La Celeste dramatically equalised 2–2 late after trailing 2–0 at halftime. Uruguay would eventually lose 4–2 in extra-time in what was their first-ever loss at the global stage, a thirty year record that started in 1924.

Afterwards, Uruguay would fail to qualify to the World Cup for the first time in the [1958 CONMEBOL qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONMEBOL\)) after losing 5–0 to Paraguay in [Asunción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asunci%C3%B3n). From there, Uruguay maintained a competitive generation through the 1960s and into the early 1970s with players from [Nacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Nacional_de_Football) and [Peñarol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1arol), who had won several [continental](299730.md) and [club world titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_Cup_\(1960%E2%80%932004\)) during that time. In [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup), Uruguay had a relatively poor World Cup, being eliminated in the group stage after a dramatic last match against the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team). [Four years later](61629.md), Uruguay reached the last eight after drawing hosts [England](9904.md) beating [France](250197.md) in the group stage. A controversial quarter-final loss against [West Germany](250204.md) marred what had been seen as a positive campaign. La Celeste would end the decade winning their [11th South American championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_South_American_Championship) in 1967 by beating Argentina 1–0 in Montevideo.

### 1970s–1980s

In 1970, Uruguay advanced to their fourth World Cup semi-final in [Mexico](157227.md), losing to eventual winners Brazil. They would eventually be eliminated in the group stage at the [1974 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup), and failed to qualify for [Argentina 1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup).



[Rodolfo Rodríguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodolfo_Rodr%C3%ADguez_\(Uruguayan_footballer\)) raises the *[Mundialito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Mundialito)* trophy won in January 1981

Uruguayan football would then see a resurgence as the U-20 team won four consecutive South American titles from 1975 to 1981, as well as reaching the U-20 World Cup semi-final in 1979. The success of this young generation would bear fruit in the 1980s, which began with Uruguay winning the [1980 World Champions' Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_World_Champions'_Gold_Cup), a tournament that pitted past World Cup winners together in celebration of the tournament's 50th anniversary. Afterwards, La Celeste went on to win the [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) and [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) Copas America back-to-back, before losing 1–0 to hosts Brazil in [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica)'s decisive title match. After failing to qualify for the [1982 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup), in [1986](157231.md), Uruguay were eliminated in the Round of 16 against eventual champions Argentina after a group stage that included West Germany, Denmark and Scotland.

### 1990s–2000s

In the [1990 World Cup](157232.md) in Italy, Uruguay were eliminated in the round of 16 against [the hosts](362466.md) 2–0 in Rome. Afterwards, Uruguay would fail to qualify for [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) after losing 2–0 to eventual winners Brazil at the [Maracanã Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracan%C3%A3_Stadium). La Celeste would then defeated the world champions to win the [1995 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) in Montevideo. Later, Uruguay were eliminated from the [1998 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) as they finished in seventh place the first time qualifying was a round-robin tournament.

For the [2002 World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONMEBOL\)), Uruguay managed to advance to a final playoff round against [Australia](4792249.md) to reach [the finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup). La Celeste would win the [decisive match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(OFC%E2%80%93CONMEBOL_play-off\)) 3–0 in Montevideo to qualify to their first World Cup since 1990. The campaign itself proved a frustrating affair, as Uruguay were narrowly eliminated in the last group match against [Senegal](1017436.md) after drawing them 3–3, after being down 3–0 at half-time.

The [2006 World Cup qualifying campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONMEBOL\)) saw another international playoff against Australia. This time, La Celeste were eliminated on [penalties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONMEBOL%E2%80%93OFC_play-off\)) in [Sydney](27862.md). This loss led to a profound paradigm shift with the hiring of [Oscar Tabarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93scar_Tab%C3%A1rez) as manager. From there, Uruguay would reach the semi-finals of the [2007 Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), and successfully qualified for the [2010 World Cup](168079.md) in South Africa.

### 2010s–2026

In 2010, a new generation of footballers, led by [Luis Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez_\(Uruguayan_footballer\)), [Diego Forlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Forl%C3%A1n) and [Edinson Cavani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinson_Cavani), formed a team that finished fourth at the World Cup. Uruguay opened the tournament with a goalless draw against France, followed by wins against South Africa (3–0) and Mexico (1–0) respectively, finishing at the top of their group with seven points. In the second round, they played [South Korea](1018627.md), defeating them 2–1 with Suárez scoring a brace, as Uruguay sealed a spot in the quarter-finals for the first time since 1970. Against Ghana, the match finished 1–1, forcing the game into extra-time. Both sides had their chances at extra time but Suárez blocked the ball with his hand in the penalty area, earning himself a red card. Ghana striker [Asamoah Gyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asamoah_Gyan) missed the subsequent penalty, forcing the game to go into penalties where Uruguay would win 4–2, sending them into the last four. They played the Netherlands in the semi-finals but were beaten 3–2. In the third-place match, they played Germany, again losing 3–2. This placed Uruguay in fourth place for the tournament, their best result in 40 years. Forlán was awarded Player of the Tournament.



Uruguay vs Saudi Arabia match at the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) in Russia

A year later, they won the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) for the first time in 16 years and broke the record for the most successful team in South America. Luis Suárez ended up as Player of the Tournament.

Uruguay was placed in Group D at the [2014 World Cup](656933.md) alongside [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team), [England](9904.md), and [Italy](362466.md). They were upset by Costa Rica in the opening match, losing 3–1 after taking the lead in the first half. They rebounded with a 2–1 victory over England, in which Suárez scored a brace right after coming back from an injury, and a 1–0 victory over Italy, placing them second in their group and earning a spot in the last 16. During the match against Italy, forward Luis Suárez [bit Italian defender Giorgio Chiellini on his left shoulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2014_FIFA_World_Cup_controversies#Uruguay_vs_Italy). Two days after the match, the [FIFA Disciplinary Committee](5920980.md) banned Suárez for nine international matches, the longest such ban in World Cup history, exceeding the eight-match ban handed to Italy's [Mauro Tassotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauro_Tassotti) for breaking the nose of Spain's [Luis Enrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Enrique_\(footballer\)) in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-Ban2014-24][^cite_note-Luis_Suarez_suspended-25][^cite_note-26] Suárez was also banned from taking part in any football-related activity (including entering any stadium) for four months and fined [CHF](361362.md)100,000 (approximately £65,700/€82,000/US\$119,000).[^cite_note-Ban2014-24][^cite_note-Luis_Suarez_suspended-25][^cite_note-Suarez_suspension_FIFA-27] In the round of 16, Uruguay played Colombia but were beaten 2–0, eliminating them from the tournament.

At the [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) and [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario) Copa América, Uruguay, missing banned striker Luis Suárez, were eliminated in the quarter-finals and group stage respectively.

Uruguay then finished second in their [qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONMEBOL\)) campaign to qualify for the [2018 World Cup](13327177.md) in Russia. There, they won their group after three victories, and advanced to the quarter-finals after a 2–1 win over [Portugal](576328.md).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] However, they were eliminated 2–0 in the quarter-finals by the eventual champions [France](250197.md).

At the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md), Uruguay was drawn into [Group H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_H) with Portugal, Ghana and [South Korea](1018627.md). They started the tournament with a 0–0 draw against South Korea, before they fell to a 2–0 defeat to Portugal. Despite a 2–0 victory against Ghana in their final group game, Uruguay was knocked out of the tournament in the group stages for the first time since [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), on goals scored following South Korea's shock 2–1 win against Portugal.[^cite_note-30]



Prior to Uruguay’s opening match against Saudi Arabia during the 2026 FIFA World Cup at [Hard Rock Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Rock_Stadium) in Miami

At the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md), Uruguay was once again drawn into [Group H](81660199.md), this time with Spain, [Cape Verde](1205575.md), and [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team). They tied their group opener against Saudi Arabia 1–1 despite holding 67% possession, their highest ever in a World Cup match on record. Their 22 shots in the second half was also the joint-most in any World Cup half since [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany_national_football_team) had 24 against Chile in 1974.[^cite_note-31] After drawing Cape Verde 2–2 and losing to Spain 1–0, Uruguay were eliminated in the group stage for the second World Cup in a row.

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [Uruguay national football team kits](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Uruguay%20national%20football%20team%20kits).



Uruguay at the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md), wearing the light blue shirt they have worn since 1910

Between 1901 and 1910, Uruguay wore a variety of different shirts during its matches. The first shirt worn was [Albion F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albion_F.C.)'s kit, in the unofficial debut of the national team against Argentina in 1901.[^cite_note-mkt-32] It was followed by a variety of shirts, including a solid green one and even a shirt with the colours of the [Artigas flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artigas_flag).

On 10 April 1910, now-defunct club [River Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Plate_F.C.) defeated Argentine side [Alumni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alumni_Athletic_Club) 2–1. That day River Plate wore its alternate jersey, a light blue one due to the home jersey was similar to Alumni's.[^cite_note-observa-33] Ricardo LeBas proposed Uruguay to wear a light blue jersey as a tribute to the victory of River Plate over Alumni. This was approved by president of the Uruguayan Association, Héctor Gómez.[^cite_note-celeste-34] The light blue (*Celeste*) jersey debuted in a [Copa Lipton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Lipton) match against [Argentina](454699.md) on 15 August 1910.[^cite_note-35]

The red shirt that was used in some previous away strips was first used at the 1935 South American championship, held in [Santa Beatriz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Beatriz) in [Peru](170691.md), which Uruguay won. It was not worn again (except for a [1962 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup) match, against [Colombia](680175.md)[^cite_note-36]) until 1991, when it was officially adopted as the away jersey.[^cite_note-37]

[Uruguay displays four stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_stars_above_Uruguay's_football_crest) in its emblem. This is unique in world football as two of the [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_\(football_badge\)) represent the gold medals received at the 1924 and 1928 Summer Olympics, which are the only editions recognised by FIFA as senior world championships.[^cite_note-Perez-38][^cite_note-FIFA1924-39][^cite_note-40] In 2021, after a FIFA employee contacted PUMA about modifying the team's crest, FIFA reconfirmed and approved once again the use of all four stars on the shirt.[^cite_note-41]

### Home stadium

Since 1930, Uruguay have played their home games at the [Estadio Centenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Centenario) in the Uruguayan capital [Montevideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montevideo). The stadium was built as a celebration of the centenary of Uruguay's first constitution, and had a capacity of 90,000 when first fully opened.[^cite_note-50] The stadium hosted several matches in the [1930 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), including the final, which was watched by a crowd of 93,000.[^cite_note-51]

## Rivalries

### Brazil

Main article: [Brazil–Uruguay football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil%E2%80%93Uruguay_football_rivalry)

The rivalry with [Brazil](149286.md) dates back to the 1916 South American Championship, It's also referred to as "El Clasico del Río Negro". Brazil–Uruguay football rivalry is also biggest and oldest football rivalry in South America. Both teams have since competed in several Copa America finals and the iconic [1950 FIFA World Cup final match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay_v_Brazil_\(1950_FIFA_World_Cup\)), where Uruguay lifted their second World Cup in front of a world record crowd at the [Maracanã](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracan%C3%A3_Stadium). The only other time they met at the World Cup was the [1970](157227.md) semi-final where Brazil won 3–1 in [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara). Since the 1980s, the two sides met in several title deciders, such as the 1980 World Champions' Gold Cup final (won by Uruguay), and the 1983, 1989, 1995 and 1999 Copa América finals. Uruguay would win the 1983 and 1995 editions, while Brazil won in 1989 and 1999. Brazil eliminated Uruguay at the Maracanã from qualifying for the [1994 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup). Since the 2000s, they have met mostly in the Copa América, as well as the regularly scheduled [World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification).

### Argentina

Main article: [Argentina–Uruguay football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Uruguay_football_rivalry)

First played in 1902, the "Clasico de Rio de la Plata" with [Argentina](454699.md) is one of the oldest international derbies in world football outside of the UK. Both teams have played in historically significant matches, such as the [1928 Olympic football final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1928_Summer_Olympics) and the [inaugural World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup_final) in 1930.

### Colombia

The Uruguay-Colombia football rivalry is a highly competitive and intense clash between two South American teams. It is defined by physical play, tactical battles, and deep history in the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) and [World Cup Qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualifiers). While Uruguay historically leads, recent matches are deadlocked, making every game high-stakes and dramatic.
