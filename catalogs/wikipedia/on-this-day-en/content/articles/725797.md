# Dashboard

This article is about a control panel placed in front of the driver or pilot of a vehicle or small aircraft. For other uses, see [Dashboard (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dashboard_\(disambiguation\)).



The dashboard of a [Bentley Continental GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bentley_Continental_GT)C

A **dashboard** (also called **dash**, **instrument panel** or **IP**, or **fascia**) is a [control panel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_panel_\(engineering\)) set within the [central console](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_console) of a [vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle), [boat](3996.md), or [cockpit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockpit) of an [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft) or [spacecraft](37910.md). Usually located directly ahead of the driver (or pilot), it displays instrumentation and controls for the vehicle's operation.[^cite_note-dictionary-1][^cite_note-OED-2]

An electronic equivalent may be called an **electronic instrument cluster**, **digital instrument panel**, **digital dash**, **digital speedometer** or **digital instrument cluster**_._

By analogy, a succinct display of various types of related [visual data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_and_information_visualization) in one place is also called a dashboard, such as a [dashboard in computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dashboard_\(computing\)).[^cite_note-3]

## Etymology



[Cabriolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabriolet_\(carriage\)) or [Stanhope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanhope_\(carriage\)), showing a leather splashboard in front of the driver and passenger, Paris, late nineteenth century

When an open-fronted horse-drawn carriage such as a [gig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gig_\(carriage\)) goes through water, the driver is liable to be splashed by water thrown up. To protect against this, a *splash-board* was placed in front: the term is first documented in 1826.[^cite_note-4] The word *[dashboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dashboard_\(carriage\))* (variants *dasher* or *dash*) subsequently came into use from the USA as an alternative: "dash" in this context means either "splash" or "bespatter".[^cite_note-dictionary-1] The first recorded written use of the term (hyphenated as *dash-board*, and applied to sleighs) dates from 1847.[^cite_note-OED-2]



[Carl Benz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Benz) driving his [Patent-Motorwagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benz_Patent-Motorwagen) Model 3. The dashboard protects against material thrown up by the front wheel

When the first cars were made in the late 19th century, they were "[horseless carriages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseless_carriage)" and placed the engine beneath the driver. The dashboard was retained to protect occupants from debris thrown up by the cars' front wheel(s).

[Émile Levassor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89mile_Levassor) revolutionised car design in the early 1890s by placing the engine at the front. The dashboard became a panel that protected vehicle occupants from the heat and oil of the engine. With gradually increasing mechanical complexity, this panel formed a convenient location for the placement of gauges and minor controls, and from this evolved the modern instrument panel, although retaining its [archaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_period_\(North_America\)) common name.

The first mass-produced automobile, the [Oldsmobile Curved Dash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldsmobile_Curved_Dash), got its name from its dashboard, which was curved like that of a sleigh.[^cite_note-5]

## Dashboard features



Dashboard instruments displaying various car and engine conditions

Where the dashboard originally included an array of simple controls (e.g., the [steering wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steering_wheel)) and instrumentation to show speed, fuel level and oil pressure, the modern dashboard may accommodate a broad array of gauges, and controls as well as information, climate control and entertainment systems. Although a clock is typically embedded within the entertainment system, some cars have a separate dashboard clock.

Contemporary dashboards may include the [speedometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speedometer), [tachometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachometer), [odometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odometer), engine coolant temperature gauge, and [fuel gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_gauge), turn indicators, gearshift position indicator, seat belt warning light, parking-brake warning light,[^cite_note-6] and engine-malfunction lights. Heavy vehicles that feature [air brakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_brake_\(road_vehicle\)), such as trucks and buses will also have gauges to indicate the available air pressure in the braking system. Other features may include a gauge for [alternator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternator) voltage, indicators for low fuel, low oil pressure, low tire pressure and faults in the airbag (SRS) systems, [glove compartment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glove_compartment), ashtray and a cigarette lighter or power outlet – as well as heating and ventilation systems, lighting controls, safety systems, entertainment equipment and information systems, e.g., [navigation systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_navigation_system).

## Padding and safety

In 1937, [Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler), [Dodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge), [DeSoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeSoto_\(automobile\)), and [Plymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth_\(automobile\)) cars came with a safety dashboard that was flat, raised above knee height, and had all the controls mounted flush.[^cite_note-7]

Padded dashboards were advocated in the 1930s by [car safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_safety) pioneer [Claire L. Straith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claire_L._Straith).[^cite_note-8] In 1948, the [Tucker 48](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_48) became the first car with a padded dashboard.[^cite_note-9]

One of the safety enhancements of the 1970s was the widespread adoption of padded dashboards. The padding is commonly [polyurethane foam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyurethane_foam), while the surface is commonly either [polyvinyl chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyvinyl_chloride) (PVC) or leather in the case of luxury models.

In the early and mid-1990s, [airbags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbag) became a standard feature of steering wheels and dashboards.

## Fashion in instrumentation



Stylised dashboard from a 1980s [Lancia Beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancia_Beta)



The dashboard design of a Mercury Grand Marquis from 1992 to 2005

In the 1940s through the 1960s, American car manufacturers and their imitators designed aesthetically shaped instruments on a dashboard accented with chrome and transparent plastic, which could be less readable, but was often thought to be more stylish. Sunlight could cause a bright glare on the chrome, particularly for a convertible.

On North American vehicles in particular, this trend lingered on until the late-1980s, which still featured dashboards with wood and fake chrome embellishment along with square instruments – long after European and Japanese manufacturers had long embraced a plainer, more functional and austere approach for dashboard and instrument panel design.

With the advent of the [VFD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_fluorescent_display), [LED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-emitting_diode) and [LCD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid-crystal_display) in consumer electronics, some manufacturers used instruments with digital readouts to make their cars appear more up to date. Some cars use a [head-up display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head-up_display) to project the speed of the car onto the windscreen in imitation of fighter aircraft, but in a far less complex display.

In recent years, spurred on by the growing aftermarket use of dash kits, many automakers have taken the initiative to add more stylistic elements to their dashboards. One prominent example of this is the Chevrolet Sonic which offers both exterior (e.g., a custom graphics package) and interior cosmetic upgrades.[^cite_note-10] In addition to OEM dashboard trim and upgrades a number of companies offer domed polyurethane or vinyl applique dash trim accent kits or "dash kits".

Manufacturers such as [BMW](3772.md), [Honda](13729.md), [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota) and [Mercedes-Benz](18564164.md) have included fuel-economy gauges in some instrument clusters, showing fuel mileage in real time, which was limited mainly to luxury vehicles and later, hybrids. Following a focus on increasing fuel economy in the late 2000s along with increased technology, most vehicles in the 2010s now come with either real-time or average mileage readouts on their dashboards. The [ammeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammeter) was the gauge of choice for monitoring the state of the charging system until the 1970s. Later it was replaced by the [voltmeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltmeter).

Today, most family vehicles have warning lights instead of voltmeters or oil pressure gauges in their dashboard instrument clusters, though sports cars often have proper gauges for performance purposes and driver appeasement along with larger trucks, mainly to monitor system function during heavy usage such as towing or off-road usage.

## Electronic instrument cluster

In an automobile, an **electronic instrument cluster**, **digital instrument panel** or *digital dash* for short, is a set of instrumentation, including the [speedometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speedometer), that is displayed with a [digital readout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_visual_display) rather than with the traditional [analog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_signal) gauges. Many refer to it either simply as a *digital speedometer* or a *digital instrument cluster.*

### History



Digital instrument cluster from a 1984 [Dodge 600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_600)

The first application of an electronic instrument cluster, in a production automobile, was in the 1976 [Aston Martin Lagonda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Martin_Lagonda). The first American manufacturer application was the 1978 [Cadillac Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadillac_Seville) with available Cadillac Trip Computer. In the United States they were an option in many [motor vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_vehicle) manufactured in the 1980s and 1990s, and were standard on some luxury vehicles at times, including some models made by [Cadillac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadillac), [Chrysler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler) and [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_\(automobile\)).

They included not only a speedometer with a digital readout, but also a [trip computer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trip_computer) that displayed factors like the outdoor temperature, travel direction, [fuel economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_economy_in_automobiles) and distance to empty (**DTE**).

In 1983, the [Renault 11 Electronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_9_&_11) was the first European hatchback to have a digital dashboard.[^cite_note-carsfromitaly.net-11] Many vehicles made today have an analog speedometer paired with the latter in digital form. In the late 1980s into the early 1990s, General Motors had touch-screen CRTs with features such as date books and hands-free cell phone integration built into cars such as the [Oldsmobile Toronado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldsmobile_Toronado), [Buick Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buick_Riviera) and [Buick Reatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buick_Reatta).

### Advantages and drawbacks

When accelerating, digital speedometers generally step through a freeze frame of [whole numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_number) at a constant [sample rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample_rate). It is as precise as the number displaced, whereas a gauged speedometer pointer could sweep through an infinite range between its major markings at 10 mph or 20 km/h intervals. The latter provides a sense of continuous acceleration albeit with less [precision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Significant_figures): a gauge reading could only be estimated to the pointer's nearest halfway point between the markings.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

The first digital instrument clusters were considered to be unpopular during the years when they were widely produced, and were heavily criticized by reviewers in automotive magazines. Some of the criticism they received was as follows:

- They were hard to see in the strong sunlight or other bright light

- They took away the sense of continuous acceleration that is provided by an analog speedometer.

  [^cite_note-15]

- They were expensive to repair in the event of a malfunction

As a result of these issues, digital instrument panels were phased out of vehicles throughout the 1990s, and have been replaced with traditional analog gauges in most vehicles (with notable exceptions from French manufacturers [Renault](162292.md) and [Citroën](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn)), including those from luxury divisions. However, many vehicles are made today with a standard or optional trip computer located independently from the speedometer.

Digital units received information from a variety of sensors installed throughout the engine and transmission, while traditional analog units were attached to a cable that provided information from the transmission. Modern analog displays receive information in the same manner as the digital units, with very few manufacturers still using the speedometer cable method.

[Automotive head-up displays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_head-up_display) have seen applications in several cars, augmenting analog gauges with a digital readout on the windshield glass.

#### LCDs

Vehicle instruments have been augmented by software-powered display panels conveying information on display panels. Digital instruments present data in the form of numeric parameters, textual messages, or graphical gauges. Unlike the electro-mechanical instrument clusters of the past, these interactive displays are much more versatile and flexible.

Many modern motorcycles are now equipped with digital speedometers, most often these are sports bikes.

Toyota is using electronic instruments for showing the cars parameters for its Yaris/Vitz model, the car employs a [vacuum fluorescent display](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_fluorescent_display) to indicate the speed, RPM, fuel level, odometer, etc.

For the 2011 model year, Chrysler began using a common dashboard across their model line that has an integrated trip computer in addition to the analog gauges. This trip computer can also be used to show a digital speedometer, making these hybrid digital-analog dashboards. the speedometer needle to be too wide, they are relying on the digital speedometer more than the analog gauge.

The French manufacturer [Citroën](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn), is using digital indicators as speedometer for many models in its range, including the [C2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn_C2), [C3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn_C3), [C4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn_C4) and [C6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn_C6).

High resolution displays

The [2007 Lamborghini Reventon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamborghini_Revent%C3%B3n) introduced one of the first high resolution LCD displays used on a production vehicle.[^cite_note-21] A trend setter that would be taken seriously by mainstream manufacturers on years to come and become a selling point of consideration during the next following decade.

The 2009 Lexus LFA was one of the first cars to use a modern LCD screen. Lexus claimed a digital speedometer was required since an analogue tachometer wouldn't be able to keep up with the rev changes of the car's engine.[^cite_note-22] This statement however was mainly marketing-driven; there is no technical reason why an analog needle would not keep up with the (far heavier) engine itself.

The [third generation Range Rover (L322)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_Rover_\(L322\)) also introduced the first use and largest TFT LCD displays used on a production luxury SUV for the facelifted 2010, and end of the cycle model.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] A trend setter that would follow further adaptation from other manufacturers.

In 2014, Audi launched[^cite_note-25] its 'virtual cockpit' on the [Audi TT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_TT), and has later introduced it to several other models. The technology has been developed[^cite_note-26] together with the Finnish company Rightware, using its Kanzi software suite.

### Railway applications

Electronic instrument clusters are being increasingly common features on [railway vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_vehicle), in which individual instruments are replaced by various forms of digital readouts. Early uses of instrument clusters often employed LEDs to display analog-type or numeric readings for [pressure gauges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure_gauge), [electrical gauges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_meter), and other displays. They have been increasingly integrated with various [cab signalling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cab_signalling) systems and together with the installation of [multi-function displays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-function_display), have simplified the [cab layout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cab_\(locomotive\)) and improved [user interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human-Computer_Interaction) with the [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer_\(railroad\)).[^cite_note-RRCabDisplay-27]
