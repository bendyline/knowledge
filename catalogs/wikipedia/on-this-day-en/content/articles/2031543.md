The **Sudan Liberation Movement/Army** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): حركة تحرير السودان *Ḥarakat Taḥrīr as-Sūdān*; [abbreviated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbreviation) **SLM**, **SLA**, or **SLM/A**) is a [Sudanese](27421.md) rebel group active in [Darfur](605837.md), [Sudan](27421.md). It was founded as the **Darfur Liberation Front**[^cite_note-Flint-90-4] by members of three indigenous ethnic groups in [Darfur](605837.md): the [Fur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_people), the [Zaghawa](1467480.md), and the [Masalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masalit_people),[^cite_note-WhoAre-5] among whom were the leaders [Abdul Wahid al-Nur](20343335.md) of the Fur, [Khamis Abakar](74121008.md) of the Masalit and [Minni Minnawi](5906722.md) of the Zaghawa.[^cite_note-WhoAre-5]

## Formation

After a drought in 1984 a small scale conflict called the [War of the Tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Tribes) sprung up in Darfur. The war was marked by increasing ethnic tension, with Arab ethnic groups forming an Arab supremacist league [Tajammu al-Arabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajammu_al-Arabi).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-\:0-7] When General [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md) and the National Islamic Front headed by Dr. [Hassan al-Turabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_al-Turabi) overthrew the Sudanese government led by [Ahmed al-Mirghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_al-Mirghani) in 1989, a large section of the population in Darfur, particularly the non-[Arab](2185.md) ethnicities in the region, became increasingly marginalized.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] These feelings were solidified in 2000 by the publication of *[The Black Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Black_Book:_Imbalance_of_Power_and_Wealth_in_the_Sudan)*, which detailed the structural inequity in the Sudan that denies non-Arabs equal justice and power sharing. In 2002 [Abdul Wahid al-Nur](20343335.md), a lawyer, Ahmad Abdel Shafi Bassey, an education student, and a third man founded the Darfur Liberation Front, which subsequently evolved into the Sudan Liberation Movement and claimed to represent all of the oppressed in the Sudan.[^cite_note-Flint-90-4]

## 2008 Sun Air hijacking

In August 2008, a local [Sun Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Air_\(Sudan\)) [Boeing 737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) carrying 100 passengers including 5 crew members, was hijacked by members belonging to the Sudan Liberation Movement or SLM.[^cite_note-10] The regional flight operated by the private Sudanese airlines Sun Air was en route from [Nyala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyala,_South_Darfur), South Darfur to Khartoum when it was diverted by the Darfuri rebels to the oasis town of [Kufra](3337477.md), in southern Libya. SLM leader [Abdel Wahid al-Nur](20343335.md) who lived in Paris at the time, denied his involvement in the hijacking and condemned the rebels' use of hijacking to bring attention to their cause.[^cite_note-11] SLA field commander Ibrahim al-Hillo suggested that the Boeing hijackers could be al-Nur sympathizers as the SLA had started to break up at the time of hijacking.[^cite_note-12] The 95 passengers were eventually freed unharmed and the 5 crew members were also released. Al Nur said of the hijacking that Khartoum was attempting to manipulate the hijacking news as a tactic to draw attention away from the government's earlier attack on the [Kalma refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalma_refugee_camp?action=edit\&redlink=1) which killed 70 civilians.[^cite_note-13] Kalma camp is located near the Nyala airport. The hijackers were detained by Libyan authorities. While the government attack occurred close to the hijacking, it didn't appear to be a motive for the hijacking.

## Libyan involvement

Before the overthrow of [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md) during the [Libyan Civil War (2011)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Civil_War_\(2011\)), the [Libyan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya) were known to support at least parts of the SLM/A, such as the SLA-Unity.[^cite_note-unity-14] In turn, elements of the SLM/A reportedly became involved in the [Second Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Civil_War_\(2014%E2%80%93present\)), fighting for different factions there in exchange for money and equipment. The SLM/A-Minnawi allied itself with the [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md)-aligned [Libyan National Army](35457665.md) (LNA), and fought alongside it in the [Battle of Derna (2018–2019)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Derna_\(2018%E2%80%932019\)), losing several fighters in the battle.[^cite_note-15] On 12 January 2019, the SLM/A-Minnawi clashed with the [Chadian](5488.md) [CCMSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Command_Council_for_the_Salvation_of_the_Republic) rebel group (an enemy of the LNA) at Gatroun in southern Libya.[^cite_note-16] Later that month, the SLM/A was accused by the CCMSR of aiding an [LNA offensive in southern Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Southern_Libya_offensive).[^cite_note-17]

## Groups and factions

### Sudan Liberation Movement (Minnawi)

The **SLM (Minnawi)**[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrazeTubianaGramizzi20166-18] is led by [Minni Minnawi](5906722.md) and signed the 2006 (Abuja) [Darfur Peace Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darfur_Peace_Agreement) in May 2006. Minnawi served as the Chairman of the [Transitional Darfur Regional Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Darfur_Regional_Authority) from its formation in 2007 to his dismissal in December 2010. The SLM-Minnawi faction formally withdrew from the peace agreement in February 2011.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Unlike most other SPLM/A factions, the SLA-MM was active not just in Darfur, but also took part in the [Sudanese conflict in South Kordofan and Blue Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_conflict_in_South_Kordofan_and_Blue_Nile).[^cite_note-21] The group has also taken part in the [South Sudanese Civil War](41382446.md), fighting for the South Sudanese government against various rebel factions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrazeTubianaGramizzi2016160-22] The SLM-Minnawi was a participant in the 2019 [Sudanese peace process](61758567.md) as of September 2019.[^cite_note-Dabanga_4Darfur_Juba-2] Minnawi was appointed as regional governor of the [Darfur Regional Government](14497212.md) in August 2021.[^cite_note-23]

The **SLM** (**al-Nur**)[^cite_note-second_revolution-24][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrazeTubianaGramizzi20166-18] was formed in 2006 and is led by [Abdul Wahid al Nur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Wahid_al_Nur). It rejected the 2006 (Abuja) Darfur Peace Agreement.[^cite_note-25] The SLM/A (al-Nur) does not officially insist on independence. The group includes both male and female fighters.[^cite_note-arte-1] The group has also taken part in the [South Sudanese Civil War](41382446.md), fighting for the South Sudanese government against various rebel factions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrazeTubianaGramizzi2016160-22] The SLM/A (al-Nur) maintains its stronghold in the [Marrah Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marrah_Mountains) as of 2021,[^cite_note-kleinfeld_amin-26] holds a territory inhabited by about 300,000 people. The area is effectively self-sufficient and mostly isolated from the rest of Sudan.[^cite_note-kleinfeld_amin-26] It runs a *de facto* government there, training new troops, and has also built several schools, where hundreds of children receive daily education.[^cite_note-arte-1] The SLM/A (al-Nur) rejected the 2019 Sudanese peace process, arguing that Arab militias continued their attacks in Darfur and that the new [Sovereignty Council of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereignty_Council_of_Sudan) included many former commanders from the Bashir Era. However, the group was suffering from internal tensions at this point, as some of its factions were clashing with each other. One splinter group led by Zanoun Abdulshafi had begun to fight alongside the Arab militias.[^cite_note-kleinfeld_amin-26] Fighting between the SLM/A (al-Nur) and pro-government forces continues as of 2021.[^cite_note-arte-1]

### Sudan Liberation Forces Alliance

The **SLFA** was created in July 2017 by joining a faction of the SLM-Unity, *Sudan Liberation Movement for Justice*, and a faction of the [Justice and Equality Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Equality_Movement) led by Abdallah Bishr Gali. As of July 2017, the SLFA's chair was El-Taher Abu Bakr Hajar with Abdallah Yahia being the deputy chair.[^cite_note-27] In March 2021, El Taher Hajar was sworn in as a member of Sudan's [Transitional Sovereignty Council](61549039.md) as part of the implementation of the [Juba Peace Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juba_Peace_Agreement).[^cite_note-Rebel_leaders_join_Sudan_Sovereignty_Council-28]

### Sudan Liberation Movement-Transitional Council

As of March 2019, the **SLM-TC** was led by El-Hadi Idris Yahya, as a breakaway group from the SLM (al-Nur); the SLM-TC was opposed to negotiations with Sudanese president [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md) and participated in the *Sudan Call* alliance. In March 2021, El-Hadi Idris was sworn as member of Sudan's [Transitional Sovereignty Council](61549039.md) as part of the implementation of the [Juba Peace Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juba_Peace_Agreement).[^cite_note-Rebel_leaders_join_Sudan_Sovereignty_Council-28] In July 2025, El-Hadi Idris was named as a member of the presidential council of the rival [Government of Peace and Unity](79260861.md) established to administer areas of Sudan controlled by the [Rapid Support Forces](43053874.md) (RSF).[^cite_note-29]

### Sudan Liberation Movement (Tambour)

The **SLM (Tambour)** was formed when [Mustafa Tambour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Tambour) split in 2018 from the SLM (al-Nur) led by [Abdul Wahid al Nur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Wahid_al_Nur).[^cite_note-30] On 31 July 2023, the SLM (Tambour) announced that its forces had joined the SAF in fighting against the RSF during the [2023 Sudan conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Sudan_conflict), claiming to have inflicted 68 casualties on the latter during fighting in [Zalingei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zalingei). Tambour's brother had previously been killed by the RSF.[^cite_note-31]

## August 2020 peace agreement

Main article: [Juba Peace Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juba_Peace_Agreement)

Minni Minawi signed a peace agreement on behalf of the Sudan Liberation Movement with the [Transitional Government of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Sudan) on 31 August 2020 and the organisation will now participate in the [transition to democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932022_Sudanese_transition_to_democracy) in Sudan through peaceful means. Under the terms of the agreement, the factions that signed will be entitled to three seats on the [sovereignty council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereignty_Council_\(Sudan\)), a total of five ministers in the [transitional cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Sudan) and a quarter of seats in the [transitional legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Legislative_Council_\(Sudan\)). At a regional level, signatories will be entitled between 30 and 40% of the seats on transitional legislatures of their home states or regions.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

## Sudanese civil war (2023–present)

Main article: [Sudanese civil war (2023–present)](73556699.md)



The SLM (al-Nur) [zone of control](81580315.md) (red) as of 3 October 2026, including [Tawila](74157994.md)&#x20;

The SLM (al-Nur) has established a zone-of-control around the town of [Tawila](74157994.md) after the withdrawal of both government and [Rapid Support Forces](43053874.md) (RSF) forces in the region. This zone of control also includes several surrounding villages including Martal, Al-Aradib, [Al-Ashra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ashra), Sortony, and Tibira in [North Darfur](767666.md), Kadner and Kankoro in [South Darfur](767650.md), and Katrom, Sabanga, Barqa, and Rofta in [Central Darfur](34420004.md).[^cite_note-46]

On January 22, 2024, the combatants that signed the [Juba Peace Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juba_Peace_Agreement) met for a summit in [Cairo](6293.md). Namely, Brigadier General Mubarak Bakhit, the Secretary of Presidential Affairs of the Gathering of Sudan Liberation Forces, Suleiman Sandal and Gibril Adam Bilal, Vice Presidents of the [Justice and Equality Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Equality_Movement), and Seif al-Din Issa Advisor to the President of the SLM-TC, however, [Minni Arko Minawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minni_Arko_Minawi) and his faction of the SLM did not attend. The gathering recommended that the Jeddah Declaration to Cessation of Hostilities be implemented, which outlined the withdrawal of all RSF personnel from Darfur.[^cite_note-47]

On March 24, 2024, Minawi announced that the 1,500 SLM fighters would join the Sudanese Armed Forces in fighting the RSF in order to "defend civilians and Sudan's sovereignty." SLM forces are to progress toward the country's center to expel the RSF from Khartoum and [Gezira State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gezira_State).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

Between 15 and 16 January 2025, clashes between the RSF and SLA erupted in [Kabhabiya](59604750.md), resulting in over 120 killed.[^cite_note-50]

On April 12, 2025, Abdul Wahid Mohammed Ahmed Al Nour issued an urgent appeal to relief agencies for assistance in dealing with a huge influx of refugees from the fighting in [Al Fashir](3956787.md) into the Tawila and Jebel Marra areas.[^cite_note-51] On November 2, 2025, Al Nour announced a mission to countries of the European Union "to engage in high-level discussions aimed at facilitating the delivery of humanitarian relief and assistance to vulnerable populations affected by the ongoing war across all regions in Sudan."[^cite_note-52]
