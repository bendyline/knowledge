**State police**, **provincial police** or **regional police** are a type of sub-national [territorial police force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_police_force) found in nations organized as [federations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation), typically in [North America](21139.md), [South Asia](21566765.md), and [Oceania](22621.md). These forces typically have jurisdiction over the relevant sub-national jurisdiction, and may cooperate in law enforcement activities with municipal or national police where either exist.

## Argentina

In [Argentina](18951905.md), as a federal country, each [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Argentina) has its own independent police force and its responsible of its funding, training and equipment. State police agencies are responsible of all the territory of a determinate state. There is almost no municipal/local law enforcement in Argentina, and if there is, they are generally limited to traffic duties.

## Australia

See also: [Law enforcement in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_Australia)

Prior to the [Federation of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Australia), each colony within Australia had numerous police forces, but these were largely [amalgamated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidation_\(business\)) well before federation.

Today each [state of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Australia), as well as the [Northern Territory](21638.md), has its own state police force.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] Municipalities do not have police forces and it is left to the state forces to police all geographic areas within their respective states. Australia does have a national police force, the [Australian Federal Police](341221.md), whose role is to enforce the laws of the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Australia), both criminal law and civil law, as well as to protect the interests of the Commonwealth, both domestically and internationally. The AFP does, however, provide policing for the [Australian Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Capital_Territory), [Jervis Bay Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jervis_Bay_Territory), and Australia's other external territories such as [Norfolk Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Island), [Christmas Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Island) and the [Cocos (Keeling) Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_\(Keeling\)_Islands).[^cite_note-3]

The state and territory police forces are:

## Brazil

See also: [Law enforcement in Brazil](4876365.md#State_nstitutions)

Each [federative unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federative_units_of_Brazil) in [Brazil](3383.md), beside its [military firefighters corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Firefighters_Corps) and its penal and scientific polices, has two main police corporations working together with different skills:

*[Civil Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Police_\(Brazil\))* — The nonuniformed judiciary police, mainly responsible for investigating criminal offenses occurring in their territories. They're mainly based on Delegates (necessarily [bachelors of laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Laws)), Clerks, Investigators and/or Agents and also responds for its Scientific Polices (responsible for [forensic science](45710.md) activities and services) in the [Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_District_\(Brazil\)) and other 8 states.

*[Military Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Police_\(Brazil\))* — The uniformed and militarily hierarchical police officers perform, under a [gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie) system, police functions of patrolling, approaching and capturing, also serving as reserve branches of the [Brazilian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Armed_Forces) (more specifically its [Brazilian Army](1047559.md)). They make up the majority of the police force in Brazil,

[^cite_note-4]

In [São Paulo](229422.md), the [Military Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Police_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo_State) also operates its [Military Firefighters Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Firefighters_Corps). ## Canada



Sûreté du Québec cruiser

[Law enforcement in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_Canada) operates at the federal, provincial, and local levels. Five [provinces of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Canada) have a dedicated police force, with jurisdiction over some or all of the province:

- [Ontario Provincial Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Provincial_Police) ([Ontario](22218.md))

- [Royal Newfoundland Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Newfoundland_Constabulary) ([Newfoundland and Labrador](21980.md))

- [Sûreté du Québec](375500.md) ([Quebec](7954867.md))

- [Saskatchewan Marshals Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan_Marshals_Service) ([Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan))

- [Alberta Sheriffs Police Service](https://rmalberta.com/news/government-of-alberta-announces-alberta-sheriffs-police-service/) ([Alberta](717.md))

The federal [Royal Canadian Mounted Police](69080.md) (RCMP) provides provincial-level policing in the remaining land area of Newfoundland and Labrador, as well as the remaining seven provinces and three territories. The RCMP began being contracted to provide provincial policing in the late 1920s as provincial police forces were disbanded and their duties contracted out to provincial divisions of the RCMP. The most recent provincial police force to be disbanded, the [British Columbia Provincial Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Provincial_Police), existed from the mid-19th century until its jurisdiction was transferred to [RCMP "E" Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCMP_%22E%22_Division) on August 15, 1950.

Provinces which have disbanded their provincial police may retain other provincial law enforcement agencies, such as [sheriff services](89516.md) or [conservation officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_officer). For example, the [Alberta Sheriffs Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Sheriffs_Branch) is responsible for traffic enforcement in [Alberta](717.md) together with the RCMP, despite being a law enforcement agency and not a police force, although there have been plans to create a new provincial police service "under the sheriffs".[^cite_note-5]

## India

Main article: [Police forces of the states and union territories of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_forces_of_the_states_and_union_territories_of_India)



The "Jaguar" Team of the [Delhi Police](8808263.md)

The [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India) delineates the roles and responsibilities of both the central and state governments, with matters related to police, public order, and law and order falling under the purview of the state governments. India has 28 state police agencies.

Each [state](375986.md) has a state police force and its own distinct [state police services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Police_Services), headed by the [director general of police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_General_of_Police) (DGP) or [commissioner of police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioner_of_Police_\(India\)) (in metropolitan areas), who is an [Indian Police Service](451884.md) officer. The state police forces operate under the authority of the respective [state governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_governments_of_India) and are empowered to enforce laws, prevent crime, investigate offenses, and maintain public order.&#x20;

Most municipal or city police agencies operate under their respective state police departments, with the exception of the [Kolkata Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata_Police). State police agencies typically have jurisdiction across the entire state. However, in [West Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bengal_Police), the jurisdiction of the state police excludes [Kolkata](47905.md), which is served by the Kolkata Police.[^cite_note-6]

The police forces of [union territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory) in India are either directly or indirectly under the purview of the [Union Government](553883.md). Unlike state police forces, the [Delhi Police](8808263.md) operate under the direct control of the Union [Ministry of Home Affairs, Government of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Home_Affairs_\(India\)) due to Delhi's status as both the national capital territory and a union territory. While they function under the respective union territory administrations for day-to-day operations and governance, their ultimate authority and oversight lie with the central government.

The [Indian Police Service](451884.md) (IPS) is not a law enforcement agency in its own right; rather it is the body to which all senior police officers of all states belong regardless of the agency for whom they work. The IPS officers provide senior level leadership to law enforcement agencies, both at the state and federal levels.

## United States



North Carolina State Highway Patrol cruiser on [I-85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_85) in 2008

Main article: [State police (United States)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_police_\(United_States\))

See also: [List of state police agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_police_\(United_States\)#List_of_State_Police_agencies)

In the United States, state police (also termed [highway patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_patrol), state patrol, or state highway patrol), or *state troopers*, are a police body unique to 49 of the U.S. states, having statewide authority to conduct law enforcement activities and criminal investigations. Hawaii, despite a widely dispersed [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) and having [four separate county-based police agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_police_\(United_States\)#Statewide_policing_in_Hawaii), still has [its own statewide policing agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaii_Department_of_Law_Enforcement#Sheriff_Division).

In general, these police agencies perform functions outside the jurisdiction of the [county sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_sheriff), such as enforcing [traffic laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_code) on [state highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_highway) and [interstate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System) expressways, overseeing the security of the state capitol complex, protecting the governor, training new officers for local police forces too small to operate an academy, providing technological and scientific services, supporting local police and helping to coordinate multi-jurisdictional task force activity in serious or complicated cases in those states that grant full police powers statewide. In some rural states, they may be responsible for the majority of [911](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/911_\(emergency_telephone_number\)) call responses. A general trend has been to bring all of these agencies under a state [department of public safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_public_safety). Additionally, they may serve under different state departments such as the highway patrol under the state [department of transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_transportation) and the [marine patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_police) under the state [department of natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_environmental_agencies_in_the_United_States).

Twenty-three [U.S. states](18618239.md) use the term *state police*, fifteen use the term *highway patrol*, seven use the term *state patrol*, and three use the term *state highway patrol*, while Alaska's agency is the "Division of Alaska State Troopers". The term *highway patrol* tends to be more common in the [southeast](816925.md) and [mountain west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_states) states. Hawaii is the only state with a Sheriff Division of the Hawaii Department of Law Enforcement with statewide jurisdiction.
