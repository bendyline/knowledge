Protests at several locations in [Ireland](14560.md) started in early November 2022 after the development of sites in various parts of the country as [asylum seeker shelters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_shelter) by the [Department of Children, Equality, Disability, Integration and Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Children,_Disability_and_Equality) (DCEDIY), as it attempted to accommodate the influx of 65,000 asylum seekers during 2022.[^cite_note-ew-journalexploit-6] Protests were held in [East Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Wall), [Ballymun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballymun), [Drimnagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drimnagh) and elsewhere in [Dublin](8504.md); [Fermoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermoy) and [Mallow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallow,_County_Cork) in [County Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Cork); [Kill, County Kildare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill,_County_Kildare); [Lismore, County Waterford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lismore,_County_Waterford); [Mullingar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullingar), [County Westmeath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Westmeath); [Inch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inch,_County_Clare), [County Clare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Clare); and [Rosslare Harbour, County Wexford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosslare_Harbour).

The protests, which the [Garda Síochána](383207.md) say numbered 307 in 2022 and at least a further 169 as of August 2023,[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-\:1-8][^cite_note-\:6-9] raised concerns over the lack of provision of prior information to local residents and concerns around the impact that local [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth), due to accommodating asylum seekers, had on facilities and housing.[^cite_note-kildareroad-10][^cite_note-rte-ew-11][^cite_note-fermoymisinfo-12][^cite_note-\:16-13] Protesters have questioned the lack of [women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee_women) and [children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee_children) in some accommodation centres where there have been a high proportion of single males.[^cite_note-fermoymisinfo-12][^cite_note-kildareroad-10][^cite_note-ew-males-14] Some protesters have also been influenced by rumours purporting asylum seekers carrying out violent or threatening acts that have been shown to be often baseless.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-\:2-16][^cite_note-\:3-17][^cite_note-\:4-18] Additionally, Gardaí stated in March 2023 that there has not been an increase in crime as a result of [international protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_Relating_to_the_Status_of_Refugees) applicants nor a need for increased presence near the shelters.[^cite_note-19] [Garda Assistant Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garda_Commissioner#Office_of_the_Garda_Commissioner) Angela Willis reported that both attendance and frequency of protests "appeared to reach a peak" in March 2023.[^cite_note-\:1-8]

The presence of [anti-immigrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-immigrant) protesters, members of [far-right](221220.md) groups and violent criminals at these protests has been highlighted [online](5897742.md) and in the mainstream media,[^cite_note-fermoymisinfo-12][^cite_note-ew-embarrassing-20][^cite_note-gangland-21] and 17 people have been arrested at the protests in 2023 as of August.[^cite_note-\:6-9] Some far-right protesters have been labelled as "hijackers" of the protests' true purpose and some demonstrations have been described as misrepresentative of local sentiment.[^cite_note-ew-embarrassing-20][^cite_note-\:10-22][^cite_note-23] Politicians [have commented](#Political_response) on the protests[^cite_note-journalleaflet-24][^cite_note-ministersmeet-25] including [Leo Varadkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Varadkar) (the [Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoiseach) at the time) who said said locals cannot veto "the kind of people who get to live in their area",[^cite_note-Varadkar_12_Jan_no_veto-26] and the topic has been raised in [Seanad Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seanad_%C3%89ireann) and in a [joint committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_committee_\(legislative\)) with [Dáil Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_%C3%89ireann).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] Plans for better consultation with locals have been [put in place](#Political_response) and government handling has been both defended and criticised within the [Oireachtas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oireachtas).[^cite_note-29]

## Background

Protests at several locations in the north and west of the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md) started in November 2018 against the placement of refugees in [direct provision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_provision) centres in rural locations.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] Proposed refugee shelters were [firebombed](184695.md) in [Moville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moville) and [Roosky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosky), as was politician [Martin Kenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Kenny)'s car in [Ballinamore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballinamore) after he spoke in defence of refugees, and demonstrations were held in Roosky, [Oughterard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oughterard), Ballinamore, [Carrickmacross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrickmacross) and [Achill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achill).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] What was the Minister of Justice and Equality [Charlie Flanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Flanagan) and others claimed the 25 November 2018 fire at a hotel-turned-direct provision centre a week before 100 asylum seekers were going to move in was arson.[^cite_note-35]

[Anti-immigrant](6491626.md) members of [far-right](221220.md) groups were involved in some of the protests.[^cite_note-36] Politicians and others took different positions on the protests, with some supporting them and others criticising them.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

In February 2022, a [major international refugee crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%932023_Ukrainian_refugee_crisis) occurred following the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), with millions of Ukrainians displaced. Alongside almost all other European countries, Ireland accepted Ukrainian refugees fleeing the war; by mid-November 2022 over 58,000 Ukrainians had come to Ireland.[^cite_note-40] November 2022 was also the beginning of the protests against the development of sites as temporary refugee shelters by the DCEDIY.[^cite_note-ew-journalexploit-6] Since the mid-2010s, Ireland has been experiencing a housing and accommodation crisis;[^cite_note-ew-journalexploit-6] the simultaneous overlap of both crises exacerbated each other; the failure to provide housing meant that refugees could not expect to find much accommodation in Ireland, and the arrival of thousands of refugees in Ireland meant further strains on housing and accommodation demands that were already peaking.[^cite_note-ew-journalexploit-6] The crises of accommodation were in spite of over 130,000 empty homes in Ireland in 2022.[^cite_note-41]

## Chronology

From late 2022, a wave of anti-immigration protests emerged across Ireland, triggered by the government's use of hotels, convents, former office buildings and other vacant properties to house asylum seekers and refugees. A recurring grievance across virtually all incidents was the perceived lack of prior consultation with local communities. The protests were often accompanied by misinformation spread on social media, and were frequently exploited by far-right organisations including the [National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_\(Ireland,_2016\)), the [Irish Freedom Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Freedom_Party) and Ireland First, who used them to promote Euroscepticism, extreme Irish nationalism and, in some cases, pro-Russian ideology.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-ew-irishtimes-43][^cite_note-ew-tampons-44][^cite_note-45]

The first major flashpoint came in November 2022 in [East Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Wall), Dublin, where protests erupted over the conversion of the Two Gateway Building, a former [ESB Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESB_Group) office block, into an emergency accommodation centre for around 100 male asylum seekers.[^cite_note-rte-ew-11][^cite_note-ew-journalexploit-6] The protests were organised by the "East Wall Protest Committee", led by solicitor and former [Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Party_\(Ireland\)) member [Malachy Steenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malachy_Steenson).[^cite_note-fermoymisinfo-12][^cite_note-ew-embarrassing-20] Protesters blocked the [Dublin Port Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Port_Tunnel) on multiple occasions and later disrupted access to the [East-Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East-Link_\(Dublin\)) toll bridge, [Samuel Beckett Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Beckett_Bridge) and [Busáras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus%C3%A1ras).[^cite_note-journalleaflet-24] Far-right figures such as [Hermann Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Kelly) of the [Irish Freedom Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Freedom_Party) used the protests to attack a range of unrelated culture-war targets.[^cite_note-ew-tampons-44] Similar protests took place in [Fermoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermoy), County Cork in the same period, where [Derek Blighe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Blighe) of Ireland First addressed a crowd of roughly 70 people; a counter-demonstration of up to 300 people was subsequently organised by Fermoy and Mallow Against Racism.[^cite_note-fermoymisinfo-12][^cite_note-fermoyracism-46] Protests also occurred in [Kill, County Kildare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill,_County_Kildare), where a fire broke out at the earmarked equestrian centre shortly after demonstrations, and an anti-immigrant motorcade protest of between 100 and 200 vehicles was later organised.[^cite_note-kill-fire-47][^cite_note-mirror-48]

### Escalation in 2023

Through 2023, the pattern of protest intensified and, in several cases, crossed into criminality. In January of that year, protesters assembled in [Drimnagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drimnagh) based on false information that a school was being permanently converted to house refugees, leading to one arrest.[^cite_note-journalleaflet-24] In [Ballymun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballymun), protesters blocked the [M50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M50_motorway_\(Ireland\)) using [sulkies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulkies) and demonstrations took place outside a [Travelodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travelodge) housing 221 international protection seekers.[^cite_note-blocktraffic-49] In [Ashtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashtown), a migrant tent camp on the banks of the [River Tolka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Tolka) was attacked by a group of men with dogs and a baseball bat; a migrant solidarity rally of around 200 people followed.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-\:2-16] In [Finglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finglas), a protest of around 200 people was accompanied by the [Garda Public Order Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garda_Public_Order_Unit) in riot gear, and a local far-right activist, Graham Carey, was subsequently arrested and charged with [incitement to hatred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incitement_to_hatred) after posting threats online.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-\:3-17] The attacks in both Finglas and Ashtown were motivated by false rumours about migrant crime.[^cite_note-\:3-17]

Attacks on physical property also became increasingly common. A 19th-century building on Sherrard Street in Dublin was set ablaze in January 2023 following rumours it would house refugees,[^cite_note-53] and a fire broke out at the former [Crown Paint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Paints) factory site in [Coolock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolock) during protests in July 2024.[^cite_note-tj-criminal-element-embedded-54] On Upper Sandwith Street in February 2023, an anti-immigration group attacked a refugee camp and set fire to tents, leading to one arrest on arson charges.[^cite_note-IT_12_May_2023-55][^cite_note-Hot_Press_15_May_2023-56] A similar incident occurred in [Ringsend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringsend) in late December 2023, where a suspected arson attack destroyed the former Shipwright pub, which had been earmarked for homeless families.[^cite_note-57] Arson attacks were also recorded in [Brittas, County Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brittas,_County_Dublin), [Tallaght](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallaght), and at a hotel in [Rosscahill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosscahill), [Oughterard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oughterard), County Galway, where four people were eventually arrested.[^cite_note-tj-senior-officer-to-investigate-fire-at-former-nursing-home-58][^cite_note-59]

In September 2023, a large and aggressive demonstration was held at [Leinster House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leinster_House), with around 200 protesters blocking TDs and Oireachtas staff from entering or leaving. A mock [gallows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallows) with an effigy and portraits of politicians, including Taoiseach Leo Varadkar and [Roderic O'Gorman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roderic_O'Gorman), was erected. Bottles of urine were alleged to have been thrown at Gardaí, thirteen people were arrested, and the [Tánaiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1naiste), [Micheál Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miche%C3%A1l_Martin), described the conduct of protesters as "fascist-like".[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-\:14-61] Beyond Dublin, significant protests took place in [Mullingar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullingar) at [Columb Barracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columb_Barracks), in [County Clare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Clare) where protesters blockaded a hotel at [Inch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inch,_County_Clare) and allegedly boarded a bus to conduct a headcount of asylum seekers,[^cite_note-clare-headcount-62] and in [Rosslare Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosslare_Harbour), where around 1,000 people attended a blockade affecting ferry traffic at [Rosslare Europort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosslare_Europort).[^cite_note-\:11-63]

### November 2023 Dublin riot

This section is an excerpt from [2023 Dublin riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Dublin_riot).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2023_Dublin_riot\&action=edit)]

On the evening of 23 November 2023, a [riot](146704.md) took place in [Dublin](8504.md), Ireland, which involved multiple incidents of vandalism, arson, and looting in the city centre as well as assaults on [Gardaí](383207.md) (Irish police) and members of the public.[^cite_note-2023_Dublin_riot_HTCU-64] Gardaí described the riot as the most violent in modern Dublin history, far surpassing the [2006 riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Dublin_riots).[^cite_note-2023_Dublin_riot_IrishTimesLallyHolland-65] Initial estimates by [Dublin City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_City_Council) suggested that the damage could cost up to €20 million,[^cite_note-66] while then-[Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoiseach), [Leo Varadkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Varadkar), stated that it could cost "tens of millions".[^cite_note-67]

The riot was triggered by a man stabbing three young children and a care assistant at around 1\:30 p.m. [GMT](12701.md) that day outside a [primary school](25058.md) in [Parnell Square East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parnell_Square), Dublin. A five-year-old girl was critically injured and the care assistant—who had tried to shield the children with her own body—was seriously injured. In the hours following the stabbings, [far-right](221220.md) activists used social media and messaging apps to spread public anger over immigration.[^cite_note-68] At a later point, the suspect was identified as Riad Bouchaker, originally from [Algeria](358.md). Bouchaker was charged with multiple counts of attempted murder and assault and currently faces trial at the [Central Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Criminal_Court_\(Ireland\)). The five-year-old girl was discharged from hospital in August 2024.

By 5 p.m., a crowd of 100 to 200 [anti-immigrant](6491626.md) demonstrators had gathered at Parnell Square. The unrest began at around 6 p.m. when demonstrators began throwing fireworks, flares and bottles at the Gardaí who were maintaining [a cordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kettling) around the crime scene. Shortly before 7 p.m., a nearby Garda car was set on fire.[^cite_note-2023_Dublin_riot_RTEFletcherGardaiAttacked-69][^cite_note-2023_Dublin_riot_HTCU-64] Rioting progressed to the adjacent [O'Connell Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Connell_Street), Dublin's main thoroughfare, and also spread to [Capel Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capel_Street) and [Parliament Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_Street,_Dublin). At its peak, the rioting crowd was estimated at 500 people.[^cite_note-2023_Dublin_riot_Telegraph_500_crowd_size-70] Several [Dublin Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Bus) vehicles, Garda vehicles and a [Luas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luas) tram were damaged or destroyed by arson and vandalism, and multiple shops were [looted](209811.md). In response, around 400 Gardaí were dispatched, including the largest deployment of Gardaí [armed with riot gear](1148542.md) in Ireland's history.[^cite_note-2023_Dublin_riot_largest-71] Approximately 60 Gardaí were assaulted during the riot, three of whom sustained serious injuries. By 10 p.m., the rioters had been dispersed, with 34 arrests made that evening and further arrests made over subsequent days.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-2023_Dublin_riot_\:9-73]

Following the riot, the [Garda Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garda_Commissioner), [Drew Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Harris), blamed the events on a "lunatic, hooligan faction driven by a far-right ideology". Other public figures criticised policing in Dublin city and called for the resignations of Harris and the [Minister for Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Justice,_Home_Affairs_and_Migration), [Helen McEntee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_McEntee). The minister subsequently survived a [vote of no confidence](208101.md) in [Dáil Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_%C3%89ireann). The government implemented additional riot-prevention measures after the incident, borrowing [water cannons](431462.md) from the [Police Service of Northern Ireland](294068.md), passing new laws enabling the use of [police body cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_body_camera), and announcing plans to expand the use of hand-held [video cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_camera), [tasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taser), and [pepper spray](157302.md).[^cite_note-74]

### Later incidents

Protests continued into 2024 and 2025. The [Coolock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolock) protests, which began in March 2024 at the site of the former [Crown Paint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Paints) factory, became among the most prolonged and violent, with multiple arson attacks, petrol bombs and bricks thrown at Gardaí and firefighters, improvised incendiary devices seized nearby, and 21 people arrested over one weekend in July 2024.[^cite_note-rte-arrests-as-public-order-unit-deployed-75] Taoiseach [Simon Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Harris) described the scenes as "reprehensible".[^cite_note-rte-arrests-as-public-order-unit-deployed-75]

In April 2025, a "National Protest" march organised by [Malachy Steenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malachy_Steenson) drew over 10,000 people from the Garden of Remembrance to the Custom House, endorsed publicly by [Conor McGregor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conor_McGregor) and notable for the mass display of Irish tricolour flags.[^cite_note-\:19-76] In October 2025, a riot broke out outside the Citywest IPAS Centre in [Saggart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saggart), triggered by the alleged rape of a 10-year-old girl by a resident of the centre; up to 2,000 demonstrators gathered before unrest broke out, with Gardaí attacked using fireworks, rocks and bottles, a Garda vehicle set alight, and attempts made to breach a police cordon using horse-drawn [sulkies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulkies).[^cite_note-\:20-77][^cite_note-\:21-78] Days later, on 31 October 2025, an IPAS centre in [Drogheda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drogheda) was deliberately set alight using an accelerant and fireworks, with four children rescued from the top floor of the building.[^cite_note-rte-gardai-confirm-ipas-centre-fire-was-started-deliberately-79]

## Other issues raised

### Lack of accommodation

The government's failure to find accommodation for asylum seekers has been criticised by the [Irish Human Rights and Equality Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Human_Rights_and_Equality_Commission), the latter saying that Ireland was in breach of their international obligations, specifically referring to an [EU directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directive_\(European_Union\)). It has also been raised by protesters to support the Ireland is Full movement, but Minister O'Gorman of the DCEDIY said the shortage is "short-term" and that "as a system, as a State, we can continue to fulfill our legal obligations and our moral obligations".[^cite_note-80]

### Gardaí handling

The [Policing Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Policing_Authority_\(Ireland\)) condemned unlawful escalations at the protests and applauded the Gardaí's balancing of the safeguarding of people and the [right to protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_protest) after middle-ranking Gardaí joined those working at the frontline of the protests in raising concerns over the frontline being "grossly undertrained" for the escalating protests.[^cite_note-81]

After similar concerns were raised to Taoiseach Varadkar by the Antoinette Cunningham of the garda representative group the Association of Garda Sergeants and Inspectors, the Taoiseach said he understood from [Simon Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Harris)'s meeting with [Drew Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Harris) (the [Garda Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garda_Commissioner)), that "the training is in place". He addressed criticism from the public over Gardaí not intervening in the protests and said "there's a way of managing" them.[^cite_note-82] He later clarified on 22 May that "over-response by authorities of the state" is part of the far-right protesters' "playbook" and that he does not want to "fall into their trap" and instead to "build a consent" around housing International Protection seekers.[^cite_note-83] Cunningham said to *[Morning Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_Ireland)* that Gardaí felt "they had not received proper training about peaceful crowd management" and criticised the commissioner's claim of "operational integrity" as frontline gardaí had no direction from senior garda management what their role was besides preventing a [breach of the peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breach_of_the_peace) on top of her concerns of the frontline being grossly untrained.[^cite_note-84] She requested that there be training relating to the [Public Order Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_Justice_\(Public_Order\)_Act,_1994) and the [Incitement to Hatred Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hate_speech_laws_by_country#Ireland) for the "spontaneous protests that can arise with very little notice" that Gardaí have less time to plan for.

The [Tánaiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1naiste), [Micheál Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miche%C3%A1l_Martin), said that the commissioner was "confident" that Gardaí had the capacity to deal with anti-refugee protests but [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Ireland\)) TD [Patrick Costello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Costello_\(Irish_politician\)) called for the commissioner to appear at the [Oireachtas Justice Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committees_of_the_Oireachtas) on the recent attacks on migrants and migrant centres. Supporters of better management of protests include the [Immigrant Council of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigrant_Council_of_Ireland), the [Movement of Asylum Seekers in Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Asylum_Seekers_in_Ireland) and Green Party TD [Neasa Hourigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neasa_Hourigan).[^cite_note-85]

## Slogans

The phrase "Ireland is full" is a slogan that has trended on [Twitter](9988187.md) (as the [hashtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashtag) "#IrelandIsFull") prior to these protests.[^cite_note-fermoymisinfo-12] It means that Ireland does not have any more accommodation or support for asylum seekers.

[Nativist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativism_\(politics\)) slogans like "Ireland for the Irish" and "Ireland belongs to the Irish" have been used by parties like the National Party.[^cite_note-86]

The anti-immigration movement claims to represent 90% of Irish people and that 90% of people polled disagree with the government's asylum seeker policy.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

## Use of the Irish flag



Irish flag mural in Fairview, Dublin seemingly erected by a local right wing and anti-immigration campaigner whose business operates from this building.

&#x20;Beginning in August 2025, the erection of Irish flags on lampposts and buildings across numerous towns and cities in Ireland was noted. Apparently taking inspiration from similar activities in England known as [Operation Raise the Colours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Raise_the_Colours), the flags have been promoted on social media by anti-migrant groups to use the flag to "reclaim" areas for Irish people and were being erected by anti-immigration campaigners.&#x20;

&#x20;Posts on social media used hashtags such as "Irish Lives Matter". Local county councils appeared to be reluctant to remove them, with concerns around harassment and abuse cited.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

## Reactions

### Political response

On 12 January 2023, Taoiseach Leo Varadkar commented that while communities have the right to be consulted by the government about who is placed there, he stated "I don't think any community can have a veto on who gets to live in their area. I think we need to be very careful not to make the mistake of confusing consultation and information with communities, which is important, with the idea that any community can have a veto on the kind of people who get to live in their area. That's not right."[^cite_note-Varadkar_12_Jan_no_veto-26]

On 3 February 2023, Minister for Housing [Darragh O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darragh_O'Brien) commented that "First and foremost, what I saw in Finglas this week and what I have seen across the country with some of the protests has to be condemned outright. People have a right to protest and have their view, even if I don't share their view. They don't have a right to intimidate people. They don't have a right to bully people. Some of the activity we have seen is reprehensible" before suggesting that a number of the protests had been "hijacked" by "far-right agitators" seeking to capitalise on local unrest.[^cite_note-\:10-22]

On 5 February 2023, President [Michael D. Higgins](559753.md) said in relation to the protests "What is unforgivable and must be opposed—publicly, vocally and unequivocally—are those who are trying to take advantage by sowing hate and building fear. We are in a position now where we have elements who are not interested in solving the long-standing problems within communities or the new arrivals. You mustn't give them the opportunity. The best way of not giving them the opportunity is to fill the place with services. These people who are going around whipping people up and so forth, you didn't see them previously making a case for housing, or for women's rights, or for equal rights of any kind".[^cite_note-92]

[Joe O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_O'Brien_\(politician\)), a [Minister of State at the DCEDIY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_State_at_the_Department_of_Children,_Disability_and_Equality) told the lower house Dáil Éireann on 30 May 2023 that the [Department of the Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_the_Taoiseach) had been making plans over several months to implement a communications strategy involving the provision of prior information, where possible, to local representatives about new migrants moving in.[^cite_note-\:5-93][^cite_note-94] In the meantime, [Social Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democrats_\(Ireland\)) leader [Holly Cairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly_Cairns) TD described the cabinet as being "at war" on the issue, with the government's response being "chaotic", criticising the lack of information.

### "Ireland for All" Solidarity Rally









Marchers during the "Ireland for All" Solidarity rally on 18 February 2023

On 18 February, around 50,000 marchers rallied in Dublin and others in Sligo under the banner of "Ireland For All". The Dublin rally was organised by the newly founded organisation, "Le Chéile", which comprises almost 50 member organisations and which had called for a march in support of "diversity not division", and [opposition to racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_to_racism).[^cite_note-99] Attendees at the Dublin rally included members and supporters of [Sinn Féin](28175.md), the [Social Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democrats_\(Ireland\)), the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Ireland\)) and [People Before Profit-Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Before_Profit-Solidarity). Other organisations present included various trade unions such as [Fórsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%B3rsa), [SIPTU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIPTU) and the [Teachers' Union of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachers'_Union_of_Ireland); the [Movement of Asylum Seekers in Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_Asylum_Seekers_in_Ireland), [Pavee Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavee_Point) and the [National Women's Council of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women's_Council_of_Ireland). Marchers were still passing the [GPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Post_Office,_Dublin) as the front of the march reached [Custom House Quay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Custom_House_Quay). When the march arrived at the Customs House, [Christy Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christy_Moore) spoke and performed, and [Bernadette McAliskey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernadette_McAliskey) addressed the rally, saying "...the question that must be asked of the nation and of individuals is 'Whose side are you on?'" She said one side is the side of humanity, decency and human rights, and that the other side is "on the road to [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism)."[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]
