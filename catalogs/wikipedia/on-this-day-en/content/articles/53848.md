**San Antonio** ([/ˌsæn ænˈtoʊnioʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*SAN an-TOH-nee-oh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); Spanish for "[Saint Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_of_Padua)") is a city in the southern part of the U.S. state of [Texas](29810.md). It is the [seventh-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population) in the United States, the [second-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Texas) in Texas (after [Houston](13774.md)), and the second-most populous city in the [Southern U.S.](179553.md), with a population of 1.43 million at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census). The [Greater San Antonio metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_San_Antonio), with an estimated 2.76 million residents, ranks as the [third-largest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Texas_metropolitan_areas) in Texas and the [24th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) in the nation.[^cite_note-\:1-13][^cite_note-QuickFacts-9] It is the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [Bexar County](91655.md).

The area was named "San Antonio" in 1691 by a Spanish expedition in honor of Saint [Anthony of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_of_Padua). A [Spanish mission and colonial outpost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas) was founded there in 1718, and San Antonio became the first chartered civil settlement in present-day Texas in 1731. It was part of the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire), then the [Mexican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mexican_Republic) from 1821 to 1836, before joining the United States after the annexation of the Republic of Texas in 1846.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Straddling the regional divide between [South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Texas) and [Central Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Texas), San Antonio anchors the southwestern corner of an urban [megaregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megaregion) colloquially known as the [Texas Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Triangle). It lies about 80 miles (130 km) from [Austin](1998.md) along the [I-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_35_in_Texas) corridor, and together, the [San Antonio–Austin metroplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio%E2%80%93Austin_metroplex) is home to approximately 5 million people.[^cite_note-sa-atxmetro-17]

San Antonio is home to five 18th-century Spanish frontier missions, including [The Alamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamo_Mission) and those preserved in [San Antonio Missions National Historical Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Missions_National_Historical_Park), which were collectively designated as [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage sites](44940.md) in 2015.[^cite_note-PBS-SanAn-18] Major attractions include the [River Walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_River_Walk), [Tower of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_the_Americas), [SeaWorld San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaWorld_San_Antonio), [Six Flags Fiesta Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Fiesta_Texas), and the [Alamo Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamo_Bowl). The city also hosts the [San Antonio Stock Show & Rodeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Stock_Show_&_Rodeo) and is home to the five-time [NBA](22093.md) champion [San Antonio Spurs](72889.md). San Antonio welcomes approximately 32 million visitors annually and is a key military hub, hosting several major [U.S. Armed Forces](32212.md) facilities, including [Fort Sam Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Sam_Houston) and nearby bases such as [Lackland](405875.md) and [Randolph Air Force Bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randolph_Air_Force_Base). The city is also home to two [*Fortune* 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) companies, the [South Texas Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Texas_Medical_Center), and the [largest majority-Hispanic population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._cities_with_large_Hispanic_populations) in the U.S., with 64% of residents identifying as Hispanic.[^cite_note-census-19]

## History

Main article: [History of San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_San_Antonio)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_San_Antonio).

At the time of European encounter, the [Payaya people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payaya_people) lived near the [San Antonio River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_River) in the [San Pedro Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_Springs) area. They called the vicinity *[Yanaguana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanaguana_\(San_Antonio\))*, meaning "refreshing waters". In 1691, a group of Spanish explorers and [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary) came upon the river and Payaya settlement on June 13, the feast day of [St. Anthony of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_of_Padua).[^cite_note-\:0-20] They named the place and river "San Antonio" in his honor.[^cite_note-21]

It was years before any Spanish settlement took place. [Father Antonio de Olivares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_de_Olivares) visited the site in 1709, and he was determined to found a mission and civilian settlement there. The viceroy gave formal approval for a combined mission and presidio in late 1716, as he wanted to forestall any French expansion into the area from their colony of [*La Louisiane*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_France\)) to the east, as well as prevent illegal trading with the Payaya. He directed [Martín de Alarcón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mart%C3%ADn_de_Alarc%C3%B3n), the governor of [Coahuila y Tejas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coahuila_y_Tejas), to establish the mission complex. Differences between Alarcón and Olivares resulted in delays, and construction did not start until 1718.[^cite_note-tshaonline.org-22] Olivares built, with the help of the [Payaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payaya_Indians) and the [Pastia people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastia_people), the [Misión de San Antonio de Valero (The Alamo)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamo_Mission_in_San_Antonio), the [Presidio San Antonio de Bexar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidio_San_Antonio_de_Bexar), the bridge that connected both, and the [Acequia Madre de Valero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acequia_Madre_de_Valero_\(San_Antonio\)).[^cite_note-Adina-5]

The families who clustered around the presidio and mission were the start of Villa de Béjar, destined to become the most important town in [Spanish Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Texas).[^cite_note-23] On May 1, the governor transferred ownership of the Mission San Antonio de Valero (later famous as The Alamo) to Fray Antonio de Olivares.[^cite_note-Adina-5] On May 5, 1718, he commissioned the Presidio San Antonio de Béxar ("Béjar" in modern Spanish orthography) on the west side of the San Antonio River, one-fourth league from the mission.[^cite_note-tshaonline.org-22]

On February 14, 1719, the Marquis of [San Miguel de Aguayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_de_Aguayo,_Texas) proposed to the king of Spain that 400 families be transported from the [Canary Islands](5717.md), [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Spain\)), or [Havana](49719.md) to populate the province of Texas. His plan was approved, and notice was given the [Canary Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islanders) (*[isleños](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle%C3%B1os)*) to furnish 200 families; the [Council of the Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_Indies) suggested that 400 families should be sent from the Canaries to Texas by way of Havana and [Veracruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veracruz_\(city\)). By June 1730, 25 families had reached [Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuba_\(island\)), and 10 families had been sent to Veracruz before orders from Spain came to stop the re-settlement.[^cite_note-TXHand-24]

Under the leadership of [Juan Leal Goraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Leal_Goraz), the group marched overland from Veracruz to the Presidio San Antonio de Béxar, where they arrived on March 9, 1731. Due to marriages along the way, the party now included 15 families, a total of 56 persons. They joined the military community established in 1718. The immigrants formed the nucleus of the villa of San Fernando de Béxar, the first regularly organized civil government in Texas. Several older families of San Antonio trace their descent from the Canary Island colonists. María Rosa Padrón was the first baby born of Canary Islander descent in San Antonio.[^cite_note-TXHand-24]



San Antonio was part of the Spanish [Viceroyalty of New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Spain).&#x20;

During the Spanish–Mexican settlement of Southwestern lands, which took place over the following century, Juan Leal Goraz Jr. was a prominent figure. He claimed nearly 100,000 square miles (64,000,000 acres; 260,000 km<sup>2</sup>) as Spanish territory and held some control for nearly three decades; this area stretched across six present-day states. San Antonio was designated as Leal Goraz's capital. It represented Mexican expansion into the area. With his robust military forces, he led exploration and establishing Spanish colonial bases as far as [San Francisco, California](49728.md). Widespread bankruptcy forced Leal Goraz Jr.'s army back into the current boundaries of Mexico; they fell into internal conflict and turmoil with neighboring entities.

San Antonio grew to become the largest Spanish settlement in Texas; it was designated as the capital of the Spanish, later [Mexican, province of Tejas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Texas). From San Antonio, the [Camino Real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_San_Antonio_Road) (today Nacogdoches Road), was built to the small frontier town of [Nacogdoches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacogdoches). Mexico allowed [European American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Americans) settlers from the United States into the territory; they mostly occupied land in the eastern part. In 1835, when [Antonio López de Santa Anna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_L%C3%B3pez_de_Santa_Anna) unilaterally abolished the [Mexican Constitution of 1824](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1824_Constitution_of_Mexico), violence ensued in many [states of Mexico](8196151.md). which led to many short-lived independent republics.[^cite_note-25] This, in addition to Mexico's abolition of slavery, and cultural differences between the Texians and the Mexicans, led to the [Texas Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Revolution).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

In a series of battles, the [Texian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texian_Army), which was made up primarily of American citizens, succeeded in forcing Mexican soldiers out of the settlement areas east of San Antonio. Under the leadership of [Ben Milam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Milam), in the [Battle of Bexar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_B%C3%A9xar#Battle), December 1835, [Texian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texian) forces captured San Antonio from forces commanded by General [Martin Perfecto de Cos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Perfecto_de_Cos), Santa Anna's brother-in-law. In the spring of 1836, Santa Anna marched on San Antonio. A volunteer force under the command of [James C. Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_C._Neill) occupied and fortified the deserted Alamo mission.[^cite_note-Papers-28]

Upon his departure, the joint command of [William Barrett Travis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_B._Travis) and [James Bowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bowie) were left in charge of defending the old mission. The [Battle of the Alamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Alamo) took place from February 23 to March 6, 1836. The outnumbered Texian force was ultimately defeated, with all of the Alamo defenders killed. These men were seen as "martyrs" for the cause of Texas freedom and "Remember the Alamo" became a rallying cry in the Texian Army's eventual success at defeating Santa Anna's army.[^cite_note-Papers-28]

[Juan Seguín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Segu%C3%ADn), who organized the company of [Tejano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tejano) patriots, who fought for Texas independence, fought at the [Battle of Concepción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Concepci%C3%B3n), the [Siege of Bexar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Bexar), and the [Battle of San Jacinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_San_Jacinto), and served as mayor of San Antonio. He was forced out of office due to threats on his life by sectarian newcomers and political opponents in 1842 and was the last Tejano mayor for nearly 150 years.[^cite_note-29] By 1850, Tejanos no longer constituted a majority of the San Antonio population, and would not resume majority status until 1968.[^cite_note-30]



Lithograph of San Antonio in 1886

In 1845, the United States finally decided to annex Texas and include it as a state in the Union. This led to the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War). Though the U.S. ultimately won, the war was devastating to San Antonio. By its end, the population of the city had been reduced by almost two-thirds, to 800 inhabitants.[^cite_note-31] Bolstered by migrants and immigrants, by 1860 at the start of the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), San Antonio had grown to a city of 15,000 people.

In the 1850s, [Frederick Law Olmsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Law_Olmsted), the landscape architect who designed [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park) in New York City, traveled throughout the Southern and [Southwest](179571.md) U.S., and published accounts of his observations. In his 1859 book about Texas, Olmsted described San Antonio as having a "jumble of races, costumes, languages, and buildings", which gave it a quality that only [New Orleans](53842.md) could rival in what he described as "odd and antiquated foreignness."[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In 1860, the [U.S. Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1860_United_States_census) lists 8,235 inhabitants in the town, including nearly 592 [enslaved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-34] Following the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), San Antonio prospered as a center of the cattle industry. During this period, it remained a frontier city, with a mixture of cultures that was different from other U.S. cities.

German immigrants founded smaller surrounding towns such as [New Braunfels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Braunfels,_Texas), [Castroville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castroville,_Texas), [Boerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boerne,_Texas), [Comfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comfort,_Texas), [Fredericksburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredericksburg,_Texas), and [Bulverde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulverde,_Texas), all towns far out from San Antonio. However, the Germans were then drawn to San Antonio for work, and many buildings and streets still bear German names such as Wurzbach, Huebner, and Jones Maltsberger, and Wiederstein. The German impact on San Antonio was great, in the early 1900s it is estimated that at least 1/3 of San Antonio was ethnically German. Many descendants of German immigrants in San Antonio spoke [Texas German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_German) up to the fifth or sixth generations. Texas German is a dialect of German that evolved when the German language was separated from Germany. Texas German is best described as an anglicized-German dialect with a Texas twang. Many older generations in New Braunfels and Fredericksburg still speak Texas German to this day.[^cite_note-35]

In 1877, following the [Reconstruction Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_Era), the [Galveston, Harrisburg, & San Antonio Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galveston,_Harrisburg,_&_San_Antonio_Railroad) became the first [railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad) to reach San Antonio, connecting it to major markets and port cities.[^cite_note-TSHA_GHSA-36] Texas was the first state to have major cities develop by railroads rather than waterways.[^cite_note-37] In Texas, the railroads supported a markedly different pattern of development of major interior cities, such as San Antonio, [Dallas](53838.md) and [Fort Worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Worth), compared to the historical development of coastal port cities in the established eastern states.

At the beginning of the 20th century, the streets of the city's downtown were widened to accommodate street cars and modern traffic. At that time, many of the older historic buildings were demolished in the process of this modernization.[^cite_note-38]

Since the late twentieth century, San Antonio has had steady [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth). The city's population has nearly doubled in 35 years, from just over 650,000 in the 1970 census to an estimated 1.2 million in 2005, through both population growth and land annexation (the latter has considerably enlarged the physical area of the city).[^cite_note-39] In 1990, the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) reported San Antonio's population as 55.6% Hispanic or Latino, 7.0% Black or African American, and 36.2% non-Hispanic white.[^cite_note-census1-40]

The San Antonio Missions National Historical Park and The Alamo became [UNESCO](21786641.md) World Heritage sites in 2015 and the city was designated a UNESCO "City of Creativity for Gastronomy" in 2017, one of only 26 [gastronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastronomy) creative cities in the world.[^cite_note-PBS-SanAn-18]

With the increase in professional jobs, San Antonio has become a destination for many college-educated persons, most recently including African Americans in a [reverse Great Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Great_Migration) from northern and western areas.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

Over 31,000 migrants who requested asylum have been released by the Border Patrol into the city in 2019 during the [National Emergency Concerning the Southern Border of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Emergency_Concerning_the_Southern_Border_of_the_United_States).[^cite_note-43]

## Geography



Satellite picture of San Antonio by ESA Sentinel-2

San Antonio is approximately 75 miles (121 km) to the southwest of its neighboring city, [Austin](1998.md), the [state capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States), about 150 miles (240 km) north of the [Mexican border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border), about 190 miles (310 km) west of [Houston](13774.md), and about 250 miles (400 km) south of the [Dallas–Fort Worth area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas%E2%80%93Fort_Worth_metroplex). The city has a total area of 465.4 square miles (1,205.4 km<sup>2</sup>); 460.93 square miles (1,193.8 km<sup>2</sup>) of San Antonio's total area is land and 4.5 square miles (11.7 km<sup>2</sup>) of it is water.[^cite_note-44] The city's gently rolling terrain is dotted with oak trees, forested land, mesquite, and cacti. The [Texas Hill Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Hill_Country) reaches into the far northern portions of the city. San Antonio sits on the [Balcones Escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balcones_Escarpment). Its altitude is approximately 662 feet (202 m) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level).[^cite_note-45]

The city's primary source of drinking water is the [Edwards Aquifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwards_Aquifer).[^cite_note-46] Impounded in 1962 and 1969, respectively, [Victor Braunig Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Braunig_Lake) and [Calaveras Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calaveras_Lake_\(Texas\)) were among the first [reservoirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir_\(water\)) in the United States built to use recycled treated wastewater for [power plant](212141.md) cooling, reducing the amount of groundwater needed for [electrical](9550.md) generation.[^cite_note-47]

### Neighborhoods

Further information: [Neighborhoods and districts of San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_and_districts_of_San_Antonio) and [Downtown San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_San_Antonio)

#### Downtown

Downtown San Antonio, the city and metro area's urban core, encompasses many of the city's famous structures, attractions, and businesses. The central business district is generally understood to cover the northern half of the "Downtown Loop"—the area bordered by Cesar Chavez to the south. Due to the size of the city and its horizontal development, downtown accounts for less than one half of one percent of San Antonio's geographic area.[^cite_note-48] The Strip (north of Downtown) houses a concentration of clubs and bars catering to the [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) community.[^cite_note-49]

#### North Central

North Central is home to several [enclaves](4848945.md) and upscale neighborhoods including [Castle Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Hills,_Texas), Shavano Park, [Hollywood Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Park,_Texas), Elm Creek, [Inwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inwood,_San_Antonio), [Stone Oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Oak,_San_Antonio), and Rogers Ranch. The area is also the location of upper-middle-class neighborhoods (Deerfield, Churchill Estates, Hunter's Creek, Oak Meadow, and Summerfield).

#### Northwest Side

Northwest Side is the location of the main campus of the [University of Texas at San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_San_Antonio), the [University of Texas Health Science Center at San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_Health_Science_Center_at_San_Antonio), and the Northwest Campus of the [University of the Incarnate Word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Incarnate_Word), which includes the Rosenberg School of Optometry. The Medical Center District is also located in Northwest Side. Companies with headquarters in the area include [Valero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valero_Energy) and [NuStar Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NuStar_Energy).

#### South Side

The South Side area of San Antonio is characterized by its predominantly [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) neighborhoods, who on average, account for about 65 percent of the population.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Large growth came to the South Side when [Toyota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota) constructed a [manufacturing plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Motor_Manufacturing_Texas). [Palo Alto College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palo_Alto_College) and [Texas A\&M University–San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A\&M_University%E2%80%93San_Antonio) are also located in the area.

#### East Side

Further information: [History of African Americans in San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_African_Americans_in_San_Antonio)

The East Side of San Antonio is home to the [San Antonio Stock Show & Rodeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Stock_Show_&_Rodeo), the [Frost Bank Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost_Bank_Center), and the [Freeman Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeman_Coliseum). This area has the largest concentration of [Black and African American](2154.md) residents.[^cite_note-52]

#### West Side

The West Side is predominantly Hispanic/Latin American and working class, with pockets of wealth in the northwest and far west. African Americans are also located in parts of San Antonio's West Side.[^cite_note-53] The West Side has undergone gentrification as of 2019.[^cite_note-54] It includes the diverse neighborhoods of Avenida Guadalupe, Collins Garden, Las Palmas, Prospect Hill, Rainbow Hills (Marbach), San Juan Gardens, Loma Park, Loma Vista, Memorial Heights, and Westwood. It is also home to the historic [Our Lady of the Lake University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_the_Lake_University) and [St. Mary's University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary's_University,_Texas).

### Flora and fauna

Natural vegetation in the San Antonio area, where undisturbed by development, includes oak-cedar woodland, oak grassland savanna, chaparral brush, and riparian (stream) woodland. San Antonio is at the westernmost limit for both Cabbage palmetto (*[Sabal palmetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabal_palmetto)*) and [Spanish moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_moss).

The native Eastern Subterranean Termite (*[Reticulitermes flavipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reticulitermes_flavipes)*) is a common [structural pest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_pest) here.[^cite_note-Baiting-55] The Formosan Termite (*[Coptotermes formosanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptotermes_formosanus)*) is an [invasive pest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasive_pest) originally from the [Far East](11672.md).[^cite_note-TAM-Formosa-56][^cite_note-Austin-57] Researchers at [Texas A\&M University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_A\&M_University) consider it to be economically devastating.[^cite_note-TAM-Formosa-56]

San Antonio has a transitional [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfa*) that borders a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BSh*) towards the west of the city[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] featuring very hot, long, and humid summers and mild winters. The area is subject to descending northern cold fronts in the winter with cool to cold nights, and is warm and rainy in the spring and fall. San Antonio falls in [USDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USDA) [hardiness zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zones) 8b (15 °F to 20 °F) and 9a (20 °F to 25 °F).[^cite_note-61]

San Antonio receives about a dozen subfreezing nights each year, typically seeing snow, sleet, or freezing rain about once every two or three winters, but accumulation and snow itself are very rare. Winters may pass without any frozen precipitation at all, and up to a decade has passed between snowfalls. According to the [National Weather Service](286989.md), there have been 32 instances of snowfall (a trace or more) in the city in the past 122 years, about once every four years. Prior to 2021 snow was most recently seen on December 7, 2017, when 1.9 inches (4.8 cm) of snow coated the city.[^cite_note-62] On January 13, 1985, San Antonio received a record snowfall of 16 inches (41 cm).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] During the [February 13–17, 2021, North American winter storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_13%E2%80%9317,_2021,_North_American_winter_storm), San Antonio was blanketed with 4–6 inches (10–15 cm) of snow. The cold air which accompanied this storm caused [massive rolling blackouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Texas_power_crisis) throughout the city until the 18th. The [February 15–20, 2021, North American winter storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_15%E2%80%9320,_2021,_North_American_winter_storm) dropped another 1–2 inches (2.5–5 cm) on the city on the 16th.

San Antonio and [New Braunfels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Braunfels), 40 miles (64 km) to the northeast, are some of the most flood-prone regions in North America.[^cite_note-65] The [October 1998 Central Texas floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_1998_Central_Texas_floods) were among the costliest floods in United States history, resulting in \$750 million in damage and 32 deaths. In 2002, from June 30 to July 7, 35 in (890 mm) of rain fell in the San Antonio area, resulting in widespread flooding and 12 fatalities.[^cite_note-66]

Tornadoes within the city limits have been reported as recently as February 2017, although they are uncommon.[^cite_note-67] An F2 tornado lands within 50 mi (80 km) of the city on average once every five years. San Antonio has experienced two F4 tornadoes, [one in 1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_outbreak_sequence_of_April_28_%E2%80%93_May_2,_1953) and another in 1973. The 1953 tornado resulted in two deaths and 15 injuries.[^cite_note-68] Based on historical data, San Antonio is the safest major city in Texas when it comes to tornadoes.[^cite_note-69]

In San Antonio, July and August tie for the average warmest months, with an average high of 95 °F (35 °C). The highest temperature ever recorded was 111 °F (44 °C) on September 5, 2000.[^cite_note-climate-summary-70] The average coolest month is January. The lowest recorded temperature ever was 0 °F (−18 °C) on January 31, 1949.[^cite_note-climate-summary-70] May, June, and October have quite a bit of precipitation. Since recordkeeping began in 1871, the average annual precipitation has been 29.03 inches (737 mm), with a maximum of 52.28 inches (1,328 mm) and a minimum of 10.11 inches (256.8 mm) in one year.[^cite_note-71]

The [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau)'s 2020 census determined San Antonio had a population of 1,434,625 residents in 2020. In 2019, the [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey) estimated San Antonio had a racial makeup of 88.4% White, 6.6% Black and African American, 0.2% American Indian and Alaska Native, 2.8% Asian, 0.1% Native Hawaiian and other Pacific Islander, 0.2% some other race and 1.7% two or more races. Ethnically, 64.5% were Hispanic or Latin American of any race.[^cite_note-\:5-79] In 2020, its racial and ethnic makeup was 23.4% non-Hispanic white, 63.9% Hispanic or Latin American of any race, 6.5% Black and African American, 3.2% Asian, and 2.3% multiracial or some other race.

According to the [2010 U.S. census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census), 1,327,407 people resided in San Antonio city proper, an increase of 16.0% since 2000. The racial composition of the city based on the 2010 U.S. census is as follows: 72.6% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_American) ([non-Hispanic whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_Whites): 26.6%), 6.9% Black or [African American](2154.md), 0.9% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), 2.4% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans), 0.1% [Native Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiians) or [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_Americans), 3.4% [two or more races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial_Americans), and 13.7% [other races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)). In addition, 63.2% of the city's population was of [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) origin, of any race.[^cite_note-census2-78] According to the [2000 U.S. census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_census), the city proper had a population of 1,144,646,[^cite_note-80] ranking it the ninth-most populated city in the country. San Antonio has a relatively lower cost of living compared to most Texas and U.S. major cities and a strong job market.[^cite_note-81] However, due to San Antonio's low density and relatively small suburban population, the metropolitan area ranked just 30th in the United States, with a population of 1,592,383 in 2000.[^cite_note-auto-82] San Antonio has a large Hispanic population with a significant African American population.[^cite_note-auto-82]

The 2011 U.S. census estimate for the eight-county [San Antonio–New Braunfels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_San_Antonio) metropolitan area placed its population at 2,194,927.[^cite_note-83] The 2017 estimate for Greater San Antonio was 2,473,974, making it the third-most populous metro area in Texas (after the [Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas%E2%80%93Fort_Worth_metroplex) and [Greater Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Houston)) and the 24th-most populous metro area in the U.S.[^cite_note-CensBur-84]

About 405,474 households, and 280,993 families resided in San Antonio. The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) as of 2010 was 2,808.5 people per square mile (1,084.4 people/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 433,122 housing units at an average density of 1,062.7 per square mile (410.3/km<sup>2</sup>). The age of the city's population was distributed as 28.5% under the age of 18, 10.8% from 18 to 24, 30.8% from 25 to 44, 19.4% from 45 to 64, and 10.4% who are 65 years of age or older. The median age was 32 years. In San Antonio, 48% of the population were males, and 52% of the population were females. For every 100 females, there were 93.5 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 89.7 males.

At the 2019 American Community Survey, there were 512,273 households and 319,673 families.[^cite_note-85] The average household size was 2.98 and the average family size was 3.83. Of the local population,[^cite_note-86] 201,960 were married-couple households and 172,741 were female households with no spouse or partner present. An estimated 85,462 households were single-person. Roughly 218,249 residents in San Antonio were foreign-born residents. For every 100 females, San Antonio had 97.1 males.[^cite_note-\:5-79]

At the 2010 U.S. census, San Antonio's median income for a household was \$36,214, and the median income for a family was \$53,100. Males have a median income of \$30,061 versus \$24,444 for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the city is \$17,487. Median rent sits around \$1,053 per month, with one-bedrooms averaging \$918 and two-bedrooms averaging \$1,099.[^cite_note-87] About 17.3% of the population and 14.0% of families are below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line). Of the total population, 24.3% of those under the age of 18 and 13.5% of those 65 and older were living below the poverty line.[^cite_note-88] In 2019, households had a median income of \$53,571 and a mean income of \$72,587.[^cite_note-89] An estimated 16.8% of the population lived at or below the poverty line.[^cite_note-90] The city of San Antonio and its metropolis was rated the poorest in 2019.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

A [Gallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_\(company\)) study in 2015 determined 4% of the city and Greater San Antonio identified as [lesbian, gay, bisexual, or transgender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT).[^cite_note-\:6-94] In 2016, San Antonio scored a 90 out of 100 in its treatment of the LGBT community.[^cite_note-95]

### Religion



[San Fernando Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_San_Fernando_\(San_Antonio\)) is the see of the Roman Catholic Archdiocese of San Antonio.

The population of Greater San Antonio is predominantly [Christian](5211.md). Owing in part to San Antonio's [Spanish and Mexican heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Latin_America), [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism) is the largest religious group in the region.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-\:01-97] In addition, American missionary work and immigration into Texas have also resulted in a substantial [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) population.[^cite_note-98]

The Catholic population forms the largest Christian group in the city and Greater San Antonio.[^cite_note-\:01-97] San Antonian Catholics are served primarily by the [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_San_Antonio). The [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church)'s Roman Catholic Archdiocese of San Antonio was established on August 27, 1784, under the then [Diocese of Galveston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Galveston).[^cite_note-99] It was elevated to archdiocese status in 1926.

According to [Sperling's BestPlaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sperling's_BestPlaces) in 2020, the second largest Christian group were [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists). The largest Baptist Christian denominations within San Antonio and its metro area were the [Baptist General Convention of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist_General_Convention_of_Texas),[^cite_note-100] the [Southern Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Baptist_Convention),[^cite_note-101] and the [Cooperative Baptist Fellowship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_Baptist_Fellowship).[^cite_note-102] [Methodists](20119.md) formed the second largest Protestant group and the third largest Christian group for the area. The [United Methodist Church](32304.md) was the most prominent Methodist denomination.[^cite_note-103] From 2017 to 2020, [Pentecostalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism) outgrew [Lutheranism](23371382.md) and tied with the [Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) as the fourth largest Christian group. A major predominantly [African American-led church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Church_\(African_American\)) is [Denver Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Heights_COGIC), affiliated with the [Church of God in Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_God_in_Christ).

After Lutherans, [Presbyterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism) were the next largest Christian denomination, followed by [Episcopalian or Anglicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism), and Christians of other traditions including the Eastern Orthodox and [Oriental Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches). The [Eastern Orthodox](10186.md) communities are divided between the [Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Archdiocese_of_America),[^cite_note-104] the [Antiochian Orthodox Archdiocese of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiochian_Orthodox_Christian_Archdiocese_of_North_America),[^cite_note-105] the [Orthodox Church in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America),[^cite_note-106] and the [Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church_Outside_Russia).[^cite_note-107] Episcopalians and Anglicans primarily are served by the [Episcopal Church in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_\(United_States\)) and the [Anglican Church in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Church_in_North_America).[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] Churches affiliated with the Episcopal Church form the [Episcopal Diocese of West Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Diocese_of_West_Texas). Overall, the Protestant demographic was predominantly [Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) as of 2020.

[Islam](6037917.md) is the second largest religion in the Greater San Antonio area.[^cite_note-\:01-97] [Eastern religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_religions) such as [Buddhism](3267529.md), [Sikhism](27964.md), and [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) also have a significant presence in San Antonio.[^cite_note-110] An estimated 0.3% of the area's population identified with [Judaism](15624.md) according to Sperling's BestPlaces and at least 10,000 Jews live in the city.[^cite_note-111] The San Antonio Jewish community began not long after the independence of the [Republic of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Texas).[^cite_note-112] The oldest synagogue in [South Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Texas) ([Temple Beth-El](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Beth-El_\(San_Antonio\))) is located in the city limits and located near [San Antonio College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_College).[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]

### Crime



San Antonio police officers

Crime in San Antonio began to rise in the early 1980s, similar to many urban areas in the US. In 1983 San Antonio had the tenth highest homicide rate in Texas with 18.5 homicides per 100,000 residents.[^cite_note-115] The number of juveniles arrested in San Antonio for violent crimes tripled between 1987 and 1994, according to the Texas Law Enforcement Management and Administration Statistics Program. The number of youths arrested for unlawfully carrying firearms doubled over the same period.

In 1993, San Antonio earned the nickname the "Drive-By City" after the [San Antonio Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Police_Department) recorded over 1,200 drive-by shootings, or an average of about 3.5 per day, which overshadowed the number in other Texas cities. A majority of the violence occurred on the east and west sides of the city, especially in areas with high poverty rates. Gang violence led to the deaths of their members and sometimes innocent bystanders, and housing projects such as the [Alazán-Apache Courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaz%C3%A1n-Apache_Courts) served as hubs for various groups, which sometimes included rival gangs.[^cite_note-116] By the end of 1993 the city hit a peak in homicides with 230 killings, the highest since 1991 when 211 were killed.[^cite_note-117]

In 2016, the number of murders hit 151, the highest toll in 20 years. A majority of the San Antonio homicide victims were Hispanic and African American men between ages 18 and 29. According to a study, 40% of the killings were either drug-related or domestic incidents.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] In 2020, San Antonio ranked the fourth U.S. city with the biggest increase in homicides.[^cite_note-120] From January to June 2020, there were 71 homicides according to the San Antonio Police Department. In 2019, there were 53 reported homicides in contrast. A total of 105 homicides occurred in 2019 in the city. Redevelopment, harmonious Black and Hispanic relations, improved police patrol, community-based peace programs, gang intervention work, and youth development organizations lowered the murder and crime rates in San Antonio to levels that had not been seen since the 1940s and 1950s.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] According to *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)*, homicide rates were relatively low compared to previous decades.[^cite_note-123]

San Antonio has a diversified economy with a gross domestic product (GDP) of approximately \$121 billion in 2018.[^cite_note-124] San Antonio's economy focuses primarily on [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military), [health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care), government–[civil service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_service), professional and business services,[^cite_note-125] [oil and gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_and_gas), and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism). Since the beginning of the 21st century, the city has become a significant location for American-based [call centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_centers) and has added a sizable [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing) sector centered around automobiles.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127] The city also has a growing technology sector.[^cite_note-128] Located about 10 miles (16 km) northwest of [Downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_San_Antonio) is the [South Texas Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Texas_Medical_Center), a conglomerate of various hospitals, clinics, and research (see [Southwest Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Research_Institute) and [Texas Biomedical Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Biomedical_Research_Institute)) and higher educational institutions.[^cite_note-129]

Over twenty million tourists visit the city and its attractions every year, contributing substantially to its economy, primarily due to The Alamo and the [River Walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_River_Walk).[^cite_note-130] The [Henry B. Gonzalez Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_B._Gonzalez_Convention_Center) alone hosts over 300 events annually with more than 750,000 convention delegates from around the world. Tourism provided over 130,000 jobs, and it had an economic impact of \$13.6 billion in the local economy according to information gathered in 2017.[^cite_note-131] The city of San Antonio received \$195 million in the same year from the hospitality industry, with revenues from hotel occupancy tax, sales taxes and others.

The city is home to one of the largest concentrations of military bases in the U.S., and has been nicknamed "Military City, USA". San Antonio and Mexico share strong economic ties and engage in important exchanges to the benefit of their business communities. To better strengthen these business and cultural ties, the City of San Antonio opened Casa San Antonio to act as the city's trade and cultural office in Mexico.[^cite_note-157]

The [Formosan termite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formosan_termite) (*Coptotermes formosanus*) causes economic devastation in the region due to the [structural damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_pest) it causes.[^cite_note-TAM-Formosa-56][^cite_note-Austin-57] It is an [invasive pest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasive_pest) originally from the [Far East](11672.md).[^cite_note-TAM-Formosa-56][^cite_note-Austin-57] First found in the state in 1957, it has since spread into the greater San Antonio area, creating a problem for the region's pecan orchards.[^cite_note-formosa-158]

## Arts and culture

Further information: [Culture of San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_San_Antonio)

San Antonio enjoys a vibrant culture which it is known for being a popular tourist destination.[^cite_note-159] The [Alamo Mission in San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamo_Mission_in_San_Antonio) ("The Alamo"), located in Downtown, is Texas' top tourist attraction. Because of the mission, San Antonio is often called "Alamo City".[^cite_note-160]

The [River Walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_River_Walk), which meanders through the Downtown area, is the city's second-most-visited attraction, giving it the additional nickname of "River City". Extended by an additional 13 miles (21 km) between 2009 and 2013, the landscaped walking and bike path line the San Antonio River from the "Museum Reach" beginning in Brackenridge Park through downtown, "Downtown Reach", past the Blue Star's "Eagleland" to the "Mission Reach" ending near Loop 410 South past [Mission Espada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Espada).[^cite_note-161]

Lined with numerous shops, bars, and restaurants, as well as the [Arneson River Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arneson_River_Theater), this attraction is transformed into a festival of lights during the Christmas and New Year holiday period (except for the Mission Reach), and is suffused with the local sounds of [folklorico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baile_Folklorico) and [flamenco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flamenco) music during the summer, particularly during celebrations such as the [Fiesta Noche del Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiesta_Noche_del_Rio).

The Downtown area also features [San Fernando Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_San_Fernando_\(San_Antonio\)), [The Majestic Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Majestic_Theatre,_San_Antonio), [Hemisfair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HemisFair_'68) (home of the [Tower of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_the_Americas), and UTSA's [Institute of Texan Cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Texan_Cultures)), [La Villita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Villita), [Market Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_Square_\(San_Antonio\)), the [Spanish Governor's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Governor's_Palace), and the historic [Menger Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menger_Hotel). The [Fairmount Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairmount_Hotel_\(San_Antonio,_Texas\)), built in 1906 and San Antonio's second oldest hotel, is in the *[Guinness World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records)* as one of the heaviest buildings ever moved intact.[^cite_note-162] It was placed in its new location, three blocks south of the Alamo, over four days in 1985, and cost \$650,000 to move.

[SeaWorld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaWorld_San_Antonio), 16 miles (26 km) west of Downtown in the city's Westover Hills district, is one of the largest marine life parks in the world. The [San Antonio Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Zoo) is in Brackenridge Park. The British company [Merlin Entertainments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlin_Entertainments) developed an aquarium attraction and indoor counterpart to SeaWorld. It is inside the Shops at Rivercenter in Downtown San Antonio and is one of Merlin's [Sea Life Aquariums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Life_Centres). The [San Antonio Aquarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Aquarium) features a number of interactive exhibits.



Aerial view of [Six Flags Fiesta Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Flags_Fiesta_Texas)

San Antonio is also home to several commercial amusement parks, including Six Flags Fiesta Texas and [Morgan's Wonderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan's_Wonderland), a theme park for children with special needs.[^cite_note-163] Kiddie Park, featuring old-fashioned amusement rides for children, was established in 1925 and is the oldest children's amusement park in the U.S.[^cite_note-164]



The McNay Art Museum

San Antonio is home to the first museum of [modern art](163543.md) in [Texas](29810.md), the [McNay Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McNay_Art_Museum).[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166] Other art institutions and museums include [ArtPace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArtPace), [Blue Star Contemporary Art Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Star_Contemporary_Art_Center), the Briscoe Western Art Museum, Ruby City, [Buckhorn Saloon & Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckhorn_Saloon_&_Museum_\(San_Antonio\)) (where visitors can experience something of cowboy culture year round), [San Antonio Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Museum_of_Art), formerly the Lonestar Brewery, Say Sí (mentoring San Antonio artistic youth), the Southwest School of Art, Texas Rangers Museum, [Texas Transportation Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Transportation_Museum), the [Witte Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witte_Museum) and the DoSeum. An outdoor display at [North Star Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Star_Mall) features 40-foot-tall (12-meter) cowboy boots.

The city's five missions, the four in the [San Antonio Missions National Historical Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Missions_National_Historical_Park) plus The Alamo, were named UNESCO World Heritage sites on July 5, 2015.[^cite_note-PBS-SanAn-18] The San Antonio Missions became the 23rd U.S. site on the World Heritage list, which includes the Grand Canyon and the Statue of Liberty; it is the first site in Texas. The new Mission Reach of the River Walk was completed in 2013, and created over 15 miles (24 km) of biking, hiking, and paddling trails that connect the Missions to Downtown and the Broadway Corridor.[^cite_note-167]

San Antonio's Howard W. Peak Greenway Trail System is a big draw. It covers more than 82 miles (132 km) and has more than 50 trailheads.[^cite_note-168]

Other places of interest include the [San Antonio Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Botanical_Garden), [Brackenridge Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brackenridge_Park), the [Japanese Tea Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Japanese_Tea_Gardens), and the [Woodlawn Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Theatre).

In 2015 work was authorized to begin on the restoration of the former [Hot Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Wells_\(San_Antonio,_Texas\)) hotel, [spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa), and [bathhouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_bathing) on the San Antonio River on the city's south side.[^cite_note-169]

### Annual events

Yearly events, such as [Fiesta San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiesta_San_Antonio) (the city's signature event), Luminaria (a contemporary arts festival) and Fiesta Noche del Rio, add a variety of entertainment options to the area, as well as strengthening the economy. Fiesta San Antonio alone carries an annual economic impact of \$340 million.[^cite_note-170] The city also plays host to the [San Antonio Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Film_Festival) every summer in August.[^cite_note-171]

### Professional sports

The city's only top-level professional sports team is the [San Antonio Spurs](72889.md) of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md). Previously the Spurs played at the [Alamodome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamodome) (which was speculatively built in an attempt to lure a professional [football](18951490.md) team to the region), and before that the [HemisFair Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HemisFair_Arena). They moved into the SBC Center in 2002 (since renamed the [Frost Bank Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frost_Bank_Center)), built with public funds.

The San Antonio metropolitan area's smaller population has so far contributed to its lack of an [NFL](21211.md), [MLB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB), [NHL](21809.md), or [MLS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS) team. City officials are said to be attempting to lure the [National Football League](21211.md) permanently to San Antonio. Former NFL Commissioner [Paul Tagliabue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tagliabue) stated San Antonio was successful in temporarily hosting the [New Orleans Saints](21720.md) following [Hurricane Katrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina), and that the city would be on the short list for any future NFL expansions.[^cite_note-173] The city has also hosted the [Dallas Cowboys](8121.md) and [Houston Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Oilers) preseason camps in the past, and the Cowboys practiced in San Antonio through 2011.[^cite_note-174] Cowboys owner [Jerry Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Jones) acknowledged his support for the city to become home to an NFL franchise.[^cite_note-175]

The Texas Red Wolves of the [American Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Basketball_Association_\(2000%E2%80%93present\)) (ABA) play at Champion Sportsplex.

San Antonio is home to the [Double-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-A_\(baseball\)) [San Antonio Missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Missions), who play at [Nelson Wolff Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_W._Wolff_Municipal_Stadium) and are the [Minor League Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_League_Baseball) affiliate of the [San Diego Padres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Padres).[^cite_note-176]

## Government

Further information: [List of mayors of San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_San_Antonio)



City Hall, San Antonio, Texas (postcard, c. 1906)



San Antonio City Hall

The city of San Antonio is operated under the [council-manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council%E2%80%93manager_government) system of government. The city is divided into 10 council districts designed to be of equal population. Each district elects one person to the city council, with the mayor elected on a citywide basis. All members of the San Antonio City Council, including the mayor, are elected to four-year terms and are limited to two terms (except for those who were in office in November 2008 and are limited to a total of two terms). [Houston](13774.md) and [Laredo](136773.md) have similar term limits to San Antonio. All positions are elected on nonpartisan ballots, as required by Texas law. Council members are paid \$45,722 and the mayor earns \$61,725 a year. The current mayor is [Gina Ortiz Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gina_Ortiz_Jones), who was elected in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_San_Antonio_mayoral_election) with 54.3% of the vote.[^cite_note-185]

The council hires a [city manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_manager) to handle day-to-day operations. The council effectively functions as the city's legislative body with the city manager acting as its chief executive, responsible for the management of day-to-day operations and execution of council legislation. The current city manager is [Erik Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Walsh).

The city operates its own electric and gas utility, [CPS Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPS_Energy). The San Antonio Police Department (SAPD) is the city's municipal body of law enforcement. The San Antonio Fire Department (SAFD) provides the city with fire protection and EMS service.

### Growth policy



Broadway Street in [Alamo Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamo_Heights,_Texas), an enclaved city

Unlike most large cities in the U.S., San Antonio is not completely surrounded by independent suburban cities, and under Texas state law it exercises [extraterritorial jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterritorial_jurisdiction) (ETJ) over much of the surrounding [unincorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unincorporated_area) land,[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192] including planning major thoroughfares and enforcing rules for platting and subdivision. It pursues an aggressive annexation policy and opposes the creation of other municipalities within its ETJ.[^cite_note-193] Nearly three-fourths of its land area has been annexed since 1960.[^cite_note-194]

In the 2000s the city annexed several long narrow corridors along major thoroughfares in outlying areas to facilitate eventual annexation of growth developing along the routes. The city planned to annex nearly 40 additional square miles by 2009.[^cite_note-195]

In May 2010, the City of San Antonio agreed to release thousands of acres of land in its extraterritorial jurisdiction along Interstate 10 to [Schertz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schertz). The agreement releases a total of 3,486 acres (14.11 km<sup>2</sup>) of San Antonio's ETJ lands north of I-10 to Schertz. The ETJ lands are in an area bordered by FM 1518 to the west, Lower Seguin Road to the north, Cibolo Creek to the east and I-10 to the south.[^cite_note-196]

Involuntary annexation is a controversial issue in those parts of unincorporated Bexar County affected by it. Residents attracted to the outlying areas by lower taxes and affordable real estate values often see annexation as a mechanism to increase [property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) rates (which are primarily driven by school district taxes, not city taxes) without a corresponding improvement in services such as police and fire protection, while the city regards its annexation policy as essential to its overall prosperity.[^cite_note-197]

Since the city has annexed areas over time, San Antonio surrounds several independent [enclave cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_suburb), including [Alamo Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamo_Heights,_Texas), [Balcones Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balcones_Heights), [Castle Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Hills,_Texas), [Hill Country Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_Country_Village,_Texas), [Hollywood Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Park,_Texas), [Kirby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirby,_Texas), [Leon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Valley,_Texas), [Olmos Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmos_Park,_Texas), [Shavano Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shavano_Park,_Texas), and [Terrell Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrell_Hills,_Texas).

### Military

San Antonio is home to U.S. Air Force Basic Military Training (AFBMT).[^cite_note-207] The Air Force only has one location for enlisted basic training: the 737th Training Group, at [Lackland Air Force Base](405875.md). ## Media

Main article: [Media in San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_in_San_Antonio)

### Print

San Antonio has one major newspaper, the *[San Antonio Express-News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Express-News)*, which has served the area since 1865. The *Express-News* circulates as the largest newspaper service in [South Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Texas). The [Hearst Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearst_Corporation), which owned a second newspaper, the *San Antonio Light*, purchased the *Express-News* from [News Corp.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Corporation_\(1980%E2%80%932013\)) in 1992 and shut down the *Light* after failing to find a buyer.

Hearst, using the *Express-News* brand, also produces *Conexión*, a weekly magazine written by an entirely Hispanic and Latin American staff with a Latino spin on weekly events. The [San Antonio *Current*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Current) is the free "alternative" paper published weekly with local political issues, art and music news, restaurant listings and reviews, and listings of events and nightlife around town. The *San Antonio Business Journal* covers general business news. *La Prensa*, a bilingual publication, also has a long history in San Antonio. They closed their doors in June 2018.[^cite_note-208] *Edible San Antonio* is San Antonio's bimonthly food magazine, it reports on the city's food culture and it's chiefs, farmers and other figures related to food.[^cite_note-209] The *[San Antonio Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_Observer)* is the only weekly [African American newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American_newspapers) in San Antonio since 1995 and the largest in South Texas.[^cite_note-210]

The *San Antonio Report*, renamed in 2020 from the Rivard Report, is the city's only digital-only news publication.[^cite_note-211] Founded in 2011 by former Express-News editor Robert Rivard, it began as a blog but has since become a non-profit news source covering civic issues.[^cite_note-212]

### Radio

About 50 radio stations can be heard in the San Antonio area; 30 of them are in the city proper. San Antonio is home to [iHeartMedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHeartMedia), the largest operator of radio stations in the U.S. Its flagship, [WOAI AM-1200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WOAI_\(AM\)), is known for its local news operation, considered among the best in the country. It is a 50,000-watt clear channel station that reaches most of North America at night. The first radio station to broadcast in South Texas was [KTSA AM-550](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTSA) in 1922.

The region's [National Public Radio](29697232.md) member is [Texas Public Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Public_Radio),[^cite_note-213] a group of three stations; KSTX 89.1 FM is NPR news/talk, KPAC 88.3 is a 24-hour classical music station, and KTXI 90.1 is a mix of NPR news/talk and classical music broadcast for the West Central Texas Hill Country. KSTX also broadcasts "Riverwalk Jazz", featuring Jim Cullum Jazz Band at The Landing, a fixture on the River Walk since 1963.

KRTU 91.7 is a non-commercial radio station based at Trinity University.[^cite_note-214] Unlike most other college radio stations in the U.S., the station plays jazz 17 hours a day and college rock/indie rock at night. College alternative station KSYM, 90.1 FM, is owned by the Alamo Community College District and operated by San Antonio College students; like KRTU, it plays the Third Coast music network during the day and alternative music at night.

Most Latin American stations in the area play [regional Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_styles_of_Mexican_music), [Tejano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tejano_music) or contemporary pop. On January 12, 2006, [Univision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univision)-owned [KMYO-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KMYO) "La Kalle 95.1" changed its format from Hispanic-Rhythmic Contemporary Hits to Spanish Oldies, then named "Recuerdo 95.1". On November 10, 2006, [Univision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univision) flipped KLTO Tejano 97.7's format to [reggaeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggaeton) in an attempt to reintroduce the format to San Antonio. Then 97.7 was flipped again to feature a rock format. The station no longer broadcasts anything in English. While still owned by Univision, it broadcast music by artists such as [Linkin Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linkin_Park), before being sold to the [Educational Media Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_Media_Foundation) and flipping once again to [Air1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air1).

95.1 was then flipped back to the "La Kalle" format again after being flipped to feature a "95X" format. KLTO was acquired and is operated as a simulcast of [KVBH-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVBH) Vibe 107.5. San Antonio radio is diversified, due to an influx of non-[Tejano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tejano) [Latinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latinos), mostly from the [East Coast](89126.md), who serve in the city's various military bases, as well as immigrants from Mexico. Therefore, just like in the rest of the country, radio station conglomerates have been changing formats in San Antonio to reflect shifting [demographics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics).

### Television



WOAI-TV is San Antonio's NBC affiliate.

Despite the relatively large size of both the city proper and the metropolitan area, San Antonio has always been a medium-sized market. It presently ranks 33rd in the United States, according to [marketing research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketing_research) firm [ACNielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACNielsen).[^cite_note-215] This is mainly because the nearby suburban and rural areas are not much larger than the city itself.

San Antonio-based television stations include [KCWX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KCWX) channel 2 ([MyNetworkTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MyNetworkTV)), [WOAI-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WOAI-TV) channel 4 ([NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC), with [CW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_CW) on DT2), [KENS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KENS) channel 5 ([CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS)), [KLRN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLRN) channel 9 ([PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS)), [KSAT-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSAT-TV) channel 12 ([ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company)), [KNIC-DT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KNIC-DT) channel 17 ([UniMás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UniM%C3%A1s)), [KABB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KABB) channel 29 ([FOX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company)), [KMYS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KMYS) channel 35 ([Roar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roar_\(TV_network\))), [KWEX-DT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KWEX-DT) channel 41 ([Univision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univision)) and [KVDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVDA) channel 60 ([Telemundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemundo)). ## Transportation

### Air



Ticket counters at [San Antonio International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_International_Airport)

[San Antonio International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_International_Airport) (SAT) is located in Uptown San Antonio, about eight miles (13 km) north of Downtown. San Antonio International Airport is the [6th busiest airport based on passenger boardings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_the_United_States) in Texas and 42nd in the United States as of 2025.[^cite_note-217] It has two terminals and is served by 20 airlines, 15 passenger and 5 cargo ones, serving many destinations throughout the United States and Mexico. [Stinson Municipal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stinson_Municipal_Airport) is a reliever airport located six miles (9.7 km) south of Downtown San Antonio. The airport has two runways serving primarily [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation) and is also home to the [Texas Air Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Air_Museum).

### Mass transit

Further information: [VIA Metropolitan Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VIA_Metropolitan_Transit)



A VIA bus, stopped at a Downtown San Antonio intersection

A bus system is provided by the city's metropolitan transit authority, [VIA Metropolitan Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VIA_Metropolitan_Transit). VIA began operating a [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) line known as [VIA Primo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VIA_Primo)[^cite_note-218] in December 2012, which connects [Downtown San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_San_Antonio) to the [South Texas Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Texas_Medical_Center), the main campus of the [University of Texas at San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_San_Antonio), and the independent enclave city of [Leon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Valley,_Texas). Additionally, VIA also offers VIAtrans [Paratransit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paratransit) Service,[^cite_note-219] a wheelchair accessible ride-share service for people with disabilities.

In August 2010, VIA Metropolitan Transit unveiled buses that are powered by diesel-electric hybrid technology.[^cite_note-viainfo.net-220] The 30 hybrid buses were put into service on VIA's express routes to serve daily commuters across the city. This set of buses follows the introduction of new vehicles powered by compressed natural gas, which were unveiled in May 2010. In the fall of 2010, VIA took delivery of three new buses that are powered by electricity from on-board batteries. These buses serve the Downtown core area, and are the first revenue vehicles VIA operates which have zero emissions.[^cite_note-viainfo.net-220]

VIA offers 90 regular bus routes and two Downtown streetcar routes.[^cite_note-221] This includes express service from Downtown to park and ride locations in the south, west, northwest, north central and northeast sides of the city, with service to major locations such as [UTSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_San_Antonio), Six Flags Fiesta Texas and [SeaWorld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaWorld). VIA also offers a special service to city events including [Spurs](72889.md) games and city parades from its park and ride locations.

### Rail

San Antonio is served by two [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) routes: the daily Chicago to San Antonio *[Texas Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Eagle)* and the thrice-weekly New Orleans to Los Angeles *[Sunset Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset_Limited).*[^cite_note-222] On the days that the *Sunset Limited* operates, a section of the *Texas Eagle* continues west with it, offering Chicago to Los Angeles through service. The [old Sunset Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_station_\(Texas\)) is now an entertainment venue owned by VIA and neighbored by the current station and the [Alamodome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamodome).[^cite_note-223][^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225]

San Antonio became the largest American city without an intra-city rail system when [Phoenix](49121.md), the former largest city without such a system, [procured one in 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_Metro_Rail). A proposed passenger rail line, Lone Star Rail, would have linked San Antonio to Austin, but was cancelled in 2016 after 19 years of planning.[^cite_note-226]

South Hausman is a suburban road that does not connect with Hausman (despite its name).

A large portion of trade between Mexico and the United States passes through the San Antonio area's interstate highway system.[^cite_note-231]

Of the five largest cities in Texas, San Antonio (within city limits) is the largest city in the state without toll roads. (Houston, Dallas, Austin, and Fort Worth have toll roads.)

San Antonio enjoys less traffic congestion than other large Texas cities. In a 2022 study by TomTom, San Antonio is only the 41st-most congested city in the U.S. and the fifth-most congested city in Texas. By comparison, Houston, McAllen, Austin, and Dallas-Fort Worth rank higher than San Antonio for traffic congestion.[^cite_note-232]

### Bicycle paths

San Antonio has about 136 miles (219 km) of bike lanes, routes or off-road paths.[^cite_note-233] Off-road trails travel along the [San Antonio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_River), linear greenways, or city parks. Although largely disconnected, the progress to create a bicycle-friendly environment was recognized when San Antonio was designated a bronze-level "Bicycle Friendly Community" in 2015 by the League of American Bicyclists.[^cite_note-234]

### Bicycle sharing

A bike sharing service was approved by the city council on June 17, 2010.[^cite_note-235] The initial program consisted of 140 bikes at 14 locations supported by a "central hub". It is expected to serve both residents and visitors. San Antonio Bike Share, a non-profit, was formed to oversee the operation, locally operated and maintained by Bike World. B-Cycle, the same system used in Denver, supplies the bike share system. It began operation in March 2011.[^cite_note-236]

### Walkability

As of 2021, [Walk Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walk_Score) ranks San Antonio as the least [walkable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkability) American city a population greater than one million and calls it car-dependent. This is due in large part to its rapid growth after [World War II](32927.md), the prevalence of [single-family zoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-family_zoning) and thousands of miles of missing or broken [sidewalks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidewalk).[^cite_note-237]

The [Howard W. Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_W._Peak) Greenway is an 84-mile system (135 km) of hiking and biking trails that roughly forms a ring around the city. It is accessible by more than 65 trailheads and connects to dozens of parks. With construction beginning in 2007, the trails consist of the [Leon Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Creek) Greenway, the [Salado Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salado_Creek) Greenway, the Westside Creeks, and the [Medina River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medina_River) Greenway.[^cite_note-238][^cite_note-239]
