A **coast guard** or **coastguard** is a [maritime security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Security_Regimes) organization of a particular country. The term embraces wide range of responsibilities in different countries, from being a heavily armed [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) force with [customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs) and security duties to being a volunteer organization tasked with [search and rescue](172599.md) without [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement) authority. In most countries, a typical coast guard's functions are distinct from those of the [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) (a [military branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch)) and the [water police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_police) (a [law enforcement agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_agency)), while in certain countries they have similarities to both.

## History

### United Kingdom



The HM Coastguard station at Girvan, Ayrshire, Scotland

The predecessor of Britain's modern [HM Coastguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Coastguard) was established in 1809 as the [Waterguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterguard), a department of the [HM Customs and Excise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Customs_and_Excise) authority, which was originally devoted to the prevention of [smuggling](28645.md). At the time, due to high UK taxation on liquors such as brandy, as well as tobacco and other luxuries, smuggling of such cargos from places such as France, Belgium, and Holland was an attractive proposition for criminals. The barrels of brandy and other [contraband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraband) were landed from the ships on England's beaches at night in small boats and later sold for profit, as later depicted in the [Doctor Syn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_Syn) series of books by [Russell Thorndike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Thorndike). The Coastguard was also responsible for giving assistance to [shipwrecks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipwrecks).

Each Waterguard station was issued with a [Manby mortar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manby_mortar), which had been invented by Captain [George William Manby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_William_Manby) in 1808. The mortar fired a shot with a line attached from the shore to the wrecked ship and was used for many years. This was the origin of the Coastguard's life saving role. In 1821 a committee of inquiry recommended that responsibility for the Preventative Waterguard be transferred to the Board of Customs. The [Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury) agreed and (in a memorandum dated 16 January 1845) directed that the preventative services, which consisted of the [Preventative Water Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterguard), cruisers, and [riding officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riding_officer) should be placed under the authority of the Board of Customs and in future should be named the "Coastguard". In 1845 the Coastguard was subordinated to the [Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty).[^cite_note-1]



The [Cape Hatteras Life-Saving Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Lookout_Coast_Guard_Station), [North Carolina](21650.md), in use from the mid-19th century

In 1829 the first UK Coastguard instructions were published and dealt with discipline and directions for carrying out preventative duties. They also stipulated that, when a ship was wrecked, the Coastguard was responsible for taking all possible action to save lives, to take charge of the vessel and to protect property.[^cite_note-2]

### United States

In the United States, the [United States Coast Guard](32223.md) was created in 1915 by the merger of two other federal agencies. The first, the [United States Revenue Cutter Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Revenue_Cutter_Service), founded in 1790 and known until 1894 as the United States Revenue-Marine, was a maritime customs enforcement agency that also assumed a supporting role to the [United States Navy](20518076.md) in wartime. The second, the [United States Life-Saving Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Life-Saving_Service), was formed in 1848 and consisted of life saving crews stationed at points along the [United States East Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_East_Coast). The Coast Guard later absorbed the [United States Lighthouse Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Lighthouse_Service) in 1939 and the functions of the [Bureau of Navigation and Steamboat Inspection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Navigation_and_Steamboat_Inspection) via a two-step process in 1942 and 1946.

## Role



U.S. Coast Guardsmen and Royal Canadian Navy sailors, on the left, working together in 2012



Coast Guard Station in [Mariehamn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariehamn), [Åland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%85land)&#x20;

Among the responsibilities that may be entrusted to a coast guard service are:

- [search and rescue](172599.md),

- enforcement of [maritime law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_law),

- safety of vessels,

- maintenance of [sea marks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_mark), and

- [border control](247927.md).

During wartime, some national coast guard organisations might have a role as a naval reserve force with responsibilities in harbor defenses, [port security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_security), naval [counter-intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterintelligence) and coastal patrols.

The coast guard may, varying by jurisdiction, be a [branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch) of a country's [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military), a law enforcement agency, or a search and rescue body. For example, the [United States Coast Guard](32223.md) is a specialized military branch with law enforcement authority, whereas the [United Kingdom](31717.md)'s [His Majesty's Coastguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Coastguard) (HMCG) is a civilian organisation whose primary role is search and rescue.[^cite_note-3] Most coast guards operate [ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship) and [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft) including [helicopters](8286923.md) and [seaplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplane) that are either owned or leased by the agency in order to fulfil their respective roles.

Some coast guards, such as the [Irish Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Coast_Guard), have only a very limited law enforcement role, usually in enforcing maritime safety law, such as by inspecting ships docked in their jurisdiction.[^cite_note-4] In cases where the coast guard is primarily concerned with *co-ordinating* rather than executing rescue operations, [lifeboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifeboat_\(rescue\)) are often provided by civilian voluntary organisations, such as the [Royal National Lifeboat Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_National_Lifeboat_Institution) in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and Ireland, whilst aircraft may be provided by the countries' [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military), such as the search and rescue [Sea Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westland_Sea_King) formerly operated by the [Royal Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Search_and_Rescue_Force) and [Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Royal_Navy_Helicopter_Search_and_Rescue), in addition to any of the HMCG's own helicopters.

## Racing stripes

Beginning in 1964 with the United States Coast Guard, many coast guards around the world have adopted high visibility color schemes to differentiate their coast guard vessels from the vessels of their respective navies. A frequent element is a high contrast "racing stripe" on the outer hull. While no international agreement exists to adopt it as a uniform marking, the 2009/2010 edition of *Jane's Fighting Ships* showed 61 nations had adopted some form of this stripe pattern for their coastal patrol and rescue vessels.

## List of coast guards by country

The following lists a selected number of coast guards around the world, illustrating the varied roles they play in the respective governments and the countries they operate in:

### Argentina

The [Argentine Naval Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Naval_Prefecture), in Spanish *Prefectura Naval Argentina* or PNA, is a service of the [Argentine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Republic)'s Ministry of National Security, charged with protecting the country's rivers, lakes and maritime territory. It therefore fulfills the functions of other countries' coast guards, and furthermore acts as a [gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie) force policing navigable rivers and lakes. They belonged to the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Argentina\)) until the 1980s, and the corps' highest official was a Navy rear-admiral. They have since been transferred to the Ministry of Interior and, more recently, to the newly created [Ministry of National Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Security_\(Argentina\)). However, in the case of armed conflict, they can be put under the Navy's command.

### Australia

Main article: [Coast guards in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_guards_in_Australia)

Responsibilities for traditional coast guard duties in Australia are distributed across various federal, state and community volunteer agencies.

#### Federal

- The **[Maritime Border Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Border_Command_\(Australia\))** is the [de facto](43594.md) coast guard of Australia. The Maritime Border Command is a joint unit of the [Australian Defence Force](67538.md) (the [Royal Australian Navy](26327.md) [Patrol Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Patrol_Boat_Group) and the [Royal Australian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Air_Force) [Surveillance and Response Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance_and_Response_Group_RAAF)) and the [Australian Border Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Border_Force) ([Marine Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Unit_\(Australian_Border_Force\)) and [Coastwatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastwatch) aircraft). It is responsible for border protection in the exclusive economic zone of Australia and its 19,650 kilometres of coastline and issues such as illegal fishing and exploitation of natural resources, maritime terrorism and piracy, biosecurity threats, and [marine pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_pollution).

- The **[Australian Maritime Safety Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Maritime_Safety_Authority)** is responsible for maritime safety and seaworthiness of Australian and foreign vessels in Australian waters including compulsory pilotage, aids to navigation, the Australian Rescue Coordination Centre and coordination of search and rescue operations, and management of Australia's international maritime obligations.

- The **[Australian Fisheries Management Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Fisheries_Management_Authority)** is responsible for the management and sustainable use of fisheries resources and for combating illegal fishing activities in the Australian Fishing Zone.

- The **[Australian Federal Police](341221.md)** supports the [Maritime Border Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Border_Command_\(Australia\)) and particularly the [Australian Border Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Border_Force) with criminal investigations, law enforcement and national security matters.

- The **[Office of Transport Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Home_Affairs_\(Australia\)#Office_of_Transport_Security)** has various responsibilities for maritime security.

#### State

Each State Government also has agencies with coast guard responsibilities. For example, in Queensland, [Maritime Safety Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Safety_Queensland) is responsible for maritime safety and the [Queensland Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_Police_Service) has a [water police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_police) unit for law enforcement along the coastline, in waterways, and for Queensland islands.

#### Community

In addition, there are several private volunteer coast guard organizations, the two largest organizations being the [Royal Volunteer Coastal Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Volunteer_Coastal_Patrol) (established in 1937) and the [Australian Volunteer Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Volunteer_Coast_Guard) (established in 1961). These volunteer organizations have no law enforcement powers, and are essentially auxiliary Search and Rescue services. In NSW these two organisations have joined to become Marine Rescue New South Wales (MRNSW) in 2009.

### Bangladesh

Main article: [Bangladesh Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Coast_Guard)

The **Bangladesh Coast Guard** ([Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language) transliteration: বাংলাদেশ কোস্ট গার্ড; translated from [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language): *বাংলাদেশ উপকূল রক্ষক*); **BCG** is the maritime law enforcement force of [Bangladesh](3454.md). It is a paramilitary force which is under the jurisdiction of the [Ministry of Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Home_Affairs_\(Bangladesh\)). Its officers are transferred from the [Bangladesh Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Navy). The Bangladesh Coast Guard also performs the duty of maritime border security of Bangladesh. The headquarters is located in [Dhaka](56656.md), Bangladesh. Currently the coast guard has 3,339 personnel[^cite_note-cga-5]

### Barbados

Main article: [Barbados Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbados_Coast_Guard)

The **Barbados Coast Guard (BCG)** is the maritime element of the [Barbados Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbados_Defence_Force), thus functioning as the [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy) of [Barbados](3455.md). Its responsibilities and duties include patrolling territorial waters and the exclusive economic zone (EEZ) of Barbados, territorial defence through marine means, counter-narcotics operations, and conducting maritime law enforcement operations.

### Belize

Main article: [Belize Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize_Coast_Guard)

The [Belize Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize_Coast_Guard) (BCG) mission includes maritime safety, maritime security, marine resources protection, maintaining sovereignty over Belize sea space, and naval defence of Belize.[^cite_note-6]

Coast Guard men and women are deployed around the clock patrolling the internal waters and territorial seas. On the northern frontier, their joint operating base at Consejo protects the local economy from the negative impacts of illegal contraband and acts as the northern cut off for drug trafficking. On their southern boundary they stand guard at the Sarstoon river ensuring sovereignty and territorial integrity of Belize. Their motto is Utrinque Paratus (Ready for Anything).[^cite_note-7]

The Coast Guard Service coordinates its activities with the [Belize Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize_Defence_Force) and the [Belize Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize_Police_Department).[^cite_note-8]

### Bosnia and Herzegovina



Bosnia and Herzegovina Granična policija surveillance boat (unit P4)

Having 18 kilometres of coastline only, Bosnia and Herzegovina does not have a force dedicated to defend its coast. The duty of patrolling its coastline falls to the [Гранична_полиција_Босне_и_Херцеговине](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%93%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%BD%D0%B0_%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0_%D0%91%D0%BE%D1%81%D0%BD%D0%B5_%D0%B8_%D0%A5%D0%B5%D1%80%D1%86%D0%B5%D0%B3%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B5?action=edit\&redlink=1) (English: Border Police).

### Brazil



Brazilian coastguard ship

In addition to the roles of a traditional navy, the [Brazilian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Navy) also carries out the role of organizing the merchant navy and other operational safety missions traditionally conducted by a coast guard. Other roles include: Conducting national maritime policy, and implementing and enforcing laws and regulations with respect to the sea and inland waters.

### Canada

Main article: [Canadian Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Coast_Guard)

The Canadian Coast Guard (CCG) is a civilian service under the [Department of National Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_National_Defence_\(Canada\)) responsible for patrolling the world's longest coastline of 243,042 km (\~151,000 mi).[^cite_note-9]

The CCG holds responsibility for all marine search and rescue throughout Canada. The CCG coordinates search and rescue operations with the [Canadian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Forces), [Royal Canadian Mounted Police](69080.md) (RCMP), and other organizations. The CCG maintains and operates seamarks, coastal light stations, vessel traffic services, marine pollution response services, marine communications systems, and provides icebreaking services. The CCG also operates all Federal scientific research and hydrographic survey vessels. To accomplish these tasks, the CCG has a sizable fleet of vessels and aircraft, all serviced from various bases and smaller stations located on three coasts ([Atlantic](698.md), [Arctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Ocean), [Pacific](23070.md)) and the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) and [St. Lawrence River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_River).[^cite_note-10]

The [Canadian Coast Guard Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Coast_Guard_Academy) is located near [Sydney, Nova Scotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney,_Nova_Scotia).

### China

Main article: [China Coast Guard](15845458.md)

The [China Coast Guard](15845458.md) (CCG) ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 中国海警) serves as a coordinating body for maritime search and rescue in the territorial waters of the People's Republic of China. It was formed on 2013 as an amalgamation of four agencies: the Public Security Border Troops under the [Ministry of Public Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Public_Security_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), [China Maritime Safety Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Maritime_Safety_Administration), [China Marine Surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Marine_Surveillance), and [China Fisheries Law Enforcement Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Fisheries_Law_Enforcement_Command?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] In March 2018, it was placed under the leadership of the [People's Armed Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Armed_Police), which is under the direct command of the [Central Military Commission](214345.md) (CMC).[^cite_note-13]

#### Hong Kong

In Hong Kong, law enforcement duties are carried out by the [Marine Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Region) of the [Hong Kong Police Force](1276176.md) and the [Customs and Excise Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_and_Excise_Department_\(Hong_Kong\)) (Ports and Maritime Command of the Boundary and Ports Branch). The [Hong Kong Maritime Rescue Co-ordination Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_Maritime_Rescue_Co-ordination_Centre) (HKMRCC) co-ordinates search and rescue vessels, aircraft and other resources of the [Fire Services Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_Services_Department), [Government Flying Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Flying_Service), [Marine Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Department_\(Hong_Kong\)) and the Marine Police.

#### Macau

In [Macau](19068.md), coast guard responsibilities fall under the purview of the [Macau Customs Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau_Customs_Service), which is in charge of conducting coast guard duties.[^cite_note-news-14]

The [Marine and Water Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_and_Water_Bureau?action=edit\&redlink=1), which is under the [Secretariat for Transport and Public Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretariat_for_Transport_and_Public_Works_\(Macau\)), helps in coordinating search and rescue operations in the region. It's done via the **Search and Rescue Coordination Centre**, formed under the **Vessel Traffic Control Centre of Macau** (Macao VTS).

### Croatia

Main article: [Croatian Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Coast_Guard)

The Croatian Coast Guard ([Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language): *Obalna straža Republike Hrvatske*) is a division of the [Croatian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Navy) responsible for protecting the interests of the [Republic of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Croatia) at [sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea). The Croatian Navy is composed of classical naval forces structured into a [flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Flotilla_\(Croatia\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the [Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard) that solely consists of ships with peacetime duties, e.g. protection of [ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology), [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing), control of [tankers](2506366.md), [ballast waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballast_water), combat against [terrorism](30636.md), [trafficking of people](19333662.md), [narcotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcotics), and similar.[^cite_note-15]

On September 13, 2007, the Croatian Parliament passed a bill establishing the Croatian Coast Guard. The Coast Guard's mission is protect sovereign rights and carry out Croatia's jurisdiction in the [Ecological and Fisheries Protection Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_and_Fisheries_Protection_Zone), the [continental shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf) and the [high seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_seas). The Coast Guard will also monitor vessels sailing in the Croatian [territorial waters](496983.md). If vessels are caught violating Croatian or international regulations and disregard warnings by the Coast Guard, Coast Guard ships and airplanes are authorized to pursue them and if necessary open fire, while taking care not to jeopardize the lives of the vessel's crew.

Under the law, the commander of the Coast Guard is a Navy officer who is appointed and relieved of duty by the [president of the republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Croatia) at the government's proposal.[^cite_note-16]

### Cyprus



Cyprus Port and Marine Police jet FPB (Fast sea Patrol Boat)



Northern Cyprus coast guard ship KKTCSG-01 at the [Port of Famagusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Famagusta)

Main articles: [Cyprus Port and Marine Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Port_and_Marine_Police) and [Cyprus Police Aviation Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Police_Aviation_Unit)

The [Cyprus Port and Marine Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Port_and_Marine_Police) ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) *Λιμενική και Ναυτική Αστυνομία* – *Limeniki kai Nautiki Astinomia*) fulfills the functions of other countries' coast guards for the [Republic of Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Cyprus). Cyprus is an [island country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_country) in the [Eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean) with about 1/3 of the island is under control of [Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Republic_of_Northern_Cyprus) after [1974 Cypriot coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Cypriot_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) and following [Turkish military Invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_invasion_of_Cyprus) based on [Treaty of Guarantee (1960)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Guarantee_\(1960\)). Due to the country's [geopolitical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geopolitical) situation, size, the recent discovery in 2011 of the [Aphrodite gas field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphrodite_gas_field) in its [Exclusive Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_Economic_Zone) and [Cyprus Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Police) being the main national [law enforcement agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_agency), the duties and responsibilities of the Cyprus Port and Marine Police are many and sometimes complex. It is a unit of the Cyprus Police, which resides under the [Ministry of Justice and Public Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_and_Public_Order_\(Cyprus\)). However it can support the [Cyprus Naval Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Navy) in wartime which resides under the Ministry of Defence. It is staffed by [Police Officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_Officer) which can be transferred to and from other units and agencies of the [Cyprus Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Police) and are tasked with the primary mission of policing the country's sea borders and the [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement) of the waters around it. The unit is equipped with patrol boats and radars but it does not operate its own helicopters. Instead, it operates in combination with the aerial unit of the Cyprus Police, the [Cyprus Police Aviation Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Police_Aviation_Unit). Main roles include law enforcement against illicit activities such as [smuggling](28645.md) (due to the fact that although the [Customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs) and Excise Department is a separate agency under the Ministry of Finance, it does not have an operational or tactical team of its own), [terrorism](30636.md), [piracy](50715.md), illegal [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing), [Illegal drug trade](218449.md), [illegal immigration](7124412.md) and is also assigned with [search and rescue](172599.md) (SAR) duties. For [counter-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-terrorism) and anti-piracy operations, the units operate in combination with the Special Anti-Terrorist Squad which is part of the [Emergency Response Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_Response_Unit_\(Cyprus\)) of the Cyprus Police. Although the prominent agencies responsible for all Search and Rescue operations are conducted by Cyprus Police Units, the agency responsible to organize the SAR system, to co-ordinate, to control and direct SAR operations in the region that the Republic of Cyprus is responsible for (which coincides with the Nicosia [FIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_information_region)) is the [Cyprus Joint Rescue Coordination Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_Joint_Rescue_Coordination_Center) or JRCC Larnaca, which is an independent agency of the Ministry of Defence.

### Djibouti

Main article: [Djiboutian Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djiboutian_Coast_Guard)

The [Djiboutian Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djiboutian_Coast_Guard), part of the [Djiboutian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djiboutian_Armed_Forces), is a [humanitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitarian) and security service. It protects Djibouti's borders and economic and security interests and defends its [territorial waters](496983.md) and its [Exclusive Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_Economic_Zone).

### Georgia

Main article: [Coast Guard of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_of_Georgia)

The Georgian Coast Guard is the maritime arm of the [Georgian Border Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Police_of_Georgia), within the Ministry for Internal Affairs. It is responsible for the maritime protection of the entire 310 km (190 mi) coastline of Georgia, as well as the Georgian [territorial waters](496983.md). The primary missions of the service are administration of the territorial waters, marine pollution protection, maritime law enforcement, search and rescue, port security and maritime defense. The former Georgian Navy was absorbed into the Coast Guard in 2009.

The headquarters and a principal Coast Guard base are located at the [Black Sea](3386.md) port of [Poti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poti). A second smaller base is in [Batumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batumi), [Adjaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjaria). Besides the Poti-based force, the Coast Guard also includes a special counter-terrorist Detachment. Maritime surveillance radar stations are maintained at [Anaklia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaklia), Poti, [Supsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supsa), [Chakvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakvi), and [Gonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonio_\(settlement\)), providing coverage of all territorial seas.

### Isle of Man

Main article: [Isle of Man Coastguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Man_Coastguard)

### Portugal

Main article: [Maritime Authority System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Authority_System)



A Portuguese Navy [*Centauro*-class patrol boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centauro-class_fiscalization_boat), one of its assets employed in the coast guard role under the Portuguese National Maritime Authority

In [Portugal](23033.md), the coast guard role is performed by several government agencies that, together, form the Maritime Authority System (*Sistema de Autoridade Marítima* or SAM). The SAM includes the [Portuguese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Navy), the [National Republican Guard (GNR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_National_Republican_Guard), the [Portuguese Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Air_Force), the [Border and Immigration Service (SEF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servi%C3%A7o_de_Estrangeiros_e_Fronteiras), the Civil Protection Authority, the National Medical Emergency Institute and the [Criminal Investigation Police (PJ)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pol%C3%ADcia_Judici%C3%A1ria).

The National Maritime Authority (*Autoridade Marítima Nacional* or AMN) is the branch of the Portuguese Navy responsible for its coast guard role. The function of AMN is performed by the Chief of Staff of the Navy himself, supported in this role by the Directorate of the Maritime Authority, which includes the Maritime Police, the Lifeguard Institute, the Lighthouse Department and the several [harbourmasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbourmaster). Besides the specific assets of the Directorate of Maritime Authority entirely dedicated to the coast guard role, the AMN also has at its disposal the other Portuguese Navy's assets that can be used both for military and public service missions.

The vessels operated within the SAM include the Maritime Police patrol boats, the Lifeguard Institute lifeboats, the harbourmasters harbour boats, the GNR Coastal Control Unit' surveillance boats and the Portuguese Navy's naval ships. The aircraft operated within the SAM include fixed-wing aircraft from the Portuguese Air Force and helicopters from the Navy, the Air Force and the Civil Protection Authority.

### Spain



An AW139 helicopter of Spain's [Sociedad de Salvamento y Seguridad Marítima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociedad_de_Salvamento_y_Seguridad_Mar%C3%ADtima)

The Kingdom of Spain operates several coast guard agencies.

The *[Sociedad de Salvamento y Seguridad Marítima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociedad_de_Salvamento_y_Seguridad_Mar%C3%ADtima)*, also known as *Salvamento Marítimo*, or by its acronym SASEMAR, is the body in charge of maritime traffic control, safety and rescue operations, as well as protection of the maritime environment, but lacks any law enforcement responsibilities. The agency runs 20 rescue coordination centres (RCC), employs a staff of 1500 and operates a fleet of 19 vessels, 54 boats, 11 helicopters and 3 airplanes.

Border protection functions in the Kingdom of Spain are carried out by the Civil Guard (*[Servicio Marítimo de la Guardia Civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servicio_Mar%C3%ADtimo_de_la_Guardia_Civil)*), with a staff of 1100 and a fleet of 67 patrol vessels and speedboats. Maritime customs functions are the responsibility of the *[Servicio de Vigilancia Aduanera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servicio_de_Vigilancia_Aduanera)* with 13 aircraft and a surface fleet of 2 high seas cutters; 18 high speed patrol vessels; and 21 medium-sized and smaller patrol vessels.

*Salvamento Marítimo* operates in the four international SAR areas assigned to Spain: Atlántico, Canarias, Estrecho and Mediterráneo, covering a combined extension of 1.5 million square kilometers. During a maritime emergency, Salvamento Marítimo is responsible for the coordination of other possible responders, like the [Spanish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Navy) or the *[Servicio Aéreo de Rescate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servicio_A%C3%A9reo_de_Rescate?action=edit\&redlink=1)*,[^cite_note-32] that comprises squadrons 801, 802 and 803 of the [Spanish Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Air_Force). Coordination on land, sea and air are made with the [North Atlantic Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Treaty_Organization) (NATO), and cooperative [European Union](9317.md)

#### Galicia

The [autonomous community](55002.md) of [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Spain\)) has its own separate coast guard service, the *Servizo de Gardacostas de Galicia* or simply *Gardacostas de Galicia*. It is the [Galician Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xunta_de_Galicia) agency responsible for coastal and fisheries surveillance; maritime search and rescue; and protection of the sea environment. The *Gardacostas de Galicia* operates a fleet of more than 20 vessels and two helicopters, from the bases of [Viveiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viveiro), [Ferrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrol,_Galicia), [A Coruña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Coru%C3%B1a), [Muxía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mux%C3%ADa), [Porto do Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto_do_Son), [Ribeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribeira,_Galicia), [Vilagarcía de Arousa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilagarc%C3%ADa_de_Arousa), [Pontevedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontevedra) and [Vigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigo).

The *Gardacostas de Galicia* was created in 2004, by the amalgamation of the former *Servizo de Vixilancia Pesqueira* (Galician Fisheries Surveillance Service) and the *Servizo de Busca e Salvamento* (Galician Search and Rescue Service).

### Sri Lanka

Main article: [Sri Lanka Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Coast_Guard)

In [Sri Lanka](26750.md), until 2009 a Coast Guard Unit existed under the Ministry of Fisheries and Aquatic Resources, responsible for fishery and natural resource protection coastal areas. A new [Department of Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Coast_Guard) was created under the *Department of Coast Guard Bill* presented to parliament in 2009. The department would be under the purview of the [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Sri_Lanka\)) and be staffed by civilian personnel. The department's responsibilities include [search and rescue](172599.md), [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_agency) within the country's [Exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone), conducting anti-smuggling and anti-immigration operations, suppression of terrorist activities in territorial waters of Sri Lanka.[^cite_note-33]

### Sweden

Main article: [Coast Guard (Sweden)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_\(Sweden\))



Swedish coast guards conducting an alcohol test

The [Swedish Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_\(Sweden\)) ([Swedish](26689.md): *Kustbevakningen*) is a Swedish civilian government agency with combination of police tasks, border control, maritime rescue and environmental control. Coast Guard officers carries the same equipment to regular Swedish police officers. Tasks:

- maritime surveillance and other control and inspection tasks as well as environmental cleanup after oil spills at sea

- co-ordinate the civilian needs for maritime surveillance and maritime information

- follow international development within the field and take part in international efforts to establish border controls, law enforcement at sea, environmental protection at sea and other maritime surveillance tasks.

The Swedish Coast Guard carries out some of its surveillance by air (from its base in Skavsta near Stockholm), and some on ice and snow (from its Luleå station). It also has marine duties on Lake Vänern, Europe's third largest lake, operating out of Vänersborg.

### Turkey

Main article: [Coast Guard Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_Command)



Patrol boat of the Turkish Coast Guard

The Coast Guard Command (CGC) of the Republic of Turkey ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *Sahil Güvenlik Komutanlığı*) is a branch of the [Turkish Armed Forces](30205.md) and was initially founded in 1859. During peacetime, it is under the command of the [Ministry of Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_\(Turkey\)). However, during war or emergency time, it falls under the command of the [Turkish Naval Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Naval_Forces). On July 13, 1982, it was assigned to the [Gendarmerie General Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie_General_Command) becoming both a military and a law enforcement service. Finally, the organization obtained its current form on January 1, 1985.[^cite_note-sgk-36]

The Coast Guard is responsible for enforcement of Turkish maritime law and controlling of the Republic of Turkey's coasts in the responsibility area. It is also the main search and rescue coordination authority for the Turkish coastal zone. The CGC consists of four area commands, as the [Black Sea](3386.md), the [Sea of Marmara](28863.md) and adjacent [straits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Straits), the [Aegean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea), and the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md).[^cite_note-sgk-36]

The Coast Guard Command maintains a fleet of coastal patrol ships and small craft, as well as helicopters and fixed-wing aircraft.[^cite_note-37]

### United Kingdom

Main article: [His Majesty's Coastguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/His_Majesty's_Coastguard)

In the [United Kingdom](31717.md), [His Majesty's Coastguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Coastguard) fulfill six of the nine functions required by the IMO; Search and Rescue, Pollution Response, Vessel Traffic Management, Maritime Safety, Accident and Disaster Response, and Maritime Security. The other three IMO functions; Customs/Border Control, Fisheries Control and Law Enforcement, are undertaken by the [UK Border Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Border_Force), [Environment Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_Agency) and [local police forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_police_force), respectively. The maintenance of [seamarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seamark) is the responsibility of [Trinity House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_House) (in England and Wales), the [Northern Lighthouse Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Lighthouse_Board) (in Scotland) and the [Commissioners of Irish Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioners_of_Irish_Lights) (in Northern Ireland). HM Coastguard is a civilian agency - as the duties traditionally associated with a military coast guard service are spread around the [Civil Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Service_\(United_Kingdom\)) and [British Armed Forces](31730.md), unlike other coast guard services around the world. It is a Category 1 emergency responder alongside the United Kingdom's regional police, fire, and ambulance services.

Lifeboat services are provided by the [Royal National Lifeboat Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_National_Lifeboat_Institution), or other independent lifeboat stations not affiliated with the RNLI, all under the coordination of the Coastguard. HM Coastguard [wet leases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_lease) commercial helicopters — [Sikorsky S-92s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_S-92) and [AgustaWestland AW189s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AgustaWestland_AW189) — to provide aerial search and rescue cover around Great Britain and Northern Ireland, as well as the [United Kingdom's emergency towing vessel fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom's_emergency_towing_vessel_fleet) in areas not served by tug brokers. On the coastline, HM Coastguard maintains a network of cliff and mud rescue teams, comprising around 4,000 volunteers, and can call upon the [National Coastwatch Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coastwatch_Institution) which mans many former Coastguard lookout stations around the coast. It is part of the [Maritime and Coastguard Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_and_Coastguard_Agency), which in itself is an [executive agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_agency) of the [Department for Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Transport).

### United States

Main article: [United States Coast Guard](32223.md)



USCG racing stripe



National Security cutters, the [USCGC *Bertholf*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Bertholf) and [USCGC *Waesche*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USCGC_Waesche_\(WMSL-751\)), cruise together.

Formed in 1915 by the merger of the [United States Revenue Cutter Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Revenue_Cutter_Service) (established as the United States Revenue-Marine in 1790 and renamed in 1894) and the [United States Life-Saving Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Life-Saving_Service) (established in 1848), and later absorbing the [United States Lighthouse Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Lighthouse_Service) in 1939 and the functions of the [Bureau of Marine Inspection and Navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Navigation) in 1942 and 1946, the United States Coast Guard (USCG) is a [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) service, a [law enforcement agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_agency), and regulatory agency. It is one of the eight [uniformed services of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniformed_services_of_the_United_States) and one of the six services of the [United States Armed Forces](32212.md). Its role includes enforcement of U.S. maritime law, coastal defense, search and rescue, environmental protection, aids to navigation, and regulation of maritime industries.[^cite_note-38]

During [peacetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacetime) the USCG falls under the administration of the [United States Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) (previously the [U.S. Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Transportation), 1967–2003, and the [United States Department of the Treasury](53667.md), 1915–1967). During [wartime](33158.md), the USCG may, at the direction of the [president of the United States](24113.md), report to the [Secretary of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Secretary_of_the_Navy); its resources, however, are integrated into U.S. military operations in wartime (see [14 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_14_of_the_United_States_Code) [§§ 3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/14/3)–[4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/14/4)).

As of 2022, the Coast Guard had 40,757 coast guardsmen on active duty, 6,240 reservists, approximately 26,000 [auxiliarists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Auxiliary), and over 7,100 full-time civilian employees.[^cite_note-workforce-39] The Coast Guard maintains an extensive fleet of 243 coastal and ocean-going patrol ships, tenders, tugs and icebreakers called "[Cutters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutter_\(boat\))", and 1650 smaller boats, as well as an extensive aviation division consisting of 201 helicopters and fixed-wing aircraft.[^cite_note-40] While the U.S. Coast Guard is next to the smallest of the U.S. armed service branches, in terms of size, the U.S. Coast Guard is the world's largest coast guard.

USCG helicopters are equipped with hoists to rescue survivors and also play a major role in law enforcement. The helicopters are able to land and take off from USCG cutters, making them an indispensable tool in fighting illegal drug traffic and the influx of undocumented immigrants attempting to illegally enter the country. The fixed-wing aircraft are used for long range search and rescue and law enforcement patrols. A construction and repair shipyard has been maintained since 1899 in the [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore) Harbor area at [Curtis Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtis_Bay,_Baltimore), [United States Coast Guard Yard at Curtis Bay, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Yard).

Today's lighthouses on the American coasts are all maintained by the U.S. Coast Guard, from the previous old [U.S. Lighthouse Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Lighthouse_Service). The list of active light houses, lighted beacons, etc. that provide detailed information on aids to navigation with their locations and characteristic signals is currently maintained by the U.S. Coast Guard in its [Light List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_List) issued each year.[^cite_note-41]

The USCG has a small service academy, equivalent to both the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy) and the [United States Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy) in terms of students earning a 4-year degree and commission upon graduation. The [United States Coast Guard Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Coast_Guard_Academy) is much smaller in size (approx. 1,000 students) and is located at [New London, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_London,_Connecticut). All enlisted coast guardsmen attend [boot camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recruit_training#U.S._Coast_Guard) at [Cape May, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_May,_New_Jersey).

Several other federal, state and local agencies operate maritime police and security units with law enforcement and search and rescue functions similar to the Coast Guard. Examples include the [New Hampshire Marine Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hampshire_Marine_Patrol) and the [U.S. Customs and Border Protection](757040.md), Air and Marine Operations, along with state [Naval Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Militia).

### Uruguay

In the [Republic of Uruguay](31843.md), the Coast Guard ([Spanish](26825.md): *Prefectura Nacional Naval-PRENA*)[^cite_note-42] is a part of the [National Navy of Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Navy_of_Uruguay). It is tasked with the broader enforcement of maritime laws, especially against smuggling, illegal fishing, drug trafficking and piracy. It patrols the country's coastline, and is also involved in maritime search and rescue (SAR) missions, as well as the protection of the marine environment. The Uruguayan Coast Guard also frequently patrols coast areas via-foot and on patrol vehicles which they are assigned jurisdictions which are called Subprefecturas. Prefectura is also responsible for the [International Port of Montevideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Port_of_Montevideo?action=edit\&redlink=1) operability. Even though the Uruguayan Coast Guard is a military organization, they frequently help out [law enforcement agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_agency)

It must also maintain the safety of navigation and be a maritime authority on rivers, the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md), the River Plate, and lakes in the jurisdiction of the Navy and intervene in the flagging of vessels.

In 2015, there are plans for the Uruguayan Coast Guard to pass and depend entirely on the [Ministry of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_\(Uruguay\)), meaning it would not be a military unit anymore.

### Vietnam

Main article: [Vietnam Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Coast_Guard)



An [offshore patrol vessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_patrol_vessel) of Vietnam Coast Guard.

[Vietnam Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Coast_Guard) (**VCG**; [Vietnamese](32511.md): *Cảnh sát biển Việt Nam*) is directly under the administration of the [Vietnamese Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_Government), and it patrols and controls in accordance with the laws of the [Socialist Republic of Vietnam](202354.md) and international treaties concerned such as the [United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea](31940.md) (UNCLOS). Besides maritime search and rescue (SAR) missions, Vietnam Coast Guard missions include protection the sovereignty and jurisdiction of the waters of Vietnam; protection of natural resources; prevention of environmental pollution; detection and prevention of acts of smuggling, piracy and illegal transportation and trafficking of illegal narcotics or precursors.

Besides the mentioned "official coast guard", Vietnam also organizes an alternative maritime law enforcement force, which is the [Vietnam Fisheries Surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_Fisheries_Surveillance). While theoretically focusing at counter illegal fishing behaviors and administer fisheries activities in the [Vietnamese water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone_of_Vietnam) corresponding to its name, the Fisheries Surveillance also shares VCG's missions to perform SAR and represents Vietnam's national interest and sovereignty exercises in the sea with its strong fleet of more than 100 medium-sized patrol vessels.
