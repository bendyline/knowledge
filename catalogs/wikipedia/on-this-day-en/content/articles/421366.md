# Mars rover

This article is about autonomous exploration vehicles. For crewed vehicles on Mars, see [Crewed Mars rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crewed_Mars_rover).



NASA's *[Curiosity](36645032.md)* rover, [selfie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selfie), 2015

A **Mars rover** is a remote-controlled [motor vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_vehicle) designed to travel on the surface of [Mars](14640471.md). [Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rover_\(space_exploration\)) have several advantages over stationary [landers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lander_\(spacecraft\)): they examine more territory, they can be directed to interesting features, they can place themselves in sunny positions to weather winter months, and they can advance the knowledge of how to perform very remote [robotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot) vehicle control. They serve a different purpose than orbital spacecraft like *[Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Reconnaissance_Orbiter)*. A more recent development is the [Mars helicopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingenuity_\(helicopter\)).

As of May 2021, there have been six successful robotically operated Mars rovers; the first five, managed by the American [NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory), were (by date of Mars landing): *[Sojourner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sojourner_\(rover\))* (1997), *[Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_\(rover\))* (2004–2010), *[Opportunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_\(rover\))* (2004–2018), *[Curiosity](36645032.md)* (2012–present), and *[Perseverance](63297153.md)* (2021–present). The sixth, managed by the [China National Space Administration](344618.md), is *[Zhurong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhurong_\(rover\))* (2021–2022).

On January 24, 2016, [NASA](18426568.md) reported that [ongoing studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Mars_Science_Laboratory#Current_status) on Mars by *Opportunity* and *Curiosity* would be searching for evidence of ancient life, including a [biosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere) based on [autotrophic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autotroph), [chemotrophic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemotroph) or [chemolithoautotrophic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithotroph#Chemolithotrophs) [microorganisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism), as well as ancient water, including [fluvio-lacustrine environments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacustrine_plain) ([plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain) related to ancient [rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River) or [lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake)) that may have been [habitable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_habitability).[^cite_note-SCI-20140124a-1][^cite_note-SCI-20140124special-2][^cite_note-SCI-20140124-3][^cite_note-SCI-20140124c-4][^cite_note-5] The search for evidence of [habitability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_habitability), [taphonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taphonomy) (related to [fossils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossils)), and [organic carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_carbon) on Mars has become a primary NASA objective.[^cite_note-SCI-20140124a-1][^cite_note-6]

The Soviet probes, [Mars 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_2) and [Mars 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_3), were physically tethered probes; *Sojourner* was dependent on the *[Mars Pathfinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Pathfinder)* base station for communication with Earth; *Opportunity*, *Spirit* and *Curiosity* were on their own. As of August 11, 2026, *Curiosity* is still active, while *Spirit*, *Opportunity*, and *Sojourner* completed their missions before losing contact. On February 18, 2021, *Perseverance*, the newest American Mars rover, successfully landed, and remains active as of August 11, 2026. On May 14, 2021, China's *Zhurong* became the first non-American rover to successfully operate on Mars.

### Past

-  *[Sojourner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sojourner_\(rover\))* rover, [Mars Pathfinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Pathfinder), landed successfully on July 4, 1997. Communications were lost on September 27, 1997. *Sojourner* had traveled a distance of just over 100 meters (330 ft).

  [^cite_note-Sojourner-17]

-  *[Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_\(rover\))* (MER-A), [Mars Exploration Rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Exploration_Rover) (MER), launched on June 10, 2003,

  [^cite_note-Mars_Exploration-18]

  &#x20;and landed on January 4, 2004. Nearly six years after the original mission limit, *Spirit* had covered a total distance of 7.73 km (4.80 mi) but its wheels became trapped in sand.

  [^cite_note-19]

  &#x20;The last communication received from the rover was on March 22, 2010, and NASA ceased attempts to re-establish communication on May 25, 2011.

  [^cite_note-20]

-  *[Opportunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_\(rover\))* (MER-B), Mars Exploration Rover, launched on July 7, 2003

  [^cite_note-Mars_Exploration-18]

  &#x20;and landed on January 25, 2004. *Opportunity* surpassed the previous records for longevity at 5,352 [sols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sol_\(day_on_Mars\)) (5498 Earth days from landing to mission end; 15 Earth years or 8 Martian years) and covered 45.16 km (28.06 mi). The rover sent its last status on 10 June 2018 when a global [2018 Mars dust storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_mission_timeline#Dust_storm) blocked the sunlight needed to recharge its batteries.

  [^cite_note-nasa.gov-21]

  &#x20;After hundreds of attempts to reactivate the rover, NASA declared the mission complete on February 13, 2019.

-  *[Zhurong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhurong_\(rover\))* launched with the [Tianwen-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Global_Remote_Sensing_Orbiter_and_Small_Rover) [CNSA](344618.md) Mars mission on July 23, 2020, landed on May 14, 2021, in the southern region of [Utopia Planitia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopia_Planitia), and deployed on May 22, 2021, while dropping a remote selfie camera on 1 June 2021.

  [^cite_note-22]

  [^cite_note-23]

  &#x20;Designed for a lifespan of 90 sols (93 Earth days),

  [^cite_note-24]

  &#x20;*Zhurong* had been active for 347 sols (356.5 days) since its deployment and traveled on Mars's surface for 1,921 m (6,302 ft).

  [^cite_note-zhurong_2205-25]

  &#x20;Since 20 May 2022, the rover was deactivated due to approaching sandstorms and Martian winter.

  [^cite_note-nature_230120-26]

  [^cite_note-vice_2303-27]

  &#x20;But the larger-than-expected build-up of dust covering its solar panels prevented it from self-reactivation. On 25 April 2023, the mission designer Zhang Rongqiao announced that the buildup of dust from the last inactivation is greater than planned, indicating the rover could be inactive "forever".

  [^cite_note-28]

### Failed

-  *[Mars 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_2)*, *[PrOP-M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PrOP-M)* rover, 1971, *Mars 2* landing failed, destroying Prop-M with it. The *Mars 2* and *3* spacecraft from the Soviet Union had identical 4.5 kilograms (9.9 lb) *Prop-M* rovers. They were to move on [skis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski) while connected to the landers with cables.

  [^cite_note-Mars2_NSSDC-29]

-  *[Mars 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_3)*, *[PrOP-M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PrOP-M)* rover, landed successfully on December 2, 1971. 4.5 kilograms (9.9 lb) rover tethered to the Mars 3 lander. Lost when the *Mars 3* lander stopped communicating about 110 seconds after landing.

  [^cite_note-Mars2_NSSDC-29]

  &#x20;The loss of communication may have been due to the extremely powerful Martian dust storm taking place at the time, or an issue with the Mars 3 orbiter's ability to relay communications.

## NASA Mars rover goals

Circa the 2010s, NASA had established certain goals for the rover program.

NASA distinguishes between "mission" objectives and "science" objectives. Mission objectives are related to progress in [space technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_technology) and development processes. Science objectives are met by the instruments during their mission in space.

The science instruments are chosen and designed based on the science objectives and goals. The primary goal of the *Spirit* and *Opportunity* rovers was to investigate "the history of water on Mars".[^cite_note-39]

The four science goals of NASA's long-term [Mars Exploration Program](32506368.md) are:

- Determine whether life ever arose on Mars

- Characterize the [climate of Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Mars)

- Characterize the [geology of Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Mars)

- Prepare for [human exploration of Mars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_exploration_of_Mars)
