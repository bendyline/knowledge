The **2014 FIFA World Cup** ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Copa do Mundo da FIFA Brasil 2014*) was the 20th [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), the quadrennial world championship for [men's national](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_men's_national_association_football_teams) [football](10568.md) teams organised by [FIFA](11049.md). It took place in Brazil from 12 June to 13 July 2014, after the country was awarded the hosting rights in 2007. It was the second time that Brazil staged the competition, the first being in [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup), and the fifth time that it was held in South America.

Thirty-one national teams advanced through [qualification competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) to join the host nation in the final tournament (with [Bosnia and Herzegovina](1008974.md) as the only debutant). A total of 64 matches were played in 12 venues located in as many host cities across Brazil. For the first time at a World Cup finals, match officials used [goal-line technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal-line_technology), as well as [vanishing spray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanishing_spray) for [free kicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_kick_\(association_football\)).[^cite_note-GLT-6] [FIFA Fan Fests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Fan_Festival) in each host city gathered a total of 5 million people, and the country received 1 million visitors from 202 countries.[^cite_note-7] [Spain](609825.md), the defending champions, were eliminated at the group stage. Host nation Brazil, who had won the [2013 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup), [lost to Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_v_Germany_\(2014_FIFA_World_Cup\)) 7–1 in the semi-finals and eventually finished in fourth place.[^cite_note-8]

In [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_final), Germany defeated Argentina 1–0 after extra time following a half-volley goal by [Mario Götze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_G%C3%B6tze) in the 113th minute of the final[^cite_note-9] to win the tournament and secure the country's fourth world title, the first after [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification) in 1990, when as West Germany they also beat Argentina by the same score in 90 minutes in the [World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_final). It was Germany's first major tournament win since [UEFA Euro 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996). Germany became the first European team to win the World Cup in the [Americas](29833.md),[^cite_note-10] and this result marked the third consecutive title won by a European team, after Italy in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) and Spain in [2010](168079.md), a record-breaking streak by the teams from the same continent continued by France [four years later](13327177.md).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-Trivia-12][^cite_note-13]

As of the 2026 tournament, this is the most recent World Cup to feature all previous winners ([Uruguay](679782.md), [Italy](362466.md), [Germany](250204.md), [Brazil](149286.md), [England](9904.md), [Argentina](454699.md), [France](250197.md) and [Spain](609825.md)).

## Host selection

Main article: [2014 FIFA World Cup bids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_bids)



FIFA president [Sepp Blatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepp_Blatter) announces Brazil as hosts, 30 October 2007

In March 2003, FIFA announced that the tournament would be held in South America for the first time since [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup), in line with its policy at the time of rotating the right to host the World Cup among different confederations.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] With the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md) hosted in South Africa, it would be the second consecutive World Cup outside Europe, which was a first for the tournament. It was also sixth time (second consecutive) in the Southern Hemisphere.[^cite_note-16] Only Brazil and Colombia formally declared their candidacy but, after the withdrawal of the latter from the process,[^cite_note-17] Brazil was officially elected as host nation unopposed on 30 October 2007.[^cite_note-18]

### Final draw

Main article: [2014 FIFA World Cup seeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_seeding)

The draw took place on 6 December 2013 at the [Costa do Sauípe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauipe) resort in [Bahia](199070.md), during which the teams were drawn by various past World Cup-winning players.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] Hosted by TV presentators [Fernanda Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernanda_Lima) and [Rodrigo Hilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_Hilbert) and conducted by the FIFA general secretary [Jérôme Valcke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A9r%C3%B4me_Valcke), the draw featured football celebrities from all FIFA World Cup champion countries such as [Cafu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cafu) (Brazil), [Fabio Cannavaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Cannavaro) (Italy), [Lothar Matthäus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothar_Matth%C3%A4us) (Germany), [Zinedine Zidane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinedine_Zidane) (France), [Mario Kempes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Kempes) (Argentina), [Fernando Hierro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Hierro) (Spain), [Geoff Hurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Hurst) (England) and [Alcides Ghiggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcides_Ghiggia) (Uruguay).[^cite_note-24]

The 32 participating teams were drawn into eight groups. In preparation for this, the teams were organised into four pots with the seven highest-ranked teams joining host nation [Brazil](149286.md) in the seeded pot.[^cite_note-25] As with the previous tournaments, FIFA aimed to create groups which maximised geographic separation and therefore the unseeded teams were arranged into pots based on geographic considerations.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Under the draw procedure, one randomly drawn team – Italy – was firstly relocated from Pot 4 to Pot 2 to create four equal pots of eight teams.[^cite_note-Draw_procedures-28]

### Officials

Main article: [2014 FIFA World Cup officials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_officials)

In March 2013, FIFA published a list of 52 prospective referees, each paired, on the basis of nationality, with two [linesmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_referee_\(association_football\)), from all six football confederations for the tournament. On 14 January 2014, the FIFA Referees Committee appointed 25 referee trios and eight support duos representing 43 countries for the tournament.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] [Yuichi Nishimura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuichi_Nishimura) from [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Football_Association) acted as referee in the opening match whereas [Nicola Rizzoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Rizzoli) from [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Football_Federation) acted as referee in the final.[^cite_note-Matches_1-4-31][^cite_note-32]

## Innovations

### Technologies



Adidas Brazuca the official ball used during most of the matches.

In order to avoid [ghost goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_goal), the 2014 World Cup introduced [goal-line technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal-line_technology) following successful trials at, among other competitions, the [2013 Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup). The chosen [Goal Control system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vieww) featured 14 high speed cameras, 7 directed to each of the goals. Data were sent to the central image-processing centre, where a virtual representation of the ball was output on a widescreen to confirm the goal. The referee was equipped with a watch which vibrated and displayed a signal upon a goal.[^cite_note-DW-45][^cite_note-Yah-46][^cite_note-TR-47] France's second goal in their [group game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E#France_vs_Honduras) against Honduras was the first time goal-line technology was needed to confirm that a goal should be given.[^cite_note-48]

Following successful trials,[^cite_note-49] FIFA approved the use of [vanishing spray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanishing_spray) by the referees for the first time at a World Cup Finals. The water-based spray, which disappears within minutes of application, can be used to mark a ten-yard line for the defending team during a [free kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_kick_\(association_football\)) and also to draw where the ball is to be placed for a free kick.[^cite_note-50]

The [Adidas Brazuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas_Brazuca) was the official [match ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_\(association_football\)) of the 2014 FIFA World Cup[^cite_note-forbes-51][^cite_note-brazucafifanews-52][^cite_note-brazucaadidasgroup-53][^cite_note-brazucafifamatchball-54] and was supplied by [Forward Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_Sports) of [Sialkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sialkot), [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-forbes-51] Adidas created a new design of ball after criticisms of the [Adidas Jabulani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas_Jabulani) used in the [previous World Cup](168079.md). The number of panels was reduced to six, with the panels being thermally bonded. This created a ball with increased consistency and aerodynamics compared to its predecessor. Furthermore, Adidas underwent an extensive testing process lasting more than two years to produce a ball that would meet the approval of football professionals.

### Cooling breaks

Due to the relatively high ambient temperatures in Brazil, particularly at the northern venues, cooling breaks for the players were introduced.[^cite_note-55] Breaks could take place at the referee's discretion after the 30th minute of each half if the [Wet Bulb Globe Temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_Bulb_Globe_Temperature) exceeded 32 °C (90 °F); the breaks would last 3 minutes, with this time made up by an extended period of stoppage time at the end of the half.

The first cooling break in a World Cup play took place during the 32nd minute of the [match between the Netherlands and Mexico in the round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Netherlands_vs_Mexico).[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] At the start of the match, FIFA listed the temperature at 32 °C (90 °F) with 68% humidity.[^cite_note-60]

### Anti-doping

The [biological passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_passport) was introduced in the FIFA World Cup starting in 2014. [Blood](3997.md) and [urine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urine) samples collected from all players before the competition, and from two players per team per match, were analysed by the [Swiss Laboratory for Doping Analyses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Laboratory_for_Doping_Analyses).[^cite_note-61] FIFA reported that 91.5% of the players taking part in the tournament were tested before the start of the competition and none tested positive.[^cite_note-62] However, FIFA was criticised for how it conducted doping tests.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

## Format

&#x20;The first round, or group stage, was a competition between the 32 teams divided among eight groups of four, where each group engaged in a [round-robin tournament](939742.md) within itself. The two highest ranked teams in each group advanced to the knockout stage.[^cite_note-regulations-33] Teams were awarded [three points for a win](4960100.md) and one for a draw. When comparing teams in a group over-all result came before head-to-head.

| Tie-breaking criteria for group play |
| --- |
| The ranking of teams in each group was based on the following criteria: Number of points [Goal difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_difference) Number of goals scored Number of points obtained in matches between tied teams Goal difference in matches between tied teams Number of goals scored in matches between tied teams Drawing of lots |

In the [knockout stage](939680.md) there were four rounds (round of 16, quarter-finals, semi-finals, and the final), with each eliminating the losers. The two semi-final losers competed in a match for third place. For any match in the knockout stage, a draw after 90 minutes of regulation time was followed by two 15 minute periods of [extra time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overtime_\(association_football\)) to determine a winner. If the teams were still tied, a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md) was held to determine a winner.[^cite_note-regulations-33]

The match schedule was announced on 20 October 2011[^cite_note-65] with the kick-off times being confirmed on 27 September 2012;[^cite_note-66] after the final draw, the kick-off times of seven matches were adjusted by FIFA.[^cite_note-67] The competition was organised so that teams that played each other in the group stage could not meet again during the knockout phase until the final (or the match for third place).[^cite_note-regulations-33] The group stage began on 12 June, with the host nation competing in the opening game as has been the format since the 2006 tournament. The opening game was preceded by an [opening ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_opening_ceremony) that began at 15\:15 local time.[^cite_note-68]

## Opening ceremony



From left to right: [Claudia Leitte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Leitte), [Pitbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitbull_\(rapper\)), and [Jennifer Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Lopez) performing at the opening ceremony at the [Arena de São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Corinthians), [São Paulo](390875.md).

Main article: [2014 FIFA World Cup opening ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_opening_ceremony)

On 12 June 2014, the 20th edition of the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) began with the opening ceremony at [Arena de São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Corinthians), [São Paulo](390875.md), Brazil. The event saw 660 dancers take to the stadium and perform in a ceremony which celebrated the nature of the country and its love of football. Following the dancers native singer [Claudia Leitte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Leitte) emerged on centre stage to perform for the crowd. She was later joined by Cuban-American rapper [Pitbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitbull_\(rapper\)), and American singer [Jennifer Lopez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Lopez) to perform the tournament's official song "[We Are One (Ole Ola)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Are_One_\(Ole_Ola\))" which had been released as an official single on 8 April 2014. Following the ceremony, the opening match was played, which saw the hosts come from behind to beat Croatia 3–1.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

## Group stage

The group stage of the cup took place in [Brazil](3383.md) from 12 June 2014 to 26 June 2014: each team played three games. The group stage was notable for a scarcity of draws and a large number of goals. The first drawn (and goalless) match did not occur until the 13th match of the tournament, between Iran and Nigeria: a drought longer than any World Cup since 1930.[^cite_note-72] The group stage produced a total of 136 goals (an average of 2.83 goals per match), nine fewer than were scored during the entire 2010 tournament.[^cite_note-73] This is the largest number of goals in the group stage since the 32-team system was implemented in 1998[^cite_note-74] and the largest average in a group stage since 1958.[^cite_note-75] World Cup holders Spain were eliminated after only two games, the quickest exit for the defending champions since Italy's from the [1950 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-76] Spain also became the fourth nation to be eliminated in the first round while holding the World Cup crown, the first one being Italy in 1950 (and again in 2010), Brazil in 1966, and France in 2002.[^cite_note-77] For the first time, two teams from Africa advanced to the second round, a feat that would be repeated in the [2022 tournament](17742072.md).

### Round of 16

All the group winners advanced into the quarter-finals. They included four teams from UEFA, three from CONMEBOL, and one from CONCACAF. Of the eight matches, five required extra-time, and two of these [required penalty shoot-outs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_World_Cup_penalty_shoot-outs); this was the first time penalty shoot-outs occurred in more than one game in a round of 16.[^cite_note-78] The goal average per game in the round of 16 was 2.25, a drop of 0.58 goals per game from the group stage.[^cite_note-79] The eight teams to win in the round of 16 included four former champions (Brazil, Germany, Argentina and France), a three-time runner-up (Netherlands), and two first-time quarter-finalists (Colombia and Costa Rica).[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] Belgium reached the quarter-finals for the first time since 1986.[^cite_note-82]

Germany qualified for the final for the eighth time with a historic 7–1 win over Brazil – the biggest defeat in Brazilian football since 1920. ### Discipline

The most notable disciplinary case was that of Uruguayan striker [Luis Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez_\(Uruguayan_footballer\)), who was suspended for nine international matches and banned from taking part in any football-related activity (including entering any stadium) for four months, following a [biting incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2014_FIFA_World_Cup_controversies#Uruguay_vs_Italy) on Italian defender [Giorgio Chiellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Chiellini). He was also fined [CHF](361362.md)100,000.[^cite_note-Suarez_suspension-106][^cite_note-Ban2014-107][^cite_note-Luis_Suarez_suspended-108] After an appeal to the [Court of Arbitration for Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Arbitration_for_Sport), Suárez was later allowed to participate in training and friendly matches with his new club [Barcelona](68187.md).[^cite_note-109]

A player was automatically suspended for the next match for the following offences:

- Receiving a red card (red card suspensions could be extended for serious offences)

- Receiving two yellow cards in two matches; yellow cards expired after the completion of the quarter-finals (yellow card suspensions were not carried forward to any other future international matches)

### Prize money

The total prize money on offer for the tournament was confirmed by FIFA as [US](18717338.md)\$576 million (including payments of \$70 million to domestic clubs and \$100 million as player insurances), a 37 percent increase from the amount [allocated in the 2010 tournament](168079.md#Prize_money). Before the tournament, each of the 32 entrants received \$1.5 million for preparation costs. At the tournament, the prize money was distributed as follows:[^cite_note-autogenerated1-122]

- \$8 million – To each team eliminated at the [group stage](#Group_stage) (16 teams)

- \$9 million – To each team eliminated in the [round of 16](#Round_of_16) (8 teams)

- \$14 million – To each team eliminated in the [quarter-finals](#Quarter-finals) (4 teams)

- \$20 million – [Fourth placed team](#Match_for_third_place)

- \$22 million – [Third placed team](#Match_for_third_place)

- \$25 million – [Runner-up](#Final)

- \$35 million – [Winner](#Final)

## Preparations and costs

Main article: [2014 FIFA World Cup preparations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_preparations)



The Estádio Nacional in [Brasília](4752.md) during its rebuild. The project was completed at a cost of US\$900 million, against an original budget of US\$300 million, making the stadium the second-most expensive football stadium in the world after England's [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md).

Costs of the tournament totalled \$11.6 billion,[^cite_note-rbc.ru-124] making it the most expensive World Cup[^cite_note-125] until it was surpassed by [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md) which cost an estimated \$14.2 billion.[^cite_note-rbc.ru-124] FIFA was expected to spend US\$2 billion on staging the finals,[^cite_note-126] with its greatest single expense being the [US\$576 million prize money pot](#Prize_money).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-122]

Although organisers originally estimated costs of US\$1.1 billion,[^cite_note-Inspection_report-127] a reported US\$3.6 billion was ultimately spent on stadium works.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] Five of the chosen host cities had brand new venues built specifically for the World Cup, while the [Estádio Nacional Mané Garrincha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Nacional_Man%C3%A9_Garrincha) in the capital [Brasília](4752.md) was demolished and rebuilt, with the remaining six being extensively renovated.[^cite_note-130]

An additional R\$3 billion (US\$1.3 billion, €960 million, £780 million at June 2014 rates) was earmarked by the Brazilian government for investment in infrastructure works and projects for use during the 2014 World Cup and beyond.[^cite_note-131] However, the failed completion of many of the proposed works provoked discontent among some Brazilians.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

The Brazilian government pledged US\$900 million to be invested into security forces and that the tournament would be "one of the most protected sports events in history."[^cite_note-CNN-135]

The [Sony Xperia Z2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Xperia_Z2) was dubbed the "official [smartphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone) of the 2014 FIFA World Cup".[^cite_note-138]

## Media

See also: [2014 FIFA World Cup broadcasting rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_broadcasting_rights)

For a fourth consecutive FIFA World Cup Finals, the coverage was provided by HBS (Host Broadcast Services), a subsidiary of [Infront Sports & Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infront_Sports_&_Media).[^cite_note-143] [Sony](26989.md) was selected as the official equipment provider and built 12 bespoke [high definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_video) production 40-foot-long containers, one for each tournament venue, to house the extensive amount of equipment required.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-redsharknews1-145] Each match utilised 37 standard camera plans, including Aerial and Cablecam, two Ultramotion cameras and dedicated cameras for interviews.[^cite_note-redsharknews1-145] [The official tournament film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_official_FIFA_World_Cup_films), as well as three matches,[^cite_note-146] will be filmed with [ultra high definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_high_definition_television) technology ([4K resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4K_resolution)), following a successful trial at the [2013 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup).[^cite_note-147]

The broadcasting rights – covering television, radio, internet and mobile coverage – for the tournament were sold to media companies in each individual territory either directly by FIFA, or through licensed companies or organisations such as the [European Broadcasting Union](10380.md), [Organización de Televisión Iberoamericana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizaci%C3%B3n_de_Televisi%C3%B3n_Iberoamericana), International Media Content, [Dentsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dentsu) and RS International Broadcasting & Sports Management.[^cite_note-148] The sale of these rights accounted for an estimated 60% of FIFA's income from staging a World Cup.[^cite_note-149] The International Broadcast Centre was situated at the [Riocentro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riocentro) in the [Barra da Tijuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barra_da_Tijuca,_Rio_de_Janeiro) neighbourhood of Rio de Janeiro.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151]

Worldwide, several games qualified as the most-watched sporting events in their country in 2014, including 42.9 million people in Brazil for the opening game between Brazil and Croatia, the 34.1 million in Japan who saw their team play Ivory Coast, and 34.7 million in Germany who saw their national team win the World Cup against Argentina,[^cite_note-152] while the 24.7 million viewers during the game between the US and Portugal is joint with the [2010 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) as the most-watched football game in the United States.[^cite_note-153] According to FIFA, over 1 billion people tuned in worldwide to watch the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) between Germany and Argentina.[^cite_note-154]

## Controversies

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of 2014 FIFA World Cup controversies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2014_FIFA_World_Cup_controversies).

The 2014 FIFA World Cup generated various controversies, including demonstrations, some of which took place even before the tournament started. Furthermore, there were various issues with safety, including the death of eight workers and a fire during construction, breaches into stadiums, an unstable makeshift staircase at the [Maracanã Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracan%C3%A3_Stadium), a [monorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monorail) collapse, and the [collapse of an unfinished overpass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belo_Horizonte_overpass_collapse) in [Belo Horizonte](82198.md).[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158][^cite_note-Monorail_collapses_BBC-159] The houses of thousands of families living in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md)’s [slums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favela) were cleared for redevelopments for the World Cup in spite of protests and resistance. Favela do Metrô, near the Maracanã Stadium, was completely destroyed as a result, having previously housed 700 families in 2010.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162]

### Protests



Anti-World Cup demonstration on the opening day; "Não vai ter Copa" ("There will be no \[World] Cup") was a common slogan from protesters

Further information: [List of 2014 FIFA World Cup controversies § Protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2014_FIFA_World_Cup_controversies#Protests)

See also: [2013 protests in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_protests_in_Brazil) and [2014 protests in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_protests_in_Brazil)

Prior to the opening ceremony of the [2013 FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) staged in Brazil, demonstrations took place outside [the venue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Nacional_Man%C3%A9_Garrincha), organised by people unhappy with the amount of public money spent to enable the hosting of the FIFA World Cup.[^cite_note-Confederations_Cup_opening_protests-163] Both the [Brazilian president](273638.md) [Dilma Rousseff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilma_Rousseff) and FIFA president [Sepp Blatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepp_Blatter) were heavily booed as they were announced to give their speeches at the 2013 tournament's opening,[^cite_note-164] which resulted in FIFA announcing that the [2014 FIFA World Cup opening ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_opening_ceremony) would not feature any speeches.[^cite_note-165] Further protests took place during the Confederations Cup as well as prior to and during the World Cup.[^cite_note-Confederations_Cup_protests1-166][^cite_note-Confederations_Cup_protests2-167][^cite_note-World_Cup_protests1-168][^cite_note-World_Cup_protests2-169][^cite_note-World_Cup_protests3-170]

### Breaches into stadiums

Further information: [List of 2014 FIFA World Cup controversies § Breaches into stadiums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2014_FIFA_World_Cup_controversies#Breaches_into_stadiums)

At the [Group B match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_B#Spain_vs_Chile) between [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_men's_national_football_team) and [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team), around 100 Chilean supporters who had gathered outside [Maracanã Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracan%C3%A3_Stadium) forced their way into the stadium and caused damage to the media centre. Military police reported that 85 Chileans were detained during the events, while others reached the stands. Earlier, about 20 Argentinians made a similar breach during Argentina's [Group F game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_F#Argentina_vs_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) against Bosnia and Herzegovina at the same stadium.[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]

### Bridge collapse

Main article: [Belo Horizonte overpass collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belo_Horizonte_overpass_collapse)

On 3 July 2014, an [overpass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overpass) under construction in [Belo Horizonte](82198.md) as part of the World Cup [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure) projects collapsed onto a busy [carriageway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carriageway) below, leaving two people dead and 22 others injured.[^cite_note-Overpass_collapses_BBC-173][^cite_note-Overpass_collapses_Reuters-174]

### Head injuries

During the tournament, FIFA received significant criticism for the way head injuries are handled during matches. Two incidents in particular attracted the most attention. First, in a group stage match, after Uruguayan defender [Álvaro Pereira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_Pereira) received a blow to the head, he lay unconscious.[^cite_note-175] The Uruguayan doctor signalled for the player to be substituted, but he returned to the match. The incident drew criticism from the professional players' union FIFPro, and from Michel D'Hooghe, a member of the FIFA executive board and chairman of its medical committee.[^cite_note-176]

Second, in the Final, German midfielder [Christoph Kramer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Kramer) received a blow to the head from a collision in the 14th minute, but returned to the match before collapsing in the 31st minute. During that time, Kramer was disoriented and confused, and asked the referee [Nicola Rizzoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Rizzoli) whether the match he was playing was in the World Cup Final.[^cite_note-177]
