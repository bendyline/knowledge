A [by-election](155434.md) was held on 9 May 2026 to elect the Australian [member of Parliament](61565.md) (MP) for the [New South Wales](21654.md) division of [Farrer](1078111.md). [One Nation](83013.md) candidate [David Farley](83140164.md) was elected, defeating independent Michelle Milthorpe. The election was triggered by the resignation of former opposition leader and [Liberal](18453.md) leader [Sussan Ley](577186.md).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

It was the first by-election since the [2025 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_federal_election), the first during [Angus Taylor](40520884.md)'s leadership of the Liberals, the first during [Matt Canavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Canavan)'s leadership of the Nationals, and the fifth during [Anthony Albanese](556745.md)'s tenure as prime minister. It was also the first time a [One Nation](83013.md) candidate was elected to the House of Representatives.

## Background

### Resignation

A [leadership spill](82390513.md) of the Liberal party room was held on 13 February 2026, where [Sussan Ley](577186.md) was defeated by [Angus Taylor](40520884.md) 34 votes to 17 for the leadership of the party.[^cite_note-4] Following the defeat, Ley fronted a press conference where she announced that she would resign from parliament "in the next couple of weeks".[^cite_note-5]

On 27 February 2026, Ley officially submitted her resignation.[^cite_note-6] On 5 March 2026, the [Speaker of the House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Australian_House_of_Representatives), [Milton Dick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Dick), announced the by-election would be held on 9 May 2026.[^cite_note-7]

### Seat details

Farrer is a rural electorate located in the far southwestern area of [New South Wales](21654.md) and includes the towns of [Albury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albury), [Corowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corowa), [Narrandera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrandera), [Leeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeton,_New_South_Wales), [Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffith,_New_South_Wales), [Deniliquin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deniliquin), [Hay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hay,_New_South_Wales), [Balranald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balranald) and [Wentworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wentworth,_New_South_Wales). A traditionally conservative seat, the seat has historically been a safe Liberal seat since Ley first won it in [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Australian_federal_election), though the Liberal Party suffered a 10% swing against it on the [two-candidate preferred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-party-preferred_vote) vote against an independent candidate in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_federal_election), and won the seat on a reduced margin of 56.19% to 43.81%.[^cite_note-8]

### Liberal

On 23 February, *[The Australian](396952.md)* reported that "the [right faction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Right_\(Liberal_Party_of_Australia\)) of the Liberal Party has gained control of candidate preselection in a bid to stem the leakage of votes to One Nation". State Liberal MP [Justin Clancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Clancy) publicly expressed interest in running as the Liberal candidate; shortly after taking office as leader of the Liberal Party [Angus Taylor](40520884.md) called Clancy to discuss the by-election.[^cite_note-tealsboost-27] On 27 February, Justin Clancy ruled out contesting Farrer. Nominations for Liberal preselection in Farrer closed on 2 March.[^cite_note-28] Media reported former [Sussan Ley](577186.md) staffer Lachlan McIntyre, and [Albury councillor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Albury) and lawyer Raissa Butkowski as candidates.[^cite_note-29] The Liberal Party held their preselection vote on 15 March.[^cite_note-30]

On 15 March, the Liberal Party preselected lawyer for the Hume Riverina Community Legal Service and [City of Albury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Albury) councillor Raissa Butkowski to contest the by-election. Butkowski is also a member of the Murray-Darling Association, an organisation that advocates for local government and water issues.[^cite_note-31]

### National

The [National Party](21927.md) will contest Farrer for the first time since losing the seat in [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Australian_federal_election), since [Coalition](1051313.md) rules in New South Wales forbid parties from running a candidate in a sitting Coalition MP's seat.[^cite_note-ag13feb-32] Media reported former Nationals senator [Perin Davey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perin_Davey), Albury mayor Kevin Mack, the party's branch chairman Brad Robertson and Murray Irrigation Ltd director Gabrielle Coupland as potential candidates.[^cite_note-tealsboost-27][^cite_note-heatsup-18] Marty Corboy, the Nationals candidate for neighbouring [Indi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Indi) in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Australian_federal_election), endorsed Perin Davey.[^cite_note-33] The Nationals selected Robertson as their candidate on 8 March.[^cite_note-robertsonselected-17] Robertson is an [Australian Army](2795.md) veteran and former commander.[^cite_note-robertsonselected-17]

### One Nation

On 13 February, party leader [Pauline Hanson](69926.md) announced that [One Nation](83013.md) will contest the by-election.[^cite_note-34] On 27 February, One Nation announced that David Farley, Leigh Wolki and Guy Cooper were the three candidates seeking pre-selection, following a process that assessed 80 candidates.[^cite_note-35] On 7 March, David Farley won preselection as the One Nation candidate.[^cite_note-\:0-22] Hanson said it was the first time a branch had selected its own candidate.[^cite_note-farleyannounced-36]

Farley is a [Narrandera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrandera)-based agricultural businessman. He was formerly the CEO of [Australian Agricultural Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Agricultural_Company).[^cite_note-farleyannounced-36] He was recently elected chair of the lobby group Speak Up 4 Water. He became a member of One Nation before Sussan Ley resigned.[^cite_note-heatsup-18]

### Independent (Milthorpe)

Michelle Milthorpe was an independent candidate in the 2025 election and was supported by the community group [Voices of Farrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voices_movement_\(Australia\)) and the [Climate 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_200) fundraising organisation.[^cite_note-37] On 13 February, she announced her intention to contest the by-election.[^cite_note-\:1-14] She is also supported by Alasdair Macleod, who is married to [Rupert Murdoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Murdoch)'s daughter [Prudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudence_Murdoch). His agribusiness Macdoch invests in [regenerative agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regenerative_agriculture) and other methods which mitigate the effects of [climate change](5042951.md).[^cite_note-38] Milthorpe is a teacher from [Jindera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jindera) and an advocate for child abuse victims.[^cite_note-milthorpeadvocate-15]

### Other candidates

[Family First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_First_Party_\(2021\)) ran Rebecca Scriven, an independent [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Australia) school worker who ran for the party in 2025.[^cite_note-FFP-23]

The [Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Greens) contested the by-election.[^cite_note-39] They ran Richard Hendrie, a mental health worker who ran for the party in 2025.[^cite_note-Greens-20]

On 14 February, [Gerard Rennick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Rennick) announced that [People First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerard_Rennick_People_First) would contest the by-election.[^cite_note-40] The party endorsed Jamie Bonnefin, an architect and the president of its NSW division.[^cite_note-41]

The [Sustainable Australia Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Australia_Party) contested Farrer for the first time since [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Australian_federal_election).[^cite_note-SAP-24]

Gary Pappin ran as an [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_politicians_in_Australia).[^cite_note-Pappin1-25]

[Legalise Cannabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legalise_Cannabis_Australia) candidate Aimee Pearson, [Shooters, Fishers and Farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooters,_Fishers_and_Farmers_Party) candidate Peter Sinclair and independent Roger Woodward were announced as candidates at the declaration of nominations.[^cite_note-42]

### Not contesting

The governing [Australian Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party) opted not to field a candidate in the by-election. Initially, Prime Minister [Anthony Albanese](556745.md) said the party would decide whether to contest the by-election "once Sussan Ley actually resigns,"[^cite_note-43] however on 13 March 2026 the party's national executive met and decided against running a candidate. Speaking on condition of anonymity, a member of the executive reportedly said "we don’t want to create a disturbance or dilute the Coalition’s anti-One Nation vote."[^cite_note-44]

[Helen Dalton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Dalton), independent state MP for the electoral district of [Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district_of_Murray), was viewed as a potential challenger for the seat, with Dalton claiming that her mobile phone had "been burning up" since Ley's resignation.[^cite_note-45] Dalton was also approached to run for the seat as a One Nation candidate.[^cite_note-46] On 5 March, Dalton confirmed that she would not be a candidate.[^cite_note-daltonnotcontesting-47]

Darren De Bortoli, the managing director of [De Bortoli Wines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Bortoli_Wines), said he was approached to run by multiple parties but declined all offers.[^cite_note-48]

Jordi Queiruga planned to run as an independent, but did not contest the seat.[^cite_note-49]

## Campaign



[Lake Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Hume), a component of the [Murray-Darling basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray-Darling_basin) in [Albury-Wodonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albury-Wodonga)

The campaign primarily focused on local issues specific to the Farrer region, especially water management in the [Murray-Darling basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray-Darling_basin), regional [healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare) and [cost of living](522934.md).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

The media resurfaced comments made by One Nation candidate David Farley in 2012 where he likened then–Prime Minister [Julia Gillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Gillard) to a "non-productive old cow". Farley and Pauline Hanson described the comments as [tongue-in-cheek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongue-in-cheek).[^cite_note-53] Farley also provoked controversy and criticism for describing the crime in [Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffith,_New_South_Wales), a city in Farrer, as "hectic"[^cite_note-54] and for sharing [suggestive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_suggestiveness) content on social media from [OnlyFans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OnlyFans) creators, which he defended as accidental.[^cite_note-55]

[The Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal-National_coalition) launched attack advertisements against opponents David Farley and Michelle Milthorpe. They criticised One Nation for having "no solutions",[^cite_note-56] and further criticised Farley for running for [preselection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preselection) to be a [Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party) candidate in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_federal_election) and for having donated to them.[^cite_note-57] They also released advertisements dubbing Milthorpe a "[teal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teal_independent)".[^cite_note-58]

[Progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism) lobby group [GetUp!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GetUp!) had raised \$400,000 for its anti–One Nation campaign by 22 April, and intended to spend at least \$600,000 attacking the party by the close of polling.[^cite_note-59]

### Debates and forums

A debate was organised by the [New South Wales Farmers' Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Farmers'_Federation) and held at the [Deniliquin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deniliquin) RSL club on 21 April. It was attended by seven of the twelve candidates and moderated by Harold Clapham.[^cite_note-60]

An [anti-abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-abortion) forum organised by Dr [Joanna Howe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_Howe) was held in Albury on 24 April. It was attended by the One Nation, Family First, and Nationals candidates as well as [Senator](165705.md) [Matt Canavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Canavan), though all candidates were reportedly invited. The event was criticised by state [MLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_South_Wales_Legislative_Council) [Amanda Cohn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Cohn), and the three candidates who attended were all endorsed by Howe.[^cite_note-Howe-61]

[Charles Sturt University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Sturt_University) organised a debate for 30 April, moderated by political journalist [Barrie Cassidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrie_Cassidy). Nine of the 12 candidates were in attendance.[^cite_note-62]

## How-to-vote cards

Political parties recommend to voters how they should [preference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preferential_voting) candidates through "[how-to-vote cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How-to-vote_card)" distributed by campaign volunteers near polling places. Parties often make agreements between themselves about these recommendations. Preferences were expected to play a key role in the by-election.[^cite_note-73]

The Liberals recommended preferences be directed to the Nationals second, and the Nationals recommended preferences be directed to the Liberals second. On 5 March, Liberal frontbencher [Andrew Bragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Bragg) publicly urged his party to preference One Nation last,[^cite_note-74] however the Liberals ultimately preferenced One Nation fourth.[^cite_note-LibPref-75]

Michelle Milthorpe did not ask voters to direct preferences, just as she did not at [the previous election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_federal_election).[^cite_note-tealsboost-27]

[Family First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_First_Party_\(2021\)) said it would not give its second preferences to One Nation because of Farley's view of women.[^cite_note-76]

Legalise Cannabis ran an open ticket, with no directions for further preferences beyond a number "1" for their candidate.[^cite_note-Legalise_Cannabis-77]

## Aftermath

The by-election marked One Nation's first ever elected candidate in the [House of Representatives](61565.md), with previous MPs having defected to the party whilst sitting in Parliament.[^cite_note-90] With the by-election following months of growing support for the party in [opinion polling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_for_the_next_Australian_federal_election), Hanson celebrated the victory and declared that One Nation was "coming after those other seats".[^cite_note-\:2-91] Milthorpe thanked her supporters, and Taylor stated that the Liberals would take "hard lessons" from the by-election.[^cite_note-\:2-91]

The by-election was interpreted as demonstrating the risk One Nation poses to the Liberal and National parties.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] Reasons for One Nation's success in Farrer were described as including growing concerns regarding immigration and the [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_economy), and distrust in politicians.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-\:3-95] Concerns were raised about the ability of the Coalition parties to remain electorally viable in regional areas.[^cite_note-\:3-95]

In the months following the by-election, Farley began to articulate policy positions away from stated One Nation policy. In September 2026, Farley stated on [*Insiders*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insiders_\(Australian_TV_program\)) that at least 100,000 additional arrivals were needed, on top of One Nation's stated maximum annual net overseas migration of 130,000 people, in order to meet demand for labour in regional areas. This comment caused condemnation from senior One Nation figures, including [James Ashby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Ashby) and [Barnaby Joyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnaby_Joyce). At that time, it was reported that Farley was struggling to develop a working relationship with Joyce, and was poised to leave One Nation.[^cite_note-96]
