# Refinery

For the restaurant, see [The Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Refinery).



Shell Haven Oil Refinery

A **refinery** is a production facility composed of a group of [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering) [unit processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_processes) and [unit operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_operation) [refining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refining) certain materials or converting [raw material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raw_material) into products of value.

## Types of refineries

Different types of refineries are as follows:

- [Petroleum oil refinery](195137.md), which converts [crude oil](23195.md) into [high-octane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octane_rating) motor spirit ([gasoline](23639.md)/petrol), [diesel oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_oil), [liquefied petroleum gases](361384.md) (LPG), [kerosene](16992.md), heating [fuel oils](392547.md), hexane, [lubricating oils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubricating_oil), [bitumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitumen), and [petroleum coke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_coke)

- [Edible oil refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_oil#Refinement) which converts [cooking oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_oil) into a product that is uniform in taste, smell and appearance, and stability

- [Natural gas processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_processing) plant, which purifies and converts raw natural gas into residential, commercial and industrial fuel gas, and also recovers [natural gas liquids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_liquids) (NGL) such as [ethane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethane), [propane](23643.md), [butanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butane) and [pentanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentane)

- [Sugar refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_refinery), which converts [sugar](27712.md) cane and sugar beets into crystallized sugar and sugar syrups

- [Salt refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_refinery?action=edit\&redlink=1), which cleans common salt ([NaCl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_chloride)), produced by the [solar evaporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_chloride#Production_and_use) of sea water, followed by washing and [re-crystallization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallization)

- Metal refineries refining metals such as [alumina](141888.md), [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper), [gold](12240.md), [lead](17747.md), [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel), [silver](27119.md), [uranium](31743.md), [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), [magnesium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesium) and [cobalt](24580536.md)

- Iron refining, a stage of refining [pig iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_iron) (typically grey cast iron to white cast iron), before [fining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finery_forge), which converts pig iron into bar iron or steel

### Sugar refining

Sugar is generally produced from [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane) or [sugar beets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_beet). As the global production of sugar from sugarcane is at least twice the production from sugar beets, this section focuses on sugarcane.[^cite_note-Ullmann-12]

#### Milling

Main article: [Sugar mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_mill)



Harvested sugar cane ready for processing

Sugarcane is traditionally refined into sugar in two stages. In the first stage, raw sugar is produced by the milling of harvested sugarcane. In a sugar mill, sugarcane is washed, chopped, and shredded by revolving knives. The shredded cane is mixed with water and crushed. The juices (containing 10-15 percent [sucrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucrose)) are collected and mixed with [lime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lime_\(mineral\)) to adjust [pH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PH) to 7, prevent decay into [glucose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose) and [fructose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fructose), and precipitate impurities. The lime and other suspended solids are settled out, and the clarified juice is concentrated in a [multiple-effect evaporator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple-effect_evaporator) to make a syrup with about 60 weight percent sucrose. The syrup is further concentrated under [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum) until it becomes [supersaturated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersaturation) and is then seeded with [crystalline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystalline) sugar. Upon cooling, sugar crystallizes out of the syrup. [Centrifuging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrifuge) then separates the sugar from the remaining liquid (molasses). Raw sugar has a yellow to brown color. Sugar is sometimes consumed locally at this stage but usually undergoes further purification.[^cite_note-13] [Sulfur dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide) is bubbled through the cane juice subsequent to crystallization in a process known as "sulfitation". This process inhibits color forming reactions and stabilizes the sugar juices to produce "mill white" or "plantation white" sugar.

The fibrous solids, called *[bagasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagasse)*, remaining after the crushing of the shredded sugarcane are burned for fuel which helps a sugar mill to become self-sufficient in energy. Any excess bagasse can be used for animal feed, to produce paper, or burned to generate electricity for the local power grid.

#### Refining

Main article: [Sugar refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_refinery)



Sugar refinery in [Arabi, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabi,_Louisiana), United States

The second stage is often executed in heavy sugar-consuming regions such as [North America](21139.md), [Europe](9239.md), and [Japan](15573.md). In the second stage, white sugar is produced that is more than 99 percent pure [sucrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sucrose). In such refineries, raw sugar is further purified by [fractional crystallization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_crystallization_\(chemistry\)).
