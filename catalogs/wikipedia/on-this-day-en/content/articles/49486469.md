**Al-Masirah** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): المسيرة *al-Masirah*, which means "The Journey") is a [Yemeni](350939.md) TV channel owned by the [Houthi movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_movement) which was launched in 2012.[^cite_note-launch-1] It is headquartered in [Beirut](37428.md), [Lebanon](17771.md).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-AW-3][^cite_note-4]

## History

The President of the board of Al-Masirah is Mohammed Abdulsalam who is also Houthis official spokesman and their chief negotiator.[^cite_note-5]

Al-Masirah was founded by the Houthi movement in January 2012 in [Beirut](37428.md), Lebanon and is located next to [Hezbollah](13919.md)’s [Al Manar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Manar) TV with backup studios at Hezbollah headquarters.[^cite_note-AW-3] The channel launched its first test broadcast on 23 March 2012, on the [Nilesat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilesat) satellite.[^cite_note-6]

## Channel frequency during Saudi-led coalition on Yemen

On 10 May 2015, Al-Masirah, along with other anti-Saudi channels, were closed on Nile Sat & Euro Sat[^cite_note-7] due to Saudi pressure on the satellite companies, which made Al-Masirah broadcast its signal instead on the Russian satellite [Express AM44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekspress-AM4).[^cite_note-8] After several months of being banned on Nile Sat, broadcast is now online on Nile Sat.

## Killed journalists and media workers

After the [Houthi takeover in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_takeover_in_Yemen), Al-Masirah lost a number of employees due to conflict.

- On 4 January 2015, Al-Masirah Journalist Khaled al-Washli was killed by an exploding bomb as he covered attempts to defuse it.

- On 17 September 2015, Bilal Sharaf al-Deen was covering an airstrike, when he was killed by a following airstrike.

  [^cite_note-12]

- On 21 January 2016, 17-year-old TV cameraman Hashem al-Hamran was mortally injured by an air-strike by the Saudi-led coalition in the city of Dahian in [Saada Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada_Governorate), when he was filming bombing raids for al-Masirah. He died from his wounds on 22 January 2016.

## U.S. seizure of online sites

On June 22, 2021, United States law enforcement agencies seized a number of domains associated with Al-Masirah.[^cite_note-16]
