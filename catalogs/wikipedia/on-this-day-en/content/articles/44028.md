The **assistant to the president for national security affairs** (**APNSA**), commonly referred to as the **national security advisor** (**NSA**),[^cite_note-TNSAaS\:1-2][^cite_note-3] is a senior aide in the [Executive Office of the President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Office_of_the_President), based at the [West Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Wing) of the [White House](33057.md).[^cite_note-White_House_appendix-4]

The national security advisor serves as the principal advisor to the [president of the United States](24113.md) on all [national security](240468.md) issues. The national security advisor participates in meetings of the [National Security Council](44026.md) (NSC) and usually chairs meetings of the principals committee of the NSC with the [secretary of state](32293.md) and [secretary of defense](43998.md) (those meetings not attended by the president). The NSA also sits on the [Homeland Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Security_Council) (HSC). The national security advisor is supported by NSC staff who produce classified research and briefings for the national security advisor to review and present, either to the NSC or the president.

The national security advisor is appointed by the president and does not require [confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_and_consent) by the [United States Senate](24909346.md). An appointment of a three- or four-star general to the role requires [Senate confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_confirmation) to maintain that rank in the new position.[^cite_note-\:0-5] The national security advisor has been [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) since May 1, 2025.

## Role

The influence and responsibilities of the national security advisor vary among administrations, depending on factors such as the individual appointed to the position and the president's approach to decision-making and White House management.[^cite_note-TNSAaS\:17-21-6] The national security advisor typically coordinates the presentation of national security policy options to the president and may also play a role in shaping or advocating for particular policies, depending on the administration.[^cite_note-TNSAaS\:10-14-7]

The national security advisor is a staff position in the [Executive Office of the President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Office_of_the_President) and does not have [line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staff_and_line) or budget authority over either the [Department of State](31975.md) or the [Department of Defense](7279897.md), unlike the secretary of state and the secretary of defense, who are Senate-confirmed officials with statutory authority over their departments.[^cite_note-8] The national security advisor is able to offer daily advice (due to the proximity) to the president independently of the vested interests of the large bureaucracies and clientele of those departments.[^cite_note-TNSAaS\:17-21-6]

In times of crisis, the national security advisor is likely to operate from the [White House Situation Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Situation_Room) or the [Presidential Emergency Operations Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Emergency_Operations_Center) (as on [September 11, 2001](5058690.md)),[^cite_note-clarke18-9] updating the president on the latest events in a crisis situation.

## History



President [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) meets in the [Oval Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Office) with his NSC [Brent Scowcroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brent_Scowcroft) about [Operation Desert Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Shield), 1991. Dick Cheney and Colin Powell are also present in the room.

The immediate predecessor to the National Security Council was the [National Intelligence Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Intelligence_Authority_\(United_States\)) (NIA), which was established by President [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman)'s Executive Letter of January 22, 1946, to oversee the Central Intelligence Group, the CIA's predecessor. The NIA was composed of the [secretary of state](32293.md) ([James F. Byrnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_F._Byrnes) and [George C. Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_C._Marshall)), the [secretary of war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_War) ([Robert P. Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_P._Patterson)), the [secretary of the Navy](44001.md) ([James Forrestal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Forrestal)), and a personal representative of President Truman ([William D. Leahy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_D._Leahy), the [chief of staff to the commander in chief](364458.md)).[^cite_note-auto-10]

The National Security Council was created at the start of the [Cold War](325329.md) under the [National Security Act of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947) to coordinate defense, foreign affairs, international economic policy, and intelligence; this was part of a large reorganization that saw the creation of the Department of Defense and the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md).[^cite_note-george-11][^cite_note-schmitz-12] The Act did not create the position of the national security advisor per se, but it did create an executive secretary in charge of the staff. In 1949, the NSC became part of the Executive Office of the President.[^cite_note-george-11]

[Robert Cutler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Cutler) was the first national security advisor in 1953, and held the job twice, both times during the [Eisenhower administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower). The system has remained largely unchanged since then, particularly since President [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy), with powerful national security advisors and strong staff but a lower importance given to formal NSC meetings. This continuity persists despite the tendency of each new president to replace the advisor and senior NSC staff.[^cite_note-george-11]

President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon)'s national security advisor, [Henry Kissinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Kissinger), enhanced the importance of the role, controlling the flow of information to the president and meeting with him multiple times per day. Kissinger also holds the distinction of serving as national security advisor and secretary of state at the same time from September 22, 1973, until November 3, 1975.[^cite_note-george-11][^cite_note-schmitz-12] He holds the record for longest term of service (2,478 days); [Michael Flynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Flynn) holds the record for shortest term, at just 24 days.

Brent Scowcroft held the job in two non-consecutive administrations: the [Ford administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_administration) and the [George H. W. Bush administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_H._W._Bush).
