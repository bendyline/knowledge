**Bosnia and Herzegovina**,[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] often referred to as **Bosnia-Herzegovina** or simply **Bosnia**, is a country in [Southeastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_Europe). Situated on the [Balkan Peninsula](4829.md), it borders [Serbia](29265.md) to the east, [Montenegro](20760.md) to the southeast, and [Croatia](5573.md) to the north and southwest, with a 20-kilometre-long (12-mile) coast on the [Adriatic Sea](23275478.md) in the south. [Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_\(region\)) has a moderate [continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate) with hot summers and cold, snowy winters. Its geography is largely mountainous, particularly in the central and eastern regions, which are dominated by the [Dinaric Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinaric_Alps). [Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzegovina), the smaller, southern region, has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) and is mostly mountainous. [Sarajevo](26786.md) is the capital and the largest city.

The area has been inhabited since at least the [Upper Palaeolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Palaeolithic), with permanent human settlement traced to the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) cultures of [Butmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butmir_culture), [Kakanj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakanj_culture), and [Vučedol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vu%C4%8Dedol_culture). After the arrival of the first [Indo-Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-Europeans), the area was populated by several [Illyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyria) and [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) civilisations. Most of modern Bosnia was incorporated into the [Roman province of Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia_\(Roman_province\)) by the mid-first century BCE. The [ancestors of the modern South Slavic peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Slavs) arrived between the sixth and ninth centuries. In the 12th century, the [Banate of Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banate_of_Bosnia) was established as the first independent Bosnian [polity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polity).[^cite_note-yw-20] It gradually evolved and expanded into the [Kingdom of Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bosnia), which became the most powerful state in the western Balkans by the 14th century.[^cite_note-yw-20] The [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) [annexed the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) in 1463 and introduced [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina). From the late 19th century until [World War I](4764461.md), the country was under [Austro-Hungarian rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_rule_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina). In the [interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period), Bosnia and Herzegovina was part of the [Kingdom of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia). After [World War II](32927.md), it was granted full republic status in the newly formed [Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia). In 1992, following the [breakup of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_of_Yugoslavia), the republic [proclaimed independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Bosnian_independence_referendum). This was followed by the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War), which lasted until late 1995 and ended with the signing of the [Dayton Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement).

Bosnia and Herzegovina has around 3.4 million inhabitants, composed chiefly of [three main ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina): [Bosniaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniaks), who form one half of the population, followed by [Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) at one-third and [Croats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croats_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) at one-sixth; minorities include [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), [Albanians, Montenegrins, Ukrainians and Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_minorities_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), who are among 17 recognized "national minorities". Bosnia and Herzegovina has a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) legislature and a [presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) made up of one member from each of the three major ethnic groups ([directorial system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorial_system)). The central government's power is minimal, as the country is largely decentralised; it consists of two highly autonomous ["entities"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_entities_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)—the [Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and [Republika Srpska](347422.md)—and a third unit, the [Brčko District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%8Dko_District), nominally under joint control, albeit governed by its own local government.&#x20;

Bosnia and Herzegovina is a [developing country](78449.md) and [an upper-middle income economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), dominated by industry and agriculture, followed by tourism and [services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy); tourism has increased significantly in recent years.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] The country has a [social security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_spending) and universal healthcare system, while [primary and secondary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) is free. It is a member of the [United Nations](31769.md), [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe), [OSCE](22796.md), [CEFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Free_Trade_Agreement), and is also a founding member of the [SEECP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-East_European_Cooperation_Process). Bosnia and Herzegovina has been an [EU candidate country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_to_the_European_Union) since 2022, and a [NATO](21133.md) membership candidate since 2010.[^cite_note-23]

## Etymology

The first preserved widely acknowledged mention of a form of the name "[Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_\(region\))" is in *[De Administrando Imperio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Administrando_Imperio)*, a politico-geographical handbook written by the [Byzantine emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Byzantine_emperors) [Constantine VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_VII) in the mid-10th century (between 948 and 952) describing the 'small land' (χωρίον) in [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Greek) of *Bosona* (Βοσώνα).[^cite_note-Moravcsik-153-24]

The name of the land is believed to derive from the name of the river [Bosna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosna_\(river\)) that courses through the Bosnian heartland. According to [philologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philology) Anton Mayer, the name *Bosna* could derive from [Illyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrian_language) \**Bass-an-as*, which in turn could derive from the [Proto-Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language) root *[\*bʰegʷ-](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction\:Proto-Indo-European/b%CA%B0eg%CA%B7-#Proto-Indo-European)*, meaning 'the running water'.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm2002-25]

The name *Herzegovina* means 'herzog's \[land]', and *herzog* derives from the German word for 'duke'.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm200222-26] It originates from the title of a 15th-century Bosnian magnate, [Stjepan Vukčić Kosača](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stjepan_Vuk%C4%8Di%C4%87_Kosa%C4%8Da), who was "Herceg \[[Herzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzog)] of Hum and the Coast" (1448).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFine1994578-27] Hum (formerly called [Zachlumia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zachlumia)) was an early medieval principality that had been conquered by the [Bosnian Banate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_Banate) in the first half of the 14th century. When the Ottomans took over administration of the region, they called it the [Sanjak of Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak_of_Herzegovina) (*Hersek*, هرسك). It was included within the [Bosnia Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_Eyalet) until the formation of the short-lived [Herzegovina Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzegovina_Eyalet) in the 1830s, which reemerged in the 1850s, after which the administrative region became commonly known as *Bosnia and Herzegovina*.[^cite_note-Encyclopedia_Britannica-28]

On initial [proclamation of independence in 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Bosnian_independence_referendum), the country's official name was the [Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), but following the 1995 [Dayton Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement) and the new [constitution](12844303.md) that accompanied it, the official name was changed to Bosnia and Herzegovina.[^cite_note-29]

### Early history

Bosnia has been inhabited since at least the [Paleolithic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic). Notably, the [Badanj Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badanj_Cave) near [Stolac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolac) features one of the oldest known cave engravings, depicting an animal figure believed to be a horse, dating back to approximately 13,000 to 12,000 BC.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] During the [Neolithic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic), significant cultures such as the [Butmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butmir) and [Kakanj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakanj) emerged along the [Bosna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosna_\(river\)). The Butmir culture, flourishing around 5100 to 4500 BC, is renowned for its distinctive ceramics and anthropomorphic figurines. Excavations near [Sarajevo](26786.md) have uncovered intricately decorated pottery and realistic human figurines from this culture.[^cite_note-33]

From the 8th century BCE, Illyrian tribes evolved into kingdoms. The most notable Illyrian kingdoms and dynasties were those of [Bardylis of the Dardani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardylis) and of [Agron of the Ardiaei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agron_of_Illyria) who created the last and best-known Illyrian kingdom. Agron ruled over the Ardiaei and had extended his rule to other tribes as well. From the 7th century BCE, bronze was replaced by iron, after which only jewelry and art objects were still made out of bronze.[^cite_note-geography-34] Illyrian tribes, under the influence of [Hallstatt cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallstatt_culture) to the north, formed regional centers that were slightly different. Parts of Central Bosnia were inhabited by the [Daesitiates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daesitiates) tribe.[^cite_note-geography-34] The Iron Age [Glasinac-Mati culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasinac-Mati_culture) is associated with the [Autariatae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autariatae) tribe.[^cite_note-geography-34]

A very important role in their life was the cult of the dead, which is seen in their careful burials and burial ceremonies, as well as the richness of their burial sites.[^cite_note-geography-34] In the 4th century BCE, the first invasion of [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) was recorded. They brought the technique of the [pottery wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potter's_wheel), new types of fibulas (small clasps resembling a safety pin) and different bronze and iron belts. They passed through en route to Greece.



[Mogorjelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogorjelo), an ancient Roman suburban Villa Rustica from the 4th century, near [Čapljina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Capljina)

In the [Neretva Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neretva_Delta) in the south, there were important [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period) influences of the Illyrian [Daors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daorson) tribe.[^cite_note-35] Their capital was *Daorson* in Ošanići near [Stolac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolac).[^cite_note-36] Daorson, in the 4th century BCE, was surrounded by [megalithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalith), 5 m high stonewalls (as large as those of [Mycenae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenae) in Greece), composed of large trapezoid stone blocks. Daors made unique bronze coins and sculptures.

Conflict between the Illyrians and [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) started in 229 BCE, but Rome did not complete its annexation of the region until AD 9. It was precisely in modern-day Bosnia and Herzegovina that Rome fought one of the most difficult battles in its history since the [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars), as described by the Roman historian [Suetonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suetonius).[^cite_note-37] This was the Roman campaign against [Illyricum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyricum_\(Roman_province\)), known as *[Bellum Batonianum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellum_Batonianum)*.[^cite_note-38] The conflict arose after an attempt to recruit Illyrians, and a revolt spanned for four years (6–9 AD), after which they were subdued.[^cite_note-39] In the Roman period, Latin-speaking settlers from the entire [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) settled among the Illyrians, and Roman soldiers were encouraged to retire in the region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm20023-40]

Following the split of the Empire between 337 and 395 AD, Dalmatia and Pannonia became parts of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire). The region was conquered by the [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths) in 455 AD. It subsequently changed hands between the [Alans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alans) and the [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns). By the 6th century, Emperor [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I) had reconquered the area for the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire). Slavs overwhelmed the Balkans in the 6th and 7th centuries. Illyrian cultural traits were adopted by the South Slavs, as evidenced in certain customs and traditions, and placenames.[^cite_note-41]

[Ledger stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ledger_stone) to [Queen Catherine of Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Catherine_of_Bosnia), the last queen of the [House of Kosača](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Kosa%C4%8Da).

The [Early Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Slavs) raided the Western Balkans, including Bosnia, in the 6th and early 7th century (amid the [Migration Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Period)), and were composed of small tribal units drawn from a single Slavic confederation known to the Byzantines as the *[Sclaveni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sclaveni)* (whilst the related *[Antes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antes_people)*, roughly speaking, colonized the eastern portions of the Balkans).[^cite_note-Columbia_University_Press-42][^cite_note-43] Tribes recorded by the ethnonyms of "Serb" and "Croat" are described as a second, later, migration of different people during the second quarter of the 7th century who could not have been particularly numerous;[^cite_note-Columbia_University_Press-42][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFine199153,_56-44][^cite_note-Heather-45] these early "Serb" and "Croat" tribes, whose exact identity is subject to scholarly debate,[^cite_note-Heather-45] came to predominate over the Slavs in the neighbouring regions. According to Noel Malcolm, the tribal Croats "settled in an area roughly corresponding to modern Croatia, and probably also including most of Bosnia proper, apart from the eastern strip of the Drina valley" while the tribal Serbs settled an area "corresponding to modern south-western Serbia (later known as [Raška](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra%C5%A1ka_\(region\))), and gradually extended their rule into the territories of [Duklja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duklja) and [Hum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zachlumia)".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm20028-46] [John Van Antwerp Fine Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Van_Antwerp_Fine_Jr.), on the other hand, describes the settling of the tribal Croats to involve Croatia, Dalmatia and Western Bosnia, with the rest of Bosnia seemingly being a territory between early Serb and Croat rule.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFine199153(I)-47]

Bosnia is also believed to be first mentioned *as a land (horion Bosona)* in Byzantine Emperor Constantine Porphyrogenitus' *De Administrando Imperio* in the mid 10th century, at the end of a chapter entitled *Of the Serbs and the country in which they now dwell*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBasic2009123-48] This has been interpreted by scholars in several ways and used especially by the Serb national ideologists to prove Bosnia as originally a "Serb" land.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBasic2009123-48] Other scholars have asserted the inclusion of Bosnia in the chapter to merely be the result of Serbian Grand Duke [Časlav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Caslav_of_Serbia)'s temporary rule over Bosnia at the time, while also pointing out Porphyrogenitus does not say anywhere explicitly that Bosnia is a "Serb land".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBasic2009123–28-49] In fact, the very translation of the critical sentence where the word *Bosona* (Bosnia) appears is subject to varying interpretation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBasic2009123-48] In time, Bosnia formed a unit under its own [ruler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulin_Ban), who called himself Bosnian.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFine199153-50] Bosnia, along with other territories, became part of [Duklja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duklja) in the 11th century, although it retained its own nobility and institutions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFine1991223-51]



Bosnia in the Middle Ages spanning the [Banate of Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banate_of_Bosnia) and the succeeding [Kingdom of Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bosnia)

In the [High Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Middle_Ages), political circumstance led to the area being contested between the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) and the Byzantine Empire. Following another shift of power between the two in the early 12th century, Bosnia found itself outside the control of both and emerged as the [Banate of Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banate_of_Bosnia) (under the rule of local *[bans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_\(title\))*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm20029–16-52][^cite_note-53] The first Bosnian ban known by name was [Ban Borić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Bori%C4%87).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFine1991288-54] The second was [Ban Kulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Kulin), whose rule marked the start of a controversy involving the [Bosnian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_Church) – considered heretical by the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church). In response to Hungarian attempts to use church politics regarding the issue as a way to reclaim sovereignty over Bosnia, Kulin held a council of local church leaders to renounce the heresy and embraced Catholicism in 1203. Despite this, Hungarian ambitions remained unchanged long after Kulin's death in 1204, waning only after an unsuccessful invasion in 1254. During this time, the population was called *Dobri [Bošnjani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bo%C5%A1njani)* ("Good Bosnians").[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] The names Serb and Croat, though occasionally appearing in peripheral areas, were not used in Bosnia proper.[^cite_note-lrb.co.uk-57]

Bosnian history from then until the early 14th century was marked by a power struggle between the [Šubić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0ubi%C4%87_family) and [Kotromanić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotromani%C4%87_dynasty) families. This conflict came to an end in 1322, when [Stephen II Kotromanić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_II,_Ban_of_Bosnia) became *Ban*. By the time of his death in 1353, he was successful in annexing territories to the north and west, as well as Zahumlje and parts of Dalmatia. He was succeeded by his ambitious nephew [Tvrtko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tvrtko_I_of_Bosnia) who, following a prolonged struggle with nobility and inter-family strife, gained full control of the country in 1367. By the year 1377, Bosnia was elevated into a kingdom with the coronation of Tvrtko as the first [Bosnian King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_Bosnia) in Mile near [Visoko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visoko_during_the_Middle_Ages) in the Bosnian heartland.[^cite_note-Mile-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

Following his death in 1391, however, Bosnia fell into a long period of decline. The [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) had started its [conquest of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_wars_in_Europe) and posed a major threat to the [Balkans](4829.md) throughout the first half of the 15th century. Finally, after decades of political and social instability, the Kingdom of Bosnia ceased to exist in 1463 after its conquest by the Ottoman Empire.[^cite_note-Encyclopedia_Britannica-28]

There was a general awareness in medieval Bosnia, at least amongst the nobles, that they shared a joint state with Serbia and that they belonged to the same ethnic group. That awareness diminished over time, due to differences in political and social development, but it was kept in Herzegovina and parts of Bosnia which were a part of Serbian state.[^cite_note-61]

### Ottoman Empire

Main articles: [Ottoman conquest of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_conquest_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and [Ottoman Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)



The [Bosnia Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_Eyalet) in 1683

The [Ottoman conquest of Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_conquest_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) marked a new era in the country's history and introduced drastic changes in the political and cultural landscape. The Ottomans incorporated Bosnia as an integral province of the Ottoman Empire with its historical name and territorial integrity.[^cite_note-62] Within Bosnia, the Ottomans introduced a number of key changes in the territory's socio-political administration; including a new landholding system, a reorganization of administrative units, and a complex system of social differentiation by class and religious affiliation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm200251–69-63]

Following Ottoman annexation, there was a steady flow of people out of Bosnia and a large number of abandoned villages in Bosnia are mentioned in the Ottoman registers while those who stayed eventually became [Muslims](19541.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm200252-64] Many Catholics in Bosnia fled to neighboring Catholic lands in the early Ottoman occupation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm200255-65] The evidence indicates that the early Muslim conversions in Ottoman Bosnia in the 15th–16th century were among the locals who stayed rather than mass Muslim settlements from outside Bosnia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm200252–55-66] In Herzegovina, many [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy) people had also embraced Islam.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm200253-67] By the late 16th and early 17th century, Muslims are considered to have become an absolute majority in Bosnia and Herzegovina. The Albanian Catholic priest [Pjetër Mazreku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pjet%C3%ABr_Mazreku) reported in 1624 that there were 450,000 Muslims, 150,000 Catholics and 75,000 Eastern Orthodox in Bosnia and Herzegovina.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm200254-68]

There was a lack of [Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church) activity in Bosnia proper in the pre-Ottoman period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm200271-69] An Orthodox Christian population in Bosnia was introduced as a direct result of Ottoman policy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm200255-65] From the 15th century and onwards, Orthodox Christians (Orthodox [Vlachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlachs) and non-Vlach Orthodox Serbs) from Serbia and other regions settled in Bosnia and Herzegovina.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm200271-73,_78-80-70] Favored by the Ottomans over the Catholics, many Orthodox churches were allowed to be built in Bosnia by the Ottomans.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm200255-65] Quite a few Vlachs also became Islamized in Bosnia, and some (mainly in Croatia) became Catholics.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm200270–81-71]



[Počitelj Citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po%C4%8Ditelj_Citadel) in [Čapljina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Capljina), built by [King Tvrtko I of Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Tvrtko_I_of_Bosnia) in 1383.

The four centuries of Ottoman rule also had a drastic impact on Bosnia's population make-up, which changed several times as a result of the empire's conquests, frequent wars with European powers, forced and economic migrations, and epidemics. A native Slavic-speaking Muslim community emerged and eventually became the largest of the ethno-religious groups due to a lack of strong Christian church organizations and continuous rivalry between the Orthodox and Catholic churches, while the indigenous [Bosnian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_Church) disappeared altogether (ostensibly by conversion of its members to Islam). The Ottomans referred to them as *kristianlar* while the Orthodox and Catholics were called *gebir* or *kafir*, meaning "unbeliever".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVelikonja200329–30-72] The Bosnian [Franciscans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscans) (and the Catholic population as a whole) were protected by official imperial decrees and in accordance and the full extent of Ottoman laws; however, in effect, these often merely affected arbitrary rule and behavior of powerful local elite.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm2002-25]

As the Ottoman Empire continued its rule in the Balkans ([Rumelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumelia)), Bosnia was somewhat relieved of the pressures of being a frontier province and experienced a period of general welfare. A number of cities, such as [Sarajevo](26786.md) and [Mostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostar), were established and grew into regional centers of trade and urban culture and were then visited by Ottoman traveler [Evliya Çelebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evliya_%C3%87elebi) in 1648. Within these cities, various Ottoman Sultans financed the construction of many works of [Bosnian architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) such as the country's first library in Sarajevo, [madrassas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasa), a school of [Sufi philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufi_philosophy), and a clock tower (*Sahat Kula*), bridges such as the [Stari Most](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stari_Most), the [Emperor's Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor's_Mosque) and the [Gazi Husrev-beg Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazi_Husrev-beg_Mosque).[^cite_note-Syed_Akhtar_Usmani_2011_p._473-73]



The [Mostar Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stari_Most) was commissioned by [Suleiman the Magnificent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suleiman_the_Magnificent) in 1557

Furthermore, several Bosnian Muslims played influential roles in the Ottoman Empire's cultural and [political history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_history) during this time.[^cite_note-Riedlmayer-74] Bosnian recruits formed a large component of the Ottoman ranks in the battles of [Mohács](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Moh%C3%A1cs) and [Krbava field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Krbava_Field), while numerous other Bosnians rose through the ranks of the Ottoman military to occupy the highest positions of power in the Empire, including admirals such as [Matrakçı Nasuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrak%C3%A7%C4%B1_Nasuh); generals such as [Isa-Beg Ishaković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isa_Bey_Ishakovi%C4%87), [Gazi Husrev-beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazi_Husrev_Bey), [Telli Hasan Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telli_Hasan_Pasha) and [Sarı Süleyman Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sar%C4%B1_S%C3%BCleyman_Pasha); administrators such as [Ferhad Pasha Sokolović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferhad_Pasha_Sokolovi%C4%87) and [Osman Gradaščević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osman_Grada%C5%A1%C4%8Devi%C4%87); and Grand [Viziers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vizier) such as the influential [Sokollu Mehmed Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokollu_Mehmed_Pasha) and [Damat Ibrahim Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damat_Ibrahim_Pasha). Some Bosnians emerged as [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) mystics, scholars such as [Muhamed Hevaji Uskufi Bosnevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhamed_Hevaji_Uskufi_Bosnevi), [Ali Džabić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_D%C5%BEabi%C4%87); and poets in the [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language), [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_language), [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), and [Persian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language).[^cite_note-Imamović,_Mustafa_1996-75]

However, by the late 17th century the Empire's military misfortunes caught up with the country, and the end of the [Great Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Turkish_War) with the [treaty of Karlowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Karlowitz) in 1699 again made Bosnia the Empire's westernmost province. The 18th century was marked by further military failures, numerous revolts within Bosnia, and several outbreaks of plague.[^cite_note-76] The Porte's efforts at modernizing the Ottoman state were met with distrust growing to hostility in Bosnia, where local aristocrats stood to lose much through the proposed [Tanzimat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzimat) reforms. This, combined with frustrations over territorial, political concessions in the north-east, and the plight of [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) [Muslim](19541.md) refugees arriving from the [Sanjak of Smederevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak_of_Smederevo) into [Bosnia Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_Eyalet), culminated in a partially unsuccessful revolt by [Husein Gradaščević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Husein_Grada%C5%A1%C4%8Devi%C4%87), who endorsed a Bosnia Eyalet autonomous from the authoritarian rule of the Ottoman Sultan [Mahmud II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_II), who persecuted, executed and abolished the [Janissaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janissary) and reduced the role of autonomous [Pashas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasha) in Rumelia. Mahmud II sent his [Grand vizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_vizier) to subdue Bosnia Eyalet and succeeded only with the reluctant assistance of [Ali Pasha Rizvanbegović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Pasha_Rizvanbegovi%C4%87).[^cite_note-Imamović,_Mustafa_1996-75] Related rebellions were extinguished by 1850, but the situation continued to deteriorate.

New nationalist movements appeared in Bosnia by the middle of the 19th century. Shortly after Serbia's breakaway from the Ottoman Empire in the early 19th century, Serbian and Croatian nationalism rose up in Bosnia, and such nationalists made irredentist claims to Bosnia's territory. This trend continued to grow in the rest of the 19th and 20th centuries.[^cite_note-77]

In 1867, as part of the Ottoman Empires administrative reforms, the Bosnia Eyalet was reorganized as the Vilayet of Bosnia.&#x20;

Agrarian unrest eventually sparked the [Herzegovinian rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzegovina_uprising_\(1875%E2%80%931877\)), a widespread peasant uprising, in 1875. The conflict rapidly spread and came to involve several Balkan states and Great Powers, a situation that led to the [Congress of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Berlin) and the [Treaty of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Berlin_\(1878\)) in 1878.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm2002131–134-78]

### Austro-Hungarian occupation and condominium

Main articles: [Austro-Hungarian campaign in Bosnia and Herzegovina in 1878](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_campaign_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_in_1878) and [Austro-Hungarian rule in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_rule_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)



Austro-Hungarian troops [enter Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_campaign_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_in_1878), 1878

At the Congress of [Berlin](3354.md) in 1878, the [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) Foreign Minister [Gyula Andrássy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyula_Andr%C3%A1ssy) obtained the occupation and administration of Bosnia and Herzegovina, and he also obtained the right to station garrisons in the [Sanjak of Novi Pazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak_of_Novi_Pazar), which would remain under [Ottoman](22278.md) administration until 1908, when the Austro-Hungarian troops withdrew from the Sanjak.

Although Austro-Hungarian officials quickly came to an agreement with the Bosnians, tensions remained and a mass emigration of Bosnians occurred.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm2002139-79] However, a state of relative stability was reached soon enough and Austro-Hungarian authorities were able to embark on a number of social and administrative reforms they intended would make Bosnia and Herzegovina into a "model" colony.

[Habsburg rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Monarchy) had several key concerns in Bosnia. It tried to dissipate the South Slav nationalism by disputing the earlier Serb and Croat claims to Bosnia and encouraging identification of Bosnian or [Bosniak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniaks) identity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHajdarpasic2015161–165-80] Habsburg rule also tried to provide for modernisation by codifying laws, introducing new political institutions, establishing and expanding industries.[^cite_note-81]



People of Sarajevo in 1883

Austria–Hungary began to plan the annexation of Bosnia, but due to international disputes the issue was not resolved until the [annexation crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_Crisis) of 1908.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlbertini200594-82] Several external matters affected the status of Bosnia and its relationship with Austria–Hungary. [A bloody coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Coup_\(Serbia\)) occurred in Serbia in 1903, which brought a radical anti-Austrian government into power in [Belgrade](55904.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlbertini2005140-83] Then in 1908, the revolt in the Ottoman Empire raised concerns that the [Istanbul](3391396.md) government might seek the outright return of Bosnia and Herzegovina. These factors caused the Austro-Hungarian government to seek a permanent resolution of the Bosnian question sooner, rather than later.

Taking advantage of the turmoil in the Ottoman Empire, Austro-Hungarian diplomacy tried to obtain provisional Russian approval for changes over the status of Bosnia and Herzegovina and published the annexation proclamation on 6 October 1908.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlbertini2005227-84] Despite international objections to the Austro-Hungarian annexation, Russians and their client state, Serbia, were compelled to accept the Austro-Hungarian annexation of Bosnia and Herzegovina in March 1909.

In 1910, Habsburg Emperor [Franz Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_I_of_Austria) proclaimed the first constitution in Bosnia, which led to relaxation of earlier laws, elections and formation of the [Bosnian parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_of_Bosnia) and growth of new political life.[^cite_note-85]



The [assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand) of [Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_of_Austria) and [Sophie, Duchess of Hohenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie,_Duchess_of_Hohenberg) by [Gavrilo Princip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavrilo_Princip) in Sarajevo, 28 June 1914

On 28 June 1914, [Gavrilo Princip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavrilo_Princip), a [Bosnian Serb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) member of the revolutionary movement [Young Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Bosnia), [assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand) the heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, [Archduke Franz Ferdinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduke_Franz_Ferdinand_of_Austria), in Sarajevo—an event that was the spark that set off [World War I](4764461.md). At the end of the war, the Bosnian Muslims had lost more men per capita than any other ethnic group in the Habsburg Empire whilst serving in the [Bosnian-Herzegovinian Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian-Herzegovinian_Infantry) of the [Austro-Hungarian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Army).[^cite_note-Schachinger-86] Nonetheless, Bosnia and Herzegovina as a whole managed to escape the conflict relatively unscathed.[^cite_note-Riedlmayer-74]

The Austro-Hungarian authorities established an auxiliary militia known as the [Schutzkorps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzkorps) with a moot role in the empire's policy of [anti-Serb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Serb_sentiment) repression.[^cite_note-Banac1988-87] Schutzkorps, predominantly recruited among the Bosnian Muslim population, were tasked with hunting down rebel Serbs (the *[Chetniks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chetniks)* and *[Komitadji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komitadji)*)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVelikonja2003141-88] and became known for their persecution of [Serbs](59512.md) particularly in Serb populated areas of eastern Bosnia, where they partly retaliated against Serbian Chetniks who in fall 1914 had carried out attacks against the Muslim population in the area.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] The proceedings of the Austro-Hungarian authorities led to around 5,500 citizens of Serb ethnicity in Bosnia and Herzegovina being arrested, and between 700 and 2,200 died in prison while 460 were executed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVelikonja2003141-88] Around 5,200 Serb families were forcibly expelled from Bosnia and Herzegovina.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVelikonja2003141-88]

### Kingdom of Yugoslavia

Main article: [History of Bosnia and Herzegovina § Kingdom of Yugoslavia (1918–41)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina#Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia_\(1918%E2%80%9341\))



"Keep/Protect Yugoslavia" (Čuvajte Jugoslaviju), a variant of the alleged last words of [King Alexander I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Yugoslavia), in an illustration of Yugoslav peoples dancing the [kolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolo_\(dance\))

Following World War I, Bosnia and Herzegovina became a province in the South Slav [Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia) (soon renamed Yugoslavia). Political life in Bosnia and Herzegovina at this time was marked by two major trends: social and economic unrest over [property redistribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distribution_\(economics\)) and the formation of several political parties that frequently changed coalitions and alliances with parties in other Yugoslav regions.[^cite_note-Riedlmayer-74]

The dominant ideological conflict of the Yugoslav state, between Croatian regionalism and Serbian centralization, was approached differently by Bosnia and Herzegovina's major [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group) and was dependent on the overall political atmosphere.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm2002163–164-91] The political reforms brought about in the newly established Yugoslav kingdom saw few benefits for the Bosnian Muslims; according to the 1910 final census of land ownership and population according to religious affiliation conducted in Austria-Hungary, Muslims owned 91.1%, Orthodox Serbs owned 6.0%, Croat Catholics owned 2.6% and others, 0.3% of the property. Following the reforms, Bosnian Muslims were dispossessed of a total of 1,175,305 hectares of agricultural and forest land.[^cite_note-92] With the initial split of the country into 33 [oblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast) Bosnia and Herzegovina was dissolved as a single entity, but the efforts of Bosnian politicians, such as [Mehmed Spaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_Spaho), ensured the six oblasts carved up from Bosnia and Herzegovina corresponded to the six sanjaks from Ottoman times and, thus, matched the country's traditional boundary as a whole.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm2002165–168-93]

With the reorganization of the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes into the Kingdom of Yugoslavia in 1929, the redrawing of administrative regions into [banates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia#Subdivisions) or *[banovinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banovina_\(region\))*, all of the previous Bosnian entities were dissolved and replaced by geographical names.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm2002168–169-94] Serbo-Croat tensions over the structuring of the Yugoslav state continued, with the concept of a separate Bosnian division receiving little or no consideration.

The [Cvetković-Maček Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cvetkovi%C4%87%E2%80%93Ma%C4%8Dek_Agreement) that created the [Croatian banate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banovina_of_Croatia) in 1939 encouraged what was essentially a [partition of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) between Croatia and Serbia.[^cite_note-Imamović,_Mustafa_1996-75] However the rising threat of [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md)'s [Nazi Germany](21212.md) forced Yugoslav politicians to shift their attention. Following a period that saw attempts at appeasement, the signing of the [Tripartite Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartite_Pact), and a [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), Yugoslavia was finally invaded by Germany on 6 April 1941.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm2002173-95]

### World War II (1941–1945)

Main article: [History of Bosnia and Herzegovina (1941–1945)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_\(1941%E2%80%931945\))



The railway bridge over the [Neretva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neretva) River in [Jablanica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jablanica,_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), twice destroyed during the 1943 [Operation Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Weiss) offensive

Once the Kingdom of Yugoslavia was conquered and partitioned by the Axis powers in [World War II](32927.md), all of the region of Bosnia and Herzegovina was ceded to the [Independent State of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_State_of_Croatia) (NDH), a [German](21212.md) and [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) puppet state led by the [Ustaše](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usta%C5%A1e). The NDH leaders embarked on a [campaign of extermination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_of_Serbs_in_the_Independent_State_of_Croatia) of Serbs, Jews, [Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) as well as dissident Croats, and, later, [Josip Broz Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito)'s [Partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Partisans) by setting up a number of [death camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasenovac_concentration_camp).[^cite_note-96] The regime systematically and brutally massacred Serbs in villages in the countryside, using a variety of tools.[^cite_note-97] The scale of the violence meant that approximately every sixth Serb living in Bosnia and Herzegovina was the victim of a massacre and virtually every Serb had a family member that was killed in the war, mostly by the Ustaše. The experience had a profound impact in the collective memory of Serbs in Croatia and Bosnia and Herzegovina.[^cite_note-98] An estimated 209,000 Serbs or 16.9% of its Bosnia population were killed on the territory of Bosnia and Herzegovina during the war.[^cite_note-99]

The Ustaše recognized both Catholicism and Islam as the national religions, but held the position [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md), as a symbol of Serb identity, was their greatest foe.[^cite_note-Ramet,_p._118-100] Although Croats were by far the largest ethnic group to constitute the Ustaše, the Vice President of the NDH and leader of the Yugoslav Muslim Organization [Džafer Kulenović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%BEafer_Kulenovi%C4%87) was a Muslim, and Muslims in total constituted nearly 12% of the Ustaše military and civil service authority.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVelikonja2003179-101]



[Eternal flame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternal_flame_\(Sarajevo\)) memorial to military and civilian [World War II](32927.md) victims in Sarajevo

Many Serbs themselves took up arms and joined the Chetniks, a Serb nationalist movement with the aim of establishing an ethnically homogeneous '[Greater Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Serbia)' state[^cite_note-102] within the Kingdom of Yugoslavia. The Chetniks, in turn, pursued a [genocidal campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chetnik_war_crimes_in_World_War_II) against ethnic Muslims and Croats, as well as persecuting a large number of [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) Serbs and other Communist sympathizers, with the Muslim populations of Bosnia, Herzegovina and [Sandžak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand%C5%BEak) being a primary target.[^cite_note-103] Once captured, Muslim villagers were systematically massacred by the Chetniks.[^cite_note-104] Of the 75,000 Muslims who died in Bosnia and Herzegovina during the war,[^cite_note-Cohen-105] approximately 30,000 (mostly civilians) were killed by the Chetniks.[^cite_note-Geiger-106] Massacres against Croats were smaller in scale but similar in action.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETomasevich1975259-107] Between 64,000 and 79,000 Bosnian Croats were killed between April 1941 to May 1945.[^cite_note-Cohen-105] Of these, about 18,000 were killed by the Chetniks.[^cite_note-Geiger-106]

A percentage of Muslims served in Nazi [*Waffen-SS*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_Waffen_Mountain_Division_of_the_SS_Handschar_\(1st_Croatian\)) units.[^cite_note-108] These units were responsible for massacres of Serbs in northwest and eastern Bosnia, most notably in [Vlasenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlasenica).[^cite_note-109] On 12 October 1941, a group of 108 prominent Sarajevan Muslims signed the [Resolution of Sarajevo Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolution_of_Sarajevo_Muslims) by which they condemned the persecution of Serbs organized by the Ustaše, made distinction between Muslims who participated in such persecutions and the Muslim population as a whole, presented information about the persecutions of Muslims by Serbs, and requested security for all citizens of the country, regardless of their identity.[^cite_note-110]

Starting in 1941, Yugoslav communists under the leadership of Josip Broz Tito organized their own multi-ethnic resistance group, the Partisans, who fought against both [Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) and Chetnik forces. On 29 November 1943, the [Anti-Fascist Council for the National Liberation of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Fascist_Council_for_the_National_Liberation_of_Yugoslavia) (AVNOJ) with Tito at its helm held a founding conference in [Jajce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jajce) where Bosnia and Herzegovina was reestablished as a republic within the Yugoslav federation in its Habsburg borders.[^cite_note-111] During the entire course of [World War II in Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_in_Yugoslavia), 64.1% of all Bosnian Partisans were Serbs, 23% were Muslims and 8.8% Croats.[^cite_note-112]

Military success eventually prompted the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) to support the Partisans, resulting in the successful [Maclean Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maclean_Mission), but Tito declined their offer to help and relied on his own forces instead. All the major military offensives by the antifascist movement of Yugoslavia against Nazis and their local supporters were conducted in Bosnia and Herzegovina and its peoples bore the brunt of the fighting. More than 300,000 people died in Bosnia and Herzegovina in World War II, or more than 10% of the population.[^cite_note-113] At the end of the war, the establishment of the [Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia), with the [constitution of 1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_Yugoslav_Constitution), officially made Bosnia and Herzegovina one of six constituent republics in the new state.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm2002191–194-114]

### Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia (1945–1992)

Main article: [Socialist Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)



Bosnia and Herzegovina's flag while part of the [Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia)

Due to its central geographic position within the Yugoslav federation, post-war Bosnia was selected as a base for the development of the military defense industry. This contributed to a large concentration of arms and military personnel in Bosnia; a significant factor in [the war that followed the break-up of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War) in the 1990s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm2002-25] However, Bosnia's existence within Yugoslavia, for the large part, was relatively peaceful and very prosperous, with high employment, a strong industrial and export oriented economy, a good education system and social and medical security for every citizen of Bosnia and Herzegovina. Several international corporations operated in Bosnia—[Volkswagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen) as part of TAS (car factory in Sarajevo, from 1972), [Coca-Cola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca-Cola) (from 1975), SKF Sweden (from 1967), [Marlboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlboro_\(cigarette\)) (a tobacco factory in Sarajevo), and [Holiday Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiday_Inn) hotels. Sarajevo was the site of the [1984 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Winter_Olympics).

During the 1950s and 1960s, Bosnia was a political backwater of Yugoslavia. In the 1970s, a strong Bosnian political elite arose, fueled in part by Tito's leadership in the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement) and Bosnians serving in Yugoslavia's diplomatic corps. While working within the Socialist system, politicians such as [Džemal Bijedić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%BEemal_Bijedi%C4%87), [Branko Mikulić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branko_Mikuli%C4%87) and [Hamdija Pozderac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamdija_Pozderac) reinforced and protected the sovereignty of Bosnia and Herzegovina.[^cite_note-115] Their efforts proved key during the turbulent period following Tito's death in 1980, and are today considered some of the early steps towards [Bosnian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Bosnia_and_Herzegovina\)). However, the republic did not escape the increasingly nationalistic climate of the time. With the fall of communism and the start of the [breakup of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_of_Yugoslavia), doctrine of tolerance began to lose its potency, creating an opportunity for nationalist elements in the society to spread their influence.

### Bosnian War (1992–1995)

Main article: [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War)

See also: [Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)



Dissolution of [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia)

On 18 November 1990, [multi-party parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Bosnian_general_election) were held throughout Bosnia and Herzegovina. A second round followed on 25 November, resulting in a [national assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) where communist power was replaced by a coalition of three ethnically based parties.[^cite_note-116] Following [Slovenia](27338.md) and [Croatia](5573.md)'s declarations of independence from Yugoslavia, a significant split developed among the residents of Bosnia and Herzegovina on the issue of whether to remain within Yugoslavia (overwhelmingly favored by Serbs) or seek independence (overwhelmingly favored by [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims_\(ethnic_group\)) and Croats).[^cite_note-The_Princeton_Encyclopedia_of_Self-Determination_1995-117]

The Serb members of parliament, consisting mainly of the [Serb Democratic Party](413319.md) members, abandoned the central parliament in Sarajevo, and formed the [Assembly of the Serb People of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Republika_Srpska\)) on 24 October 1991, which marked the end of the three-ethnic coalition that governed after the elections in 1990. This Assembly established the Serbian Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina in part of the territory of Bosnia and Herzegovina on 9 January 1992. It was renamed [Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republika_Srpska_\(1992%E2%80%931995\)) in August 1992. On 18 November 1991, the party branch in Bosnia and Herzegovina of the ruling party in the Republic of Croatia, the [Croatian Democratic Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Democratic_Union) (HDZ), proclaimed the existence of the [Croatian Community of Herzeg-Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Republic_of_Herzeg-Bosnia) in a separate part of the territory of Bosnia and Herzegovina with the [Croatian Defence Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Defence_Council) (HVO) as its military branch.[^cite_note-ICTY:_Prlić_et_al._(IT-04-74)-118] It went unrecognized by the [Government of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), which declared it illegal.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]



[UN](31769.md) troops in front of the [Executive Council Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece%E2%80%93Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_Friendship_Building), burned after being struck by tank fire during the [siege of Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sarajevo), 1995

A declaration of the sovereignty of Bosnia and Herzegovina on 15 October 1991 was followed by a [referendum for independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Bosnian_independence_referendum) on 29 February and 1 March 1992, which was boycotted by the great majority of Serbs. The turnout in the independence referendum was 63.4 per cent and 99.7 per cent of voters voted for independence.[^cite_note-121] Bosnia and Herzegovina declared independence on 3 March 1992 and received international recognition the following month on 6 April 1992.[^cite_note-122] The [Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) was admitted as a member state of the [United Nations](31769.md) on 22 May 1992.[^cite_note-123] Serbian leader [Slobodan Milošević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobodan_Milo%C5%A1evi%C4%87) and Croatian leader [Franjo Tuđman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franjo_Tu%C4%91man) are believed to have agreed on a [partition of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) in March 1991, with the aim of establishing [Greater Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Serbia) and [Greater Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Croatia).[^cite_note-124]

Following Bosnia and Herzegovina's declaration of independence, Bosnian Serb militias mobilized in different parts of the country. Government forces were poorly equipped and unprepared for the war.[^cite_note-ICTY:_Naletilić_and_Martinović_verdict_—_A._Historical_background-125] International recognition of Bosnia and Herzegovina increased diplomatic pressure for the [Yugoslav People's Army](536643.md) (JNA) to withdraw from the republic's territory, which they officially did in June 1992. The Bosnian Serb members of the JNA simply changed insignia, formed the [Army of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Republika_Srpska) (VRS), and continued fighting. Armed and equipped from JNA stockpiles in Bosnia, supported by volunteers and various paramilitary forces from Serbia, and receiving extensive humanitarian, logistical and financial support from the [Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro), [Republika Srpska](347422.md)'s offensives in 1992 managed to place much of the country under its control.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalcolm2002234–239-126] The Bosnian Serb advance was accompanied by the [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing) of Bosniaks and Bosnian Croats from VRS-controlled areas. Dozens of concentration camps were established in which inmates were subjected to violence and abuse, including rape.[^cite_note-ICTY:_Kunarac,_Kovač_and_Vuković_judgement_—_Foča-127] The ethnic cleansing culminated in the [Srebrenica massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebrenica_massacre) of more than 8,000 Bosniak men and boys in July 1995, which was ruled to have been a [genocide](12441.md) by the [International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Criminal_Tribunal_for_the_former_Yugoslavia) (ICTY).[^cite_note-128] Bosniak and Bosnian Croat forces also committed war crimes against civilians from different ethnic groups, though on a smaller scale.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-haverford-131][^cite_note-un-132] Most of the Bosniak and Croat atrocities were committed during the [Croat–Bosniak War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croat%E2%80%93Bosniak_War), a sub-conflict of the Bosnian War that pitted the [Army of the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) (ARBiH) against the HVO. The Bosniak-Croat conflict ended in March 1994, with the signing of the [Washington Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Agreement_\(1994\)), leading to the creation of a joint Bosniak-Croat [Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), which amalgamated HVO-held territory with that held by the [Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Republic_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) (ARBiH).&#x20;

### Recent history

Main article: [2014 unrest in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_unrest_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)



[Tuzla](524638.md) government building burning after [anti-government clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_unrest_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) on 7 February 2014

On 4 February 2014, the [protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_unrest_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) against the [Government of the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), one of the country's two entities, dubbed the Bosnian Spring, the name being taken from the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring), began in the northern town of [Tuzla](524638.md). Workers from several factories that had been privatised and gone bankrupt assembled to demand action over jobs, unpaid salaries and pensions.[^cite_note-133] Soon protests spread to the rest of the Federation, with violent clashes reported in close to 20 towns, the biggest of which were [Sarajevo](26786.md), [Zenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenica), [Mostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostar), [Bihać](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biha%C4%87), [Brčko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%8Dko) and Tuzla.[^cite_note-134] The Bosnian news media reported that hundreds of people had been injured during the protests, including dozens of police officers, with bursts of violence in Sarajevo, in the northern city of Tuzla, in Mostar in the south, and in Zenica in central Bosnia. The same level of unrest or activism did not occur in Republika Srpska, but hundreds of people also gathered in support of protests in the city of [Banja Luka](385109.md) against its separate government.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]

The protests marked the largest outbreak of public anger over high unemployment and two decades of political inertia in the country since the end of the Bosnian War in 1995.[^cite_note-138] According to a report made by [Christian Schmidt](39499479.md) of the [Office of High Representative](2386573.md) in late 2021, Bosnia and Herzegovina experienced intensified political and ethnic tensions, that could have potentially broken the country apart and plunged it back into war once again.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] Comments by [Milorad Dodik](4218129.md) in 2021 in favor of secession from national institutions by Republika Srpska, including creation of its own armed forces raised concerns about a return to civil war.[^cite_note-141]

On 15 December 2022, Bosnia and Herzegovina was recognised by the European Union as a candidate country for [accession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_to_the_European_Union) following the decision of the [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council).[^cite_note-sarajevotimes-142]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)

See also: [List of mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mountains_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), [lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lakes_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), and [rivers of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)



[Sutjeska National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutjeska_National_Park) contains [Maglić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magli%C4%87_\(mountain\)), the highest mountain in Bosnia and Herzegovina, as well as [Perućica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru%C4%87ica), one of the last remaining [primeval forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old-growth_forest) in Europe



[Kozara Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozara), [Kozara National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozara_National_Park)



[Štrbački buk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0trba%C4%8Dki_buk) waterfall on the [Una](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Una_\(Sava\)) River, [Una National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Una_National_Park)



[Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina) river canyon, [Drina National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina_National_Park)

Bosnia and Herzegovina is in the western [Balkans](4829.md), bordering [Croatia](5573.md) (932 km or 579 mi) to the north and west, [Serbia](29265.md) (302 km or 188 mi) to the east, and [Montenegro](20760.md) (225 km or 140 mi) to the southeast. It has a coastline about 20 kilometres (12 miles) long surrounding the town of [Neum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neum).[^cite_note-coastline-143][^cite_note-144] It lies between latitudes [42°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_parallel_north) and [46° N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/46th_parallel_north), and longitudes [15°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_meridian_east) and [20° E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_meridian_east).

The country's name comes from the two alleged regions [Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_\(region\)) and [Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzegovina), whose border was never defined. Historically, Bosnia's official name never included any of its many regions until the Austro-Hungarian occupation.

The country is mostly mountainous, encompassing the central [Dinaric Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinaric_Alps). The northeastern parts reach into the [Pannonian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Basin), while in the south it borders the [Adriatic](23275478.md). The Dinaric Alps generally run in a southeast–northwest direction, and get higher towards the south. The highest point of the country is the peak of [Maglić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magli%C4%87_\(mountain\)) at 2,386 metres (7,828.1 feet), on the Montenegrin border. Other major mountains include [Volujak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volujak_\(mountain\)), [Zelengora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelengora), [Lelija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lelija), [Lebršnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebr%C5%A1nik), [Orjen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orjen), [Kozara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozara), [Grmeč](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grme%C4%8D), [Čvrsnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Cvrsnica), [Prenj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenj), [Vran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vran), [Vranica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vranica), [Velež](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vele%C5%BE_\(Bosnia_and_Herzegovina\)), [Vlašić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vla%C5%A1i%C4%87_\(Bosnian_mountain\)), [Cincar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincar), [Romanija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanija), [Jahorina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahorina), [Bjelašnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bjela%C5%A1nica), [Treskavica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treskavica) and [Trebević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trebevi%C4%87). The geological composition of the Dinaric chain of mountains in Bosnia consists primarily of [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) (including [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) limestone), with deposits of [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese), [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite), [lead](17747.md), and [salt](1605200.md) present in some areas, especially in central and northern Bosnia.[^cite_note-145]

Overall, nearly 50% of Bosnia and Herzegovina is forested. Most forest areas are in the centre, east and west parts of Bosnia. Herzegovina has a drier Mediterranean climate, with dominant [karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst) topography. Northern Bosnia ([Posavina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posavina)) contains very fertile agricultural land along the [Sava](587845.md) river and the corresponding area is heavily farmed. This farmland is a part of the Pannonian Plain stretching into neighboring Croatia and Serbia. The country has only 20 kilometres (12 miles) of coastline,[^cite_note-Encyclopedia_Britannica-28][^cite_note-coastline-143] around the town of Neum in the [Herzegovina-Neretva Canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzegovina-Neretva_Canton). Although the city is surrounded by Croatian peninsulas, by international law, Bosnia and Herzegovina has a [right of passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_passage) to the outer sea.

[Sarajevo](26786.md) is the capital[^cite_note-Constitution-1] and largest city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECIA2019-8] Other major cities include [Banja Luka](385109.md) and [Prijedor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prijedor) in the northwest region known as [Bosanska Krajina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosanska_Krajina), [Tuzla](524638.md), [Bijeljina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijeljina), [Doboj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doboj) and [Brčko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%8Dko) in the northeast, [Zenica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenica) in the central part of the country, and [Mostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostar), the largest city in the southern region of [Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzegovina).

There are seven major rivers in Bosnia and Herzegovina:[^cite_note-fao-146]

- The [Sava](587845.md) is the largest river of the country, and forms its northern [natural border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_border) with Croatia. It drains 76%

  [^cite_note-fao-146]

  &#x20;of the country's territory into the [Danube](21209639.md) and then the [Black Sea](3386.md). Bosnia and Herzegovina is a member of the [International Commission for the Protection of the Danube River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Commission_for_the_Protection_of_the_Danube_River) (ICPDR).

- The [Una](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Una_\(Sava\)), [Sana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sana_\(river\)) and [Vrbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrbas_\(river\)) are right tributaries of the Sava. They are in the northwestern region of [Bosanska Krajina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosanska_Krajina).

- The [Bosna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosna_\(river\)) river gave its name to the country, and is the longest river fully contained within it. It stretches through central Bosnia, from its source near [Sarajevo](26786.md) to Sava in the north.

- The [Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina) flows through the eastern part of Bosnia, and for the most part it forms a natural border with Serbia.

- The [Neretva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neretva) is the major river of Herzegovina and the only major river that flows south, into the Adriatic Sea.

### Biodiversity

Main article: [Environment of Bosnia and Herzegovina § Biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina#Biodiversity)

[Phytogeographically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytogeography), Bosnia and Herzegovina belongs to the [Boreal Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boreal_Kingdom) and is shared between the Illyrian province of the [Circumboreal Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumboreal_Region) and Adriatic province of the [Mediterranean Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Basin). According to the [World Wide Fund for Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Fund_for_Nature) (WWF), the territory of Bosnia and Herzegovina can be subdivided into four [ecoregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion): [Balkan mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_mixed_forests), [Dinaric Mountains mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinaric_Mountains_mixed_forests), [Pannonian mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_mixed_forests) and [Illyrian deciduous forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyrian_deciduous_forests).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-147] The country had a 2018 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index) mean score of 5.99/10, ranking it 89th globally out of 172 countries.[^cite_note-FLII-Supplementary-148] In Bosnia and Herzegovina [forest cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_cover) is around 43% of the total land area, equivalent to 2,187,910 hectares (ha) of forest in 2020, down from 2,210,000 hectares (ha) in 1990. For the year 2015, 74% of the forest area was reported to be under [public ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_ownership) and 26% [private ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_property).[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

## Politics

Main article: [Politics of Bosnia and Herzegovina](3606.md)

### Government



[Denis Bećirović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Be%C4%87irovi%C4%87)\
[Chairman of the Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Presidency_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)



[Borjana Krišto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borjana_Kri%C5%A1to)\
[Chairwoman of the Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Council_of_Ministers_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)

As a result of the 1995 [Dayton Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement), the civilian peace implementation is supervised by the [High Representative for Bosnia and Herzegovina](2386573.md) selected by the international [Peace Implementation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Implementation_Council) (PIC). The High Representative is the highest political authority in the country. The High Representative has many governmental and legislative powers, including the dismissal of elected and non-elected officials. Due to the vast powers of the High Representative over [Bosnian politics](3606.md) and essential [veto](74661.md) powers, the position has also been compared to that of a [viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy).[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154]

Politics take place in a framework of a [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy), whereby [executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) is exercised by the [Council of Ministers of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina). [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) is vested in both the Council of Ministers and the [Parliamentary Assembly of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina). Members of the Parliamentary Assembly are chosen according to a [proportional representation](23298.md) (PR) system.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156]



Bosnia and Herzegovina consists of the [Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) (FBiH), [Republika Srpska](347422.md) (RS) and [Brčko District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%8Dko_District) (BD)

Bosnia and Herzegovina has several levels of political structuring, according to the [Dayton Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement). The most important of these levels is the division of the country into two ["entities"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entities_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina): the [Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and [Republika Srpska](347422.md). The Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina covers 51% of Bosnia and Herzegovina's total area, while Republika Srpska covers 49%. The entities, largely based on the territories held by the two warring sides at the time, were formally established by the Dayton Agreement due to the tremendous changes in Bosnia and Herzegovina's ethnic structure. At the national level, there exists only a finite set of exclusive or joint competencies, whereas the majority of authority rests within the entities.[^cite_note-157] [Sumantra Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumantra_Bose) describes Bosnia and Herzegovina as a consociational [confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation).[^cite_note-158]

The [Brčko District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%8Dko_District) in the north of the country was created in 2000 from land from both entities. It officially belongs to both but is governed by neither and operates under a decentralised system of local government. For election purposes, Brčko District voters can choose to participate in either the Federation or Republika Srpska elections. The Brčko District has been praised for maintaining a multiethnic population and a level of prosperity significantly above the national average.[^cite_note-OHR-159]



Bosnia and Herzegovina's [government building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece%E2%80%93Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_Friendship_Building) in [Sarajevo](26786.md)



[Palace of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_the_Republic,_Banja_Luka) in [Banja Luka](385109.md)

The third level of Bosnia and Herzegovina's political subdivision is manifested in [cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantons_of_the_Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina). They are unique to the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina entity, which consists of ten of them. Each has a cantonal government, which is under the law of the Federation as a whole. Some cantons are ethnically mixed and have special laws to ensure the equality of all constituent people.[^cite_note-Mansfield_2003_pp._2052–2093-160]

The fourth level of political division in Bosnia and Herzegovina are the [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina). The Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina is divided into 79 municipalities, and Republika Srpska into 64. Municipalities also have their own local government, and are typically based on the most significant city or place in their territory. As such, many municipalities have a long tradition and history with their present boundaries. Some others, however, were only created following the recent war after traditional municipalities were split by the [Inter-Entity Boundary Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Entity_Boundary_Line). Each canton in the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina consists of several municipalities, which are divided into local communities.[^cite_note-CoR-161]

Besides entities, cantons, and municipalities, Bosnia and Herzegovina also has four "official" cities. These are: [Banja Luka](385109.md), [Mostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostar), [Sarajevo](26786.md) and [East Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isto%C4%8Dno_Sarajevo). The territory and government of the cities of Banja Luka and Mostar corresponds to the municipalities of the same name, while the cities of Sarajevo and East Sarajevo officially consist of several municipalities. Cities have their own city government whose power is in between that of the municipalities and cantons (or the entity, in the case of Republika Srpska).

More recently, several central institutions have been established (such as a [defense ministry](9473138.md), security ministry, state court, [indirect taxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirect_tax) service and so on) in the process of transferring part of the jurisdiction from the entities to the state. The representation of the government of Bosnia and Herzegovina is by elites who represent the country's three major groups, with each having a guaranteed share of power.

The [Chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Presidency_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) of the [Presidency of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) rotates among three members ([Bosniak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniaks), [Serb](59512.md), [Croat](69801.md)), each elected as the chair for an eight-month term within their four-year term as a member. The three members of the Presidency are elected directly by the people, with Federation voters voting for the Bosniak and the Croat and the Republika Srpska voters voting for the Serb.

The [Chair of the Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Council_of_Ministers_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) is nominated by the Presidency and approved by the parliamentary [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina). The Chair of the Council of Ministers is then responsible for appointing a Foreign Minister, Minister of Foreign Trade and others as appropriate.

The Parliamentary Assembly is the lawmaking body in Bosnia and Herzegovina. It consists of two houses: the [House of Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Peoples_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and the House of Representatives. The House of Peoples has 15 delegates chosen by parliaments of the entities, two-thirds of which come from the Federation (5 Bosniaks and 5 Croats) and one-third from the Republika Srpska (5 Serbs). The House of Representatives is composed of 42 Members elected by the people under a form of proportional representation, two-thirds elected from the Federation and one-third elected from Republika Srpska.[^cite_note-Heath-Brown_2017_p._221-162]

The [Constitutional Court of Bosnia and Herzegovina](11273863.md) is the supreme, final arbiter of legal matters. It is composed of nine members: four members are selected by the [Federal House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_of_the_Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), two by the [National Assembly of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Republika_Srpska\)) and three by the President of the [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights) after consultation with the Presidency, who cannot be Bosnian citizens.[^cite_note-163]

However, the highest political authority in the country is the High Representative for Bosnia and Herzegovina, the chief [executive officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_officer) for the international civilian presence in the country and is selected by the [Peace Implementation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Implementation_Council) (mostly [European Union](9317.md) members). Since 1995, the High Representative has been able to bypass the elected parliamentary assembly, and since 1997 has been able to remove elected officials. The methods selected by the High Representative have been criticized as undemocratic.[^cite_note-164] International supervision [is to end when the country is deemed politically and democratically stable and self-sustaining](2386573.md#Conditions_for_closure_of_the_Office_of_the_High_Representative). An opinion poll conducted in May 2026 found that people perceived the country's biggest problems to be crime and corruption (30.2% of respondents), the state of the economy (16.8%) and the political situation (14.9%). Healthcare (26.2%), income (22.5%) and security (19.8%) were the most mentioned issues that people said affected their daily lives.[^cite_note-v567-165]

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Bosnia and Herzegovina](3611.md)

See also: [Accession of Bosnia and Herzegovina to the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_to_the_European_Union)



[Željko Komšić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDeljko_Kom%C5%A1i%C4%87), Croat member of the [Bosnian Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), and [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton), U.S. Secretary of State, 13 December 2011

[European Union integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_to_the_European_Union) is one of the main political objectives of Bosnia and Herzegovina; it initiated the [Stabilisation and Association Process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilisation_and_Association_Process) in 2007. Countries participating in the SAP have been offered the possibility to become, once they fulfill the necessary conditions, Member States of the EU.[^cite_note-166] Bosnia and Herzegovina has been a candidate country for EU accession since December 2022.[^cite_note-sarajevotimes-142] Accession talks are set to begin following the impeding of more reforms.[^cite_note-167]

The implementation of the [Dayton Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement) in 1995 has focused the efforts of policymakers in Bosnia and Herzegovina, as well as the international community, on regional stabilization in the countries-successors of the [former Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia).[^cite_note-Usa_2012_p._269-168]

Within Bosnia and Herzegovina, relations with its neighbors of [Croatia](5573.md), [Serbia](29265.md) and [Montenegro](20760.md) have been fairly stable since the signing of the Dayton Agreement. On 23 April 2010, Bosnia and Herzegovina received the [Membership Action Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_NATO#Membership_Action_Plan) from [NATO](21133.md), which is the last step before full membership in the alliance. Full membership was initially expected in 2014 or 2015, depending on the progress of reforms.[^cite_note-169] In December 2018, NATO approved a Bosnian Membership Action Plan.[^cite_note-170]

Bosnia and Herzegovina is the 61st most peaceful country in the world, according to the 2024 [Global Peace Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Peace_Index).[^cite_note-171]

### Military

The [Armed Forces of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) (OSBiH) were unified into a single entity in 2005, with the merger of the [Army of the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and the [Army of Republika Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Republika_Srpska), which had defended their respective regions.[^cite_note-Lobjakas_2005-172] The [Ministry of Defence](19307491.md) was formed in 2004.[^cite_note-NATO_2004-173]

The Bosnian military consists of the [Bosnian Ground Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and [Air Force and Air Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_and_Anti-Aircraft_Defence_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina).[^cite_note-European_Western_Balkans_2017-174] The Ground Forces number 7,200 active and 5,000 reserve personnel.[^cite_note-mod.gov.ba_2016-175] They are armed with a mix of American, Yugoslav, Soviet, and European-made weaponry, vehicles, and military equipment. The Air Force and Air Defense Forces have 1,500 personnel and about 62 aircraft. The Air Defense Forces operate [MANPADS](1389714.md) hand-held missiles, [surface-to-air missile](182664.md) (SAM) batteries, anti-aircraft cannons, and radar. The Army has recently adopted remodeled [MARPAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MARPAT) uniforms, used by Bosnian soldiers serving with the [International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force) (ISAF) in [Afghanistan](737.md). A domestic production program is now underway to ensure that army units are equipped with the correct ammunition.[^cite_note-176]

Beginning in 2007, the Ministry of Defence undertook the army's first ever international assistance mission, enlisting the military to serve with ISAF peace missions to Afghanistan, [Iraq](7515928.md) and the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md) in 2007. Five officers, acting as officers/advisors, served in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. 45 soldiers, mostly acting as base security and medical assistants, served in Afghanistan. 85 Bosnian soldiers served as base security in Iraq, occasionally conducting infantry patrols there as well. All three deployed groups have been commended by their respective international forces as well as the Ministry of Defence of Bosnia and Herzegovina. The international assistance operations are still ongoing.[^cite_note-NATO_2015-177]

The Air Force and Anti-Aircraft Defence of Bosnia and Herzegovina was formed when elements of the Army of the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and the [Republika Srpska Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republika_Srpska_Air_Force) were merged in 2006. The Air Force has seen improvements in the last few years with added funds for aircraft repairs and improved cooperation with the [Ground Forces](1376.md) as well as to the citizens of the country. The Ministry of Defence is pursuing the acquisition of new aircraft including helicopters and perhaps even fighter jets.[^cite_note-178]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)

See also: [List of companies of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_companies_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)



Estimated development of real GDP per capita of Bosnia and Herzegovina, since 1952

During the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War), the economy suffered €200 billion in material damages, roughly €356.80 billion in 2025 (inflation adjusted).[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180] Bosnia and Herzegovina faces the dual-problem of rebuilding a war-torn country and introducing transitional liberal market reforms to its formerly mixed economy. One legacy of the previous era is a strong industry; under former republic president [Džemal Bijedić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%BEemal_Bijedi%C4%87) and [Yugoslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) President [Josip Broz Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito), metal industries were promoted in the republic, resulting in the development of a large share of Yugoslavia's plants; [SR Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) had a very strong industrial export oriented economy in the 1970s and 1980s, with large scale exports worth millions of [US\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USD).

For most of Bosnia's history, agriculture has been conducted on privately owned farms; fresh food has traditionally been exported from the republic.[^cite_note-181]

The war in the 1990s, caused a dramatic change in the Bosnian economy.[^cite_note-182] GDP fell by 60% and the destruction of physical infrastructure devastated the economy.[^cite_note-183] With much of the production capacity unrestored, the Bosnian economy still faces considerable difficulties. Figures show GDP and per capita income increased 10% from 2003 to 2004; this and Bosnia's shrinking [national debt](695460.md) being negative trends, and high unemployment 38.7% and a large [trade deficit](4810.md) remain cause for concern.

The national currency is the (Euro-pegged) [convertible mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_convertible_mark) (KM), controlled by the [currency board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_board). Annual inflation is the lowest relative to other countries in the region at 1.9% in 2004.[^cite_note-184] The international debt was \$5.1 billion (as of 31 December 2014). [Real GDP](12594.md) growth rate was 5% for 2004 according to the [Central Bank of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and Statistical Office of Bosnia and Herzegovina.

Bosnia and Herzegovina has displayed positive progress in the previous years, which decisively moved its place from the lowest income equality rank of [income equality rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_income_equality) fourteen out of 193 nations.[^cite_note-185]

According to [Eurostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostat) data, Bosnia and Herzegovina's PPS GDP per capita stood at 29 per cent of the EU average in 2010.[^cite_note-186]

The [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) (IMF) announced a loan to Bosnia worth US\$500 million to be delivered by [Stand-By Arrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMF_Stand-By_Arrangement). This was scheduled to be approved in September 2012.[^cite_note-187]

The [United States Embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_the_United_States,_Sarajevo) in [Sarajevo](26786.md) produces the Country Commercial Guide – an annual report that delivers a comprehensive look at Bosnia and Herzegovina's commercial and economic environment, using economic, political, and market analysis.

By some estimates, [grey economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_economy) is 25.5% of GDP.[^cite_note-188]

In 2017, exports grew by 17% when compared to the previous year, totaling €5.65 billion.[^cite_note-189] The total volume of [foreign trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade) in 2017 amounted to €14.97 billion and increased by 14% compared to the previous year. Imports of goods increased by 12% and amounted to €9.32 billion. The [coverage of imports by exports](4810.md) increased by 3% compared to the previous year and now it is 61 percent. In 2017, Bosnia and Herzegovina mostly exported [car seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_seat), [electricity](9550.md), [processed wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_processing), [aluminium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium) and [furniture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furniture). In the same year, it mostly imported [crude oil](23195.md), [automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car), [motor oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_oil), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) and [briquettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briquette).[^cite_note-190]

The unemployment rate in 2017 was 20.5%, but [The Vienna Institute for International Economic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vienna_Institute_for_International_Economic_Studies) is predicting falling unemployment rate for the next few years. In 2018, the unemployment should be 19.4% and it should further fall to 18.8% in 2019. In 2020, the unemployment rate should go down to 18.3%.[^cite_note-191]



The [Avaz Twist Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avaz_Twist_Tower) in [Sarajevo](26786.md), the tallest building in Bosnia and Herzegovina

On 31 December 2017, the [Council of Ministers of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) issued the report on public debt of Bosnia and Herzegovina, stating the public debt was reduced by €389.97 million, or by more than 6% when compared to 31 December 2016. By the end of 2017, public debt was €5.92 billion, which amounted to 35.6 percent of GDP.[^cite_note-192]

As of 31 December 2017, there were 32,292 registered companies in the country, which together had revenues of €33.572 billion that same year.[^cite_note-193]

In 2017, the country received €397.35 million in [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment), which equals to 2.5% of the GDP.[^cite_note-194]

In 2017, Bosnia and Herzegovina ranked third in the world in terms of the number of new jobs created by foreign investment, relative to the number of inhabitants.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196]

In 2018, Bosnia and Herzegovina exported goods worth 11.9 billion KM (€6.07 billion), which is 7.43% higher than in the same period in 2017, while imports amounted to 19.27 billion KM (€9.83 billion), which is 5.47% higher.[^cite_note-197]

The average price of new apartments sold in the country in the first six months of 2018 is 1,639 km (€886.31) per square metre. This represents a jump of 3.5% from the previous year.[^cite_note-198]

### Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)

[Mehmed Paša Sokolović Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_Pa%C5%A1a_Sokolovi%C4%87_Bridge) in [Višegrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi%C5%A1egrad), a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md) and [Andrićgrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andri%C4%87grad)

According to projections by the [World Tourism Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Tourism_Organization), Bosnia and Herzegovina had the third highest tourism growth rate in the world between 1995 and 2020.[^cite_note-Traveloholic_Tours-216][^cite_note-Travel_Talk_Tours_2017-217]

In 2017, 1,307,319 tourists visited Bosnia and Herzegovina, an increase of 13.7%, and had 2,677,125 overnight hotel stays, a 12.3% increase from the previous year. 71.5% of the tourists came from foreign countries.[^cite_note-218]

In 2018, 1.883.772 tourists visited Bosnia and Herzegovina, an increase of 44,1%, and had 3.843.484 overnight hotel stays, a 43.5% increase from the previous year. Also, 71.2% of the tourists came from foreign countries.[^cite_note-219]

In 2006, when ranking the best cities in the world, [Lonely Planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonely_Planet) placed [Sarajevo](26786.md), the [national capital](181337.md)[^cite_note-Constitution-1] and host of the [1984 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Winter_Olympics), as #43 on the list.[^cite_note-220] Tourism in Sarajevo is chiefly focused on historical, religious, and cultural aspects. In 2010, Lonely Planet's "Best in Travel" nominated it as one of the top ten cities to visit that year.[^cite_note-221] Sarajevo also won travel blog Foxnomad's "Best City to Visit" competition in 2012, beating more than one hundred other cities around the entire world.[^cite_note-222]

[Međugorje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medjugorje) has become one of the most popular pilgrimage sites for [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholics) from around the world and has turned into Europe's third most important religious place, where each year more than 1 million people visit.[^cite_note-223] It has been estimated that 30 million pilgrims have come to Međugorje since the reputed apparitions began in 1981.[^cite_note-reuters-224] Since 2019, pilgrimages to Međugorje have been officially authorized and organized by the [Vatican](13393.md).[^cite_note-Vatican_News_2019-225]

Bosnia has also become an increasingly popular skiing and [Ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) destination. The mountains that hosted the winter olympic games of [Bjelašnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bjela%C5%A1nica), [Jahorina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahorina), and [Igman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igman) are the most visited skiing mountains in Bosnia-Herzegovina. Bosnia and Herzegovina remains one of the last undiscovered natural regions of the southern area of the [Alps](981.md), with vast tracts of wild and untouched nature attracting adventurers and nature lovers. *[National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic)* named Bosnia and Herzegovina as the best mountain biking adventure destination for 2012.[^cite_note-test-226] The central [Bosnian Dinaric Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinaric_Alps) are favored by hikers and mountaineers, as they contain both [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) and [Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate) climates. [Whitewater rafting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafting) has become somewhat of a [national pastime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_sport) in Bosnia and Herzegovina.[^cite_note-Zuvela_2007-227] The primary rivers used for whitewater rafting in the country include the [Vrbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrbas_\(river\)), [Tara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tara_\(Drina\)), [Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina), [Neretva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neretva), and [Una](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Una_\(Sava\)).[^cite_note-All_about_buses_2018-228] Meanwhile, the most prominent rivers are the Vrbas and Tara, as they both hosted [The 2009 World Rafting Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Rafting_Federation).[^cite_note-IRF_admin_2010-229][^cite_note-Sito-Sucic_2009-230] The reason the Tara river is immensely popular for whitewater rafting is because it contains the deepest [river canyon](78478.md) in Europe, the [Tara River Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tara_River_Canyon).[^cite_note-Tara_Sport_2019-231][^cite_note-The_Telegraph_2016-232]

Most recently, the *[Huffington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HuffPost)* named Bosnia and Herzegovina the "9th Greatest Adventure in the World for 2013", adding that the country boasts "the cleanest water and air in Europe; the greatest untouched forests; and the most wildlife. The best way to experience is the three rivers trip, which purls through the best the Balkans have to offer."[^cite_note-233]

### Transport

[Sarajevo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarajevo_International_Airport)

[Sarajevo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarajevo_International_Airport), also known as Butmir Airport, is the main [international airport](1519705.md) in Bosnia and Herzegovina, located 3.3 [NM](21289.md) (6.1 km; 3.8 mi) southwest of the [Sarajevo main railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarajevo_main_railway_station)[^cite_note-AIP-234] in the city of [Sarajevo](26786.md) in the suburb of [Butmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butmir).

Railway operations in Bosnia and Herzegovina are successors of the [Yugoslav Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Railways) within the country boundaries following independence from the [former Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) in 1992. Today, they are operated by the [Railways of the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railways_of_the_Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) (ŽFBiH) in the [Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and by [Republika Srpska Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republika_Srpska_Railways) (ŽRS) in [Republika Srpska](347422.md).

### Telecommunications

Main article: [Telecommunications in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)

The Bosnian communications market was fully [liberalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalization) in January 2006. The three [landline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landline) telephone operators predominantly provide services in their operating areas but have nationwide licenses for domestic and international calls. Mobile data services are also available, including high-speed [EDGE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_Data_rates_for_GSM_Evolution), [3G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G) and [4G](486547.md) services.[^cite_note-235]

*[Oslobođenje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslobo%C4%91enje)* (Liberation), founded in 1943, is one of the country's longest running continuously circulating newspapers. There are many national publications, including the *[Dnevni avaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnevni_avaz)* (Daily Voice), founded in 1995, and *Jutarnje Novine* (Morning News), to name but a few in circulation in Sarajevo.[^cite_note-236] Other local periodicals include the Croatian *[Hrvatska riječ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrvatska_rije%C4%8D)* newspaper and Bosnian *[Start](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Start_\(Bosnian_magazine\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)* magazine, as well as *Slobodna Bosna* (*Free Bosnia*) and *BH Dani* (*BH Days*) weekly newspapers. *[Novi Plamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Plamen)*, a monthly magazine, was the most left-wing publication. International news station [Al Jazeera](30204052.md) maintains a [sister channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_channel) catering to the [Balkan](4829.md) region, [Al Jazeera Balkans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_Balkans), broadcasting out of and based in Sarajevo.[^cite_note-237] Since 2014, the [N1 platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N1_\(TV_channel\)) has broadcast as an affiliate of [CNN International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN_International), with offices in Sarajevo, [Zagreb](55906.md) and [Belgrade](55904.md).[^cite_note-238]

As of 2021, Bosnia and Herzegovina ranked second highest in [press freedom](105323.md) in the region, after [Croatia](5573.md), and is placed 58th internationally.[^cite_note-239]

As of December 2021, there are 3,374,094 internet users in the country, or 95.55% of the entire population.[^cite_note-240]

## Demographics

Main articles: [Demographics of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and [Bosnians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnians)

See also: [Demographic history of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)

According to the [1991 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_population_census_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), Bosnia and Herzegovina had a population of 4,369,319, while the 1996 World Bank Group census showed a decrease to 3,764,425.[^cite_note-241] Large population migrations during the [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars) in the 1990s have caused demographic shifts in the country. Between 1991 and 2013, political disagreements made it impossible to organize a census. A census had been planned for 2011,[^cite_note-242] and then for 2012,[^cite_note-243] but was delayed until October 2013. The [2013 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_population_census_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) found a total population of 3,531,159 people,[^cite_note-Popis2013-12] a drop of approximately 20% since 1991.[^cite_note-244] The 2013 census figures include non-permanent Bosnian residents and for this reason are contested by Republika Srpska officials and Serb politicians (see Ethnic groups below).[^cite_note-WithoutSerbAgreement-245]

According to [United Nations Population Fund](63952.md), between 2013 and 2020, Bosnia and Herzegovina was losing 25,000 people yearly. The demographic expert Stevo Palašić concluded that between 1991 and 2023, Bosnia and Herzegovina lost 1.4 million people or one-third of its population. [Tim Judah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Judah) considers the 2022 state agency estimate of 3.4 million inhabitants "extremely optimistic" and wrongly based on the 2013 census. Pašalić, who based his calculation on the official data, estimates the number to 2.9 million. Other demographic experts from Bosnia and Herzegovina, including Miodrag Pantović and Nermin Oruč, estimate that the total population in Bosnia and Herzegovina is slightly below 1.5 million.[^cite_note-246]

Bosnia and Herzegovina is home to three ethnic "[constituent peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)", namely [Bosniaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniaks), [Serbs](59512.md) and [Croats](69801.md), plus a number of smaller groups including [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina).[^cite_note-249] According to data from the 2013 census published by the [Agency for Statistics of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistical_system_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina#The_Agency_for_Statistics_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_\(BHAS\)), Bosniaks constitute 50.1% of the population, Serbs 30.8%, Croats 15.5% and others 2.7%, with the remaining respondents not declaring their ethnicity or not answering.[^cite_note-Popis2013-12] The census results are contested by the [Republika Srpska](347422.md) statistical office and by [Bosnian Serb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) politicians.[^cite_note-250] The dispute over the census concerns the inclusion of non-permanent Bosnian residents in the figures, which Republika Srpska officials oppose.[^cite_note-WithoutSerbAgreement-245] The [European Union](9317.md)'s statistics office, [Eurostat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostat), concluded in May 2016 that the census methodology used by the Bosnian statistical agency is in line with international recommendations.[^cite_note-251]

### Languages

Bosnia's constitution does not specify any official languages.[^cite_note-rights-2][^cite_note-252][^cite_note-Footitt-4] However, the [Dayton Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Agreement) was written in four versions—[Bosnian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_language), [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language), English, and [Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language). Academics Hilary Footitt and Michael Kelly describe this as "de facto recognition of three official languages" at the state level. The equal status of Bosnian, Serbian, and Croatian was verified by the [Constitutional Court](11273863.md) in 2000.[^cite_note-Footitt-4] It ruled that the provisions of the [Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and Republika Srpska constitutions on language were incompatible with the state constitution, since they only recognised Bosnian and Croatian (in the case of the Federation) and Serbian (in the case of Republika Srpska) as official languages at the entity level. As a result, the wording of the entity constitutions was changed and all three languages were made official in both entities.[^cite_note-Footitt-4] The three [standard languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_language) are fully [mutually intelligible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_intelligibility) and are known collectively under the appellation of [Serbo-Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbo-Croatian), though this term is not formally recognized in the country. Use of one of the three languages has become a marker of ethnic identity.[^cite_note-253] Michael Kelly and Catherine Baker argue, "The three official languages of today's Bosnian state...represent the symbolic assertion of national identity over the pragmatism of mutual intelligibility".[^cite_note-254]

According to the 1992 [European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Charter_for_Regional_or_Minority_Languages) (ECRML), Bosnia and Herzegovina recognizes the following minority languages: [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_language), [Montenegrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegrin_language), [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language), [Italian](14708.md), [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language), [Macedonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_language), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language), [Romani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_language), [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language), [Rusyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusyn_language), [Slovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_language), [Slovene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovene_language), [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language), [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language), and Jewish ([Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish) and [Ladino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaeo-Spanish)).[^cite_note-255]

The German minority in Bosnia and Herzegovina are mostly remnants of [Danube Swabians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Swabians), who settled in the area after the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) claimed the Balkans from the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). [Expulsions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_and_expulsion_of_Germans_\(1944%E2%80%931950\)) and [(forced) assimilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Germans) after the two [World wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_war) drastically diminished the number of ethnic Germans in Bosnia and Herzegovina.[^cite_note-256]

In the 2013 census, 52.86% of the population consider their mother tongue Bosnian, 30.76% Serbian, 14.6% Croatian, and 1.57% another language, with 0.21% not giving an answer.[^cite_note-Popis2013-12]

### Education

Main article: [Education in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)



The [University of Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sarajevo)'s [Faculty of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_of_Law,_University_of_Sarajevo)



[Gimnazija Mostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimnazija_Mostar) in [Mostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostar)&#x20;

Primary schooling lasts for nine years. Secondary education is provided by general and technical secondary schools (typically [Gymnasiums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_\(school\))) where studies typically last for four years. All forms of secondary schooling include an element of [vocational training](56551.md). Pupils graduating from general secondary schools obtain the *[Matura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matura)* and can enroll in any tertiary educational institution or academy by passing a qualification examination prescribed by the governing body or institution. Students graduating technical subjects obtain a [Diploma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diploma).[^cite_note-258]

The first bespoke higher-education institution was a school of [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) philosophy established by [Gazi Husrev-beg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazi_Husrev_Bey) in 1531. Numerous other religious schools then followed. In 1887, under the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), a [Sharia](28840.md) law school began a five-year program.[^cite_note-259]

In the 1940s, the [University of Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sarajevo) became the city's first secular higher education institute. In the 1950s, post-baccalaurate graduate degrees became available.[^cite_note-260] Severely damaged during the [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War), it was recently rebuilt in partnership with more than 40 other universities. ### Media

Main article: [Mass media in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)



[BHRT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_and_Television_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) headquarters in Sarajevo

Some television, magazines, and newspapers in Bosnia and Herzegovina are state-owned, and some are for-profit corporations funded by [advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising), [subscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscription_business_model), and other sales-related revenues. The [Constitution of Bosnia and Herzegovina](12844303.md) guarantees [freedom of speech](21401843.md).

As a [country in transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_economy) with a post-war legacy and a [complex domestic political structure](3606.md), Bosnia and Herzegovina's media system is under transformation. In the early post-war period (1995–2005), media development was guided mainly by international donors and cooperation agencies, who invested to help reconstruct, diversify, democratize and professionalize media outlets.[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-EJC-263]

Post-war developments included the establishment of an independent Communication Regulatory Agency, the adoption of a Press Code, the establishment of the Press Council, the decriminalization of libel and defamation, the introduction of a rather advanced Freedom of Access to Information Law, and the creation of a Public Service Broadcasting System from the formerly state-owned broadcaster. Yet, internationally backed positive developments have been often obstructed by domestic elites, and the professionalisation of media and journalists has proceeded only slowly. High levels of partisanship and linkages between the media and the political systems hinder the adherence to professional code of conducts.[^cite_note-EJC-263]

### Literature

Main article: [Literature of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)



[Ivo Andrić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivo_Andri%C4%87) with his wife [Milica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milica_Babi%C4%87-Jovanovi%C4%87), upon learning he had won the [Nobel Prize in Literature](23385442.md)

Bosnia and Herzegovina has a rich literature, including the [Nobel Prize](21201.md) winner [Ivo Andrić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivo_Andri%C4%87) and poets such as [Antun Branko Šimić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antun_Branko_%C5%A0imi%C4%87), [Aleksa Šantić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksa_%C5%A0anti%C4%87), [Jovan Dučić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jovan_Du%C4%8Di%C4%87) and [Mak Dizdar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mak_Dizdar), writers such as [Zlatko Topčić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zlatko_Top%C4%8Di%C4%87), [Meša Selimović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me%C5%A1a_Selimovi%C4%87), [Semezdin Mehmedinović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semezdin_Mehmedinovi%C4%87), [Miljenko Jergović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miljenko_Jergovi%C4%87), [Isak Samokovlija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isak_Samokovlija), [Safvet-beg Bašagić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safvet-beg_Ba%C5%A1agi%C4%87), [Abdulah Sidran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulah_Sidran), [Petar Kočić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petar_Ko%C4%8Di%C4%87), [Aleksandar Hemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_Hemon), [Mehmed Begić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_Begi%C4%87) and [Nedžad Ibrišimović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ned%C5%BEad_Ibri%C5%A1imovi%C4%87).

The [National Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarajevo_National_Theatre) was founded in 1919 in [Sarajevo](26786.md) and its first director was dramatist [Branislav Nušić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branislav_Nu%C5%A1i%C4%87). Magazines such as *[Novi Plamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Plamen)* or *Sarajevske sveske* are some of the more prominent publications covering cultural and literary themes.

By the late 1950s, Ivo Andrić's works had been translated into a number of languages. In 1958, the [Association of Writers of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Writers_of_Yugoslavia) nominated Andrić as its first ever candidate for the [Nobel Prize in Literature](23385442.md)

### Art

Main article: [Bosnian and Herzegovinan art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_and_Herzegovinan_art)



[Stećci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ste%C4%87ak) from [Radimlja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ste%C4%87ak_necropolis_Radimlja), near [Stolac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolac) (13th century)

The [art of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_art) was always evolving and ranged from the original medieval tombstones called [Stećci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ste%C4%87ak) to paintings in [Kotromanić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotromani%C4%87_dynasty) court. Twenty stećak necropolis sites in Bosnia and Herzegovina were added on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2006.[^cite_note-264] However, only with the arrival of [Austro-Hungarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) did the painting renaissance in Bosnia really begin to flourish. The first educated artists from European academies appeared with the beginning of the 20th century. Among those are: [Gabrijel Jurkić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabrijel_Jurki%C4%87), [Petar Šain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petar_%C5%A0ain), [Roman Petrović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Petrovi%C4%87) and [Lazar Drljača](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazar_Drlja%C4%8Da). After [World War II](32927.md), artists like [Mersad Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersad_Berber) and [Safet Zec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safet_Zec) rose in popularity. In 2007, [Ars Aevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ars_Aevi), a museum of contemporary art that includes works by renowned world artists, was founded in Sarajevo.

### Music

Main article: [Music of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina)

See also: [List of Bosnia and Herzegovina patriotic songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_patriotic_songs) and [Bosnian root music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_root_music)



[Bosniaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosniaks) dancing a traditional [kolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolo_\(dance\))

Typical Bosnian songs are *[ganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganga_\(music\)), rera*, and the traditional Slavic music for the folk dances such as *[kolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolo_\(dance\))*, while from the Ottoman era the most popular is [Sevdalinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevdalinka). Pop and Rock music has a tradition here as well, with the more famous musicians including [Dino Zonić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Zoni%C4%87), [Goran Bregović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goran_Bregovi%C4%87), [Davorin Popović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davorin_Popovi%C4%87), [Kemal Monteno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemal_Monteno), [Zdravko Čolić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zdravko_%C4%8Coli%C4%87), [Elvir Laković Laka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvir_Lakovi%C4%87_Laka), [Edo Maajka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_Maajka), [Hari Varešanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Vare%C5%A1anovi%C4%87), [Dino Merlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Merlin), [Mladen Vojičić Tifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mladen_Voji%C4%8Di%C4%87_Tifa), [Željko Bebek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDeljko_Bebek), etc. Other composers such as [Đorđe Novković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90or%C4%91e_Novkovi%C4%87), [Al' Dino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al'_Dino), [Haris Džinović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haris_D%C5%BEinovi%C4%87), [Kornelije Kovač](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kornelije_Kova%C4%8D), and many [rock and pop bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_ensemble#Rock_and_pop_bands), for example, [Bijelo Dugme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijelo_Dugme), [Crvena jabuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crvena_jabuka), [Divlje jagode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divlje_jagode), [Indexi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indexi), [Plavi orkestar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plavi_orkestar), [Zabranjeno Pušenje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabranjeno_Pu%C5%A1enje), [Ambasadori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambasadori), [Dubioza kolektiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubioza_kolektiv), who were among the leading ones in the former Yugoslavia. Bosnia is home to the composer [Dušan Šestić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_%C5%A0esti%C4%87), the creator of the [National Anthem of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Anthem_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and father of singer [Marija Šestić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marija_%C5%A0esti%C4%87), to the jazz musician, educator and Bosnian jazz ambassador [Sinan Alimanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinan_Alimanovi%C4%87), composer [Saša Lošić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%C5%A1a_Lo%C5%A1i%C4%87) and pianist [Saša Toperić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%C5%A1a_Toperi%C4%87). In the villages, especially in [Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzegovina), Bosniaks, Serbs and Croats play the ancient [gusle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gusle). The gusle is used mainly to recite epic poems in a usually dramatic tone.

Probably the most distinctive and identifiably "Bosnian" of music, Sevdalinka is a kind of emotional, melancholic folk song that often describes sad subjects such as love and loss, the death of a dear person or heartbreak.[^cite_note-265] Sevdalinkas were traditionally performed with a [saz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C4%9Flama), a Turkish string instrument, which was later replaced by the accordion.[^cite_note-266] However the more modern arrangement is typically a vocalist accompanied by the accordion along with snare drums, upright bass, guitars, clarinets and violins. In 2024, Sevdalinka was included on the [UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Intangible_Cultural_Heritage_Lists).[^cite_note-unesco-267]

&#x20;\




[Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbs_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) from [Bosanska Krajina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosanska_Krajina) in traditional clothing

Rural folk traditions in Bosnia and Herzegovina include the shouted, [polyphonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyphony) ganga and "ravne pjesme" (*flat song*) styles, as well as instruments like a droneless [bagpipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagpipes), wooden [flute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flute) and [šargija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0argija). The gusle, an instrument found throughout the [Balkans](4829.md), is also used to accompany ancient [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) [epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_poetry) [poems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry). There are also Bosnian folk songs in the [Ladino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaeo-Spanish) language, derived from the area's Jewish population.

*Bosnian roots music* came from [Central Bosnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bosnia), [Posavina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posavina), the [Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina) valley and [Kalesija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalesija). It is usually performed by singers with two violinists and a šargija player. These bands first appeared around [World War I](4764461.md) and became popular in the 1960s. This is the third oldest music after the Sevdalinka and [ilahija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasheed). Self-taught people, mostly in two or three members of the different choices of old instruments, mostly in the violin, sacking, saz, drums, flutes (*zurle*) or wooden flute, as others have already called, the original performers of Bosnian music that can not be written notes, transmitted by ear from generation to generation, family is usually hereditary. It is thought to be brought from Persia-Kalesi tribe that settled in the area of the present Sprečanski valleys and hence probably the name Kalesija. In this part of Bosnia, it is the most common. This kind of music was enjoyed by all three peoples in Bosnia, Bosniaks, Serbs and Croats, and it contributed a lot to reconcile people socializing, entertainment and other organizations through festivals. In Kalesija, it is maintained each year with the original Bosnian Festival music.

### Cuisine

Main article: [Bosnia and Herzegovina cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_cuisine)



Bosnian meat platter that contains, among other things, [ćevapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%86evapi), which is considered the [national dish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_dish) of Bosnia and Herzegovina

Bosnian cuisine uses many spices, in moderate quantities. Most dishes are light, as they are boiled; the sauces are fully natural, consisting of little more than the natural juices of the vegetables in the dish. Typical ingredients include tomatoes, potatoes, onions, [garlic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlic), [peppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsicum), [cucumbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucumber), carrots, [cabbage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabbage), [mushrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushroom), [spinach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinach), [zucchini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zucchini), [dried beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bean), fresh beans, [plums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plum), milk, [paprika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paprika) and cream called [pavlaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smetana_\(dairy_product\)). Bosnian cuisine is balanced between [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture) and [Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_world) influences. As a result of the [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_cuisine) administration for almost 500 years, Bosnian food is closely related to Turkish, [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_cuisine) and other former Ottoman and [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_cuisine) cuisines. However, due to Austrian rule, there are many influences from [Central Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_cuisine). Typical meat dishes include primarily beef and [lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_and_mutton). Some local specialties are [ćevapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%86evapi), [burek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B6rek), [dolma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolma), [sarma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarma_\(food\)), [pilav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilaf), [goulash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goulash), [ajvar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajvar) and a whole range of Eastern sweets. Ćevapi is a grilled dish of minced meat, a type of [kebab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kebab), popular in former Yugoslavia and considered a national dish in Bosnia and Herzegovina[^cite_note-Encyclopedia_Britannica-28] and [Serbia](29265.md).[^cite_note-269][^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271]

Local wines come from [Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzegovina) where the climate is suitable for growing grapes. Herzegovinian *loza* (similar to Italian [Grappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grappa) but less sweet) is very popular. Plum (*rakija*) or apple (*jabukovača*) alcohol beverages are produced in the north. In the south, distilleries used to produce vast quantities of [brandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandy) and supply all of ex-Yugoslav alcohol factories (brandy is the base of most [alcoholic drinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_drink)).

Coffeehouses, where [Bosnian coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_coffee) is served in [džezva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cezve) with [rahat lokum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_delight) and [sugar cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_sugar), are common in [Sarajevo](26786.md) and every city in the country. Coffee drinking is a favorite Bosnian pastime and part of the culture. Bosnia and Herzegovina is the ninth country in the entire world by per capita coffee consumption.[^cite_note-272]

The [Bosna Royal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KK_Bosna_Royal) [basketball](3921.md) club from Sarajevo were [European Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euroleague_Basketball) in [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978%E2%80%9379_FIBA_European_Champions_Cup). The [Yugoslavia men's national basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_men's_national_basketball_team), which won medals in every world championship from 1963 through 1990, included Bosnian players such as [FIBA Hall of Famers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Hall_of_Fame) [Dražen Dalipagić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dra%C5%BEen_Dalipagi%C4%87) and [Mirza Delibašić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirza_Deliba%C5%A1i%C4%87). Bosnia and Herzegovina regularly qualifies for the [European Championship in Basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket), with players including [Mirza Teletović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirza_Teletovi%C4%87), [Nihad Đedović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihad_%C4%90edovi%C4%87) and [Jusuf Nurkić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jusuf_Nurki%C4%87). The [Bosnia and Herzegovina national under-16 team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_men's_national_under-16_and_under-17_basketball_team) won two gold medals in 2015, winning both [2015 European Youth Summer Olympic Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_at_the_2015_European_Youth_Summer_Olympic_Festival) as well as the [2015 FIBA Europe Under-16 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIBA_Europe_Under-16_Championship).

Women's basketball club [Jedinstvo Aida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDKK_Jedinstvo_Tuzla) from [Tuzla](524638.md) won the [Women's European Club Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroLeague_Women) in 1989 and went to the [Ronchetti Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronchetti_Cup) final in 1990, led by [Razija Mujanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razija_Mujanovi%C4%87).[^cite_note-276]

The Bosnian [chess](5134.md) team was [Champion of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Chess_Championship) seven times, in addition to club [ŠK Bosna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0K_Bosna) winning four [European Chess Club Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Chess_Club_Cup). Chess grandmaster [Borki Predojević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borki_Predojevi%C4%87) has also won two European Championships. The most impressive success of Bosnian Chess was a runner-up position at the [31st Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/31st_Chess_Olympiad) in 1994 in [Moscow](19004.md), featuring Grandmasters [Predrag Nikolić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predrag_Nikoli%C4%87), [Ivan Sokolov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Sokolov_\(chess_player\)) and [Bojan Kurajica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bojan_Kurajica).

Middle-weight [boxer](4243.md) [Marijan Beneš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marijan_Bene%C5%A1) has won several Championships of Bosnia and Herzegovina, Yugoslav Championships and the [European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Amateur_Boxing_Championships).[^cite_note-277] In 1978, he won the World Title against [Elisha Obed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisha_Obed) from [The Bahamas](3451.md).



[Edin Džeko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edin_D%C5%BEeko) playing for [Bosnia and Herzegovina](1008974.md) in 2015

[Association football](10568.md) is the most popular sport in Bosnia and Herzegovina. It dates from 1903, but its popularity grew significantly after [World War I](4764461.md). Bosnian clubs [FK Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Sarajevo) and [Željezničar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_%C5%BDeljezni%C4%8Dar_Sarajevo) won the [Yugoslav Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_First_League), while the [Yugoslav national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) included Bosnian players of all ethnic backgrounds and generations, such as [Safet Sušić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safet_Su%C5%A1i%C4%87), [Zlatko Vujović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zlatko_Vujovi%C4%87), [Mehmed Baždarević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_Ba%C5%BEdarevi%C4%87), [Davor Jozić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davor_Jozi%C4%87), [Faruk Hadžibegić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faruk_Had%C5%BEibegi%C4%87), [Predrag Pašić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predrag_Pa%C5%A1i%C4%87), [Blaž Slišković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bla%C5%BE_Sli%C5%A1kovi%C4%87), [Vahid Halilhodžić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vahid_Halilhod%C5%BEi%C4%87), [Dušan Bajević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_Bajevi%C4%87), [Ivica Osim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivica_Osim), [Josip Katalinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Katalinski), [Tomislav Knez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomislav_Knez), [Velimir Sombolac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velimir_Sombolac) and numerous others.&#x20;

The [Bosnia and Herzegovina national football team](1008974.md) played at the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md), its first major tournament.[^cite_note-278] Players on the team again includes notable players of all country's ethnic background, such as then and now captains [Emir Spahić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir_Spahi%C4%87), [Zvjezdan Misimović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvjezdan_Misimovi%C4%87) and [Edin Džeko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edin_D%C5%BEeko), defenders like [Ognjen Vranješ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ognjen_Vranje%C5%A1), [Sead Kolašinac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sead_Kola%C5%A1inac) and [Toni Šunjić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_%C5%A0unji%C4%87), midfielders like [Miralem Pjanić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miralem_Pjani%C4%87) and [Senad Lulić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senad_Luli%C4%87), striker [Vedad Ibišević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedad_Ibi%C5%A1evi%C4%87), etc. Former Bosnian footballers include [Hasan Salihamidžić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasan_Salihamid%C5%BEi%C4%87), who became only the second Bosnian to ever win a [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) trophy, after [Elvir Baljić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvir_Balji%C4%87). He made 234 appearances and scored 31 goals for German club [FC Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich). [Sergej Barbarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergej_Barbarez), who played for several clubs in the German [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga). including [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund), [Hamburger SV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger_SV) and [Bayer Leverkusen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer_04_Leverkusen) was joint-top scorer in the [2000–01 Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_Bundesliga) season with 22 goals. [Meho Kodro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meho_Kodro) spent most of his career playing in Spain, most notably with [Real Sociedad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Sociedad) and [FC Barcelona](68187.md). [Elvir Rahimić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvir_Rahimi%C4%87) made 302 appearances for Russian club [CSKA Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFC_CSKA_Moscow) with whom he won the [UEFA Cup](232175.md) in [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_UEFA_Cup_Final). [Milena Nikolić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milena_Nikoli%C4%87), member of the [women's national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_women's_national_football_team), was the [2013–14 UEFA Women's Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League) [top scorer](1985338.md#Top_scorers_by_tournament).[^cite_note-uefa.com-nikolic-scorer-279]

Bosnia and Herzegovina was the world champion of [volleyball at the 2004 Summer Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volleyball_at_the_2004_Summer_Paralympics) and [volleyball at the 2012 Summer Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volleyball_at_the_2012_Summer_Paralympics). Many among those on the team lost their legs in the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War). Its [national sitting volleyball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_national_sitting_volleyball_team) is one of the dominant forces in [the sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitting_volleyball) worldwide, winning nine European Championships, three World Championships and two [Paralympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volleyball_at_the_Summer_Paralympics) gold medals.

[Tennis](29773.md) is also gaining a lot of popularity after the recent successes of [Damir Džumhur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damir_D%C5%BEumhur) and [Mirza Bašić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirza_Ba%C5%A1i%C4%87) at [Grand Slam](197638.md) level. Other notable tennis players who have represented Bosnia and Herzegovina are [Tomislav Brkić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomislav_Brki%C4%87), [Amer Delić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amer_Deli%C4%87) and [Mervana Jugić-Salkić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mervana_Jugi%C4%87-Salki%C4%87).
