The **Cape Verde national football team** ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *seleção nacional de futebol de Cabo Verde*), recognised as **Cabo Verde** ([Kabuverdianu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabuverdianu_language): *Kabu Verdi*) by [FIFA](11049.md),[^cite_note-4] represents [Cape Verde](18962637.md) in men's international [football](10568.md), and is controlled by the [Cape Verdean Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verdean_Football_Federation). Nicknamed **Blue Sharks** (*Tubarões Azuis* in Portuguese), the national team played its first match on 19 April 1978 against [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_national_football_team), a match they lost 0–1. Following the federation's affiliation with the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) and FIFA in 1982, the national team entered [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) qualification for the first time in 1992 and made its first [FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) appearance in 2003. Cape Verde plays the majority of its home matches at the [Estádio Nacional de Cabo Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Nacional_de_Cabo_Verde). They qualified for the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) for the first time in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Africa_Cup_of_Nations). Since then, they also appeared in the [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) and [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) tournaments, reaching the quarter-finals in 2013 and 2023.

Cape Verde qualified for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) for the first time in [2026](36581929.md), becoming the smallest country by land area and the second least populated (after [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland_national_football_team)) to qualify for the World Cup, with a land area of 4,033 square kilometres (1,557 sq mi) and a population of just under 525,000, until [Curaçao](24862626.md) broke both records just five weeks later by qualifying for the same tournament.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-bbc-6] In the [group stage](81660199.md), they finished second with three draws: 0–0 against the reigning [European champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) [Spain](609825.md), 2–2 with [Uruguay](679782.md), and 0–0 with [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team).[^cite_note-apnews_2026-06-21-7] Cape Verde qualified for the knockout rounds and were eliminated in a 3–2 loss to [defending champions](17742072.md) [Argentina](454699.md) after extra time.

## History

### Early History

[Cape Verde](18962637.md) became independent from [Portugal](23033.md) in 1975. The national team's first international was a 1–0 defeat to [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_national_football_team) on 29 May 1978, in a tournament in [Guinea-Bissau](12186.md).[^cite_note-8] The [Cape Verdean Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verdean_Football_Federation) was formed in 1982, and joined [FIFA](11049.md) in 1986.[^cite_note-9]

On 2 November 2002, Cape Verde faced a non-African team for the first time, in a scoreless friendly against [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_national_football_team).

[Cape Verdeans abroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verdean_diaspora), who are more numerous than the population of the islands themselves, are a major source of players for the national team. Most of Cape Verde's current international footballers play outside Cape Verde (mainly in Europe, but also in Asia), and some were born outside the islands.[^cite_note-FIFA1-10]

On 4 September 2009, Cape Verde faced [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_national_football_team) in a friendly, resulting in a 2–0 victory.

### 2010s

On 24 May 2010, Cape Verde played out a 0–0 draw in a friendly match against a full-strength [Portugal](576328.md).[^cite_note-11] At the time, Portugal was third in the FIFA rankings and Cape Verde were 117th.[^cite_note-12] Their first World Cup qualifying campaign was for the [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) tournament, in which Cape Verde was eliminated in the first round after one draw and a defeat against [Algeria](1029528.md).[^cite_note-FIFA2-13] In the [qualification campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)) for the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) and the [2006 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), Cape Verde advanced to the final round after their first victory in a World Cup qualifier, beating [Swaziland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaziland_national_football_team). In the final round, the team made an impression with its first-ever away victory against [Burkina Faso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burkina_Faso_national_football_team), but finished fifth in [its group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_Group_2) and failed to qualify for either tournament.[^cite_note-FIFA2-13]

Then coach [João de Deus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Carlos_Pires_de_Deus) from Portugal brought in several new players from European leagues for the [World Cup](168079.md) and [AFCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) [qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification).[^cite_note-FIFA1-10] Cape Verde finished second in its group in the [second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_second_round), ahead of [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_national_football_team) and [Mauritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritius_national_football_team), but behind [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team), and did not advance to the [third round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_third_round). Then, Cape Verde made their AFCON debut in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), after stunning Cameroon 3–2 [on aggregate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggregate_score), following a 2–1 defeat at the [Stade Ahmadou Ahidjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Ahmadou_Ahidjo) in [Yaoundé](57671.md), having won the home leg 2–0 in [Praia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praia).

Cape Verde were drawn into Group A of the [finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), alongside [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_national_football_team), [Morocco](1024865.md) and the host nation [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_football_team); they played the opening match of the tournament at [Soccer City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer_City) in [Johannesburg](58893.md), Gauteng, against the hosts on 19 January 2013. Pulled from the fourth pot during the group stage drawing of the tournament, Cape Verde actually had the highest FIFA ranking of any team in their group at the time of the drawing, ranking at 51st overall, followed by Morocco (71st), South Africa (72nd) and Angola (83rd).&#x20;

Cape Verde drew with South Africa 0–0 in the tournament's first match, before drawing with [Morocco](1024865.md) 1–1. [Platini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platini_\(Cape_Verdean_footballer\)) scored Cape Verde's first ever goal at the AFCON, before they won their first ever AFCON match against [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_national_football_team), 2–1 (despite an early own goal by captain [Nando Maria Neves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nando_Maria_Neves)), reaching the quarter-finals.

On 2 February 2013, Cape Verde faced [Ghana](1029620.md) in the [Nelson Mandela Bay Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela_Bay_Stadium) in [Port Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Elizabeth), registering 16 shots on Ghana's goal to their eight, with seven shots on target to their two. Ghana eventually knocked Cape Verde out.[^cite_note-14]

On 15 October 2014, Cape Verde became the first of two nations to qualify for the [2015 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) alongside [Algeria](1029528.md), joining the host nation [Equatorial Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_Guinea_national_football_team) after defeating Mozambique 1–0 at home.[^cite_note-15] The team, under newly appointed manager [Rui Águas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rui_%C3%81guas_\(footballer\)), picked up where [Lúcio Antunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BAcio_Antunes) left off and managed to finish in the top two of the [group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification#Group_F) with two matches remaining to play in the qualification process, having been drawn in a group together with [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique_national_football_team), [Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger_national_football_team) and [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team).[^cite_note-16] On 15 November 2014, Cape Verde secured first place in their group, finishing as Group F winners by defeating Niger 3–1 at home, with one match remaining to play for qualification.[^cite_note-17]

Cape Verde were drawn into Group B of the final tournament, together with [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team), [Tunisia](1029953.md) and [DR Congo](1030243.md). On 18 January 2015 they played their first match against Tunisia at the [Estadio de Ebibeyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_de_Ebibeyin). The match ended in a 1–1 draw, with [Héldon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9ldon) leveling the score off a [penalty kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_kick_\(association_football\)) in the 78th minute. Cape Verde then drew 0–0 against DR Congo four days later, with their group stage placement depending on the final match results of both teams.[^cite_note-18] Facing off against Zambia on 26 January, the match ended in 0–0 draw, leaving both Cape Verde and Zambia eliminated from the Cup.[^cite_note-19] Contested during a [tropical storm](8282374.md), with 26 mm (1.0 in) of rainfall, Cape Verde exited the tournament tied with DR Congo for points and undefeated, finishing behind them on goal difference.[^cite_note-20]

On 31 March 2015, Cape Verde won 2–0 against Portugal at the [Estádio António Coimbra da Mota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Ant%C3%B3nio_Coimbra_da_Mota) in [Estoril](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estoril).[^cite_note-21]

### 2020s: Rapid rise and World Cup qualification

Cape Verde qualified for the [2023 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) and were drawn into Group B. They defeated [Ghana](1029620.md) 2–1 in the first group stage match when [Garry Rodrigues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Rodrigues) scored a late match-winning goal in the second minute of stoppage time. In their second match, Cape Verde defeated [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozambique_national_football_team) 3-0. This result, along with others in the group, meant that Cape Verde entered the final group match already having secured passage to the knockout stage as the winners of Group B. In the last group stage match, Cape Verde faced [Egypt](1022953.md). After the Egypt had scored a go-ahead stoppage time goal, [Bryan Teixeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Teixeira) equalised deep into stoppage time to secure a 2–2 draw. In the Round of 16, Cape Verde faced [Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritania_national_football_team), and captain [Ryan Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Mendes) scored a crucial penalty late in the second half to send them to the quarter-finals, where Cape Verde faced South Africa. The match ended in a goalless draw after 120 minutes, thus sending it to penalties. Cape Verde were knocked out of the tournament after failing to convert four of their five penalty kicks, losing the shootout 1–2.

#### 2026 FIFA World Cup



2026 FIFA World Cup 2026 match on 21 June, showing Cape Verde players in red jerseys competing against Uruguay in light blue in [Miami Gardens, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Gardens,_Florida), United States.

Cape Verde qualified for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) after winning its qualifying group in October 2025.[^cite_note-bbc-6] In its opening game, the team held future World Cup champion [Spain](609825.md) to a goalless draw, exceeding expectations and earning Cape Verde its first ever point. As Spain was ranked significantly higher than Cape Verde, the draw brought attention to all of the team's players, with the team's goalkeeper, [Vozinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vozinha), gaining almost 30 million followers on social media platform [Instagram](31591547.md).[^cite_note-23] In its second match, the team tied [Uruguay](679782.md) 2–2, scoring its first World Cup goals, and once again, exceeding expectations.[^cite_note-apnews_2026-06-21-7] With a third (0–0) draw against [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team), Cape Verde finished undefeated and as runners-up in its group.[^cite_note-24]

Cape Verde faced the defending champions [Argentina](454699.md) in the Round of 32, holding them to a 1–1 score after the regulatory 90-minute period before going on to lose 3–2 in [extra time](633242.md). The team's performance at the World Cup almost instantly became highly regarded as one of the biggest underdog stories in World Cup history, and the match with Argentina as one of the greatest individual World Cup games of all time.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] Vozinha was voted into the Dream XI [all-star team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_All-Star_Team),[^cite_note-29] while [Sidny Lopes Cabral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidny_Lopes_Cabral)'s equalizing goal against Argentina was named the [Goal of the Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Goal_of_the_Tournament).[^cite_note-30]

## Stadiums

Main articles: [Estádio da Várzea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_da_V%C3%A1rzea) and [Estádio Nacional de Cabo Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Nacional_de_Cabo_Verde)

The national team played at [Estádio da Várzea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_da_V%C3%A1rzea) in [Praia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praia), on [Santiago Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago,_Cape_Verde). The stadium reopened in 2006 and has a capacity of 8,000.

Currently, the team plays the majority of its home matches at the [Estádio Nacional de Cabo Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Nacional_de_Cabo_Verde). The *Tubarões Azuis* matches have also been held at the 5,000-seat [Adérito Sena Municipal Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Municipal_Ad%C3%A9rito_Sena) in [Mindelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindelo).
