The **Presidential Palace** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Προεδρικό Μέγαρο; [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): Cumhurbaşkanlığı Sarayı) is the official residence and principal workplace of the [president of Cyprus](1671074.md). It is located in the [Strovolos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strovolos) area of [Nicosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicosia) and is surrounded by a thick pine woodland. The building was formerly called **Government House**.

## History

The original building was a prefabricated structure erected in November 1878 on a site known as Snake Hill, on which [Richard I of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_I_of_England) is said to have set up camp.[^cite_note-spots-1] The building was shipped by the [War Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_Office) from England to [Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Ceylon), its original destination; by the time it reached Egypt, it was no longer required in Ceylon and was diverted to Cyprus. The building arrived in [Larnaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larnaca) and was transported to Nicosia in boxes on the backs of camels.[^cite_note-keshishian-2]

The building was burned down during the [1931 Cyprus Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_Cyprus_Revolt). As a result of the disturbances, a special law was enacted by [Sir Ronald Storrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Storrs), [Governor of Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Cyprus), on 21 December 1931, whereby the Greek Cypriot inhabitants had to pay for the construction of a new [Government House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_House). A total of £34,315 was demanded, payable by 30 June 1932; the amounts required from various towns included £14,000 from Nicosia, £5,500 from [Limassol](57577.md), £5,000 from [Famagusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famagusta), £2,000 from Larnaca, £500 from [Paphos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paphos), and £6,315 from various villages.[^cite_note-keshishian-2]

The new building was designed by [Maurice Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Webb_\(architect\)), and constructed by J V Hamilton & L F Weldon of the Nicosia Public Works Department. The main structure was built of sandstone from [Gerolakkos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerolakkos), with harder sandstone from Limassol used for the staircases. Construction was completed in 1937, at a total cost of £70,000. Among the Palace's most prominent features are the [British coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_the_United_Kingdom) and four [gargoyles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gargoyle) with human heads depicting the British general foreman in charge of construction, the head mason, the head carpenter, and an unknown labourer.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

Originally named Government House, the building was renamed the Presidential Palace after Cyprus [gained independence from British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_and_Z%C3%BCrich_Agreements) in 1960.[^cite_note-spots-1] It suffered fire damage by the [Cypriot National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypriot_National_Guard) and [EOKA-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EOKA-B) during the [1974 Cypriot coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Cypriot_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), and was rebuilt by the Public Works Department and Philippou Brothers in 1977. The rebuilding costs were paid by the Greek government.[^cite_note-5]

On 28 May 2010, it was announced that the building was to have a €1.2 million upgrade to reduce its carbon emissions. The project included solar panels in the car park, a new ventilation system, and new windows.[^cite_note-6] A further €2.7 million was spent to build a new hall for Cyprus' tenure as [European Union](9317.md) President from July to December 2012.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]
