**Chengdu**[^cite_note-5] is the [capital city](181337.md) of the [Chinese](5405.md) [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China) of [Sichuan](65185.md). With a population of 20,937,757 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chinese_census),[^cite_note-citypopulation.de-2] it is the country's fourth most populous city and the only city with a population of over 20 million apart from provincial-level [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct-administered_municipality). It is traditionally the hub of [Western China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_China).

Chengdu is in central Sichuan. The surrounding [Chengdu Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Plain) is known as the "Country of Heaven"[^cite_note-6] and the "Land of Abundance". Its prehistoric settlers included the [Sanxingdui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanxingdui) culture. The site of [Dujiangyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dujiangyan), an ancient irrigation system, is designated as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-7] The [Jin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_River_\(Sichuan\)) flows through the city. Chengdu's culture reflects that of its province, Sichuan; in 2011, it was recognized by [UNESCO](21786641.md) as a city of gastronomy.[^cite_note-8] It is associated with the [giant panda](12713.md), a Chinese national symbol that inhabits the area of Sichuan; the city is home to the [Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Research_Base_of_Giant_Panda_Breeding).

Founded by the [Kingdom of Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_\(kingdom\)) in the 4th century BC, Chengdu is unique as the only Chinese settlement that has maintained its name unchanged throughout the [imperial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China#Imperial_China), [republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), and [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) eras for more than two thousand years. It was the capital of [Liu Bei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Bei)'s [Shu Han Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_Han) during the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms) Era, as well as several other local kingdoms during the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages).[^cite_note-\:1-9] During [World War II](32927.md), refugees from eastern China fleeing from the Japanese settled in Chengdu. After the war, Chengdu was briefly the capital of the [Nationalist republican government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_government) until it withdrew to [Taipei](57648.md) on the island of [Taiwan](25734.md). Under the PRC, Chengdu's importance as a link between Eastern China and Western China expanded, with railways built to [Chongqing](171752.md) in 1952, and [Kunming](197181.md) and Tibet afterward.[^cite_note-\:1-9] In the 1960s, Chengdu became an important defense industry hub.

Chengdu is one of the most important economic, financial, commercial, cultural, transportation, research, and communication centers in China. Its economy is diverse, characterized by the [machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_industry), [automobile](163778.md), [medical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine), [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_industry), and [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology) industries. Chengdu is a leading financial hub, ranking 35th globally on the 2021 [Global Financial Centres Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index).[^cite_note-GFCI2-10] Chengdu also hosts many international companies; more than 315 [Fortune 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) companies have established branches in the city.[^cite_note-Games_a_financial_winner_for_city-11] Chengdu is the third Chinese city with two international airports after [Beijing](18603746.md) and [Shanghai](27643.md).[^cite_note-\:4-12] [Chengdu Shuangliu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Shuangliu_International_Airport), and the newly built [Tianfu International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Tianfu_International_Airport), a hub of [Air China](297958.md) and [Sichuan Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Airlines), is one of the 30 [busiest airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_airports_by_passenger_traffic) in the world, and the [Chengdu railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_railway_station) is one of the six biggest in China. Chengdu is considered a "Beta + (global second-tier)" city classification (along with Barcelona and Washington, D.C.) according to the [GaWC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network).[^cite_note-13] As of 2023, the city also hosts 23 foreign consulates, the fourth most in China behind Beijing, Shanghai, and [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou).[^cite_note-Games_a_financial_winner_for_city-11] Chengdu is the seat of the [Western Theater Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Theater_Command) region of the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md).[^cite_note-14] In 2023, Chengdu became the third Chinese city to host the [Summer World University Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Summer_World_University_Games), after [Beijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Summer_Universiade) and [Shenzhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Summer_Universiade). In 2025, the city also hosted the [World Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Games). It is considered one of China's best cities to live in,[^cite_note-theguardian.com-15][^cite_note-16] and also a [national central city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_central_city).[^cite_note-17]

Chengdu is a major [city for scientific outputs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_scientific_output), ranking at #21 globally and 2nd in Western China after [Xi'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an).[^cite_note-18] The city is home to the greatest number of [universities and research institutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Sichuan) in Western China. Notably, these include: [Sichuan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_University), [University of Electronic Science and Technology of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Electronic_Science_and_Technology_of_China), [Southwestern University of Finance and Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_University_of_Finance_and_Economics), [Southwest Jiaotong University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Jiaotong_University), [Chengdu University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_University_of_Technology), [Sichuan Normal University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Normal_University), and [Xihua University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xihua_University).[^cite_note-\:2-19]

## Name

The name *Chengdu* is attested in sources dating back to the [Warring States period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period). It has been called the only major city in China to have remained at an unchanged location with an unchanged name throughout the [imperial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_China), [republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), and [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) eras.[^cite_note-cd.wenming.cn-20] However, it also had other names; for example, it was briefly known as "Xijing" (Western Capital) in the 17th century.[^cite_note-eccp-21] The etymology of the name is unclear. The earliest and most widely known explanation, although not generally accepted by modern scholars,[^cite_note-22] is provided in the 10th-century geographical work *[Universal Geography of the Taiping Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Geography_of_the_Taiping_Era)*, which states that the ninth king of [Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_\(state\))'s [Kaiming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiming_dynasty) named his new capital Chengdu after a statement by [King Tai of Zhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Tai_of_Zhou) that a settlement needed "one year to become a town, two to become a city, and three to become a metropolis."[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] (The character for *cheng* [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): [成](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%88%90) may mean "turned into" while *du* [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): [都](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E9%83%BD) can mean either a [metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis) or a capital).

The present spelling is based on [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin) [romanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Chinese); its [Postal Map romanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_postal_romanization) was "Chengtu". Its former status as the seat of the Chengdu [Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu_\(administrative_division\)) prompted [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo)'s spellings "Sindafu", "Sin-din-fu",[^cite_note-marco-25][^cite_note-yule-26] and the [Protestant missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_Sichuan)' romanization "Ching-too Foo".[^cite_note-Britannica1878-27]

Although the city's official name has remained (almost) constant, the surrounding area has sometimes been known by other names, including "[Yizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_Province)". Chinese nicknames for the city include the "Turtle City", variously derived from the old [city walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_city_wall)' shape on a map or a legend that [Zhang Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Yi_\(strategist\)) had planned their course by following a turtle's tracks; the "Brocade City" (see [Sichuan brocade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_embroidery)), a contraction of the earlier "City of the Brocade Official", after an imperial office established under the [Western Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Han_dynasty); the "Hibiscus City" (Rongcheng, [蓉](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E8%93%89)城), from the [hibiscus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibiscus) which King [Meng Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meng_Chang) of the [Later Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Shu) ordered planted upon the city wall during the [10th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dynasties_and_Ten_Kingdoms_period).[^cite_note-nix-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

According to [Étienne de la Vaissière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tienne_de_la_Vaissi%C3%A8re), "Baghshūr" (lit. 'pond of salt water') may be the [Sogdian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sogdian_language) name for the region of Chengdu. This toponym is attested near [Merv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merv), but not far from Chengdu are found the large saltwater wells of the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze) basin.[^cite_note-31]

## Emblem

The city emblem, adopted in 2011, is inspired by the [Golden Sun Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Sun_Bird), an ancient relic unearthed in 2001 from the [Jinsha Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinsha_site).[^cite_note-32]

## History



The archaeological site of [Jinsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinsha_\(archaeological_site\)) is a major discovery in Chengdu in 2001.

### Early history

Archaeological discoveries at the [Sanxingdui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanxingdui) and [Jinsha Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinsha_site) have established that the area surrounding Chengdu was inhabited over four thousand years ago, in the 18th–10th century BC. At the time of China's [Xia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xia_dynasty), [Shang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty), and [Zhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_dynasty) [dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_dynasties), it represented a separate ancient [bronze-wielding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) culture that, following its partial [sinification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinification), became known to the Chinese as [Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_\(kingdom\)).[^cite_note-sage1-33][^cite_note-yinkedeng-34] Shu was conquered by [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_\(state\)) in 316 BC, and the settlement was re-founded by Qin general [Zhang Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Yi_\(strategist\)).

#### Pre-Qin to Qin and Han dynasties

In the early stage of the [Xia dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xia_dynasty) or even earlier, the ancient Shu Kingdom, located on the Chengdu Plain, had developed a relatively advanced bronze civilization, becoming an important source of Chinese civilization and one of the birthplaces of the Chinese nation. According to records, there were five dynasties in the ancient Shu Kingdom, and their capitals were Qushang (now Wenjiang District, Chengdu), Piyi (now Pidu District), Xindu, and Guangdu. At the end of the Spring and Autumn period (around the 4th century BC), the fifth King Kaiming moved the capital to Chengdu. According to "Taiping Huanyu Ji", the name of the city is borrowed from the history of the establishment of the capital in the Western Zhou dynasty. The allusion in Zhou Wang Qianqi's "one year, he lived in a cluster, two years became a city, and three years Chengdu" is to the name Chengdu; it has been used to this day. Therefore, Chengdu has become a rare city in China and the world that has not changed its name since its establishment. Some people think that Chengdu is a transliteration of ancient Shu place names. There is a saying that "Guangdu, Xindu, and Chengdu" are collectively referred to as the "Three Capitals of Ancient Shu". Nowadays, many cultural relics of the ancient Shu Kingdom are located on the Chengdu Plain, including Sanxingdui Ruins, Jinsha Ruins, Yufu Ancient City Ruins, and Wangcong Temple. Jinsha Ruins, located in Chengdu's urban area, is a pinnacle of ancient Shu culture.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

The Golden Mask of the Shang and Zhou dynasties at the Jinsha Site.

The ancient state of Shu was the first target to be conquered by the Qin state in the process of unifying the world. King Huiwen of Qin had prepared for this for many years and opened the Shiniu Road (i.e., the Jinniu Road) from Qin to Shu. In 316 BC, King Huiwen of Qin took advantage of the mutual attack between Ba and Shu and sent Sima Cuo to lead his army into Shu along the Shiniu Road, capturing the land in a few months. After that, the king of Qin abolished the three Shu Hou and finally established Shu County, with the county seat in Chengdu, the former capital of Shu. In 311 BC, Zhang Yi of the Qin dynasty built the Chengdu city wall according to the system of the capital Xianyang, building a large city and a small city. In 256 BC, King Zhao of Qin appointed Li Bing as the governor of Shu County. During his tenure, he presided over the construction of the world-famous Dujiangyan Water Conservancy Project. The Chengdu Plain has been fertile and wild for thousands of miles since then. After decades of operation, Chengdu replaced Guanzhong Plain in the late Qin dynasty and was called the "Land of Abundance"; this reputation has endured to this day.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

During the Han dynasty, the Chengdu economy, especially its brocade industry, prospered, becoming an important source of tribute to the court. The imperial court invested in Chengdu and specifically set up the Jinguan management and built "Jinguan City" in the southwest of Chengdu; "Jinguan City" and "Jincheng" became nicknames for Chengdu. In the second year of Emperor Ping of the Yuan dynasty, Chengdu's population reached 76,000 households, or about 354,000 people, making it one of the most populous cities at the time. Towards the six major cities. In the third year of the reign of Emperor Jing of the Han dynasty (141 BC), the Wen Dang, the prefect of Shu County, established the world's earliest local government-run school, "Wenweng Shishi", in Chengdu. In the Han dynasty, Chengdu's literature and art also reached a high level. All the most famous literary masters in the Han dynasty were from Chengdu, including Sima Xiangru, Yang Xiong, and Wang Bao.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-认识成都历史沿革-43]

In the former Han dynasty, the whole country was divided into 14 prefectural governors' departments, among which the Yizhou governor was established in Luoxian (now Guanghan City, Sichuan), and the governor later moved to Chengdu. In the first year of Emperor Guangwu's reign (25 years) in the Eastern Han dynasty, Gongsun Shu established himself as the emperor in Chengdu, and the country's name was "married family". In the twelfth year of Jianwu in the Later Han dynasty (36 years), the Great Sima Wuhan of the Eastern Han dynasty finally captured Chengdu after five years of war, and his family perished in the process. In the fifth year of Zhongping (188), Emperor Ling of Han, the court accepted Liu Yan's suggestion and changed the provincial governors to state shepherds with actual recruitment and command power. In the fifth year of Chuping (194), it moved to Chengdu. At that time, the Yizhou Provincial Governor's Department was the place where the Hu people in the Western Regions were operating.[^cite_note-\:5-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

### Imperial era



The [Dujiangyan Irrigation System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dujiangyan) built in 256 BC still functions today.





Tomb doors from [Pi County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi_County) showing men in *[hanfu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Chinese_clothing)*, one with a shield and the other a broom (1st or 2nd century).



Sixth-century Sichuan brocade

Under the [Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Han_dynasty), the [brocade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brocade) produced in Chengdu became fashionable and was exported throughout China. A "Brocade Official" (錦官; *jǐnguān*) was established to oversee its production and transaction. After the fall of the Eastern Han, [Liu Bei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Bei) ruled [Shu Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_Han), the southwestern part of the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms), from Chengdu. His [minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_\(China\)) [Zhuge Liang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuge_Liang) called the area the "Land of Abundance".[^cite_note-47] Under the [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty), Chengdu was considered the second most prosperous city in China after [Yangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangzhou).[^cite_note-48] Both [Li Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Bai) and [Du Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Fu) lived in the city. Li Bai praised it as "lying above the [empyrean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empyrean)." The city's present Caotang ("Grass Hall") was constructed in 1078 in honor of an earlier, more humble structure of that name erected by Du Fu in 760, the second year of his 4-year stay. The [Taoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism) Qingyang Gong ("Green Goat Temple") was built in the 9th century.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

Chengdu was the capital of [Wang Jian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Jian_\(Former_Shu\))'s [Former Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Shu) from 907 to 925, when it was conquered by the [Later Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Tang). The [Later Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Shu) was founded by [Meng Zhixiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meng_Zhixiang) in 934, with its capital at Chengdu. Its second and last king, [Meng Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meng_Chang), beautified the city by ordering [hibiscus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibiscus) to be planted upon the [city walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_city_wall).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

The [Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) conquered the city in 965, introducing the first widely used [paper money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_money) in the world. [Su Shi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su_Shi) praised it as "the southwestern metropolis". At the fall of the Song, a rebel leader set up a short-lived kingdom known as Great Shu ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): [大蜀](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A7%E8%9C%80), *Dàshǔ*). Allegedly, the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_Empire) called for the death of a million people in the city, but the city's population had fewer than 30,000 residents (not Chengdu prefecture). The aged males who had not fled were killed, while in typical fashion, the women, children, and artisans were enslaved and deported. During the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty), most of Sichuan's residents were deported to Hunan due to the insurgency by western ethnic tribes in western Sichuan. [Marco Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Polo) visited Chengdu[^cite_note-marco-25][^cite_note-53] and wrote about the [Anshun Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anshun_Bridge) or an earlier version of it.[^cite_note-54]

At the fall of the [Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), the rebel [Zhang Xianzhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Xianzhong) established his Great Western Kingdom ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): [大西](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%A4%A7%E8%A5%BF)) with its capital at Chengdu; it lasted only from 1643 to 1646.[^cite_note-eccp-21] Zhang was said to have massacred a large number of people in Chengdu and throughout Sichuan. In any case, Chengdu was said to have become a virtual ghost town frequented by tigers[^cite_note-dai2-55] and the depopulation of Sichuan necessitated the resettlement of millions of people from other provinces during the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty). Following the [Columbian Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbian_Exchange), the [Chengdu Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Plain) became one of China's principal sources of [tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_cultivation_in_China). [Pi County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pi_County) was considered to have the highest quality in Sichuan, which was the center of the country's [cigar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cigar) and [cigarette](38327.md) production, the rest of the country long continuing to consume [snuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snuff_\(tobacco\)) instead.[^cite_note-Britannica1878-27]

### Modern era



Huangchengba in 1911

In 1911, Chengdu's branch of the [Railway Protection Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_Protection_Movement) helped trigger the [Wuchang Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuchang_Uprising), which led to the [Xinhai Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinhai_Revolution) that overthrew the Qing dynasty.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

During [World War II](32927.md), the capital city of China was forced to move inland from [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nanking) to [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Wuhan) in 1937 and from Wuhan to Chengdu, then from Chengdu to [Chongqing](171752.md) in 1938, as the [Kuomintang](16903.md) (KMT) government under *Generalissimo* [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek) ultimately retreated to [Sichuan](65185.md) to escape from the invading Japanese forces. They brought with them to Sichuan businesspeople, workers, and academics who founded many of the industries and cultural institutions that continue to make Chengdu an important cultural and commercial production center.[^cite_note-\:5-44]

Chengdu became a military center for the KMT to regroup in the [War of Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War). Chengdu was beyond the reach of the Imperial Japanese ground forces and escort fighter planes. However, the Japanese frequently flew in the then-highly advanced twin-engine long-ranged [G3M "Nell"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_G3M) [medium bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_bomber) to conduct massive [aerial bombardments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_bombardment_of_cities) of both civilian and military targets in [Chongqing and Chengdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Chongqing).[^cite_note-58] The massed formation of the G3M bombers provided heavy firepower against [Chinese fighter planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Chinese_Nationalist_air_force_\(1937%E2%80%9345\)) assigned to the defense of Chongqing and Chengdu, which continued to cause problems for the Japanese attacks.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]



An all-air war was fought over Chengdu between the Chinese Air Force and the Imperial Japanese Army and Navy air forces; an I-16 fighter shown here at the [Datangshan Aviation Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Aviation_Museum)

Slow and vulnerable obsolescent Chinese fighter aircraft burning low-grade fuel were still sufficiently dangerous in the hands of capable pilots against the Japanese *[schnellbomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schnellbomber)*-[terror bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_casualties_of_strategic_bombing) raiders;[^cite_note-61] on 4 November 1939, for instance, Capt. [Cen Zeliu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cen_Zeliu) (*[Wade-Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade-Giles): Shen Tse-Liu*) led his [17th Fighter Squadron, 5th Fighter Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Chinese_Nationalist_air_force_\(1937%E2%80%931945\)) of seven cannon-equipped [Dewoitine D.510](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewoitine_D.500) fighters in a level head-on attack against an incoming coming raid of 72 [IJANF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy_Air_Service) [G3M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_G3M) bombers (Capt. Cen chose this tactic knowing that the operation of the [Hispano-Suiza HS.404 20mm autocannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispano-Suiza_HS.404) in his D.510 is likely to fail under the [g-loads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G-force) of a high-deflection diving attack), with Capt. Cen pummeling the lead G3M of the IJN's *[13th Kōkūtai's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dk%C5%ABtai) CO* Captain Kikushi Okuda with cannon fire, sending the G3M crashing down in flames over Chengdu, along with three other G3M bombers destroyed in the Chengdu raid that day.[^cite_note-62] With the death of Captain Okuda in the air battle over Chengdu, the [IJN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy) *[Kaigun-daisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranks_of_the_Imperial_Japanese_Navy)* (海軍大佐) became the highest-ranking IJN Air officer to be *[killed-in-action](368513.md)* in the *War of Resistance/World War II* thus far.[^cite_note-63]

In mid- to late-1940, unknown to the Americans and European allies, the Imperial Japanese appeared in the skies over Chongqing and Chengdu with the world's most advanced fighter plane at the time: the [A6M "Zero" fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsubishi_A6M_Zero) that dominated the skies over China against the increasingly obsolete Russian-made [Polikarpov I-15/I-153s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polikarpov_I-15) and [I-16s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polikarpov_I-16) that were the principal fighter planes of the Chinese Nationalist Air Force.[^cite_note-visitpearlharbor.org-64] This would later prove to be a [rude awakening](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/rude%20awakening) for the [Allied forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_forces_\(World_War_II\)) in the Pacific War following the [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor).[^cite_note-65] One of the first American ace fighter pilots of the war and original volunteer fighter pilot for the Chinese Nationalist Air Force, [Major Huang Xinrui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Huang_Xinrui) (nicknamed "Buffalo" by his comrades) died as a result of battling the Zero fighters along with his squadronmates Cen Zeliu and Lin Heng (younger brother of renowned architect [Lin Huiyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin_Huiyin)) defending [Chengdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Shuangliu_International_Airport#History) on 14 March 1941.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-visitpearlharbor.org-64][^cite_note-68]

Following the [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) at the end of 1941, the United States began setting up stations at airbases in China. In 1944, the American [XX Bomber Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XX_Bomber_Command) launched [Operation Matterhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Matterhorn), an ambitious plan to base [B-29 Superfortresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-29_Superfortress) in Chengdu and strategically bomb the [Japanese Home Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_archipelago).[^cite_note-hittinghomepg4-69] The operating base was located in [Xinjin Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjin_Airport) in the southwestern part of the Chengdu metropolitan area.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-xinhua-71] Because the operation required a massive airlift of fuel and supplies over the Himalayas, it was not a significant military success, but it did earn Chengdu the distinction of launching the first serious retaliation against the Japanese homeland.[^cite_note-72]



[People's Liberation Army](66890.md) troops entered Chengdu on 27 December 1949

During the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War), Chengdu was the last major city on the [mainland China](55079.md) to be held by the Kuomintang. President Chiang Kai-shek and his son [Chiang Ching-kuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Ching-kuo) directed the defense of the city from the Chengdu Central Military Academy (黄埔军校成都分校) until 1949, when [Communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_China) forces [took the city on 27 December](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_campaign). The [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) took the city without any resistance after a deal was negotiated between the People's Liberation Army and the commander of the KMT Army guarding the city. On 10 December [the remnants of the Nationalist Chinese government evacuated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Nationalist_Party_retreat_to_Taiwan) to [Taiwan](25734.md).[^cite_note-Cookc-73][^cite_note-74]

The [Chengdu Tianfu New Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianfu_New_Area) is a sustainable planned city that will be outside of Central Chengdu. The city is also planned to be self-sustaining, with every residence being a two-minute walk from a park.[^cite_note-Davies-75]

#### The Great City

In 2019, Chengdu overtook [Shenzhen](83368.md), China's technology hub, as the best-performing Chinese economy.[^cite_note-South_China_Morning_Post_Publisher-76] The city has surged in population in the last two decades.[^cite_note-theguardian.com-15] Investments into the Europe-Chengdu Express Railway have been made, providing even more opportunities for the city to grow.[^cite_note-South_China_Morning_Post_Publisher-76] As a way to preserve farmland and accommodate the growing population of Chengdu, China is building a hyper-dense satellite city centered around a central mass-transit hub called the Great City, where any destination within the city is within a 15-minute walk.[^cite_note-businessinsider1-77][^cite_note-The_Atlantic_Monthly_Group-78] This proto-type city is intended to provide affordable, high-quality lifestyle, which provides people-oriented spaces that [does not require a car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_the_car_on_societies) to navigate.[^cite_note-The_Atlantic_Monthly_Group-78]

Their current urban-planning focus in the city of Chengdu is to make the city 'a city within a park' rather than creating parks within a city.[^cite_note-theguardian.com-15] The Great City falls in line with the Chengdu 'park city' initiative, prioritizing the environment, public space, and quality of life. It will consist of 15% park and green space and be situated on a 1.3 km<sup>2</sup> (0.50 sq mi) area.[^cite_note-The_Atlantic_Monthly_Group-78] Although 25% of the space will be dedicated to roads, one half of the roads will be [pedestrian-oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedestrian_zone). This transit system provides direct service to Chengdu.[^cite_note-businessinsider1-77] It is expected that the city will consume 48% less energy than cities of similar size.[^cite_note-The_Atlantic_Monthly_Group-78]

The goal of the 'park city' project is to enable a city like Chengdu to compete with [Beijing](18603746.md) and [Shanghai](27643.md) without stripping it of its character. The city of Chengdu is already known for its focus on quality of life, which includes affordable housing, good public schools, trees, and bike lanes.[^cite_note-theguardian.com-15]

## Geography



Map including Chengdu (labeled as CH'ENG-TU (walled) [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 成都) ([AMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Map_Service), 1958)

The vast plain on which Chengdu is located has an elevation ranging from 450 to 720 meters (1,480 to 2,360 feet).

Northwest Chengdu is bordered by the high and steep [Longmen Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longmen_Mountains) in the north-west and in the west by the [Qionglai Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qionglai_Mountains), the elevation of which exceeds 3,000 m (9,800 ft) and includes Miao Jiling (5,364 m, 17,598 ft) and Xiling Snow Mountain (5,164 m, 16,942 ft). The western mountainous area is also home to a large primitive forest with abundant biological resources and a [giant panda](12713.md) habitat.[^cite_note-79] East of Chengdu stands the low [Longquan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longquan_Mountains) and the west bordering area of the hilly land of the middle reaches of [Min River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_River_\(Sichuan\)), an area noted by several converging rivers. Since ancient times, Chengdu has been known as "the Abundant Land" owing to its fertile soil, favorable climate, and novel Dujiangyan Irrigation System.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82]

Chengdu is located at the western edge of the [Sichuan Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Basin) and sits on the [Chengdu Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Plain); the dominating terrain is plains. The [prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China#Prefecture_level) ranges in latitude from 30° 05' to 31° 26' N, while its longitude ranges from 102° 54' to 104° 53' E, stretching for 192 kilometers (119 mi) from east to west and 166 km (103 mi) south to north, administering 12,390 km<sup>2</sup> (4,780 sq mi) of land. Neighboring prefectures are [Deyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deyang) (NE), [Ziyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziyang,_Sichuan) (SE), [Meishan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meishan) (S), [Ya'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ya'an) (SW), and the [Ngawa Tibetan and Qiang Autonomous Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngawa_Tibetan_and_Qiang_Autonomous_Prefecture) (N). The urban area, with an elevation of 500 m (1,600 ft), features a few rivers, three of them being the [Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_River_\(Sichuan\)), [Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu_River_\(Sichuan\)), and [Sha Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sha_River). Outside of the immediate urban area, the topography becomes more complex: to the east lies the [Longquan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longquan_Mountains) (龙泉山脉) and the Penzhong Hills ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 盆中丘陵); to the west lie the [Qionglai Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qionglai_Mountains), which rise to 5,364 m (17,598 ft) in Dayi County. The highest point in Chengdu is Daxuetang (also known as Miaojiling) in Xiling Snow Mountain, Dayi County, at an altitude of 5,364 meters. The lowest point is the riverbank at the exit of the Tuojiang River in Jianyang City, with an altitude of 359 meters.[^cite_note-wz-83][^cite_note-84]

### Climate

Chengdu has a [monsoon](57630.md)-influenced [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cwa*) and is largely warm, with high [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity) year-round. It has four distinct seasons, with moderate rainfall concentrated mainly in the warmer months, relieving both sweltering summers and freezing winters. The [Qin Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Mountains) (Qinling) to the far north help shield the city from cold [Siberian winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_anticyclone) in the winter; because of this, the short winter is milder than in the Lower [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River). The daily mean temperature in January is 5.9 °C (42.6 °F), and snow is rare, though there are a few periods of frost each winter. The summer is hot and humid, but not to the extent of the "[Three Furnaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Furnaces)" cities of [Chongqing](171752.md), [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan), and [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing), all of which lie in the Yangtze basin.[^cite_note-85] The 24-hour daily mean temperature in July and August is around 25 °C (77 °F), with afternoon highs sometimes reaching 33 °C (91 °F); sustained heat as found in much of eastern China is rare. Rainfall occurs most frequently in July and August, with very little in the cooler months. Chengdu also has one of the lowest annual sunshine totals nationally,[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] with less sunshine annually than much of Northern Europe.[^cite_note-88] With monthly percent possible sunshine ranging from 15 percent in December to 32 percent in August, the city receives 1006 hours of bright sunshine annually. Spring (March–April) tends to be sunnier and warmer in the day than autumn (October–November). The annual mean is 16.9 °C (62.4 °F), and extremes have ranged from −6.5 °C (20 °F) to 39.4 °C (102.9 °F).

## Cityscape

As of February 2026, the world's third-largest building by floor area, the [New Century Global Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Century_Global_Center), is located in the city. The 100-meter-tall (330 ft) structure is 500 by 400 meters (1,600 by 1,300 ft) in size with 1,700,000 square meters (18,000,000 sq ft) of floor area, housing retail outlets, a movie theaters, offices, hotels, a water park with artificial beach and waves and a Mediterranean-style village comprising a large 5-star hotel, a skating rink and a 15,000-spot parking area.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]

### Literature

Some of China's most important literature comes from Chengdu. The city has been home to literary giants, such as [Sima Xiangru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sima_Xiangru) and [Yang Xiong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Xiong_\(author\)), two masters of [Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu_\(poetry\)), a mixture of descriptive prose and verse during the Tang dynasty; [Li Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Bai) and [Du Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Fu), the most eminent poets of the [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) and [Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) dynasties respectively; [Yang Shen'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Shen'an?action=edit\&redlink=1), a famous scholar of the Ming dynasty; and [Guo Moruo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guo_Moruo) and [Ba Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_Jin), two well-known modern writers. [Chang Qu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang_Qu), a historian of Chengdu during the Jin dynasty, compiled the earliest local historical records, the [Record of Hua Yang State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_of_Hua_Yang_State?action=edit\&redlink=1). [Zhao Chongzuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhao_Chongzuo?action=edit\&redlink=1), a poet in Chengdu during the Later Shu Kingdom, edited [Among the Flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Among_the_Flowers?action=edit\&redlink=1), the first anthology of [Ci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ci_\(poetry\)) in China's history. [Meng Chang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meng_Chang), the king of Later Shu, wrote the first couplet for the Spring Festival, which says, "A harvest year accepts celebrations, good festivals foreshadow long springs."[^cite_note-108]

In 2023, Chengdu hosted the [81st World Science Fiction Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/81st_World_Science_Fiction_Convention), having beat out Winnipeg, Canada, in site-selection voting in 2021.[^cite_note-109]

### Fine art

During the period of the Five Dynasties, Huang Quan, a painter in Chengdu, initiated the Fine-Brush Flower-and-Bird Painting School with other painters. At that time, "Hanlin Painting Academy" was the earliest royal academy in China.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111]

### Religion

See also: [Christianity in Sichuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Sichuan), [Catholic Church in Sichuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Sichuan), and [Protestantism in Sichuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_Sichuan)



[Cathedral of the Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Conception_Cathedral,_Chengdu), seat of the [Diocese of Chengdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Chengdu)

Chengdu contains official,[^cite_note-112] Roman Catholic[^cite_note-113] and Protestant congregations, some of which are [underground churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_church).

The Apostolic Vicariate of Szechwan (now known as [Diocese of Chengdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Chengdu)) was established on 15 October 1696. [Artus de Lionne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artus_de_Lionne), a French missionary of [Paris Foreign Missions Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Foreign_Missions_Society), was appointed as the first Apostolic Vicar.

In 1890, the [Canadian Methodist Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Methodist_Mission) was seeking additional stations in Asia. In February 1891, Dr. [Virgil Chittenden Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil_Chittenden_Hart?action=edit\&redlink=1), who had been Superintendent of the New York Methodist Mission Society of Central China, recommended at a meeting that Chengtu be the site of its first Mission. During the meeting, it was proposed that he lead the contingent to establish it, having built western hospitals as well as boys' and girls' schools at missions he established on the Yangtze and Gan Rivers 1866–88. On 9 May 1891, Hart arrived in Chengtu; two weeks later, he bought a home and had it subdivided into living quarters and a dispensary for the later-arriving Missionary staff to move into.[^cite_note-114]

On 24 June 1892, the doors of Chengtu's first Protestant Mission Headquarters were opened, with over 1,000 people from the community in attendance. The first Methodist religious service was held the following Sunday, with only a few attendants. The first western dispensary in Sichuan was opened on 3 November 1892 with sixteen patients seeking care. The mission site became so popular that a larger space was secured near Chengtu's East Gate in the spring of 1893. This site is where the city's first Methodist church ([Sï-Shen-Tsï Methodist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%AF-Shen-Ts%C3%AF_Methodist_Church)) and hospital were built. These were later razed by rioting Chinese in 1895, and the Mission staff retreated to Chongqing and later Shanghai to escape the marauders. Dr. Virgil Hart traveled to Peking to demand redress, and full payment of retribution was collected from Sichuan Viceroy Liu Ping Chang. The mission compound was quickly rebuilt, only to be destroyed once more in the riots of 1901. These were rebuilt a second time and later missionaries would relocate and expand the Boys' and Girls' Schools just south of the city, dedicating the Divinity College as Hart College in 1914; a part of the [West China Union University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_China_Union_University), that is now Sichuan University and the [West China School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_China_Medical_Center) (Huaxiyida).[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] During the Cultural Revolution, the Sï-Shen-Tsï Methodist Church building was no longer in use and the building was entrusted to the nearby Chengdu City Second People's Hospital for management. The hospital used the chapel as a kindergarten and the office of the hospital equipment department. In 1984, the hospital returned the chapel building to the church.[^cite_note-118]

In December 2018, the authorities attempted to close a 500-member underground church, the [Early Rain Covenant Church](76489484.md), led by [Pastor Wang Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Yi_\(pastor\)). Over 100 members of the church were arrested, including the pastor and his wife. The church's kindergarten and theological college were raided, and its media outlets were shut down. Before his arrest, church member Li Yingqiang declared, "Even if we are down to our last five, worship and gatherings will still go on because our faith is real. \[…] Persecution is a price worth paying for the Lord." Police are said to have told one member that the church had been declared an illegal organization. Chinese media were banned from reporting the events. Video footage, which found its way onto Western social media, showed arrests and photographs alleged to be of injuries inflicted by the police.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-scmp.com-120][^cite_note-121] From a photo of Ms. Jiang's detention warrant it appears that the authorities have charged the church's leaders with "inciting subversion of state power," which carries a maximum sentence of 15 years.[^cite_note-122]

In 2012, a [Chabad Jewish Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chabad_house) was established in Chengdu, after moving five times, a permanent location was secured at [Wuhou District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhou_District).[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

### Theater



Sichuan Opera

The saying "Shu opera towers above all other performances in the world" reflects the achievement of [Sichuan opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_opera) and [Zaju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaju) (an ancient form of comedic drama involving dancing, singing, poetry, and miming). In the city, the first opera, "Bullfighting," was written in the Warring States period. The first detailed opera was staged at the royal court of the Shu Kingdom during the Three Kingdoms period. China's first clearly recorded Zaju was also performed in Chengdu. Tombs of witty Han dynasty poets were excavated in Chengdu. And [face-changing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bian_lian) masks and fire breathing remain hallmarks of the Sichuan opera.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

### Language

The native language in Chengdu is Chengdu dialect, a form of [Sichuanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuanese_Mandarin).[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] The Chengdu dialect has been influential in Chinese comedy and rap music.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]

### Culinary art and tea culture

See also: [Sichuan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_cuisine)



A teahouse in [People's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Park_\(Chengdu\)) in Chengdu

The distinct characteristic of Sichuan cuisine is the use of spicy chilies and peppercorns. Famous local dishes include [Mapo doufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapo_doufu), Chengdu [Hot pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_pot), and [Dan Dan Mien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Dan_Noodles). Both Mapo Doufu and Dan Dan Mien contain [Sichuan peppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_pepper). An article\[^cite_note-[www.latimes.com-134](http://www.latimes.com-134/)] by the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* (2006) called Chengdu "China's party city" for its carefree lifestyle. Chengdu has more [tea houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_house) and bars than [Shanghai](27643.md) despite having less than half the population. In 2023, there were more than 30,000 teahouses in Chengdu,[^cite_note-135] and there were 3,566 legally registered bars, nightclubs, and dance halls in the city.[^cite_note-136] A statistical report in 2019 showed that Chengdu had more bars than Shanghai, becoming the city with the most bars in China.[^cite_note-137] Chengdu's tea culture dates back over a thousand years, including its time as the starting point of the Southern [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road).[^cite_note-138]

Chengdu is officially recognized and named by [UNESCO](21786641.md) as the "City of Gastronomy".\[^cite_note-[www.unesco.org-139](http://www.unesco.org-139/)]

#### Teahouse



Loose jasmine tea



**Longjing tea**

Tea houses are ubiquitous in the city, ranging from ornate traditional establishments with bamboo furniture to simple modern ones. Teas on offer include [jasmine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmine_tea), [longjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longjing_tea) and [biluochun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biluochun) tea. Tea houses are popular venues for playing [mahjong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahjong), getting a massage, or having one's ears cleaned.[^cite_note-140] Some larger tea houses offer live entertainment such as Sichuan opera performances.[^cite_note-141]

#### Hot pot



Hot pot

Chengdu is known for its hot pot.[^cite_note-142] Hot pot is a traditional Sichuanese dish, made by cooking vegetables, fish, and/or meat in boiling spicy broth. A type of food suitable for a gathering of friends, hot pot attracts both locals and tourists. Hot pot restaurants can be found throughout Chengdu.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144]

### Mahjong

&#x20;Mahjong originated in the [Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) and [Qing Dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) in China. Most scholars believe that it is related to the evolution of the card "horse hanging" in the Ming Dynasty, which was gradually standardized in the late Qing Dynasty.[^cite_note-145] Another view is that it originated from "leaf play" or ancient divination, but the evidence is insufficient.[^cite_note-146] Since then, mahjong has become popular rapidly and spread overseas with immigrants, forming diversified rules and becoming a global cultural symbol.[^cite_note-147]&#x20;



Mahjong

[Mahjong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahjong) has been an essential part of most people's lives in many local communities. After daytime work, people gather at home or at street tea houses to play Mahjong. On sunny days, locals like to play Mahjong on the sidewalks to enjoy the sunshine and spend time with friends.

Mahjong is the most popular form of entertainment among locals for several reasons. Chengdu locals have simplified the rules and made it easier to play than Cantonese Mahjong. Also, Mahjong in Chengdu is a way to meet old friends and to strengthen family relationships. In fact, many business people negotiate deals while playing Mahjong.[^cite_note-148]

### Rural tourism: Nong Jia Le

Chengdu claims to have first practiced the modern business model of 'Nong Jia Le' (Happy Rural Homes). It refers to the practice of suburban and rural residents converting their homes into restaurants, hotels, and entertainment spaces to attract city dwellers.[^cite_note-149]

Nong Jia Le features different styles and price levels and has been thriving around Chengdu. They offer gateways for city dwellers to escape the city, serve delicious, affordable homemade dishes, and provide mahjong facilities.[^cite_note-150]

### Digital Cultural Technology Lab

The Chengdu Digital Cultural Technology Lab, a collaboration between the Chengdu Science and Technology Bureau and local universities, focuses on applying VR and AR technologies to cultural heritage preservation. The lab creates high-fidelity digital models of historical sites and artifacts, enabling virtual tours and academic research, thereby expanding public access to Chengdu's cultural resources.[^cite_note-151]

### World natural and cultural heritage sites

#### Mount Qingcheng



[Mount Qingcheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Qingcheng)

[Mount Qingcheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Qingcheng) is amongst the most important Taoism sites in China. It is situated in the suburbs of [Dujiangyan City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dujiangyan_City) and connected to downtown Chengdu 70 km (43 mi) away by the Cheng-Guan Expressway.[^cite_note-\:13-152]

At its peak, 1,600 m (5,200 ft) above sea level, Mount Qingcheng enjoys a cool climate, yet remains lush and green year-round, surrounded by hills and waterways. Mount Qingcheng's Fujian Temple, Tianshi Cave, and Shizu Hall are among the better-known Taoist holy sites. Shangqing Temple is noted for an evening phosphorescent glow locally referred to as "holy lights".[^cite_note-\:13-152]

#### Dujiangyan Irrigation System

The [Dujiangyan Irrigation System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dujiangyan_Irrigation_System) (58 km (36 mi) from downtown Chengdu) is the oldest existing irrigation project in the world, with a history of over 2000 years, diverting water without a dam to distribute water and filter sand, with inflow quantity control. Libing and his son built the system. The irrigation system prevents floods and droughts throughout the Plain of Chengdu.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154]

#### Sichuan Giant Panda Sanctuaries



Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding

Covering a total of 9,245 km<sup>2</sup> (3,570 sq mi) over 12 distinct counties and 4 cities, [Sichuan Giant Panda Sanctuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Giant_Panda_Sanctuaries), lie on the transitional alp-canyon belt between the Sichuan Basin and the Qinghai-Tibetan Plateau. It is the largest remaining continuous habitat for giant pandas and is home to more than 80 percent of the world's wild giant pandas. Globally speaking, it is also the most abundant temperate zone of greenery. The reserves of the habitat are 100–200 km (62–124 mi) away from Chengdu.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156]

The Sichuan Giant Panda Sanctuaries are among the best-known of their kind in the world, with Wolong Nature Reserve generally considered the "homeland of pandas". It is a core habitat with unique natural conditions, complex landforms, and a temperate climate, supporting diverse wildlife. Siguniang Mountain, sometimes called the "Oriental Alpine," is approximately 230 km (140 mi) away from downtown Chengdu, and is composed of four adjacent peaks of the Traversal Mountain Range. Among the four peaks, the fourth and highest stands 6,250 m (20,510 ft) above sea level, and is perpetually covered by snow.[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158]

### Culture of poetry and the Three Kingdoms

#### Wuhou Shrine



Wuhou Shrine gateway

Wuhou Shrine (Temple of Marquis Wu; 武侯祠) is perhaps the most influential museum of [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms) relics in China. It was built in the [Western Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Jin) period (265–316) in honor of [Zhuge Liang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuge_Liang), the famous military and political strategist who was Prime Minister of the [Shu Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_Han) State during the Three Kingdoms period (220–280). The Shrine features the Zhuge Liang Memorial Temple and the Hall of [Liu Bei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Bei) (founder of the Shu Han state), along with statues of other historical figures of Shu Han and cultural relics such as stone inscriptions and tablets. The Huiling Mausoleum of Liu Bei represents a unique pattern of enshrining both the emperor and his subjects in the same temple, a rarity in China.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161]

#### Du Fu thatched cottage

Main article: [Du Fu Thatched Cottage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Fu_Thatched_Cottage)

[Du Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Fu) was one of the most noted Tang dynasty poets; during the [Lushan-Shi Siming Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Lushan_Rebellion), he left [Xi'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an) (then Chang'an) to take refuge in Chengdu. With the help of his friends, the thatched cottage was built along the Huanhua Stream in the west suburbs of Chengdu, where Du Fu spent four years of his life and produced more than 240 now-famous poems. During the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty), people began constructing gardens and halls on the site of his thatched cottage to honor his life and memory. Currently, a series of memorial buildings representing Du Fu's humble life stand on the riverbank, along with a large collection of relics and various editions of his poems.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163]

### Ancient Shu civilization

#### Jinsha Site



Jinsha gold mask

The [Jinsha Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinsha_site) is the first significant archeological discovery in China of the 21st century and was selected in 2006 as a "key conservation unit" of the nation. The Jinsha Relics Museum is located in the northwest of Chengdu, about 5 km (3.1 mi) from downtown. As a theme-park-style museum, it is for the protection, research, and display of Jinsha archaeological relics and findings. The museum covers 300,000 m<sup>2</sup> (3,200,000 sq ft), and houses relics, exhibitions, and a conservation center.[^cite_note-164]

#### Golden Sun Bird



The [Golden Sun Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Sun_Bird)

Archaeologists from the Jinsha Ruins excavated the [Golden Sun Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Sun_Bird) on 25 February 2001. In 2005, it was designated as the official logo of Chinese cultural heritage by the China National Relic Bureau.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166]

The round, foil plaque dates back to the ancient Shu region in 210 BC and is 94.2 percent pure gold, making it extremely thin. It contains four birds flying around the perimeter, representing the four seasons and directions. The sun-shaped cutout in the center contains 12 sunlight beams, each representing one of the 12 months of the year. The exquisite design is remarkable for a 2,200-year-old piece.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168]

#### Sanxingdui Museum

Situated in the northeast of the state-protected [Sanxingdui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanxingdui) Site, the original complex of [Sanxingdui Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanxingdui_Museum) was founded in August 1992 and opened in 1997. It is the representative work of the master architect Zheng Guoying. The original museum covers 1,000 acres and was included in the first batch of national first-class museums.[^cite_note-\:8-169]

&#x20;The new Sanxingdui Museum complex was founded in March 2022. It covers an area of 54,400 square meters, which is about 5 times the size of the old museum. It was built for new cultural relics after major archaeological excavations. It displays more than 2,000 precious cultural relics such as bronze, jade, gold, pottery, and bone, and comprehensively and systematically displays the archaeological excavations and latest research results of Sanxingdui.[^cite_note-\:8-169]



Sanxingdui bronze head

The main collection highlights the Ancient City of Chengdu, the [Shu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_Han) State & its culture, while displaying thousands of valuable relics, including earthenware, jade wares, bone objects, gold wares, and bronzes unearthed from [Shang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty) sacrificial sites.[^cite_note-170]

### Buddhist and Taoist culture

#### Daci Temple

The [Daci Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daci_Temple) (大慈寺), a temple in downtown Chengdu, was first built during the Wei and Jin dynasties, with its cultural height during the Tang and Song dynasties. [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang), a Tang dynasty monk, was initiated into monkhood and studied for several years here; during this time, he gave frequent sermons in Daci Monastery.[^cite_note-171]



Wenshu Monastery

#### Wenshu Monastery

Also known as Xinxiang Monastery, Wenshu Monastery (文殊院) is Chengdu's best-preserved Buddhist temple. Originally built during the Tang dynasty, it has a history of 1,300 years. Parts of [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang)'s skull are held in consecration here (as a [relic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relic)). The traditional home of scholar Li Wenjing is on the outskirts of the complex.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173]

#### Baoguang Buddhist Temple

Located in Xindu District, Baoguang Buddhist Temple (宝光寺) enjoys a long history and a rich collection of relics. It is believed to have been constructed during the [East Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Han) period and to have appeared in written records from the Tang dynasty onward. It was destroyed during the Ming dynasty in the early 16th century. In 1607, the ninth year of the reign of the [Kangxi Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor) of the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty), it was rebuilt.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175]

#### Qingyang Palace



Qingyang Palace

Located in the western part of Chengdu, Qingyang Palace (青羊宫) is not only the largest and oldest Taoist temple in the city, but also the largest Taoist temple in Southwestern China. The only existing copy of the *Daozang Jiyao* (a collection of classic Taoist scriptures) is preserved in the temple.[^cite_note-\:10-176][^cite_note-177]

According to history, Qingyang Temple was the place where [Lao Tzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_Tzu) preached his famous [Dao De Jing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dao_De_Jing) to his disciple, Ying Xi.[^cite_note-\:10-176]

### Featured streets and historic towns

#### Kuanzhaixiangzi Alleys

Kuanzhaixiangzi Alleys (宽窄巷子) were first built during the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) for [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu) soldiers. The lanes remained residential until 2003, when the local government turned the area into a mixed-use strip of restaurants, teahouses, bars, avant-garde galleries, and residential houses. Historic architecture has been well preserved in the Wide and Narrow lanes.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180]

#### Jinli



Jinli Street at night

Nearby Wuhou Shrine, [Jinli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinli) is a popular commercial and dining area that reflects the traditional architecture of western [Sichuan](65185.md). "Jinli" (锦里) is the name of an old street in Chengdu dating from the Han dynasty and means "making perfection more perfect."[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-\:11-182]

The ancient Jinli Street was one of the oldest and most commercialized streets in the history of the Shu state and was well known throughout the country during the Qin, Han, and Three Kingdoms periods. Many aspects of Chengdu's urban life are present in the current-day Jinli area: teahouses, restaurants, bars, theaters, handicraft stores, local snack vendors, and specialty shops.[^cite_note-\:11-182][^cite_note-183]

#### Huanglongxi Historic Town



Huanglongxi Historic Town

Facing the [Jinjiang River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_River_\(Sichuan\)) to the east and leaning against Muma Mountain to the north, the ancient town of [Huanglongxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huanglongxi) is approximately 40 km (25 mi) southeast of Chengdu. It was a large military stronghold for the ancient Shu Kingdom. The head of the Shu Han State in the Three Kingdoms period was seated in Huanglongxi. For some time, the general government offices for Renshou, Pengshan, and Huayang counties were also located here. The ancient town has preserved the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) architectural style, as seen in its street, shop, and building designs.[^cite_note-184]

#### Chunxi Road



Dr. [Sun Yat-sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yat-sen) Square at [Chunxi Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chunxi_Road)

Located in downtown Chengdu, [Chunxi Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chunxi_Road) ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 春熙路) is a trendy, bustling commercial strip with a long history. It was built in 1924 and named after a passage from the [Tao Te Ching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao_Te_Ching). Today, it is one of the most well-known and popular fashion and shopping centers of Chengdu, lined with shopping malls, luxury brand stores, and boutique shops.[^cite_note-185][^cite_note-186]

#### Anren Historic Town

Anren Historic Town is located 39 km (24 mi) west of Chengdu. It was the hometown of [Liu Wencai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Wencai), a Qing dynasty warlord, landowner, and millionaire. His 27 historic mansions have been well preserved and turned into museums. Three old streets built during the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) period are still in use by residents today—museums in Anren house a rich collection of more than 8 million relics and artifacts. A museum dedicated to the [2008 Sichuan earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Sichuan_earthquake) was built in 2010.[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189]

#### Luodai Historic Town

Luodai was built, like many historic structures in the area, during the Three Kingdoms period. According to legend, the [Shu Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shu_Han) emperor [Liu Shan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Shan) dropped his jade belt into a well when he passed through this small town. Thus, the town was named 'lost belt' ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 落带). It later evolved into its current name [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 洛带 with the same pronunciation, but a different first character.[^cite_note-190]

Luodai Historic Town is one of the five major Hakka settlements in China. Three or four hundred years ago, a group of Hakka people moved from coastal cities to Luodai. It has since grown into the largest community for [Hakka people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_people).[^cite_note-191]

### Electronics and IT industries

Chengdu has long been an established national hub for the electronics and IT industries. Chengdu's growth accelerated alongside China's domestic telecom services sector, which, along with India's, accounts for over 70 percent of the world's telecommunications market. Several key national electronics R\&D institutes are located in Chengdu. Chengdu Hi-tech Industrial Development Zone has attracted a variety of multinationals, at least 30 [Fortune 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) companies and 12,000 domestic companies, including [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel), [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM), [Cisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisco), [Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia), [Motorola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorola), [SAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAP_AG), [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens), [Canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_\(company\)), [HP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewlett-Packard), [Xerox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xerox), [Microsoft](19001.md), [Tieto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tieto), [NIIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NIIT), [MediaTek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MediaTek), and [Wipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wipro), as well as domestic powerhouses such as [Lenovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenovo).[^cite_note-207] [Dell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dell) opened its second major China operations center in 2011[^cite_note-208] in Chengdu as its center in [Xiamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiamen) expands in 2010.[^cite_note-Dell-209]

Intel Capital acquired a strategic stake in Primetel, Chengdu's first foreign technology company in 2001.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211] Intel's Chengdu factory, set up in 2005 is its second in China, after its Shanghai factory, and the first such large-scale foreign investment in the electronics industry in interior mainland China.[^cite_note-technode1-212] Intel, the world's largest chipmaker, has invested US\$600 million in two assembly and testing facilities in Chengdu.[^cite_note-technode1-212] Following the footsteps of Intel, Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corporation (SMIC), the world's third largest [foundry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiconductor_fabrication_plant), set up an assembly and testing plant in Chengdu in 2006.[^cite_note-213] [AMD](2400.md), Intel's rival, had set up an R\&D center in this city in 2008.[^cite_note-214][^cite_note-215]

In November 2006, IBM signed an agreement with the Chengdu High-Tech Zone to establish a Global Delivery Center, its fourth in China after [Dalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalian), [Shanghai](27643.md), and [Shenzhen](83368.md), within the [Chengdu Tianfu Software Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Tianfu_Software_Park). Scheduled to be operational by February 2007, this new center will provide multilingual application development and maintenance services to clients globally in English, Japanese and Chinese, and to the IBM Global Procurement Center, recently located to the southern Chinese city of Shenzhen.[^cite_note-216] On 23 March 2008, IBM announced at the "West China Excellent Enterprises CEO Forum" that the southwest working team of [IBM Global Business Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM_Global_Business_Services) is now formally stationed in Chengdu. On 28 May 2008, Zhou Weikun, president of IBM China, disclosed that IBM Chengdu would increase its staff number from the present 600 to nearly 1,000 by the end of the year.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218]

In July 2019, [Amazon Web Services](1691376.md), the cloud computing company, signed a deal with the Chengdu High-Tech Zone to establish an innovation center. This project was intended to attract international businesses and enterprises to the area, promote cloud computing in China, and develop artificial intelligence technologies.[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220]

Chengdu is a major base for communication infrastructure, with one of China's nine top-level postal centers and one of six national telecom exchange hubs.[^cite_note-221]

In 2009, Chengdu hosted the [World Cyber Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Cyber_Games) Grand Finals (11–15 November). It was the first time China hosted the world's largest computer and video game tournament.[^cite_note-222]

### Financial industry

Chengdu is a leading financial hub in the Asia-Pacific region and ranks 35th globally and 6th in China (after [Shanghai](27643.md), [Hong Kong](13404.md), [Beijing](18603746.md), [Shenzhen](83368.md) and [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou)) in the 2021 [Global Financial Centres Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index).[^cite_note-GFCI2-10] Chengdu has attracted a large number of foreign financial institutions, including [Citigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citigroup), [HSBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSBC), [Standard Chartered Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chartered_Bank), [JPMorgan Chase](231001.md), [ANZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_and_New_Zealand_Banking_Group) and [MUFG Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MUFG_Bank).[^cite_note-223]

ANZ's data services center, established in 2011 in Chengdu, employs over 800 people. In March 2019, the bank recruited further staff to support its data analytics and [big data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_data) efforts.[^cite_note-224] In 2020, ANZ temporarily repurposed its Chengdu data center to an IT helpdesk, as part of the bank's pandemic response.[^cite_note-225]

Historically, Chengdu has marked its name in the history of financial innovation. The world's first paper [currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency) 'Jiao Zi' was seen in Chengdu in the year 1023, during the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty).[^cite_note-226]

Now, Chengdu is not only the gateway of Western China for foreign financial institutions, but also a popular town for Chinese domestic financial firms. The Chinese monetary authority, [People's Bank of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Bank_of_China) (China's central bank), set its southwestern China headquarters in Chengdu City.[^cite_note-227] In addition, almost all domestic banks and securities brokerage firms located their regional headquarters or branches in At the same time, the local financial firms of Chengdu are strengthening their presences nationally, notably, Huaxi Securities, Sinolink Securities, and Bank of Chengdu. Moreover, in addition to banks and brokerage firms, the city's expanding local economy attracted more financial service firms to capitalize on the economic growth. Grant Thornton, KPMG, PWC and Ernst & Young are the four global accountants and business advisers with Western China head offices in the city.[^cite_note-228][^cite_note-229]

It is expected that by 2012, value-added financial services will account for 14 percent of the added-value services industry and 7 percent of regional GDP. By 2015, those figures are expected to grow to 18 percent and 9 percent, respectively.[^cite_note-230]

### Modern logistic industry

Because of its logistic infrastructure, professional network, and resources in science, technology, and communication, Chengdu has become home to 43 foreign-funded logistic enterprises, including UPS, TNT, DHL, and Maersk, as well as several well-known domestic logistic enterprises, including COSCO, CSCL, SINOTRANS, CRE, Transfar Group, South Logistic Group, YCH, and STO. By 2012, the logistics industry in Chengdu will achieve RMB 50 billion in value-added, with average annual growth exceeding 18 percent. Ten new international direct flights will be in service; five railways will operate five scheduled block container trains; and 50 large logistics enterprises are expected to have annual operating revenue exceeding RMB 100 million.[^cite_note-231][^cite_note-232]

### Modern business and trade

Chengdu is the largest trade center in western China, with a market that covers all of Sichuan province and exerts influence on six provinces, cities, and districts. Chengdu ranks first among cities in western China in terms of the scale of foreign investment in commerce and trade.[^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234] By 2012, total retail sales of consumer goods in Chengdu reached RMB 331.77 billion, up 16 percent annually on average.[^cite_note-235]

### Convention and exhibition industry

Boasting the claim of being "China's Famous Exhibition City" and "China's Most Competitive Convention and Exhibition City", Chengdu leads central and western China in the scale of its convention economy.[^cite_note-\:14-236][^cite_note-237] It has been recognized as one of the three largest convention and exhibition cities in China.[^cite_note-\:14-236] In 2010, direct revenue from the convention and exhibition industry was RMB 3.21 billion, with a year-on-year growth of 27.8 percent.[^cite_note-238] The growth reached a historical high.

### Software and service outsourcing industry

In 2006, Chengdu was listed as one of the first service outsourcing base cities in China by the [Ministry of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Science_and_Technology_\(China\)).[^cite_note-239] Among the Top 10 service outsourcing enterprises in the world, Accenture, IBM, and Wipro are based in Chengdu.[^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241] In addition, 20 international enterprises including Motorola, Ubi Soft Entertainment, and Agilent, have set up internal shared service centers or R\&D centers in Chengdu.[^cite_note-242] Maersk Global Document Processing Center and Logistic Processing Sub-center, DHL Chengdu Service Center, Financial Accounting Center for DHL China, and Siemens Global IT Operation Center will be put into operation.[^cite_note-\:15-243] In 2010, offshore service outsourcing in Chengdu realized a registered contract value of US\$336 million, 99 percent higher than the previous year.[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-\:15-243]

### New energy industry

Chengdu was granted the title "National High-Tech Industry Base for New Energy Industry" (新能源产业国家高技术产业基地) by the [National Development and Reform Commission](10465505.md).[^cite_note-245] Chengdu ranked first again in the list of China's 15 "Cities with Highest Investment Value for New Energies" released at the beginning of 2011. Shuangliu County, under its jurisdiction, entered "2010 China's Top 100 Counties of New Energies".[^cite_note-246] In 2012, Chengdu's new energy industry reached an investment over 20 billion RMB and sales revenue of 50 billion RMB.[^cite_note-247][^cite_note-248]

### Electronics and information industry

Chengdu is home to the most competitive IT industry cluster in western China, an important integrated circuit industry base in China, and one of the five major national software industry bases.[^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250]

Manufacturing chains are already formed in integrated circuits, optoelectronics displays, digital video & audio, optical communication products, and original-equipment products of electronic terminals,[^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252] including companies as IBM, Intel, Texas Instruments, Microsoft, Motorola, Nokia, Ericsson, Dell, Lenovo, Foxconn, Compal, and Wistron.[^cite_note-253][^cite_note-254]

### Automobile industry

Chengdu has built a comprehensive automotive industry system. It has preliminarily formed an integrated system encompassing trade, exhibitions, entertainment, R\&D, and the manufacturing of spare parts and whole vehicles (e.g., sedans, coaches, sport utility vehicles, trucks, special vehicles). There are whole-vehicle makers, such as Dongfeng-PSA (Peugeot-Citroën), Volvo, FAW-Volkswagen, FAW-Toyota, Yema, and Sinotruk Wangpai, as well as nearly 200 core parts makers covering German, Japanese, and other vehicle lines.

In 2011, Volvo announced that its first manufacturing base in China, with an investment of RMB 5.4 billion, would be built in Chengdu. By 2015, the automobile production capacity of Chengdu's Comprehensive Function Zone of Automobile Industry is expected to reach 700,000 vehicles and 1.25 million in 2020.[^cite_note-255]

### Modern agriculture

Chengdu enjoys favorable agricultural conditions and rich natural resources. It is an important base for high-quality agricultural products. Chengdu is home to a national commercial grain and edible oil production base, a vegetable and food supply base, a key agricultural products processing center, and a logistics distribution center for western China.[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257]

### Defense industry

### Built environments

In 1988, the Implementation Plan for a Gradual Housing System Reform in Cities and Towns marked the beginning of overall housing reform in China's urban areas.[^cite_note-265] More than 20 real estate companies set up in Chengdu, which was the first step for Chengdu's real estate development.

The comprehensive [Funan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_River_\(Sichuan\)) renovation project in the 1990s was another step towards promoting Chengdu's environmental development.[^cite_note-266][^cite_note-267] The Funan River Comprehensive Improvement Project won the [UN-Habitat Scroll of Honour Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN-Habitat_Scroll_of_Honour_Award) in 1998,[^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269] as well as winning the "Local Initiative Award" by the [International Council for Local Environmental Initiatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Council_for_Local_Environmental_Initiatives) in 2000.[^cite_note-270]

Construction of the Five Main Roads & One Bridge project began in 1997. Three roads served the eastern part of the city; the other two led south. It established the foundation of the Eastern and Southern sub-centers of Chengdu. The two major sub-centers determined people's eastward and southward living trends. Large numbers of buildings appeared around the east and south of the 2nd Ring Road. The Shahe River renovation project, along with the Jin River project, also sparked a trend among residents living along both rivers. It was said that the map of Chengdu should be updated every three months.[^cite_note-271]

A speculative housing boom occurred in the late 1990s and early 2000s.[^cite_note-\:9-206]<sup>: 167 </sup> In 2000, dozens of commercial real estate projects also appeared.[^cite_note-272] While promoting the real estate market, the Chinese government encouraged citizens to buy their own houses by providing considerable subsidies for a certain period. Houses were included in commodities.

### Railway

Chengdu is the primary railway hub city and rail administrative center in southwestern China. The [China Railway Chengdu Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Railway_Chengdu_Group) manages the railway system of Sichuan Province, Chongqing City, and Guizhou Province. Chengdu has four main freight railway stations. Among them, Chengdu North Marshaling Station is one of the largest in Asia.[^cite_note-277] Since April 2013, companies can ship goods three times a week (initially only once a week)[^cite_note-278] to Europe on trains originating from Chengdu Qingbaijiang Station bound for [Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81%C3%B3d%C5%BA), Poland. It is the first express cargo train linking China and Europe, taking 12 days to complete the full journey.

Four major passenger stations are servicing Chengdu: [Chengdu railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_railway_station) (commonly referred to as the "North Station"), [Chengdu South railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_South_railway_station) (ChengduNan Station), [Chengdu East railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_East_railway_station) (ChengduDong Station), and [Chengdu West railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_West_railway_station) (ChengduXi Station).[^cite_note-279] Additionally, Chengdu Tianfu Station is under construction.

Chengdu is the terminus of [Baoji–Chengdu railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baoji%E2%80%93Chengdu_railway), [Chengdu–Chongqing railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu%E2%80%93Chongqing_railway), [Sichuan–Qinghai railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan%E2%80%93Qinghai_railway) (eventually connecting to Lanzhou), [Chengdu–Dazhou railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dazhou%E2%80%93Chengdu_railway), [Shanghai–Wuhan–Chengdu high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai%E2%80%93Wuhan%E2%80%93Chengdu_high-speed_railway), Sichuan–Qinghai railway, [Xi'an–Chengdu high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an%E2%80%93Chengdu_high-speed_railway), [Chengdu–Guiyang high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu%E2%80%93Guiyang_high-speed_railway), [Chengdu–Yibin high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu%E2%80%93Yibin_high-speed_railway) (eventually connecting to Kunming) and [Chengdu–Dujiangyan intercity railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu%E2%80%93Dujiangyan_intercity_railway).

The Chengdu–Dujiangyan intercity railway is a high-speed rail line connecting Chengdu with the satellite city of [Dujiangyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dujiangyan_City) and the [Mountain Qingcheng World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Qingcheng). The line is 65 km (40 mi) in length with 15 stations. [CRH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Railways_CRH1) train sets on the line reach a maximum speed of 220 km/h (140 mph) and complete the full trip in 30 minutes. The line was built in 18 months and entered operation on 12 May 2010.[^cite_note-autogenerated3-280]

### Metropolitan expressways

Chengdu's transport network is well developed, and the city serves as the starting point for many national highways, with major routes running from Sichuan to Shanxi, Sichuan to Tibet, and Sichuan to Yunnan.

Several major road projects have been constructed: a 15 km (9.3 mi) tunnel from Shuangliu Taiping to Jianyang Sancha Lake; alteration of the National Expressway 321, from [Jiangyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangyang) to [Longquanyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longquanyi). There will also be a road connecting Longquan Town to Longquan Lake; it connects to the Chengdu–Jianyang Expressway, shortening the journey by 10 km (6.2 mi). By the end of 2008, there were ten expressways connecting downtown Chengdu to its suburbs. The expressways include Chenglin Expressway, extensions of Guanghua Avenue and Shawan Line, and an expressway from Chengdu to [Heilongtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilongtan).

The [toll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_\(road_usage\))-free Chengjin Expressway in the east of Chengdu is 38.7 km (24.0 mi) long. It takes about 30 minutes to drive from central Chengdu to [Jintang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jintang_County).

The expressway between Chengdu and Heilongtan (Chengdu section), heading south of the city, is 42 km (26 mi) long. It is also toll-free, and the journey from downtown Chengdu to Heilongtan will take only half an hour.

The extension of Guanghua Avenue heads west out of the city. It makes the journey time from [Chongzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongzhou) City to Sanhuan Road less than half an hour.

The extension of Shawan Road going north is designed for travel at 60 km/h (37 mph). After it is connected to the expressways Pixian–Dujiangyan and Pixian–Pengzhou, it will take only 30 minutes to reach [Pengzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pengzhou) from Chengdu.

### River transport

Historically, the Jinjiang River (also known as the Nanhe River) was used for boat traffic in and out of Chengdu.[^cite_note-286] To ensure that Chengdu's goods have access to the Yangtze River efficiently, [inland port](1341936.md) cities of [Yibin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yibin) and [Luzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzhou)—both of which are reachable from Chengdu within hours by expressways—on the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze) have commenced large-scale port infrastructure development.[^cite_note-287][^cite_note-288] As materials and equipment for the rebuilding of northern Sichuan are sent in from the East Coast to Sichuan, these ports will see significant increases in throughput.[^cite_note-289]

## Education and research

Wen Weng, administrator of Chengdu in the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty), established the first local public school in the world, now named Shishi (literally "stone house"). The school site has not changed for more than 2,000 years, which remains the site of today's [Shishi High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Shishi_High_School).[^cite_note-290] [No. 7 High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_No.7_High_School) and [Shude High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Shude_High_School) are also two famous local public schools in Chengdu.

Chengdu is a leading scientific research city, one of the only two cities in the [Western China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_China) region (alongside [Xi'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi'an)), ranking in the top 25 cities worldwide by scientific research outputs.[^cite_note-\:7-291] It is consistently ranked # 1 as the [center of higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_First-Class_Construction) and scientific research in [Southwest China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_China).[^cite_note-\:3-292] The city is home to more than 58 universities,[^cite_note-293] with the two reputable ones being [Sichuan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_University) and the [University of Electronic Science and Technology of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Electronic_Science_and_Technology_of_China), ranking # 77 and #151 worldwide, respectively.[^cite_note-294]

## Sports

### Soccer



Chengdu Phoenix Hill Sports Park Football Stadium

Soccer is a popular sport in Chengdu. [Chengdu Tiancheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Tiancheng_F.C.), Chengdu's soccer team, played in the 42,000-seat Chengdu Sports Stadium in the [Chinese League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_League_One). The club was founded on 26 February 1996 and was formerly known as Chengdu Five Bulls, named after their first sponsor, the Five Bulls Cigarette Company. English professional soccer club Sheffield United F.C took over the club on 11 December 2005.[^cite_note-299] The club was later promoted into the [China Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Super_League) until they were embroiled in a match-fixing scandal in 2009. Punished with relegation the owners eventually sold their majority on 9 December 2010 to Hung Fu Enterprise Co., Ltd and Scarborough Development (China) Co., Ltd.[^cite_note-300][^cite_note-301] On 23 May 2013 the Tiancheng Investment Group announced the acquisition of the club.[^cite_note-302]

Currently, [Chengdu Rongcheng F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Rongcheng_F.C.) plays in the [Chinese Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Super_League).

[Longquanyi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Longquanyi_Stadium) was one of the four venues which hosted the [2004 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_AFC_Asian_Cup). Chengdu, along with Shanghai, Hangzhou, Tianjin, and Wuhan, hosted the [2007 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup).

### Tennis

Chengdu is the hometown of Grand Slam champions [Zheng Jie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_Jie) and [Yan Zi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yan_Zi_\(tennis\)), who won the women's double championships at both the Australian Open and Wimbledon in 2006, and [Li Na](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Na) who won the [2011 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_French_Open) and [2014 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Australian_Open), has led to increased interest in tennis in Chengdu. Over 700 standard tennis courts have been built in the city in the past 10 years (2006–2016), and the registered membership for the Chengdu Tennis Association has grown to over 10,000 from the original 2,000 in the 1980s.[^cite_note-303]

Chengdu is now part of an elite group of cities that host ATP (Association of Tennis Professionals) Champions Tour tournaments, along with London, Zürich, São Paulo, and Delray Beach. Chengdu Open, an ATP Championships Tour starting in 2009, have successfully invited star players including [Pete Sampras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Sampras), Marat Safin, Carlos Moya, Tomas Enqvist, and Mark Philippoussis.[^cite_note-304]

### *Overwatch*

Chengdu was represented in the [Overwatch League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overwatch_League) by the [Chengdu Hunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Hunters), the first major [esports](564204.md) team to represent Chengdu. They played in the League's Pacific Division from 2019 until 2022.

### *League of Legends*

Chengdu hosted the [2024 Mid-Season Invitational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mid-Season_Invitational) from 1 May to 19 May at the Chengdu Financial City Performing Arts Center. South Korean team [Gen.G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen.G) defeated home favorites [Bilibili Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilibili_Gaming) 3–1 in a rematch of their upper bracket final match. Before the [2024 League of Legends World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_League_of_Legends_World_Championship) grand finals, it was also announced that Chengdu would also host the [2025 tournament Final](78265754.md).

### Multi-sport events

Chengdu hosted the [2021 Summer World University Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Summer_World_University_Games), originally scheduled to take place from 8–19 August 2021. Still, the delayed Summer Olympics in Tokyo from 2020 to 2021 caused the proposed dates to be moved due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The games would eventually be delayed to 28 July – 8 August 2023 due to COVID-19 concerns. The city hosted the [2025 World Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_World_Games).

### Major sports venues



Chengdu Dong'an Lake Sports Park

The [Chengdu Sports Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Sports_Center) is located in downtown Chengdu, covering 140 acres (57 ha) and has 42,000 seats. As one of Chengdu's landmarks, it is the city's first large multipurpose venue, capable of hosting sports competitions, training, social activities, and performances. It is the home stadium of Chengdu's soccer team, the Chengdu Blades. The stadium hosted the [2007 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup).

The Sichuan International Tennis Center, located 16 km (10 mi) away from Chengdu's Shuangliu International Airport, covers an area of 250,000 m<sup>2</sup> (2,700,000 sq ft). It is the largest tennis center in southwest China and the fourth in China to meet ATP competition standards, after Beijing, Shanghai, and Nanjing. This center features 36 standard tennis courts and 11,000 seats. Since 2016, the [Chengdu Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Open), an ATP Championship Tour tournament, has been held here annually.

The [Chengdu Goldenport Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Goldenport_Circuit) was a motorsport racetrack that hosted the [A1 Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_Grand_Prix), [Formula V6 Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_V6_Asia), [China Formula 4 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Formula_4_Championship), and China GT Championship from 2007 to 2018. It was replaced by the [Chengdu Tianfu International Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Tianfu_International_Circuit), which opened in 2023.
