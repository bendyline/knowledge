**Coahuila**,[^cite_note-7] formally **Coahuila de Zaragoza**,[^cite_note-9] officially the **Free and Sovereign State of Coahuila de Zaragoza**,[^cite_note-10] is one of the 31 [states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Mexico) of Mexico. The largest city and state capital is the city of [Saltillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltillo); the second largest is [Torreón](195856.md) and the third largest is [Monclova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monclova) (a former state capital); the fourth largest is [Piedras Negras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedras_Negras,_Coahuila); and the fifth largest is [Ciudad Acuña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Acu%C3%B1a).

Coahuila borders the Mexican states of [Nuevo León](222247.md) to the east, [Zacatecas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zacatecas) to the south, and [Durango](222234.md) and [Chihuahua](23962301.md) to the west. To the north, Coahuila accounts for a 512-kilometre (318 mi) stretch of the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border), adjacent to the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [Texas](29810.md) along the course of the [Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande) (Río Bravo del Norte). With an area of 151,563 square kilometres (58,519 sq mi), it is the nation's third-largest state. It comprises 38 municipalities *([municipios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipio_\(Mexico\)))*. In the 2020 Census, Coahuila had a population of 3,146,771.[^cite_note-inegi2026-5]

## History

The name Coahuila derives from native terms for the region, and has been known by variations such as Cuagüila and Cuauila. Some historians believe that this means "flying serpent", "place of many trees", or "place where serpents creep". The official name of the state is Coahuila de Zaragoza, in honor of General [Ignacio Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacio_Zaragoza).

The Spanish explored the north of Mexico some decades after their victory in [Tenochtitlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenochtitlan), the capital of the Aztecs. Such exploration was delayed because the northern climate was harsher and there was no gold. The first Spanish settlement in the region now called Coahuila was at [Minas de la Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minas_de_la_Trinidad?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1577. Saltillo was settled in 1586, to form part of the province of [Nueva Vizcaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Vizcaya,_New_Spain) of the Vice-royalty of [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain). Later it became one of the first provinces of [Nueva Extremadura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Extremadura) to be explored by Europeans. Among the 16th century settlers of Saltillo and other communities in Nueva Vizcaya were [Tlaxcalans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlaxcala_\(Nahua_state\)), who founded an independent community bordering Saltillo, called [San Esteban de Nueva Tlaxcala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Esteban_de_Nueva_Tlaxcala).&#x20;

[Coahuila y Tejas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coahuila_y_Tejas) was one of the constituent states of the newly independent [United Mexican States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Mexican_States) under their [1824 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1824_Constitution_of_Mexico), and included [Texas](29810.md), Coahuila and [Nuevo León](222247.md). Later in the same year Nuevo León was detached, but Texas remained a part of the state until 1836, when it seceded to form the [Republic of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Texas). Monclova was the capital of the state from 1833 to 1835.

In 1840, Coahuila briefly became a member of the short lived [Republic of the Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_the_Rio_Grande). In the mid-19th century, the [Sánchez Navarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A1nchez_Navarro_latifundio) family owned a ranch of 6,700,000 ha (16,500,000 acres) mostly in Coahuila. It was the largest privately owned property in the Americas.[^cite_note-DeLay-11]

In the 1840s and 1850s, Coahuila was the target of frequent [Comanche raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comanche%E2%80%93Mexico_Wars).

On February 19, 1856, [Santiago Vidaurri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Vidaurri) annexed Coahuila to his state, [Nuevo León](222247.md), but it regained its separate status in 1868.

During the [Mexican Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Revolution), [Francisco Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Villa) attacked the city of [Torreón](195856.md).

On April 4, 2004, the border city of Piedras Negras was flooded. More than 30 people died and more than 4,000 lost their homes.[^cite_note-12]

In 2007 Coahuila became the first state in Mexico to offer [civil unions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_union) (Pacto Civil de Solidaridad) to same-sex couples.[^cite_note-13]

## Archaeological finds

In July 2025, archaeologists from Mexico’s National Institute of Anthropology and History ([INAH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_Nacional_de_Antropolog%C3%ADa_e_Historia)) and [CONANP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_natural_areas_of_Mexico) discovered a pre-Hispanic mortuary cave in the desert mountains of Coahuila. Accessed via a narrow 50‑cm vertical shaft, the cave contained the remains of around 17 individuals, including men, women, and children, who were interred in bundled form on baskets or mats. The semi-circular chamber preserved 12 complete skulls (five of which belonged to children), and while evidence of modern looting was present, the burials share significant similarities with those at Coahuila’s [Candelaria Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candelaria_Cave). The finds are being preserved at the Regional Museum of La Laguna in [Torreón](195856.md).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

## Geography

The [Sierra Madre Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_Oriental) runs northwest to southeast through the State, and the higher elevations are home to the [Sierra Madre Oriental pine-oak forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_Oriental_pine-oak_forests). The northernmost fingers of the Sierra Madre Oriental, the [Sierra del Burro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_del_Burro) and the [Sierra del Carmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_del_Carmen), reach to the border with the United States at the [Rio Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande).

East of the range, the land slopes gently toward the Rio Grande, and is drained by several rivers, including the [Salado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salado_River,_Mexico) and its tributary, the Sabinas River. The [Tamaulipan mezquital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamaulipan_mezquital), a [dry shrubland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deserts_and_xeric_shrublands) [ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion), occupies the eastern portion of the State, and extends across the Rio Grande into southern Texas.

The portion of the State west of the Sierra Madre Oriental lies on the [Mexican Plateau](12631416.md), and is part of the [Chihuahuan Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chihuahuan_Desert). The [Bolsón de Mapimí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bols%C3%B3n_de_Mapim%C3%AD) is a large [endorheic basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endorheic_basin) which covers much of the western portion of the State and extends into adjacent portions of Chihuahua, Durango, and Zacatecas. The [Nazas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazas_River), which flows east from Durango, and the [Aguanaval River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aguanaval_River), which flows north from Zacatecas, empty into lakes in the Bolsón. [Torreón](195856.md), the most populous city in the State, lies on the Nazas in the irrigated [Laguna Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Region), the *(Comarca Lagunera)*, which straddles the border of Coahuila and Durango.

Coahuila contains two [biosphere reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere_reserve). [Maderas del Carmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maderas_del_Carmen) lies on the northern border of the State, and includes sections of the Chihuahuan desert and [sky islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_island) of pine-oak forest in the [Sierra del Carmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_del_Carmen). The springs, lakes, and wetlands of the [Cuatro Ciénegas Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuatro_Ci%C3%A9negas_Basin) lie west of Monclova on the west slope of the Sierra Madre.

Coahuila is largely arid or semi-arid, but the rivers of the State support extensive irrigated agriculture, particularly [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton). The [Parras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parras) district in the southern part of the State produces wines and brandies. The [pine-oak forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_Oriental_pine-oak_forests) of the Sierra Madre produce timber.

The last population census run across Mexico in the year 2020, reports Coahuila de Zaragoza as having 3,146,771 inhabitants, which, considering its size, means that the state has a very low density, in fact as low as only 15 persons per square kilometer.

Coahuila's population is mainly made up of [Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo). Fewer than 7,500 natives reside in Coahuila, or merely 0.3% of the total population. According to the 2020 Census, 1.46% of Coahuila's population identified as Black, [Afro-Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Mexican), or of African descent.[^cite_note-19]

The rest of the demographic particulars in the state are very similar to national averages, such as a high life expectancy (reaching 75 years of age) and a [Catholic](606848.md) majority.

## Economy

About 95% of Mexico's [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) reserves are found in Coahuila, which is the country's top mining state.

[Saltillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltillo) and the Southeast region have one of the largest automobile industry in the country and the major industry in the state, hosting companies such as [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors_Corporation) and [Stellantis](64563392.md) assembly plants.[^cite_note-20]

[Torreón](195856.md) has [Met-Mex Peñoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Met-Mex_Pe%C3%B1oles), a mining company. The city is the world's largest [silver](27119.md) producer and Mexico's largest [gold](12240.md) producer. It also has Lala, a [dairy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy) products company, which produces 40% of Mexico's [milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk) consumption and distribution.

As of 2005, Coahuila's economy represents 3.5% of Mexico's total [gross domestic product](12594.md) or US\$22,874 million.[^cite_note-21] Coahuila's economy has a strong focus on export oriented manufacturing (i.e. [maquiladora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquiladora) / [INMEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INMEX?action=edit\&redlink=1)). As of 2005, 221,273 people are employed in the manufacturing sector.[^cite_note-22] Foreign direct investment in Coahuila was US\$143.1 million for 2005. The average wage for an employee in Coahuila is approximately 190 pesos per day.

On the other hand, Coahuila is the Mexican state with the highest level of [public debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_debt) in the nation.
