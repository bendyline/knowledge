**Nagorno-Karabakh** ([/nəˌɡɔːrnoʊ kərəˈbɑːk/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/25/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Persent101-Nagorno-Karabakh.wav/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Persent101-Nagorno-Karabakh.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/25/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Persent101-Nagorno-Karabakh.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Persent101-Nagorno-Karabakh.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, [*nə-GOR-noh kər-ə-BAHK*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-3] lit. 'Upper Karabakh') is a region located within the territory of present-day [Azerbaijan](746.md), covering the southeastern stretch of the [Lesser Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Caucasus) mountain range. Part of the greater region of [Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh), it spans the area between Lower Karabakh and [Syunik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syunik_Province). Its terrain mostly consists of mountains and forestland.

Most of Nagorno-Karabakh was governed by [ethnic Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_people) under the breakaway [Republic of Artsakh](1000530.md) – also known as the Nagorno-Karabakh Republic (NKR) – from the end of the [first Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) between [Armenia](10918072.md) and Azerbaijan in 1994 to the announcement of the dissolution of the republic in September 2023. Representatives from the two sides held numerous inconclusive peace talks mediated by the [OSCE Minsk Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OSCE_Minsk_Group) regarding the region's disputed status, with its majority-Armenian population over time variously advocating either for Artsakh's independence from both states or for its integration into Armenia.[^cite_note-4]

The region is usually equated with the administrative borders of the former [Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorno-Karabakh_Autonomous_Oblast) (NKAO), comprising 4,400 km<sup>2</sup> (1,700 sq mi); however, the region's historical extent encompasses approximately 8,223 km<sup>2</sup> (3,175 sq mi).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

On 27 September 2020, the [Second Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) broke out with an Azerbaijani offensive in Nagorno-Karabakh and the surrounding territories.[^cite_note-aljaz-7] Azerbaijan made significant gains during the war, regaining all of the [occupied territories surrounding Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian-occupied_territories_surrounding_Nagorno-Karabakh) and capturing one-third of Nagorno-Karabakh, including [Shusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusha_District_\(NKAO\)) and [Hadrut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrut_District_\(NKAO\)).[^cite_note-Global_Voices_2023-8][^cite_note-Baku_Research_Institute_2022-9][^cite_note-Cheterian_2022-10][^cite_note-Lynch_2020-11] The war ended on 10 November 2020 when [a trilateral ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_ceasefire_agreement) was signed between Azerbaijan, Armenia, and Russia, under which all the remaining occupied territories surrounding Nagorno-Karabakh were formally returned to Azerbaijani control. The Republic of Artsakh became an isolated [rump state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rump_state) connected with Armenia only by the narrow [Lachin corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachin_corridor), which was controlled by Russia.

On 19 September 2023, after [a blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_Nagorno-Karabakh) lasting several months, Azerbaijan launched a fresh large-scale [military offensive](74857564.md) in Nagorno-Karabakh.[^cite_note-offensive-radiofreeurope-12][^cite_note-offensive-reuters-13][^cite_note-\:0-14][^cite_note-offensive-media-15][^cite_note-offensive-france24-16] The Artsakh forces collapsed rapidly, resulting in an Azerbaijani victory, the dissolution of the Republic of Artsakh,[^cite_note-inter_2024-17] the [forced mass removal of almost the entire Armenian population](74922032.md) from the region,[^cite_note-The_Guardian-18] and the entry of Azerbaijani security forces into the former Artsakh capital of [Stepanakert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepanakert), known as Khankendi by Azerbaijan.[^cite_note-19] On 1 January 2024, the Republic of Artsakh was formally dissolved,[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-inter_2024-17] marking the end of a millennia-old Armenian presence in Nagorno-Karabakh.[^cite_note-cbc1-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] Various political analysts, along with residents of Nagorno-Karabakh, accused Azerbaijan of committing [ethnic cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_cleansing).[^cite_note-\:0-14][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

## Etymology

For the etymology of Karabakh, see [Karabakh § Etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh#Etymology).



June 2001 NASA photograph of the snow-covered Lesser Caucasus to the south of the Greater Caucasus. Around the year 1800, the [Karabakh Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh_Khanate) was based in the southeast corner of the Lesser Caucasus. It extended east into the lowlands, hence the name *Nagorno-* or "Highland-" Karabakh for the western part.

The prefix *Nagorno-* derives from the Russian [attributive adjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjective#Form) *nagorny* ([нагорный](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D0%BD%D0%B0%D0%B3%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%BD%D1%8B%D0%B9#Russian)), which means "highland". The [Azerbaijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_language) names of the region include the similar adjectives *dağlıq* (mountainous) or *yuxarı* (upper). Such words are not used in the [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language) name, but appeared in the region's official name during the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) era as [Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorno-Karabakh_Autonomous_Oblast). Other languages apply their own wording for *mountainous*, *upper*, or *highland*; for example, the official name used for the [Nagorno-Karabakh Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorno-Karabakh_Republic) in French is *Haut-Karabakh*, meaning "Upper Karabakh".

The names for the region in the various local languages all translate to "mountainous Karabakh", or "mountainous black garden":

- [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language): [Լեռնային Ղարաբաղ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D4%BC%D5%A5%D5%BC%D5%B6%D5%A1%D5%B5%D5%AB%D5%B6%20%D5%82%D5%A1%D6%80%D5%A1%D5%A2%D5%A1%D5%B2), [transliterated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Armenian) *Leṙnayin Ġarabaġ*, pronounced [\[lɛrnɑˈjin ʁɑɾɑˈbɑʁ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Armenian) [listen](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/3/36/Nagorno-Karabakh_AM.ogg/Nagorno-Karabakh_AM.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>

- [Azerbaijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_language): *[Dağlıq Qarabağ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Da%C4%9Fl%C4%B1q%20Qaraba%C4%9F)*, Дағлыг Гарабағ, lit. 'mountainous Karabakh', pronounced [\[dɑɣˈlɯɣ ɡɑˈɾɑbɑɣ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Azerbaijani) [listen](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/ed/Az-NagornoKarabakh.ogg/Az-NagornoKarabakh.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>\
  or *Yuxarı Qarabağ*, Јухары Гарабағ, lit. 'upper Karabakh', pronounced [\[juxɑˈɾɯ ɡɑˈɾɑbɑɣ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Azerbaijani) [listen](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/65/Az-YukhariKarabakh.ogg/Az-YukhariKarabakh.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>

- [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): [Нагорный Карабах](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D0%9D%D0%B0%D0%B3%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%BD%D1%8B%D0%B9%20%D0%9A%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B1%D0%B0%D1%85), [transliterated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian) *Nagorny Karabakh*, pronounced [\[nɐˈɡornɨj kərɐˈbax\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian)

Armenians call Nagorno-Karabakh *Artsakh* ([Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language): Արցախ)[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] [Urartian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartian) inscriptions (9th–7th centuries BC) use the name *Urtekhini* for the region. [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) sources called the area *Orkhistene*.[^cite_note-31]

## History

Main article: [History of Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nagorno-Karabakh)

### Antiquity and Early Middle Ages



The [Amaras Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaras_Monastery), founded in the 4th century by St [Gregory the Illuminator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_the_Illuminator). In the 5th century, [Mesrop Mashtots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesrop_Mashtots), inventor of the [Armenian alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_alphabet), established at Amaras the first school to use his script.

[^cite_note-32]

[^cite_note-33]



[The monastery at Gandzasar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandzasar_monastery) was commissioned by the [House of Khachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hasan-Jalalyan) and completed in 1238

Nagorno-Karabakh falls within the lands occupied by peoples known to modern archaeologists as the [Kura-Araxes culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kura-Araxes_culture) who lived between the two rivers [Kura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kura_\(Caspian_Sea\)) and [Araxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Araxes_River).[^cite_note-Edens-34]

The ancient population of the region consisted of various [autochthonous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) local and migrant tribes who were mostly non-Indo-Europeans.[^cite_note-Ethno-History-35] According to the prevailing western theory, these natives intermarried with Armenians who came to the region after its inclusion into Armenia in the 2nd (or possibly earlier, the 4th) century BC.[^cite_note-36] Other scholars suggest that the Armenians settled in the region as early as the 7th century BC.[^cite_note-37]

Around 180 BC, Artsakh became one of the 15 provinces of the [Armenian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Kingdom) and remained so until the 4th century.[^cite_note-38] While formally having the status of a province (*nahang*), Artsakh possibly formed a [principality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality) on its own – like Armenia's province of Syunik. Other theories suggest that Artsakh was a [royal land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_land), belonging directly to the king of Armenia.[^cite_note-39] King [Tigran the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigran_the_Great) of Armenia (who ruled from 95 to 55 BC) founded in Artsakh one of four cities named Tigranakert after himself.[^cite_note-40] The ruins of the ancient [Tigranakert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigranakert_of_Artsakh), located 50 km (30 mi) north-east of [Stepanakert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepanakert), are being studied by a group of international scholars.

In 387 AD, after the partition of Armenia between the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and [Sassanid Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid_Empire), two Armenian provinces – Artsakh and [Utik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utik) – became part of the Sassanid [satrapy of Caucasian Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania_\(satrapy\)), which in turn came under strong Armenian religious and cultural influence.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] At the time the population of Artsakh and Utik consisted of Armenians and several Armenized tribes.[^cite_note-Ethno-History-35]

Armenian culture and civilization flourished in the early medieval Nagorno-Karabakh. In the 5th century, the first-ever Armenian school was opened on the territory of modern Nagorno-Karabakh at [Amaras Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaras_Monastery) through the efforts of St. [Mesrop Mashtots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesrop_Mashtots), the inventor of the [Armenian alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_alphabet).[^cite_note-43] St. Mesrop was very active in [preaching the Gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelism) in Artsakh and Utik. Overall, Mesrop Mashtots made three trips to Artsakh and Utik, ultimately reaching pagan territories at the foothills of the [Greater Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Caucasus).[^cite_note-44] The 7th-century Armenian linguist and grammarian Stephanos Syunetsi stated in his work that Armenians of Artsakh had their own dialect, and encouraged his readers to learn it.[^cite_note-45]

### High Middle Ages

Main article: [Principality of Khachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Khachen)

Around the mid 7th century, the region was conquered by the invading Muslim Arabs through the [Muslim conquest of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia). Subsequently, it was ruled by local governors endorsed by the [Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate). According to some sources, in 821 the Armenian[^cite_note-46] prince [Sahl Smbatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahl_Smbatian) revolted in Artsakh and established the [House of Khachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Khachen), which ruled Artsakh as a [principality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Khachen) until the early 19th century.[^cite_note-Atlas-47] According to other sources, Sahl Smbatian "was of the Zamirhakan family of kings", and in the year 837–838 he acquired sovereignty over Armenia, Georgia, and Albania.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] The name "Khachen" originated from Armenian word *khach*, which means "cross".[^cite_note-50] By 1000 the House of Khachen proclaimed the [Kingdom of Artsakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Artsakh) with [John Senecherib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Senecherib?action=edit\&redlink=1) as its first ruler.[^cite_note-51] Initially [Dizak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizak) in southern Artsakh also formed a kingdom ruled by the ancient [House of Aranshahik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranshahik), descended from the earliest Kings of Caucasian Albania. In 1261, after the daughter of the last king of Dizak married the king of Artsakh, Armenian[^cite_note-52] prince [Hasan Jalal Dola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hasan-Jalalyan), the two states merged into one[^cite_note-Atlas-47] Armenian[^cite_note-53] Principality of Khachen. Subsequently, Artsakh continued to exist as a de facto independent principality.

### Late Middle Ages

Main articles: [Karabakh Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh_Khanate) and [Melikdoms of Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melikdoms_of_Karabakh)



The [Shusha fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusha_fortress), built by the [Karabakh Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh_Khanate) ruler [Panah Ali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panah_Ali_Khan) in the 18th century



The semi-independent [*Five Principalities*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melikdoms_of_Karabakh) ([Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language): Խամսայի Մելիքություններ) of Karabakh (Gyulistan, Jraberd, Khachen, Varanda, and Dizak), widely considered to be the last relic of Armenian statehood (15th–19th century).

[^cite_note-54]

[^cite_note-55]

In the 15th century, the territory of Karabakh was part of the states ruled subsequently by the [Kara Koyunlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara_Koyunlu) and [Ak Koyunlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak_Koyunlu) [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples) tribal confederations. According to [Abu Bakr Tihrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr_Tihrani), during the period of [Jahan Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahan_Shah) (1438–1468), the ruler of Kara Koyunlu, Piri bey Karamanli held the governorship of Karabakh.[^cite_note-56] However, according to [Robert H. Hewsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_H._Hewsen), the [Turkoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_people) lord Jahan Shah (1437–1467) assigned the governorship of upper Karabakh to local Armenian princes, allowing a native Armenian leadership to emerge consisting of five noble families led by princes who held the titles of *[meliks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melik)*.[^cite_note-Atlas-47] These dynasties represented the branches of the earlier House of Khachen and were the descendants of the medieval kings of Artsakh. Their lands were often referred to as the Country of [Khamsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Khachen) ('five' in Arabic). In a Charter (2 June 1799) of the Emperor [Paul I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_I_of_Russia) titled "About their admission to Russian suzerainty, land allocation, rights and privileges", it was noted that the Christian heritage of the Karabakh region and all their people were admitted to the Russian suzerainty.[^cite_note-57] However, according to Robert Hewsen, the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) recognised the [sovereign](28791.md) status of the five princes in their domains by the charter of Emperor Paul I dated 2 June 1799.[^cite_note-Relations-58]

The Armenian meliks were granted supreme command over neighbouring Armenian principalities and Muslim khans in the Caucasus by the Iranian king [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah), in return for the meliks' victories over the invading [Ottoman Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turks) in the 1720s.[^cite_note-59] These five principalities[^cite_note-60] in Karabakh were ruled by Armenian families who had received the title Melik (prince) and were the following:

- **Principality of *Gulistan*** – under the leadership of the Melik-Beglarian family

- **Principality of *Jraberd*** – under the leadership of the Melik-Israelian family

- **Principality of *[Khachen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khachen)*** – under the leadership of the [Hasan-Jalalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasan_Jalalyan) family

- **Principality of *Varanda*** – under the leadership of the Melik-Shahnazarian family

- **Principality of *[Dizak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizak)*** – under the leadership of the Melik-Avanian family

From 1501 to 1736, during the existence of the [Safavid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Empire), the province of Karabakh was governed by the [Ziyadoghlu Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziyadoghlu_Qajar) dynasty, until [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah) took over Karabakh from their rule.[^cite_note-61] The Armenian meliks maintained full control over the region until the mid-18th century. In the early 18th century, Iran's Nader Shah took Karabakh out of control of the Ganja khans in punishment for their support of the [Safavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid), and placed it under his own control[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-Adigezal-63] In the mid-18th century, as internal conflicts between the meliks led to their weakening, the Karabakh Khanate was formed. The [Karabakh khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh_khanate), one of the largest [khanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanates_of_the_Caucasus) under [Iranian](14653.md) suzerainty,[^cite_note-64] was headed by [Panah-Ali khan Javanshir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panah-Ali_khan_Javanshir). For the reinforcement of the power of Karabakh khanate, Khan of Karabakh, Panah-Ali khan Javanshir, built up "[the fortress of Panahabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusha_fortress) (today Shusha)" in 1751. During that time, Otuziki, [Javanshir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanshir_clan), Kebirli, and other Turkic tribes constituted the majority of the overall population.

### Modern era



[Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Karabakh_Khans) of the former ruler (khan) of [Shusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusha). Taken from a postcard from the late 19th–early 20th century.



Aftermath of the [Shusha massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusha_massacre): Armenian half of Shusha destroyed by Azerbaijani armed forces in 1920, with the defiled [Armenian Cathedral of the Holy Savior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazanchetsots_Cathedral) in the background.

[Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh) (including modern-day Nagorno-Karabakh), became a [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) by the [Kurekchay Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Treaty_of_Kurakchay), signed between [Ibrahim Khalil Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Khalil_Khan) of Karabakh and general [Pavel Tsitsianov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Tsitsianov) on behalf of Tsar [Alexander I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Russia) in 1805, according to which the Russian monarch recognized Ibrahim Khalil Khan and his descendants as the sole hereditary rulers of the region.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] However, its new status was only confirmed following the outcome of the [Russo-Persian War (1804-1813)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1804-1813\)), when through the loss in the war, Persia formally ceded Karabakh to the Russian Empire per the [Treaty of Gulistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Gulistan) (1813),[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] before the rest of [Transcaucasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasia) was incorporated into the Empire in 1828 by the [Treaty of Turkmenchay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turkmenchay), which came as an outcome of the [Russo-Persian War (1826-1828)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1826-1828\)).

In 1822, 9 years after it passed from Iranian to Russian control, the Karabakh Khanate was dissolved and the area became part of the [Elizavetpol Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizavetpol_Governorate) within the Russian Empire. In 1823 the five districts corresponding roughly to modern-day Nagorno-Karabakh were 90.8% Armenian-populated.[^cite_note-Tbilisi_1866-72][^cite_note-Bournoutian,_George_A._1994,_page_18-73]

### Soviet era

Main article: [Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorno-Karabakh_Autonomous_Oblast)



Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast in the Soviet era.



Ethnic make-up of Nagorno-Karabakh in the late Soviet era.

After the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution), Karabakh became part of the [Transcaucasian Democratic Federative Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasian_Democratic_Federative_Republic), but this soon dissolved into separate [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia), [Azerbaijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Democratic_Republic), and [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Republic_of_Georgia) states. Over the next two years (1918–1920), there were a series of [short wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian%E2%80%93Azerbaijani_war_\(1918%E2%80%931920\)) between Armenia and Azerbaijan over several regions, including Nagorno-Karabakh. Between 1918 and 1920, Nagorno-Karabakh's [de jure](43594.md) affiliation with Armenia or Azerbaijan was disputed and not adjudicated by the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

In July 1918, the First Armenian Assembly of Nagorno-Karabakh declared the region self-governing and created a National Council and government.[^cite_note-nesl.edu-76] Later, [Ottoman Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Army_\(1861%E2%80%931922\)) troops entered Karabakh, meeting armed resistance by Armenians.

After the defeat of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) in World War I, [British Army](4887.md) troops occupied Karabakh. The British command provisionally affirmed [Khosrov bey Sultanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khosrov_bey_Sultanov) (appointed by the Azerbaijani government) as the governor-general of Karabakh and [Zangezur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zangezur_Uyezd), pending a final decision by the [Paris Peace Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Conference,_1919).[^cite_note-77] The decision was opposed by Karabakh Armenians. In February 1920, the Karabakh National Council preliminarily agreed to Azerbaijani jurisdiction, while Armenians elsewhere in Karabakh continued guerrilla fighting, never accepting the agreement.[^cite_note-nesl.edu-76] The agreement itself was soon annulled by the Ninth Karabagh Assembly, which declared union with Armenia in April.[^cite_note-nesl.edu-76][^cite_note-78]

In April 1920, while the Azerbaijani army was locked in Karabakh fighting local Armenian forces, Azerbaijan was [taken over](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army_invasion_of_Azerbaijan) by [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik). On 10 August 1920, Armenia signed a preliminary agreement with the Bolsheviks, agreeing to a temporary Bolshevik occupation of these areas until final settlement would be reached.[^cite_note-79] In 1921, [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army_invasion_of_Armenia) and [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army_invasion_of_Georgia) were also taken over by the Bolsheviks. After the [Sovietization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovietization) of Armenia and Azerbaijan, the [Kavbiuro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavbiuro) (Caucasian Bureau of the Central Committee of the [Russian Communist Party (Bolshevik)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Communist_Party_\(Bolshevik\))) decided that Karabakh would remain within [Azerbaijan SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_SSR) with broad regional autonomy, with the administrative centre in the city of [Shusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusha) (the administrative centre was later moved to [Stepanakert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepanakert)).[^cite_note-80] The oblast's borders were drawn to include Armenian villages and to exclude as much as possible Azerbaijani villages.[^cite_note-81] The resulting district ensured an Armenian majority.

With the Soviet Union firmly in control of the region, the [conflict](4695860.md) over the region died down for several decades until the beginning of the dissolution of the Soviet Union in the late 1980s and early 1990s, when the question of Nagorno-Karabakh re-emerged. Accusing the Azerbaijani SSR government of conducting forced Azerification of the region, the majority Armenian population, with ideological and material support from the [Armenian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_SSR), started a movement to have the autonomous oblast transferred to the Armenian SSR.[^cite_note-82] In August 1987, Karabakh Armenians sent a petition for union with Armenia with tens of thousands of signatures to Moscow.[^cite_note-83]

### War and secession

Main article: [First Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nagorno-Karabakh_War)



A restored Armenian [T-72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-72), knocked out of commission while attacking Azeri positions in [Askeran District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Askeran_District_\(NKAO\)), serves as a war memorial on the outskirts of Stepanakert.

On 13 February 1988, Karabakh Armenians began demonstrating in [Stepanakert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepanakert), in favour of unification with the Armenian republic. Six days later they were joined by mass marches in [Yerevan](34352.md). On 20 February, the Soviet of People's Deputies in Karabakh voted 110 to 17 to request the transfer of the region to Armenia. This unprecedented action by a regional Soviet brought out tens of thousands of demonstrations both in Stepanakert and Yerevan, but Moscow rejected the Armenians' demands. The confrontation between the Azeris and the police near Askeran degenerated into the [Askeran clash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Askeran_clash), which left two Azeris dead, one of them allegedly killed by an Azeri police officer. Fifty Armenian villagers and an unknown number of Azeris and police officers were injured.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-dewaal-86] Large numbers of refugees left Armenia and Azerbaijan as violence began against the minority populations of the respective republics.[^cite_note-87] On 7 July 1988, the European Parliament passed a resolution that condemned the violence employed against Armenian demonstrators in Azerbaijan, and supported the demand of the Armenians for reunification with the Soviet Republic of Armenia.[^cite_note-88]

On 29 November 1989, direct rule in Nagorno-Karabakh was ended and the region was returned to Azerbaijani administration.[^cite_note-89] The Soviet policy backfired, however, when a joint session of the Armenian [Supreme Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Soviet) and the National Council, the legislative body of Nagorno-Karabakh, proclaimed the unification of Nagorno-Karabakh with Armenia. On 26 November 1991 Azerbaijan [abolished the status of Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_on_Abolishment_of_Nagorno-Karabakh_Autonomous_Oblast), rearranging the [administrative division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_administrative_divisions_of_Nagorno-Karabakh) and bringing the territory under direct control of Azerbaijan.[^cite_note-90]

On 10 December 1991, in a referendum boycotted by local Azerbaijanis,[^cite_note-dewaal-86] Armenians in Nagorno-Karabakh approved the creation of an independent state. A Soviet proposal for enhanced autonomy for Nagorno-Karabakh within Azerbaijan satisfied neither side and [a full-scale war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) subsequently erupted between Azerbaijan and Nagorno-Karabakh, with the latter receiving support from Armenia.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] According to Armenia's former president, [Levon Ter-Petrossian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levon_Ter-Petrossian), the Karabakh leadership approach was maximalist and "they thought they could get more."[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

The struggle over Nagorno-Karabakh escalated after both Armenia and Azerbaijan attained independence from the Soviet Union in 1991. In the post-Soviet [power vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_vacuum), military action between Azerbaijan and Armenia was heavily influenced by the [Russian military](25709.md). Furthermore, both the [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Armenia) and [Azerbaijani military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_Armed_Forces) employed a large number of mercenaries from [Ukraine](31750.md) and Russia.[^cite_note-ratios-98] Between fifteen and twenty-five hundred [Afghan mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen), along with fighters from [Chechnya](6095.md), participated in the fighting on Azerbaijan's side. Russia provided Armenia with heavy artillery and tanks.[^cite_note-dewaal-86] Many survivors from the Azerbaijani side found shelter in 12 emergency camps set up in other parts of Azerbaijan to cope with the growing number of internally displaced people due to the first Nagorno-Karabakh war.[^cite_note-99]

By the end of 1993, the conflict had caused about 30,000 casualties[^cite_note-100] and created hundreds of thousands of refugees on both sides. By May 1994, the Armenians were in control of 14% of the territory of Azerbaijan.[^cite_note-101] At that stage, for the first time during the conflict, the Azerbaijani government recognized Nagorno-Karabakh as a third party in the war and started direct negotiations with the Karabakh authorities. As a result, a [ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishkek_Protocol) was reached on 12 May 1994 through Russian mediation.

### 2020 war and ceasefire agreement

Main articles: [Second Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) and [2020 Nagorno-Karabakh ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_ceasefire_agreement)

On 27 September 2020, a [new war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) erupted in Nagorno-Karabakh and the surrounding territories.[^cite_note-aljaz-7] The United Nations strongly condemned the conflict and called on both sides to deescalate tensions and resume meaningful negotiations without delay.[^cite_note-117]

The war ended on 10 November 2020, when [a trilateral ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_ceasefire_agreement) was signed among Azerbaijan, Armenia, and Russia.[^cite_note-118] According to the ceasefire agreement, Azerbaijan regained all of the [occupied territories surrounding Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian-occupied_territories_surrounding_Nagorno-Karabakh) and capturing one-third of Nagorno-Karabakh proper, including [Shusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusha_District_\(NKAO\)) and [Hadrut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrut_District_\(NKAO\)).[^cite_note-Global_Voices_2023-8][^cite_note-Baku_Research_Institute_2022-9][^cite_note-Cheterian_2022-10][^cite_note-Lynch_2020-11][^cite_note-119]

### Blockade (2022–2023)

Main article: [Blockade of Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_Nagorno-Karabakh)

In December 2022, Azerbaijanis claiming to be environmental activists blocked the [Lachin corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachin_corridor), the sole road connecting Nagorno-Karabakh to Armenia and the outside world.[^cite_note-BBC2-120] On 23 April 2023, Azerbaijani forces installed a checkpoint on the Lachin corridor.[^cite_note-121] The blockade led to a humanitarian crisis for the population in Artsakh and imports of essential goods were blocked, as well as humanitarian convoys of the Red Cross and the Russian peacekeepers, trapping the 120,000 residents of the region.[^cite_note-\:1-122][^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126] Limited traffic had been conducted by Russian peacekeepers and the [International Committee of the Red Cross](5023832.md) to transport patients in need of medical care and provide humanitarian supplies.[^cite_note-BlackoutsRationing-127][^cite_note-128] However, starting on 15 June 2023, Azerbaijan intensified the blockade, blocking all passage of food, fuel, and medicine from the Red Cross and the Russian peacekeepers through the Lachin corridor.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-\:1-122] On 19 September, Azerbaijan [launched a military operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Nagorno-Karabakh_clashes).

### Azerbaijani offensive (2023)

Main article: [2023 Azerbaijani offensive in Nagorno-Karabakh](74857564.md)

On 19 September 2023, Azerbaijan [launched a military offensive](74857564.md) on Nagorno-Karabakh.[^cite_note-offensive-radiofreeurope-12][^cite_note-offensive-reuters-13][^cite_note-\:0-14][^cite_note-offensive-media-15][^cite_note-offensive-france24-16] One day after the offensive started, on 20 September, an agreement on establishing a complete cessation of hostilities in Nagorno-Karabakh was reached at the mediation of the [Russian peacekeeping command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_operations_in_Nagorno-Karabakh) in Nagorno-Karabakh.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-offensive-CNN-132] Azerbaijan held a meeting with representatives of the Artsakh Armenian community on 21 September in [Yevlakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevlakh) to start the process of re-integrating the region into Azerbaijan.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134] Ceasefire violations by Azerbaijan were nonetheless reported by both Artsakh and local residents in [Stepanakert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepanakert) on 21 September.[^cite_note-ceasefirevio12-135][^cite_note-ceasefirevio22-136] In the aftermath of the offensive, an [exodus of Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exodus_of_Armenians_from_Nagorno-Karabakh) from the region started.[^cite_note-The_Guardian-18] On 28 September 2023, the Republic of Artsakh agreed to dissolve itself by 1 January 2024.[^cite_note-inter_2024-17]

## Geography



The [Sarsang Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarsang_Reservoir)



A view of the forested mountains of Nagorno-Karabakh

Nagorno-Karabakh has a total area of 4,400 square kilometres (1,699 sq mi).[^cite_note-137] Approximately half of Nagorno-Karabakh terrain is over 950 metres (3,120 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level).[^cite_note-Zurcher-138] The borders of Nagorno-Karabakh resemble a kidney bean with the indentation on the east side. It has tall mountain ridges along the northern edge and along the west and mountainous south. The part near the indentation of the kidney bean itself is a relatively flat valley, with the two edges of the bean, the districts of [Martakert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardakert_District_\(NKAO\)) and [Martuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martuni_District_\(NKAO\)), having flatlands as well. Other flatter valleys exist around the [Sarsang reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarsang_reservoir), [Hadrut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrut_District_\(NKAO\)), and the south. The entire region lies, on average, 1,100 metres (3,600 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level).[^cite_note-Zurcher-138] Notable peaks include the border mountain [Murovdag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murovdag) and the [Great Kirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kirs) mountain chain in the junction of [Shusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusha_District_\(NKAO\)) and Hadrut districts. The territory of modern Nagorno-Karabakh forms a portion of the historic region of Karabakh, which lies between the rivers [Kura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kura_\(Caspian_Sea\)) and [Araxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Araxes_River), and the modern Armenia-Azerbaijan border. Nagorno-Karabakh in its modern borders is part of the larger region of Upper Karabakh.

Nagorno-Karabakh does not directly border Armenia but is connected to the latter through the [Lachin corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachin_corridor), a mountain pass under the control of the [Russian peacekeeping forces in Nagorno-Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_peacekeepers_in_Nagorno-Karabakh).

The major cities of the region are [Stepanakert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepanakert), which once served as the capital of the unrecognised Nagorno-Karabakh Republic, and [Shusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusha), which lies partially in ruins. Vineyards, orchards, and mulberry groves for silkworms are developed in the valleys.[^cite_note-139]

## Environment

Nagorno-Karabakh's environment vary from [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe) on the Kura lowland through dense forests of [oak](39696.md), [hornbeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornbeam), and [beech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beech) on the lower mountain slopes to [birchwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch) and [alpine meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_meadow) higher up. The region possesses numerous [mineral springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_spring) and deposits of [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [lead](17747.md), [gold](12240.md), [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble), and [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone).[^cite_note-140]

## Demographics



Ethnic groups of the region in 1995, after the deportations of Armenians and Azerbaijanis. ([See entire map](http://www.lib.utexas.edu/maps/commonwealth/ethnocaucasus.jpg))

### Antiquity – 1918

Historically, the inhabitants of Nagorno-Karabakh — then part of the [province of Artsakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artsakh_\(historical_province\)) — were confirmed by [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) and [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) sources "long before our era" to be Armenian.[^cite_note-beglaryan-141] In the early 15th century, German traveller [Johann Schiltberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Schiltberger) after visiting the region stated that "although the [Muslims](19541.md) had taken possession of Karabagh, there were still Armenian villages in the region".[^cite_note-twochroniclesonthehistoryofkarabagh-142] Historian [Victor Schnirelmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Schnirelmann) writes that "In the mid-18th century, … [Turkic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples) … gained access to the highland territories \[of Karabakh] and began to settle in Shusha … by the end of the 18th century, a substantial number of its former Armenian inhabitants had left Nagorny Karabagh. Just at the turn of the 19th century, the Turkic population significantly outnumbered the local Armenians, but this only lasted … \[until the] end of the 1820s, \[when] the Armenians began to come back to Karabagh, and they accounted for the majority of its population by the mid-19th century".[^cite_note-143] [Edmund Herzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Herzig) and Marina Kurkchiyan present an alternative view that "Armenians had already been a majority in some areas such as mountainous Karabagh", compared to the [Yerevan province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erivan_Governorate) which had "regained an Armenian majority for the first time in several hundred years."[^cite_note-thearmenianspastandpresent-144]

According to an 1823 [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) survey published in Tiflis (present-day [Tbilisi](42298.md)) in 1866,[^cite_note-twochroniclesonthehistoryofkarabagh-142] Armenians made up 97 percent of the population in the [five districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melikdoms_of_Karabakh) ([*mahals*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D9%85%D8%AD%D9%84#Persian)) of Nagorno-Karabakh,[^cite_note-145] thus proving, contrary to claims in [Azerbaijani historiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falsification_of_history_in_Azerbaijan), that Armenians formed an overwhelming majority of Nagorno-Karabakh prior to 1828. Historian [George Bournoutian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bournoutian) writes that Russian statistics from 1810 show that Armenians made up 21 percent of the Karabakh region's population; In 1823, the Armenian population of Karabakh had increased by 30 percent "after the return of those who had fled the region", and by 1832, the Armenian population had increased to one-third of Karabakh. Moreover, the "one-third" of the population of Karabakh composed of Armenians resided in one-third of the territory of Karabakh, the mountainous territory (i.e. Nagorno-Karabakh), where they "constituted an overwhelming majority of the population."[^cite_note-twochroniclesonthehistoryofkarabagh-142]

### 1918–1920

According to Armenian sources, the "historical Nagorno-Karabakh" region had a population of 300,000–330,000 in 1918–1920, rising to 700,000–800,000 by 1988. As a result of "[Turkish](22278.md)-[Azerbaijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Democratic_Republic) aggression", the region's population declined by 20 percent in 1918–1920.[^cite_note-thepursuitofinternationalcriminaljustice-146] In this period, [Azerbaijani forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_Land_Forces) carried out massacres against Armenians in [Ghaibalishen, Jamilli, Karkijahan, and Pahlul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaibalikend_massacre) (600–700 dead),[^cite_note-147] [Stepanakert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepanakert) (several hundred dead),[^cite_note-148] and [Shusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusha_massacre) (several hundred[^cite_note-149] to 12,000 dead).[^cite_note-lazarevsky-150] As a result of the Shusha massacre, 5,000–6,000 Armenians were displaced to the regions of [Varanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanda_Region) and [Dizak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizak).[^cite_note-151] By 11 April 1920, some thirty villages in Nagorno-Karabakh had been "devastated" by Azerbaijani forces as a result of the uprising, leaving 25,000 homeless (including nearly 6,000 refugees from Shusha).[^cite_note-152]

### 1921–1987

1923 statistics indicate that the NKAO was 94.8 percent Armenian, numbering 149,600, whilst the Azerbaijani population numbered 7,700. Historian Cory Welt writes of a "discrepancy" of the Armenian population jumping by over 25,000 individuals between the 1921 and 1923 censuses, also pointing out that the Armenian population declined to 111,700 in 1926, thus indicating an "unexplained drop" of 38,000 individuals.[^cite_note-welt-153] In the 1920s, the NKAO had a population of 131,500 people, 94.4 percent (124,136) of whom were ethnic Armenians and only 5.6 percent (7,364) of whom were ethnic Azerbaijanis.[^cite_note-thearmenianspastandpresent-144] In 1933, Nagorno-Karabakh had a population of 147,308, 10,751 (7.3 percent) of whom were urban dwellers, and 136,557 (92.7 percent) of whom were rural residents.[^cite_note-154] On 1 January 1973, the oblast had a population of 153,000.[^cite_note-155]

#### Discrimination and stagnation

&#x20;Whilst the region was a part of the Azerbaijan SSR, the Armenian share of the population dropped from 94.7 to 76.9 percent, whilst the Azerbaijani share of the population quadrupled from 5.1 to 21.5 percent[^cite_note-thearmenianspastandpresent-144] as a result of "migratory influx"[^cite_note-thepursuitofinternationalcriminaljustice-146] — indicative of the socio-economic difficulties local Armenians experienced under Soviet Azerbaijani leadership which led them to emigrate from Karabakh.[^cite_note-thearmenianspastandpresent-144] Emeritus professor of law [M. Cherif Bassiouni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._Cherif_Bassiouni) writes of the stagnation of the Armenian population "due to the discriminatory policies of Azerbaijani authorities that compelled Armenians to emigrate"; also adding that 600,000 Armenians from Karabakh reside in Armenia and the countries of the [CIS](36870.md).[^cite_note-thepursuitofinternationalcriminaljustice-146] According to historian Deon Geldenhuys, "\[t]his was due to Baku's deliberate promotion of Azerbaijani settlement in Karabagh as part of a policy of 'cultural de-Armenization' of the region"; further adding that Azerbaijan "neglected the economic needs of the territory".[^cite_note-156] Stuart J. Kaufman, a professor of political science and international relations,[^cite_note-157] writes of the difficulties of Karabakh Armenians:

> … Armenian-language education was not easily available, Armenian history was not taught at all, and those who went to Armenia for training were discriminated against in competing for jobs in the province, … Underinvestment in the region—also blamed on Baku—meant less economic development and poor infrastructure even by Soviet standards, and therefore fewer jobs overall, especially for Armenians. Cultural ties with Armenia were strangled in red tape in Baku, and a decision to make Armenian-language television available in the region was left unimplemented. One result of these policies was a continuing exodus of Armenians from Karabagh in search of greener pastures.[^cite_note-158]

### 1988–present

Following the [Sumgait pogrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumgait_pogrom) and the exodus of [Azerbaijanis from Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijanis_in_Armenia), Azerbaijanis in Stepanakert and Armenians in Shusha were [expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_violence_in_Shusha_and_Stepanakert) in September 1988. As [local Armenian forces](1000530.md) gained possession of Nagorno-Karabakh and [surrounding districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian-occupied_territories_surrounding_Nagorno-Karabakh) (amounting to 14 percent of the internationally recognised territory of Azerbaijan) during the [First Nagorno-Karabakh war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nagorno-Karabakh_war), hundreds of thousands of Azerbaijanis were expelled from their lands.[^cite_note-dewaal-86] During the [Second Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Nagorno-Karabakh_War), Azerbaijan regained control over the surrounding districts and southern parts of the former NKAO, thus displacing approximately 70,000 Armenians.[^cite_note-159] In September 2023, Azerbaijan launched an [offensive](74857564.md) and retook control of Nagorno-Karabakh, ultimately causing the [expulsion of ethnic Armenians](74922032.md) from the region, who composed 99% of the remaining population.[^cite_note-160]

### Rail

During the rule of the Soviet Union, the [Yevlakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevlakh)–[Aghdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghdam)–[Stepanakert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stepanakert) railway line connected the Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Region with the main part of Azerbaijan. In 1993, After the Nagorno-Karabakh war and the abandonment of Aghdam, the line's service was cut back to service only between Yevlax and [Kətəlparaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C9%99t%C9%99lparaq), without any section in Nagorno-Karabakh. Services were restored in August 2025.[^cite_note-172] The former line between Kətəlparaq and Stepanakert has been almost completely destroyed.

In land controlled by the former Republic of Artsakh, the ([Tbilisi](42298.md)–[Gyumri](1529357.md)–)[Yerevan](34352.md)–[Nakhchivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhchivan_\(city\))–[Horadiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horadiz)–[Shirvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Eirvan,_Azerbaijan)(–[Baku](4566.md)) main railway was also dismantled between [Ordubad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordubad) and Horadiz, as well as a branch-line from [Mincivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mincivan,_Zangilan) to the Armenian city of [Kapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapan). Currently, the Azerbaijani trains only travel to Horadiz. The railway at the [Nakhchivan Autonomous Republic](385358.md) still operates, but it is separated from the main Azerbaijani lines, and only has a connection to [Iran](14653.md). Construction of a new Horadiz-Agbend line began in 2023, with the first section completed in September 2025.[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174]
