**Democratic Centre** (identified electorally as **Democratic Centre – Firm Hand, Big Heart**; [Spanish](26825.md): *Centro Democrático – Mano firme, corazón grande*) is a [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative)[^cite_note-15] and [right-wing populist](8354382.md)[^cite_note-nuso-5] political party in [Colombia](5222.md). It was founded in 2013 by [Álvaro Uribe](243680.md), former [President of Colombia](251302.md), former [Vice President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_Colombia) [Francisco Santos Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Santos_Calder%C3%B3n) and former [Minister of Finance and Public Credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_and_Public_Credit) [Óscar Iván Zuluaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93scar_Iv%C3%A1n_Zuluaga).[^cite_note-16] The party is [right-wing](56522.md)[^cite_note-semana.com-17] to [far-right](221220.md).[^cite_note-BBC-9][^cite_note-Alsema-10][^cite_note-lemonde-11]

## History

The party was born due to the estrangement between [Juan Manuel Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Manuel_Santos) and President [Álvaro Uribe](243680.md). Santos served as Minister of Defense during the Uribe administration. In 2005, Santos was one of the principal founders of the Social Party of National Unity (Party of the U), which was created by supporters of Álvaro Uribe and supported Uribe's re-election in 2006.[^cite_note-18]

However, after Santos won the [2010 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Colombian_presidential_election), Santos made multiple decisions which displeased Uribe, such as increasing diplomatic relations with neighbors like Venezuela and advocating for a more moderate political course.[^cite_note-19] Uribe was particularly critical of Santos's peace talks with the far-left guerrilla group [FARC](26100.md#Peace_talks) starting in 2011.[^cite_note-\:2-20][^cite_note-\:1-21] The Democratic Centre party was formally established on 20 January 2013 as a political vehicle for Uribe's supporters and became the main opposition force to Santos, particularly regarding the FARC peace process and Santos's 2014 re-election bid.[^cite_note-\:1-21]

During the [2020 United States elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_elections), Democratic Centre promoted [Republican Party](32070.md) candidates in the United States, especially in [Florida](18933066.md), sharing their support for the [President of the United States](24113.md), [Donald Trump](4848272.md). The party's involvement with promoting political candidates in a foreign election drew controversy among some observers.[^cite_note-\:0-22]

With President [Iván Duque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iv%C3%A1n_Duque)'s unpopularity rate reaching 70%, the government was faced with [the largest demonstrations in the country's history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932020_Colombian_protests) from 2019 to 2020. Demonstrators opposed the government's plans to make the labour market more flexible, weaken the public pension fund in favour of private entities, and raise the retirement age. Labor unions also protested against the tax reform, which aimed to reduce the taxes paid by companies, and against the [privatisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatisation) under way.[^cite_note-23] [Major protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Colombian_protests) also erupted in 2021. In 2022, the party declared it was in opposition to the left-wing presidency of [Gustavo Petro](13391962.md).[^cite_note-24]

The party was considered a regional partner of [European Conservatives and Reformists Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Conservatives_and_Reformists_Party) until 2022.

Although created as a decidedly right-wing party, its members now come from a wide range of political backgrounds, including former politicians of the right-wing [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Conservative_Party), the center-right [Social Party of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Party_of_National_Unity), and from the left-wing [Alternative Democratic Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_Democratic_Pole), such as Senator Everth Bustamante.[^cite_note-25]

The party is a strong advocate of [economic liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism). It intends to lower corporate taxation and further liberalise the economy in order to attract international investors, particularly to promote the exploitation of natural resources. It is also strongly focused on domestic security, including the elimination of terrorist threats, and promoting social cohesion.[^cite_note-\:2-20]

On international policy issues, the party is [pro-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-American).

## Electoral history

In the [2014 congressional elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Colombian_parliamentary_election), the Democratic Centre won 20 seats in the [Senate](645242.md) and 19 seats in the [Chamber of Representatives](645347.md).

In the [2014 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Colombian_presidential_election), the Democratic Centre chose [Óscar Iván Zuluaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93scar_Iv%C3%A1n_Zuluaga), the Senator and Finance Minister during the Uribe Administration, as its presidential nominee. Zuluaga won the first round, but subsequently lost the second round to incumbent President [Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Manuel_Santos).[^cite_note-26]

Despite the second round loss, the electoral performance in both congressional and presidential elections provided the Democratic Centre with a platform to establish itself as the major opposition party in Colombia. The party has since taken steps to improve its political infrastructure given that it lagged in funding and party organisation compared to more traditional political parties.
