**Fars Province**,[^cite_note-6] historically known as **Pars**, **Farsistan**, and **Persis**,[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-Sykes-11] is one of the 31 [provinces of Iran](448160.md). Its capital is the city of [Shiraz](39622.md).[^cite_note-Fars_Province_Structure-12]

Fars province has an area of 122,400 km<sup>2</sup> and is located in Iran's southwest, in [Region 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Iran).[^cite_note-13] It neighbours the provinces of [Bushehr](448037.md) to the west, [Hormozgan](448064.md) to the south, [Kerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerman_province) and [Yazd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazd_province) to the east, [Isfahan](448061.md) to the north, and [Kohgiluyeh and Boyer-Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohgiluyeh_and_Boyer-Ahmad_Province) to the northwest.

## Etymology

The [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) word *Fârs* (فارس), derived from the earlier form *Pârs* (پارس), which is in turn derived from *_Pârsâ_* ([𐎱𐎠𐎼𐎿](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%F0%90%8E%B1%F0%90%8E%A0%F0%90%8E%BC%F0%90%8E%BF)), the native [Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian) name for the region and the source of the [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) word *[Persis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persis)*. The [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) word *Persia* also originates from this region, ultimately through the [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) *Persis*.[^cite_note-14]

Pars is the historical homeland of the [Persian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-Iranica:_Fars-16] It was the homeland of the [Achaemenid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) and [Sasanian Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) dynasties of Iran, who reigned on the throne by the time of the ancient [Persian Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Empire). The ruins of the Achaemenid capitals [Pasargadae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasargadae) and [Persepolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis), among others, demonstrate the ancient history of the region. Due to the historical importance of this region, the entire country has historically been also referred to as *Persia* in the West.[^cite_note-Iranica:_Fars-16][^cite_note-17] Prior to [caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate) rule, this region was known as *Pars*.[^cite_note-18]

## History

### Persis

Main articles: [Persis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persis) and [Pars (Sasanian province)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pars_\(Sasanian_province\))



The ruins of [Persepolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis)



A Sassanid relief showing the investiture of [Ardashir I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardashir_I)



[Sarvestan Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarvestan_Palace) in [Sarvestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarvestan)

The ancient [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians) were present in the region from about the 10th century BC, and became the rulers of the largest empire the world had yet seen under the [Achaemenid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_dynasty) which was established in the mid 6th century BC, at its peak stretching from [Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrace)-[Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(ancient_kingdom\)), [Bulgaria](3415.md)-[Paeonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paeonia_\(kingdom\)) and [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) proper in the west, to the [Indus Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley) in its far east.[^cite_note-book-19] The ruins of [Persepolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis) and [Pasargadae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasargadae), two of the four capitals of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), are located in Pars.

The Achaemenid Empire was defeated by [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) in 333 BC, incorporating most of their vast empire. Shortly after this the [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire) was established. However, it never extended its power in Pars beyond the main trade routes, and by the reign of [Antiochus I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiochus_I) or possibly later Persis emerged as an independent state that minted its own coins.[^cite_note-20]

The Seleucid Empire was subsequently defeated by the [Parthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire) in 238 BC, but by 205 BC, the Seleucid king [Antiochus III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiochus_III) had extended his authority into Persis and it ceased to be an independent state.[^cite_note-21]

Babak was the ruler of a small town called Kheir. Babak's efforts in gaining local power at the time escaped the attention of [Artabanus IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artabanus_IV), the Parthian [Arsacid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire) Emperor of the time. Babak and his eldest son [Shapur I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapur_I) managed to expand their power over all of Persis.

The subsequent events are unclear. Following the death of Babak around 220, [Ardashir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardashir_I) who at the time was the governor of Darabgird, got involved in a power struggle of his own with his elder brother [Shapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapur_I).

At this point, Ardashir moved his capital further to the south of Persis and founded a capital at [Ardashir-Khwarrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardashir-Khwarrah) (formerly Gur, modern day [Firouzabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firouzabad)).[^cite_note-22] After establishing his rule over Persis, [Ardashir I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardashir_I) rapidly extended the territory of his [Sassanid Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid_Empire) Empire, demanding fealty from the local princes of Pars, and gaining control over the neighboring provinces of [Kerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerman_province), [Isfahan](448061.md), [Susiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susiana), and Mesene.

Artabanus marched a second time against Ardashir I in 224. Their armies clashed at [Hormizdegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormizdegan), where [Artabanus IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artabanus_IV) was killed. Ardashir was crowned in 226 at [Ctesiphon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ctesiphon) as the sole ruler of Persia, bringing the 400-year-old [Parthian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire) to an end, and starting the virtually equally long rule of the [Sassanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanian_Empire), over an even larger territory, once again making Persia a leading power in the known world, only this time along with its arch-rival and successor to Persia's earlier opponents (the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) and the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire)); the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire).

The Sassanids ruled for 425 years, until the [Muslim armies conquered the empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia). Afterwards, the Persians started to convert to [Islam](6037917.md), this making it much easier for the new Muslim empire to continue the expansion of Islam.

Persis then passed hand to hand through numerous [dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynasty), leaving behind numerous historical and ancient monuments; each of which has its own values as a world heritage, reflecting the history of the province, [Iran](14653.md), and [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia). The ruins of [Bishapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishapur), [Persepolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis), and [Firouzabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firouzabad) are all reminders of this. The Arab invaders brought about an end to centuries of Zoroastrian political and cultural dominance over the region; supplanted as the faith of the ruling class in the 7th century by Islam, which and over the next 200 years gradually expanded to include a majority of the population.

## Demographics

### Language and ethnicity

The main ethnic group in the province consists of indigenous [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people) (including [Larestani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lari_people_\(Iran\)) and the [Basseri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basseri)), while [Qashqai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qashqai_people), [Lurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lur_people), [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Arabs), [Kurds](17068.md), [Georgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Georgians), and [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians) constitute minorities.



Majority Ethnic groups in fars province

Due to the geographical characteristics of Fars and its proximity to the [Persian Gulf](24761.md), Fars has long been a residing area for various peoples and rulers of Iran. However, the tribes of Fars including, [Mamasani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamasani_\(tribe\)) [Lurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lurs), [Khamseh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khamseh) and Kohkiluyeh have kept their native and unique cultures and lifestyles which constitute part of the cultural heritage of [Iran](14653.md) attracting many [tourists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Iran). [Kurdish tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_tribes) include Uriad, [Zangana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zangana_\(tribe\)), [Chegini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chegini_\(tribe\)), [Kordshuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kordshuli_\(tribe\)) and [Kuruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuruni_\(tribe\)).[^cite_note-iranicaonline.org-23]

Among the hundreds of thousands of [Georgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Georgians) and [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians) that were transplanted to [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) under [Shah Abbas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Abbas_I), his predecessors, and successors, a certain amount of them were to guard the main caravan routes; many were settled around [Āspās](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asupas) and other villages along the old [Isfahan](223149.md)-[Shiraz](39622.md) road. By now the vast majority Caucasians that were settled in Pars have lost their cultural, linguistic, and religious identity, having mostly been assimilated into the population.[^cite_note-iranicaonline.org-23]

### Population

At the time of the 2006 National Census, the province's population was 4,220,721 people in 1,014,690 households.[^cite_note-2006_Fars_Province-24] The following census in 2011 counted 4,596,658 people in 1,250,135 households, of whom 67.6% were registered as urban dwellers (urban/suburbs), 32.1% villagers (small town/rural), and 0.3% nomad [tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe).[^cite_note-2011_Fars_Province-25] The 2016 census measured the population of the province as 4,851,274 people in 1,443,027 households.[^cite_note-2016_Fars_Province-2]

## Climate and wildlife

There are three distinct climatic regions in the Pars province. First, the mountainous area of the north and northwest with moderate cold winters and mild summers. Secondly, the central regions, with relatively rainy mild winters, and hot dry summers. The third region located in the south and southeast has cold winters with hot summers. The average temperature of Shiraz is 16.8 °C, ranging between 4.7 °C and 29.2 °C.[^cite_note-52]

The geographical and climatic variation of the province causes varieties of plants; consequently, variation of wildlife has been formed in the province. Additional to the native animals of the province, many kinds of birds migrate to the province every year.[^cite_note-Iran_fars-shiraz-53] Many kinds of ducks, storks and swallows migrate to this province in an annual parade. The main native animals of the province are gazelle, deer, mountain wild goat, ram, ewe and many kinds of birds. In the past, like in [Khuzestan Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khuzestan_Plain), the [Persian lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_lion) had occurred here.[^cite_note-Humphreys1999-54][^cite_note-Firouz05-55]

The province of Pars includes many protected wildlife zones. The most important protected zones are:

- Toot Siah (Black Berry) Hunt Forbidden Zone, which is located at the end of Boanat region.

- Basiran Hunt Forbidden Zone, which is located 4 kilometers south to Abadeh;

- [Bamu National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamu_National_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1), which is located north-east of Shiraz;

- Estahban Forest Park (Parke Jangaly), which is located on the outskirts of Touraj mountain;

- Hermoodlar Protected Zone, which is located east to [Larestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larestan).

## Economy

Agriculture is of great importance in Pars.[^cite_note-56] The major products include cereal (wheat and barley), citrus fruits, dates, sugar beets and cotton. Pars has major petrochemical facilities, along with an oil refinery, a factory for producing tires, a large electronics industry, and a sugar mill. [Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Iran) is also a large industry in the province. UNESCO has designated an area in the province, called [Arzhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Parishan) (known as *Dasht e Arjan*) as a biosphere reserve. Shiraz, provincial capital of Pars, is the namesake of [Shirazi wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirazi_wine). A large number of wine factories existed in the city.

## Transportation

[Shiraz Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_Airport) is the main international airport of the province and the second in the country. The cities of Jahrom, Lar and Lamerd also have airports linking them with Shiraz and Tehran and nearby [Persian Gulf](24761.md) countries such as the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE) and [Bahrain](18933277.md). Shiraz is along the main route from [Tehran](57654.md) to southern Iran.

## Higher education

The Pars province is home to many higher education institutes and universities. The main universities of the province include [Shiraz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_University), [Shiraz University of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_University_of_Arts), [Shiraz University of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_University_of_Medical_Sciences),[^cite_note-57] [Shiraz University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_University_of_Technology), [Salman Farsi University of Kazerun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salman_Farsi_University_of_Kazerun), [Jahrom University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahrom_University), [Jahrom University of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahrom_University_of_Medical_Sciences), [Fasa University of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasa_University_of_Medical_Sciences), [Islamic Azad University of Shiraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Azad_University_of_Shiraz), and [Islamic Azad University of Jahrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Azad_University_of_Jahrom).

The name of Fārs is undoubtedly attested in Assyrian sources since the third millennium B.C.E. under the form Parahše. Originally, it was the "land of horses" of the Sumerians (Herzfeld, pp. 181-82, 184-86). The name was adopted by Iranian tribes which established themselves there in the 9th century B.C.E. in the west and southwest of Urmia lake. The Parsua (Pārsa) are mentioned there for the first time in 843 B.C.E., during the reign of Salmanassar III, and then, after they migrated to the southeast (Boehmer, pp. 193-97), the name was transferred, between 690 and 640, to a region previously called Anšan (q.v.) in Elamite sources (Herzfeld, pp. 169-71, 178-79, 186). From that moment the name acquired the connotation of an ethnic region, the land of the Persians, and the Persians soon thereafter founded the vast Achaemenid empire. A never-ending confusion thus set in between a narrow, limited, geographical usage of the term—Persia in the sense of the land where the aforesaid Persian tribes had shaped the core of their power—and a broader, more general usage of the term to designate the much larger area affected by the political and cultural radiance of the Achaemenids. The confusion between the two senses of the word was continuous, fueled by the Greeks who used the name Persai to designate the entire empire. It lasted through the centuries of Arab domination, as Fārs, the term used by Muslims, was merely the Arabicized version of the initial name.
