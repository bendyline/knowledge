**Mopti** ([Fulfulde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_language): 𞤁𞤭𞥅𞤱𞤢𞤤 𞤃𞤮𞥅𞤩𞤼𞤭𞥅, transliterated *Diiwal Moobti*) is the fifth [administrative region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_region) of [Mali](19127.md), covering 26,550 km<sup>2</sup>. Its capital is the city of [Mopti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mopti). During the [2012 Northern Mali conflict](36856978.md), the frontier between Southern Mali which is controlled by the central government and the rebel-held North ran through Mopti Region.[^cite_note-3]

## Geography

Mopti Region borders [Tombouctou Region](1164276.md) to the north; the [Bandiagara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandiagara_Region) and [Douentza Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douentza_Region) to the east; [San Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Region) to the south; and the [Ségou Region](1382091.md) to the west.[^cite_note-4]

The population in the 2009 census was 2,037,330. The region contains a number of ethnic groups including [Fulani](102383.md), [Malinke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malinke_people), and [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_people).

The [Niger River](21437.md) crosses the region and is joined by the [Bani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bani_River), an important tributary, at the city of Mopti.

The region is separated into several areas: the [Inland Niger Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Niger_Delta) around Mopti, the [Bandiagara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandiagara) cliffs and the plain of [Bankass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bankass) along the Burkina Faso frontier. [Mount Hombori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hombori), the highest point in Mali at 1153 meters, is in the Mopti Region, near the [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hombori) of the same name.

In terms of its climate, Mopti Region is considered part of the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel).

The largest towns of the region are Mopti, [Sévaré](8445305.md) (which lies within Mopti Commune), [Djenné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djenn%C3%A9), and [Youwarou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youwarou).

## Transportation and economy



The [Great Mosque of Djenné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mosque_of_Djenn%C3%A9), an example of [Sudano-Sahelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudano-Sahelian_architecture) architecture

An airport at Mopti provides air service for the region, while the Niger River provides transportation to [Koulikoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koulikoro) and [Ségou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9gou) to the west and [Tombouctou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu) and [Gao](1255721.md) to the east.

The region is well-[irrigated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) and its agriculture is well-developed, with particularly successful fishing. Mopti serves as an important commercial crossroads between Mali's north, south and bordering nations. Tourism is also well-developed, notably in the cities of Djenné and Mopti (the former of which boasts the [Great Mosque of Djenné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mosque_of_Djenn%C3%A9), the largest mud structure in the world) and in [Dogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogon_people) country.

## History



A [copper-alloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_copper_alloys) bracelet from [Djenné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djenn%C3%A9), dating between the 11th-16th centuries

[^cite_note-5]

Though Mopti's location, once a [Bozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozo_people) village named Sanga, had long been inhabited, rapid expansion began under Seku Amadu's [Massina Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massina_Empire) around 1820. Expansion continued under the [Toucouleur Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toucouleur_Empire) of El Hadj [Umar Tall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umar_Tall) as well as the [French](5843419.md) [colonial](7299.md) administration.

## Culture

The region is a melting pot, made up of various ethnic groups, which live in harmony with one another. Common languages of the area include [Fulani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulani_language), [Dogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogon_language), [Songhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_language), [Bozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozo_languages), [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_language) and [Tamashek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamashek).

### World Heritage Sites

The city of [Djenné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djenn%C3%A9) in the region has been named a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) by [UNESCO](21786641.md) since 1988,[^cite_note-6] being one of four such sites in Mali. In 2009, Mali placed several prominent regional sites on its UNESCO tentative list for future World Heritage nomination, including the [Komoguel Mosque of Mopti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Mosque_of_Mopti) and the [Hamdullahi Historic City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamdullahi).[^cite_note-7] The [Bandiagara Escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandiagara_Escarpment) has also been inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1989[^cite_note-8] but now lies in the neighboring Bandiagara Region.[^cite_note-9]

## Demographics

The Mopti Region is the eighth most populated region in Mali, with a population of 935,579 in 2022.[^cite_note-2022census-12] With a total fertility rate at 5.1 births per woman, Mopti has a lower TFR than the Malian national average of 6.1 births per woman.[^cite_note-censustfr-13]

In 2022, the [Fula](102383.md), [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_people), and [Bozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozo_people) represented the three largest ethnic groups in the region. Mopti has the highest concentration of Fula and Bozo people in Mali. Minorities in Mopti include the [Songhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_people), [Dogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogon_people), [Soninke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soninke_people), [Tuareg](103054.md), [Mandinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandinka_people), and [Mossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mossi_people).[^cite_note-censusculture-14]

### Religion

The 2022 census found that 99.10% of the population in Mopti was [Muslim](19541.md), 0.84% was [Christian](18337522.md), 0.01% were adherents of [traditional faiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_traditional_religions), 0.01% practiced other religions, and 0.04% had [no religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non_religion).[^cite_note-censusculture-14]
