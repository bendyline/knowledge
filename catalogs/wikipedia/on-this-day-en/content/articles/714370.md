# State funeral

For the film, see [State Funeral (2019 film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Funeral_\(2019_film\)).



The coffin of [John Curtin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Curtin), [Prime Minister of Australia](24117.md) from 1941 to 1945, [lying in state](1505399.md) inside King's Hall, [Old Parliament House, Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Parliament_House,_Canberra), on July 6, 1945

A **state funeral** is a public [funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral) ceremony, observing the strict rules of [protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etiquette), held to honour people of national significance. State funerals usually include much pomp and ceremony as well as religious overtones and distinctive elements of military tradition. Generally, state funerals are held in order to involve the general public in a [national day of mourning](2111211.md) after the family of the deceased gives consent. A state funeral will often generate mass publicity from both national and global media outlets.

## History

State funerals already existed in [antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history). In ancient Athens, for example, fallen soldiers were regularly buried in a public ceremony.[^cite_note-1] In the Roman Empire, a state funeral (*funera publica*) could be instructed by the senate for the city of Rome, whereas city councils could instruct a communal state funeral.[^cite_note-2]

## By country

### Australia

Surveyor General Lieutenant Colonel [William Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Light) (1786–1839) received Australia's first, and South Australia's first, state funeral on October 10, 1839, in Adelaide.[^cite_note-3] Government offices were closed for the day of the funeral, the State's Governor and officials joined the funeral procession, and thousands attended.[^cite_note-4]

The second state funeral in Australia, the first held in Victoria, was in Melbourne on January 21, 1863, for explorers [Robert O'Hara Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_O'Hara_Burke) and [William John Wills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_John_Wills).[^cite_note-5]

### Bangladesh

There are a total of 12 people who have received state funerals. The first person to receive the state funeral of Bangladesh was national poet [Kazi Nazrul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazi_Nazrul_Islam) in 1976. The most recent person to receive a state funeral was former first Lady and former first female prime minister of Bangladesh, [Begum Khaleda Zia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begum_Khaleda_Zia) in 2025.[^cite_note-6]

### Canada

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [State funerals in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funerals_in_Canada).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=State_funerals_in_Canada\&action=edit)]

[State funerals in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funerals_in_Canada) are public events held to commemorate former [governors general](38152.md), [prime ministers](24135.md), other members of the [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Canada) who died in office, and, at the cabinet's discretion, other eminent Canadians. With ceremonial, military, and religious elements incorporated, state funerals are offered and executed by the governor general-[in-council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen-in-Council), who provides a dignified manner for the Canadian people to mourn a national public figure. Provincial and territorial governments may also perform state funerals for citizens in their particular jurisdictions. However, most state funerals are federal affairs.

As Canada shares the person of [its monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Canada) with the other [Commonwealth realms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm), funerals for Canada's sovereigns, as well as for their consorts, typically take place in the monarch's oldest and most populous realm, the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funerals_in_the_United_Kingdom). In Canada, a commemoration service is conducted by the federal crown-in-council and sometimes by provincial crowns, as well.

### Finland

Main article: [State funerals in Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funerals_in_Finland)



A [state funeral of President Martti Ahtisaari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funeral_of_Martti_Ahtisaari) on 10 November 2023

In Finland, state funerals continue only for [Finnish presidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Finland). In exceptional cases, the [Finnish Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Government) may grant or finance a funeral for a very long-serving [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Finland), [speaker of the parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Parliament_of_Finland), and persons of special national importance. This practice has been in force in Finland since 1989.[^cite_note-7]

### India

On 17 November 2012, [Bal Thackeray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bal_Thackeray) was accorded a state funeral in the city of Mumbai. On 10 October 2024, businessman [Ratan Tata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratan_Tata) was given a state funeral in [Mumbai](19189.md).[^cite_note-8] Former prime minister Dr. [Manmohan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manmohan_Singh) was given a state funeral on 28 December 2024.[^cite_note-9]

### Italy

In [Italy](14532.md), state funerals are granted by law to the current and former presidents of the constitutional entities, such as the [Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Italy), [Parliament](1417859.md), the Government, and the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_\(Italy\)), as well as to [ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Italy) who die while in office. State funerals can also be granted by decree of the Council of Ministers to individuals who provided exceptional service to the country, [citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_citizenship) who brought honor to the nation, or those who died in the line of duty or as victims of [terrorism](30636.md) or [organized crime](22625.md).[^cite_note-ItaSF-10]

The official protocol provides for:[^cite_note-ItaSF-10]

- the coffin surrounded by six members in high uniform of either the [Carabinieri](45173.md) or the same [Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_forces_of_Italy) the departed belonged to

- an [honor guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guard_of_honour) to the coffin at the entrance and the exit of the place in which the ceremony is held

- the presence of one representative of the [Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Italy)

- an official commemorative oration

- other honors that can be arranged by the [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Italy)

Public mourning, either national or local, is declared following the provisions of the prime minister's decrees. The [flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Italy) are flown at [half-mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-mast) outside public buildings, while inside they display two [black ribbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_ribbon#Sign_of_mourning), with the exceptions provided for military flags when required by [military protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_protocol).[^cite_note-ItaSF-10]

If the departed held a public office, the body can [lie in state](1505399.md) in the building of the office's institution. In other cases, the will of the family, the traditions of the office, or the local customs are followed.[^cite_note-ItaSF-10]

Outside the cases provided for by protocol, for example during [natural events](58921.md) that deeply impact the community, solemn funerals can be arranged and the six people who carry the coffins are members of the [Civil Protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protezione_Civile).[^cite_note-ItaSF-10]

### Thailand

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Thai royal funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_royal_funeral). See also: [Thai funeral § Government officials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_funeral#Government_officials).

[Thai royal funerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_royal_funeral) ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): พระราชพิธีพระบรมศพ; [RTGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Thai_General_System_of_Transcription): *Phra Ratcha Phithi Phra Borommasop*) are elaborate events, organised as royal ceremonies akin to state funerals. They are held for deceased members of the [royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_royal_family), and consist of numerous rituals which typically span several months to over a year. Featuring a mixture of Buddhist and [animist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animist) beliefs, as well as Hindu symbolism, these rituals include the initial rites that take place after death, a lengthy period of [lying-in-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lying-in-state), during which Buddhist ceremonies take place, and a final [cremation](175142.md) ceremony. For the highest-ranking royalty, the cremation ceremonies are grand public spectacles, featuring the pageantry of large funeral processions and ornate purpose-built funeral pyres or temporary crematoria known as *[merumat](#merumat)* or *[men](#men)*. The practices date to at least the 17th century, during the time of the [Ayutthaya Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayutthaya_Kingdom). Today, the cremation ceremonies are held in the royal field of [Sanam Luang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanam_Luang) in the historic centre of [Bangkok](56615.md).

### United Kingdom

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [State funerals in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funerals_in_the_United_Kingdom).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=State_funerals_in_the_United_Kingdom\&action=edit)]

In the [United Kingdom](31717.md), state funerals are usually reserved for [monarchs](47063.md). The most recent was the [state funeral of Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funeral_of_Queen_Elizabeth_II) on 19 September 2022.[^cite_note-11]

A state funeral may also be held to honour a highly distinguished figure following the approval of the monarch and [Parliament](13964.md) (of the expenditure of public funds).[^cite_note-State_funerals_in_the_United_Kingdom_parliament.uk-12] The last non-royal state funeral in the United Kingdom was that of Sir [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill) on [30 January 1965.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Winston_Churchill)[^cite_note-State_funerals_in_the_United_Kingdom_Croft2014-13]

Other funerals, including those of senior members of the [British royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_royal_family) and high-ranking public figures, may share many of the characteristics of a state funeral without being categorised as such; for these, the term "ceremonial funeral" is used. In the past fifty years, ceremonial funerals have been held for [Louis Mountbatten, 1st Earl Mountbatten of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Mountbatten,_1st_Earl_Mountbatten_of_Burma) (1979); [Diana, Princess of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana,_Princess_of_Wales) (1997); [Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_the_Queen_Mother) (2002); [Margaret Thatcher, Baroness Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher,_Baroness_Thatcher) (2013);[^cite_note-State_funerals_in_the_United_Kingdom_parliament.uk-12] and [Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Philip,_Duke_of_Edinburgh) (2021). Ceremonial funerals have tended in general to follow the ritual patterns of a state funeral (if on a somewhat smaller scale).

An exception was the funeral of [the Duke of Windsor (former King Edward VIII)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_VIII), in 1972. Although the exiled Duke opted for a private family event instead of a full state funeral, it had one distinctive feature of a sovereign's funeral: traditional recitation of Edward's titles as monarch by the Garter King of Arms.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

A [gun carriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_carriage) has been used to transport the [coffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffin) between locations since [Queen Victoria's funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria's_funeral) (1901); it is also accompanied by a procession of [military bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_band) and detachments along with mourners and other officials. There may also be a [lying in state](1505399.md) and other associated ceremonies.[^cite_note-State_funerals_in_the_United_Kingdom_Bland1986-16]

### United States

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [State funerals in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funerals_in_the_United_States).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=State_funerals_in_the_United_States\&action=edit)]

In the [United States](3434750.md), state funerals are the official funeral rites conducted by the [federal government](195149.md) in the nation's capital, [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), that are offered to a sitting or former [president](24113.md), a [president-elect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President-elect_of_the_United_States), high government officials and other civilians who have rendered distinguished service to the nation.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-State_funerals_in_the_United_States_State_Funeral_Traditions-18] Administered by the [Military District of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_District_of_Washington) (MDW), a command unit of the [Joint Force Headquarters National Capital Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Force_Headquarters_National_Capital_Region), state funerals are greatly influenced by [protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_\(diplomacy\)), steeped in [tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tradition), and rich in [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History). However, the overall planning as well as the decision to hold a state funeral, is largely determined by the family of the honoree, upon invitation by the government.[^cite_note-19]
