The **2026 Women's Africa Cup of Nations**, commonly referred to as **WAFCON 2026**, was the 14th edition (16th if editions of the tournament without hosts are included) of the [Women's Africa Cup of Nations](47015413.md), the biennial international [football](10568.md) championship organised by [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) for the women's national teams of [Africa](5334607.md).

The tournament was played in [Morocco](19291.md) for the third consecutive edition becoming the first country to achieve this feat.[^cite_note-\:0-2] Venues in [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca), [Fez](39760328.md) and [Rabat](57400.md) were used for the tournament.

Despite originally being planned to be a 12 team tournament, [CAF](715265.md) decided to expand the tournament to 16 teams after qualification ended.[^cite_note-3] Qualification took place between February and October 2025 to decide who qualified, while hosts [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco_women's_national_football_team) qualified automatically. [Cape Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde_women's_national_football_team) and [Malawi](32514505.md) both made their debuts.

The tournament also doubled as the African [qualifiers](62284023.md) for the [2027 FIFA Women's World Cup](60983910.md) in [Brazil](3383.md). The top four teams qualified for the World Cup, while two more teams advanced to the [inter-confederation play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup_qualification_\(inter-confederation_play-offs\)).

[Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_women's_national_football_team) were [the defending champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Women's_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_final), having beaten [Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco_women's_national_football_team) 3–2 in the 2024 final in [Rabat](57400.md). They failed to defend the title after losing 1–0 to eventual champions [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_women's_national_football_team) in the quarter-finals. Nigeria, who had their worst ever WAFCON after failing to make the semi-finals for the first time ever, also failed to qualify for the [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) for the first time in their history. Cameroon won their first title, and became only the fourth team to win the tournament, after beating debutants [Malawi](32514505.md) in the final.

## Host selection

-  [Morocco](19291.md) – Morocco expressed an interest in hosting the tournament after organizing the previous two tournaments in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Women's_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Women's_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).

-  [Tanzania](30118.md) – In November 2022, [Tanzania Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_Football_Federation) president, [Wallace Karia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Karia), stated they could host the tournament, with venues in [Mbeya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbeya), [Arusha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arusha), [Tanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanga,_Tanzania), [Tabora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabora), [Dodoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodoma) and [Mwanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwanza) being touted as possible venues.

  [^cite_note-4]

  &#x20;This would have been Tanzania's first time hosting and first time the event was held in [East Africa](260987.md).

On 17 October 2024, Morocco were confirmed as hosts for the third consecutive time,[^cite_note-5] becoming the first country to host three editions in a row and the third overall to host the tournament three times, alongside South Africa and Nigeria.

### Hosting problems

The South Africa Deputy Minister of Sports, Arts and Culture, Bertha Peace Mabe, announced on 1 February 2026 that they would host after Morocco withdrew as hosts.[^cite_note-6] However, the South Africa Minister of Sports, Arts and Culture, [Gayton McKenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gayton_McKenzie), refuted the claim two days later, stating that Peace Mabe had been misunderstood, that Morocco remained the host, and that South Africa had indicated a willingness to host if necessary.[^cite_note-7] On 13 February 2026, [CAF](715265.md) president, [Patrice Motsepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Motsepe), reaffirmed that Morocco were still the hosts due to the fact that the other nations interested in hosting wanted to change the dates, which the federation strongly opposed.[^cite_note-8] On 5 March 2026, the [CAF](715265.md) announced the tournament was postponed to 25 July – 16 August 2026.[^cite_note-9]

A total of 39 CAF member nations entered the competition. With hosts Morocco automatically qualified, the remaining 38 teams contested two qualifying rounds to decide the other 11 places in the final tournament.[^cite_note-10] [Chad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_women's_national_football_team), who were set to make their debut in WAFCON qualifying, withdrew before the first round due to financial difficulties. [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_women's_national_football_team) withdrew prior to the first leg of the first round due to a lack of competitions and preparation.[^cite_note-11]

In early October 2025, ahead of the [second and final round of qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Women's_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification#Second_round), reports emerged suggesting that the tournament would be expanded to 16 teams, though no details were provided regarding potential format changes.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] However, on 25 October, the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) (CAF) dismissed these claims, clarifying that the qualification process would determine 11 teams to join the hosts.[^cite_note-14] Subsequent reports indicated that CAF was nonetheless considering expansion.[^cite_note-15] On 3 November 2025, CAF officially confirmed the expansion from 12 to 16 teams. As the qualification phase for the tournament had already concluded, the four highest-ranked teams based on the [FIFA Women's World Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Women's_World_Ranking) of 7 August 2025, among those eliminated in the final qualifying round were selected to complete the list of 16 participating nations.[^cite_note-16]

Of the 16 qualified teams, 9 had taken part in the [2024 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Women's_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), while [Botswana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botswana_women's_national_football_team), [DR Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DR_Congo_women's_national_football_team) and [Tunisia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisia_women's_national_football_team) missed out, having qualified in 2024. [Cape Verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde_women's_national_football_team) and [Malawi](32514505.md) both made their first appearance in the final tournament.[^cite_note-17]

## Venues

During the draw on 15 January 2026, it was announced that two stadiums in [Rabat](57400.md) alongside [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca) and [Fez](39760328.md), were the three cities chosen to host the competition.[^cite_note-\:0-2] The [Fez Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fez_Stadium) and both stadiums in [Rabat](57400.md) hosted the recently concluded [2025 Africa Cup of Nations](57061770.md). The [Larbi Zaouli Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larbi_Zaouli_Stadium) in Casablanca was the only venue to have previous experience in hosting the women's edition, having been used in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Women's_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).

## Group stage

All times are local, [CET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Time) ([UTC+1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+01\:00)).

The top two teams of each group advanced to the quarter-finals.

### Tiebreakers

Teams were ranked according to [points](4960100.md) (3 points for a win, 1 point for a draw, 0 points for a loss).

If two teams were tied on points, the following tiebreaking criteria were applied, in the order given, to determine the rankings (Regulations Article 74):[^cite_note-regulations-23]

1. Points in head-to-head matches match between the two tied teams;

2. [Goal difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_difference) in all group matches;

3. Goals scored in all group matches;

4. Drawing of lots.

If more than two teams were tied, the following criteria were applied instead:

1. Points in matches among the tied teams;

2. Goal difference in matches among the tied teams;

3. Goals scored in matches among the tied teams;

4. If after applying all criteria above, two teams were still tied, the above criteria were applied only to matches played between the two teams in question. If this does not resolve the tie, the next three criteria were applied;

5. Goal difference in all group matches;

6. Goals scored in all group matches;

7. Drawing of lots.
