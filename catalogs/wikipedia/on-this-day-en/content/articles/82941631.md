**Anita Orbán** (born 3 July 1974) is a Hungarian politician and diplomat who has served as [deputy prime minister of Hungary](82534645.md) and [minister of foreign affairs](25144043.md) since 2026. In 2026, she was elected a member of the [National Assembly](585416.md) and became a leading foreign policy figure of the [Tisza Party](76606756.md).

## Early life and education

Orbán was born in [Berettyóújfalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beretty%C3%B3%C3%BAjfalu), where she lived until the age of 18.[^cite_note-\:0-1] She then moved to [Budapest](36787.md) to pursue her studies. In 1997, she graduated from [Corvinus University of Budapest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvinus_University_of_Budapest) (then University of Economic Sciences) with a bachelor's degree in economics and an [MBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Business_Administration), and began her career as a financial controller at Matáv, the predecessor of [Magyar Telekom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magyar_Telekom).

She later moved to the [United States](3434750.md) on scholarship with her then-husband, Krisztián Orbán, where she pursued advanced studies. She earned a master's degree in history from [Tufts University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufts_University) in 2001 and both a master's and PhD in international law and diplomacy from the [Fletcher School of Law and Diplomacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fletcher_School_of_Law_and_Diplomacy), becoming in 2007 the first Hungarian to receive a doctorate from the institution.[^cite_note-2]

Her academic work focused on the expansion of Russian and post-Soviet companies into European markets after 1990. She is the author of *Power, Energy and the New Russian Imperialism* (2008), which examines Russia’s geopolitical ambitions in the energy sector.[^cite_note-3] During her time in the U.S., she also worked as a teaching assistant in international relations.&#x20;

In the early 2000s, Orbán worked as an editor and later columnist for the Hungarian weekly *[Heti Válasz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heti_V%C3%A1lasz)*, covering foreign policy and public affairs.[^cite_note-4] During this period, she was associated with the foreign policy circle of [János Martonyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A1nos_Martonyi) within [Fidesz](404647.md) and frequently appeared at political and academic forums. She was critical of the foreign policy of Prime Minister [Ferenc Gyurcsány](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferenc_Gyurcs%C3%A1ny) (2004–2009), especially its perceived unpredictability, and strongly supported diversification of energy sources. She advocated for projects such as the [Nabucco gas pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabucco_gas_pipeline) and warned early on about Russia's potential use of energy as a geopolitical tool.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

## Political career

Ahead of the [2010 parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Hungarian_parliamentary_election), Orbán was nominated as a [Fidesz](404647.md) candidate for the [Budapest's 16th constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_16th_constituency), but withdrew before the vote, citing health reasons.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

Following the election, she joined Hungary's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, serving as [Ambassador-at-Large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador-at-large) for [Energy Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security) from 2010 to 2015.[^cite_note-5] In this role, she represented Hungary and the [Visegrád Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visegr%C3%A1d_Group) in international forums, including appearances before the [European Parliament](9581.md) and the [U.S. Congress](31756.md). She worked under foreign ministers such as [János Martonyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A1nos_Martonyi), [Tibor Navracsics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibor_Navracsics), and [Péter Szijjártó](26166814.md).

She also chaired energy cooperation initiatives in the Danube region and served as director of the Constellation Energy Institute, a [think tank](37101.md) promoting [Central and Eastern European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_and_Eastern_Europe) energy cooperation.[^cite_note-6]

Orbán left government service in 2015, amid a broader shift in Hungarian [energy policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_policy) toward closer cooperation with Russia.

## Business career

After leaving diplomacy, Orbán moved to [London](17867.md) and entered the [energy sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_industry). She worked as a senior advisor at [Cheniere Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheniere_Energy)'s London office and later as Vice President for International Affairs at [Tellurian Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tellurian_Inc.) (2017–2020).[^cite_note-7]

In 2021, she returned to Hungary as Deputy CEO for Corporate Affairs at [Vodafone Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone_Hungary).[^cite_note-8] Following the company's acquisition, she joined [Vodafone Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone_Group) in London as Director for Public Affairs and [ESG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental,_social,_and_governance).[^cite_note-9] She was also a board member and later chair at the Czech chemical company [Draslovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draslovka_Holding?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-10]

## Return to politics

On 24 January 2026, Orbán became the foreign affairs leader of the [Tisza Party](76606756.md) under [Péter Magyar](76453946.md). She was elected to the [National Assembly](585416.md) on the party's national list in the [2026 parliamentary election](79337461.md) and was appointed as Hungary's foreign minister on 12 May 2026.[^cite_note-11]

Her foreign policy platform emphasizes strengthening relations with neighboring countries, restoring ties within the [Visegrád Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visegr%C3%A1d_Group) (particularly [Poland](22936.md)), maintaining a constructive relationship with the [European Union](9317.md), and reducing Hungary's dependence on Russian energy.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

## Personal life

Orbán's former husband is Krisztián Orbán, an economist and founder of the Oriens [investment fund](30857467.md).[^cite_note-12] She has three children.

Orbán is a common Hungarian surname; as such, Orbán is unrelated to former Hungarian prime minister [Viktor Orbán](750582.md) or [Balázs Orbán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bal%C3%A1zs_Orb%C3%A1n_\(politician\)), another prominent [Fidesz](404647.md) politician.[^cite_note-\:0-1]
