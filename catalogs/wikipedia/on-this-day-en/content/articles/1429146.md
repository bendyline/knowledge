**Deir ez-Zor** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): دَيْرُ ٱلزَّوْرِ / دَيْرُ ٱلزُّور, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Dayru z-Zawr / Dayru z-Zūr*) is the largest city in eastern [Syria](7515849.md) and the [seventh largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Syria) in the country. Located on the banks of the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates) 450 km (280 mi) to the northeast of the capital [Damascus](8914.md), Deir ez-Zor is the capital of the [Deir ez-Zor Governorate](7445749.md).[^cite_note-tourism-3]

## Etymology

**Ed-Deir** is a common shorthand for Deir ez-Zor. In the [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language) language of the [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people) Christian population, Zeʿūrta ([ܙܥܘܪܬܐ](https://glosbe.com/arc/en/%DC%99%DC%A5%DC%98%DC%AA%DC%AC%DC%90)) means "little"; hence, *Dīrā Zeʿūrta* means "small habitation".[^cite_note-4] The current name, which has been extended to the surrounding region, indicates an ancient site for one of the [Early Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christianity) secluded Syriac monasteries established during the persecution of Christians in the Roman Empire and [Apostolic Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Age) throughout [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia).[^cite_note-5] Although **Deir** ([ܕܝܪܐ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%DC%95%DC%9D%DC%AA%DC%90)), which is Arabic (borrowed from Syriac) for "monastery", is believed to have been kept throughout the various [Medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) and [modern age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_age) renamings, **Zor**, which indicates the riverbank bush, appeared only in some late Ottoman records of the [Deir ez-Zor Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_Vilayet).[^cite_note-6]

Many different romanizations are used, including *Deir Ezzor*, *Deir Al-Zor*, *Deir-al-Zour*,[^cite_note-7] *Dayr Al-Zawr*, *Der Ezzor*, *Deir Azzor*, *Der Zor*, and *Deirazzor*.

### Ancient history

Further information: [Dura-Europos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dura-Europos); [Mari, Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mari,_Syria); and [Circesium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circesium)



From the discoveries of [Dura-Europos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dura-Europos) to the southeast of Deir ez-Zor

[Archaeological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological) findings in Deir ez-Zor indicate that the area has been inhabited since the ninth millennium BC. While the region was not densely populated, it was an urban area subordinate kingdoms like the Kingdom of [Mari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mari,_Syria), which rose in the third millennium BC.[^cite_note-\:2-8]

During the third millennium BC, the [Amorites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amorites) settled the area and established the kingdom of [Yamhad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamhad), one of whose urban centers was the city of Deir Ez-Zor (alongside [Mayadeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayadeen), Qars, and Tarka and its capital of [Aleppo](159244.md)). The city was not adversely affected during the succession of major empires (such as the [Akkadian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_Empire), [Old Assyrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Assyrian_Empire), [Babylonian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_Empire), [Hittite Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittite_Empire), [Middle Assyrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Assyrian_Empire) and [Neo-Assyrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Assyrian_Empire)), as Deir al-Zour was too small to be considered a threat and the region was incorporated into [Assyria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria) during the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age).[^cite_note-\:2-8]

In the third century BC, [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) crossed the region and built the city of [Dura-Europos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dura-Europos). Although influenced by [Greek culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_culture), the Semitic [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic) language remained prevalent in the city. When [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_\(region\)) came under the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) in 64 BC, Deir Ez-Zor was a small, marginal village known as [Azdra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azdra?action=edit\&redlink=1), which the Romans made the center of the region and founded a strong military garrison. Deir Ez-Zor came under the reign of Queen [Zenobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenobia) of Palmyra in the third century, within an autonomous federation of the Roman Empire.[^cite_note-9]

### Muslim conquest

Further information: [Iyad ibn Ghanm § Conquest of Upper Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iyad_ibn_Ghanm#Conquest_of_Upper_Mesopotamia)



The march of [Khalid ibn al-Walid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalid_ibn_al-Walid)'s army from [Iraq](7515928.md) to [the Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Levant)



The [al-Jazira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Mesopotamia) region (Upper Mesopotamia) in the 8th century. [Iyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iyad_ibn_Ghanm) played a leading role in the Muslim conquest of the region.

After the end of the [Ridda wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridda_wars) in the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md), [Abu Bakr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr) sent four armies to [the Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Levant), led by [Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazid_ibn_Abi_Sufyan), [Abu Ubaidah ibn al-Jarrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Ubaidah_ibn_al-Jarrah), [Amr ibn al-Aas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amr_ibn_al-Aas), and [Shurahbil ibn Hasana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shurahbil_ibn_Hasana). Because of the strength and size of the armies of the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), Abu Bakr ordered [Khalid ibn al-Walid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalid_ibn_al-Walid) to march with half of the Muslim Arab army to the Levant and command the armies there.

Khalid set off with his army towards Sham and opened [Bosra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosra) and then defeated the Byzantines at the [Battle of Ajnadayn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ajnadayn). After [Umar ibn Al-Khattab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umar_ibn_Al-Khattab) became [caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph) in 13 AH (634 AD), Khalid was replaced by [Abu Ubaidah ibn al-Jarrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Ubaidah_ibn_al-Jarrah). Abu Ubaidah was ordered to complete the [conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests). He took [Damascus](8914.md), [Baalbek](58207.md), [Homs](77533.md), [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama) and [Latakia](195594.md).

After the successive defeats of the [Byzantine army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_army), the [Byzantine Emperor Heraclius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Emperor_Heraclius) requested the help of the [Assyrian Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Christians) in [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia). They mobilized a large army and headed towards Homs, now the base of Abu Ubaidah in northern [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_\(region\)), which they besieged. Heraclius also sent soldiers from [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria). [Omar ibn al-Khattab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_ibn_al-Khattab) wrote to [Saad ibn Abi Waqqas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saad_ibn_Abi_Waqqas) to request support Abu Ubaidah with forces from Iraq, who were then organized under [Iyad ibn Ghanm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iyad_ibn_Ghanm). When the Byzantines who were besieging Homs heard about the army coming from Iraq, they withdrew from Homs. Saad ordered Iyad to invade [Upper Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Mesopotamia), which he conquered in 17 AH, including Deir Ez-Zor.

At the time, Deir Ez-Zor were adherents of Syriac Christianity and Judaism. There was a [Christian monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_monastery) in Monastery of the Hermits, which became Omari Mosque. Many of the town's indigenous Christians left.[^cite_note-10]

During the [Abbasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid) era, Deir Ez-Zor grew. The agriculture in the region prospered because of advances in irrigation. The small town, now called 'Deir Al-Rumman,' did not record any significant events during the decline of the Abbasid state and the ensuing [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk) period until its destruction by the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) in the thirteenth century.[^cite_note-\:2-8]

### Ottoman Era

Further information: [Ottoman Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria)



The extent of the Ottoman Empire in 1566, upon the death of [Suleiman the Magnificent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suleiman_the_Magnificent)

#### First Ottoman Era (1517–1864)



[Ibrahim Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Pasha_of_Egypt), the ruler of Syria (1831–1840)

The first [Ottoman](22278.md) era extended from the date the [Ottomans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria) entered Syria in 1517 until 1864, where the Ottomans found Deir Ez-Zor a small town on the upper [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates) and chose it as a center for their employees and settled in some of tribal [sheikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh) to protect the trade route between [Aleppo](159244.md) and [Baghdad](4492.md) and The tribe members began to visit it to communicate the men of power and buy their needs.[^cite_note-\:2-8]

Some Arab and European travellers visited it and described its construction, economy, and the nature of its inhabitants. According to the description, "Its houses are adjacent over an artificial hill, and its inhabitants are strong, polite, and welcome guests. Their crops were wheat, barley, cotton, and corn, along with orchards full of fruit species, including [palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_tree) trees, lemons, and oranges, the chess game is common among elders".[^cite_note-\:2-8]

Deir ez-Zor was repeatedly subjected to [Bedouin](61770.md) attacks. With the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) preoccupied with wars and its [sultans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultans_of_the_Ottoman_Empire) deemed corrupt, the people armed themselves with guns and organized a militia that resulted in a decline in attacks. However, because they had to rely on themselves the city failed to prosper.[^cite_note-\:2-8]

As security improved, commercial convoys started passing through the area, and Deir ez-Zor became a waystation for travelers. [Caravanserai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravanserai) were established and the road between [Aleppo](159244.md) and [Baghdad](4492.md) began to emerge from isolation. Local merchants traveled to [Hauran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauran) in early spring and returned in the fall. [Aleppo](159244.md), Baghdad, [Mardin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardin) and [Urfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urfa) were additional destinations.[^cite_note-\:2-8]

In 1831 Ibrahim Pasha took over Deir ez-Zor and annexed it to [Hama Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama_Sanjak) and appointed Maejun Agha governor of the city, [Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Army) rule remained until 1840 when the authority of the [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria) returned to the city, Perhaps the most prominent feature of Ibrahim Pasha's rule is the proliferation of weapons among the city's inhabitants, especially rifles, known as "Brahimiyat," which constituted a major tool to defend the city and repel [Bedouin](61770.md) attacks.[^cite_note-11]

#### Second Ottoman Era (1864–1918)

##### Zor Sanjak

Further information: [Zor Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zor_Sanjak)



Ottoman Syria until World War I. Present borders in grey.

On 2 January 1858, the [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria) government launched a military campaign under the command of [Omar Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Pasha) (Croatian) consisting of 500 soldiers to subdue the tribes in the Euphrates region. The campaign reached Deir Ez-Zor city and fought against the city's residents, where 16 Ottoman soldiers were killed. After the [Ottoman army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_army) subdued the city, Omar Pasha recruited 16 young men from the city to replace the Ottoman soldiers killed.[^cite_note-Ayyash_1989_152-12]

In 1864 the city revolted against Ottoman rule, and Soraya Pasha, the governor of Aleppo, sent a military force to suppress it. After the campaign, Soraya Pasha came to Deir ez-Zor. He made it the center of the district's headquarters ([Qaimakamiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaimaqam)), and he returned to Aleppo after appointing Omar Pasha governor, whose rule did not last more than 6 months. Khalil Bey Saqib was appointed as [Kaymakam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaymakam) of Deir ez-Zor after it was annexed to Aleppo.

During his reign, it established the government house (Dar Al Saraya), a military barracks, a hospital and some trade markets. Some of the arrivals from [Urfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urfa) settled in the city to help Khalil Bey Saqib with the administration, as well as starting campaigns to settle the Bedouin in urban centers on the Euphrates.[^cite_note-13]

In 1868, the [Qaimakamiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaimaqam) was transformed to the [Zor Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zor_Sanjak), which did not report to the [wali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wali) but reported directly to the [Grand Vizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Vizier) in [Istanbul](3391396.md). Its ruler ([Mutasarrıf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutasarr%C4%B1f)) was granted wide powers and its area was extended to include the city of [Raqqa](858965.md) and [Hasakah](7573335.md).[^cite_note-Ayyash_1989_152-12]

The rulers ([Mutasarrıfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutasarr%C4%B1f)) solidified security, especially during Arslan Pasha's reign, and were interested in organizing and planning the city, building schools and streets and established the first public park. They also built bridges on the Euphrates and some mosques and encouraged [afforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afforestation) and they used boats to cross the Euphrates. They reformed the tax system and introduced European uniforms into the city and did not generalize it.

The era of the [Zor Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zor_Sanjak) lasted 54 years, where 29 [Mutasarrıfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutasarr%C4%B1f) successively ruled it, the most recent being Hilmi Bey, who left the city with the Ottoman army on 6 November 1918. The continuous change of rulers (Mutasarrıfs) and lack of resources and disruption of conditions in the Ottoman Empire affected negatively on the urban, economic, cultural and social activity of the city. The outbreak of [World War I](4764461.md) in 1914 brought calamities, with many young people being recruited, famine and disease spread, livelihoods were confiscated, trade stopped and agriculture declined. But in the opinion of historian Abdul Qadir Ayyash, Deir ez-Zor owed its civilization to the Ottoman rulers despite their mistakes.[^cite_note-Ayyash_1989_152-12]

### Armenian genocide

Further information: [Deir ez-Zor camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_camps) and [Armenian genocide](140376.md)



The Armenian leader Papasian considers the last remnants of the murders at Deir ez-Zor in 1915–1916.

At the beginning of [World War I](4764461.md) in 1914, the Ottoman Empire began systematic campaigns to kill and displace [Armenians](387816.md). Beginning April 1915, this was carried out through massacres, forced deportations, and displacement, which were marches under harsh conditions designed to lead to the death of the deportees. Researchers estimate the number of Armenian victims as between 1 million and 1.5 million.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

Deir ez-Zor was the last destination of the forced displacement of Armenian convoys and the scene of killings and slaughter by the Turkish [gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie), where the [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria) authorities planned to exterminate Armenians. These plans failed because the people of Deir ez-Zor regretted what happened to the Armenian men, women, and children, prompting the mayor Haj [Fadel Al-Aboud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fadel_Al-Aboud) to help protect them and provide them with food, housing, safety and livelihoods.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

Despite Armenians coming to the region as part of death marches, the liberation that they achieved ultimately benefited the city, increasing population and growth rates. Historically, the city of Deir ez-Zor has been a special place for Armenians in [Syria](7515849.md) and the [Armenian Diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Diaspora). The [Armenian genocide Memorial Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Genocide_Memorial_Church,_Der_Zor), which was officially built in 1991, includes a museum containing some remains, collectibles and maps for memory of the martyrs who died in that area by the [Ottoman Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turks). The city later became a pilgrimage destination for hundreds of thousands of Armenians on 24 April each year, after being declared in 2002 by [Catholicos Aram I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicos_Aram_I) of the [Armenian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Orthodox) of [Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilicia) as a pilgrimage to the Armenians.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

### Post World War I

Further information: [Fadel Al-Aboud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fadel_Al-Aboud), [Haj Fadel Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haj_Fadel_Government), [Occupation of Zor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Zor), and [Arab Kingdom of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria)

#### First government of Haj Fadel



[Fadel Aboud Al-Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fadel_Aboud_Al-Hassan), Head of Deir ez-Zor Government in 1918



[King Faisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Faisal_I) in 1920

Trouble broke out in the city of Deir al-Zour after the [Ottomans](22278.md) left on 6 November 1918, where people began looting and stealing from each other across the area, so it was necessary to have a strong authority for protecting the city and its people and that led Al-Hassan who was the mayor to form his first government in the city and asking all tribal leaders in the villages and surrounding districts to support him and pledge allegiance to him. One of the priorities of this government was maintain the security and running the affairs of the city. This government later known as the "[Haj Fadel Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haj_Fadel_Government)".[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-Sabbagh_2017_34–35-22]

The government continued until the arrival of Sharif Nasser, the cousin of prince [Faisal Bin Al-Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_bin_Hussein), on 1 December 1918, and [Mar'i Pasha al-Mallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar'i_Pasha_al-Mallah) on 7 December 1918.[^cite_note-23]

#### British period

On 11 January 1919, the [British army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_army) occupied the city via the Iraqi border and annexed it to Iraqi territory. The British government took care of the security and cleanliness of the city and set up a primary school that started teaching English. [Fadel Al-Aboud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fadel_Al-Aboud) remained mayor, During this period, Fadel Al-Aboud and a number of leaders of the [Baggara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Baggara) tribe, [Agedat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Uqaydat) and other tribes represented the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates) region at the [Syrian National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_National_Congress) held in late June 1919 Which declared on 8 March 1920 the independence of Syria and establishment of [Arab Kingdom of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria) and the appointment of [Faisal Ibn Al-Sharif Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_I_of_Iraq) as King.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

The people of Deir ez-Zor sought to get rid of British rule and wrote their wish to the [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) government in [Damascus](8914.md), The Iraqi officers of the Iraqi "[Al-'Ahd Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-'Ahd_\(Iraq\))" in Damascus wanted to occupy Deir ez-Zor to make it a base to liberate Iraq from the [British occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_occupation_of_Iraq). So they appointed [Ramadan al-Shallash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan_al-Shallash) as governor of [Raqqa](858965.md) to be a step to liberate Deir ez-Zor, Officer Ramadan al-Shallash came and occupied Deir ez-Zor with the help of her people and "Albu Saraya" clan, and British troops withdrew on 27 December 1919 to the Iraqi border.[^cite_note-26]

#### Second government of Haj Fadel

On 27 December 1919, Ramadan al-Shallash took over the administration of Deir Ez-Zor as a military ruler, and his authority was nominal and the real ruling was to the city's notables, and they were not satisfied with his actions. Hence, they took him out of the city after two months.[^cite_note-27]

After the [Battle of Maysalun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Maysalun) on 24 July 1920 and occupation of Damascus by the French forces, the city of Deir ez-Zor was in a state of chaos and insecurity, which prompted Al-Hassan to form his second government, Which has done great services in protecting the city and maintaining the security of its people despite its limited capabilities. This government continued its work until 23 November 1920, when it was dissolved by a decision of the French occupation authorities.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-\:0-29]

King [Faisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_I_of_Iraq) left Syria for Hauran then [Haifa](55606.md) and from there to [Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Como,_Italy) in Italy then to London in October 1920 at the invitation of the [British royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_royal_family), Upon his departure, the monarchy in Syria ended and began the [French Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_mandate_of_Syria) era.

### French Mandate

Further information: [French mandate of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_mandate_of_Syria)



General [Yusuf al-Azma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_al-Azma)



General [Henri Gouraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Gouraud_\(general\))

In July 1920, French General [Henri Gouraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Gouraud_\(general\)) issued an ultimatum to the government of [King Faisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Faisal_I), known as the "Gouraud ultimatum", he set four days to accept it.

Although the Syrian government accepted the ultimatum and accepted the demands of General Gouraud to demobilize the Syrian army and withdraw the soldiers from the mounds of the village of [Majdal Anjar](23093567.md) in violation of the decision of the [Syrian National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_National_Congress), on 24 July 1920, French troops began to march on the orders of General Goubeier (By order of General Gouraud) towards Damascus, While the Syrian army stationed on the border was retreating, and when General Gouraud asked about this matter, replied that Faisal's message by accepting the ultimatum had reached him after the deadline.

On 24 July 1920, the [Battle of Maysalun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Maysalun) ended with the loss of the Syrian army and the death of the [Minister of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_War) [Yusuf al-'Azma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_al-'Azma), After its control over the entire Syrian territory, France resorted to the fragmentation of Syria into several independent states or entities:

- [State of Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Damascus) (1920).

- [State of Aleppo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Aleppo) (1920).

- [Alawite State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alawite_State) (1920).

- [The State of Greater Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Lebanon) (1920).

- [Jabal Druze State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Druze_State) (1921).

- [Sanjak of Alexandretta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak_of_Alexandretta) (1921).

The city of Deir ez-Zor, [Raqqa](858965.md) and [Al-Hasakah](7573335.md) were followed to [Aleppo](159244.md).

When the [French colonial forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_forces) entered Deir Ez-Zor on 9 November 1921, the region was locally ruled by Fadel Al-Aboud, a member of an aristocratic family; after a while, protests and demonstrations against occupation broke out, A group of French armored vehicles and dozens of soldiers encircled the house of Fadel Al-Aboud, where he was arrested and transferred to the military airport of Deir al-Zour and then transported by military aircraft to [Aleppo](159244.md), where he was imprisoned in the [castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleppo_Citadel) and during his imprisonment he met with the leader [Ibrahim Hanano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Hanano), In June 1922 he was released and returned to Deir Ez-Zor.[^cite_note-archive.org-30][^cite_note-\:0-29]

Later, Fadel Al-Aboud was sentenced to exile to the city of [Jisr al-Shughour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jisr_al-Shughour) after he was accused of preparing a revolt against [French colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonialism) in protest against the military campaign by the French army against the Bukhabur tribes that refused to pay taxes to the French colonizer, and insulting Wali Deir al-Zour Khalil Isaac, who was cooperating with the French.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-archive.org-30]

In June 1922, under the pressure of the Syrian people and the continued demonstration, Gouraud declared the creation of a Syrian federation on a federal basis between Damascus, Aleppo, and [Alawite state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alawite_state), provided that the Federation should have a president elected for one non-renewable year, The council of the Federation held its first meeting in June 1922 in the city of Aleppo and issued resolution No. / 1 / to form the Federal Government, [Subhi Barakat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subhi_Barakat), who is close to the French colonial authorities, was elected president of the federation.

### Independence



Flag of Independence in the Syrian Republic 1946



Former [Deir ez-Zor suspension bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_suspension_bridge) crossing the [Euphrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates)

The city was neglected during the reign of the [first Syrian republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Syrian_Republic) and illiteracy was rampant by 95%. However, some achievements belonged to that stage, such as building the suspension bridge and the establishment of the first bank in addition to the palace of justice, the national library, the city museum, and the municipal stadium; during this period, literary and cultural clubs increased, electricity existed, and cafes became widespread.

The city participated vigorously in the sixtieth strike in 1936 and saw a large march on 10 February 1936; this strike led to the signing of the independence agreement between Syria and France and the arrival of the national bloc to power after parliamentary elections held at the end of the year in which three deputies represented the city.[^cite_note-Ayyash_1989_157-46]

In 1941, the twenty-fifth government was formed in the modern history of Syria and the tenth in the era of the [first Syrian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Syrian_Republic). The first after [Taj al-Din al-Hassani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taj_al-Din_al-Hasani) became president, which gathered the various pillars of politics in Syria, during which the independence of Syria was proclaimed. The federal rule recognized the financial and administrative independence of [Lattakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattakia) and [Sweida](2590570.md). In this government, Mohammad Bey al-Ayesh took over [the Ministry of National Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy) to be the first minister from Deir ez-Zor and the Eastern Province. After his tenure, it dedicated the tradition of allocating a ministerial seat to a [bourgeoisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeoisie) in Deir Ez-Zor in successive [Syrian Governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Government). And in the same year (1941), British-led forces defeated the [Vichy French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_French) during the [Syria–Lebanon campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria%E2%80%93Lebanon_campaign), which included a battle over Deir ez-Zor. They handed administration of the region to the [Free French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_French).[^cite_note-Ayyash_1989_157-46][^cite_note-47]

The city has maintained its struggle and political role in addition to its civil activity even during the independence phase; in 1946, the wheat uprising against Governor Makram al-Atassi began due to the monopoly of the authority with the good wheat in the city, and the people succeeded in obtaining their rights.

In 1952 cotton cultivation was widespread, and automated pumping engines were introduced, which increased the area of arable land and cotton became the first crop of the city instead of wheat. The discovery of oil and salt during the reign of the second Syrian republic near the city helped to develop and expand urbanization and the increase the number of public and private companies that working in it, as well as increasing migration from the [countryside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countryside) towards it.[^cite_note-48]

### Civil war

#### Protests (2011–2012)

Further information: [Deir ez-Zor clashes (2011–14)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_clashes_\(2011%E2%80%9314\))





The destroyed city of Deir Ez-Zor in 2018.

Deir ez-Zor was one of the first cities that saw large demonstrations at the start of the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md). The demonstrations began in the city on 15 March 2011, which was the first day in the movement of protests demanding the overthrow of the Syrian government. On 15 April 2011, a large demonstration was launched from the city's stadium despite the using of live bullets by the [security forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Public_Security_Police) and the militias supporting it.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

In the demonstrations on Friday, 22 April 2011 (the sixth Friday in the history of the [Syrian revolution](30741795.md)), the statue of [Basil al-Assad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassel_al-Assad) was shot down, until then, the Syrian government had been cautious about the protests in Deir ez-Zor, because of their clan nature and the size of their area and the presence of quantities of weapons in it stored from the days of the Iraq war. When the demonstrators headed towards the statue of Basil al-Assad, the [riot police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Public_Security_Police) fired only in the air. It is said that the demonstrators were not shot and prevented from dropping the statue because the security commanders did not know or appreciate the reaction that the people of the city could issue if one of the demonstrators were killed.[^cite_note-51]

Syrian security forces took complete control of the city in August 2011, but the [Free Syrian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Syrian_Army) (associated with the National Coalition for Syrian Revolutionary and Opposition Forces) returned to take control in June 2012.

In recent years, Deir ez-Zor has remained a focal point in counterterrorism operations, with remnants of the Islamic State (IS) conducting insurgent attacks[^cite_note-52] in the region. Its strategic location near the Iraq-Syria border has made it a critical area for both Syrian government forces and international coalitions monitoring extremist movements.[^cite_note-53]

#### Partial ISIS takeover (2014–2017)

See also: [Siege of Deir ez-Zor (2014–2017)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Deir_ez-Zor_\(2014%E2%80%932017\)), [Eastern Syria campaign (September–December 2017)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Syria_campaign_\(September%E2%80%93December_2017\)), and [Deir ez-Zor campaign (2017–19)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_campaign_\(2017%E2%80%9319\))

By the beginning of 2014, [ISIS](9087364.md) announced the annexation of the city after ousting the [FSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Syrian_Army) and a detachment of [Syrian Armed Forces](27479.md) remained besieged in a small section of Deir ez-Zor.[^cite_note-54] The Deir ez-Zor district remained one of the few Syrian Government strongholds in eastern Syria for more than two years.

ISIS militants launched an [offensive in May 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmyra_offensive_\(May_2015\)), capturing [Palmyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadmur) and cutting off the remaining supply line to Deir ez-Zor.[^cite_note-siege-55] The city was then effectively under siege by ISIS, leaving supplies to be solely delivered by transport helicopters.[^cite_note-siege-55] ISIS militants attempted to stop the supplies for besieged Syrian soldiers by attacking the Deir ez-Zor Airbase daily. However, their attempts failed due to the presence of the elite [Republican Guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Guard_\(Syria\)) of the 104th Airborne Brigade led by Brigadier General [Issam Zahreddine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issam_Zahreddine).[^cite_note-siege-55]

From 10 April 2016 to 31 August 2017, the World Food Programme supplied the city with food and essential relief items through a high-altitude airdrop service. With a Russian contracted Il-76 aircraft and parachute systems provided by Canada, the US, and Russia, a total of 8015 pallets with an average weight of 754 kg were dropped into the besieged city of Deir ez-Zor. 309 flights were performed during the operational period.

#### Assad government control (2017–2024)

In early September 2017, the Syrian Arab Army, moving from [al-Sukhnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Sukhnah,_Syria), reached the stronghold and joined the besieged garrison. Shortly after that, [the siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Deir_ez-Zor_\(2014%E2%80%932017\)) of both the city as well as the [city's airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_Airport) were lifted.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] By 3 November 2017, the SAA had [fully recaptured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Deir_ez-Zor_\(September%E2%80%93November_2017\)) the city.[^cite_note-58] Concurrently with its operations to capture Deir ez-Zor, the Syrian Army launched a [campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Syria_campaign_\(September%E2%80%93December_2017\)) to secure the whole western bank of the Euphrates, which ended on 17 December 2017.

From 8 September 2017 to 23 March 2019, [a military operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_campaign_\(2017%E2%80%9319\)) east of the Euphrates River led by the [Syrian Democratic Forces](48205248.md) (SDF) and the [CJTF–OIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJTF%E2%80%93OIR) took place against the last bastion of the Islamic State in Syria. The campaign ended with a decisive victory for the SDF and its allies, and resulted in the capture of all of ISIL territory in the Deir ez-Zor Governorate after the [Battle of Baghuz Fawqani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Baghuz_Fawqani).

In December 2022, [oil workers were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Deir_ez-Zor_attack) by ISIS.[^cite_note-59] In late August 2023, multiple [skirmishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_clashes_\(2023\)) took place between SDF fighters and local Arab tribes which left dozens killed.[^cite_note-60] On 6 December 2024, pro-government forces withdrew from the city during the [Deir ez-Zor offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_offensive_\(2024\)).[^cite_note-61] Subsequently, the Syrian Democratic Forces captured the city.[^cite_note-62]

Following clashes between rebel forces under the [Military Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Operations_Command_\(Syria\)) and the SDF, the city ended up in the hands of rebel forces after the SDF withdrew on 11 December 2024.[^cite_note-63]

## Economy

The city and its rural surrounding is a fertile and prosperous farming area, with livestock-breeding (for [awassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awassi_\(sheep\)) sheep), cereals and cotton crops. Many [agribusiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agribusiness) institutions work there as well.

Since the discovery of [light crude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_crude) petroleum in the [Syrian desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_desert) it has become a centre for the country's [petroleum extraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_extraction) industry.[^cite_note-64] It is also a minor centre for tourism with many tourist facilities such as traditional French-style riverbank restaurants, up to 5-star hotels, a hub for trans-desert travel and an airport ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA) code: [DEZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_Airport)) in Al-Jafra suburb. There are [salt mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_mine) nearby.

## Culture



[Armenian genocide](140376.md) museum in Deir ez-Zor

The majority of Deiries (from Deir ez-Zor) are Arab Muslims, with a few [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_people) and [Assyrian/Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people) families.

Deir ez-Zor was the final concentration place for [Deir ez-Zor Camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_Camps) for annihilating the Armenian deportation caravans. Tens of thousands of surviving men, women and children were systematically killed on the banks of the [Euphrates River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates_River). The [Armenian genocide Memorial Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Genocide_Memorial_Church,_Der_Zor) commemorated the memory of Genocide victims who lost their lives, but it was destroyed on 21 September 2014 by ISIS.

Successive waves of new settlers from surrounding countrysides and provinces were heavily related to severe drought in late 1950s and 1990s most of them looking for standard jobs and giving up their farming and herding lifestyle. The [Mesopotamian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_Arabic) variety of Arabic is used in the city; a slight influence of the [Aleppo dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Arabic) can be noticed as well. Dominated by [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) [Muslims](19541.md), Christianity in Deir ez-Zor can be traced back to the [Apostolic Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Age), with few active churches and chapels belong to different congregations.

The city was also famous for the [Deir ez-Zor suspension bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_suspension_bridge) ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الجسر المعلق) which spanned the Euphrates[^cite_note-tourism-3] and was destroyed in 2013 during the civil war. The [Deir ez-Zor Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_Museum) keeps thousands of antiquities collected from nearby archaeological sites in Northern Mesopotamia.

Main campuses of *[Al-Furat University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Furat_University)* and *[Aljazeera University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aljazeera_University)* are also located there.[^cite_note-65] Many other polytechnic schools and professional institutes provide tertiary education are based in the city as well.

The local daily newspaper *Al Furat* and few other publications are published there and circulated in neighbouring [Al-Hasakah](7573335.md) and [Raqqa](858965.md) governorates.

## International relations

As of 2010, Deir ez-Zor is home to the third Armenian diplomatic mission in Syria; the Honorary Consulate of Armenia, opened on 11 February 2010.[^cite_note-66]

[Deir ez-Zor International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_International_Airport) is a domestic and international terminal and important hub mostly connecting with Damascus and destinations in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) region.
