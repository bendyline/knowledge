# List of roller coaster rankings



[The Smiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Smiler) is the roller coaster with the most [inversions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roller_coaster_inversion) in the world.

[Roller coasters](137471.md) are [amusement rides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amusement_ride) developed for [amusement parks](137327.md) and modern theme parks. Early iterations during the 16th and 17th centuries, popularized in Russia, involved wooden sleds that carried riders down large slides made from ice. The first roller coasters to attach a train to a wooden track appeared in France in the early 1800s.[^cite_note-1800s_roller_coasters-1] Although [wooden roller coasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wooden_roller_coaster) are still being produced, [steel roller coasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_roller_coaster) were introduced in the mid-20th-century and eventually became more common. They can be found on every continent except [Antarctica](18959138.md).[^cite_note-Census-2]

Amusement parks often compete to build the tallest, fastest, and longest rides to attract thrill seekers and boost park attendance.[^cite_note-Amusement_park_competition-3] Ranked by height, speed, length, and number of [inversions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roller_coaster_inversion), roller coasters often became the focal point for competing parks. Computer-simulated models led to innovations that produced more intense thrills while improving quality and durability.[^cite_note-Coaster_Wars-4] The debut of [Magnum XL-200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnum_XL-200) in 1989 at [Cedar Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Point) introduced the first complete-circuit roller coaster to exceed 200 feet (61 m) in height,[^cite_note-CP_opening_weekend-5][^cite_note-20th_anniversary_video-6] marking a pivot point in the industry and a new era sometimes referred to as the Coaster Wars.[^cite_note-Coaster_Wars-4] This period saw increasing competition as parks sought to be the latest to set a new world record, and some records were held for less than a year.[^cite_note-Coaster_War_Resurgence-7]

The pace of competition eventually slowed, however.[^cite_note-Coaster_Wars-4][^cite_note-Coaster_War_Resurgence-7] The now-defunct [Kingda Ka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingda_Ka), previously the tallest coaster in the world at 456 feet (139 m),[^cite_note-Kingda_Ka-8] held the height record from 2005 until its closure in 2024.[^cite_note-Coaster_Wars-4][^cite_note-Coaster_War_Resurgence-7] [Formula Rossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_Rossa), formerly the world's fastest at 149 mph (240 km/h), held the speed record for 15 years.[^cite_note-Formula_Rossa-9] [Steel Dragon 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Dragon_2000), with its track length of 8,133 feet (2,479 m), was the longest in the world for more than 25 years. These records were eventually surpassed by [Falcons Flight](78648389.md) in 2025, which opened as the tallest, fastest, and longest roller coaster in the world. Other notables include [The Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beast_\(roller_coaster\)), the world's longest wooden coaster since its opening in 1979, featuring a track length of 7,361 feet (2,244 m),[^cite_note-Beast-RCDB-10] and [The Smiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Smiler), which set a world record with fourteen inversions in 2013.[^cite_note-Smiler-11]
