The **England national rugby union team** represents England in international men's [rugby union](25405.md). They participate annually in the [Six Nations Championship](26547.md) with [France](955656.md), [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland_national_rugby_union_team), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_rugby_union_team), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_rugby_union_team) and [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_rugby_union_team). England have won the championship on 29 occasions (as well as sharing 10 victories), winning the [Grand Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_\(rugby_union\)) 14 times and the [Triple Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Crown_\(rugby_union\)) 26 times, making them the most successful outright winners in the tournament's history. They are currently the only team from the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere) to win the [Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_World_Cup), having won the tournament in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Rugby_World_Cup), and have been runners-up on three further occasions.

The team is run by the [Rugby Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_Football_Union) (RFU). Its history of the team extends back to 1871 when the England rugby team played their [first official test match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1871_Scotland_versus_England_rugby_union_match), losing 0–1 to Scotland. England dominated the early Home Nations Championship (now the Six Nations Championship) which started in 1883. Following the [schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_rugby_league#The_schism_in_England) of rugby football in 1895 into union and league, England did not win the Championship again until 1910. They first played against [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_national_rugby_union_team) in 1905, [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_rugby_union_team) in 1906, and [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_national_rugby_union_team) in 1909. England was one of the teams invited to take part in the inaugural Rugby World Cup in [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Rugby_World_Cup). They progressed to the final in the second tournament in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Rugby_World_Cup), but lost to the eventual champions Australia. Following their Grand Slam in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Six_Nations_Championship), England went on to win the 2003 Rugby World Cup, defeating Australia in extra time. They contested the final again in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Rugby_World_Cup) in defence of their title but lost to South Africa. They reached the final for the fourth time in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup), but lost to South Africa.

A number of countries have suffered their biggest-ever losses to England: [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_national_rugby_union_team), [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_national_rugby_union_team), [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_rugby_union_team), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_rugby_union_team) and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_national_rugby_union_team).

England players traditionally wear a white shirt with a rose embroidered on the chest, white shorts, and navy blue socks with a white trim. England's home ground is [Twickenham Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twickenham_Stadium) where they first played in 1910. The team is administered by the RFU. Five former players have been inducted into the [International Rugby Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Rugby_Hall_of_Fame). One of them is also a member of the [IRB Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRB_Hall_of_Fame). Seven other former players are members of the IRB Hall of Fame, four solely for their accomplishments as players, two solely for their achievements in other roles in the sport, and one for achievements both as a player and administrator. They have been branched in many different squads since the start of England Rugby, such as junior teams and [women's teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_women's_national_rugby_union_team).

## History

Main article: [History of the England national rugby union team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_England_national_rugby_union_team)

### Early years



The England team before playing in their [first international match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1871_Scotland_versus_England_rugby_union_match), versus [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_rugby_union_team) in Edinburgh, 1871

The expansion of rugby in the first half of the 19th century was driven by former pupils from many of England's [public schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_school_\(United_Kingdom\)), especially [Rugby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_School), who, upon finishing school, took the game with them to universities, to London, and to the counties.[^cite_note-OriginsoRugby-7] [England's first international match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1871_Scotland_versus_England_rugby_union_match) was against [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_rugby_union_team) on 27 March 1871; not only was this England's first match, but it is also noted as being the first rugby union international.[^cite_note-ShortHist-8] Scotland won the match by one goal and a try to England's one unconverted try,[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-Lewis-10] in front of a crowd of 4,000 people at [Raeburn Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raeburn_Place), Edinburgh.[^cite_note-Rugby1870s-11] A subsequent international took place at [the Oval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Oval) in London on 5 February 1872, when England defeated Scotland by a goal, a [drop goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drop_goal) and two tries to Scotland's one drop goal.[^cite_note-First3Games-12][^cite_note-5Feb1872-13] The early matches did not use a structured points system; this would not be introduced until after 1890 when a suitable format for the scoring system had been devised.[^cite_note-Lewis-10] Up until 1875, international rugby matches were decided by the number of goals scored (conversions and dropped goals), but from 1876 the number of tries scored could be used to decide a match if the teams were level on goals.[^cite_note-14]

In 1875, England played their first game against [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland_national_rugby_union_team) at the Oval, winning by one goal, one drop goal and one try to nil;[^cite_note-Lewis-10] this was Ireland's first test match.[^cite_note-15feb1875-15][^cite_note-IreFirstGames-16] England defeated Scotland in 1880 to become the first winners of the [Calcutta Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta_Cup).[^cite_note-28Feb1880-17][^cite_note-Timelines1880s-18] Their first match against [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_rugby_union_team) was played on 19 February 1881 at Richardson's Field in [Blackheath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackheath,_London),[^cite_note-Timelines1880s-18][^cite_note-19Feb1881-19] where England recorded their largest victory, winning by seven goals, six tries, and one drop goal to nil,[^cite_note-Lewis-10] and scoring 13 tries in the process.[^cite_note-19Feb1881-19] The subsequent meeting the following year at [St. Helen's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Helen's_Rugby_and_Cricket_Ground) in Swansea was a closer contest, with England defeating Wales by two goals and four tries to nil.[^cite_note-Lewis-10][^cite_note-16Dec1882-20] Two years later, England emerged as the inaugural winners at the first [Home Nations championship](26547.md).[^cite_note-SixNationsWinners-21] In 1889, they played their first match against a non-home nations team when they defeated the [New Zealand Natives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1888%E2%80%9389_New_Zealand_Native_football_team) at Rectory Field in Blackheath[^cite_note-NativeTour1888-22][^cite_note-NZNativesMatches-23] by one goal and four tries to nil.[^cite_note-Lewis-10] England shared the Home Nations trophy with Scotland in 1890.[^cite_note-RugbyHist6N-24]

England first played [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_national_rugby_union_team) (known as the "All Blacks") in 1905 at [Crystal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Palace_National_Sports_Centre) in London. New Zealand scored five tries, worth three points at the time, to win 15–0.[^cite_note-ABGame79-25] England played [France](955656.md) for the first time in March 1906 in Paris, winning 35–8, and later that year they first faced [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_rugby_union_team) (known as the "Springboks"), again at Crystal Palace. [James Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Peters_\(rugby\)) was withdrawn from the England squad when the South Africans refused to play against a black player;[^cite_note-Darkie_Peters-26] the match was drawn 3–3. England first played [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_national_rugby_union_team) (known as the "Wallabies") in January 1909 at Blackheath's Rectory Field, where they were defeated 9–3.[^cite_note-1900s-27]



Illustration by [Frank Gillett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Frank_Gillett) showing the England versus [The Original All Blacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Original_All_Blacks) Test attended by a then-record crowd of at least 50,000. The New Zealanders won 15–0.

The year 1909 saw the opening of [Twickenham Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twickenham_Stadium) as the RFU's new home, heralding a golden era for English rugby union. England's first international at Twickenham in 1910 brought them victory over Wales on their way to winning the [International Championship](26547.md) (known from then as the Five Nations) for the first time since [1892](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1892_Home_Nations_Championship). Although England did not retain the Five Nations title in 1911, they did share it (with Ireland) in 1912. England then achieved their first Five Nations [Grand Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Slam_\(rugby_union\)) in 1913, another in 1914, and a third in 1921 after the First World War. A further two consecutive Grand Slams followed for the England team in 1924 and 1925,[^cite_note-SNHiistory-28] this despite having started 1925 with an 17–11 loss to the "[Invincibles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Invincibles_\(rugby_union\))" in front of 60,000 fans at Twickenham.[^cite_note-Invincibles-29]

After winning a sixth Grand Slam in 1928, England were subjected to a [7–0 defeat by the Springboks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931%E2%80%9332_South_Africa_rugby_union_tour_of_Britain_and_Ireland) in front of 70,000 spectators at Twickenham in January 1932. Following the expulsion of France from the International Championship in 1931 due to professionalism, which reverted the Five Nations tournament back to the Home Nations,[^cite_note-SNHisBBC-30] England proceeded to win the 1934 and 1937 championships with a [Triple Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Crown_\(rugby_union\)), and achieved [their first victory over the All Blacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935%E2%80%9336_New_Zealand_rugby_union_tour_of_Britain,_Ireland_and_Canada) at Twickenham in January 1936.[^cite_note-ABGame311-31][^cite_note-Polanski38-19-32]

When the Five Nations resumed with the readmission of France in 1947 after the Second World War, England shared the championship with Wales. The early Five Nations competitions of the 1950s were unsuccessful for England, winning one match in the 1950 and 1951 championships.[^cite_note-SNHiistory-28] England won the 1953 Five Nations, and followed this up with a Grand Slam in 1957, and win in 1958. England broke France's four-championship streak by winning the 1963 Championship.[^cite_note-SNHiistory-28] After this victory, England played three Tests in the Southern Hemisphere and lost all three: 21–11 and 9–6 against the All Blacks, and 18–9 against Australia.[^cite_note-RugbyData22-33] England did not win a single match in 1966, and managed only a draw with Ireland. They did not win another Championship that decade.

[Don White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_White_\(footballer\)) was appointed as England's first coach in 1969. According to former Northampton player [Bob Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Taylor_\(rugby_union,_born_1942\)), "Don was chosen because he was the most forward-thinking coach in England".[^cite_note-GuardianObit-34] His first match in charge was an 11–8 victory over South Africa at Twickenham in 1969. Of the eleven games England played with White in charge they won three, and drew one and lost seven. He resigned as England coach in 1971.

England had wins against Southern Hemisphere teams in the 1970s; with victories over South Africa in 1972, New Zealand in 1973 and Australia in 1973 and 1976. The [1972 Five Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Five_Nations_Championship) was not completed due to [the Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles) in Northern Ireland when Scotland and Wales refused to play their Five Nations away fixtures in Ireland. England played in Dublin in 1973 and were given a standing ovation lasting five minutes. After losing 18–9 at [Lansdowne Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansdowne_Road), the England captain, [John Pullin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Pullin) famously stated, "We might not be very good but at least we turned up."[^cite_note-TurnedUp-35]

England started the following decade with a Grand Slam victory in the 1980 Five Nations – their first for 23 years.[^cite_note-1980GS-36] However in the [1983 Five Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Five_Nations_Championship), England failed to win a game and picked up the [wooden spoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wooden_spoon_\(award\)).[^cite_note-WoodenSpoon-37] In the first [Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Rugby_World_Cup) in New Zealand and Australia, England were grouped in [pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_stage) A alongside Australia, [Japan](759561.md) and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_national_rugby_union_team). England lost their first game 19–6 against Australia. They went on to defeat Japan and the United States, and met Wales in their quarter-final, losing the match 16–3.[^cite_note-1987RWC-38]

In 1989, England won matches against [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_rugby_union_team) and [Fiji](1074616.md), followed by victories in their first three Five Nations games of 1990. They lost to Scotland in their last game however, giving Scotland a Grand Slam. England recovered in the following year by winning their first Grand Slam since 1980. England hosted the [1991 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Rugby_World_Cup) and were in pool A, along with the All Blacks, [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_rugby_union_team) and the United States. Although they lost to the All Blacks in pool play, they qualified for a quarter-final going on to defeat France 19–10. England then defeated Scotland 9–6 to secure a place in the final against Australia which they lost 12–6.[^cite_note-1991RWC-39]

The next year, England completed another Grand Slam and did not lose that year, including a victory over the Springboks. In the lead up to the [1995 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Rugby_World_Cup) in South Africa, England completed another Grand Slam – their third in five years. In the World Cup, England defeated [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_national_rugby_union_team), Italy and [Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoa_national_rugby_union_team) in pool play and then defeated Australia 25–22 in their quarter-final. England's semi-final was dominated by the All Blacks and featured four tries, now worth five points each, by [Jonah Lomu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonah_Lomu); England lost 45–29.[^cite_note-ABGame992-40] They then lost the third-place play-off match against France.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-41]

### Professional era

England won their 20th Triple Crown title in 1997, but came second in the [1997 Five Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Five_Nations_Championship) after a narrow defeat to France at [Twickenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twickenham_Stadium). Sir [Clive Woodward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Woodward) replaced [Jack Rowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Rowell) as the England head coach later that year. On 6 December 1997, England drew with New Zealand at Twickenham, after being [heavily defeated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_South_Africa_rugby_union_tour_of_Europe) by [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_rugby_union_team) at the same venue the week before and by [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_New_Zealand_rugby_union_tour_of_Britain_and_Ireland) in Manchester two weeks previously. In 1998, [England toured Australia, New Zealand and South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_England_rugby_union_tour_of_Australasia_and_South_Africa); many of the experienced players were unavailable for what was to become nicknamed the "Tour from Hell" during which England lost all of their matches including a punishing defeat by the Wallabies.[^cite_note-Times_TFH-42][^cite_note-BBC_TFH-43] In the last Five Nations match on 11 April 1999, with England poised to win the championship, Welsh centre [Scott Gibbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Gibbs) sliced through six English tackles to score a try in the last minute, and the ensuing conversion by [Neil Jenkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Jenkins) handed the final [Five Nations title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Five_Nations_Championship) to Scotland.



Celebrations at [Trafalgar Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trafalgar_Square) after England's [2003 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Rugby_World_Cup) victory

England commenced the new millennium by winning the inaugural [Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Six_Nations_Championship), although they lost their last match to Scotland.[^cite_note-2000SN-44] They successfully defended their title [the following year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Six_Nations_Championship), but missed out on the Grand Slam by losing (14–20) to Ireland in a postponed match at Lansdowne Road.[^cite_note-2001SN-45] Although France won the [2002 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Six_Nations_Championship), England defeated the other Home Nations teams to win the Triple Crown.[^cite_note-2002SN-46] In 2002, England [beat Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_England_rugby_union_tour_of_Argentina) (26–18) in Buenos Aires,[^cite_note-Argentina2002-47] and in the [Autumn internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_end-of-year_rugby_union_internationals) they defeated New Zealand (31–28),[^cite_note-48] Australia (32–31),[^cite_note-Aust2002-49] and South Africa (53–3) at Twickenham.[^cite_note-SA2002-50] At the [2003 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Six_Nations_Championship), England won the Grand Slam for the first time since 1995, followed by wins over Australia and the All Blacks on their [June summer tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_England_rugby_union_tour_of_the_Southern_Hemisphere).

Going into the [2003 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Rugby_World_Cup) as one of the tournament favourites,[^cite_note-RWC03Favourites-51] England reached the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Rugby_World_Cup_final) on 22 November 2003 against host Australia. The game went into extra time with the score tied at 14–14, after one penalty apiece and with just seconds to spare, a match-winning drop goal by fly-half [Jonny Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonny_Wilkinson) brought the final score to 20–17, making England rugby world champions for the first time. Not only was this England's first Rugby World Cup victory, but it was the nation's first world title since the [England national football team](9904.md) won the [1966 FIFA World Cup](61629.md). On 8 December, the England team were greeted by 750,000 supporters on a victory parade through London before meeting Queen [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II) at [Buckingham Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckingham_Palace).[^cite_note-bigcrowd-52]



The England national squad training for the 2007 Rugby World Cup at the [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath)

England finished third in the [2004 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Six_Nations_Championship) after losing their matches to both France and Ireland.[^cite_note-2004SN-53] Clive Woodward resigned as head coach on 2 September and [Andy Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Robinson) was appointed to replace him.[^cite_note-WoodResign-54] Robinson's first [Six Nations campaign in 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Six_Nations_Championship) resulted in fourth place for England,[^cite_note-2005SNTables-55] and although they defeated Australia at Twickenham in the [Autumn internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_end-of-year_rugby_union_internationals),[^cite_note-AustEng051112-56] this was followed by a loss to the All Blacks.[^cite_note-ABEng051119-57]

A loss to South Africa in the [2006 Autumn internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_end-of-year_rugby_union_internationals)[^cite_note-58] was England's eighth defeat in nine test matches, their worst losing streak. Andy Robinson resigned as head coach after this run, and attack coach [Brian Ashton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Ashton_\(rugby_union\)) was appointed as his replacement in December.[^cite_note-AshtonCoach-59] England started the [2007 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Six_Nations_Championship) with a Calcutta Cup victory over Scotland.[^cite_note-EngSco1-07-60] That year's championship included a historic match at [Croke Park](322713.md) which England lost (13–43), their heaviest defeat against Ireland.[^cite_note-RecordIreLoss-61]

At the [2007 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Rugby_World_Cup), England were grouped in Pool A with Samoa, [Tonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonga_national_rugby_union_team), South Africa, and the United States. They progressed to the knockout stage despite a 36–0 loss to South Africa, and narrowly defeated Australia in the quarter-finals. England then faced hosts France in the semi-finals and triumphed (14–9) to qualify for the final, where they were subjected to a second defeat by the Springboks at this World Cup, losing the match 15–6. England followed up their World Cup disappointment with two consecutive second-place finishes in the Six Nations Championship, behind Wales ([2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Six_Nations_Championship)) and Ireland ([2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Six_Nations_Championship)). Former England team captain [Martin Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Johnson_\(rugby_union\)) took up the job of head coach in July 2008 but, unable to replicate his on-field success in the management role, he resigned in November 2011 following a miserable [Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Rugby_World_Cup) which featured a series of on- and off-field controversies and ended in quarter-final defeat by France.

In March 2012, the [Rugby Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_Football_Union) appointed [Stuart Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Lancaster_\(rugby_union\)), the former Elite Rugby Director at [Leeds Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_Tykes), as England's head coach.[^cite_note-62] He had previously been employed in the position on a short-term basis, assisted by existing forwards coach [Graham Rowntree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Rowntree), and [Andy Farrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Farrell). Lancaster was considered a success in his first campaign as head coach: defending champions England took second place in the [2012 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Six_Nations_Championship) after losing to Wales at Twickenham, but successfully defended the Calcutta Cup by defeating Scotland at [Murrayfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrayfield). England finished the year on a high when they beat World Cup holders New Zealand at Twickenham in the [Autumn internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_end-of-year_rugby_union_internationals); the England team dominated the match and completely outplayed the All Blacks, who had been unbeaten in 20 matches.[^cite_note-63]

At the [2013 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Six_Nations_Championship), England again finished in second place behind Wales, and were deprived of the opportunity to win the Grand Slam for the first time since 2003 when they were defeated 30–3 by Wales in Cardiff. It was the first time since 1974 that every team in the Six Nations managed to win at least three competition points (the equivalent of a win and a draw, or three draws). However, England retained the Calcutta Cup by defeating Scotland at Twickenham. Lancaster took an experimental side on a [summer tour of Argentina in 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_England_rugby_union_tour_of_Argentina); after beating a [South American XV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_XV) on 2 June, England achieved a 2–0 series victory over Argentina, their first away series win against *the Pumas* for 32 years.[^cite_note-64]

In 2015, England hosted the [Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Rugby_World_Cup), but were eliminated in the pool stage.[^cite_note-65] Despite this setback, and following the appointment of new head coach [Eddie Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Jones_\(rugby_union\)), England won the Grand Slam in the [2016 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Six_Nations_Championship), and remained unbeaten for the whole of 2016, including a series whitewash of Australia in Sydney. They went on to equal the world record of 18 consecutive test wins with a 61–21 victory over Scotland in securing the [2017 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Six_Nations_Championship).

2018 began well for England, seeing off a spirited challenge from Italy, and winning a tight contest against Wales in the first two rounds of the [Six Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Six_Nations_Championship). However, it was not until June before England recorded another win, as the team lost their remaining games against Scotland, France and eventual Grand Slam winners Ireland at home in Twickenham. On their [summer tour of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_England_rugby_union_tour_of_South_Africa), England lost the first two matches, before winning the third test against a mostly second-string Springbok side. That autumn, after adding former New Zealand and United States coach [John Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mitchell_\(rugby_union\)) to the coaching setup, England won the return match against South Africa by a single point, and lost an equally close contest with New Zealand, both in controversial circumstances.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] England rounded out the year with wins over Japan and Australia. The win over Australia continued an unbroken run of victories over the Wallabies under former Australia coach Eddie Jones.

England finished second in the [2019 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Six_Nations_Championship). They beat Ireland, France, Italy but lost to Wales and drew 38–38 with Scotland after leading 31–0. In the [2019 Rugby World Cup warm-up matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup_warm-up_matches) they defeated Wales at Twickenham before losing in Cardiff. They then recorded their largest win over Ireland with a 57–15 victory at Twickenham before defeating Italy at [St James' Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_James'_Park). In the [2019 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup), England became the first team to qualify for the quarter-finals following a win over Argentina in [Chōfu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%8Dfu). After their final match was cancelled due to [Typhoon Hagibis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Hagibis), England topped [Pool C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup_Pool_C) and faced Australia in the quarter-finals. England won the quarter-final, recording a seventh consecutive victory over Australia which resulted in the Wallabies largest ever Rugby World Cup defeat. England then defeated New Zealand in the semi-final, equalling New Zealands largest Rugby World Cup defeat with a victory in which the All Blacks were kept scoreless for 57 minutes. On 2 November 2019, England were defeated (12–32) by South Africa in the [2019 Rugby World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup_final).

In the [2020 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Six_Nations_Championship), England were defeated in their opening game against France in Paris before recording victories against Scotland at Murrayfield and Ireland and Wales at Twickenham. The tournament was then halted due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and resumed with the matches played in empty stadiums in October. In the postponed matches, England recorded a bonus point win over Italy in Rome before France defeated Ireland by eight points which meant that England won the championship on points difference. Due to the pandemic, the scheduled 2020 Autumn Internationals were replaced by the [Autumn Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn_Nations_Cup). England defeated Georgia, Wales and Ireland before facing France in the final match, winning after extra time at Twickenham to secure the tournament.

England finished fifth in the [2021 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Six_Nations_Championship), beating France and Italy but losing to all three home nations for the first time since 1976 and conceding defeat to Scotland at Twickenham for the first time since 1983. In the [2021 Autumn Internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_end-of-year_rugby_union_internationals), England played three matches at Twickenham. They secured a victory over Tonga, recorded their 8th consecutive victory over Australia (32–15) and also beat the current World Champions South Africa (27–26).

England finished third in the [2022 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Six_Nations_Championship) after defeating Italy and Wales but lost Ireland, France and Scotland. In the [2022 tour of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_England_rugby_union_tour_of_Australia), England faced the Wallabies in a three-test series. They lost the first test match in Perth, but won the last two test matches in Brisbane, and in Sydney to win the test series 2–1. In the [2022 Autumn Internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_end-of-year_rugby_union_internationals) they lost to Argentina, beat Japan, drew with New Zealand, and lost to South Africa.

England finished fourth in the [2023 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Six_Nations_Championship). England defeated Italy and Wales but was beaten by Scotland, France and Ireland. At the [2023 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rugby_World_Cup), England beat Argentina, Japan, Chile, and Samoa to go undefeated in the pool stage. In the quarter-final, they defeated Fiji before losing to the eventual champions South Africa in the semi-final. They ended their campaign with a win over Argentina in the bronze final to finish the tournament in third place.

England finished third in the [2024 Six Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Six_Nations_Championship). They defeated Italy, Wales and Ireland, but lost to Scotland and France. In the [2024 English tour of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_England_rugby_union_tour_of_New_Zealand), England faced the All Blacks in a two-test series. They were defeated in both test matches in Dunedin and in Auckland. In the [2024 Autumn Internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_end-of-year_rugby_union_internationals), England lost to New Zealand, Australia, South Africa, but won against Japan.

## Home stadium

Main article: [Twickenham Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twickenham_Stadium)



Aerial view of Twickenham Stadium



Twickenham before a match in November 2012

Twickenham is the largest dedicated rugby stadium in the world. In the early years, the English rugby team used a number of venues in several different locations around England before settling at Twickenham Stadium in 1910. After sell-out matches at [Crystal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Palace_National_Sports_Centre) against New Zealand in 1905 and South Africa in 1906, the [Rugby Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_Football_Union) (RFU) decided to invest in their own ground and arranged for sportsman and entrepreneur [Billy Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Williams_\(cricketer,_born_1861\)) to find a home ground for English Rugby. The land for the ground was purchased in 1907 for [£](270673.md)5,572 12[s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shilling) and 6[d](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_penny_sterling), and construction began the following year.[^cite_note-Twickenhamhist-68]

The first international test match at Twickenham took place on the 15th of January 1910 between England and Wales. The home team won (11–6), beating Wales for the first time since 1898.[^cite_note-FirstTest-69] The stadium was expanded in 1927 and again in 1932, but there were no further upgrades until 1981 when a new South stand was built[^cite_note-70] and the 1990s when new North, East and West stands were built;[^cite_note-Twickenhamhist-68] the South stand was replaced in 2005 and 2006 to make the stadium into a complete bowl. England played their first test match at the redeveloped Twickenham on the 5th of November 2006 against the All Blacks,[^cite_note-CeleMatch-71] losing the test match (20–41), in front of a record crowd of 82,076.[^cite_note-RuthlessABs-72]

The pitch at Twickenham was replaced in June 2012 with a hybrid 'Desso' type, which uses artificial fibres entwined with real grass. This type of pitch surface is particularly hard wearing in wet conditions.[^cite_note-73]

### England home matches outside Twickenham

Although England have played home matches almost exclusively at Twickenham since 1910, they have occasionally used alternative English venues. England home matches have been hosted at Leicester's [Welford Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welford_Road_Stadium) (1923), London's [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_\(1923\)) (1992), [Old Trafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Trafford) (1997 and 2009), Huddersfield's [McAlpine Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McAlpine_Stadium) (twice in 1998),[^cite_note-Huddersfield-74] Manchester's [Etihad Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Manchester_Stadium) (2015), and [St James' Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_James'_Park) in Newcastle upon Tyne (2019).

### "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot"

Main article: [Swing Low, Sweet Chariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_Low,_Sweet_Chariot)

"Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" is commonly sung at England fixtures, especially at Twickenham. In the last match of the 1988 season, against Ireland, three of England's tries were scored by [Chris Oti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Oti). A group of boys from the Benedictine school [Douai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douai_School), following a tradition at their school games, sang the song on his final try, and other spectators around the ground joined in.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-SwingLow2-87] Since then "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" became a song to sing at England home games.[^cite_note-88] In 1991, the RFU marketing director Mike Coley wanted the team to launch a song leading up to that year's Rugby World Cup. He had wanted to use "[Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/And_did_those_feet_in_ancient_time)", but it was used in the [Rugby League Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990%E2%80%9391_Challenge_Cup) final that year, so the song was changed at short notice to "Swing Low". There were a number of versions recorded and the version released did reach the top 40 in the [UK Singles Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Singles_Chart) during the competition.[^cite_note-89]

## Playing kit

Wikimedia Commons logo

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [England national rugby union team kits](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/England%20national%20rugby%20union%20team%20kits).



[Tom Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Palmer_\(rugby_union\)) wearing the traditional white England shirt at the [2011 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Rugby_World_Cup)

England rugby union players typically wear all-white jerseys and white shorts, with predominantly navy blue socks. The emblem on the jerseys is a red [rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose). As of May 2025, the strip is manufactured by [Castore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castore) and the shirt sponsor is [O<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O2_\(brand\)).[^cite_note-90] The [away strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Away_colours) is usually red or dark grey (described as "anthracite");[^cite_note-91] prior to the introduction of the grey strip, red was the traditional change colour. Navy blue has also been used in the past and was reintroduced for the 2016–17 and 2020–21 seasons.[^cite_note-92] Purple was used during the 2009 autumn internationals, reflecting the traditional colour of the original England [tracksuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracksuit) from the 1960s until the 1980s.[^cite_note-93] The away strip was black for the first time during the [2011 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Rugby_World_Cup). About that kit, the RFU stated that they had requested approval from the New Zealand Union, which said "it has no qualms with England wearing the strip".[^cite_note-94]



The red rose has been the emblem of England since the first international v Scotland in 1871

The [Rugby Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_Football_Union) (RFU) had created the national side's emblem prior to an English team being sent to [Edinburgh](9602.md) to play a Scottish side. A red rose was chosen to be the side's emblem.[^cite_note-rose-95] The white kit worn by the national team was taken from the kit used at [Rugby School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_School).[^cite_note-rose-95] Alfred Wright, an employee of the Rugby Football Union, is credited with the standardisation and new design of the rose, which up until 1920 had undergone many variations in its depiction.[^cite_note-rose-95] The Wright design is thought to have been used without minor alteration until the late 1990s.[^cite_note-rose-95] It was not until 1997 that the rose was modernised,[^cite_note-96] when [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) became the official strip supplier (with the stem section of the rose being green rather than brown as previously).[^cite_note-97]

In 2003, England first used a skin-tight strip. This was intended to make it more difficult for the opposition to grasp the shirt when tackling.[^cite_note-TightStrip-98]

## Performances

### Six Nations

England competes annually in the [Six Nations Championship](26547.md), which is played against five other European nations: [France](955656.md), [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland_national_rugby_union_team), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_rugby_union_team), [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_rugby_union_team), and [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_rugby_union_team). The Six Nations started out as the Home Nations Championship in 1883 which England won with a [Triple Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple_Crown_\(rugby_union\)). England have won the title outright 29 times (a record for the tournament) and shared victory ten times. Their longest wait between championships was 18 years (1892–1910). During the Six Nations, England also contests the [Calcutta Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcutta_Cup) with Scotland (which England first won in 1880) and the [Millennium Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Trophy) with Ireland (which England first won in 1988). The matches between England and France are traditionally known as "[Le Crunch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Crunch)".

England have contested every [Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_World_Cup) since the tournament began in [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Rugby_World_Cup), reaching the final four times and winning the title in the [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Rugby_World_Cup) tournament.

In the [inaugural tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Rugby_World_Cup), England finished second in their pool before losing to Wales (3–16) in the quarter-finals. They again finished pool runners-up in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Rugby_World_Cup) but bounced back to beat France (19–10) in the quarter-finals, and then Scotland (9–6) in the semi-finals, en route to a (6–12) defeat to Australia in the final at Twickenham on 2 November 1991.

In [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Rugby_World_Cup), England topped their pool and defeated Australia (25–22) at the quarter-final stage before being beaten by the All Blacks (29–45) in the semi-finals. They lost their bronze medal match (9–19) to France.

In the [1999 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Rugby_World_Cup), England again finished second in their pool. Although they proceeded to win a play-off game against Fiji (45–24), they went out of the tournament in the quarter-finals, losing (21–44) to South Africa.

England topped their pool in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Rugby_World_Cup) and progressed to the playoffs. They beat Wales (28–17) and France (24–7) in the quarter and semi-finals. With a drop goal in the last minute of extra time, England won the final (20–17) against Australia in Sydney on 22 November 2003.

England made a poor start to their title defence in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Rugby_World_Cup), with a below par victory over the United States and a heavy (0–36) defeat to South Africa, leaving the title holders on the brink of elimination at the pool stage. Improved performances against Samoa and Tonga ensured that England again reached the knockout stage as pool runners-up. They defeated Australia (12–10) in the quarter-finals, and beat France (14–9) in the semi-finals. The victory over France gave England the chance to play in their 2nd consecutive final. In the final, held in Paris on 20 October, England lost (6–15) to South Africa, their second defeat by the Springboks during the 2007 tournament.

England topped their pool in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Rugby_World_Cup). They reached the quarter-finals but were defeated (12–19) to France.

In [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Rugby_World_Cup), England became the first host nation to fail to qualify for the knockout stage, after losing to Wales and Australia in the pool stage.

In [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup), England topped their pool. They defeated Australia (40–19) and New Zealand (19–7) in the knockout stage. On 2 November 2019, they suffered a (12–32) defeat to South Africa in the final in Yokohama, becoming World Cup runners-up for the third time in their history.

In [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rugby_World_Cup), England topped their pool. They defeated Fiji in the quarter-finals (30–24) before losing to the eventual champions South Africa (15–16) in the semi-finals. They beat Argentina in the bronze medal match (26–23) to finish the tournament in 3rd place.

England's [Jonny Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonny_Wilkinson) is the highest points scorer in the Rugby World Cup, having scored 277 points between 1999 and 2011. England have the fourth most points and the fourth most tries scored in the Rugby World Cup.

When the [World Rugby Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Rugby_Rankings) were first introduced in early September 2003, England were ranked 1st; they fell to 2nd for a week in November 2003 before regaining 1st place. They again dropped to 2nd in the rankings, and then to 3rd, from mid-June 2004. Following the [2005 Six Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Six_Nations_Championship), they fell to 6th in the world rankings, where they remained until moving up to 5th place in December of that year. After a decline in form in 2006, England finished the year ranked 7th; however, they bounced back to 3rd in 2007 due to their good run in that year's [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Rugby_World_Cup), where they finished runners-up.

Their ranking slipped again in 2008 and during the [2009 Six Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Six_Nations_Championship) they dropped to their lowest ranking of 8th, where they remained for the duration of the [2009 autumn internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_end-of-year_rugby_union_internationals). After a brief resurgence that saw them rise to a ranking of 4th in the world, England again slipped following a poor showing at the [2011 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Rugby_World_Cup) and were ranked 6th in February 2012. The team entered the [2015 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Rugby_World_Cup) ranked 4th, but after failing to progress beyond the pool stage, England again sank to 8th in the world in November 2015. In March 2016, after securing the Grand Slam in the [2016 Six Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Six_Nations_Championship), England rose to second place, where they remained the following year after winning the [2017 Six Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Six_Nations_Championship). A poor fifth-place performance in the [2018 Six Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Six_Nations_Championship) saw them fall to sixth place.

In October 2019, England defeated [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_national_rugby_union_team) in the semi-final of the [2019 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rugby_World_Cup) to top the World Rugby Rankings for the first time since 2004.[^cite_note-110] After losing the final to [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_rugby_union_team), England were ranked third. In November 2020, they regained second place following New Zealand's loss to [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_national_rugby_union_team).

During the [2021 Six Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Six_Nations_Championship), a fifth-place finish saw England fall from second to third after defeats to [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_rugby_union_team), [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_rugby_union_team) and [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ireland_national_rugby_union_team). After [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_national_rugby_union_team) won 5 matches in a row during the [2021 Rugby Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Rugby_Championship), England fell to fourth until defeats of Australia and South Africa in the [2021 Autumn Nations Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_end-of-year_rugby_union_internationals) saw them regain third place.

During the [2022 Six Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Six_Nations_Championship), England again suffered three defeats to Scotland, Ireland and [France](955656.md) and fell from third to fifth.

England entered the [2023 Six Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Six_Nations_Championship) in fifth, but defeats to Scotland, France and Ireland again saw them fall to sixth.

During the [2023 World Cup warm-ups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rugby_World_Cup_warm-up_matches), England suffered further defeats away to Ireland and Wales and a first ever home loss to [Fiji](1074616.md) to enter the 2023 World Cup in eighth. A third-placed finish at the [2023 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Rugby_World_Cup) saw England rise to fifth.

During the [2024 Six Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Six_Nations_Championship), England retained fifth position despite away defeats against Scotland and France.

### Notable players

See also: [List of England national rugby union players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_England_national_rugby_union_players)

Five former England representatives have been inducted into the [International Rugby Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Rugby_Hall_of_Fame) – [Bill Beaumont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Beaumont), [Martin Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Johnson_\(rugby_union\)), [Jason Leonard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Leonard), [Wavell Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavell_Wakefield) and [Jonny Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonny_Wilkinson).[^cite_note-IRHFList-113][^cite_note-IRHF2007-114]

Seven former England internationals are also members of the [IRB Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRB_Hall_of_Fame). Four of them – Johnson,[^cite_note-IRB_Hall_October_2011-115] [Alan Rotherham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Rotherham), [Harry Vassall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Vassall)[^cite_note-Rotherham-Vassall-116] and [Robert Seddon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Seddon)[^cite_note-2013_IRB_HOF-117] – were inducted for their accomplishments as players. Two other former England players, [John Kendall-Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kendall-Carpenter) and [Clive Woodward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clive_Woodward), were inducted into the IRB Hall for non-playing accomplishments in the sport.[^cite_note-IRB_Hall_October_2011-115] Another former England player, [Alfred St. George Hamersley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_St._George_Hamersley), was inducted for achievements as both a player and a rugby administrator.[^cite_note-Hamersley-118]

### Club versus country

Although the England team is governed by the [Rugby Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_Football_Union) (RFU), players have been contracted to their clubs since the advent of professionalism in late 1995. Since then, players have often been caught in a "power struggle" between their clubs and the RFU; this is commonly referred to as a "club versus country" conflict.[^cite_note-Tuck195-212] The first major dispute between England's top clubs (who play in the [English Premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Premiership_\(rugby_union\))) and the RFU occurred in 1998, when some of the clubs refused to release players to tour Australia, New Zealand and South Africa.[^cite_note-NonRelease98-213] The tour became known as the "Tour from hell" after an England squad of second-string players were defeated in all four Tests, including a 76–0 defeat by Australia.[^cite_note-Burnt-214] The clubs also withdrew from the [1998–99 Heineken Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_Heineken_Cup).[^cite_note-Morgan56-215]

In 2001, the top clubs and the RFU formed "England Rugby" to help govern the club and international game. The parties agreed to restrict the number of matches at club and international level that elite players (a group of 50 or 60 players selected by the RFU) could play in order to reduce player burnout and injuries.[^cite_note-EnglandRugby2001-216] In return for releasing players from club commitments, the clubs were to receive compensation from the RFU. This agreement was considered central to the England victory in the [2003 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Rugby_World_Cup). Clive Woodward, England coach from November 1997, resigned in 2004 because he was unable to get the access to the players that he wanted: "I wanted more from the union – more training days with the players, more influence over the way they were treated – and ended up with less."[^cite_note-Woodwardquits-217] [Andy Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Robinson), Woodward's successor, blamed the lack of control over players for his team's unsuccessful record.[^cite_note-AndyInterview-218] [Brian Ashton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Ashton_\(rugby_union\)), who took over from Robinson, intentionally named his playing squad for Six Nations matches in 2007 early in the hope that their clubs would not play them in the weekend prior to a Test.[^cite_note-SquadsEarly-219] The RFU and the Premiership clubs are negotiating a similar deal to the one in 2001 that will enable international players to be released into the England squad prior to international matches.[^cite_note-NewDeal-220]

### Training

[Pennyhill Park Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennyhill_Park_Hotel) in [Bagshot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagshot), [Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey), is the chosen training base for the team in the [2015 Rugby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Rugby_World_Cup). [Loughborough University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loughborough_University), [Bisham Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisham_Abbey) and the [University of Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bath) grounds served as training bases prior to this agreement. [Martin Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Johnson_\(rugby_union\)) noted the hotel's facilities and its proximity to Twickenham and Heathrow as deciding factors in this decision.[^cite_note-221] The team had their own pitchside gym and fitness rooms constructed on the hotel premises at the start of the long-term arrangement. Since its completion in 2010 the team also regularly use [Surrey Sports Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey_Sports_Park) at the [University of Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Surrey) in nearby [Guildford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guildford) for much of their training.[^cite_note-222]

## Media coverage

England's [summer internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-year_rugby_union_internationals) are televised live on [Sky Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Sports),[^cite_note-Summer_test_TV_rights-223] while their [autumn internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn_rugby_union_internationals) are televised live by [TNT Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_Sports_\(United_Kingdom\)).[^cite_note-Autumn_test_TV_rights-224] Highlights of the autumn tests are currently not shown by a free-to-air broadcaster. As of 2024, all Six Nations games are broadcast free-to-air on the BBC and [ITV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Sport).[^cite_note-Six_Nations_TV_rights-225]
