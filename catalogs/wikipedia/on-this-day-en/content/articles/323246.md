A **goldsmith** is a [metalworker](266443.md) who specializes in working with [gold](12240.md) and other [precious metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precious_metal). Modern goldsmiths mainly specialize in jewelry-making but historically, they have also made [silverware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutlery), [platters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platter_\(dishware\)), [goblets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goblet), decorative and serviceable utensils, and [ceremonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial) or [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious) items.

Goldsmiths must be skilled in forming metal through [filing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File_\(tool\)), [soldering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazing), [sawing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saw), [forging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forging), [casting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casting_\(metalworking\)), and [polishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polishing). The trade has very often included [jewelry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewelry)-making skills, as well as the very similar skills of the [silversmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silversmith). Traditionally, these skills had been passed along through [apprenticeships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apprenticeship); more recently jewelry arts schools, specializing in teaching goldsmithing and a multitude of skills falling under the jewelry arts umbrella, are available. Many universities and junior colleges also offer goldsmithing, silversmithing, and metal arts fabrication as a part of their fine arts curriculum.

## Gold

Compared to other metals, gold is [malleable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malleable), [ductile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ductile), rare, and it is the only solid metallic [element](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_element) with a [yellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow) [color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color). It may easily be melted, fused, and cast without the problems of oxides and gas that are problematic with other metals such as [bronzes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze), for example. It is fairly easy to "pressure weld", wherein, similarly to clay, two small pieces may be pounded together to make one larger piece. Gold is classified as a [noble metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noble_metal)—because it does not react with most elements. It usually is found in its [native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_metal) form, lasting indefinitely without oxidization and tarnishing.

## History



A goldsmith workshop during the mid-seventeenth century

Gold has been worked by humans in all [cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture) where the metal is available, either indigenously or imported, and the history of these activities is extensive. Superbly made objects from the ancient cultures of [Africa](5334607.md), [Asia](689.md), [Europe](9239.md), [India](14533.md), [North America](21139.md), [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica), and [South America](26769.md) grace museums and collections throughout the world. The [Copper Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_Age) [Varna culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna_culture) ([Bulgaria](3415.md)) from the 5th millennium BC is credited with the earliest found instances of gold metallurgy.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] The associated [Varna Necropolis treasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna_Necropolis) contains the oldest golden [jewellery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewellery) in the world with an approximate age of over 6,000 years.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]



[Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztecs) father teaches his son to cast gold

Some pieces date back thousands of years and were made using many techniques that still are used by modern goldsmiths. Techniques developed by some of those goldsmiths achieved a skill level that was lost and remained beyond the skills of those who followed, even to modern times.[^cite_note-5] Researchers attempting to uncover the chemical techniques used by ancient artisans have remarked that their findings confirm that "the high level of competence reached by the artists and craftsmen of these ancient periods who produced objects of an artistic quality that could not be bettered in ancient times and has not yet been reached in modern ones."[^cite_note-6]

In [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) Europe goldsmiths were organized into [craft guilds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craft_guilds) and usually were one of the most important and wealthiest of the guilds in a city. The guild kept records of members and the marks they used on their products. These records, when they survive, are very useful to historians. Goldsmiths often acted as [bankers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banker), since they dealt in gold and had sufficient security for the safe storage of valuable items, though they were usually restrained from lending at interest, which was regarded as [usury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usury). In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), goldsmithing normally included silversmithing as well, but the brass workers and workers in other [base metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_metal) normally were members of a separate guild, since the trades were not allowed to overlap. Many [jewelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeweler) also were goldsmiths.



A [Brahmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmin) goldsmith from [Goa](40010153.md), 16th century India

The [Sunar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunar) caste is one of the oldest communities in goldsmithing in India, whose superb gold artworks were displayed at [The Great Exhibition of 1851](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Exhibition_of_1851) in London. In India, '[Daivadnya Brahmins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daivadnya_Brahmin?action=edit\&redlink=1)', Vishwakarma (Viswabrahmins, Acharis) '[Sunar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunar)' are the goldsmith castes.

The [printmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printmaking) technique of [engraving](60125.md) developed among goldsmiths in Germany around 1430, who had long used the technique on their metal pieces. The notable [engravers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engraver) of the fifteenth century were either goldsmiths, such as [Master E. S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_E._S.), or the sons of goldsmiths, such as [Martin Schongauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Schongauer) and [Albrecht Dürer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer).

## Contemporary goldsmithing



Modern goldsmith at work

A goldsmith might have a wide array of skills and knowledge at their disposal. [Gold](12240.md), being the most [malleable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malleable) metal, offers unique opportunities for the worker. Modern goldsmiths may frequently use a wide variety of other metals, especially [platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum) alloys. 24 [karat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karat_\(purity\)) gold is pure and was historically known as [fine gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_gold).[^cite_note-7]

Because it is so soft, however, 24 karat gold is rarely used. It is usually [alloyed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloy) to make it stronger and to create different colors. Depending on the metals used to create the alloy, the color can change.

The goldsmith will use a variety of tools and machinery, including the [rolling mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_mill), the [drawplate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drawplate), and perhaps, [swage blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swage_block) and other forming tools to make the metal into shapes needed to build the intended piece. Then parts are fabricated through a wide variety of processes and assembled by [soldering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazing), which requires high skill level to master. A fine goldsmith can to a tolerance approaching that of precision machinery, but largely using only his eyes and hand tools. Quite often the goldsmith's job involves the making of mountings for [gemstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemstone), in which case they often are referred to as *jewelers*.

'Jeweller', however, is a term mostly reserved for a person who deals in jewellery (buys and sells) and not to be confused with a goldsmith, silversmith, gemologist, diamond cutter, and diamond setters. A 'jobbing jeweller' is the term for a jeweller who undertakes a small basic amount of jewellery repair and alteration.
