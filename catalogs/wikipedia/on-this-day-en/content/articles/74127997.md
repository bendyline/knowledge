The **2025–26 UEFA Champions League** was the 71st season of Europe's premier club [football](10568.md) tournament organised by [UEFA](32332.md), and the 34th season since it was rebranded from the [European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Champion_Clubs'_Cup) to the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md).

[The final](76987191.md) was played on 30 May 2026 at [Puskás Aréna](27530864.md) in [Budapest](36787.md), Hungary.[^cite_note-3] Defending champion [Paris Saint-Germain](357488.md) successfully defended the title, defeating [Arsenal](2174.md) 4–3 on [penalties](511302.md) after a 1–1 draw, to become the second club to win back-to-back titles in the UEFA Champions League era, matching [Real Madrid](26413.md)'s feat in the [2016–17 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_UEFA_Champions_League).[^cite_note-Reuters2026Final-4][^cite_note-5]

As winners of the Champions League, Paris Saint-Germain automatically qualified for the [2026–27 UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%E2%80%9327_UEFA_Champions_League) league phase, the [2026 FIFA Intercontinental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_Intercontinental_Cup) final, the [2029 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2029_FIFA_Club_World_Cup) group stage, and earned the right to play against [Aston Villa](42173.md), the winners of the [2025–26 UEFA Europa League](78224540.md) in the [2026 UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_UEFA_Super_Cup).

This was the first Champions League season to feature six clubs from one nation, with [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association) earning an extra spot through [UEFA coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_coefficient) ranking and [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) having won the previous season's [UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Europa_League).[^cite_note-6]

## Association team allocation

A total of 82 teams from 53 of the 55 [UEFA](32332.md) member associations participated in the 2025–26 UEFA Champions League (the exceptions being [Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein_Football_Association) which did not organise a domestic league and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Football_Union) which was suspended). The association ranking based on the [UEFA association coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_coefficient#Men's_association_coefficient) was used to determine the number of participating teams for each association:[^cite_note-regulations-7]

- Associations 1–5 each had four teams.

- Association 6 had three teams.

- Associations 7–15 each had two teams.

- Associations 16–55 (except [Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein_Football_Association)

  [\[Note LIE\]](#cnote_Note_LIE)

  &#x20;and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Football_Union)

  [\[Note RUS\]](#cnote_Note_RUS)

  ) each had one team.

- The winners of the [2024–25 UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Champions_League) and [2024–25 UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Europa_League) were each given an additional entry if they did not qualify for the 2025–26 UEFA Champions League through their domestic league.

- The two associations with the best [coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_coefficient#Men's_association_coefficient) in the 2024–25 season each had one European Performance Spot into the league phase. The winners of the UEFA Champions League and Europa League could not fill the European Performance Spots.

### Association ranking

For the 2025–26 UEFA Champions League, the associations were allocated places according to their 2024 [UEFA association coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_coefficient#Men's_association_coefficient), which took into account their performance in European competitions from 2019–20 to 2023–24.[^cite_note-country_coefficients-8] The table reflects Russia's ongoing suspension from UEFA.

Apart from the allocation based on the association coefficients, associations could have additional teams participating in the Champions League, as noted below:

- (EPS) – European Performance Spot, the additional berths for associations who finished in the top 2 of the [2024–25 association coefficients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_coefficient#Men's_association_coefficient)

- (TH) – Additional berth for UEFA Champions League title holders

- (EL) – Additional berth for UEFA Europa League title holders

### Teams

The labels in the parentheses show how each team qualified for the place of its starting round:

- TH: Champions League title holders

- EL: Europa League title holders

- 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, etc.: League positions of the previous season

- EPS: European Performance Spots – the additional berths given to clubs from the two associations with the highest [coefficient points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_coefficient#Men's_association_coefficient) in 2024–25

The label of European Performance Spots and title holders already qualified via league position are superscripted.

The second qualifying round, third qualifying round and play-off round were divided into Champions Path (**CH**) and League Path (**LP**).

The league phase draw for the 2025–26 UEFA Champions League took place at the [Grimaldi Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimaldi_Forum) in [Monaco](19261.md) on 28 August 2025. The 36 teams were divided into four pots of nine teams each based on their [UEFA club coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_coefficient#Men's_club_coefficient), except for the Champions League title holders, who were automatically placed as the top seed in pot 1.

The 36 teams were manually drawn and then automated software drew their eight different opponents at random, determining which of their matches were at home and which ones away. Each team faced two opponents from each of the four pots, one at home and one away. Teams could not face opponents from their own association, and could only be drawn against a maximum of two sides from the same association.[^cite_note-14]

[Bodø/Glimt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Bod%C3%B8/Glimt), [Kairat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Kairat), [Pafos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pafos_FC) and [Union Saint-Gilloise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royale_Union_Saint-Gilloise) made their debut appearances in the league phase/group stage. Kairat, based in [Almaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almaty), became the easternmost team to ever qualify for the Champions League proper,[^cite_note-Kairat_FourFourTwo-15][^cite_note-16] and, after being drawn to play away against [Sporting CP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporting_CP), broke the record for the longest trip in UEFA competitions, travelling 4,294 miles (6,911 km) from Almaty to [Lisbon](18091.md).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] In addition, Bodø/Glimt, based in [Bodø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bod%C3%B8_\(town\)) within the [Arctic Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Circle), became the northernmost European team to ever compete in the Champions League.[^cite_note-19] This marked the first time a Norwegian team participated in the competition proper since the [2007–08 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_UEFA_Champions_League),[^cite_note-20] and was also the first season since [2005–06](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_UEFA_Champions_League) in which no Ukrainian sides qualified for the group stage/league phase.[^cite_note-21]

A total of 16 national associations were represented in the league phase.

The top eight ranked teams received a bye directly to the round of 16. Teams ranked from 9th to 24th contested the knockout phase play-offs, with teams ranked from 9th to 16th seeded for the draw. Teams ranked from 25th to 36th were eliminated from all competitions.

## Knockout phase

Main article: [2025–26 UEFA Champions League knockout phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_UEFA_Champions_League_knockout_phase)

In the [knockout phase](939680.md), teams played against each other over [two legs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-legged_tie) on a home-and-away basis, except for the one-match final. The top eight teams from the final standings in the league phase entered at the round of 16. In the knockout phase, there was no country protection, with teams from the same association able to face each other in any round. Teams could also face opponents they played during the league phase.

The mechanism of the draws for each round was as follows:[^cite_note-regulations-7]

- In the draw for the knockout phase play-offs, the eight teams finishing the league phase in positions 9–16 were seeded, and the eight teams finishing the league phase in positions 17–24 were unseeded. The draw was split into four sections based on the predetermined bracket, with the seeded teams in each section drawn against one of their two possible unseeded opponents. The seeded teams hosted the second leg.

- In the draw for the round of 16, the eight teams finishing the league phase in positions 1–8 were seeded, and the eight winners of the knockout phase play-offs were unseeded. Again, the draw was split into four sections based on the predetermined bracket, with the seeded teams in each section drawn against one of their two possible unseeded opponents. The seeded teams hosted the second leg.

In the quarter-finals and semi-finals, both the exact match pairings and order of legs were predetermined based on the tournament bracket. The teams with the better league phase ranking played the second leg of each round at home if they continued advancing. Should a seeded team have been beaten, the team that eliminated them took over their seeding position. The winner of semi-final 1 was designated as the "home" team for the final (for administrative purposes, as it was played at a neutral venue).
