**Middlesbrough** ([/ˈmɪdəlzbrə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/87/En-uk-Middlesbrough.ogg/En-uk-Middlesbrough.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/87/En-uk-Middlesbrough.ogg/En-uk-Middlesbrough.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*MID-əlz-brə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)), [colloquially](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colloquially) known as **Boro**, is a port town and [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority) of [North Yorkshire](143759.md), located in [North East England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_England). Situated on the southern bank of the [River Tees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Tees), Middlesbrough forms part of the [Borough of Middlesbrough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Middlesbrough), the [Teesside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesside) [built-up area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Built-up_area) and the [Tees Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Valley). The town is governed by the [Middlesbrough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Council) [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority), which is divided into twenty wards, as part of the [Tees Valley Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Valley_Combined_Authority) along with [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darlington), [Hartlepool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartlepool), [Redcar and Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redcar_and_Cleveland), and [Stockton-on-Tees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton-on-Tees). In 2021, it had a population of 148,215.

There was a priory at the site from 686 until 1537, but no urban settlement until Joseph Pease selected it as the site for the [Stockton and Darlington Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_and_Darlington_Railway) in 1829. After this, it rapidly grew into a major centre of iron production. By the mid-1870s, it produced one third of the United Kingdom's [pig iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_iron) output and was known as *Ironopolis*. Steel production, ship building, and the chemical industry were also major industries. Since [World War II](32927.md), these industries have declined in major fashion, but [Teesport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesport) and the [Wilton International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilton_International) industrial site remain major employers.

## History

Further information: [History of Middlesbrough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Middlesbrough)

### Medieval period

Middlesbrough started as a [Benedictine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine) priory on the south bank of the river Tees; its name was possibly derived from it being midway between the holy sites of [Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_England) and [Whitby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitby). The earliest recorded form of Middlesbrough's name is "Mydilsburgh". Some believe the name means 'middle fortress', since it was midway between the two religious houses of Durham and Whitby; others state that it is an [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) personal name (*Midele* or *Myhailf*) combined with *burgh*, meaning town.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

In 686, a monastic cell was consecrated by [St Cuthbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Cuthbert) at the request of [St Hilda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Hilda), Abbess of [Whitby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitby). The cell evolved into [Middlesbrough Priory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Priory). The manor of Middlesburgh belonged to [Whitby Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitby_Abbey) and [Gisborough Priory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gisborough_Priory).[^cite_note-page-7] [Robert Bruce, Lord of Cleveland and Annandale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_de_Brus,_1st_Lord_of_Annandale), granted and confirmed, in 1119, the church of St. Hilda of Middleburg to Whitby.[^cite_note-auto5-8] Up until its closure on the [Dissolution of the Monasteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Monasteries) by [Henry VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VIII) in 1537, the church was maintained by 12 [Benedictine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine) monks, many of whom became vicars, or rectors, of various places in Cleveland.[^cite_note-9]

After settlement by the [Angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxons), the area became home to [Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking) settlers. Names of Viking origin (with the suffix *by*, meaning *village*[^cite_note-10]) are abundant; for example, [Ormesby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormesby), [Stainsby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stainsby,_North_Yorkshire) and [Tollesby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tollesby). These were once separate villages named after [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings) called Orm, Steinn and Toll; they are now areas of Middlesbrough that were recorded in [Domesday Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book), of 1086.

### Port development

In 1801, Middlesbrough was a small farming [township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Township_\(England\)) with a population of just 25. From 1829 onwards, it experienced rapid growth. In 1828, the influential [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers) banker, coal-mine owner and [Stockton and Darlington Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton_and_Darlington_Railway) shareholder [Joseph Pease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Pease_\(railway_pioneer\)) sailed up the River Tees to find a suitable new site downriver of Stockton on which to place new coal [staithes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staithe). As a result, in 1829, he and a group of Quaker businessmen bought the Middlesbrough farmstead and associated estate, some 527 acres (213 ha) of land and established the Middlesbrough Estate Company.

Through the company, the investors set about a new coal port development (designed by [John Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Harris_\(railway_engineer\))) on the southern banks of the Tees.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-ENE-12] The first coal shipping staithes at the port (known as "Port Darlington") were constructed with a settlement to the east established on the site of Middlesbrough farm as labour for the port, taking on the farm's name as it developed into a village.[^cite_note-GazDock-13][^cite_note-Middlesbrough_College-14]

The port was linked to the S\&DR on 27 December 1830 via a branch that extended to an area just north of the current [Middlesbrough railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_railway_station).[^cite_note-15]

The success of the port meant it soon became overwhelmed by the volume of imports and exports; in 1839, work started on a dock to the east of Middlesbrough.[^cite_note-GazDock-13] The first water for the dock was let in on 19 March 1842, while the formal opening took place on 12 May 1842.

### Victorian era

Further information: [Bolckow Vaughan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolckow_Vaughan), [Teesside Steelworks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesside_Steelworks), and [Dorman Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorman_Long)



A poem by Ian Horn: "Where alchemists were born below Cleveland's Hills. A giant blue dragonfly across the Tees reminds us every night. We built the world, every metropolis came from the Ironopolis."

Iron dominated the Tees area since 1841, when [Henry Bolckow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Bolckow) in partnership with [John Vaughan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Vaughan_\(ironmaster\)), founded the Vulcan [iron foundry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_foundry) and [rolling mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_mill). Vaughan introduced the new 'Bell Hopper' system of closed blast furnaces developed at the [Ebbw Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebbw_Vale) works. The new system and nearby abundant supply of [Ironstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironstone) in the [Eston Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eston_Nab) in 1850, made the works a success with the area becoming known as the "Iron-smelting centre of the world" and [Bolckow, Vaughan & Co., Ltd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolckow,_Vaughan) the largest company in existence at the time.[^cite_note-ICE-16]

Middlesbrough's population grew quickly from 40 people in 1829 to 7,600 by 1851.[^cite_note-ENE-12] [Pig iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_iron) production rose tenfold between 1851 and 1856 and, by the mid-1870s, Middlesbrough was producing one third of the nation's pig iron output. During this time, the town earned the nickname *Ironopolis*.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-Ballarat-18]



The Old Town Hall in 2013

On 21 January 1853, Middlesbrough received its [Royal Charter of Incorporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Charter_of_Incorporation),[^cite_note-19] giving the town the right to have a mayor, aldermen and councillors. Henry Bolckow became mayor, in 1853.[^cite_note-page-7]

In the latter half of the 19th century, Old Middlesbrough was starting to decline and was overshadowed by developments built around the [new town hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Town_Hall), south of the [original town hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_Hall,_Middlesbrough).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]



Gibson House (Boho Four)

On 15 August 1867, a Reform Bill was passed, making Middlesbrough a new parliamentary borough; Bolckow was elected member for Middlesbrough the following year.

The town's rapid expansion continued throughout the second half of the 19th century, fuelled by the iron and steel industry, and the population reached 90,000 by the dawn of the 20th century.[^cite_note-ENE-12]

### Second World War

Middlesbrough was the first major British town and industrial target to be bombed during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), although the official date of the Blitz is a few months later. The steel-making capacity and railways for carrying steel products were obvious targets. The [Luftwaffe](17885.md) first bombed the town on 25 May 1940, when a lone bomber dropped 13 bombs between South Bank Road and the South Steel plant.[^cite_note-22] More bombing occurred throughout the course of the war, with the railway station put out of action for two weeks in 1942.[^cite_note-23]

By the end of the war, more than 200 buildings had been damaged or destroyed in the Middlesbrough area. Areas of early- and mid-Victorian housing were demolished and much of central Middlesbrough was redeveloped. Heavy industry was relocated to areas of land better suited to the needs of modern technology. Middlesbrough itself began to take on a completely different look.[^cite_note-24]

### Post-war development



The A66 from a multi-storey car park; the road is raised, with Wilson Street running alongside it (2006)

The transition from post-war industrial to modern non-industrial Middlesbrough changed the town; many buildings were replaced and roads built. The [A66 road](341497.md) was built through the town in the 1980s and the Royal Exchange was demolished to make way for it.

[Middlesbrough F.C.](298603.md)'s modern [Riverside Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_Stadium) opened on 26 August 1995, next to Middlesbrough Dock. The club moved from [Ayresome Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayresome_Park), which had been its home for 92 years.

The original St Hilda's area of Middlesbrough, after decades of decline and clearance, was given the new name of *Middlehaven*, in 1986, on investment proposals to build on the land.[^cite_note-auto1-25] [Middlehaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlehaven) has since had new buildings built there including [Middlesbrough College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_College) and the Riverside Stadium amongst others. Also situated at Middlehaven is the *Boho* zone, offering office space to the area's business, to attract new companies and also *Bohouse* housing.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Some of the street names from the original grid-iron street plan of the town still exist in the area today.

The expansion of Middlesbrough southwards, eastwards and westwards continued throughout the 20th century, absorbing villages such as [Linthorpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linthorpe), [Acklam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acklam,_Middlesbrough), [Ormesby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormesby), [Marton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marton,_Middlesbrough) and [Nunthorpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunthorpe);[^cite_note-ENE-12] it continues to the present day.

### Administrative history

Middlesbrough was historically a [township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Township_\(England\)) in the [civil parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_parish) of [West Acklam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acklam,_Middlesbrough), in the [Langbaurgh Wapentake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langbaurgh_Wapentake) of [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire).[^cite_note-37] Despite having no church building, a [perpetual curacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_curacy) of Middlesbrough was created in 1744, also covering the neighbouring township of [Linthorpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linthorpe); the curacy of Middlesbrough gradually came to be treated as a civil parish.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

After construction of the modern town began in 1830, there was a need for more urban forms of local government. In 1841, a body of [improvement commissioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvement_commissioners) was set up covering the township of Middlesbrough and part of the township of Linthorpe.[^cite_note-40] The commissioners were superseded in 1853, when the same area was made a [municipal borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_borough). The borough boundaries were extended in 1858, 1866 and 1887.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] When elected county councils were created in 1889 under the [Local Government Act 1888](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1888), Middlesbrough became a [county borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_borough). It was then independent from the new [North Riding County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Riding_County_Council), whilst remaining part of the [North Riding of Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Riding_of_Yorkshire) for ceremonial purposes.[^cite_note-43]

By this time, the borough covered the civil parish of Middlesbrough and parts of the parishes of Linthorpe (where the old township had been made a separate parish in 1866), [Marton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marton,_Middlesbrough) and West Acklam. The [Local Government Act 1894](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1894) said that parishes could no longer straddle borough boundaries and so the parish of Middlesbrough was enlarged to cover the whole borough.[^cite_note-VoB-44][^cite_note-45]



Acklam Hall, built 1680s: a manor house in the parish of West Acklam, which historically included Middlesbrough. The parish was absorbed into Middlesbrough in 1932

The borough was further enlarged in 1913, taking in the rest of Linthorpe and the [North Ormesby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Ormesby) area from the parish of Ormesby.[^cite_note-46] In 1932, it gained West Acklam and smaller parts from other parishes when the [Middlesbrough Rural District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Rural_District) was abolished.[^cite_note-VoB-44]

In the latter half of the 20th century, Middlesbrough was affected by three reforms in:&#x20;

- [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Commission_for_England_\(1958%E2%80%931967\)), Middlesbrough became part of the [Teesside County Borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesside_County_Borough)

- [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972), it became the [county town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_town) of the [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_\(county\)) non-metropolitan county until its abolition

- [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Commission_for_England_\(1992\)), the Borough of Middlesbrough became a unitary authority of North Yorkshire.

In 2011, a [local enterprise partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_enterprise_partnership) was formed from the former Cleveland boroughs and the [Borough of Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Darlington) called [Tees Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Valley). In 2016, the area appointed a [combined authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_authority) mayor.

### Climate

Middlesbrough has an [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) typical for the United Kingdom. Being sheltered from prevailing south-westerly winds by the [Lake District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_District) and [Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennines) to the west, and the [Cleveland Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Hills) to the south, it is in one of the relatively dry parts of the country, receiving on average 596 millimetres (23.5 inches) of rain per year. Temperatures range from mild summer highs in July and August typically around 21 °C (70 °F), to winter lows in December and January falling to around 1 °C (34 °F).

Seasonal variations are small and both the mild summers and cool winters are far removed from the average climates of the latitude (54.5°N). This is mainly due to the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles) being a relatively small land mass surrounded by water, the mild south-westerly [Gulf Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Stream) air that dominates the British Isles, and the propensity for cloud cover to limit temperature extremes. In nearby [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), more than ten degrees further north, there are coastal [Bothnian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bothnian_Bay#Climate) climates with warmer summers than Middlesbrough; winters in Middlesbrough can be less cold than those at lower latitudes in mainland Europe.

## Industry



Captain Cook Square

Industry in the town was once dominated by steelmaking, shipbuilding and chemical industries. Since the late 20th century and into the 21st century,[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] the demise of much of the heavy industry in the area, newer technologies, such as the digital sector, have emerged.[^cite_note-51]

### Engineering

Middlesbrough remains a stronghold for engineering-based manufacturing and engineering contract service businesses. To help support this, the TeesAMP (Advanced Manufacturing Park) is designed to accommodate businesses associated with advanced manufacturing and emerging technologies.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

### Port



PD Ports' offices on Queen's Square

[Teesport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesport), owned by [PD Ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PD_Ports), is a major contributor to the economy of Middlesbrough and the port owners have their offices in the town. The port is 1 mile (1.6 km) from the [North Sea](21179.md) and 4 miles (6.4 km) east of Middlesbrough, on the Tees. In 2019, it handled over 4,350 vessels each year and around 27 million tonnes of cargo, with the estate covering approximately 779 acres.[^cite_note-54] Steel, petrochemical, agribulks, manufacturing, engineering and high street commerce operations are all supported through Teesport, in addition to the renewable energy sector, in both production and assembly facilities.

### Industrial history

In 1875, Bolckow, Vaughan & Co opened the Cleveland Steelworks in Middlesbrough, beginning the transition from iron production to steel and, by the turn of the century, the area had become one of the major steel centres in the country and possibly the world. In 1900, [Bolckow, Vaughan & Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolckow_Vaughan) had become the largest producer of steel in Great Britain. In 1914, [Dorman Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorman_Long), another major steel producer from Middlesbrough, became the largest company in Britain, employing a workforce of over 20,000 and, by 1929, it was the dominant steel producer on Teesside after taking over Bolckow, Vaughan & Co and acquiring its assets. It was possibly the largest steel producer in Britain at the time.[^cite_note-55]

The steel components of the [Sydney Harbour Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Harbour_Bridge) (1932) were engineered and fabricated by [Dorman Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorman_Long) of Middlesbrough. The company was also responsible for the [New Tyne Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyne_Bridge) in Newcastle-upon-Tyne.[^cite_note-DLong201407-56]

Several large shipyards also lined the Tees, including the [Sir Raylton Dixon & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Raylton_Dixon), which produced hundreds of steam freighters including the infamous [SS Mont-Blanc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Mont-Blanc), the steamship which caused the 1917 [Halifax Explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax_Explosion) in Canada.

The area is still home to the nearby large [Wilton International industrial site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilton_International) which, until 1995, was largely owned by [Imperial Chemical Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Chemical_Industries) (ICI). The fragmentation of that company led to smaller manufacturing units being owned by multinational organisations. The last part of ICI itself completely left the area in 2006 and the remaining companies are now members of the [North East of England Process Industry Cluster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_of_England_Process_Industry_Cluster) (NEPIC).

### Video games

Several video game companies have been based in Middlesbrough in the 21st century, including [Double Eleven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Eleven_\(company\)),[^cite_note-57] [SockMonkey Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SockMonkey_Studios) and Atomic Planet.

### Healthcare



James Cook University Hospital

The [South Tees Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Tees_Hospitals_NHS_Foundation_Trust) has the [James Cook University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cook_University_Hospital) in the town. ## Amenities

### Parks



Albert Park

[Albert Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Park,_Middlesbrough) was donated to the town by Henry Bolckow in 1866. It was formally opened by [Prince Arthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Arthur,_Duke_of_Connaught_and_Strathearn) on 11 August 1868 and consists of a 30-hectare (74-acre) site. The park underwent a considerable period of restoration from 2001 to 2004, during which a number of the park's landmarks saw either restoration or revival.

[Stewart Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Park,_Middlesbrough) was donated to the people of Middlesbrough in 1928 by Councillor Thomas Dormand Stewart and encompasses Victorian stable buildings, lakes and animal pens. It is also home to the Captain Cook Birthplace Museum. During 2011 and 2012, the park underwent major refurbishment. It hosted the [BBC Radio 1's Big Weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_1's_Big_Weekend) in the summer of 2019.[^cite_note-62] There is a [farmers' market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmers'_market) held in the park on the final Sunday of each month.[^cite_note-63]

[Newham Grange Leisure farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newham_Grange_Leisure_farm) in the suburb of Coulby Newham has operated continuously in this spot since the 17th century, becoming a [farm park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farm_parks?action=edit\&redlink=1) and conservation centre farm with the first residential development of the suburb in the 1970s.

### Buildings

Buildings of Middlesbrough









Landmarks from top left to bottom right:\
Acklam Hall, Grey Towers, Webb House/ The Dorman Long Office and The Middlesbrough Empire

The terraced [Victorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_architecture) streets surrounding the town centre are elements of Middlesbrough's social and historical identity, and the vast streets surrounding Parliament Road and Abingdon Road a reminder of the area's wealth and rapid growth during industrialisation.

The outer areas of the town have several country halls, most are of Victorian origin. Former halls include Marton Hall (on the grounds of [Stewart Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Park,_Middlesbrough)), Gunnergate Hall ([Coulby Newham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coulby_Newham)), [Tollesby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tollesby) Hall and [Park End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_End,_Middlesbrough) House.[^cite_note-68] Halls that are still extant, such as Newham Hall,[^cite_note-69] Nunthorpe Hall,[^cite_note-70] Grey Towers[^cite_note-71] and Coulby Manor.[^cite_note-72] The oldest domestic building is Acklam Hall of 1678. Built by Sir [William Hustler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hustler), it is also the only [Grade I listed building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listed_building#Categories_of_listed_building) in Midddlesbrough.[^cite_note-nhle-acklam-65][^cite_note-73]

[Middlesbrough Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Town_Hall), designed by [George Gordon Hoskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Gordon_Hoskins) and built between 1883 and 1889 is a Grade II\* [listed building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listed_building)[^cite_note-nhle-th-66] used for municipal purposes and as an entertainment venue. The Middlesbrough Empire was designed by [Ernest Runtz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Runtz); it was built in 1897 as a theatre and has been a nightclub since 1993 The first artist to perform in building as a [Music Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_Hall) was [Lillie Langtry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lillie_Langtry).[^cite_note-74] It became an early nightclub (1950s), then a bingo hall and is now once again a nightclub. The [Middlesbrough Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Theatre), in Linthorpe, was opened by [Sir John Gielgud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_John_Gielgud) in 1957; it was one of the first new theatres built in England after the Second World War.

The Dorman Long office on Zetland Road, constructed between 1881 and 1891, is the only commercial building ever designed by [Philip Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Webb), the architect who worked for Sir Isaac Lowthian Bell.

### Bridges

Bridges of Middlesbrough





Left: Tees Transporter Bridge, built in 1911\
Right: Tees Newport Bridge

[Sir William Arrol & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_William_Arrol_&_Co.) of Glasgow built the [Tees Transporter Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Transporter_Bridge) (1911), via a 1907 [Act of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Parliament), which spans the river between Middlesbrough and [Port Clarence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Clarence). It is a Grade II\* [listed building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listed_building).[^cite_note-nhle-tb-67] Some of the film *[Billy Elliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Elliot)* was filmed on the bridge.[^cite_note-visitmiddlesbrough.com-75] At 850 feet (260 m) long and 225 feet (69 m) high, it is one of the largest of its type in the world. Since reopening after restoration and flood protection work in 2013 and 2015[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] the bridge has been closed for long periods due to safety concerns.[^cite_note-78] In August 2022, the mayor of Middlesbrough announced that one of the legs is sinking, and that the estimated costs of repairs have been increasing: the bridge's future remains under consideration.[^cite_note-79]

The [Tees Newport Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Newport_Bridge) opened further up the river in 1934. Newport bridge still stands and is passable by traffic; it formerly lifted vertically in the centre.

## Education

### University

[Teesside University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesside_University) traces back to 1930 at the opening of Constantine Technical College, located on Borough Road, in the town centre. The then college expanded through acquiring adjacent buildings, such as Middlesbrough High School, and by building Middlesbrough Tower. It became *Teesside Polytechnic* in 1969.[^cite_note-80]

In 1992, the polytechnic gained university status, becoming the *University of Teesside*. Extramural classes had previously been provided by the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds) [Adult Education Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_Education_Centre) on Harrow Road, from 1958 to 2001.[^cite_note-81] It was rebranded in 2009 to *Teesside University*. It further expanded in size and courses available, with student numbers increasing to approximately 20,000 studying at the university in 2018.[^cite_note-82]



Teesside University Library

The university is a major presence in the town.[^cite_note-83] It has a growing reputation for developing digital businesses particularly in the field of [digital animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_animation) and for hosting the Animex festival.[^cite_note-84]

The Boho zone in the town now houses a large number of these start-up digital businesses.[^cite_note-85] The university has 18,000 students, 2,400 staff and operates a campus in Middlesbrough town-centre; it has benefited from around £250 million of investment in recent years, including the £30 million *Campus Heart* scheme.

The university supports a total of 2,570 full-time jobs across the Tees Valley, North East and UK economies per annum. The university contributes additional wealth to the local, regional and national economies as measured by Gross Value Added (GVA). It is estimated this contributes a total of £124 million GVA per annum. The total direct, indirect and induced spending impacts associated with full-time international students and UK students from outside of the North East is approximately £18.9 million per annum. It is estimated this spending supports 158 full-time jobs per annum in Tees Valley and contributes additional wealth of £9.3 million per annum to the local economy.[^cite_note-86]

Current university departments include: Business, Arts and Media, Computing, Health and Life Sciences, Science and Engineering, and Social Sciences and Law. In addition to teaching [computer animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_animation) and games design, it co-hosts the annual [Animex International Festival of Animation and Computer Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animex_International_Festival_of_Animation_and_Computer_Games). The university has links with James Cook University Hospital in the town.

### Judaism



The synagogue in Park Road South, which opened in 1938 and closed in 1998

Middlesbrough once held a Jewish population large enough to sustain one [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism) synagogue, though that congregation went defunct in 1998. [Ashkenazi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews) from Poland and Russia started to settle in the town from 1862 and formed Middlesbrough Hebrew Congregation in 1870 with a temporary [synagogue](48559.md) in the upstairs portion of a boot and shoe shop in Hill Street. The synagogue constructed its own building in Brentnall Street in 1874. Upon commencing construction of the new building, the local paper reported "the Jew could lay the foundation stone of his synagogue today in Middlesbrough with as great a sense of freedom as Solomon felt when he laid the foundation stone of his glorious temple in Jerusalem well nigh 3000 years ago and without even that sense of exile which possessed his ancestors when they sat down by the waters of Babylon and wept." It moved to a new building in Park Road South in 1938.[^cite_note-JCR-101][^cite_note-\:1-102]

The community reported generally harmonious relations with the non-Jewish community, including local churches. One Orthodox Jewish family reported especially friendly relations with vicar Wareham of St. Barnabas church, often giving him [matzos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matzos) at [Passover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passover) to teach his congregation, and receiving gifts of petrol to help with visits to Jewish congregation after air raids.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-\:1-102]

In 1939, the Jewish community opened a refugee hostel for girls rescued from the Nazis after the *[Kristallnacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristallnacht)* in Austria and Germany. A UK programme called *[Kindertransport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindertransport)* had brought up to 10,000 mostly Jewish children to Britain in 1938-39.[^cite_note-104] The hostel was home for between 16 and 25 girls and was funded voluntarily rather than by the government.[^cite_note-HCJC-105][^cite_note-106]

There are two Jewish cemeteries; the older one has plots dting from 1885.[^cite_note-HCJC-105]

Editions of the *[Jewish Year Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Year_Book)* record the growth and decline of Middlesbrough's Jewish population. It was about 100 in 1896–97 and peaked at 750 in 1935. It then declined to 30 in 1998, when the synagogue in Park Road South was ceremonially closed.[^cite_note-JCR-101] The decline of the Jewish population is part of a larger pattern also seen in Newcastle and Sunderland, caused by younger Jews moving to the bigger cities, such as London, for more opportunities and more liberal or reform congregations.[^cite_note-107]

### Islam



Jamia Mosque, Waterloo Road

The [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) community is represented in several [mosques](19894.md) in Middlesbrough. Muslim sailors visited the town from about 1890 and, in 1961, Azzam and Younis Din opened the first [Halal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halal) [butcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butcher) shop.[^cite_note-multiple-108]

The first mosque was a house in Grange Road in 1962.[^cite_note-multiple-108] There are around seven in Middlesbrough; the most prominent of which are the Al-Madina Jamia Mosque, on Waterloo Road; the Dar ul Islam Central Mosque, on Southfield Road; and the Abu Bakr Mosque & Community Centre,[^cite_note-109] which is currently temporarily situated on Cannon Park Way.

### Sikhism

The Sikh community established its first [gurdwara](482849.md) in Milton Street in 1967. After a time in Southfield Road, the centre is now in Lorne Street and was opened in 1990.[^cite_note-multiple-108]

### Hinduism

There is a Hindu Cultural Centre in Westbourne Grove, North Ormesby, which was opened in 1990.[^cite_note-multiple-108]

### Festivals and fairs



Wilkinson Lake

The [Middlesbrough Mela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Mela) is an annual, multi-cultural festival attracting an audience of up to 40,000 to enjoy a mix of live music, food, craft and fashion stalls. It began in Middlesbrough's Central Gardens, now Centre Square, and is either held there or in Albert Park.[^cite_note-110]

### Theatres and music venues

Middlesbrough also has a healthy musical heritage. A number of bands and musicians hail from the area, including [Chris Rea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Rea), [Paul Rodgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Rodgers) and [Micky Moody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micky_Moody).

[Middlesbrough Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Town_Hall) is the pre-eminent theatre venue. It has two concert halls: a classic Victorian concert hall with a [proscenium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proscenium) stage and seating 1,190; the other, under the main hall, is called the Middlesbrough Crypt and has a capacity of up to 600. The venue is run by Middlesbrough Council and is funded, in part, by Arts Council England as a [National Portfolio Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Portfolio_Organisation) specialising in music.[^cite_note-111] It was refurbished with the assistance of the [National Lottery Heritage Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lottery_Heritage_Fund) and reopened in 2018.[^cite_note-112]

The [Middlesbrough Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Theatre) (formerly the Little Theatre) is in the suburb of [Linthorpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linthorpe). It was designed by architects Elder & De Pierro[^cite_note-113] and was the first purpose designed theatre to be erected in post-war England, when it was opened on 22 October 1957 by [Sir John Gielgud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_John_Gielgud).[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

### Museums



Dorman Museum

The [Dorman Memorial Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorman_Memorial_Museum), which was founded by Sir [Arthur Dorman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Dorman) and specialises in social and local history.

The [Captain Cook Birthplace Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Cook_Birthplace_Museum), which was opened on 28 October 1978 in celebration of the 250th anniversary of [Captain James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_James_Cook)'s birth in nearby Marton.



Ormesby Hall

Though just outside the boundary of Middlesbrough, within a joint preservation area with Redcar and Cleveland, [Ormesby Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormesby_Hall) is an 18th-century palladian mansion, once owned by the [Pennyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennyman_baronets) family; it is now a [National Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Trust) property.

In July 2000, the Transporter Bridge Visitor Centre was opened to commemorate the building of the [Middlesbrough Transporter Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Transporter_Bridge).[^cite_note-allan111-116]

### Arts and galleries



MIMA Gallery

The town has three art galleries. [Middlesbrough Institute of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Institute_of_Modern_Art), known locally as *MIMA*, is a purpose built contemporary art gallery which opened in January 2007. It replaced the Cleveland Gallery (closed 1999) and Cleveland Crafts Centre (closed 2003).

The Middlesbrough Art Weekender is a contemporary art festival organised by the Auxiliary that has been held in central Middlesbrough since 2017.[^cite_note-117] In 2019, it was held over the weekend of 26–29 September and included the works of artists such as [Emily Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Hesse) and [Karina Smigla-Bobinski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karina_Smigla-Bobinski).[^cite_note-118] The Auxiliary Warehouse space, which was opened also as part of the 2019 Middlesbrough Art Weekender, is a recent addition to the contemporary art community.[^cite_note-119]

The Platform A Gallery is a contemporary art space at the end of platform 1 of Middlesbrough station.[^cite_note-120]

### Artworks







Sculptures: 40,000 Years of Modern Art, Bottle O' Notes and the Temenos Sculpture

The [Temenos Sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temenos_Sculpture), designed by sculptor [Anish Kapoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anish_Kapoor) and designer [Cecil Balmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_Balmond), is sited to the north-west side of the Riverside Stadium. The steel structure, consisting of a pole, a circular ring and an oval ring, stands approximately 110 m long and 50 m high and is held together by steel wire. It was unveiled in 2010 at a cost of £2.7 million.

Near the town hall is the *Bottle of Notes*; it was unveiled in 1993 and is the UK's only public sculpture by [Claes Oldenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claes_Oldenburg). Drawing on its local surroundings, the sculpture was fabricated in South Tyneside by former ship builders,[^cite_note-121] and its outside is the first line from the journals of [Captain James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_James_Cook): "We had every advantage we could desire in observing the whole of the passage of the Planet Venus over the Sun's disc." Cook was born in the area in 1728.[^cite_note-122]

## In popular culture

Middlesbrough has featured in many television programmes, including *[The Fast Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fast_Show)*, *[Inspector George Gently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_George_Gently)*, *[Steel River Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_River_Blues)*, *[Spender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spender)*, *[Play for Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Play_for_Today)* (*The Black Stuff*; latterly the drama *[Boys from the Blackstuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boys_from_the_Blackstuff)*) and *[Auf Wiedersehen, Pet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auf_Wiedersehen,_Pet)*.[^cite_note-126]

Film director [Ridley Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Scott) is from the North East and based the opening shot of *[Blade Runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_Runner)* on the view of the old ICI plant at Wilton. He said: "There's a walk from Redcar...I'd cross a bridge at night and walk above the steel works. So that's probably where the opening of *Blade Runner* comes from. It always seemed to be rather gloomy and raining, and I'd just think "God, this is beautiful." You can find beauty in everything, and so I think I found the beauty in that darkness." It has been claimed that the site was also considered as a shooting location for one of the films in Scott's [*Alien* franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_\(franchise\)).[^cite_note-127]

In the 2009 action thriller [*The Tournament*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tournament_\(2009_film\)), Middlesbrough is that year's location where the assassins' competition is being held. In November 2009, the mima art gallery was used by the presenters of *[Top Gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Gear_\(2002_TV_series\))* as part of a challenge. The challenge was to see if car exhibits would be more popular than normal art.[^cite_note-128]

In 2010, filmmaker [John Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Walsh_\(filmmaker\)) made the satirical documentary *[ToryBoy The Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ToryBoy_The_Movie)* about the [2010 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_general_election) in [the Middlesbrough constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) and sitting MP Stuart Bell's alleged laziness as an MP.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

In March 2013, Middlesbrough was used as a stand in for 1969 [Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne) in BBC's *Inspector George Gently*, starring [Martin Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Shaw) and [Lee Ingleby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Ingleby); the footage appeared in the episode "Gently Between The Lines" (episode 1 of series 6).[^cite_note-132]

In 2024, BBC Comedy commissioned *[Smoggie Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoggie_Queens)* from [Hat Trick Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hat_Trick_Productions), an "out and out comedy centred around a gang of friends who are fiercely proud of their North Eastern town of Middlesbrough and their small pocket of the LGBTQ+ community."[^cite_note-133] Writer [Phil Dunning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Dunning?action=edit\&redlink=1) described the show as "a love letter to the town".[^cite_note-134] The series aired on [BBC Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Three) in November 2024.

## Sport

### Football



The Riverside Stadium

[Middlesbrough FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_FC) is a [Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship) [football](10568.md) team, owned by local haulage entrepreneur [Steve Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Gibson_\(businessman\)). The 34,000 capacity[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] [Riverside Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_Stadium) is owned and host to home games by the club since 1995, when they left [Ayresome Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayresome_Park). Founder members of the [Premier League](11250.md) in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Premier_League_1992-93), Middlesbrough won the [Football League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Cup) in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Football_League_Cup_Final),[^cite_note-137] and were beaten finalists in the [2005-06 UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005-06_UEFA_Cup).[^cite_note-138] In 1905, they made Britain's first £1,000 transfer when they signed [Alf Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf_Common) from local rivals [Sunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunderland_AFC).[^cite_note-Proud-139] [Middlesbrough Ironopolis FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Ironopolis_FC) was briefly based in the town during the late 19th century; it later dissolved. These days, Middlesbrough have players such as [Darragh Lenihan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darragh_Lenihan), [Hayden Hackney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayden_Hackney) and [Tommy Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Conway).

### Rugby Union

[Middlesbrough RUFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_RUFC), founded in 1872 having have played their home games at [Acklam Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acklam_Park) since 1929, and Acklam RUFC are in [Durham/ Northumberland Division One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham/Northumberland_1). Both are members of [Yorkshire Rugby Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Rugby_Football_Union).

### Racing

The town hosts multiple road races through the year, including the annual Middlesbrough 10k (formerly Tees Pride 10k) road race. First held in 2005, the one-lap circuit event and associated fun runs were held in the Acklam area of the town, before being moved to the town centre in 2021.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

On 1 May 2016, Middlesbrough hosted the start of Stage 3 to the [2016 Tour de Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Tour_de_Yorkshire). The stage and race ended in [Scarborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough,_North_Yorkshire).[^cite_note-142]

### Other



Middlesbrough Golf Club

[Middlesbrough Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Cricket_Club) have played at Acklam Park since 1930 and play in [North Yorkshire and South Durham Cricket League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Yorkshire_and_South_Durham_Cricket_League). [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_County_Cricket_Club) have played 45 [County Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Championship) games in Middlesbrough; the most recent being in 1996.

[Speedway racing](230182.md) was staged at Cleveland Park Stadium from 1928 until the 1990s, with the [Middlesbrough Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Bears).

Athletics has two local clubs serving the area: Middlesbrough & Cleveland Harriers, and Middlesbrough AC (Mandale). Training facilities at the Middlesbrough Sports Village opened in 2015, replacing Clairville Stadium.[^cite_note-143] Notable athletes to train at both facilities are World and European Indoor Sprint Champion [Richard Kilty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Kilty), British indoor long jump record holder [Chris Tomlinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Tomlinson). The sports village includes a running track with grandstand, an indoor gym and café, football pitches, as well as a cycle circuit and velodrome.

Next to the sports village is a skateboard park and Middlesbrough Tennis World.[^cite_note-144]

[Tees Valley Mohawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Valley_Mohawks) and [Teesside Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesside_Lions) basketball teams play in the [NBL Division 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_League_\(England\)).
