The **National Assembly of Zimbabwe**, previously the **House of Assembly** until 2013, is the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house) of the [Parliament](3394393.md) of [Zimbabwe](34399.md). It was established upon Zimbabwe's [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Zimbabwe#Independence_and_the_1980s) in 1980 as one of [two chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) of parliament. Between the abolition of the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Zimbabwe) in 1989 and its reestablishment in 2005, the House of Assembly was the [sole chamber](204304.md) of parliament.

Since the [2023 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Zimbabwean_general_election), the National Assembly has had 280 members. Of these, 210 are elected in [single-member constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-member_district). 60 seats are [reserved for women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserved_political_positions), and are elected by [proportional representation](23298.md) in 10 six-seat constituencies based on the country's [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Zimbabwe). The last 10 seats are reserved for youth and are also elected through proportional representation in 10 one-seat constituencies based on the provinces as well.[^cite_note-3]

[Jacob Mudenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Mudenda) has been [Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_\(politics\)) of the National Assembly since September 2013.

## History

Under the [1980 Constitution](30927578.md), 20 of the 100 seats in the House of Assembly were [reserved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserved_political_positions) for the country's [white minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whites_in_Zimbabwe), although whites and other ethnic minorities made up only 5% of the population at the time. These seats were abolished by [constitutional amendment](261586.md) in 1987.[^cite_note-4]

This size of 100 seats was used for two elections, the [1980 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Rhodesian_general_election,_1980) held immediately before independence and the [1985 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwean_parliamentary_election,_1985). The [1990 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwean_general_election,_1990) was the first election after the abolition of the white-reserved seats, and also expanded the House of Assembly to 120 seats, a size which was retained for the [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwean_parliamentary_election,_1995) and [2000 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwean_parliamentary_election,_2000).

With the [2005 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_parliamentary_election,_2005), the House of Assembly was expanded to 150 members. 120 members were directly elected in single member [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency) using the plurality (or [first-past-the-post](261709.md)) system. The [President](39434128.md) appointed twelve additional members and eight [provincial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Zimbabwe) governors who held reserved seats in the House. The remaining ten seats were held by traditional chiefs who were chosen by their peers. All members served five-year terms.

Following the [2008 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwean_general_election,_2008), the House of Assembly was expanded to 210 seats and composed entirely of elected representatives.[^cite_note-5] The appointed and [*ex officio* members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_officio_member) were transferred to the Senate. The [Seventh House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Parliament_of_Zimbabwe) was opened on August 26, 2008. The additional system of 60 seats reserved for women was established for the [2013 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwean_general_election,_2013). An additional 10 seats - one for each Province - reserved for a youth quota was established for the [2023 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Zimbabwean_general_election).
