The **2025 Pacific typhoon season** was the second consecutive season to feature slightly above-average activity in terms of named storms and typhoons. However, it was a below-average season in terms of [accumulated cyclone energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accumulated_cyclone_energy) (ACE) and super typhoons. Of the twenty-seven named tropical storms, thirteen became typhoons and only one managed to intensify into a super typhoon. Many storms were also weak and short-lived, particularly before and after the peak season activity. The season ran throughout 2025, though most tropical cyclones typically [develop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclogenesis) between June and November. In addition, the season's first named storm, [Wutip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Wutip_\(2025\)), developed on June 11, the fifth-latest date for a typhoon season to produce a named storm,[^cite_note-3] while the season’s last named storm, [Koto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Koto), dissipated on December 3. In addition, the season’s first typhoon, [Danas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Danas_\(2025\)), reached that intensity on July 6. Furthermore, the season’s first and only super typhoon, [Ragasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Ragasa) reached that intensity on September 21 before it struck the Philippines on the following day.

The scope of this article is limited to the Pacific Ocean north of the equator between [100°E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100th_meridian_east) and the [180th meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/180th_meridian). Within the northwestern Pacific Ocean, two separate agencies assign names to tropical cyclones, which can result in a system having two names. The [Japan Meteorological Agency](457886.md) (JMA)[^cite_note-4] names a [tropical cyclone](8282374.md) when it is estimated to have 10-minute [sustained winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_sustained_wind) of at least 65 km/h (40 mph) anywhere in the basin. The [Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Atmospheric,_Geophysical_and_Astronomical_Services_Administration) (PAGASA) assigns names to tropical cyclones that move into or form as a tropical depression within the [Philippine Area of Responsibility](4272946.md) (PAR), defined as the area between 135°E–115°E and 5°N–25°N, regardless of whether the JMA has already named the system. Tropical depressions monitored by the United States [Joint Typhoon Warning Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Typhoon_Warning_Center) (JTWC)[^cite_note-6] are given a numerical designation with a "W" suffix, which means "west", a reference to the western Pacific region.[^cite_note-STY-1]

During the year, several national meteorological services and scientific agencies forecast how many tropical cyclones, tropical storms, and typhoons will form during a season and/or how many tropical cyclones will affect a particular country. These agencies include the Tropical Storm Risk (TSR) Consortium of [University College London](52029.md), [PAGASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAGASA), Vietnam's [National Center for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Hydro-Meteorological_Forecasting) (NCHMF) and Taiwan's [Central Weather Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Weather_Administration) (CWA).

On January 23, 2025, PAGASA released its first outlook in their monthly seasonal climate outlook predicting the first half of 2025. They expected zero to three tropical cyclones that would form or enter the [Philippine Area of Responsibility](4272946.md) (PAR) from January to March, while two to five tropical cyclones are predicted to form or enter PAR between April to June.[^cite_note-PAG_1-10] The agency also noted that [La Niña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ni%C3%B1a) conditions are expected to persist between January and March, with transition [El Niño-Southern Oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ni%C3%B1o-Southern_Oscillation) (ENSO) conditions also anticipated during April to June.[^cite_note-PAG_1-10]

On May 7, 2025, TSR issued its first extended-range forecast, predicting slightly below-average activity with 25 named storms, 15 typhoons, and 8 intense typhoons. The outlook was influenced by expected neutral ENSO conditions, strong easterly wind anomalies in April, the absence of early-season storm activity, and a continued negative phase of the [Pacific decadal oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_decadal_oscillation) (PDO), all of which are factors typically associated with reduced typhoon activity.

PAGASA had also updated its second and final outlook on July 14 for July to September, predicting six to ten tropical cyclones would form or enter PAR during those months, while five to nine of those would form or enter PAR between October to December.[^cite_note-PAG_2-11] The agency also indicated that the ENSO condition would also persist between July to September, and would further continue until the first quarter of the following year.[^cite_note-PAG_2-11]

TSR released its updated extended-range forecast on August 7, maintaining the number of named storms and typhoons but reducing the number of intense typhoons to 7. They also significantly decrease the prediction of the [accumulated cyclone energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accumulated_cyclone_energy) (ACE) index due to the lower-than-expected storm activity in June and July and the ongoing negative PDO phase.

## Seasonal summary



### Background

The 2025 Pacific typhoon season has no official season limit, although most tropical cyclones typically develop between June and November. In this season, 41 tropical cyclones formed, with 27 strengthening into named storms. Thirteen of them intensified into typhoons, and one further intensified into a super typhoon.

### Early season activity



Three systems active on July 24: Severe Tropical Storm Co-May (far left); Tropical Storm Francisco (upper center); and Tropical Storm Krosa (lower right).

The season officially began with the formation of a tropical depression on February 11 near the [Philippines](23440.md). The season became relatively inactive for four months, until [Tropical Storm Wutip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Wutip_\(2025\)) developed on June 11, becoming the fifth-latest named storm on record in the western Pacific basin. It also ended a 169-day period with no named storm activity in the basin. Wutip caused widespread damage across parts of the Philippines, Vietnam, and China, leaving 17 people dead and resulting in [US\$](18717338.md)253 million in damage. On the same day, another low-pressure area formed east of the Philippines; it developed into Tropical Depression Auring the next day. Auring moved northward and crossed Taiwan before weakening into a remnant low. On June 21, Tropical Depression 02W formed north of the Northern Mariana Islands, which developed into Tropical Storm Sepat two days later off the coast of Japan. On June 24, Tropical Depression 03W developed west of the Philippines.



Satellite animation of Typhoon Danas making landfall in [Budai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budai,_Chiayi), [Chiayi County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiayi_County), Taiwan on July 6 before weakening.

July was notably active, producing eight named storms, including one not recognized by the JMA. The month began with Tropical Storm Mun, which largely spared affected areas. The season's first typhoon, [Danas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Danas_\(2025\)), formed on July 3 and made landfall over [Budai, Chiayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budai,_Chiayi) in [Taiwan](25734.md) as a Category 3-equivalent typhoon, becoming the first typhoon to strike the island's west-central coast since [Typhoon Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Wayne_\(1986\)) in 1986. On July 11, two systems formed: Tropical Storm Nari and Tropical Depression 07W, with the former storm making landfall over [Cape Erimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Erimo) in Japan. Later in the month, [Tropical Storm Wipha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Wipha_\(2025\)) developed near the Philippine coast, passing through the Babuyan Islands as a severe tropical storm before heading to southern China and Vietnam, where it made landfall over [Taishan, Guangdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taishan,_Guangdong) in China and between the provinces of [Hưng Yên](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C6%B0ng_Y%C3%AAn_province) and [Ninh Bình](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninh_B%C3%ACnh) in Vietnam, causing 60 fatalities and significant damage throughout its path.&#x20;



Highest Tropical Cyclone Wind Signal issued by PAGASA for Severe Tropical Storm Co-May in each province in the Philippines.

By the end of July, three more cyclones had developed: Francisco, [Co-May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Co-May), and Krosa. Co-May formed northeast of [Ilocos Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Sur), intensifying into a minimal typhoon as it loitered west-southwestward and then tracked southwestward off the west coast of Luzon due to a [Fujiwhara interaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujiwhara_interaction) with Tropical Storm Francisco, which was situated northeast of Luzon at that time, as well also being situated in the eastern semicircle of a monsoon gyre. Co-may later made two landfalls over [Agno, Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agno,_Pangasinan) and [Candon, Ilocos Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candon,_Ilocos_Sur) in the Philippines, becoming the strongest and first tropical cyclone to affect the [Ilocos Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Region) since [Chan-hom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Chan-hom_\(2009\)) of [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Pacific_typhoon_season). Co-may later weakened into a tropical depression after crossing through the rugged terrain of [Cordillera Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Range) before regained tropical storm status again on July 27 when the storm moved through the [Ryukyu Islands](8458939.md). Co-may made two final landfalls in China, one [Zhujiajian Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhujiajian_Island), [Zhejiang](96602.md) on July 29, and another in [Fengxian District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fengxian_District), [Shanghai](27643.md) on July 30. Overall, Co-may claimed the lives 55 people and caused US\$73 million in damage. The month concluded with the formation of Tropical Storm Bailu on July 31.

### Peak season activity



Two simultaneous active tropical cyclones on September 20: Typhoons [Ragasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Ragasa) (left) and Neoguri (upper right)

August was slightly active compared to the previous month. Early on August 2, the remnant low of [Hurricane Iona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Iona) entered the basin from the Central Pacific and briefly became a tropical depression. Two tropical depressions formed between August 2 and 4, while [Typhoon Podul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Podul) formed on August 6. Podul made landfall over [Taimali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taimali) in Taiwan as a Category 1-equivalent typhoon and then in China as a tropical storm, causing moderate damage there and claiming two lives. Tropical Depression Fabian formed on August 7 and Tropical Depression 17W ten days later. Tropical Storm Lingling formed near Japan on August 17, causing minimal damage, while [Typhoon Kajiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Kajiki_\(2025\)), which formed southeast of [Luzon](69375.md) on August 22, caused extensive damage in [northern Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Vietnam) after it brushed [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) and then skirting the southern part of [Hainan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan_Island), killing a total of 17 people and causing US\$244 million in damage. [Tropical Storm Nongfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Nongfa) formed last on August 27 and affected the [North Central Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Central_Coast) of Vietnam three days later, concluding the month of August.[^cite_note-Jacinto-13]



Typhoon Ragasa nearing Babuyan Islands on September 22

The month of September began with [Tropical Storm Peipah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Peipah_\(2025\)) on September 2, tracking northeast along the [subtropical ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_ridge) before affecting Japan and then becoming extratropical on September 5. [Severe Tropical Storm Tapah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Tapah_\(2025\)) formed near [Vigan, Ilocos Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigan,_Ilocos_Sur) on the same day, making landfall in [Taishan, Guangdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taishan,_Guangdong), China as a Category 1-typhoon on September 8, before interacting with rugged terrain. During the peak of the season, three tropical cyclones formed: Mitag, Ragasa, and Neoguri. Tropical Storm Mitag formed near [Legazpi, Albay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legazpi,_Albay) and eventually crossed through the [Cordillera Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Luzon\)), slightly weakened into a depression before regaining tropical storm status again in the South China Sea. Mitag made landfall over [Shanwei, Guangdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanwei,_Guangdong), China before rapidly deteriorating at the same time on September 20. [Typhoon Ragasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Ragasa) became the first super typhoon of the season, eventually reaching Category 5-equivalent super typhoon status near the [Babuyan Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babuyan_Islands). Ragasa eventually made landfall over Panuitan Island in [Calayan, Cagayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calayan,_Cagayan) on September 22 before undergoing an eyewall replacement cycle afterwards. The storm then affected Southern China and Vietnam before being declared as having dissipated on September 25.[^cite_note-14]

Typhoon Neoguri then formed near [Wake Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Island) and strengthened into a Category 4-equivalent typhoon amid high SSTs, then weakened for a time due to a combination of upwelling and high wind shear, but restrengthened into an unusually high-latitude Category 4 typhoon near [40°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40th_parallel_north), making it one of the strongest unusually high-latitude cyclones on record. [Typhoon Bualoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Bualoi) formed on September 22 near [Yap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yap) and made multiple landfalls in the Philippines and Vietnam, causing extensive damages and numerous fatalities in those countries.

### Late season activity



Tropical Storm Matmo strengthening over the Philippine Sea on October 2 before its landfall.

October began with the formation of [Typhoon Matmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Matmo_\(2025\)), which made landfall over [Dinapigue, Isabela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinapigue,_Isabela) as a Category 1-typhoon before it reemerged through [West Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Philippine_Sea) and strengthened into a Category-2 typhoon. Matmo then brushed through Southern China, resulting in at least 39 deaths and damage amounting to \$3.86 billion (2025 [USD](18717338.md)), the costliest in the basin of the year. Typhoon Halong then formed next, becoming a Category 4-typhoon on October 7, with winds of up to 225 km/h (140 mph). Halong directly affected [Hachijō-jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachij%C5%8D-jima) thereafter before transitioning to extratropical on October 10. Typhoon Nakri stalled near the [Ryukyu Islands](8458939.md) as a tropical storm before becoming a typhoon just south of Japan. [Severe Tropical Storm Fengshen](81360224.md) formed next on October 12, which affected some parts of [Bicol Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Region), [Southern Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Tagalog), including [Metro Manila](72340.md) in the Philippines as a tropical storm between October 18 and 19 before eventually dissipated off the coast of Vietnam four days later. At that time after Fengshen made multiple landfalls in the Philippines, a tropical depression named Salome by PAGASA was formed on October 20. PAGASA then upgraded Salome into a tropical storm (later downgraded to a depression) three days later before the system become fully dissipated on the same day.



High-resolution imagery of Typhoon Fung-wong nearing Luzon prior to its landfall in Dinalungan, Aurora on November 9.

On the last day of October, [Kalmaegi](81485989.md) formed, persisting into November and made multiple landfalls in [Visayas](72418.md) and [Palawan](240395.md) and later Vietnam, causing significant damage and high death toll, primarily as a result of the freshwater flooding. This made Kalmaegi the deadliest typhoon in this basin for this year, with over 288 deaths.[^cite_note-15] On November 6, Tropical Storm Fung-wong formed. It rapidly intensified in the [Philippine Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea) as a Category 4-equivalent typhoon, and further intensified as a super typhoon based on [PAGASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAGASA) before it made landfall over [Dinalungan, Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinalungan,_Aurora). Fung-wong then curved northeastwards, gradually weakened after it interacted with the Cordillera Central, which helped the storm to significantly weaken. Fung-wong made another landfall over [Hengchun Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hengchun_Township) in [Pingtung County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingtung_County), Taiwan on November 12 as a tropical storm, and shortly after weakened into a tropical depression. The system then dissipated the following day.&#x20;

November saw the formation of Koto and 34W. [Koto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Koto) traversed most of Visayas in the Philippines before it went on into a Category 1-typhoon over the South China Sea. Koto then stalled as it moves over unfavorable conditions before it dissipated on December 3. Meanwhile, the disturbance that is associated with [Cyclone Senyar](81689422.md), crossed and entered through the Western Pacific basin on November 27. The JMA and JTWC started issuing advisories, with the latter gave the system as 34W as it subsequently upgraded into a depression. Three days later, both agencies discontinued advisories as it dissipated.&#x20;

The 41st tropical depression formed on December 1, with the [PAGASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAGASA) naming it Wilma as it entered the PAR. Wilma struggled to intensify due to unfavorable conditions. PAGASA also issued [Tropical Cyclone Wind Signal No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Cyclone_Wind_Signals#Tropical_Cyclone_Wind_Signal_No._1) over numerous areas of [Visayas](72418.md), [Mindanao](20546.md), and [Palawan](240395.md) on December 4. Wilma made two landfalls over [Hilabaan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilabaan_Island) in [Dolores, Eastern Samar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolores,_Eastern_Samar) and in [Oras, Eastern Samar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oras,_Eastern_Samar) before declaring it a remnant low on December 7.

Main article: [Tropical Storm Wutip (2025)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Wutip_\(2025\))

A tropical depression formed over the South China Sea on June 9.[^cite_note-16] On the next day, the JTWC designated it as *Tropical Depression 01W*.[^cite_note-17] The JMA upgraded it to Tropical Storm Wutip on June 11. The storm strengthened further while moving to the west-northwest.[^cite_note-18] At 18\:00 UTC, the JMA further upgraded the system to a severe tropical storm.[^cite_note-19] Wutip hit the southwest coast of China's Hainan Island at around 15\:00 UTC on June 13,[^cite_note-20] before reemerging over the [Gulf of Tonkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Tonkin) shortly after.[^cite_note-21] On June 14, the JTWC reported that Wutip had rapidly intensified into a minimal typhoon, although its deep convection had largely collapsed.[^cite_note-22] Later that day, Wutip made its second landfall near [Leizhou City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leizhou) in [Guangdong Province](65109.md) at around 12\:30 CST (04\:30 UTC).[^cite_note-23] After landfall, it weakened into a minimal tropical storm, as satellite imagery indicated that its eye had filled in.[^cite_note-24] The JMA downgraded Wutip to a tropical depression on the same day when it was inland, and continued tracking the system until it dissipated at 12\:00 UTC on June 15.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

The precursor to Wutip, along with the [southwest monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_monsoon), triggered a [lahar flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahar) near [Kanlaon Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanlaon_Volcano) in the Philippines.[^cite_note-27] In [Hainan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan), Wutip brought damaging winds with gusts of 191–217 km/h (119–135 mph; 103–117 kn).[^cite_note-28] In Vietnam, water levels in several rivers reached their highest marks in 30 years.[^cite_note-\:3-29] Total damage reached 1.317 trillion dong (US\$52.4 million).[^cite_note-auto6-30]

On June 11, the JMA announced that a tropical depression had developed east of the Philippines.[^cite_note-31] On the same day, the [PAGASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAGASA) started monitoring the disturbance as a low-pressure area while it was still inside the [Philippine Area of Responsibility](4272946.md) (PAR).[^cite_note-32] By the following day, it had intensified into a tropical depression and was named *Auring* by PAGASA.[^cite_note-33] PAGASA reported that Auring made landfall in Taiwan and later weakened into a remnant low due to the frictional effects of its landfall.[^cite_note-PAGASATD2-34][^cite_note-35] The agency issued its final advisory when the system exited the PAR,[^cite_note-PAGASATD-36] while the JMA continued to monitor it until it was last noted on June 13.[^cite_note-37]

On June 12, Taiwan's [Central Weather Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Weather_Administration) issued a heavy rain warning for the counties of [Yilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yilan_County,_Taiwan), [Hualien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hualien_City), [Taitung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taitung_County), and [Pingtung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingtung_County).[^cite_note-38] Heavy rainfall was recorded in [Daliao District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daliao_District), with 205.5 mm (8.1 in) of precipitation recorded.[^cite_note-39] At least one fatality and four injuries were reported due to the system.[^cite_note-40] In China, the depression brought moisture northward along the edge of the [Pacific high](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pacific_High), resulting in significant rainfall in [Zhejiang](96602.md) and [Shanghai](27643.md), with 52.9 mm (2.1 in) recorded in [Shanyang, Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanyang,_Shanghai).[^cite_note-41] In the Philippines, Auring brought moderate to heavy rainfall and thunderstorms to the provinces of [Batanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batanes) and [Cagayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan).[^cite_note-PAGASATD2-34]

### Tropical Storm Sepat

| Peak intensity | 65 km/h (40 mph) (10-min); 1004 [hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\)) ([mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\))) |

A tropical depression formed north of the [Northern Mariana Islands](21402.md) on June 21.[^cite_note-42] As it continued to develop, the JTWC designated it *02W* on the next day.[^cite_note-43] The system was situated in an area of low to moderate wind shear near the [tropical upper-tropospheric trough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_upper-tropospheric_trough), which limited outflow. Despite this, it still managed to intensify into a tropical storm on June 23, receiving the name *Sepat* from the JMA.[^cite_note-44] Steered northwest by a subtropical ridge located to its northeast, Sepat strengthened as its structure improved.[^cite_note-45]

Sepat's convective activity decreased significantly on June 24, halting any intensification.[^cite_note-46] Later that day, the JTWC assessed that Sepat had weakened into a tropical depression as it slowed and entered a drier, more hostile environment.[^cite_note-47] A trough approaching from the west began to impart strong wind shear on the depression as it neared Japan and turned north-northeastward.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] Further affected by colder sea surface temperatures, Sepat initiated its [extratropical transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extratropical_transition) early on June 26, and as a result, the JTWC discontinued all advisories on Sepat.[^cite_note-50]

A tropical depression developed southeast of [Okinawa Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okinawa_Island) on July 31. On August 22, a tropical depression formed off the east coast of Luzon, named *Isang* by PAGASA.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] Isang made landfall over [Casiguran, Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casiguran,_Aurora) that morning,[^cite_note-150] and by the next day, it strengthened into Tropical Storm Kajiki while crossing the South China Sea. Kajiki strengthened further into a typhoon and peaked in intensity while passing south of China's Hainan island. The JTWC classified it as a Category 2-equivalent typhoon with a 23-nautical-mile-wide (43 km) eye.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-four-152][^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154] It then weakened slightly over the [Gulf of Tonkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Tonkin),[^cite_note-155] before making landfall on Vietnam between [Nghệ An](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngh%E1%BB%87_An) and [Hà Tĩnh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A0_T%C4%A9nh) around 18\:00 ICT (11\:00 UTC) on August 25. Both the JMA and JTWC issued final advisories on August 31 as the system dissipated. Its remnants later crossed into the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) and eventually redeveloped into [Deep Depression LAND 04](78605703.md#Deep_Depression_LAND_04) over the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) on September 6.

Meanwhile, in [Thailand](30128.md), Nongfa's remnants and the ongoing [monsoon trough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon_trough) caused widespread flooding in the [Pa Sak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pa_Sak_River), [Yom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_River), and [Nan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nan_River) basins.[^cite_note-171] Districts in [Phetchabun Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phetchabun_Province) and [Phitsanulok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phitsanulok) reported flash floods and crop damage. Authorities activated emergency response centers, controlled dam releases, and coordinated evacuations to mitigate further impacts.

In [Yaizu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaizu), a greenhouse collapse seriously injured a farmer.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185] Flooding was reported in [Kamakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura).[^cite_note-186] A tornado and severe winds injured several people and left about 17,000 households without power. Both the [Tōkaidō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dkaid%C5%8D_Shinkansen) and [San'yō Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San'y%C5%8D_Shinkansen) lines were suspended.[^cite_note-187] Losses totaled to US\$150 million.[^cite_note-188]

A tropical depression formed southeast of [Iwo Jima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwo_Jima) on October 4.[^cite_note-283] Moving northwest, it steadily intensified, becoming Tropical Storm Halong on October 5, and a typhoon two days later.[^cite_note-284][^cite_note-285] That day, it rapidly intensified to the equivalent of a Category 4 on the Saffir-Simpson scale as it developed a clear eye.[^cite_note-286] Halong tracked northeastward on October 8,[^cite_note-287] and began weakening the next day due to wind shear and dry air.[^cite_note-288] JTWC issued its final advisory on October 10 as Halong transitioned into an extratropical system.[^cite_note-289] JMA continued monitoring it until declaring it extratropical at 22\:10 JST (13\:10 UTC).[^cite_note-290]

Halong generated strong waves that left one person dead in Japan.[^cite_note-291] In [Hachijojima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachijojima), wind speeds were measured at 107 knots (123 mph) and record rainfall amounts of 349 millimetres (13.7 in) were observed.[^cite_note-292] Over 2,700 customers lost water on the island and another 2,200 customers lost power. Several roads were left impassible on these islands due to flooding and downed trees.[^cite_note-293] In Western Alaska, flood and high wind watches were issued as the extratropical remnants of Halong approached, bringing [hurricane-force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaufort_scale#Modern_scale) winds to the [Bering Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bering_Sea).[^cite_note-294] The storm caused widespread damage in the [Yukon-Kuskokwim Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukon-Kuskokwim_Delta), where winds reached 107 miles per hour (172 km/h) in the [Kusilvak Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusilvak_Mountains).[^cite_note-Fairbanks1-295] Record flooding hit [Kipnuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kipnuk) and [Kwigillingok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwigillingok), where homes were swept away and several residents went missing.[^cite_note-296] One fatality was confirmed in Kwigillingok, with two others missing.[^cite_note-297] The storm caused an estimated US\$125 million in damages to Alaska.[^cite_note-298]

Koto, along with the shearline, generated flooding and landslides that left two people missing in the Philippines, exacerbating effects left by recent [Typhoon Kalmaegi](81485989.md) and [Typhoon Fung-wong](81509932.md).[^cite_note-376]

## Storm names

See also: [Tropical cyclone naming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclone_naming) and [History of tropical cyclone naming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_tropical_cyclone_naming)

Within the northwest Pacific Ocean, both the JMA and PAGASA assign names to tropical cyclones that develop in the region, which can result in a tropical cyclone having two names.[^cite_note-Padgett_Dec_99-417] The JMA's RSMC[^cite_note-418] Tokyo–Typhoon Center assigns international names to tropical cyclones on behalf of the [World Meteorological Organization](33584.md)'s Typhoon Committee should they be judged to have 10-minute sustained wind speeds of 65 km/h (40 mph).[^cite_note-TC-419] PAGASA names tropical cyclones which move into or form as a tropical depression in their PAR, located between 135°E and 115°E and between 5°N and 25°N, even if the cyclone has had an international name assigned to it.[^cite_note-Padgett_Dec_99-417] Should the list of names for the region be exhausted, then names will be taken from an auxiliary list, the first ten of which are published each season. Unused names are marked in gray. The names of significant tropical cyclones will be retired by both [PAGASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_retired_Philippine_typhoon_names) and [the Typhoon Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_retired_Pacific_typhoon_names_\(JMA\)) in the spring of 2026.[^cite_note-TC-419]

### International names

Main article: [List of retired Pacific typhoon names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_retired_Pacific_typhoon_names)

During the season, 27 tropical storms developed in the Western Pacific, and all of them were named by the JMA once they had 10-minute sustained winds of 65 km/h (40 mph). The JMA selected the names from a list of 140 names, that had been developed by the 14 members nations and territories of the [ESCAP/WMO Typhoon Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_retired_Pacific_typhoon_names#Background).[^cite_note-Retired_typhoons-420] Retired names were announced during the 58th Session of the Typhoon Committee; though replacement names will be announced in 2027. During the season, the names *[Co-May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Co-May)*, *[Nongfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Nongfa)*, *[Ragasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Ragasa)*, and *[Koto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Koto)* were used for the first (and only, in the cases of *Co-May* and *Ragasa*) time, after they replaced *[Lekima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Lekima)*, *[Faxai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Faxai)*, *[Hagibis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Hagibis)*, and *[Kammuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Kammuri)*, which were retired following the [2019 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Pacific_typhoon_season).&#x20;

#### Retirement

At their 58th Session in March 2026, the ESCAP/WMO Typhoon Committee announced that the names *[Wipha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Wipha_\(2025\))*, *[Co-May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Co-May)*, *[Mitag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Mitag_\(2025\))*, *[Ragasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Ragasa)*, *[Bualoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Bualoi)*, *[Matmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Matmo_\(2025\))*, *[Kalmaegi](81485989.md)*, and *[Fung-wong](81509932.md)* would be removed from the naming lists due to the damages and deaths they caused respectively, and they will not be used again for another typhoon name.[^cite_note-421] Replacement names will be announced in early 2027.[^cite_note-422]

During the season, PAGASA used its own naming scheme for the 23 tropical cyclones, the highest since [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Pacific_typhoon_season), that either developed within or moved into their self-defined area of responsibility. This was the same list used during [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Pacific_typhoon_season) and is scheduled to be used again in 2029. All of the names were the same except for *[Jacinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Nongfa)*, *[Mirasol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Mitag_\(2025\))* and *[Opong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Bualoi)*, which replaced *[Jolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Conson)*, *[Maring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Kompasu)* and *[Odette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Rai)*, respectively. These three names, as well as [*Uwan*](81509932.md) and [*Verbena*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Koto) after replacing *[Urduja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Kai-tak)* and *[Vinta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Tembin)* in 2017, were used for the first (and only, in the cases of *Mirasol*, *Opong*, and *Uwan*) time.[^cite_note-PAGASA_Names-423]

#### Retirement

On March 19, 2026, [PAGASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAGASA) announced that they removed the names *[ Crising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Wipha_\(2025\))*, *[ Emong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Co-May)*, *[ Mirasol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Mitag_\(2025\))*, *[Nando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Ragasa)*, *[Opong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Bualoi)*, *[Tino](81485989.md)*, and *[Uwan](81509932.md)* from its rotating naming lists due to the number of deaths and amount of damage each one caused and the contributing damages from other tropical cyclones, and they will not be used again for another typhoon name within the [PAR](4272946.md). They were replaced with *Chico*, *Elias*, *Magyawan*, *Nilad*, *Omar*, *Tala*, and *Urbano*[^cite_note-424] respectively for the 2029 season.[^cite_note-425]
