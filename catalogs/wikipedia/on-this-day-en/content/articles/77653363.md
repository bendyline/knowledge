**Manuel Osa Nsue Nsua** (born 21 July 1976) is an Equatoguinean politician and banker who has served as the [prime minister of Equatorial Guinea](692145.md) since August 2024. He previously served as the head of the national bank from 2012 until his appointment as prime minister.

## Early life

Osa was born on 21 July 1976, in [Andom-Onvang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andom-Onvang?action=edit\&redlink=1), in the municipality of [Nsok-Nsomo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsok-Nsomo), Equatorial Guinea.[^cite_note-auto5-5] At the age of six, he moved to Spain to live with his sister in [Palma de Mallorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_de_Mallorca).[^cite_note-auto6-6] In Spain, he earned his bachelor’s degree and then enrolled at the [University of the Balearic Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Balearic_Islands), studying business sciences and economics.[^cite_note-auto6-6] He received degrees in both fields and later studied further at [Pompeu Fabra University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeu_Fabra_University), where he earned a [master's degree](170346.md) in financial management and business accounting in 2005.[^cite_note-auto5-5]

## Career

After Osa graduated from Pompeu Fabra, he briefly served with the General Directorate of Economy of the Autonomous Community of the Balearic Islands, before then joining the [Santander Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander_Bank) later in 2005.[^cite_note-auto6-6] Serving with the Palma de Mallorca branch, he rose from account manager to executive director to the branch's general director.[^cite_note-auto6-6] In this role, he was the supervisor of the bank's operations in four zones and also helped make decisions for the [Madrid](41188263.md) office.[^cite_note-auto6-6]

In 2012, Osa returned to his birth country and became the [chief executive officer](52234.md) of the [Banco Nacional de Guinea Ecuatorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banco_Nacional_de_Guinea_Ecuatorial?action=edit\&redlink=1) (BANGE, *National Bank of Equatorial Guinea*).[^cite_note-auto-1][^cite_note-7] The bank, based in [Malabo](48823.md), is the only private lender headquartered in Equatorial Guinea.[^cite_note-auto2-2][^cite_note-auto1-8] Osa took over BANGE as it was on the verge of bankruptcy and successfully turned it around, with *[Jeune Afrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeune_Afrique)* dubbing him the bank's "savior".[^cite_note-auto5-5] As CEO and the general director of the bank, he oversaw major expansion, reaching 22 branches by 2017 and establishing its first office in Spain that year.[^cite_note-auto1-8] By 2019, offices had been established in all but two of the districts in the country.[^cite_note-9] In addition to leading the bank in Equatorial Guinea, Osa also served on the board of directors for its Cameroon office.[^cite_note-auto4-3]

Osa received several honors for his direction of BANGE.[^cite_note-auto3-10] In 2016, he was named the Equatorial Guinea Banking CEO of the Year and BANGE was named the country's bank of the year as part of the [Global Banking & Finance Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Banking_&_Finance_Awards?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-auto3-10] BANGE was named the Equatorial Guinea Bank of the Year by *[The Banker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Banker)* for 2017, with the publication highlighting its stability despite a market crash.[^cite_note-11] Osa also received an award from the *[African Banker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Banker?action=edit\&redlink=1)* in 2019.[^cite_note-12] BANGE was named the country's Bank of the Year by *The Banker* again in 2022.[^cite_note-13] Osa also served with the [Bange Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bange_Business_School?action=edit\&redlink=1) as the chairman of the board of directors, starting in 2020.[^cite_note-14]

On 16 August 2024, president [Teodoro Obiang Nguema Mbasogo](263622.md) decreed Osa as the next [Prime Minister of Equatorial Guinea](692145.md), succeeding [Manuela Roka Botey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuela_Roka_Botey), who had resigned along with the rest of the government for being "ineffective".[^cite_note-auto-1][^cite_note-auto2-2][^cite_note-auto4-3] In the president's decree, Osa was named to oversee the country's "administrative coordination".[^cite_note-auto-1] His appointment was made as the country entered into an economic crisis.[^cite_note-auto2-2]
