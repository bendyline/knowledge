The **2025–26 NBA season** was the 80th season of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md) (NBA). The regular season began on October 21, 2025 and ended on April 12, 2026. The third edition of the in-season [NBA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Cup) tournament was held from October 31 through December 16, 2025, with the [New York Knicks](80108544.md) winning it against the [San Antonio Spurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_San_Antonio_Spurs_season). The [2026 NBA All-Star Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_NBA_All-Star_Game) was played on February 15, 2026, at the [Intuit Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuit_Dome) in [Inglewood, California](107640.md).[^cite_note-2] The [play-in tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Play-in_tournament) to attempt and win a playoff spot was played from April 14 to 17, 2026, followed the next day by the opening of the playoffs. In a rematch of the NBA Cup final, the Knicks defeated the Spurs in five games in the [2026 NBA Finals](82427430.md).

### Retirements

- On June 29, 2025, [Bojan Bogdanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bojan_Bogdanovi%C4%87) announced his retirement from professional basketball, citing recurring foot injuries. He played for six teams in his ten-year NBA career.

  [^cite_note-3]

- On August 18, 2025, [Marco Belinelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Belinelli) announced his retirement from professional basketball. He spent 13 seasons in the NBA and won an NBA championship with the [San Antonio Spurs](72889.md) in [2014](39572289.md).

  [^cite_note-4]

- On August 19, 2025, [John Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wall) announced his retirement from professional basketball. He played for three teams in his 13-year career.

  [^cite_note-5]

- On August 30, 2025, [Jeremy Lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Lin) announced his retirement from professional basketball. He played for eight teams in his nine-year NBA career. He is best known for the "Linsanity" phenomenon when he played with the [New York Knicks](72855.md) in the [2011–12 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_NBA_season). He later won an NBA championship with the [Toronto Raptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Raptors) in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_NBA_Finals).

  [^cite_note-6]

- On October 15, 2025, [Malcolm Brogdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Brogdon) announced his retirement from professional basketball. He played for five teams in his nine-year career and was named the [NBA Rookie of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Rookie_of_the_Year_Award) in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_NBA_season).

  [^cite_note-7]

- On November 22, 2025, [Chris Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Paul) announced that the 2025–26 season would be his last after playing for seven teams during his 21-year career. He was named NBA Rookie of the Year in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005-06_NBA_season) and was the last remaining active player from the [2005 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_NBA_draft). He announced his retirement from professional basketball on February 13, 2026.

  [^cite_note-8]

- On December 2, 2025, [Danilo Gallinari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danilo_Gallinari) announced his retirement from professional basketball. He played for eight teams over a sixteen-year career.

  [^cite_note-9]

- On May 7, 2026, [P.J. Tucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P.J._Tucker) announced his retirement from professional basketball. He played for eight teams over a fourteen-year career. Known for his defense, he won a championship with the [Milwaukee Bucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milwaukee_Bucks) in 2021.

- On May 12, 2026 [Damion Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damion_Lee) announced his retirement from professional basketball. He played for three teams in his seven-year career, winning a championship with the [Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors) in 2022.

### Draft

The [2025 NBA draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_draft) took place on June 25–26 at [Barclays Center](1658814.md) in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), New York.[^cite_note-10]

### Free agency

[Free agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_agency_\(sports\)) negotiations began on June 30, 2025, at 6\:00 p.m. ET. Players may be signed after the [July moratorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_moratorium) on July 6 at 12\:00 p.m. ET.

### NBA Cup

Main article: [2025 NBA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_Cup)

The [NBA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Cup) returned for the 2025–26 season, with the same basic structure:[^cite_note-49]

- All games except the championship final counting towards the regular-season standings.

- Six intraconference pools of five (three pools per conference).

- This season, the group games against each of the other teams in their pool (two at home and two on the road), were held primarily on Fridays between October 31 and November 28. Group games were also played on Tuesday, November 25 and Wednesday, November 26.

- The winners of each pool (three teams per conference) and two wild-card teams (one team per conference) advanced to a single-elimination tournament held on December 9, 10, 13 and 16.

- To compensate, the NBA's regular-season scheduling formula was modified so only 80 games for each team were initially announced during the offseason. The first two rounds of the tournament counted as regular-season games 81 and 82. The championship game was an extra 83rd game that did not count toward the regular season. Teams that did not qualify for the in-season tournament knockout round, or were eliminated in the quarterfinals, were scheduled additional games against other teams that were eliminated in the same conference (if possible) and round to reach 82 games.

- The semifinals and championship game were held at [T-Mobile Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-Mobile_Arena) in [Paradise, Nevada](124384.md) for the third consecutive season.

## Play-in tournament

See also: [NBA play-in tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_play-in_tournament)

The top six seeds in each conference advanced to the main rounds of the [2026 NBA playoffs](79524481.md), while the next four seeds participated in a [Page playoff system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Page_playoff_system) tournament from April 14–17, 2026. The 7th-place team hosted the 8th-place team in the double-chance round needing to win one game to advance, with the winner clinching the 7th seed in the playoffs. The 9th-place team hosted the 10th-place team in the elimination round requiring two wins to advance, with the loser being eliminated from the contention. The loser in the double-chance round hosted the elimination-round game-winner, with the winner clinching the 8th seed and the loser being eliminated.[^cite_note-52]

## Uniform and logo changes

- The [Orlando Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_Magic) unveiled a new logo and uniforms, taking inspiration from the team's original look from 1989 to 2000. The "shooting ball with stars" logo returned with an updated look while the wordmarks were updated with a star replacing the letter "A" in the words "Orlando" and "Magic", which are reflected in their new uniforms. The team's "Icon" uniform was changed to a blue color while the "Statement" uniform was changed to a black and blue pattern inspired by the team's original warmup gear.

  [^cite_note-97]

- The [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Lakers) unveiled a new purple Statement Edition uniform. The Lakers' white wordmark is outlined in gold, and the white jersey numbers have a gold drop shadow. The design removes the previous black side panel, leaving only purple and gold, with "Los Angeles Lakers" along the side trim. The primary logo appears at the center of the shorts, while the "LA" mark is shown on both sides for the first time on a core uniform. "Leave a Legacy" is placed above the jock tag, and "17" at the back of the neck represents the team's championships.

  [^cite_note-98]

- The [Phoenix Suns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_Suns) unveiled a new black Statement Edition uniform. The uniform takes inspiration from [Arizona](21883824.md)'s [sunsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset) with a [purple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple) and [orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(colour\)) *The Valley* gradient throughout its design. A bold [Phoenix](49121.md) wordmark is placed on the chest at an angle where the left side is leaning downwards. The team's sunburst logo was reimagined with the Valley-inspired gradient streaks across the shorts, while the classic sunburst logo is on the center of the beltline. The Suns would wear this uniform for the first time on October 31 against the [Utah Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Jazz) in their first [2025 NBA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NBA_Cup) game.

  [^cite_note-99]

- The NBA, in partnership with [Topps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topps), announced that players who played their first NBA game would wear an "NBA Debut" patch on their uniforms, which were then removed and authenticated after the game on an autographed, one-on-one numbered rookie card. Additionally, previous season's winners of the [Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Most_Valuable_Player), [Rookie of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Rookie_of_the_Year) and [Defensive Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Defensive_Player_of_the_Year) would wear a gold NBA logo patch on the back of their uniforms for the entire season.

  [^cite_note-100]

  &#x20;All award winners and members of last season's [All-NBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-NBA_Team), [All-Rookie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_All-Rookie_Team) and [All-Defensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_All-Defensive_Team) teams also wore a special patch on their uniforms (or lapels in the case of [Coach of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Coach_of_the_Year) and [Executive of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Executive_of_the_Year) winners) during their first game of the season. These patches depicted specific silhouettes tied to each player's award or year-end team selection. After the game, these uniforms are auctioned off by [Sotheby's](541267.md) throughout the season.

  [^cite_note-101]

  &#x20;[LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James) would also wear a special patch on his uniform commemorating his record 23rd NBA season, starting with the January 12 game at the [Sacramento Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_Kings). This design featured his trademark chalk toss silhouette in front of each of the four colors corresponding to the teams he played: navy for his first stint with the [Cleveland Cavaliers](72868.md), red for the [Miami Heat](72850.md), wine for his second stint with the Cavaliers, and gold for the Los Angeles Lakers.

This was the first season of new 11-year deals with the [ESPN family of networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_on_ESPN), [NBC Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_on_NBC), [Amazon Prime Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_on_Prime_Video) and [NBA TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_TV). The ESPN networks and NBA TV renewed their contract with a modified schedule. In December 2025, the *[Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_Journal)* reported that [DAZN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAZN) was in advanced talks to acquire a majority stake in [Main Street Sports Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Street_Sports_Group), the owner of the FanDuel Sports Networks. If the deal went through, DAZN and Main Street would integrate their live streaming platform in the United States and collaborate on programming.[^cite_note-169] Afterwards, *[Sports Business Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Business_Journal)* reported that Main Street had missed a payment to the [St. Louis Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Cardinals) baseball team and that the company would be forced to dissolve operations at the end of the 2025–26 season if the deal with DAZN did not go through by the end of January 2026.[^cite_note-170] In January, *Sports Business Journal* reported that Main Street had missed payments to "several, if not all 13" of its NBA teams. As a result, Main Street and the teams with missed payments entered a 15-day grace period.[^cite_note-171] Two days later, *Sports Business Journal* reported that talks with DAZN had stopped progressing and all [MLB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB) teams except the [Tampa Bay Rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Rays) had not received payments on-time.[^cite_note-172]

On January 8, 2026, all nine MLB teams with agreements with FanDuel Sports Network terminated their contracts with the company due to missed payments.[^cite_note-173] In February 2026, most former FanDuel MLB teams announced production and distribution deals with [MLB Local Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_Local_Media).[^cite_note-174] [*Puck*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puck_\(media_company\)) reported that Main Street intends to wind down FanDuel Sports Network in mid-April after the close of the 2025–26 NBA and NHL seasons.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176]
