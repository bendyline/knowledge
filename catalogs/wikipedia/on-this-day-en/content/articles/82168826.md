From January 23 to January 27, 2026, a very large and expansive [winter storm](244878.md), [unofficially referred to](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_storm_naming_in_the_United_States) as **Winter Storm Fern** by [The Weather Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Channel) and various news outlets,[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-nyt-fern-name-26-11] or **[Snowmageddon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowmageddon)** in some areas,[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] caused deadly and catastrophic [ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_storm) and [snow](28191.md) impacts across a very long stretch of land, encompassing [Northern Mexico](48157875.md) to the [Southern](179553.md) and [Northeastern United States](431669.md) and into [Canada](5042916.md). The storm brought over two feet of snow to some locations in New Mexico and New England, and up to an inch of ice in the Southern United States. The ice storm impacts led to widespread power outages lasting for up to a week in some locations.

Developing in part from an [upper-level low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper-level_low) on January 22, the winter storm steadily moved eastwards across the [Central United States](2031425.md), dropping a very large swath of wintry [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_types). By January 25, the system had moved into the Northeast, where it began to transition into a [nor'easter](402179.md). Gusty winds and heavy snow continued to occur in [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England), even as it pulled away from the coastline the following day, leaving many parts of North America with a mix of rain, [sleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_pellets), [freezing rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freezing_rain), and snow. Meteorologists described the storm as "potentially historic" in its impact, with the storm at one point encompassing nearly 2,000 miles (3,200 km) of length stretching from the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border) far into [eastern regions of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Canada). Twenty-four [U.S. state governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state_governors) issued [emergency declarations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_declarations) in response to the storm. [Winter weather alerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_weather_alert) were also issued across a similar length of terrain, affecting up to 230 million people. The storm resulted in over 10,000 flights nationwide in the United States being cancelled or postponed. [Travel bans](26647363.md) were also enacted in numerous states.

As of February 23, 174 deaths had been confirmed due to the storm, becoming the deadliest winter storm in [North America](21139.md) since [Winter Storm Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_13%E2%80%9317,_2021_North_American_winter_storm), which caused the [2021 Texas power crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Texas_power_crisis) and killed at least 290 people.[^cite_note-14] Over a million customers lost power, mostly concentrated within the [South](179553.md) where a damaging and crippling ice storm occurred, causing power lines and trees to snap and break under the immense weight. Icy conditions also led to hazardous travel afterwards. Damages are estimated to be in excess of [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_dollar)\$4 billion.[^cite_note-Verisk_damage-7] Overall, a large swath of snowfall ranging from 1–2 feet (12–24 in; 30–61 cm) occurred from Texas to Maine, and in some cases (particularly in the South) broke daily snowfall records. In the [Mid-Atlantic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_states) and Northeast, where the storm became a [nor'easter](402179.md), several locations along the [I-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_95) corridor—specifically [Philadelphia](50585.md), Pennsylvania, and [New York City](645042.md)—saw their heaviest snowfall accumulation since a [nor'easter in February 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_31_%E2%80%93_February_3,_2021_nor'easter). Several days after the storm, the [Carolinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolinas) were affected by [yet another powerful winter storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_30_%E2%80%93_February_2,_2026_North_American_bomb_cyclone), compounding recovery costs there.

On January 21, 2026, news media and meteorological outlets began publicly forecasting that a large-scale winter storm would impact a significant portion of the [continental United States](83759.md) and potentially regions of Canada.[^cite_note-nyt-fern-name-26-11] The storm originated from the effect of a wave in the [upper atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_atmosphere) stretching the [polar vortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_vortex), which normally sits over [northern Canada](79987.md) and [Alaska](624.md), southwards. The cold air from this effect had been forecast to interact with moisture off the coast of [California](5407.md) and the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md) to create the expected ice and snow that would impact large portions of North America, especially the United States.[^cite_note-15] The storm first developed on January 22 over the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) as a [cold-core low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold-core_low) moving southeastwards towards the [Baja California peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baja_California_peninsula). Due to the immense size and expected impact of the winter storm, a [NOAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOAA_Hurricane_Hunters) [Gulfstream IV-SP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulfstream_IV) flew into the system to collect data in order to improve forecasting accuracy.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-11a-26-17] The system developed over the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains) midday January 23,[^cite_note-\:0-18] as a broad [trough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trough_\(meteorology\)) coalesced over the Plains and [Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains).[^cite_note-19] Wintry precipitation associated with the developing system began to progress to the east (primarily producing sleet and freezing rain), spreading into northern [Texas](29810.md), southern regions of [Oklahoma](22489.md), and southern [Arkansas](1930.md).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

History of the winter storm seen through [GOES-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOES-19)

Later that night, a shift in precipitation to snow began over central and eastern Oklahoma and far-western Arkansas, with an increase in moisture expected to lead to snowfall rates of up to 1 inch (2.5 cm) per hour.[^cite_note-22] Snow started to fall in central Arkansas in the early hours of January 24.[^cite_note-23] By 15\:00 UTC on January 24, the [Weather Prediction Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_Prediction_Center) (WPC) began issuing storm summary bulletins on the system, as a [low pressure wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-pressure_area) had settled along a [cold front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_front) near the [Gulf Coast](11969.md), met by a [barometric ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge_\(meteorology\)) over the Plains.[^cite_note-24] Several [tornado warnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_warning) and [watches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_watch) were issued in [Alabama](303.md), [Georgia](48830.md), and [Florida](18933066.md), with gusts of 71 mph (114 km/h) reported in [Montgomery, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery,_Alabama).[^cite_note-SPC_Reports-5] The system later underwent [Miller B type cyclogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_classification) as a new area of low pressure developed off the coast of the [Southeast](816925.md) on January 25, strengthening as it moved northwards near [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) offshore south of [Cape Cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cod) early the following day.[^cite_note-25] As the system pulled away from the coastline and snow began to taper off, the WPC issued its last storm summary bulletin at 21\:00 UTC on January 26.[^cite_note-26]

The system brought snowfall to [Maritime Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Maritimes) beginning late on January 25.[^cite_note-\:9-27] The storm continued along the coast of [Atlantic Canada](71095.md) through January 27.[^cite_note-28] The snowfall largely ended across the Maritimes by the morning of January 27, though [Cape Breton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Breton_Island) continued to see snowfall close to noon.[^cite_note-\:9-27] The system passed just south of the [Avalon Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalon_Peninsula) late that day.[^cite_note-29] Heavy snow and strong winds occurred in [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_\(island\)).[^cite_note-\:14-30]

More than 20 [state governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_United_States_governors) issued [emergency declarations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_declarations) in anticipation of the storm.[^cite_note-auto-31][^cite_note-Weather.com-32] A large swath of [winter weather alerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_weather_alert) were issued by the [NWS](286989.md), including [winter storm warnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_storm_warning) and [ice storm warnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_storm_warning) stretching from the [Four Corners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Corners) region to as far northeast as the state of [Maine](19977.md), encompassing a near 2,000-mile (3,200 km) stretch of contiguous alerts comprising a population of more than 230 million people.[^cite_note-Weather.com-32]

From January 23 to January 25, the number of counties under a winter storm warning was the highest recorded.[^cite_note-\:0-18][^cite_note-33] [National Guardsmen](37988.md) from 12 states were activated in advance of the storm.[^cite_note-34]

Over 560 flights in the United States were cancelled by 4\:40 p.m. EST on January 23, almost 4,000 on January 24, and over 9,000 on January 25.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] Several sporting events were also postponed.[^cite_note-37]

At [Camp Minden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Army_Ammunition_Plant), Louisiana, 250,000 meals, 400,000 liters of water, and 30 generators were pre-positioned, according to a memo from the [Federal Emergency Management Agency](73797.md) issued on January 23. There were also shuttle drivers stationed in Pennsylvania, Texas, Louisiana, and Georgia to transport materials quickly. Additionally, 28 urban search and rescue teams were on standby.[^cite_note-38]

#### Southwest

A picture of a produce aisle. The produce bins are green and the floors are concrete. All the bins for produce are empty.

Empty shelves at a grocery store in [Corpus Christi, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Christi,_Texas), in advance of the storm

A state of emergency was declared by New Mexico governor [Michelle Lujan Grisham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Lujan_Grisham) and allocated \$200,000 to support communities in [New Mexico](21649.md).[^cite_note-39]

[Texas](29810.md) governor [Greg Abbott](1492735.md) declared a state of emergency on January 21, mobilizing the state's [Division of Emergency Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Division_of_Emergency_Management) (TDEM) to activate response resources.[^cite_note-40] The [Texas Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Rangers_\(baseball\)) cancelled a Fan Fest event that was set to take place on January 24.[^cite_note-nbc-41] [DoorDash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DoorDash) activated their Severe Weather Protocol and temporarily suspended its operations in Northwest Texas, including the cities of [Amarillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarillo,_Texas) and [Lubbock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubbock,_Texas), from January 23 at 7\:00 p.m. CST to January 24 at 12\:00 p.m. CST due to the hazardous road conditions.[^cite_note-42]

#### South

Governor [Kay Ivey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kay_Ivey) declared a [state of emergency](298608.md) for 19 counties in northern [Alabama](303.md), mainly for the likelihood of significant ice storm damage.[^cite_note-43]

On January 22, [Arkansas](1930.md) governor [Sarah Huckabee Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Huckabee_Sanders) declared a state of emergency, and the Arkansas Division of Emergency Management was authorized \$250,000 from the Disaster Response and Recovery Fund of Arkansas.[^cite_note-44]

[Georgia](48830.md) governor [Brian Kemp](27301061.md) also declared a state of emergency and [mobilized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobilization) 500 [National Guardsmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_National_Guard).[^cite_note-45]

[Louisiana](18130.md) governor [Jeff Landry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Landry) declared a state of emergency on January 18 and the Governor's Office of Homeland Security and Emergency Preparedness activated its Crisis Actions Team.[^cite_note-46] Residents were noted to have bought [firewood](481098.md) in order to stay warm during the storm and its aftermath.[^cite_note-47] The [Louisiana Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Department_of_Transportation) (LDOTD) said that crews were stocked on [deicing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deicing) solutions and that they would have crews pre-treating roads beginning in shifts on January 23.[^cite_note-48] [Entergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entergy) stated that they would monitor the power situation and respond "as soon as it is safe to do so".[^cite_note-49]

A state of emergency was declared by [Mississippi](16949861.md) governor [Tate Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tate_Reeves) beginning on January 23.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

[North Carolina](21650.md) governor [Josh Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Stein) declared a state of emergency on January 21.[^cite_note-52]

#### Mid-Atlantic

A state of preparedness was issued by Governor [Patrick Morrisey](37898732.md) for [West Virginia](32905.md) on January 21.[^cite_note-53] A state of emergency was issued by [Virginia](32432.md) governor [Abigail Spanberger](58910530.md) on January 22.[^cite_note-nbc-41] Maryland governor [Wes Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wes_Moore) declared a state of preparedness.[^cite_note-54] The [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland) allowed for move-ins for the second semester as early as January 23 due to the inclement weather.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

A state of emergency was declared for [New York](8210131.md) by Governor [Kathy Hochul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Hochul) on January 23. Early voting for special elections in [New York City](645042.md) were cancelled for January 25.[^cite_note-57] [LaGuardia Airport](248547.md) closed at 1 p.m. on January 25 during the storm while [NJ Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ_Transit) and [NYC Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYC_Ferry) also suspended service.[^cite_note-58] All state parks in [New Jersey](21648.md) were closed on January 24 at 5\:00 p.m.[^cite_note-59] [SEPTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEPTA) also began suspending service on January 25 at 2\:00 p.m.[^cite_note-philly-60]

States of emergencies were also declared by [Maryland](18858.md) governor [Wes Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wes_Moore),[^cite_note-61] [New Jersey](21648.md) governor [Mikie Sherrill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikie_Sherrill),[^cite_note-62] [Pennsylvania](23332.md) governor [Josh Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Shapiro),[^cite_note-63] [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware) governor [Matt Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Meyer),[^cite_note-64] [West Virginia](32905.md) governor [Patrick Morrisey](37898732.md),[^cite_note-65] and Washington, D.C. mayor [Muriel Bowser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muriel_Bowser).[^cite_note-66]

#### Midwest

[Indiana](21883857.md) governor [Mike Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Braun) declared a state of emergency and a statewide disaster declaration on January 24.[^cite_note-67] The declaration came after heavy snow accumulation, with some reports indicating 1–4 inches (2.5–10.2 cm) of snowfall accumulation on the evening of January 24.[^cite_note-\:1-68] The declaration was also issued in anticipation of forecasts released earlier on January 24 projecting 12–18 inches (30–46 cm) of snowfall accumulation in the state from January 24 to January 25, primarily in the southern portions of the state.[^cite_note-69]

In [Iowa](26810748.md), cold temperatures required schools to be cancelled on January 23 and delayed on January 26.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

[Missouri](19571.md) governor [Mike Kehoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Kehoe) issued a state of emergency and activated the [state's National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_National_Guard) as well as its Emergency Operations Center.[^cite_note-73] [Kansas](16716.md) governor [Laura Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Kelly) declared a state of disaster emergency on the morning of January 23.[^cite_note-74]

#### Ohio Valley

[Tennessee](30395.md) governor [Bill Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Lee_\(Tennessee_politician\)) declared a state of emergency for the entirety of Tennessee on January 22.[^cite_note-75] A [Memphis Grizzlies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis_Grizzlies) game against the [Denver Nuggets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Nuggets) was postponed.[^cite_note-76] The [Ohio Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Department_of_Transportation) mobilized 1,500 snowplows.[^cite_note-77] [Kentucky](16846.md) governor [Andy Beshear](44388893.md) declared a state of emergency on January 23.[^cite_note-78] Judge-Executive Tim Hutchins declared a state of emergency for [Nelson County, Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_County,_Kentucky).[^cite_note-79] [Ohio](22199.md) governor [Mike DeWine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_DeWine) declared a state of emergency on January 24 and suspended normal state purchasing requirements to procure necessary resources.[^cite_note-80] Across Ohio and parts of [Northern Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Kentucky), numerous snow emergencies were issued.[^cite_note-81]

#### New England

[Connecticut](6466.md) governor [Ned Lamont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ned_Lamont) declared a state of emergency on the night of January 24 and scheduled it to take effect on January 25 at noon. Concurrent with the emergency declaration was a statewide travel ban for all semi trucks, RVs, tankers, and vehicles towing trailers. The truck ban was lifted on January 26 at 6\:30 a.m. EST.[^cite_note-82] [Tweed New Haven Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweed_New_Haven_Airport) was shut down in result of the storm.[^cite_note-83]

Boston Mayor [Michelle Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Wu) declared a snow emergency on January 24. Boston Public Schools suspended classes for January 26.[^cite_note-84]

### United States



[Henry Hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Hub) [natural gas prices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_prices) during the storm

By the afternoon of January 24, over 135,000 power outages were reported.[^cite_note-guardian-119] Almost 700,000 were without power by the morning of January 25.[^cite_note-120] By mid-day, that number was over 1 million, primarily in Texas, Louisiana, the [Mississippi Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_Delta), and the southern [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River) basin.[^cite_note-auto1-121] That number still rested around 700,000 by mid-day on January 26.[^cite_note-122] [Meteorologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteorologist) from the [National Weather Service](286989.md) estimated that there were around 300 million people under winter precipitation and/or cold warnings or advisories.[^cite_note-123] By mid-morning of January 26, over half of the contiguous United States was covered by snow.[^cite_note-124]

January 25 was set to go down as one of the days with the most weather-related flight cancellations in US history, with more than 10,000 cancellations by midday.[^cite_note-125] With over 11,000 by end of day, it became the worst day for cancellations since March 30, 2020, at the height of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-\:3-127] A total of over 20,000 flights had been cancelled from January 23–26.[^cite_note-\:3-127] Dozens of [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) trains were also cancelled, with some routes being impacted up to a week after the storm had passed.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

#### South

Over 1,000 flights were cancelled at [Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartsfield%E2%80%93Jackson_Atlanta_International_Airport) in [Atlanta, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta,_Georgia) for January 25.[^cite_note-152] Significant ice accumulations were reported throughout the state, with a peak of 0.60 in (15 mm) of ice in [Toccoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toccoa,_Georgia).[^cite_note-153]

Over 400 traffic accidents were reported in [North Carolina](21650.md) during the storm, with portions of [I-26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_26_in_North_Carolina) and [I-85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_85_in_North_Carolina) closing.[^cite_note-171] In [Buncombe County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buncombe_County,_North_Carolina), a 31-year-old man died from hypothermia-related causes, while two people died in [South Carolina](27956.md), in [Greenwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwood_County,_South_Carolina) and [Laurens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurens_County,_South_Carolina) counties, respectively, from the extreme cold.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174]

The city of [Nashville](22018.md), Tennessee, set a new precipitation record on January 24, with 1.92 in (49 mm) of precipitation. Some of that was freezing rain, leading to the worst ice storm in decades for the region.[^cite_note-175] Additionally, [Nashville Electric Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Electric_Service) recorded the highest number of outages at one time in its history, peaking at 230,000. The state as a whole still recorded more than 250,000 outages by January 26, the highest number of any state by that time.[^cite_note-176] A total of 29 fatalities was confirmed by the [Tennessee Department of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Department_of_Health).[^cite_note-\:15-91] A driver died in a single-vehicle crash in [Dyersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyersburg,_Tennessee).[^cite_note-177] A [Brownsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownsville,_Tennessee) man suffered a heart attack while shoveling snow. Two other individuals died amid the storm, one an 11-year-old in [Crockett County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crockett_County,_Tennessee), the other in [Obion County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obion_County,_Tennessee).[^cite_note-178] There was a total of two fatalities in Obion County. Six fatalities occurred in [Shelby County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelby_County,_Tennessee). [Davidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidson_County,_Tennessee) and [Knox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knox_County,_Tennessee) counties both had three fatalities. [Fayette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayette_County,_Kentucky), [Hardin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardin_County,_Tennessee), [Hawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawkins_County,_Tennessee), [Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson_County,_Tennessee), and [Hickman](91728.md) counties had two fatalities each. [Cheatham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheatham_County,_Tennessee), [Hancock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hancock_County,_Tennessee), and [Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_County,_Tennessee) had one fatality each.[^cite_note-\:15-91] 18 others were injured in more than 160 additional weather-related crashes across Tennessee.[^cite_note-179] As many as 300,000 people were estimated to have lost power as a result of damaging ice accumulation.[^cite_note-CNN_impacts-180] Numerous other accidents were reported; officials stated that residents should stay off the roads unless absolutely necessary.[^cite_note-CNN_impacts-180]

The storm affected at least nine radio stations across the country, most in the South, particularly in Georgia and Mississippi, though specific affected stations were not noted. The [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) activated the Disaster Information Reporting System (DIRS), with the FCC encouraging radio stations to report their status online through the system. As of January 26, 2026, no TV stations were affected. The FCC granted [special temporary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_temporary_authority) for [Citizens Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens_Band) users, relaxing rules for those frequencies.[^cite_note-181]

#### Northeast and Mid-Atlantic



Snow at 1\:41 p.m. on January 25 on Crooke Ave, [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn)

By 6 a.m. on January 25, 177 car crashes were reported during the storm in [Virginia](32432.md), 14 of those resulted in injuries.[^cite_note-\:22-204] Two people were killed in accidents in [Fairfax](91327.md) and [Pittsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsylvania_County,_Virginia) counties, but weather was reportedly not a factor in either.[^cite_note-\:22-204][^cite_note-\:24-205] There was a fatality from a weather-related crash in [Frederick County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_County,_Virginia).[^cite_note-\:23-104] The number of crashes rose to 440 by January 26 at 4 a.m.[^cite_note-206] [Dulles International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulles_International_Airport) recorded 7 in (18 cm) of snow.[^cite_note-207] The snowy conditions also forced the express lanes on [I-95](4290790.md) and [I-395](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_395_\(Virginia%E2%80%93District_of_Columbia\)) to close.[^cite_note-208] [Virginia State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_State_Police) reported a total of 506 crashes and 38 injuries.[^cite_note-\:24-205] There were three weather-related fatalities in Fairfax County.[^cite_note-\:21-103]

On January 25, [Maryland State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland_State_Police) responded to a rollover crash on [I-695](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_695_\(Maryland\)) in [Baltimore County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_County,_Maryland).[^cite_note-217] The [Baltimore/Washington International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore/Washington_International_Airport) in [Maryland](18858.md) saw 11.3 in (29 cm) of snow while [Clarksburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarksburg,_Maryland) saw 11.8 in (30 cm) by 9 a.m. on January 26.[^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219] All state offices were closed that day as well.[^cite_note-220] A private snowplow struck a man just before 1 p.m. in [Anne Arundel County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Arundel_County,_Maryland), causing non-life-threatening injuries.[^cite_note-221] In [Washington D.C.](108956.md), the district picked up 6.9 in (18 cm) of snow and ice accumulation.[^cite_note-222] As a result, federal offices were closed on January 26.[^cite_note-223] All flights were grounded out of [Reagan National Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagan_National_Airport) on January 25, resuming the following day.[^cite_note-224] The snowstorm also forced classes to be cancelled in Washington D.C. from January 26–28, and school openings were delayed on January 29–30.[^cite_note-225]

&#x20;Meanwhile, [Pittsburgh](25101.md) saw its largest snowfall since the [February 5-6, 2010 North American blizzard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_5-6,_2010_North_American_blizzard), totaling 11.2 in (28 cm) of snow in the city. However, many areas throughout the region received well over a foot, and some areas received over 20 in (51 cm).[^cite_note-226] This came as Pittsburgh’s snowplow fleet was hampered with much of the snowplow fleet needing repaired, causing a state of emergency to be issued the following morning as officials urged drivers to avoid driving if possible, and to stick to the main roads.[^cite_note-227]

On January 25, there were eleven suspected weather-related fatalities reported in [New York](8210131.md), with exact causes not yet disclosed.[^cite_note-11deathsinNY-235][^cite_note-236][^cite_note-guardian-119][^cite_note-\:6-237] By January 31st, the Mayor's Office confirmed that as many as 13 people had died on the streets of New York from [exposure](146879.md), most if not all of whom were [homeless](19394651.md) and/or suffering from [mental illness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_disorder). Mayor [Zohran Mamdani](64618347.md) stated that city outreach workers were continuing to identify, check in on, and offer assistance to potential at-risk individuals,[^cite_note-238] while the city's [Department of Homeless Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Department_of_Homeless_Services) said that "no one who is homeless and seeking shelter in New York City during a code blue will be denied."[^cite_note-239] A 60-year-old retired [New York State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Police) officer died while attempting to shovel snow near a church in [Floral Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floral_Park,_New_York).[^cite_note-\:6-237] [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park) reported a daily record of snow, at 11.4 in (29 cm).[^cite_note-\:6-237] Nearby, [Torrington, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrington,_Connecticut), picked up 16.5 in (42 cm) of snow.[^cite_note-240] [Bridgeport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeport,_Connecticut) had its seventh snowiest day on record, dating back to 1948.[^cite_note-241] Public libraries in the city were said to remain closed on January 26.[^cite_note-CNN_impacts-180] Numerous other attractions also remained closed.[^cite_note-242] The [Citi Bike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citi_Bike) service shut down on at noon on January 25 in [New York City](645042.md) due to the hazardous weather. It was reopened at 10 a.m. on January 26.[^cite_note-CNN_impacts-180][^cite_note-243] Several cities in [Upstate New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upstate_New_York), such as [Albany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany,_New_York), [Binghamton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binghamton,_New_York) and [Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York) had record snowfall for January 25. The heavy snow led to travel bans for both [Ulster County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_County,_New_York) and [Duchess County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchess_County,_New_York).[^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245]



A [pedestrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedestrian) wearing [snowshoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowshoes) crosses [First Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)) in [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan).

A snowplow truck struck and killed a woman in [Norwood, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwood,_Massachusetts), while walking with her husband on January 25.[^cite_note-\:4-139][^cite_note-\:5-246] [I-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Turnpike)'s speed limit was reduced to 40 mph (64 km/h) on the afternoon.[^cite_note-\:12-247] By 8 p.m. [Massachusetts State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_State_Police) responded to 149 crashes as well as 234 disabled vehicles. Seven were injured due to the crashes. 493 flights arriving and departing [Logan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_International_Airport) were cancelled by 10\:30 p.m. on January 25. On January 26, over 500 flights were cancelled. Many areas received over 20 in (51 cm) of snow by early afternoon, with 22 in (56 cm) in [Sterling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling,_Massachusetts). By 2 p.m. on January 26, 18.9 in (48 cm) of snow had been recorded at Logan International. The last time [Boston](24437894.md) saw a winter storm of 1 foot (30 cm) or more was [January 29, 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2022_North_American_blizzard). Logan International Airport would receive a total of 23.2 in (59 cm) of snow, the eighth highest snow accumulation on record for Boston. [Gloucester](116744.md) saw 27 in (69 cm) of snow.[^cite_note-\:5-246] Between January 25 and January 26 at 4 p.m., [Massachusetts State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_State_Police) responded 222 crashes, 12 of which had injuries. The department also reported 402 disabled vehicles, 13 motorist assists, seven vehicle fires, and four abandoned vehicles.[^cite_note-\:12-247] The [Red Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Line_\(MBTA\)) had delays on January 28 and January 29 due to the effects of the storm. The [Commuter Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBTA_Commuter_Rail) had delays on January 29 as well as cancellations from January 27–29.[^cite_note-248]

Further north, nearly 2 ft (61 cm) of snow fell in the [New Hampshire Seacoast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seacoast_Region_\(New_Hampshire\)).[^cite_note-249] Between 1 p.m. on January 25 and 9 p.m. on January 26, [New Hampshire State Troopers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hampshire_State_Trooper) responded to 46 crashes, seven causing injuries, as well as assisted 77 that went off the road and roughly 38 additional motorists. In total, they responded to over 160 weather-related calls. The state police also reduced speed limits on several interstates and two turnpikes.[^cite_note-\:12-247] The [Patrick Leahy Burlington International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Leahy_Burlington_International_Airport) in [Vermont](32578.md) saw four flight cancellations by the night of January 26.[^cite_note-250]

Over 20 in (51 cm) of snow was reported in [Kittery, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kittery,_Maine).[^cite_note-251] Further north in the state, a [Bombardier Challenger 650](937178.md) carrying six people [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bangor_Bombardier_Challenger_650_crash) in the process of takeoff at [Bangor International Airport](1682920.md) near [Bangor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor,_Maine) during winter weather conditions caused by the snowstorm, killing all occupants. The cause of the accident is currently unknown.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253][^cite_note-254]

### Canada

#### Central

Snow began in southern [Ontario](22218.md) on January 25. Over 560 flights were cancelled by 3pm at [Toronto Pearson International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Pearson_International_Airport), which reported its largest single-day snowfall since records began in 1937, receiving a total of 46 cm (18 in) and a total of 61 cm (24 in) of snow accumulated in [Downtown Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Toronto).[^cite_note-255] Thirty-three flights were cancelled at the [Billy Bishop Toronto City Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Bishop_Toronto_City_Airport). A potent [lake-enhanced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake-effect_snow) snow band stalled over much of Toronto for several hours, prompting [Toronto](64646.md) to activate its major snow response plan for the second time in 2026 just prior to 5pm. The [Ontario Provincial Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Provincial_Police) responded to around 100 car crashes over 24 hours.[^cite_note-256] [A Bombardier Global 6500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Global_Express) also skidded off the taxiway after landing at [Pearson Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Pearson_International_Airport); no injuries were reported.[^cite_note-257] By 5\:30 p.m., roughly 50 centimetres (20 in) fell in parts of the core of Toronto.[^cite_note-258] This caused all [Greater Toronto Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Toronto_Area) Schools and Boards to close. Many post-secondary institutions also closed notably, [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto), [York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_University), and [Toronto Metropolitan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Metropolitan_University).[^cite_note-259] In [Waterloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterloo,_Ontario), snow and road conditions contributed to two accidents on the [Highway 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Highway_7) ramp on January 26. The driver of the former had minor injuries while the latter resulted in a fatality.[^cite_note-\:20-118] Schools and buses resumed operations on January 27.[^cite_note-260]

Further east, 14 cm (5.5 in) of snow was recorded at both [Ottawa](22219.md) and [Montreal](7954681.md).[^cite_note-\:7-261][^cite_note-\:8-262] 21 flights leaving Ottawa were cancelled by 6am on January 26 and 154 accidents occurred from the morning to 3pm EST. [Via Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Rail) saw some cancellations and delays.[^cite_note-\:7-261] 3,500 Hydro-Quebec customers were without power, primarily on the [Island of Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_of_Montreal).[^cite_note-\:8-262] Two women died in Montreal from the extreme cold during a power outage that occurred at the same time.[^cite_note-Global_News_Quebec-109][^cite_note-Montreal-110]

#### Atlantic

Snow fell beginning late on January 25 and until the morning of January 27 across the [Maritimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Maritimes). Southern [New Brunswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Brunswick) saw 10–25 cm (3.9–9.8 in) of snow, with estimates of 25–40 cm (9.8–15.7 in) in [Grand Manan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Manan).[^cite_note-\:9-27] Most school closures occurred in southeastern New Brunswick. At 12\:45 p.m., 45 customers were affected by three outages.[^cite_note-\:10-263]

Northern and western parts of [Nova Scotia](21184.md) saw 30–40 cm (12–16 in) of snow. The [Halifax area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia) received 21–30 cm (8.3–11.8 in) while [Sluice Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sluice_Point,_Nova_Scotia) recorded 42 cm (17 in) of snow.[^cite_note-\:9-27] Most schools in Nova Scotia were closed on January 26. Government offices were closed until noon of January 27. Many universities had delayed openings. Most community colleges were closed or had delayed openings.[^cite_note-\:11-264] All schools were closed on January 27 as well.[^cite_note-\:10-263] Some flights out of the [Halifax Stanfield Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax_Stanfield_International_Airport) were delayed.[^cite_note-\:11-264] Bad airfield conditions led the airport to temporarily suspend operations between 8am and 11\:15 a.m.. Ten outages were active at 12\:15 p.m. impacting 67 customers.[^cite_note-\:10-263]

[Prince Edward Island](23071.md) reported close to 15 cm (5.9 in) of snow. [Saint Georges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George's_Parish,_Prince_Edward_Island) saw 27 cm (11 in) of snow.[^cite_note-\:9-27] All schools were cancelled on January 27. Offices opened at 1 p.m. in the [West Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Prince_\(locality\)) area while offices elsewhere remained closed for January 27. Some health services were suspended or delayed. Two customers were without power in Prince Edward Island at 12\:45 p.m. [Marine Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Atlantic) cancelled two sailing on January 27. A [Bay Ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Ferries) departure was delayed.[^cite_note-\:10-263]

&#x20;In [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_\(island\)), schools were closed in the southeastern part of the [Avalon Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avalon_Peninsula) while schools in the northeast closed two hours early on January 27. At 12\:30 p.m., courts in [St. John's](70581.md) closed. [Memorial University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_University_of_Newfoundland) campuses closed at 1 p.m. in St. John's. Facilities in [Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise,_Newfoundland_and_Labrador) were suspended at 2 p.m. At 4\:30 p.m., recreation facilities in St. John's closed. A tractor trailer jack-knifed in [Appleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appleton,_Newfoundland_and_Labrador).[^cite_note-\:14-30]

### Mexico

Winds in Tamaulipas reached up to 56 miles per hour (90 km/h). Temperatures in [Nuevo Laredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuevo_Laredo) and [Guerrero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrero,_Tamaulipas) reached 25 °F (−4 °C).[^cite_note-\:2-89] In [Chihuahua](23962301.md), wind gusts reached 72 km/h (45 mph), forcing closures.[^cite_note-265]

White-out rains and strong winds occurred in the [Yucatán Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucat%C3%A1n_Peninsula) on January 26. 25–50 mm (0.98–1.97 in) of rain fell in parts of [Cancun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canc%C3%BAn), resulting in flooding. The passage of the cold front caused the temperatures in the city dropped from 29 °C (84 °F) to 24 °C (75 °F). Arctic air from the storm caused the temperature to further drop from 25 °C (77 °F) at midday to 19 °C (66 °F) at night.[^cite_note-266] At least 98 flights to [Cancun International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canc%C3%BAn_International_Airport) were cancelled.[^cite_note-267]
