**Malaysia**[^cite_note-19] is a country in [Southeast Asia](28741.md). A [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation) [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy), it consists of [13 states and three federal territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_federal_territories_of_Malaysia), separated by the [South China Sea](74209.md) into two regions: [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia) on [Mainland Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Southeast_Asia) and [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia) on the island of [Borneo](4518.md). Peninsular Malaysia shares land and [maritime borders](29613061.md) with [Thailand](30128.md), as well as maritime borders with [Singapore](27318.md), [Vietnam](202354.md), and [Indonesia](14579.md); East Malaysia shares land borders with [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei) and Indonesia, and maritime borders with the [Philippines](23440.md) and Vietnam. [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md) is the country's national capital, [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Malaysia_by_population), and the seat of the [legislative branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Malaysia) of the [federal government](9660028.md), while [Putrajaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putrajaya) is the federal administrative capital, representing the seat of both the [executive branch](9660028.md#Executive) and the [judicial branch](944219.md) of the federal government. With a population of over 34 million, it is the world's [43rd-most populous country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population).

The country has [its origins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Malaysia) in the [Malay kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Kingdom_\(disambiguation\)), which, from the 18th century on, became subject to the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), along with the British [Straits Settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_Settlements) protectorate. During [World War II](32927.md), [British Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya), along with other nearby British and American colonies, was occupied by the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan). Following three years of occupation, Malaya was briefly unified as the [Malayan Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Union) in 1946 until 1948 when it was restructured as the [Federation of Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Malaya). The country achieved independence on [31 August 1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(Malaysia\)). On [16 September 1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Day), independent Malaya united with the then [British crown colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_colony) of [North Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_North_Borneo), [Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_Sarawak), and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Singapore) to become Malaysia. In August 1965, Singapore was [separated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_in_Malaysia) from the federation and became an [independent country](35695188.md).

Malaysia is [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical) and is one of 17 [megadiverse countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse_countries); it is home to numerous [endemic species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Malaysia). The country is [multiethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Malaysia) and [multicultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Malaysia), which has a significant effect on its politics. About half the population is ethnically [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Malays), with minorities of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Chinese), [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indian), and [indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Asal). The [Bumiputra policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumiputera_\(Malaysia\)) grants the Malay majority a constitutionally enshrined privileged status over Malaysian citizens who are Chinese or Indians via [affirmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action) measures. The official language is [Malaysian Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Malay), a standard form of the [Malay language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language). [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_English) remains an active second language. While recognising [Islam](6037917.md) as the [official religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_religion), the constitution grants [freedom of religion](46006.md) to non-Muslims though conversion out of Islam is forbidden while conversion to it is allowed. The government is modelled on the [Westminster parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_parliamentary_system), and the legal system is based on [common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law). The [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) is an [elected monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elected_monarch), chosen among the [nine state rulers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchies_of_Malaysia) every five years. The [head of government](37570.md) is the [prime minister](496731.md).

[The country's economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Malaysia) was once heavily reliant on agriculture and primary commodities but is now driven by the services and manufacturing sectors. Malaysia ranks highly in [peacefulness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Peace_Index) and [passport strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henley_Passport_Index) and has a [newly industrialised economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newly_industrialised_economy) that is relatively open and state-oriented. The country is a founding member of the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md) (OIC), the [East Asia Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia_Summit) (EAS), and the [Association of Southeast Asian Nations](106539.md) (ASEAN), as well as a member of the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement) (NAM), the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations), and the [Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Economic_Cooperation) (APEC).

## Etymology

The name [*Malaysia*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Malaysia) is a combination of the word [*Malays*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Malays) and the Latin-Greek suffix [*-ia*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/-ia)/[-ία](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/-%CE%AF%CE%B1),[^cite_note-\:3-20] which can be translated as 'land of the Malays'.[^cite_note-CIA_Fact_Book-21] Similar-sounding variants have also appeared in accounts older than the 11th century, as [toponyms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponym) for areas in Sumatra or referring to a larger region around the Strait of Malacca.[^cite_note-Barnard-22] The Sanskrit text *[Vayu Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vayu_Purana)*, thought to have been in existence since the first millennium CE, mentioned a land named 'Malayadvipa', which was identified by certain scholars as the modern [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Other notable accounts are by Ptolemy's 2nd-century *[Geographia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographia)*, which used the name *Malayu Kulon* for the west coast of [Golden Chersonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Chersonese), and the 7th-century [Yijing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yijing_\(monk\))'s account of *Malayu*.[^cite_note-Barnard-22]

At some point, the [Melayu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melayu_Kingdom) took its name from the *Sungai Melayu*.[^cite_note-SejarahBahasaMelayu-28][^cite_note-29] *Melayu* then became associated with [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya),[^cite_note-Barnard-22] and remained associated with various parts of Sumatra, especially Palembang, where the founder of the [Malacca Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate) is thought to have come from.[^cite_note-Andaya-30] It is only thought to have developed into an [ethnonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnonym) as Malacca became a regional power in the 15th century. [Islamisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamisation) established an [ethnoreligious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnoreligious) identity in Malacca, with the term *Melayu* beginning to appear as interchangeable with *Melakans*. It may have specifically referred to local Malay speakers who were loyal to the Malaccan Sultan. The initial Portuguese use of *Malayos* reflected this, referring only to the ruling people of Malacca. The prominence of traders from Malacca led *Melayu* to be associated with Muslim traders, and from there became associated with the wider cultural and linguistic group.[^cite_note-Barnard-22] Malacca and later [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor_Sultanate) claimed they were the centre of Malay culture, a position supported by the British which led to the term *Malay* becoming more usually linked to the Malay peninsula rather than Sumatra.[^cite_note-Andaya-30]

Before the onset of European colonisation, the Malay Peninsula was known natively as *[Tanah Melayu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanah_Melayu)* ('Malay Land').[^cite_note-31] Under a racial classification created by German scholar [Johann Friedrich Blumenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Friedrich_Blumenbach), the natives of [maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia) were grouped into a single category, the [Malay race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_race).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] Following the expedition of French navigator [Jules Dumont d'Urville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Dumont_d'Urville) to [Oceania](22621.md) in 1826, he later proposed the terms of *Malaysia*, *[Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronesia)*, and *[Melanesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesia)* to the *[Société de Géographie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9_de_G%C3%A9ographie)* in 1831, distinguishing these Pacific cultures and island groups from the existing term *[Polynesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia)*. Dumont d'Urville described Malaysia as "an area commonly known as the East Indies".[^cite_note-34]

In 1850, the English ethnologist [George Samuel Windsor Earl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Samuel_Windsor_Earl), writing in the *Journal of the Indian Archipelago and Eastern Asia*, proposed naming the islands of Southeast Asia as "Melayunesia" or "Indunesia", favouring the former.[^cite_note-35] The name Malaysia gained some use to label what is now the Malay Archipelago.[^cite_note-36] In modern terminology, *Malay* remains the name of an [ethnoreligious group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnoreligious_group) of [Austronesian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_people) predominantly inhabiting the Malay Peninsula and portions of the adjacent islands of [Southeast Asia](28741.md), including the east coast of Sumatra, the coast of [Borneo](4518.md), and smaller islands that lie between these areas.[^cite_note-37]

The state that gained independence from the United Kingdom in 1957 took the name *the [Federation of Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Malaya)*, chosen in preference to other potential names such as *Malaysia*[^cite_note-MALAYA'S_FUTURE_NAME-38] and *[Langkasuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkasuka)*,[^cite_note-MALAYA'S_FUTURE_NAME-38] after the historic kingdom located at the upper section of the Malay Peninsula in the first millennium CE.[^cite_note-Mapping-39][^cite_note-40]

In 1963, the name *Malaysia* was adopted when the existing states of the Federation of Malaya, plus Singapore, North Borneo, and Sarawak, formed a new federation.[^cite_note-Bound_for_Glory-41][^cite_note-43] One theory posits the name was chosen so that *si* represented the inclusion of Singapore, North Borneo, and Sarawak.[^cite_note-Bound_for_Glory-41] Politicians in the Philippines contemplated renaming their state Malaysia before the modern country took the name.[^cite_note-44]

## History

Main article: [History of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Malaysia)

Map showing the extent of the Malacca Sultanate, covering much of the Malay Peninsula and some of Sumatra

The [Malacca Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate) played a major role in spreading Islam throughout the Malay Archipelago.

Evidence of modern human habitation in Malaysia dates back 40,000 years.[^cite_note-45] In the Malay Peninsula, the first inhabitants are thought to be [Negritos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negritos).[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] Areas of Malaysia participated in the Maritime Jade Road between 2000 BC to 1000 AD.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Traders and settlers from [India](14533.md) and [China](5405.md) arrived as early as the first century AD, establishing trading ports and coastal towns in the second and third centuries. Their presence resulted in strong Indian and Chinese influences on the local cultures, and the people of the Malay Peninsula adopted the religions of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) and [Buddhism](3267529.md). [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) inscriptions appear as early as the fourth or fifth century.[^cite_note-52] The Kingdom of [Langkasuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langkasuka) arose around the second century in the northern area of the Malay Peninsula, lasting until about the 15th century.[^cite_note-Mapping-39] Between the 7th and 13th centuries, much of the southern Malay Peninsula was part of the maritime [Srivijayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya) empire. By the 13th and the 14th century, the [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit) empire had successfully wrested control over most of the peninsula and the Malay Archipelago from Srivijaya.[^cite_note-53] In the early 15th century, [Parameswara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parameswara_\(king\)), a runaway king of the former [Kingdom of Singapura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Singapura) linked to the old Srivijayan court, founded the [Malacca Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate).[^cite_note-54] The spread of Islam increased following Parameswara's conversion to that religion. Malacca was an important commercial centre during this time, attracting trade from around the region.[^cite_note-state.gov-55]

Dutch fleet vs Portuguese armada

The Dutch fleet battling with the Portuguese armada as part of the [Dutch–Portuguese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch%E2%80%93Portuguese_War) in 1606 to gain control of [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_City)

In 1511, [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca) was conquered by [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Malacca),[^cite_note-state.gov-55] after which it was taken by the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Malacca) in 1641. In 1786, the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya) established a presence in Malaya, when the Sultan of Kedah leased [Penang Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang_Island) to the British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company). The British obtained the town of Singapore in 1819,[^cite_note-56] and in 1824 took control of Malacca following the [Anglo-Dutch Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Dutch_Treaty_of_1824). By 1826, the British directly controlled Penang, Malacca, Singapore, and the island of [Labuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_Labuan), which they established as the crown colony of the [Straits Settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_Settlements). By the 20th century, the states of [Pahang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahang), [Selangor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selangor), [Perak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perak), and [Negeri Sembilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negeri_Sembilan), known together as the [Federated Malay States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_Malay_States), had British [residents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resident_\(title\)) appointed to advise the Malay rulers, to whom the rulers were bound to defer by treaty.[^cite_note-57] The remaining five states on the peninsula, known as the [Unfederated Malay States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfederated_Malay_States), while not directly under British rule, also accepted British advisers around the turn of the 20th century. Development on the peninsula and Borneo were generally separate until the 19th century. Under British rule the [immigration](4599312.md) of Chinese and Indians to serve as labourers was encouraged.[^cite_note-58] The area that is now [Sabah](28678.md) came under British control as [North Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo) when both the [Sultan of Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Brunei) and the [Sultan of Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Sulu) transferred their respective territorial rights of ownership, between 1877 and 1878.[^cite_note-59] In 1842, Sarawak was ceded by the Sultan of Brunei to [James Brooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Brooke), whose successors ruled as the [White Rajahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Rajahs) over an independent [kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raj_of_Sarawak) until 1946, when it became a [crown colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_Sarawak).[^cite_note-60]

The [Klang War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klang_War) (1867–1874) began as a dispute between Raja Abdullah and Raja Mahadi over the Klang chieftaincy, with Mahadi rejecting Abdullah's appointment by Sultan [Abdul Samad of Selangor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Samad_of_Selangor). The conflict drew in rival Chinese [secret societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_societies)—the [Ghee Hin Kongsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee_Hin_Kongsi) largely backing Mahadi and the [Hai San Secret Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hai_San_Secret_Society) supporting Abdullah—as well as Bugis-descended and Malay chiefs who viewed Mahadi as more legitimate. Fighting spread after Mahadi captured the Klang Fort and Abdullah fled to Malacca, disrupting tin production, trade routes, and British commercial interests in the Klang Valley. At the same time, the neighbouring [Larut Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larut_Wars) (1861–1874) between the Hai San and Ghee Hin in Perak similarly destabilised tin-rich districts and alarmed British merchants. Appeals from Malay chiefs for mediation combined with British concerns over regional disorder paved the way for formal intervention, culminating in the [Pangkor Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangkor_Treaty) of 1874 which established a system of British residents in the Malay states.[^cite_note-Azmi2012-61] As [Frank Swettenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Swettenham) later wrote, "It is that though the circumstances demanded intervention, we came into the Malay States at the invitation of the Malay Rulers, to teach them a better form of administration."[^cite_note-AzmiPerak-62][^cite_note-63]

In the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), the [Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army) [invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_Malaya) and occupied [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_Malaya), [North Borneo, Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_British_Borneo), and [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_Singapore) for over three years.[^cite_note-64] During this time, ethnic tensions were raised and nationalism grew.[^cite_note-65] Popular support for independence increased after Malaya was reconquered by Allied forces.[^cite_note-66] Post-war British plans to unite the administration of Malaya under a single crown colony called the [Malayan Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Union) met with strong opposition from the [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)), who opposed the weakening of the Malay rulers and the granting of citizenship to the [ethnic Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Chinese). The Malayan Union, established in 1946, and consisting of all the [British possessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_possessions) in the Malay Peninsula except for [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Singapore), was quickly dissolved and replaced on 1 February 1948 by the [Federation of Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Malaya), which restored the autonomy of the rulers of the Malay states under British protection.[^cite_note-67]



[Malayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Malaya) police officers questioning an [ethnic Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Malaysian) civilian during the [Malayan Emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Emergency) in 1949

During this time, the mostly ethnically Chinese rebels under the leadership of the [Malayan Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Communist_Party) launched guerrilla operations designed to force the British out of Malaya. The [Malayan Emergency (1948–1960)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Emergency) involved a long anti-insurgency campaign by [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) troops in Malaya.[^cite_note-68] On 31 August 1957, [Malaya became an independent member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_Declaration_of_Independence) of the Commonwealth of Nations.[^cite_note-69] Subsequently, a comprehensive plan was devised to unite Malaya with the crown colonies of North Borneo (known as [Sabah](28678.md) upon joining), [Sarawak](28258.md), and [Singapore](27318.md). The envisioned federation was originally intended to take place on 31 August 1963, to coincide with the commemoration of Malayan independence. However, due to the necessity of conducting a survey on the level of support for the federation in Sabah and Sarawak by the [United Nations](31769.md), as requested by opponents of the federation such as [Indonesia](14579.md)'s [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno) and the [Sarawak United Peoples' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawak_United_Peoples'_Party), the date of the federation was postponed until 16 September 1963.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

The federation brought heightened tensions including a [conflict with Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia%E2%80%93Malaysia_confrontation) as well as continual conflicts against the Communists in Borneo and the Malay Peninsula, which escalated to the [Sarawak Communist Insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawak_Communist_Insurgency) and [Second Malayan Emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Malayan_Emergency) together with several other issues such as the [cross-border attacks into Sabah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_border_attacks_in_Sabah) by [Moro pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_pirates) from the southern islands of the Philippines, Singapore [being separated from the Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Singapore_\(Malaysia\)#Separation) in 1965,[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-TIME_Art-74] and racial strife. This strife culminated in the [13 May race riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13_May_Incident) in 1969.[^cite_note-Race_war-75] After the riots, the controversial [New Economic Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_New_Economic_Policy) was launched by Prime Minister [Tun Abdul Razak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tun_Abdul_Razak), trying to increase the share of the economy held by the *[bumiputera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumiputera_\(Malaysia\))*.[^cite_note-New_Economic_Policy-76] Under Prime Minister [Mahathir Mohamad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahathir_Mohamad) there was a period of rapid economic growth and urbanisation beginning in the 1980s. The economy shifted from being agriculturally based to one based on manufacturing and industry. Numerous mega-projects were completed, such as the [Petronas Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petronas_Towers), the [North–South Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North%E2%80%93South_Expressway_\(Malaysia\)), the [Multimedia Super Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimedia_Super_Corridor), and the new federal administrative capital of [Putrajaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putrajaya).[^cite_note-Bound_for_Glory-41][^cite_note-77]

In the late 1990s, the [Asian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_financial_crisis) impacted the country, nearly causing their currency, stock, and property markets to crash; however, they later recovered.[^cite_note-78] The [1MDB scandal](47550643.md) was a major global [corruption](42043879.md) scandal that implicated then-Prime Minister [Najib Razak](523641.md) in 2015.[^cite_note-79] The scandal contributed to the first change in the ruling political party since independence in the [2018 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Malaysian_general_election).[^cite_note-Opposition_scores-80] In the 2020s, the country was gripped by [a political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Malaysian_political_crisis) that coincided with health and [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_in_Malaysia) crises caused by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Malaysia).[^cite_note-81] This was then followed by an earlier [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Malaysian_general_election) in November 2022, which resulted in the first hung parliament in the nation's history.[^cite_note-82] On 24 November 2022, [Anwar Ibrahim](19264815.md) was sworn in as the 10th [Prime Minister of Malaysia](496731.md), leading a [grand coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_coalition#Malaysia) government.[^cite_note-83]

## Government and politics

Malaysia is a [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_monarchy) [constitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy) [elective monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elective_monarchy) and the only federal country in [Southeast Asia](28741.md).[^cite_note-84] The system of government is closely modelled on the [Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system) parliamentary system, a legacy of [British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire).[^cite_note-FITA-85] The head of state is the King, whose official title is the [Yang di-Pertuan Agong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_di-Pertuan_Agong). The King is elected to a five-year term by and from among the nine hereditary [rulers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchies_of_Malaysia) of the [Malay states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_states). The other four states, which have titular [Governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_di-Pertua_Negeri), do not participate in the selection. By informal agreement the position is rotated among the nine,[^cite_note-FITA-85] and has been held by [Ibrahim Iskandar of Johor](8717363.md) since 31 January 2024. The King's role has been largely ceremonial since changes to the [constitution](534791.md) in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_amendments_to_the_Constitution_of_Malaysia), picking ministers and members of the upper house.[^cite_note-dfat-86]

Legislative power is divided between federal and state legislatures. The bicameral federal [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Malaysia) consists of the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house), the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_of_Malaysia) and the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house), the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Malaysia).[^cite_note-87] The 222-member House of Representatives is elected for a maximum term of five years from single-member constituencies. All 70 senators sit for three-year terms; 26 are elected by the 13 state assemblies, and the remaining 44 are appointed by the King upon the Prime Minister's recommendation.[^cite_note-state.gov-55] The parliament follows a multi-party system and the government is elected through a [first-past-the-post](261709.md) system.[^cite_note-state.gov-55][^cite_note-88] Parliamentary [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Malaysia) are held at least once every five years.[^cite_note-state.gov-55] Before 2018, only registered voters aged 21 and above could vote for the members of the House of Representatives and, in most of the states, for the state legislative chamber. Voting is not mandatory.[^cite_note-89] In July 2019, a bill to lower the [voting age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_age) to 18 years old was officially passed.[^cite_note-90]

[Executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power) is vested in the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Malaysia), led by the [Prime Minister](496731.md). The prime minister must be a member of the House of Representatives, who in the opinion of His Majesty the King, commands the support of a majority of members. The Cabinet is chosen from members of both houses of Parliament.[^cite_note-state.gov-55] The Prime Minister is both the [head of cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Malaysia) and the [head of government](37570.md).[^cite_note-dfat-86] As a result of the [2018 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Malaysian_general_election), Malaysia was governed by the [Pakatan Harapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakatan_Harapan) (PH) [political alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_alliance),[^cite_note-Opposition_scores-80] although Prime Minister [Mahathir Mohamad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahathir_Mohamad) resigned amid a [political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Malaysian_political_crisis) in 2020. In March 2020, the [Perikatan Nasional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perikatan_Nasional) (PN) coalition formed under Prime Minister [Muhyiddin Yassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhyiddin_Yassin),[^cite_note-91] before Muhyiddin lost majority support and was replaced by deputy Prime Minister [Ismail Sabri Yaakob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_Sabri_Yaakob), a veteran politician from the [United Malays National Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Malays_National_Organisation) (UMNO), in August 2021.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] As a result of the [2022 Malaysian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Malaysian_general_election), a hung parliament was elected. [Anwar Ibrahim](19264815.md) of the PH coalition was appointed as the new Prime Minister to lead the coalition government of PH, [Barisan Nasional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisan_Nasional), [Gabungan Parti Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabungan_Parti_Sarawak), [Gabungan Rakyat Sabah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabungan_Rakyat_Sabah) and several other political parties and independents. Meanwhile, PN, the only political coalition not in the coalition government, became the Opposition.

[Malaysia's legal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Malaysia) is based on [common law](85441.md).[^cite_note-state.gov-55] Although [the judiciary](944219.md) is theoretically independent, its independence has been called into question and the appointment of judges lacks accountability and transparency.[^cite_note-94] The highest court in the judicial system is the [Federal Court](14222422.md), followed by the [Court of Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Appeal_of_Malaysia) and two [high courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Courts_of_Malaysia), one for Peninsular Malaysia and one for East Malaysia. Malaysia also has a special court to hear cases brought by or against royalty.[^cite_note-criminal_court_system-95]

[Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Malaysia) is a significant force in politics.[^cite_note-state.gov-55] [Affirmative actions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action#Malaysia) such as the [New Economic Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_New_Economic_Policy)[^cite_note-New_Economic_Policy-76] and the [National Development Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Development_Policy) which superseded it, were implemented to advance the standing of the *bumiputera*, consisting of Malays and the indigenous tribes who are considered the original inhabitants of Malaysia, over non-*bumiputera* such as [Malaysian Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Chinese) and [Malaysian Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians).[^cite_note-96] These policies provide preferential treatment to *bumiputera* in employment, education, scholarships, business, and access to cheaper housing and assisted savings. However, it has generated greater interethnic resentment.[^cite_note-97] There is ongoing [debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Malaysia#Secularism) over whether the laws and society of Malaysia should reflect [Islamism](15012.md) or [secularism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism).[^cite_note-98] Islamic criminal laws passed by the [Pan-Malaysian Islamic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Malaysian_Islamic_Party) with the support of UMNO state assemblymen in the state legislative assembly of Kelantan have been unenforced by the federal government on the basis that criminal laws are the responsibility of the federal government.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

After UMNO lost power at the [2018 Malaysian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Malaysian_general_election), Malaysia's ranking increased by 9 places in the 2019 [Democracy Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Index) to 43rd compared to the previous year; the country is classified as a 'flawed democracy'.[^cite_note-102] Malaysia's ranking in the 2020 [Press Freedom Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_Freedom_Index) increased by 22 places to 101st compared to the previous year, making it one of two countries in Southeast Asia without a 'Difficult situation' or 'Very Serious situation' with regards to press freedom.[^cite_note-103] However, it fell 18 places the following year due to the policies of the PN government.[^cite_note-104]

Malaysia is marked at 48th and 62nd place according to the 2021 [Corruption Perceptions Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index), indicating above-average levels of [corruption](42043879.md). [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House) noted Malaysia as "partly free" in its 2018 survey.[^cite_note-105] A lawsuit filed by the [United States Department of Justice](52563.md) alleged that at least \$3.5 billion involving former prime minister [Najib Razak](523641.md) had been stolen from Malaysia's [1MDB](25429123.md) state-owned fund, known as the [1Malaysia Development Berhad scandal](47550643.md).[^cite_note-\:0-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] As of 2025, it is ranked 13th on the [Global Peace Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Peace_Index).[^cite_note-109]

Malaysia is a [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation) of [13 states and three federal territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_and_federal_territories_of_Malaysia).[^cite_note-110] Out of these, 11 states and two federal territories are in [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia), whereas the other two states and one federal territory comprise [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia).

The country has three tiers of government—[federal](9660028.md), [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_governments_of_Malaysia), and local.[^cite_note-\:1-111] Governance of the states is divided between the federal and the state governments, with different powers reserved for each, and the federal government has direct administration of the federal territories.[^cite_note-112] Each state has a unicameral [State Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_legislative_assemblies_of_Malaysia) whose members are elected from single-member constituencies. State governments are led by [Chief Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heads_of_state_governments_of_Malaysia),[^cite_note-state.gov-55] who are state assembly members from the majority party in the assembly. In each of the states with a hereditary ruler, the Chief Minister is normally required to be a [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)), appointed by the ruler upon the recommendation of the [Prime Minister](496731.md).[^cite_note-113] Until [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Malaysian_general_election), state elections were held concurrently with the general election by [convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_convention_\(political_custom\)), except for those in [Sarawak](28258.md).[^cite_note-dfat-86] Following the [2020–2022 political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%932022_Malaysian_political_crisis), only [Pahang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahang), [Perak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perak) and [Perlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perlis) opted to conduct their state elections simultaneously with the general elections.[^cite_note-114]

Positioned below the federal and state governments, [local governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Malaysia) represent the lowest tier of governance in Malaysia.[^cite_note-\:1-111] As of 2024, Malaysia is divided between 19 cities, 40 municipalities, 91 district-level councils, and four statutory agencies.[^cite_note-115] While the federal constitution assigns local authorities to the exclusive jurisdiction of state governments,[^cite_note-116] in practice, the [federal Ministry of Housing and Local Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Housing_and_Local_Government_\(Malaysia\)) oversees the regulation of local laws and policies.[^cite_note-\:1-111][^cite_note-117] [Mayors](101146.md#Malaysia) (or presidents for municipal and district councils) and [councillors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Councillor) are appointed by the respective state governments, or in the case of the federal territories, by the federal government.[^cite_note-\:2-118]

Federal laws assign land matters, including the delineation of [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_in_Malaysia), to the purview of state governments.[^cite_note-119] Except Perlis and the federal territories, each state is divided into districts, which are further subdivided into [mukims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukim). In [Sabah](28678.md) and Sarawak, districts are grouped into divisions.[^cite_note-120] In contrast to local governments that manage municipal administration and infrastructure development,[^cite_note-121] districts are solely utilised for land taxation.[^cite_note-122]

The 13 states are based on historical Malay kingdoms, and 9 of the 11 Peninsular states, known as the [Malay states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_states), retain their royal families. [The King](376412.md) is elected by and from [the nine rulers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_of_Rulers) to serve a five-year term.[^cite_note-state.gov-55] This King appoints governors serving a four-year term for the states without monarchies, after consultations with the chief minister of that state. Each state has its written constitution.[^cite_note-Lim_Hong_Hai-123] Sabah and Sarawak have considerably more autonomy than the other states, most notably having separate immigration policies and controls, and unique residency status.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126] Federal intervention in state affairs, lack of development, and disputes over oil [royalties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royalties) have occasionally led to statements about [secession](144732.md) from leaders in several states such as [Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang), [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor), [Kelantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelantan), Sabah and Sarawak, although these have not been followed up and no serious independence movements exist.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130]

### Foreign relations and military

Main articles: [Foreign relations of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Malaysia) and [Malaysian Armed Forces](19115.md)



(L-R) Malaysian Prime Minister [Anwar Ibrahim](19264815.md), US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md), Thai Prime Minister [Anutin Charnvirakul](60867789.md) and Cambodian Prime Minister [Hun Manet](42929019.md) after signing the [Kuala Lumpur Peace Accord](81433775.md), which took place during the [47th ASEAN Summit](81396138.md), 26 October 2025

A founding member of [ASEAN](106539.md)[^cite_note-131] and [OIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OIC),[^cite_note-132] the country participates in many international organisations such as the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN),[^cite_note-133] [APEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APEC),[^cite_note-134] the [D-8 Organization for Economic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-8_Organization_for_Economic_Cooperation),[^cite_note-135] and NAM.[^cite_note-136] It has chaired ASEAN, OIC, and NAM in the past.[^cite_note-state.gov-55] A former [British colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colony), it is also a member of the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations).[^cite_note-137] [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md) was the site of the first [EAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asia_Summit) in 2005.[^cite_note-govtnz-138]

Malaysia's [foreign policy](233404.md) is officially based on the principle of neutrality and maintaining peaceful relations with all countries, regardless of their political system.[^cite_note-kln-139] The government attaches a high priority to the security and stability of [Southeast Asia](28741.md),[^cite_note-govtnz-138] and seeks to further develop relations with other countries in the region. Historically the government has tried to portray Malaysia as a progressive Islamic nation[^cite_note-kln-139] while strengthening relations with other [Islamic states](2646438.md).[^cite_note-govtnz-138] A strong tenet of Malaysia's policy is national sovereignty and right of a country to control its domestic affairs.[^cite_note-dfat-86] Malaysia signed the UN treaty on the [Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons).[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

The [Spratly Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spratly_Islands) are disputed by many states in the area, and [a large portion of the South China Sea](32005536.md) is claimed by [China](5405.md). Unlike its neighbours of [Vietnam](202354.md) and the [Philippines](23440.md), Malaysia historically avoided conflicts with China.[^cite_note-142] However, after the encroachment of Chinese ships in Malaysian territorial waters,[^cite_note-143] and breach of airspace by their military aircraft, Malaysia has become active in condemning China.[^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145] [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei) and Malaysia in 2009 announced an end to claims of each other's land, and committed to resolve issues related to their maritime borders.[^cite_note-146] The Philippines has a [dormant claim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Borneo_dispute) to the eastern part of [Sabah](28678.md).[^cite_note-MYIDPHSG-147] [Singapore](27318.md)'s land reclamation has caused tensions,[^cite_note-148] and minor maritime and land border disputes exist with [Indonesia](14579.md).[^cite_note-MYIDPHSG-147][^cite_note-149]



[Sukhoi Su-30MKM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-30MKM) of the RMAF

The [Malaysian Armed Forces](19115.md) has three branches: the [Malaysian Army](1966311.md), [Royal Malaysian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Malaysian_Navy) and the [Royal Malaysian Air Force](1137609.md). There is no conscription, and the required age for voluntary military service is 18. The military uses 1.5% of the country's GDP, and employs 1.23% of Malaysia's manpower.[^cite_note-150] Malaysian peacekeeping forces of [MALBATT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MALBATT) have contributed to many U.N. peacekeeping missions, such as in [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Operation_in_the_Congo), [Iran–Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_Military_Observer_Group), [Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Transition_Assistance_Group), [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Transitional_Authority_in_Cambodia), [Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Protection_Force), [Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Operation_in_Somalia_II), [Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Interim_Administration_Mission_in_Kosovo), [East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Integrated_Mission_in_East_Timor), and [Lebanon](787766.md).[^cite_note-state.gov-55][^cite_note-151]

The [Five Power Defence Arrangements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Power_Defence_Arrangements) is a regional security initiative that has been in place for almost 40 years. It involves joint military exercises held among Malaysia, Singapore, Australia, New Zealand, and the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-152] Joint exercises and war games have also been held with Brunei,[^cite_note-153] China,[^cite_note-154] India,[^cite_note-155] Indonesia,[^cite_note-156] Japan,[^cite_note-157] and the United States.[^cite_note-158] Malaysia, Philippines, Thailand, and Vietnam have agreed to host joint security force exercises to secure their maritime border and tackle issues such as illegal immigration, [piracy](50715.md), and [smuggling](28645.md).[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] Previously there were fears that extremist militant activities in the Muslim areas of the [southern Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_conflict_in_the_Philippines)[^cite_note-162] and [southern Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Thailand_insurgency)[^cite_note-163] would spill over into Malaysia. Because of this, Malaysia began to increase its border security.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165]

### Human rights

See also: [Human rights in Malaysia](3645990.md), [LGBT rights in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Malaysia), and [Capital punishment in Malaysia](8574786.md)

[Homosexuality](5488304.md) is [illegal in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Malaysia),[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167] and authorities have imposed punishments such as [caning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caning_in_Malaysia) and imprisonment.[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169] [Human trafficking](19333662.md) and [sex trafficking in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_trafficking_in_Malaysia) are significant problems.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] There have also been cases of vigilante executions and beatings against LGBT individuals in Malaysia.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] The illegality of homosexuality in Malaysia has also been the forefront of [Anwar Ibrahim](19264815.md)'s [sodomy trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_Ibrahim_sodomy_trials), which Anwar has called politically motivated, a characterisation supported by the [Working Group on Arbitrary Detention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_Group_on_Arbitrary_Detention), along with [Amnesty International](18947898.md) and the [Human Rights Watch](46530.md).[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176]

The [death penalty](8574786.md) is in use for serious crimes such as [murder](20171.md), [terrorism](30636.md), and [drug trafficking](218449.md), some of which were once mandatory.[^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178] However, in July 2023, following the passing of the [Abolition of Mandatory Death Penalty Act 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_Mandatory_Death_Penalty_Act_2023), mandatory death penalty was abolished for all crimes in Malaysia, thereby allowing judges to impose the capital punishment at their own discretion and on a case-by-case basis. The new law also abolished all [natural life imprisonment](148681.md), replacing it with 30 to 40 years prison terms instead.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Malaysia)

Relief map of Malaysia

[Topographic map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topographic_map) of Malaysia; [Mount Kinabalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kinabalu) is the highest summit in the country.

Malaysia is the [66th largest country by total land area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_outlying_territories_by_total_area), with a total area of 330,803 km<sup>2</sup> (127,724 sq mi).[^cite_note-2010_stats-13] [West Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Malaysia) shares [land borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_of_Malaysia) with [Thailand](30128.md), while [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia) shares land borders with [Indonesia](14579.md) and [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei).[^cite_note-CIA_Fact_Book-21] [Singapore](27318.md) is separated from the West by the [Straits of Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Johor) and is linked to it by [a narrow causeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor%E2%80%93Singapore_Causeway) and [a bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia%E2%80%93Singapore_Second_Link). The country also shares [maritime boundaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_boundaries) with Indonesia, [Vietnam](202354.md)[^cite_note-182] and the [Philippines](23440.md).[^cite_note-183] The land borders are defined in large part by geological features such as the [Perlis River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perlis_River), the [Golok River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golok_River) and the Pagalayan Canal, whilst some of the maritime boundaries are the subject of ongoing contention.[^cite_note-CIA_Fact_Book-21] Brunei forms what is almost an [enclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclave) in Malaysia,[^cite_note-184] with the state of [Sarawak](28258.md) dividing it into two parts. Malaysia is the only country with territory on both the [Asian mainland](689.md) and the [Malay Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Archipelago).[^cite_note-Marshall-185] The [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca), lying between [Sumatra](28679.md) and Peninsular Malaysia, is one of the most important thoroughfares in global commerce, carrying 40% of the world's trade.[^cite_note-Schuman-186] The [southernmost point of mainland Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_points_of_Asia) is located at [Tanjung Piai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Piai), in the state of [Johor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johor).[^cite_note-187]

The two parts of Malaysia, separated from each other by the [South China Sea](74209.md), share a largely similar landscape in that both Peninsular and East Malaysia feature [coastal plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_plains) rising to [hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hills) and [mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountains).[^cite_note-CIA_Fact_Book-21] Peninsular Malaysia, containing 40% of Malaysia's land area,[^cite_note-Marshall-185] extends 740 km (460 mi) from north to south, and its maximum width is 322 km (200 mi).[^cite_note-Swee-Hock-188] It is divided between its east and west coasts by the [Titiwangsa Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titiwangsa_Mountains),[^cite_note-189] rising to a peak elevation of 2,183 metres (7,162 ft) at [Mount Korbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Korbu),[^cite_note-AZ-190] part of a series of [mountain ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_range) running down the centre of the peninsula.[^cite_note-Marshall-185] These mountains are heavily [forested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest),[^cite_note-191] and mainly composed of [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) and other [igneous rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous_rock). Much of it has been eroded, creating a [karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst) landscape.[^cite_note-Marshall-185] The range is the origin of some of Peninsular Malaysia's river systems.[^cite_note-192] The coastal plains surrounding the peninsula reach a maximum width of 50 kilometres (31 mi), and the peninsula's coastline is nearly 1,931 km (1,200 mi) long, although [harbours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour) are only available on the western side.[^cite_note-Swee-Hock-188]



View towards St. John's Peak before sunrise on Mount Kinabalu, with the distant city lights of Kota Kinabalu visible far right, Sabah, Borneo

East Malaysia, on the island of [Borneo](4518.md), has a coastline of 2,607 km (1,620 mi).[^cite_note-CIA_Fact_Book-21] It is divided between coastal regions, hills and [valleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley), and a mountainous interior.[^cite_note-Marshall-185] The [Crocker Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocker_Range) extends northwards from Sarawak,[^cite_note-Marshall-185] dividing the state of [Sabah](28678.md). It is the location of the 4,095 m (13,435 ft) high [Mount Kinabalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kinabalu),[^cite_note-Richmond-193][^cite_note-Thiessen-194] the [tallest mountain in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mountains_in_Malaysia). Mount Kinabalu is located in the [Kinabalu National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinabalu_National_Park), which is protected as one of the four [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Sites](44940.md) [in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Malaysia).[^cite_note-195] The highest mountain ranges form the border between Malaysia and Indonesia. Sarawak contains the Mulu Caves, the largest cave system in the world, in the [Gunung Mulu National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunung_Mulu_National_Park), another World Heritage Site.[^cite_note-Marshall-185] Also in Sarawak is the [Rajang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajang_River), the [largest river in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Malaysia).

Around the two halves of Malaysia are [numerous islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Malaysia), the largest of which is [Banggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banggi).[^cite_note-196] The local climate is [equatorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate) and characterised by the annual southwest (April to October) and northeast (October to February) [monsoons](57630.md).[^cite_note-Swee-Hock-188] The temperature is moderated by the presence of the surrounding oceans.[^cite_note-Marshall-185] Humidity is usually high, and the average annual rainfall is 250 cm (98 in).[^cite_note-Swee-Hock-188] The climates of the Peninsula and the East differ, as the climate on the peninsula is directly affected by wind from the mainland, as opposed to the more maritime weather of the East. Local climates can be divided into three regions, highland, lowland, and coastal.[^cite_note-Marshall-185] [Climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Malaysia) will cause [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise) and increased rainfall, increasing flood risks and leading to droughts.[^cite_note-197]

### Biodiversity and conservation

Main articles: [Wildlife of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Malaysia), [Environmental issues in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_Malaysia), [Conservation in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_in_Malaysia), and [List of national parks in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_in_Malaysia)

Tiger resting on an artificial log platform

A [Malayan tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayan_tiger) in the [National Zoo of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Zoo_of_Malaysia)

Malaysia signed the Rio [Convention on Biological Diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_Biological_Diversity) on 12 June 1993, and became a party to the convention on 24 June 1994.[^cite_note-198] It has subsequently produced a [National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_action_plan), which was received by the convention on 16 April 1998.[^cite_note-199] The country is [megadiverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megadiverse) with a high number of species and high levels of [endemism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism).[^cite_note-megadiverse-200] It is estimated to contain 20% of the world's animal species.[^cite_note-Alexander-201] High levels of endemism are found on the diverse forests of [Borneo](4518.md)'s mountains, as species are isolated from each other by lowland forest.[^cite_note-Marshall-185]

There are about 210 mammal species in the country.[^cite_note-Richmond-193] Over 620 species of birds have been recorded in [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia),[^cite_note-Alexander-201] with many endemic to the mountains there. A high number of endemic bird species are also found in [Malaysian Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Borneo).[^cite_note-Marshall-185] 250 [reptile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptile) species have been recorded in the country, with about 150 species of [snakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake)[^cite_note-Richmond2007-202] and 80 species of [lizards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizard).[^cite_note-Richmond-193] There are about 150 species of [frogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frog),[^cite_note-Richmond-193] and thousands of [insect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insect) species.[^cite_note-Richmond-193] The [Exclusive economic zone of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone_of_Malaysia) is 334,671 km<sup>2</sup> (129,217 sq mi) and 1.5 times larger than its land area. It is mainly in the [South China Sea](74209.md).[^cite_note-203][^cite_note-204] Some of its waters are in the [Coral Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_Triangle), a biodiversity hotspot.[^cite_note-205] The waters around the island of [Sipadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sipadan) are the most biodiverse in the world.[^cite_note-Alexander-201] Bordering East Malaysia, the [Sulu Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sea) is a biodiversity hotspot, with around 600 [coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral) species and 1200 [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish) species.[^cite_note-Gardeners-206] The unique biodiversity of Malaysian Caves always attracts lovers of [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) from all over the world.[^cite_note-207]

Nearly 4,000 species of [fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungi), including [lichen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen)-forming species, have been recorded from Malaysia. Of the two fungal groups with the largest number of species in Malaysia, the [Ascomycota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascomycota) and their asexual states have been surveyed in some habitats (decaying wood, marine, and freshwater ecosystems, as parasites of some plants, and as agents of biodegradation), but have not been or have been only poorly surveyed in other habitats (as endobionts, in soils, on dung, as human and animal pathogens); the [Basidiomycota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basidiomycota) are only partly surveyed: [bracket fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bracket_fungi), mushrooms and toadstools have been studied, but Malaysian rust and smut fungi remain little known. Without a doubt, many more fungal species in Malaysia are yet to be recorded, and it is likely that many of those, when found, will be new to science.[^cite_note-208]

Red flower made of 5 petals surrounding a depressed centre, on the forest floor surrounded by dead leaves and small green plants

Some species of *[Rafflesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafflesia)* can grow up to 1 m (3 ft 3 in) in diameter, making them the largest flowers in the world.

About two-thirds of Malaysia was covered in [forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest) as of 2007,[^cite_note-Swee-Hock-188] with some forests believed to be 130 million years old.[^cite_note-Richmond-193] The forests are dominated by [dipterocarps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipterocarpaceae).[^cite_note-WWF-209] Lowland forest covers areas below 760 m (2,490 ft),[^cite_note-Swee-Hock-188] and formerly East Malaysia was covered in [such rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo_lowland_rain_forests),[^cite_note-WWF-209] which is supported by its hot wet climate.[^cite_note-Marshall-185] There are around 14,500 species of [flowering plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flowering_plant) and [trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree).[^cite_note-Richmond-193] Besides [rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest), there are over 1,425 km<sup>2</sup> (550 sq mi) of [mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangroves) in Malaysia,[^cite_note-Swee-Hock-188] and a large amount of peat forest. At higher altitudes, [oaks](39696.md), [chestnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chestnut), and [rhododendrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhododendron) replace dipterocarps.[^cite_note-Marshall-185] There are an estimated 8,500 species of [vascular plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_plant) in Peninsular Malaysia, with another 15,000 in the East.[^cite_note-210] The forests of East Malaysia are estimated to be the habitat of around 2,000 tree species, and are one of the most biodiverse areas in the world, with 240 different species of trees every hectare.[^cite_note-Marshall-185] These forests host many members of the *[Rafflesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafflesia)* genus, the largest flowers in the world,[^cite_note-WWF-209] with a maximum diameter of 1 m (3 ft 3 in).[^cite_note-Richmond2-211]

[Logging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logging), along with cultivation practices, has devastated tree cover, causing severe environmental degradation in the country. Over 80% of [Sarawak](28258.md)'s rainforest has been logged.[^cite_note-Marshall-185] Floods in East Malaysia have been worsened by the loss of trees, and over 60% of the peninsula's forests have been cleared.[^cite_note-Richmond2-211] With current rates of [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_and_environmental_impact_of_palm_oil#Deforestation), mainly for the [palm oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_oil_production_in_Malaysia) industry, the forests are predicted to be extinct by 2020.[^cite_note-Marshall-185][^cite_note-212] [Deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation_in_Malaysia) is a major problem for animals, fungi and plants, having caused species such as *[Begonia eiromischa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begonia_eiromischa)* to go extinct.[^cite_note-Tigers-213] Most remaining forest is found inside reserves and national parks.[^cite_note-Richmond2-211] Habitat destruction has proved a threat for [marine life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_life).[^cite_note-Gardeners-206] [Illegal fishing](6346579.md) is another major threat,[^cite_note-Gardeners-206] with fishing methods such as [dynamite fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamite_fishing) and poisoning depleting marine ecosystems.[^cite_note-Artificial-214] [Leatherback turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leatherback_turtle) numbers have dropped by 98% since the 1950s.[^cite_note-Richmond2007-202] Hunting has also been an issue for some animals,[^cite_note-Richmond2-211] with [overconsumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overconsumption) and the use of animal parts for profit endangering many animals, from marine life[^cite_note-Gardeners-206] to [tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger).[^cite_note-Tigers-213] Marine life is also detrimentally affected by uncontrolled tourism.[^cite_note-Go-215]

The [Malaysian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_government) aims to balance economic growth with environmental protection, but has been accused of favouring big business over the environment.[^cite_note-Richmond2-211] Some state governments are now trying to counter the environmental impact and pollution created by deforestation;[^cite_note-WWF-209] and the federal government is trying to cut logging by 10% each year. A total of [28 national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Malaysia) have been established, 23 in East Malaysia and five in the peninsula.[^cite_note-Richmond2-211] Tourism has been limited in biodiverse areas such as the island of Sipadan.[^cite_note-Go-215] [Wildlife trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_trafficking) is a large issue, and the Malaysian government has held talks with the governments of [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Brunei) and [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Indonesia) to standardise anti-trafficking laws.[^cite_note-216]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Malaysia)



Development of real GDP per capita, 1820 to 2018

Malaysia is a relatively [open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_economy) state-oriented and [newly industrialised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newly_industrialised_country) [market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy).[^cite_note-217] It has the world's [34th-largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) by nominal GDP and the [28th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(PPP\)) by [PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity). In 2017, the large [service sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_sector) contributed to 53.6% of total [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP), the industrial sector 37.6%, and the small agricultural sector roughly 8.8%.[^cite_note-218] Malaysia has a low official [unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_unemployment_rate) of 3.4% as of 2024.[^cite_note-219] Its foreign exchange reserves are the world's [24th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_foreign-exchange_reserves).[^cite_note-220] It has a labour force of about 15 million, which is the world's [34th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_labour_force).[^cite_note-221] Malaysia's large [automotive industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_Malaysia) ranks as the world's [22nd-largest by production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_motor_vehicle_production).[^cite_note-222]

Malaysia is the world's 23rd-largest [exporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_exports) and 25th-largest [importer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_imports).[^cite_note-223][^cite_note-224] However, economic inequalities exist between different ethnic groups.[^cite_note-225] The Chinese make up about one-quarter of the population, but account for 70% of the country's market capitalisation.[^cite_note-226] Chinese businesses in Malaysia are part of the larger [bamboo network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_network), a network of [overseas Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese) businesses in the Southeast Asian market sharing common family and cultural ties.[^cite_note-Weidenbaum-227]

International trade, facilitated by the shipping route in adjacent [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca), and manufacturing are the key sectors.[^cite_note-228][^cite_note-BNM-229][^cite_note-Schuman-186] Malaysia is an exporter of natural and agricultural resources, and petroleum is a major export.[^cite_note-state.gov-55] Malaysia is member of the [OPEC+](166346.md) [cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartel).[^cite_note-g417-230] Malaysia has once been the largest producer of [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin),[^cite_note-231] [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber) and [palm oil](57561.md) in the world. Manufacturing has a large influence in the country's economy,[^cite_note-BNM-229] although Malaysia's economic structure has been moving away from it.[^cite_note-WPRO-232] Malaysia remains one of the world's largest producers of palm oil.[^cite_note-233]

[Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Malaysia) is the third-largest contributor to Malaysia's GDP, after the manufacturing and commodities sectors.[^cite_note-234] In 2019, the sector contributed about 15.9% to the total GDP. According to the [World Tourism Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Tourism_Organization), Malaysia was the fourteenth-most visited country in the world, and the fourth-most visited country in Asia in 2019, with over 26.1 million visits.[^cite_note-tourism-235] Malaysia was ranked 38th in the Travel and Tourism Competitiveness Report 2019.[^cite_note-236] Its international tourism receipts in 2019 amounted to \$19.8 billion.[^cite_note-tourism-235]

The country has developed into a centre of [Islamic banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_banking) and has the highest number of female workers in that industry.[^cite_note-iht001-237] Knowledge-based services are also expanding.[^cite_note-WPRO-232] In 2020, Malaysia exported high-tech products worth \$92.1 billion, the second-highest in ASEAN, after Singapore.[^cite_note-238] Malaysia was ranked 34th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2025,[^cite_note-239][^cite_note-240] and 32nd in the [Global Competitiveness Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Competitiveness_Report) in 2022.[^cite_note-241]

[Railway transport in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Malaysia) is state-run, and spans some 2,783 kilometres (1,729 mi).[^cite_note-242] As of 2016, Malaysia has the world's [26th-largest road network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_road_network_size), with some 238,823 kilometres (148,398 mi) of [roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road). Malaysia's inland [waterways](174851.md) are the world's [22nd-longest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_waterways_length), and total 7,200 km (4,474 mi).[^cite_note-243] Among [Malaysia's 114 airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Malaysia),[^cite_note-244] the [busiest one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Malaysia) is [Kuala Lumpur International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuala_Lumpur_International_Airport) (KLIA), located in the [Sepang District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepang_District); it is also the [12th-busiest airport in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Asia). Among the seven federal [ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port), the major one is [Port Klang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Klang),[^cite_note-245] which is the [13th-busiest container port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_container_ports).[^cite_note-246] Malaysia's [flag carrier](465816.md) is [Malaysia Airlines](153162.md), providing international and domestic air services.[^cite_note-247]

[Malaysia's telecommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_in_Malaysia) network is second only to [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_in_Singapore)'s in [Southeast Asia](28741.md), with 4.7 million fixed-line subscribers and more than 30 million cellular subscribers.[^cite_note-Tele-248][^cite_note-249] There are 200 [industrial parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_park) along with specialised parks such as [Technology Park Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_Park_Malaysia) and [Kulim Hi-Tech Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulim_Hi-Tech_Park).[^cite_note-MIDA-250] [Fresh water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_water) is available to over 95% of the population, with [groundwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater) accounting for 90% of the freshwater resources.[^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252] Although rural areas have been the focus of great development, they still lag behind areas such as the West Coast of [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia).[^cite_note-253] The [telecommunication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunication) network, although strong in [urban areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area), is less available to the [rural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural) population.[^cite_note-Tele-248]

[Malaysia's energy](22939829.md) infrastructure sector is largely dominated by [Tenaga Nasional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenaga_Nasional), the largest [electric utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_utility) company in Southeast Asia. Customers in Peninsular Malaysia are connected to electricity through the [National Grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Grid_\(Malaysia\)).[^cite_note-254] The other two electric utility companies in the country are [Sarawak Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarawak_Energy) and [Sabah Electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabah_Electricity).[^cite_note-255] In 2013, Malaysia's total power generation capacity was over 29,728 [megawatts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megawatts). Total electricity generation was 140,985.01 [GWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GWh) and total electricity consumption was 116,087.51 GWh.[^cite_note-256] [Energy production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_Malaysia) in Malaysia is largely based on oil and natural gas, owing to Malaysia's oil and natural gas reserves, which are the fourth largest in the [Asia-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific) region.[^cite_note-Unimap-257]

According to the [Malaysian Department of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Statistics_Malaysia), the country's population was 32,447,385 in 2020,[^cite_note-2020_census-15] making it the [42nd-most populated country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_population_in_2010). According to a 2012 estimate, the population is increasing by 1.54% per year. Malaysia has an average [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) of 96 people per km<sup>2</sup>, ranking it [116th in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_territories_by_population_density) for population density. People within the 15–64 [age group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_group) constitute 70.3% of the total population; the 0–14 age group corresponds to 21.5%; while senior citizens aged 65 years or older make up 8.1%.[^cite_note-2026_pop-7] In 1960, when the first official census was recorded in Malaysia, the population was 8.11 million. 90.1% of the population are [Malaysian citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysians).[^cite_note-2026_pop-7]

Malaysian citizens are divided along local ethnic lines, with 70.6% considered *[bumiputera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumiputera_\(Malaysia\))*.[^cite_note-2026_pop-7] The largest group of bumiputera are [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)), who are defined in the constitution as [Muslims](19541.md) who practise Malay customs and culture. They play a dominant role politically.[^cite_note-258] Bumiputera status is also accorded to the non-Malay indigenous groups of Sabah and Sarawak: which includes [Dayaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayaks) ([Iban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iban_people), [Bidayuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bidayuh), [Orang Ulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Ulu)), [Kadazan-Dusun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadazan-Dusun), [Melanau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanau), [Bajau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajau) and others. Non-Malay bumiputeras make up more than half of [Sarawak](28258.md)'s population and over two-thirds of [Sabah](28678.md)'s population.[^cite_note-259][^cite_note-260] There are also indigenous or aboriginal groups in much smaller numbers on the peninsula, where they are collectively known as the [Orang Asli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orang_Asli).[^cite_note-261] Laws over who receives bumiputera status vary between states.[^cite_note-262]

There are also two other non-Bumiputera local ethnic groups. 22.1% of the population are [Malaysian Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Chinese), while 6.5% are [Malaysian Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indian).[^cite_note-2026_pop-7] The local Chinese have historically been more dominant in the business community. Local Indians are mostly of [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_people) descent.[^cite_note-263][^cite_note-Barbara-264] [Malaysian citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_citizenship) is not automatically granted to those born in Malaysia, but is granted to a child born of two Malaysian parents outside Malaysia. [Dual citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_citizenship) is not permitted.[^cite_note-265] Citizenship in the states of Sabah and Sarawak in [Malaysian Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Borneo) are distinct from citizenship in [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia) for immigration purposes. Every citizen is issued a biometric smart chip [identity card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identity_card) known as *[MyKad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MyKad)* at the age of 12 and must carry the card at all times.[^cite_note-266]

The population is concentrated on Peninsular Malaysia,[^cite_note-267] where about 80 percent of Malaysia's population live.[^cite_note-CIA_Fact_Book-21] 75.1% of the population is urban.[^cite_note-2020_census-15] Due to the rise in labour-intensive industries,[^cite_note-268] the country is estimated to have over 3 million migrant workers; about 10% of the population.[^cite_note-269] Sabah-based NGOs estimate that out of the 3 million that make up the population of Sabah, 2 million are illegal immigrants.[^cite_note-270] Malaysia hosts a population of refugees and asylum seekers numbering approximately 171,500. Of this population, approximately 79,000 are from [Myanmar](19457.md), 72,400 from the [Philippines](23440.md), and 17,700 from [Indonesia](14579.md). Malaysian officials are reported to have turned deportees directly over to human smugglers in 2007, and Malaysia employs [RELA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RELA_Corps), a volunteer militia with a history of controversies, to enforce its immigration law.[^cite_note-271]&#x20;

The constitution grants freedom of religion, while establishing Islam as the "religion of the Federation".[^cite_note-273][^cite_note-malaysianbar.org.my-274] The freedom to change or leave a religion is limited for Muslims.[^cite_note-x263-275] According to the Population and Housing Census 2020 figures, ethnicity and religious beliefs correlate highly. Approximately 63.5% of the population practise [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Malaysia), 18.7% practise [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Malaysia), 9.1% [Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Malaysia), 6.1% [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Malaysia) and 1.3% practise [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism), [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism) and other traditional [Chinese religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_folk_religion). 2.7% declared no religion or practised other religions or did not provide any information.[^cite_note-2020_census-15] The states of [Sarawak](28258.md) and [Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang), as well as the federal territory of [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md) have non-Muslim majorities.[^cite_note-276][^cite_note-277]

[Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) of the [Shafi'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi'i) school of [jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisprudence) is the dominant branch of [Islam in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Malaysia),[^cite_note-Peletz2002-278][^cite_note-279] while 18% are [nondenominational Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondenominational_Muslims).[^cite_note-280] The Malaysian constitution strictly defines what makes a "Malay", defining Malays as those who are Muslim, speak Malay regularly, practise Malay customs, and lived in or have ancestors from [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Brunei), Malaysia and [Singapore](922794.md).[^cite_note-Marshall-185] Statistics from the 2010 Census indicate that 83.6% of the Chinese population identify as Buddhist, with significant numbers of adherents following Taoism (3.4%) and Christianity (11.1%), along with small Muslim populations in areas like Penang. The majority of the Indian population follows Hinduism (86.2%), with a significant minority identifying as Christians (6.0%) or Muslims (4.1%). Christianity is the predominant religion of the non-Malay *bumiputera* community (46.5%), while 40.4% identify as Muslims.[^cite_note-2020_census-15]

[Muslims](19541.md) are obliged to follow the decisions of [Syariah Courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syariah_Court) (i.e. Shariah courts) in matters concerning their religion. The Islamic judges are expected to follow the Shafi'i legal school of Islam, which is the main *[madhhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhhab)* ('school of thought') of Malaysia.[^cite_note-Peletz2002-278] The jurisdiction of Syariah courts is limited to Muslims in matters such as [marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marriage), [inheritance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inheritance), [divorce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorce), [apostasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostasy), [religious conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_conversion), custody, and limited Islamic criminal law. No other criminal or civil offences are under the jurisdiction of the Syariah courts, which have a similar hierarchy to the [civil courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts_of_Malaysia). The civil courts do not hear matters related to Islamic practices.[^cite_note-281]

The official and national language of Malaysia is [Malaysian Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Malay),[^cite_note-CIA_Fact_Book-21] a standardised form of the [Malay language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language).[^cite_note-282] The previous official terminology was *Bahasa Malaysia* (lit. 'Malaysian language')[^cite_note-283][^cite_note-284][^cite_note-285] but now government policy uses *Bahasa Melayu* (Malay language) to refer to the official language[^cite_note-286] and both terms remain in use.[^cite_note-287][^cite_note-288] The National Language Act 1967 specifies the [Latin (Rumi) script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_alphabet) as the official [script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing_system) of the national language, but does not prohibit the use of the traditional [Jawi script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawi_script).[^cite_note-National_Language_Act_1967-289]

[English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) remains an active [second language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_language), with its use allowed for some official purposes under the National Language Act of 1967.[^cite_note-National_Language_Act_1967-289] In [Sarawak](28258.md), English is an official state language alongside Malay.[^cite_note-lang1-290][^cite_note-lang2-291][^cite_note-lang3-292] Historically, English was the de facto administrative language; Malay became predominant after the 1969 race riots ([13 May incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13_May_incident)).[^cite_note-History-293] [Malaysian English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_English), also known as Malaysian Standard English, is a form of English derived from [British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English). Malaysian English is widely used in business, along with [Manglish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manglish), which is a colloquial form of English with heavy Malay, [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language), and [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) influences. The government discourages the use of non-standard Malay but has no power to issue compounds or fines to those who use what is perceived as improper Malay on their advertisements.[^cite_note-294][^cite_note-295]

Malaysia is also home to [111 living indigenous languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Malaysia).[^cite_note-296] Some Orang Asli languages are now extinct, such the Low Country [Semang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semang) dialects once spoken in Penang.[^cite_note-297] Many other languages are used in Malaysia, which contains speakers of 137 living languages.[^cite_note-298] [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia) contains speakers of 41 of these languages.[^cite_note-299] The native tribes of [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia) have their own languages which are related to, but easily distinguishable from, Malay. [Iban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iban_language) is the main tribal language in Sarawak while [Dusunic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dusunic_languages) and [Kadazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Kadazan_language) languages are spoken by the natives in [Sabah](28678.md).[^cite_note-300] [Chinese Malaysians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Malaysians) predominantly speak Chinese dialects from the [southern part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China) of [China](5405.md). The more common [Chinese varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_varieties) in the country are [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese), [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese), [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien), and so on.[^cite_note-301] The [Tamil language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) is used predominantly by the majority of [Malaysian Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Indians).[^cite_note-302] A small number of Malaysians have [European ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe) and speak [creole languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_language), such as the Portuguese-based [Malaccan Creoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese-based_creole_languages#Southeast_Asia),[^cite_note-303] and the Spanish-based [Chavacano language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavacano_language).[^cite_note-304]

### Health

Main articles: [Health in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Malaysia) and [Healthcare in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Malaysia)

Malaysia operates an efficient and widespread [two-tier healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-tier_healthcare) system, consisting of a [universal healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_healthcare) system and a co-existing private healthcare system; provided by highly subsidised healthcare through its extensive network of public hospitals and clinics.[^cite_note-305] The [Ministry of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_\(Malaysia\)) is the main provider of healthcare services to the country's population.[^cite_note-306] Malaysia's healthcare system is considered to be among the most developed in [Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_systems_by_country#Asia), which contributes to its thriving [medical tourism industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_tourism_in_Malaysia).[^cite_note-307]

Malaysia spent 3.83% of its GDP on healthcare in 2019.[^cite_note-308] In 2020, the overall [life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) in Malaysia at birth was 76 years (74 years for males and 78 years for females),[^cite_note-309] and it had an [infant mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality_rate) of 7 deaths per 1000 births.[^cite_note-310] Malaysia had a [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) of 2.0 in 2020, which is just below the [replacement level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-replacement_fertility) of 2.1.[^cite_note-311] In 2020, the country's crude [birth rate](515898.md) was 16 per 1000 people, and the crude [death rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_rate) was 5 per 1000 people.[^cite_note-312][^cite_note-313]

In 2021, the principal cause of death among Malaysian adults was [coronary artery disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronary_artery_disease), representing 17% of the medically certified deaths in 2020 – being followed by [pneumonia](52135.md); which accounted for 11% of the deaths.[^cite_note-314] [Transport accidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_accidents) are considered a major health hazard, as Malaysia, relative to its population, has one of the [highest traffic fatality rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_traffic-related_death_rate) in the world.[^cite_note-315] [Smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoking_in_Malaysia) is also considered a major health issue across the country.[^cite_note-316]

### Education

Main article: [Education in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Malaysia)



[Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(Malaysia\)), Putrajaya

The education system of Malaysia features a non-compulsory kindergarten education followed by six years of compulsory primary education and five years of optional secondary education.[^cite_note-317] Schools in the primary education system are divided into two categories: national primary schools, which teach in [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language) (i.e.:[Sekolah Kebangsaan Sungai Binjai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekolah_Kebangsaan_Sungai_Binjai), [SK Bukit Tiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Bukit_Tiu)), and vernacular schools, which teach in [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language) or [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) (i.e.:[SJK(C) Pin Hwa 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SJK\(C\)_Pin_Hwa_2), SJK(T) Bandar Mentakab).[^cite_note-318]

Secondary education (i.e.:[SMK Sura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SMK_Sura), [Kajang High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajang_High_School)) is conducted for five years. In the final year of secondary education, students sit for the [Malaysian Certificate of Education examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sijil_Pelajaran_Malaysia).[^cite_note-319] Since the introduction of the [matriculation programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Matriculation_Programme) in 1999, students who completed the 12-month programme in matriculation colleges can enrol in local universities. However, in the matriculation system, only 10% of places are open to non-*[bumiputera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumiputera_\(Malaysia\))* students.[^cite_note-320]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Malaysia)

The wooden frame of a house under construction, with the floor raised off the ground

A traditional house being built in [Sabah](28678.md)

Malaysia has a multi-ethnic, multicultural, and multilingual society. Substantial influence exists from [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_culture) and [Indian cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_India), dating back to when foreign trade began. Other cultural influences include the [Persian](965233.md), [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_culture), and [British cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_Kingdom). Due to the structure of the government, coupled with the [social contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_contract_\(Malaysia\)) theory, there has been minimal [cultural assimilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_assimilation) of ethnic minorities.[^cite_note-321] Some cultural disputes exist between Malaysia and neighbouring countries, notably [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Indonesia).[^cite_note-322]

In 1971, the government created a "National Cultural Policy", defining Malaysian culture. It stated that Malaysian culture must be based on the culture of the indigenous peoples of Malaysia, that it may incorporate suitable elements from other cultures, and that Islam must play a part in it.[^cite_note-Papers-323] It also promoted the [Malay language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language) above others.[^cite_note-Cinema-324] This government intervention into culture has caused resentment among non-Malays who feel their cultural freedom was lessened. Both Chinese and Indian associations have submitted memorandums to the government, accusing it of formulating an undemocratic culture policy.[^cite_note-Papers-323]

### Fine arts

Main article: [Malaysian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_art)

See also: [Music of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Malaysia) and [Malaysian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_literature)

Making Malaysian batik

A craftsman making [batik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batik). [Malaysian batik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_batik) is usually patterned with floral motifs with light colouring.

Traditional Malaysian art was mainly centred on the areas of [carving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carving), [weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving), and [silversmithing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silversmith).[^cite_note-325] Traditional art ranges from handwoven [baskets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basket) from rural areas to the [silverwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_silver) of the Malay courts. Common artworks included ornamental [kris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kris), [beetle nut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beetle_nut) sets, and woven [batik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batik) and [songket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songket) fabrics. Indigenous East Malaysians are known for their wooden [masks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mask).[^cite_note-Marshall-185] Each ethnic group have distinct performing arts, with little overlap between them. However, Malay art does show some [North Indian](1552939.md) influence due to the historical influence of [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_art).[^cite_note-Garland-326]

Traditional Malay music and performing arts appear to have originated in the [Kelantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelantan)-[Pattani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patani_\(historical_region\)) region with influences from [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_India), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_China), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Thailand), and [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Indonesia). The music is based around [percussion instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percussion_instrument),[^cite_note-Garland-326] the most important of which is the [gendang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendang) (drum). There are at least 14 types of traditional drums.[^cite_note-q694-327] [Drums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum) and other traditional percussion instruments and are often made from natural materials.[^cite_note-q694-327] [Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music) is traditionally used for storytelling, celebrating life-cycle events, and occasions such as a harvest.[^cite_note-Garland-326] It was once used as a form of long-distance communication.[^cite_note-q694-327] In [East Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia), [gong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong)-based musical ensembles such as [agung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agung) and [kulintang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulintang) are commonly used in ceremonies such as [funerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral) and [weddings](32893.md).[^cite_note-Matusky-328] These ensembles are also common in neighbouring regions such as in [Mindanao](20546.md) in the [Philippines](23440.md), [Kalimantan](165470.md) in [Indonesia](14579.md), and [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei).[^cite_note-Matusky-328]

Malaysia has a strong oral tradition that has existed since before the arrival of writing and continues today. Each of the Malay Sultanates created their own literary tradition, influenced by pre-existing oral stories and by the stories that came with Islam.[^cite_note-EncyclopediaMy-329] The first Malay literature was in the [Arabic script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_script). The earliest known Malay writing is on the [Terengganu Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terengganu_Inscription_Stone), made in 1303.[^cite_note-Marshall-185] Chinese and Indian literature became common as the number of speakers increased in Malaysia, and locally produced works based on languages from those areas began to be produced in the 19th century.[^cite_note-EncyclopediaMy-329] English has also become a common literary language.[^cite_note-Marshall-185] In 1971, the government took the step of defining the literature of different languages. Literature written in Malay was called "the national literature of Malaysia", literature in other *bumiputera* languages was called "regional literature", while literature in other languages was called "sectional literature".[^cite_note-Cinema-324] Malay poetry is highly developed, and uses many forms. The *Hikayat* form is popular, and the *pantun* has spread from Malay to other languages.[^cite_note-EncyclopediaMy-329]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Malaysian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_cuisine)

A glass of Teh tarik

*[Teh tarik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teh_tarik)*

Nasi lemak in a plate

*[Nasi lemak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasi_lemak)*

The national drink and dish of Malaysia

[^cite_note-330]

[^cite_note-331]

Malaysia's cuisine reflects the multi-ethnic makeup of its population.[^cite_note-Eckhardt-332] Many cultures from within the country and from surrounding regions have greatly influenced the cuisine. Much of the influence comes from the Malay, [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_culture), [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_India), [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Thailand), [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_culture), and Sumatran cultures,[^cite_note-Marshall-185] largely due to the country being part of the ancient [spice route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_route).[^cite_note-Independent-333] The cuisine is similar to those of [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singaporean_cuisine) and Brunei,[^cite_note-Richmond2-211] and also bears resemblance to [Filipino cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_cuisine).[^cite_note-Marshall-185] The different states have varied dishes,[^cite_note-Richmond2-211] and often the food in Malaysia is different from the original dishes.[^cite_note-Barbara-264]

Sometimes food not found in its original culture is assimilated into another; for example, [Chinese restaurants](32171094.md) in Malaysia often serve Malay dishes.[^cite_note-334] Food from one culture is sometimes also cooked using styles taken from another culture,[^cite_note-Richmond2-211] For example, *[sambal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambal) belacan* ([shrimp paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrimp_paste)) are commonly used as ingredients by Chinese restaurants to create the [stir fried water spinach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stir_fried_water_spinach) (*kangkung belacan*).[^cite_note-335] This means that although much of Malaysian food can be traced back to a certain culture, they have their own identity.[^cite_note-Independent-333] [Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) is a staple food and an important constituent of the country's culture.[^cite_note-336] [Chili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_pepper) is commonly found in local cuisine, although this does not necessarily make them spicy.[^cite_note-Eckhardt-332]

### Media

Main article: [Mass media in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media_in_Malaysia)



Logo of [Radio Televisyen Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Televisyen_Malaysia), the country's main public broadcaster

Malaysia's main newspapers are owned by the government and political parties in the ruling coalition,[^cite_note-337][^cite_note-BBC-338] although some major opposition parties also have their own, which are openly sold alongside regular newspapers. A divide exists between the media in the two halves of the country. [Peninsular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia)-based media gives a low priority to news from [the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Malaysia) and often treats the eastern states as colonies of the peninsula.[^cite_note-Mirror-339] As a result of this, [Sarawak](28258.md) launched [TV Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Sarawak) as an internet streaming beginning in 2014, and later as a TV station on 10 October 2020[^cite_note-340] to overcome the low priority and coverage of Peninsular-based media and to solidify the representation of East Malaysia.[^cite_note-341] The media have been blamed for increasing tension between [Indonesia](6532964.md) and Malaysia, and giving [Malaysians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysians) a bad image of [Indonesians](4885606.md).[^cite_note-342] The country has Malay, English, Chinese, and Tamil daily newspapers.[^cite_note-Mirror-339] Kadazandusun and Bajau news are available only via TV broadcast [Berita RTM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berita_RTM).[^cite_note-Berita_RTM-343] Written Kadazan news was once included in publications such as [The Borneo Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Borneo_Post), the Borneo Mail, the [Daily Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Express_\(Malaysia\)), and the [New Sabah Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Sabah_Times), but publication has ceased with the newspaper or as a section.[^cite_note-344][^cite_note-345]

[Freedom of the press](105323.md) is limited, with numerous restrictions on publishing rights and information dissemination.[^cite_note-346] The government has previously tried to crack down on opposition papers before elections.[^cite_note-BBC-338] In 2007, a government agency issued a directive to all private television and radio stations to refrain from broadcasting speeches made by opposition leaders,[^cite_note-347] a move condemned by politicians from the opposition [Democratic Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Action_Party).[^cite_note-348] Sabah, where all tabloids but one are independent of government control, has the freest press in Malaysia.[^cite_note-Mirror-339] Laws such as the [Printing Presses and Publications Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_Presses_and_Publications_Act) have also been cited as curtailing freedom of expression.[^cite_note-McAdams-349]

### Holidays and festivals

Main article: [Public holidays in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Malaysia)

Temple at night illuminated with light from decorations

Malaysia's largest [Buddhist temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_temple)—[Kek Lok Si](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kek_Lok_Si) in [Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang)—illuminated in preparation for [Chinese New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_New_Year)

Malaysians observe several holidays and festivities throughout the year. Some are federally gazetted [public holidays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays) and some are observed by individual states. Other festivals are observed by particular ethnic or religious groups, and the main holiday of each major group has been declared a public holiday. Malaysia has two official national holidays: *[Hari Merdeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Merdeka)* (Independence Day) on 31 August, commemorating the independence of the Federation of Malaya in 1957--is the most widely observed holiday[^cite_note-Marshall-185]--and the more recent [Malaysia Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_Day) on 16 September (since 2010) which commemorates the Malaysia Federation in 1963 with the states of [Sabah](28678.md) and [Sarawak](28258.md) both in East Malaysia.[^cite_note-350] Other notable national holidays are [Labour Day](18051.md) (1 May) and the King's birthday (first week of June).[^cite_note-Marshall-185]

[Muslim holidays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_holidays) are prominent as Islam is the state religion; *Hari Raya Puasa* (also called *Hari Raya Aidilfitri*, Malay for [Eid al-Fitr](154944.md)), *Hari Raya Haji* (also called *Hari Raya Aidiladha*, Malay for [Eid al-Adha](358226.md)), *[Maulidur Rasul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulidur_Rasul)* (birthday of the Prophet), and others being observed.[^cite_note-Marshall-185] Malaysian Chinese celebrate festivals such as [Chinese New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_New_Year) and others relating to traditional Chinese beliefs. [Wesak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesak) Day is observed and celebrated by Buddhists. Hindus in Malaysia celebrate *[Deepavali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepavali)*, the festival of lights,[^cite_note-MSU-351] while *[Thaipusam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaipusam)* is a religious rite which sees pilgrims from all over the country converge at the [Batu Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batu_Caves).[^cite_note-352] Malaysia's Christian community celebrates most of the holidays observed by Christians elsewhere, most notably Christmas and Easter. In addition to this, the Dayak community in Sarawak celebrate a harvest festival known as *[Gawai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gawai_Dayak)*,[^cite_note-353] and the Kadazandusun community celebrate *[Kaamatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaamatan)*.[^cite_note-354] Despite most festivals being identified with a particular ethnic or religious group, celebrations are universal. In a custom known as "open house" Malaysians participate in the celebrations of others, often visiting the houses of those who identify with the festival.[^cite_note-MIDA-250]

### Sports

Main article: [Sport in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Malaysia)

A woman and a man in black outfits with red belts practising the martial art of Silat Melayu

Traditional sports, such as the martial art style [Silat Melayu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silat_Melayu), persist alongside modern sports.

Popular sports in Malaysia include [association football](10568.md), [badminton](3956.md), [field hockey](10886.md), [bowls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowls), [tennis](29773.md), [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(sport\)), [martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_arts), [horse riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_riding), [sailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing), and [skate boarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skate_boarding).[^cite_note-MIDA-250] Football is the most popular sport in Malaysia.[^cite_note-355] Badminton matches also attract thousands of spectators, and since 1948 Malaysia has been one of four countries to hold the [Thomas Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Cup), the world team championship trophy of men's badminton.[^cite_note-356] The Malaysian Lawn Bowls Federation was registered in 1997.[^cite_note-357] Squash was brought to the country by members of the British army, with the first competition being held in 1939.[^cite_note-358] The [Squash Racquets Association of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_Racquets_Association_of_Malaysia) was created on 25 June 1972.[^cite_note-359] The men's [national field hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_men's_national_field_hockey_team) ranked [10th in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIH_Men's_World_Ranking) as of June 2022.[^cite_note-360] The 3rd [Hockey World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_World_Cup) was hosted at [Merdeka Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merdeka_Stadium) in Kuala Lumpur, as well as the 10th cup.[^cite_note-361] The country also has its own [Formula One](10854.md) track—the [Sepang International Circuit](944336.md), with the first [Malaysian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Grand_Prix) held in 1999.[^cite_note-362] Traditional sports include [Silat Melayu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silat_Melayu), the most common style of martial arts practised by [ethnic Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Malays).[^cite_note-363]

The Federation of Malaya Olympic Council was formed in 1953 and received recognition by the IOC in 1954. It first participated in the [1956 Melbourne Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Melbourne_Olympic_Games). The council was renamed the [Olympic Council of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Council_of_Malaysia) in 1964, and [has participated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_at_the_Olympics) in all but one Olympic games since its inception. The largest number of athletes ever sent to the Olympics was 57 to the [1972 Munich Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Munich_Olympic_Games).[^cite_note-364] Besides the Olympic Games, Malaysia also participates in the [Paralympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralympic_Games).[^cite_note-365] Malaysia has competed at the [Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Games) since 1950 as Malaya, and since 1966 as Malaysia, and the games were hosted in Kuala Lumpur in 1998.[^cite_note-366][^cite_note-367]
