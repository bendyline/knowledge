From June 2026 until late September 2026, [North America](21139.md) was struck by a severe [heatwave](293270.md). Temperature records were tied or broken in several major cities across the United States and Canada. In Canada, the extreme heat brought large thunderstorms on July 1, [Canada Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Day), which contributed to widespread flooding and thousands of homes being without power. In the United States, the extreme conditions strained public services, including public transit and the power grid, with delays and hundreds of thousands of homes without power for part of the heat wave.

The initial [heat dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_dome) existed over the [eastern United States](509486.md) and eastern Canada from June 28 until July 5; a peak of 106 °F (41 °C) was recorded in [Atlantic City, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_City,_New_Jersey), on July 4. A second heat dome is forming over the western half of the continent, with heat advisories currently in the [southwest United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_United_States) and [Alaska](624.md). As of August 5, 2026, there have been 70 deaths attributed to this heatwave.[^cite_note-1]

This heatwave was concurrent with the [FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), [United States' Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(United_States\)), and [Canada Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Day), resulting in participants having to be in shade and hydrate frequently.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

## Background

On June 11, the United States's [National Weather Service](286989.md) found [El Niño](317311.md) conditions were present, with the possibility of a "Super El Niño" as the year goes on. Climate scientists predict that with these [2026–2027 El Niño](83914690.md) conditions, there would be higher temperatures and more of a likelihood of heat waves.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] CNN has stated that El Niño and [human-caused climate change](5042951.md) both contribute to the increased severity of heat waves.[^cite_note-6]

## Canada



Weather forecast for July 2, 2026, showing very high temperatures in Ontario and Quebec



Canada Day storm damage in [Ottawa](22219.md).

### Eastern Canada

In Canada, [Ontario](22218.md) and [Quebec](7954867.md) were under "orange" or "yellow" heat warnings, with temperatures expected to reach upwards of 37 °C (99 °F) in some areas.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Record high temperatures affected much of Ontario and Quebec on July 1, with a humidex of 46 °C (115 °F) recorded in [Ottawa](22219.md). This extreme heat eventually led to large thunderstorms on July 1, and caused widespread flooding and storm damage across Ontario and Quebec, with a record 118 mm (4.6 in) of rain in Ottawa.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Many [Canada Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Day) celebrations in the [National Capital Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Capital_Region_\(Canada\)) had to be cancelled.[^cite_note-11] In Quebec, 100,000 homes supplied by [Hydro-Québec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydro-Qu%C3%A9bec) lost power on July 2. By the next morning, 50,000 homes still were without power.[^cite_note-12] During peak heat of 30 °C (86 °F) with a humidex of 36 °C (97 °F), roughly 44,000 customers lost power in [Halifax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halifax,_Nova_Scotia) on July 3. On July 4, the [Winterland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winterland,_Newfoundland_and_Labrador) area in [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_\(island\)) set a daily high 25.7 °C (78.3 °F), surpassing the record set in 2005.[^cite_note-\:5-13]

Ontario recorded the highest temperature since the [1936 North American heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_North_American_heat_wave) on July 13. [Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armstrong,_Thunder_Bay_District,_Ontario) set a new all-time high 40.7 °C (105.3 °F). Toronto recorded its hottest day since 2011 on July 14. Late on July 14, a cold front moved through southern Ontario bringing temperatures back down.[^cite_note-\:8-14]

### Western Canada

With the heat dome in the eastern half of the country over, a second heat dome formed over much of the western United States and Canada.[^cite_note-15] Seven communities set new records while four others had tied temperature records in [Manitoba](18926.md) on July 12, the hottest being [McCreary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCreary,_Manitoba) which set a new record of 36.5 °C (97.7 °F).[^cite_note-\:7-16] On July 22, twelve communities in [British Columbia](3392.md) broke daily heat records. Among them was [Lytton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lytton,_British_Columbia), which recorded a temperature of 41.2 °C (106.2 °F).[^cite_note-\:10-17]

### Northern Canada

Daily temperature records were set in [Nunavut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunavut) on July 4. [Cambridge Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Bay) broke the record set in 1930, recording 22.1 °C (71.8 °F). [Kugaaruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kugaaruk) set a temperature record of 27.5 °C (81.5 °F).[^cite_note-\:5-13]

## United States

With an average temperature of 76.89 °F (24.94 °C), July 2026 was the hottest month in American history.[^cite_note-18] The summer also finished as the hottest on record for the US.[^cite_note-19]

### Eastern United States



Weather forecast for July 2, showing extreme high temperatures in many areas of the Eastern United States



Road sign in New Jersey warning of excessive heat during the hottest day in 15 years, July 2, 2026

Nearly half of the United States, 180 million people, were under a "major" or "extreme" heat risk by the [National Weather Service](286989.md) starting on June 28.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Temperatures were expected to reach between 100–110 °F (38–43 °C) in many areas.[^cite_note-22] On June 29, the [heat index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_index) measured at [O'Hare International Airport](248449.md) in [Chicago](6886.md) was 101 °F (38 °C).[^cite_note-23] The next night, the low temperature was just 81 °F (27 °C) in [La Crosse, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Crosse,_Wisconsin), tying the all time record warm low temperature.[^cite_note-24] On July 2, ambient temperatures in [New York City](645042.md) exceeded 100 °F (38 °C) for the first time since 2012.[^cite_note-25] [Boston](24437894.md) also reached 100 °F (38 °C) that day, setting a new daily high temperature record and marking the 29th time the city reached the century mark.[^cite_note-26] [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) reduced speeds for trains in the [Northeast Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor) due to the extreme heat, and several trains were cancelled as well.[^cite_note-27] [SEPTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEPTA) and [NJ Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ_Transit) also faced delays and cancellations due to the heat.[^cite_note-28] The heat wave resulted in a fatality in [Bethel Township, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethel_Township,_Berks_County,_Pennsylvania). Additionally, over 17,000 customers lost electricity in New York City during the heat wave.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] [PJM Interconnection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PJM_Interconnection), which serves power to 67 million people, got near, if not surpassed, its record for power demand, which was previously set in 2006 at 165 [gigawatts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigawatts). Surging electrical demand as a result of higher temperatures along with loads from [data centers](579730.md). The [US Department of Energy](62866.md) ordered all electrical generators to run at full capacity, even backup units, for the second time this summer.[^cite_note-31]

On July 3, following another day of record heat across [New Jersey](21648.md), including [Atlantic City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_City,_New_Jersey), severe thunderstorms in the state resulted in 250,000 power outages. This resulted in many people being unable to use air conditioning.[^cite_note-32] The storms also amplified issues on NJ Transit from heat, forcing additional delays and suspensions.[^cite_note-33]

The heat wave coincided with the [US Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Day_\(United_States\)) on July 4, causing many celebration plans to be canceled, especially in the eastern portion of the country.[^cite_note-Aboulenein-34] Parades were canceled or delayed in Washington, DC, Philadelphia, and elsewhere.[^cite_note-Aboulenein-34][^cite_note-35]

Due to the heat wave during July 1–4, at least 44 heat-related fatalities were reported: 29 in New Jersey, 7 in Pennsylvania, 4 in Illinois, 3 in New York, and one in Mississippi. In addition, one heat-related death occurred in Mississippi on June 27.[^cite_note-36]

From July 4–6, several rounds of severe thunderstorms moved across the United States that broke up the heat dome. However, those storms resulted in over 1.3 million power outages and four fatalities.[^cite_note-37] The storms that broke the heat wave also possibly led to the largest hailstone in [Connecticut](6466.md) state history.[^cite_note-38]

Record heat returned to the region on July 15, when [Lehigh Valley International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley_International_Airport) recorded a high temperature of 98 °F (37 °C), tying the daily record. Heating was limited on July 16, however, due to smoke from the [2026 Canadian wildfires](83302792.md).[^cite_note-39]

During [Tropical Storm Bertha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Bertha_\(2026\)), southern [Louisiana](18130.md) was placed under both a [tropical storm warning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_storm_warning) and [extreme heat warning](5899797.md) for the first time since 2005.[^cite_note-40] [Lake Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Charles,_Louisiana) recorded a heat index of 121 °F (49 °C).[^cite_note-41]

A third major July heat dome began to impact the United States late in the month. Around 70 million were under heat advisories.[^cite_note-\:12-42] Heat indexes of over 100 °F (38 °C) extended from Minnesota to Mississippi.[^cite_note-\:11-43] An all-time record high of 112 °F (44 °C) was reported at the [Rapid City Regional Airport, South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_City_Regional_Airport), on July 26.[^cite_note-\:11-43][^cite_note-44]

Extreme [humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humidity) surged into the [Northeastern United States](431669.md) on August 7. [New York City](645042.md) reached a dew point of 79 °F (26 °C), just one degree shy of the all time record.[^cite_note-45]

### Western United States

Following the advent of the heat dome in the eastern United States, a second heat dome started to form across the western and central United States on July 6.[^cite_note-46] [Phoenix, Arizona](49121.md), reached a high of 113 °F (45 °C) on July 6–8.[^cite_note-\:1-47] [Salt Lake City, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City), recorded a new all-time high on July 12, reaching 109 °F (43 °C).[^cite_note-\:6-48] Also in Utah, the temperature in [Deseret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deseret,_Utah) reached an all-time high of 111 °F (44 °C). Additionally, local all-time records were set when [Miles City, Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_City,_Montana), reached 115 °F (46 °C) and [Sheridan, Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheridan,_Wyoming), reached 109 °F (43 °C).[^cite_note-\:9-49]

The third major heat dome affected the Southwest in late July,[^cite_note-\:12-42] with heat indexes of over 100 °F (38 °C) in southern California and Nevada.[^cite_note-\:11-43] Temperatures of up to 118–120 °F (48–49 °C) were recorded. Across the west on August 1, there were 65 records that were either tied or set new records.[^cite_note-50] In southern Nevada, emergency daytime cooling stations opened from August 6-11.[^cite_note-51]

In early August, extreme heat in [Colorado](5399.md) resulted in 72 hospitalizations.[^cite_note-52]
