The **Islamic Republic of Iran Navy**,[^cite_note-2] also referred to as **Iranian Navy**, is the [naval warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_warfare) branch of the [Islamic Republic of Iran Army](48259216.md).[^cite_note-Middle_East_Institute-3][^cite_note-The_Iran_Primer-4] It is one of the two naval forces of the [Iranian Armed Forces](14305018.md), the other is the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy](4676345.md).

NEDAJA is charged with forming Iran's first line of defense in the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md), [Persian Gulf](24761.md) and abroad.[^cite_note-Middle_East_Institute-3] It is generally considered to be a conventional [green-water navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green-water_navy), as it mostly operates regionally,[^cite_note-The_Iran_Primer-4] namely in the [Red Sea](26191.md), [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), and northwest quarter of the [Indian Ocean](14580.md).[^cite_note-Middle_East_Institute-3] The Navy aims to develop [blue-water capabilities:](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-water_navy) in July 2016, it announced plans to establish a presence in the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md),[^cite_note-fas.org_2016-5] and as of May 2021 had sent ships into the region.[^cite_note-6]

NEDAJA shares many functions and responsibilities with the IRGC Navy, with distinctions in [military strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_strategy) and equipment: In contrast to the IRGC Navy, which is equipped with small [fast-attack craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast-attack_craft), the backbone of the Artesh naval inventory consists of larger [surface ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_ship), including [frigates](11464.md) and [corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette), and [submarines](28825.md).[^cite_note-The_Iran_Primer-4]

The Artesh Navy [had a large fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_ships_of_the_Islamic_Republic_of_Iran_Navy) prior to 2026, and was ranked highly among the world's top fleets,[^cite_note-7] and was described in 2012 as maintaining "robust" capabilities by regional standards.[^cite_note-8] As of 2019, the Navy had several joint exercises with [Russia](25391.md) and [China](5405.md) called the [Marine Security Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Security_Belt), which it aims to conduct annually.[^cite_note-mehrconfession-9]

## History and overview

Main article: [Naval history of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_history_of_Iran)

See also: [Achaemenid navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_navy), [Sasanian navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_navy), and [Afsharid navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afsharid_navy)

An Iranian navy in one form or another has existed since [Achaemenid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_family_tree) times and the [First Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Persian_Empire) around 500 BC. The Islamic Republic of Iran Navy came into being when the former **Imperial Iranian Navy** (IIN) of the [Pahlavi Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavi_era) was renamed following the [Iranian Revolution](347268.md) in 1979.

### 1939–1979

The Iranian navy was rebuilt after being almost completely destroyed during the [Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Soviet_invasion_of_Iran) in [World War II](32927.md). Following World War II, the fleet began replacing destroyed warships with [destroyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destroyers), [frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frigates) and many smaller vessels, including [powerboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powerboat) and [hovercraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hovercraft), many of which originated from the US and UK, which had played a part in destroying much of the original equipment in World War II.

[Mohammad Reza Pahlavi](20065598.md), the last [Shah of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_monarchs_of_Persia), ordered four modern [general purpose destroyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidd-class_destroyer) from the United States and eight modified [*Kortenaer*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kortenaer-class_frigate) frigates from [Royal Schelde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Schelde), but both contracts were canceled after the 1979 Iranian revolution. The destroyers were instead commissioned in the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) as the [*Kidd* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidd-class_destroyer), while construction of the frigates had not yet started.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]



Frigate [IRIS *Sahand*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS_Sahand_\(1969\)) on flames after being attacked by the [US Navy](20518076.md) during the [Tanker War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanker_War), 1988

Following this was the US-led arms embargo on Iran and the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War). The arms embargo restricted Iran's ability to maintain and equip its navy. It had to find new sources of armaments. Equipment and weaponry were imported from the Soviet Union, China, North Korea and later, Russia. Iran acquired three Kilo-class submarines from Russia, as well as 10 [Houdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_021_class_missile_boat#Houdong_class) fast attack craft from [China](5405.md). [Russia](25391.md) and [India](14533.md) were reportedly assisting Iran with training and operating its Kilo-class submarines. Iran acquired additional mine warfare capability, and upgraded some of its older surface ships.

In December 1997, Rear Admiral Mohammad Karim Tavakoli, commander of the First Naval Zone, with his headquarters at [Bandar Abbas](464348.md), claimed that the Iranian Navy had completed design work on three multirole corvettes and a small submarine, to be built in Iran.

### 2000–2020

In terms of major surface ships, Iran relied on its [*Alvand*-class frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvand-class_frigate), and the new [*Moudge*-class frigates](10946960.md), which were indigenously developed in Iran and are reverse-engineered *Alvand* class with modern electronics, radar and armament. As of 2012, its largest ships were four frigates and three [corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette). These ships are supported by three [Russian](25391.md)-built SSK [Kilo-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilo-class_submarine) [attack submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_submarine) and [Ghadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghadir-class_submarine) and [Nahang-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahang-class_submarine) [mini submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_submarine).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

In December 2014, Iran conducted joint wargames involving the Iranian Army, Air Force and Navy. The naval phase took part in a wide area, ranging from the Persian Gulf to the northern Indian Ocean and to the Gulf of Aden. New systems were tested, including new anti-ship cruise missiles, an electro-magnetic and acoustic naval mine-sweeping system and the "Fateh" submarine.

In July 2016, the Navy said that it would establish a presence in the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md), of unspecified duration.[^cite_note-fas.org_2016-5]

In August 2019, Iran's navy deployed two warships, the destroyer *Sahand* and the supply ship/replenishment oiler *Kharg*, to the Gulf of Aden to protect commercial shipping.[^cite_note-navytimes.com_2019-14] In September 2019, the head of the Iran navy said it was ready to defend its marine borders, and denied US and Saudi claims that Iran had orchestrated recent attacks on Saudi oil sites.[^cite_note-reuters.com_2019-15]

In November 2019, the Islamic Republic News Agency reported that Iran's navy had sent a fleet of 64 ships to the Gulf of Aden to "safeguard Iran's interests" in an "insecure seafaring region." The month prior, a maritime coalition led by the United States had launched operations in the Gulf of Aden.[^cite_note-bloomberg.com_2019-16] The Iran and US navies subsequently encountered each other in the Strait of Hormuz on 23 November 2019, with no conflict.[^cite_note-haaretz.com_2019-17]

On 4 December 2019, the Iranian Navy's head Rear Admiral [Hossein Khanzadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossein_Khanzadi) stated that the 'Marine Security Belt' exercises with China and Russia would begin on 27 December in the northern Indian Ocean.[^cite_note-timesofisrael.com_2019-18] On 30 December 2019, Rear Admiral Khanzadi acknowledged during a televised interview with the semi-official [Mehr News Agency](2707394.md) that the Iranian Navy conducted joint exercises with Russia and China and will continue to do so on an annual basis.[^cite_note-mehrconfession-9] Khanzadi stated that the drills were now needed due to a lack of coordination.[^cite_note-mehrconfession-9] He also stated that invitations which invited other countries to participate in the drills were unsuccessful.[^cite_note-mehrconfession-9]

In December 2019, Khanzadi said that efforts by other countries to form alliances against Iran in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) were "pointless".[^cite_note-mehrconfession-9]

In September 2022, for the first time, the Navy captured two [U.S. Navy](20518076.md) sea drones in the Red Sea.[^cite_note-19] They were soon returned to U.S. control.

### 2026 Iran war

In February 2026, [the United States and Israel began to launch strikes](82533582.md) on Iran. From early March 2026, the Navy began to suffer significant losses. Shortly after the beginning of the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), on 1 March 2026, US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) said nine IRIN vessels had been sunk. Iran has yet to confirm these numbers.[^cite_note-25] On 3 March 2026, [United States Central Command](338482.md) (CENTCOM) reported that it had eliminated eleven Iranian naval vessels in the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md). CENTCOM provided video footage of the elimination of one vessel.[^cite_note-26]

On 4 March 2026, [Pete Hegseth](34076202.md), the [US Secretary of Defense](43998.md), announced that a US [submarine](28825.md) had sunk the [IRIS *Dena*](74329424.md) 75 kilometres (47 mi) off the coast of [Sri Lanka](26750.md), between 6am and 7am local time (0\:30 to 1\:30 GMT), with a [Mark 48 torpedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_48_torpedo); periscope footage was released later. The ship was believed to have 180 personnel on board, and was underway after participating in the Indian International Fleet Review 2026 and [MILAN exercises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_\(naval_exercise\)). Sri Lankan authorities reported recovering 87 bodies and 32 critically injured.[^cite_note-27]

The operational status of the remainder of Iran's naval vessels remains unclear.

Other Iranian vessels, including the submarine IRIS *Fateh*, were reportedly destroyed or badly damaged in strikes linked to operations by the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) and US forces. Because of these losses, analysts say parts of Iran's navy are becoming increasingly weakened and less effective in combat.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

## Structure

### Persian Gulf and Arabian Sea

The Iranian Navy is divided into [three naval districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Fleet_\(Iran\)) in the Persian Gulf and in the Arabian Sea. The vast majority of surface ships and submarines operate in the Persian Gulf and the Arabian Sea.

In 1977, the bulk of the fleet was shifted from [Khorramshahr](464970.md) to the new headquarters at [Bandar-e Abbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar-e_Abbas). [Bushehr](205669.md) was the other main base. Smaller facilities were located at Khorramshahr, [Khark Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khark_Island), and [Bandar-e Imam Khomeini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar-e_Imam_Khomeini) (formerly known as Bandar-e Shahpur).

The naval base at [Bandar Beheshti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar_Beheshti) (formerly known as Chah Bahar) on the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md) had been under construction since the late 1970s and in late 1987 still was not completed. Smaller facilities were located near the Strait of Hormuz.[^cite_note-auto-35]

Smaller facilities include:

- [Abu Musa](931858.md) – small docking facility on the island's west end; located near Abu Musa Airport

- [Bandar Beheshti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar_Beheshti) (Chah Bahar) – port and base facilities in the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md)

- [Bandar-e Khomeini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar-e_Khomeini) – small sheltered base located near the border with [Iraq](7515928.md)

- [Bandar-e Mahshahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahshahr) – small base located near Bandar-e Khomeini

- [Bushehr](205669.md) – repair and storage facility in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md); home to Navy Technical Supply Center and R\&D center

- [Jask](19973566.md) – small base located across from [Oman](22316.md) and [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE) in southeastern Iran at the mouth of the [Straits of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Hormuz)

- [Kharg Island](2210066.md) – base on the island and located northwest of Bushehr, home to hovercraft fleet

- [Khorramshahr](464970.md) – former naval HQ; now repair and shipbuilding facilities

- [Larak](2832280.md) – small base on the island and near Bandar-e Abbas

- [Qeshm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qeshm) – small port facility near Kharg and Bandar-e Abbas

- [Sirri](9169338.md) – island port facility located in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) and across from [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE)

### Caspian Sea

The [Fourth Naval District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Fleet_\(Iran\)) in the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) is headquartered at [Bandar-e Anzali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar-e_Anzali). It is the major training base and home of the force, which consisted of a few [patrol boats](536773.md) and a [minesweeper](217118.md).[^cite_note-auto-35]

### Marine, Missile, and Aviation forces

The [Islamic Republic of Iran Navy Marine Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Iran_Navy_Marine_Command) includes four marine infantry brigades.

The Navy's [Missile Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Iran_Navy_Missile_Command) operates the [Kowsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowsar), [Nasr-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasr-1), [Noor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noor_\(missile\)), [Ghader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qader_\(missile\)), [Ghadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghadir_\(missile\)), and [Ra'ad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra'ad_\(anti-ship_missile\)) missiles.[^cite_note-36] The Missile Command also operates the [Abu Mahdi missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Mahdi_\(missile\)), made public in 2020.[^cite_note-37]

The [Islamic Republic of Iran Navy Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Iran_Navy_Aviation) includes about 85 helicopters and 13 fixed-wing aircraft.

Finally [Noshahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noshahr) is home to Iman Khomeini University for Naval Science, the naval staff college.

### 2000–2010



Iran has three Russian-built Kilo-class submarines patrolling the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). Iran is also producing its own submarines as of 2010.

[^cite_note-40]



Kilo-class submarines of Iran

In August 2000, Iran announced that it had launched its first domestically produced light submarine or [swimmer delivery vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimmer_delivery_vehicle), named the Al-Sabiha 15 because of its 15-meter (49 ft) length, in a ceremony at the [Bandar Abbas](464348.md) naval base. In May 2005, the Iran navy announced that it had launched its first Ghadir-class [midget submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midget_submarine) and in March 2006 announced that it had launched another submarine named *Nahang* (Persian: whale).

In 2000, the Islamic Republic of Iran Navy Aviation significantly improved its capability by taking delivery, from Russia, of a number of [Mi-8 AMT (Mi-171)](957233.md) transport/attack helicopters. Under a contract signed in 1999, Russia agreed to supply 21 [Mi-171s](957233.md) to Iran. Delivery was completed in 2001. The exact number destined for the navy was unknown. In summer 2001, there were indications that Iran would order a further 20 Mi-171s, although as of mid-2004, it was not known if this had occurred.

In November 2002, sources at Iran's [Aerospace Industries Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_Industries_Organisation) (AIO) and the [China Aerospace Science and Industry Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Aerospace_Science_and_Industry_Corporation) (COSIC) confirmed that the two groups were working on common anti-ship missile production and development. The effort, which Iranian sources call [Project Noor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Noor?action=edit\&redlink=1), covers the short-range C-701 and the long-range C-802 weapons developed by COSIC's China National Precision Machinery Import and Export Co subsidiary. The possibility that a formal collaborative project was under way was first raised in 1998, when Iran displayed an Anti-Ship missile design similar to the 15-kilometer (9.3 mi) range [C-701](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-701) shortly after the Chinese system was unveiled.

An AIO spokesperson confirmed that Project Noor involves the C-701. Officials in the same company describe the weapon as "a long-range, turbojet-powered, sea-skimming Anti-Ship missile," which better fits the 120 km (75 mi) range [C-802](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-802), and suggests that the co-operation agreement may cover both weapon systems. In early 2004, Iran announced the release of a new cruise missile program named Raad (Thunder). The Raad appears to be a modification of the Chinese [HY-2 (CSSC-3)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silkworm_missile) [anti-ship missile](140725.md), one of a series of missiles China developed from the original Soviet-era [P21 (SS-N-2C)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-15_Termit) design.

In September 2003, Iran's domestically produced [Sina-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sina-class_fast_attack_craft) (reverse engineered from the [*Kaman* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman-class_fast_attack_craft)) missile boat [*Paykan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS_Paykan), equipped with modern anti-ship missiles and modern electronics, entered service in the Islamic Republic of Iran Navy. The ship was launched in the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) to protect Iran's interests there and was mentioned among the achievements of the Iranian Navy by Rear Admiral [Habibollah Sayyari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habibollah_Sayyari).

In September 2006, Iran commissioned their second self-made Sina-class missile boat, [*Joshan*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIS_Joshan). Built in memory of the original *Joshan*, lost in the Persian Gulf during [Operation Praying Mantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Praying_Mantis) in April 1988. According to Iran's Navy commander Admiral Kouchaki, *Joshan* has a claimed speed of over 45-[knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_\(unit\)) (83 km/h; 52 mph)[^cite_note-41] and "enjoys the world's latest technology, especially with regard to its military, electrical and electronic systems, frame and chassis, and it has the capabilities required for launching powerful missiles."



Domestically produced Iranian frigate [*Jamaran*](40302891.md)

In 2002, Iran announced it would start the production of its first domestically produced destroyer. By most international standards, the ship, the first of the [*Moudge* class](10946960.md), would be considered a light frigate or a corvette. In November 2007, Iran's rear admiral Habibollah Sayyari announced that Iran would launch its first domestically produced [destroyer](8779.md), [*Jamaran*](40302891.md), though internationally rated as a frigate, and an Iranian Ghadir-class submarine. It is said to be a sonar evading stealth submarine. Initially known as *Moje*, then *Moje I*, finally *Jamaran*, appears to be a development of the *Alvand* class.

The *Moudge* or *Moje*-class guided missile frigate entered service in 2010. Another frigate in the same class, named *Damavand*, was commissioned in the port of Bandar Anzali in the Caspian Sea in 2013. This ship, just like *Jamaran*, has the capability to carry helicopters, anti-ship missiles, [surface-to-air missiles](182664.md), [torpedoes](31424.md), modern guns and air defence guns. The ship is also equipped with electronic warfare devices. The two mentioned frigates brought Iran's frigate arsenal from three to five., Two others are being built, to be added to Iran's fleet of warships in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md).

In March 2006, the navy deployed a submarine named *Nahang* (Whale), with pictures broadcast by state media showing a minisub.

In February 2008, the Iranian Defense Ministry announced that 74 domestically produced "gunboats" (small missile boats) had entered service with the Iranian Navy.[^cite_note-42] The Navy reported as to having the [Hoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoot_\(missile\)) supercavitating torpedo and the [Thaqeb (missile)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaqeb_\(missile\)) in trials or service, though reliable information is scarce.

### 2010–2020



A [Qader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qader_\(missile\)) missile system, during the Velayat-90 Naval Exercise in 2012

In February 2010, Iran's Deputy Navy Commander Captain [Mansour Maqsoudlou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansour_Maqsoudlou?action=edit\&redlink=1) announced that Iran was planning to design and manufacture domestically built aircraft carriers. The initial designs for building the carriers were approved as of 2010 and the process of research and the design for the aircraft carrier is currently being looked into by the Iranian government.

In 2012, Iran overhauled one of its Kilo-class submarines, IRS *Younis*. Iran completed this re-haul at Bandar Abbas naval base. The Iranian Navy modernized and re-commissioned the 1,135-ton [*Bayandor*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayandor-class_corvette) corvettes, equipped with Noor anti-ship cruise missiles and torpedo launchers. Another modern frigate named *Sahand*, with 2,000 tons displacement was being fitted with weapons and equipment in Bandar Abbas naval base, and was planned for launch in 2013.

In July 2012, foreign analysts reported that Iran was gaining new deployment capabilities, allegedly to strike at US warships in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) in the case of an armed conflict, amassing an arsenal of anti-ship missiles, while expanding its fleet of [Fast attack craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_attack_craft) and [submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarines). Many of the systems were developed with foreign assistance, such as Silkworm anti-ship missiles, which are Chinese-made, and high-speed torpedoes based on Russian designs. In weeks prior, Iranian leadership had been threatening to shut down shipping in the gulf region as retaliation for any attacks by the United States on its nuclear facilities.[^cite_note-washingtonpost.com_2012-43][^cite_note-44]

In February 2019, newspapers reported that Iran unveiled a domestically produced submarine capable of firing cruise missiles.[^cite_note-45] In November 2019, Iran's navy announced the mass production of the Jask cruise missile, which is launched from Iranian submarines. It unveiled a vertical takeoff and landing ([V/STOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V/STOL)) naval drone named Pelican-2, which had already been deployed on "naval fleets in international waters."[^cite_note-xinhuanet.com_2019-46]
