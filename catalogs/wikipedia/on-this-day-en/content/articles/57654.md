**Tehran**[^cite_note-7] is the [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitals_of_Iran) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Iran) of [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-8] It is also the capital of [Tehran province](202698.md) and the administrative center for [Tehran County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_County) and its [Central District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_District_\(Tehran_County\)).[^cite_note-Tehran_Province_Structure-9] With a population of around 9 million in the city,[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] and 16.8 million in the metropolitan area,[^cite_note-13] Tehran is the [most populous city](17288411.md) in Iran and [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia),[^cite_note-14] the [largest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_metropolitan_areas_of_the_Middle_East) in the [Middle East](19323.md) and the 24th-most-populous metropolitan area in the world. [Greater Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Tehran) includes several municipalities, including [Eslamshahr](7031187.md), [Shahriar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahriar,_Tehran_province), [Qods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qods,_Iran), [Malard](17437690.md), [Golestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golestan,_Tehran), [Varamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varamin), [Pakdasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakdasht), [Qarchak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qarchak), [Nasimshahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasimshahr), [Parand](1414890.md), [Pardis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardis), [Andisheh](1414854.md), [Fardis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fardis), [Tajrish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajrish), and [Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rey,_Iran).

In [classical antiquity](252905.md), part of the territory of present-day Tehran was occupied by [Rhages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray,_Iran), a prominent [Iranian](14653.md) city that was not destroyed in the medieval [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia) and [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquest_of_Khwarezmia) invasions.[^cite_note-15] Rey is absorbed into the metropolitan area of Greater Tehran. Tehran was first chosen as the capital of Iran in 1786 by [Agha Mohammad Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agha_Mohammad_Khan_Qajar) of the [Qajar dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty), because of its proximity to Iran's territories in the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus)—which were contested in the [Russo-Iranian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_Wars)—and to avoid the vying factions of prior ruling Iranian dynasties; the [capital of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitals_of_Iran) had been moved several times throughout its long history, with Tehran becoming the 51st. Under [Reza Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Shah), Tehran witnessed Iran's first [institute of higher learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_ul-Funun_\(Persia\)), [bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Bank_of_Persia), [railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran%E2%80%93Rey_Railway), and museum. Large-scale construction works began in the 1920s, and Tehran became a destination for mass migrations from all over Iran, especially in the 20th century.[^cite_note-16] Tehran was at the center of the [Iranian Revolution](347268.md), and experienced major bombing damage during the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War), and the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).

Tehran is home to many historical sites, including the [World Heritage Site](44940.md) [Golestan Palace](313051.md) of the [Qajar dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) and the [Masoudieh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masoudieh_Mansion), [Sa'dabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa'dabad_Complex), [Niavaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niavaran_Complex) and [Marmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble_Palace_\(Tehran\)) palace complexes of both the Qajar and [Pahlavi dynasties](338883.md). Landmarks include the [Azadi Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadi_Tower), a memorial built in 1971 to mark the [2,500th anniversary of the Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2,500-year_celebration_of_the_Persian_Empire); the [Milad Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milad_Tower), the world's [sixth-tallest self-supporting tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_towers), completed in 2007; and the [Tabiat Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabiat_Bridge), completed in 2014.[^cite_note-archdaily-17][^cite_note-tabnak-18][^cite_note-Mohammad-19][^cite_note-20]

Tehran has been described as a cultural "melting pot", hosting more [Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Azerbaijanis) than any other city in the world.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Tehran is served by [Imam Khomeini International Airport](716542.md), alongside the domestic [Mehrabad Airport](2025041.md), [a central railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_railway_station), [Tehran Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Metro), the [Tehran Bus Rapid Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Bus_Rapid_Transit) system, [trolleybuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Tehran), and [a large network of highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Expressways_in_Tehran).

Because of [air pollution](10934212.md), earthquakes and [water scarcity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_water_shortage), there have been plans to relocate the capital to another area, although none have been approved. A 2016 survey of 230 cities across the globe by [Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercer_\(consulting_firm\)) ranked Tehran 203rd for [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life).[^cite_note-27] According to the [Global Destinations Cities Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism#MasterCard_Global_Destination_Cities_Index) in 2016, Tehran was among the top ten fastest [growing tourism destinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Iran).[^cite_note-28] In 2016, the [Tehran City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_City_Council) declared 6 October "Tehran Day", celebrating the date in 1907 when the city officially became the capital of Iran.[^cite_note-29]

## Etymology

Various theories on the origin of the name Tehran have been put forward. Iranian linguist Zana Kamrani, in an article "Shemiran-Tehran", suggests that Tehran, and Kehran mean "the warm place", and "Shemiran" means "the cool place". He lists cities with the same base and suffix and studied the components of the word in ancient [Iranian languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_languages), and came to the conclusion that Tehran and Kehran meant the same thing in different Iranian language families, as the constant "t" and "k" are close to each other in such languages. He also provided evidence that cities named "Shemiran" were colder than those named "Tehran" or "Kehran". He considered other theories not consider the ancient history of Iranian languages, such as "Tirgan" theory and "Tahran" theory [folk etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_etymology).[^cite_note-30]

The official City of Tehran website says that "Tehran" comes from the Persian words "Tah" meaning "end", or "bottom", and "Ran" meaning "\[mountain] slope"; the bottom of the mountain (ته کوه), referring to Tehran's position at the foot of the [Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz) mountains.[^cite_note-31]

In English, it is also spelt "**Teheran**",[^cite_note-32] with both variants being used in books since at least 1800, and "Teheran" being the dominant form from after WWII until shortly before the Islamic Revolution.[^cite_note-33]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Tehran).

Archaeological remains from the ancient city of [Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray,_Iran) suggest that settlement in Tehran dates back over 6,000 years.[^cite_note-34]

### Classical era

Tehran is in the historical [Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_\(region\)) region ([Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian_language): 𐎶𐎠𐎭 *Māda*) in northwestern Iran. By the time of the [Median Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes), part of present-day Tehran was a suburb of the prominent Median city of Rhages ([Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian_language): 𐎼𐎥𐎠 *Ragā*). In the [Avesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avesta)'s *[Videvdat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vendidad)* (i, 15), Rhages is mentioned as the 12th sacred place created by [Ohrmazd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahura_Mazda).[^cite_note-Minorsky-35] In [Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) inscriptions, Rhages appears as a province ([Bistun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behistun_Inscription) 2, 10–18). From Rhages, [Darius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_I) sent reinforcements to his father [Hystaspes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hystaspes_\(father_of_Darius_I\)), who was putting down a rebellion in [Parthia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthia) (Bistun 3, 1–10).[^cite_note-Minorsky-35] Some [Middle Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Persian) texts give Rhages as the birthplace of [Zoroaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroaster),[^cite_note-36] although modern historians generally place the birth of Zoroaster in [Khorasan province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorasan_province).

[Mount Damavand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Damavand), the highest peak of Iran, which is located near Tehran, is an important location in [Ferdowsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdowsi)'s *[Šāhnāme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahnameh)*,[^cite_note-Tafazolli-37] an Iranian [epic poem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_poem) based on the [ancient legends of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_mythology). It appears in the epics as the homeland of the [protoplast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protoplast_\(religion\)) [Keyumars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyumars), the birthplace of King [Manuchehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuchehr), the place where King [Fereydun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fereydun) bound the dragon fiend [Zahhak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zahhak) (Bivarasp), and the place where [Arash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arash) shot his arrow.[^cite_note-Tafazolli-37]

### Medieval period

In 641, during the reign of the [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire), [Yazdgerd III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazdegerd_III) issued his last appeal to the nation from Rhages, before fleeing to [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorasan).[^cite_note-Minorsky-35] Rhages was dominated by the [Parthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire) [House of Mihran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Mihran), and [Siyavakhsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siyavakhsh)—the son of [Mehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihran_Bahram-i_Chubin), the son of [Bahram Chobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahram_Chobin)—who resisted the seventh-century [Muslim invasion of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia).[^cite_note-Minorsky-35] Because of this resistance, when the Arabs captured Rhages, they ordered the town destroyed and rebuilt anew by the aristocrat [Farrukhzad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farrukhzad).[^cite_note-Minorsky-35]

In the ninth century, Tehran was a well-known village, but less so than Rhages, flourishing nearby. Rhages was described in detail by tenth-century Muslim geographers.[^cite_note-Minorsky-35] Despite the interest that Arabian [Baghdad](4492.md) displayed in Rhages, the number of Arabs in the city remained insignificant and the population mainly consisted of Iranians of all classes.[^cite_note-Minorsky-35][^cite_note-38]

The [Oghuz Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oghuz_Turks) invaded Rhages in 1035 and again in 1042, but the city was recovered under the [Seljuks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire) and the [Khwarezmians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khwarazmian_dynasty).[^cite_note-Minorsky-35] Medieval writer [Najm od Din Razi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najm_al-Din_Razi) declared the population of Rhages about 500,000 before the [Mongol invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquest_of_Khwarezmia). In the 13th century, the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) invaded Rhages, laid the city to ruins, and massacred many of its inhabitants.[^cite_note-Minorsky-35] Others escaped to Tehran. In July 1404, [Castilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile) ambassador [Ruy González de Clavijo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruy_Gonz%C3%A1lez_de_Clavijo) visited Tehran on a journey to [Samarkand](51491.md), the capital of Turco-Mongol conqueror [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur), the ruler of Iran at the time. He described it in his diary as an unwalled region.

### Early modern era

Italian traveler [Pietro della Valle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_della_Valle) passed through Tehran overnight in 1618, and in his memoirs called the city *Taheran*. English traveler [Thomas Herbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Thomas_Herbert,_1st_Baronet) entered Tehran in 1627, and mentioned it as *Tyroan*. Herbert states that the city had about 3,000 houses.[^cite_note-britannica1911-39]



A portrait of the Shah of Iran [Agha Mohammad Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agha_Mohammad_Khan_Qajar), at London's [V\&A Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_and_Albert_Museum)

In the early 18th century, [Karim Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karim_Khan_Zand) of the [Zand dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zand_dynasty) ordered a palace and a government office built in Tehran, possibly to declare the city his capital; but he later moved his government to [Shiraz](39622.md). Eventually, Qajar king [Agha Mohammad Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agha_Mohammad_Khan_Qajar) chose Tehran as the capital of Iran in 1786.[^cite_note-books.google.nl-40] Khan's choice of his capital was based on a similar concern for the control of both northern and southern Iran.[^cite_note-books.google.nl-40] He was aware of the loyalties of the inhabitants of former capitals [Isfahan](223149.md) and Shiraz to the [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) and Zand dynasties respectively, and was wary of the power of the local notables in these cities.[^cite_note-books.google.nl-40] Thus, he probably viewed Tehran's lack of a substantial urban structure as a blessing, because it minimized the chances of resistance to his rule by the notables and by the general public.[^cite_note-books.google.nl-40] He had to remain within close reach of [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_\(Iran\)) and Iran's integral [northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasus) and [southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Caucasus) [Caucasian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus)[^cite_note-books.google.nl-40]—at that time not yet irrevocably lost per the treaties of [Golestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Gulistan) and [Turkmenchay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turkmenchay) to the neighboring [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire)—which would follow in the course of the 19th century.[^cite_note-41]



Architectural landmarks of 19th-century Tehran

After 50 years of [Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar) rule, the city still barely had more than 80,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-books.google.nl-40] Up until the 1870s, Tehran consisted of a walled citadel, a roofed [bazaar](556904.md), and the three main neighborhoods of [Udlajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oudlajan), Chale-Meydan, and Sangelaj, where the majority resided.

During the long reign of [Naser al-Din Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naser_al-Din_Shah_Qajar) (1848-1896), Tehran witnessed Iran's first [institute of higher learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_ul-Funun_\(Persia\)), [bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Bank_of_Persia), [railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran%E2%80%93Rey_Railway) and museum.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] The [Golestan Palace](313051.md) was significantly rebuilt and expanded in 1865 by Haji Ab ol Hasan Mimar Navai, including the Brilliant Hall, the Mirror Hall and the [Edifice of the Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shams-ol-Emareh). The city expanded rapidly through multiple development plans.[^cite_note-45] The first development plan in 1855 emphasized traditional spatial structure. The second, under the supervision of [Dar al-Fonun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_al-Fonun) in 1878, included new city walls, in the form of a perfect octagon with an area of 19 square kilometers, mimicking the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) cities of Europe.[^cite_note-Vahdat_Za-2011-46] Tehran was 19.79 square kilometers and had expanded more than fourfold.[^cite_note-47]

### Late modern era



The [Triumph of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triumph_of_Tehran): [Sardar Asad II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali-Qoli_Khan_Bakhtiari) and [Sepahsalar e Tonekaboni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Vali_Khan_Tonekaboni) conquering Tehran in July 1909

Growing awareness of civil rights resulted in the [Constitutional Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Constitutional_Revolution) and the [first constitution of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Constitution_of_1906) in 1906. On 2 June 1907, the parliament passed a law on local governance known as the *Baladie* ([municipal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_law)), providing a detailed outline of issues such as the role of councils within the city, the members' qualifications, the election process, and the requirements to be entitled to vote.

[Mohammad Ali Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Ali_Shah_Qajar) abolished the constitution and [bombarded the parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_bombardment_of_the_Majlis) with the help of the Russian-controlled [Cossack Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Cossack_Brigade) on 23 June 1908. That was followed by the capture of the city by the revolutionary forces of [Ali-Qoli Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali-Qoli_Khan_Bakhtiari) (Sardar Asad II) and [Mohammad Vali Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Vali_Khan_Tonekaboni) (Sepahsalar e Tonekaboni) on 13 July 1909. As a result, the monarch was exiled and replaced by his son [Ahmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Qajar), and the parliament was re-established.

During the [Persian campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_campaign_\(World_War_I\)) of [World War I](4764461.md), Russian forces that were occupying the northwest of Iran marched around [Qazvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazvin) and approached Tehran, causing a crisis and the dissolution of [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Consultative_Assembly). [Ahmad Shah Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Qajar) and his entourage decided to leave Tehran and move the capital to another place, sparking fears of rebellion in other cities.[^cite_note-Bahar-1992-48] During the [Battle of Robat Karim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Robat_Karim), Iranian forces led by Heydar Latifiyan prevented the Russians from taking Tehran, despite the latter winning the battle.[^cite_note-49] This also allowed government functions to be moved to Qom and then to Isfahan, while the monarchy remained in Tehran.[^cite_note-Bahar-1992-48]

#### Pahlavi era



Under [Reza Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Shah)'s reign, Tehran underwent a transformative modernization, with the establishment of urban infrastructure, modern streets, educational institutions, and public amenities, marking a pivotal step toward a structured, contemporary capital.

[^cite_note-50]

After World War I, the constituent assembly elected [Reza Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Shah) of the [Pahlavi dynasty](338883.md) as the new monarch, who immediately suspended the Baladie law of 1907, replacing the decentralized and autonomous city councils with centralist approaches to governance and planning.[^cite_note-Vahdat_Za-2011-46] From the 1920s to the 1930s, under the rule of Reza Shah, the city was rebuilt. Several old buildings, including parts of the [Golestan Palace](313051.md), [Takyeh Dowlat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takyeh_Dowlat), and [Toopkhaneh Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toopkhaneh_Square), were replaced with modern buildings influenced by classical Iranian architecture, particularly the buildings of the [National Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Melli_Iran), the police headquarters, the telegraph office, and the military academy.



The [University of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tehran) was founded in 1934, a landmark in Iran's modernization. Its establishment introduced advanced education, modern infrastructure, and public amenities, transforming Tehran into a vibrant, contemporary capital.

Changes to the urban fabric began with the street-widening act of 1933, which served as a framework for changes in all other cities. The [Grand Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Bazaar,_Tehran) was divided in half and many historic buildings were demolished and replaced by wide straight avenues,[^cite_note-51] and the traditional texture of the city was replaced with intersecting cruciform streets that created large roundabouts in major public spaces such as the bazaar. As an attempt to create a network for easy transportation within the city, the old citadel and city walls were demolished in 1937, replaced by wide streets cutting through the urban fabric. By 1937, the city was heavily influenced by modernist planning patterns of zoning and gridiron networks.[^cite_note-Vahdat_Za-2011-46]

During [World War II](32927.md), Tehran was occupied as a result of the [Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Soviet_invasion_of_Iran). In 1943, Tehran was the site of the [Tehran Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Conference), attended by U.S. President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt), Soviet premier [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin), and British Prime Minister [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill).

The establishment of the planning organization of Iran in 1948 resulted in the first socioeconomic development plan covering the period from 1949 to 1955. These plans not only failed to slow the unbalanced growth of Tehran but, with the 1962 land reforms that Reza Shah's son and successor, [Mohammad Reza Shah](20065598.md) named the *[White Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Revolution)*, Tehran's chaotic growth was further accentuated.

In the 1960s and 1970s, Tehran developed rapidly under Mohammad Reza Shah. Modern buildings altered the face of Tehran, and ambitious projects were planned for the following decades. To resolve the problem of [social exclusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_exclusion), the first comprehensive plan was approved in 1968. The consortium of Iranian architect [Abd-ol-Aziz Farmanfarmaian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdol-Aziz_Mirza_Farmanfarmaian) and the American firm of [Victor Gruen Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gruen) identified the main problems blighting the city as high-density suburbs, air and water pollution, inefficient infrastructure, unemployment, and rural-urban migration. Eventually, the whole plan was marginalized by the [1979 Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Revolution) and the subsequent [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War).[^cite_note-Vahdat_Za-2011-46]

Tehran's most famous landmark, the [Azadi Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadi_Tower), was built by the order of the Shah in 1971. It was designed by [Hossein Amanat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossein_Amanat), an architect whose design won a competition, combining elements of classical [Sasanian architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_architecture) with post-classical Iranian architecture. Formerly known as the *Shahyad Tower*, it was built to commemorate the [2,500th anniversary of the Imperial State of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2,500-year_celebration_of_the_Persian_Empire).

### Islamic Republic



[Azadi Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadi_Tower) during the 1979 Iranian Revolution

Following the 1979 [Iranian Revolution](347268.md), Tehran underwent profound transformations in its urban landscape, political structure, and social dynamics. The revolution led to the establishment of the Islamic Republic of Iran, which brought about a shift from a pro-Western monarchy to a theocratic system based on Islamic principles. This change was reflected in various aspects of Tehran's development.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] Politically, the revolution resulted in the consolidation of power by forces loyal to [Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini](26232.md), the leader of the revolution. Several million Tehranis greeted [Khomeini's return to Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhollah_Khomeini's_return_to_Iran).[^cite_note-55] This period saw the establishment of new governance structures aligned with Islamic ideologies.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

During the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) from 1980 to 1988 and as a part of the [War of the cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_cities), Tehran was repeatedly targeted by airstrikes and [Scud missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud_missile) attacks leading to substantial infrastructural damage.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

#### 21st century

The 435-meter-high [Milad Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milad_Tower), one of the proposed development projects of pre-revolutionary Iran,[^cite_note-SadCity-60] was completed in 2007 and has become a famous landmark of Tehran. [Tabiat Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabiat_Bridge), a 270-meter [pedestrian overpass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footbridge)[^cite_note-archdaily-17] that was designed by award-winning architect [Leila Araghian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leila_Araghian), was completed in 2014. The city municipality started planting a large number of imported palm trees, replacing endemic plane trees.[^cite_note-61] In the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War) of June 2025, Tehran was attacked by Israeli airstrikes[^cite_note-62] and, in an unprecedented move, the people of Tehran committed mass evacuations.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] The city has been under attack in the ongoing [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-x238-66] On 8 March 2026, Israeli strikes on oil depots near Tehran caused the city to be engulfed in a cloud of thick black smoke, causing a dangerous black [acid rainfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_rain).[^cite_note-b673-67] Residents were advised by Iranian authorities to stay indoors, wear masks when outside, and conserve food and fuel.[^cite_note-z891-68]

After the ceasefire, [Bloomberg](3057255.md) reports that Tehran has suffered extensive destruction: buildings torn open, piles of rubble, and neighborhoods partially devastated. An analysis by the American agency of the Iranian capital estimates that 2,816 buildings were destroyed there: about 32% were linked to military activities, 25% to industry, 21% to civilian uses, while 19% belonged to the commercial sector and 2% to government infrastructure. The municipality of Tehran also states that more than 39,000 residential units were severely damaged by the bombings.[^cite_note-69]

### Language and ethnicity

With its cosmopolitan atmosphere, Tehran is home to diverse ethnic and linguistic groups from all over the country. The present-day dominant language is the [Tehrani variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehrani_accent) of the [Persian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language), and the majority of people identify themselves as [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people).[^cite_note-Mohammad-19][^cite_note-tabnak-18] Historically the native language of the Tehran–Ray region was not Persian, which is linguistically Southwest Iranian and originates in [Fars](448049.md), but an extinct Persian dialect and [Northwestern Iranian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Iranian_languages).[^cite_note-72]

[Iranian Azeris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Azerbaijanis) are the second-largest ethnic group, comprising about 10–15% [^cite_note-Iran-Azeris-73][^cite_note-Country_Study_Guide-Azerbaijanis-74] of the population. Ethnic [Mazanderanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazanderani_people) are the third-largest, comprising about 5% of the population.[^cite_note-75] Tehran's other ethnic communities include [Kurds](17068.md), [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Armenians), [Georgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Georgians), [Bakhtyaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhtiari_people), [Talysh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talysh_people), [Baloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people), [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrians_in_Iran), [Arabs](2185.md), [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Jews), and [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians_in_Iran). In the 2010 census by the Sociology Department of the [University of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tehran), in many districts of Tehran across various socio-economic classes in proportion to population sizes of each district and socio-economic class, 63% of the people were born in Tehran, 98% knew Persian, 75% identified themselves as ethnic Persian, and 13% had some degree of proficiency in a European language.[^cite_note-tabnak-18]

There was a drastic change in ethnic-social composition in the early 1980s. After the political, social, and economic consequences of the 1979 Revolution and the years that followed, many Iranian citizens, mostly Tehranis, left Iran. The majority of [Iranian emigrations](9573713.md) have left for the [United States](2234641.md), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranians_in_Germany), [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Iranians), and [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Canadians). With the start of the Iran–Iraq War, a second wave of inhabitants fled the city, especially during the Iraqi air offensives on Tehran. With most major powers backing Iraq at the time, economic isolation gave yet more reason for many inhabitants to leave Tehran and the country. Having left all they had and having struggled to adapt to a new country and build a life, most of them never came back when the war was over. During the war, Tehran received a great number of migrants from the west and the southwest of Iran bordering [Iraq](7515928.md).

### Religion

See also: [Religion in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Iran) and [List of religious centers in Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_religious_centers_in_Tehran)

The majority of religious Tehrani are officially [Twelver Shia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelver_Shia) [Muslims](19541.md), which has been the main religion since the 16th-century [Safavid conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_conversion_of_Iran_to_Shia_Islam). Other religious communities include followers of the [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) and [Mystic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) branches of Islam, Christian denominations, Judaism, [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism), and the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith).

In the 2016 "Tehran Survey", when residents of Tehran were asked about the importance of religion in their life, 53.5% considered it to be "very important / important", 31.1% to be "rather important", 10.5% to be "not very important" and 4.8% to be "not at all important."[^cite_note-76]

There are many religious centres scattered around Tehran, from old to newly built centres, including [mosques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_religious_centers_in_Tehran#Mosques_and_madrasehs), [churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_religious_centers_in_Tehran#Churches), [synagogues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_religious_centers_in_Tehran#Synagogues), and [Zoroastrian fire temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_religious_centers_in_Tehran#Fire_temples). Tehran has a very small third-generation Indian [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) community with a local [gurdwara](482849.md) that was visited by the Indian Prime Minister, [Manmohan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manmohan_Singh) in 2012.[^cite_note-Indian_Prime_Minister_in_Tehran-77]

## Geography

### Location and subdivisions



The districts of Tehran

City of Tehran is bounded in the north by the southern end and the highest point of Alborz Mountain Range ([Tochal Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochal), 3,963m), in the east by a hilly stretch of Alborz (namely, [Sorkheh Hesar National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorkheh_Hesar_National_Park), part of [Khojir National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khojir_National_Park) and the northeastern [badlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badlands)), in the southeast by [Raga Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rey,_Iran) and in the west and southwest by fertile meadows and many villages, fed by numerous rivers and streams, mainly [Karaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaj_River) and [Jajrud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jajrud_\(river\)).

The metropolis is divided into 22 municipal districts, each with its administrative center. Of the 22 municipal districts, 20 are located in [Tehran County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_County)'s [Central District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_District_\(Tehran_County\)), while districts [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_1_\(Tehran\)) and [20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_20_\(Tehran\)) are respectively located in the counties of [Shemiranat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shemiranat_County) and [Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_County,_Iran).

### Topography

Northern Tehran is at the foot of Alborz, starting at an elevation of 1,800 meters (5,900 ft), with many houses and streets on steep slopes and hills. There is a stretch of hills in the middle of the city (which historically divided Tehran and Shemiran) with its highest point at 1,500 metres (4,900 ft). Southwards and westwards of those hills, Tehran is less steep. On the east, there are numerous hills, with the highest point at 1,950 metres (6,400 ft). The southern end of Tehran (Rey) is at 1,100 metres (3,600 ft), next to a single mountain (Raga) at 1,500 metres (4,900 ft).

### Climate



A 2012 urban sustainability analysis of the metropolitan area of Tehran, using the 'Circles of Sustainability' method of the [UN Global Compact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Global_Compact) Cities Programme

Tehran lies at the southern foot of the [Alborz Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz_Mountains) and the northern edge of the [Iranian Central Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_plateau), giving the city a complex climate. According to the [Köppen climate classification system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification_system), the bulk of metropolitan Tehran is classified as a [cold semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid) (*BSk*), while lower elevation suburbs and plains (near Mehrābād Airport) verge on a hot semi-arid (*BSh*) classification. At the same time, the elevated northern districts (such as [Shemiran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shemiran)) approach a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate#Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) (*Csa*) type.

The city's wide elevation range—from approximately 1,100 m above sea level in the southern plains to over 1,800 m in the northern suburbs—yields pronounced topographic micro-climates.[^cite_note-Tools-81] The elevation rises up to 2,000 m (6,600 ft) at the end of [Velenjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velenjak) in northern Tehran. Northern parts of Tehran tend to be cooler, receive slightly more precipitation (including more snow), and retain more greenery. In contrast, the more open, lower-lying southern sections of the city experience stronger daytime heating, less relief through breezes, and larger temperature differences between day and night. The difference between daily maximum and minimum temperatures (known as [diurnal range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnal_range)) can be modest (≈ 10–12 °C) in built-up urban zones but may exceed 15–20 °C in suburbs or peripheral areas during clear, dry nights.[^cite_note-Tehran_Geography-82]

Urban expansion, the "[heat-island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island)" effect, and reduced vegetation have contributed to modest upward shifts in mean temperatures, particularly during summer nights. The dominance of dry summers means that water management and green-space planning carry particular significance for Tehran's long-term habitability.[^cite_note-Labzovskii_confident_spaceborne_monitoring-83]

Summers in Tehran are long and dry. Daytime highs often soar to over 35 °C, and during peak [heat waves](293270.md) they may approach or even exceed 40 °C. Nighttime minima during midsummer typically hover around 20–25 °C, though cooler nights are common in the northern districts. Winters are cool in central areas but are notably colder in the higher elevations north of the city: daytime highs in January average around 7–9 °C, while overnight lows in the city centre fall to near 0–2°C or slightly below and routinely dip below zero in the suburbs and mountain-adjacent zones.[^cite_note-Tehran_Geography-82]

Annual total rainfall for central Tehran averages roughly 230–300 mm. The wet season spans from late autumn through early spring (roughly November to May), while the summer months (June to September) are extremely dry and often see no measurable rainfall. Most precipitation falls from frontal systems moving in from the northwest and through the [Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz) range, with occasional convective storms. Snowfall occurs almost every winter, though accumulation in central Tehran is usually brief, while the northern districts and Alborz foothills receive heavier and longer-lasting snow.[^cite_note-84]

Air currents have a great effect on Tehran's weather. The prevailing wind blowing from the west causes the west of Tehran to always be exposed to fresh air. Although this wind brings smoke and pollution from the western industrial areas, its strong wind takes polluted air out of Tehran.[^cite_note-Tehran_Geography-82]

The main direction of the prevailing wind is northwest to southeast.[^cite_note-hamshahrionline-85] Other air currents that blow in the area are:

1. [Tochal breeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochal): With the rapid cooling of the Alborz mountain range at night, a local high-pressure center is formed on [Mount Tochal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochal). This cold current flows down the mountain because of high pressure. Thus, a gentle breeze blows into the city from the north at night.

   [^cite_note-Tehran_Geography-82]

2. Southern and southeastern regional winds: These winds blow from the desert plains in the hot months of the year.

   [^cite_note-Tehran_Geography-82]

3. Western winds: these winds are the Westerlies that affect Tehran throughout the year and can be called the prevailing wind.

   [^cite_note-Tehran_Geography-82]

#### Significant weather events

In February 2005, heavy snow covered all parts of Tehran. Snow depth was recorded as 15 cm (6 in) in the south and 100 cm (39 in) in the north. One newspaper reported that it had been the worst weather in 34 years. Ten thousand bulldozers and 13,000 municipal workers were deployed to keep the main roads open.[^cite_note-BBC-86][^cite_note-chn-87] On 5 and 6 January 2008, a wave of heavy snow and low temperatures covered Tehran in a thick layer of snow and ice, forcing the Council of Ministers to declare a [state of emergency](298608.md) and close down the capital from 6 to 7 January.[^cite_note-IRNA-88] On 3 February 2014, Tehran received heavy snowfall, specifically in the northern parts of the city, with a depth of 2 metres (6.6 ft). In one week of successive snowfalls, roads were made impassable in some areas, with the temperature ranging from −8 to −16 °C (18 to 3 °F).[^cite_note-BBC_News-89] On 3 June 2014, a severe thunderstorm with powerful [microbursts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbursts) created a [haboob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haboob), engulfing Tehran in sand and dust and causing five deaths, with more than 57 injured. This event knocked down numerous trees and power lines. It struck between 17\:00 and 18\:00, dropping temperatures from 33 to 19 °C (91 to 66 °F) within an hour. The dramatic temperature drop was accompanied by wind gusts reaching nearly 118 km/h (73 mph).[^cite_note-AccuWeather-90]

In July and August 2025, Tehran experienced extreme heat and severe drought conditions, marking one of the most acute environmental crises in the city's recent history. Temperatures exceeded 40 °C during a prolonged heatwave, coinciding with the fifth consecutive year of below-average rainfall. Reservoir levels in the surrounding region fell, with water storage at [Karaj Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaj_Dam) reduced by 58%, [Lar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lar_Dam) by 34%, Taleghan by 32%, and the combined [Latyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latyan_Dam) and [Mamloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamloo_Dam) reservoirs by 47% compared to normal capacity. Inflows to major dams were reported to be 43% lower than the previous year.[^cite_note-91] The water shortage prompted authorities to announce temporary public holidays and the closure of government offices and banks to conserve electricity and water.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

### Environmental issues

See also: [Environmental issues in Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_Tehran), [Environmental issues in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_Iran), and [List of earthquakes in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_Iran)



Heavy air pollution in Tehran

Tehran is located near two major [fault lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_lines). The city's wide elevation range—from approximately 1,100 m above sea level in the southern plains to over 1,800 m in the northern suburbs—yields pronounced topographic micro-climates[^cite_note-Tools-81]. Tehran suffers from severe [air pollution](10934212.md), 80% of it due to cars.[^cite_note-107] The remaining 20% is due to [industrial pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_pollution). Other estimates suggest that motorcycles account for 30% of air and 50% of [noise pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_pollution) in Tehran.[^cite_note-108] The city is considered one of the strongest sources of greenhouse gas emissions in the Middle East. Enhanced concentration of carbon dioxide over Tehran, that likely originate from the anthropogenic urban sources in the city, is easily detectable from satellite observations throughout the year.[^cite_note-Labzovskii_confident_spaceborne_monitoring-83] A plan to move the capital has been discussed many times in prior years, due mainly to the environmental issues of the region. In 2010, the government announced that "for security and administrative reasons, the plan to move the capital from Tehran has been finalized."[^cite_note-Relocation-109] There are plans to relocate 163 state firms and several universities from Tehran to avoid damages from a potential earthquake.[^cite_note-Relocation-109][^cite_note-110]

City officials are engaged in a battle to reduce air pollution. They have, for instance, encouraged taxis and buses to convert from petrol engines to [compressed natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_natural_gas). The government has set up a "Traffic Zone" covering the city centre during peak traffic hours. Entering and driving inside this zone is only allowed with a special permit. There are efforts to raise people's awareness of the hazards of pollution. Pollution Indicator Boards have been installed all around the city to monitor the level of [particulate matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_particulate_matter) (PM2.5/PM10), [nitrogen dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_dioxide) (NO<sub>2</sub>), ozone (O<sub>3</sub>), [sulfur dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide) (SO<sub>2</sub>), and [carbon monoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_monoxide) (CO).

## Economy

See also: [Economy of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Tehran), [Industry of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_of_Iran), and [Communications in Iran](64706.md)



[Tehran Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Stock_Exchange)

Tehran is the economic centre of Iran.[^cite_note-111] About 30% of Iran's public-sector workforce and 45% of its large industrial firms are located in the city, and almost half of these workers are employed by the government.[^cite_note-112] Most of the remainder of workers are factory workers, shopkeepers, laborers, and transport workers.

Few foreign companies operate in Tehran, because of the government's complex international relations. But before the 1979 Revolution, many foreign companies were active in Iran.[^cite_note-113] Tehran's present-day modern industries include the manufacturing of automobiles, electronics and electrical equipment, weaponry, textiles, sugar, cement, and chemical products. It is also a leading centre for the sale of carpets and furniture. The oil refining companies of [Pars Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pars_Oil), [Speedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepahan_Oil_Company), and [Behran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behran_Oil_Company) are based in Tehran.

Tehran relies heavily on private cars, buses, motorcycles, and taxis, and is one of the most car-dependent cities in the world. The [Tehran Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Stock_Exchange), is a full member of the [World Federation of Exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Federation_of_Exchanges) and a founding member of the [Federation of Euro-Asian Stock Exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Euro-Asian_Stock_Exchanges).[^cite_note-114]

### Fashion industry

Design, manufacturing, distribution, marketing, retail, advertising, and other sectors of the [fashion industry in Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_in_Iran) have been able to grow significantly according to the needs of the country. In particular, a large number of male and female models are working in Tehran's fashion advertising and promotion sections. Despite the lack of adequate laws to support models, payments to female models have been considered high. Also, modeling of children is usually prohibited in Tehran. Clothing manufacturers are closely related to other fashion sectors in Tehran. ## Infrastructure

### Highways and streets

See also: [List of Expressways in Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Expressways_in_Tehran) and [Automotive industry in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_Iran)



Fajr Bridge, [Hemmat Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemmat_Expressway)

Following the Islamic Revolution in 1979, the political system changed from [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy) to [Islamic republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_republic). Then the construction of political power in the country needed to change so that new spectrums of political power and decision-making centers emerged in Iran. Motives, desires, and actions of these new political power decision-making centers in Iran made them rename streets and public places throughout the country, especially Tehran. For example Shahyad square changed to [Azadi square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadi_Square) and Pahlavi street changed to [Valiasr street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valiasr_Street).[^cite_note-121] The metropolis of Tehran is equipped with a large network of highways and interchanges.

According to the head of Tehran Municipality's Environment and Sustainable Development Office, Tehran was designed to have a capacity of about 300,000 cars, but more than five million cars are on the roads.[^cite_note-122] The automotive industry has recently developed, but international sanctions influence the production processes periodically.[^cite_note-123] According to local media, Tehran has more than 200,000 taxis plying the roads daily,[^cite_note-124] with several types of taxi available in the city. Airport taxis have a higher cost per kilometer as opposed to regular green and yellow taxis in the city.

### Buses

See also: [Trolleybuses in Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Tehran) and [Tehran Bus Rapid Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Bus_Rapid_Transit)



[Tehran's bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Bus_Rapid_Transit)

Buses have served the city since the 1920s. Tehran's transport system includes conventional buses, [trolleybuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus), and [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) (BRT). The city's four major bus stations include the South Terminal, the East Terminal, the West Terminal, and the northcentral Beyhaghi Terminal.

The trolleybus system was opened in 1992, using a fleet of 65 [articulated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articulated_bus) trolleybuses built by [Czech Republic](5321.md)'s [Škoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0koda_Works).[^cite_note-Murray-125] This was the first trolleybus system in Iran.[^cite_note-Murray-125] In 2005, trolleybuses were operating on five routes, all starting at [Imam Hossein Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Hossein_Square).[^cite_note-tm265-126] Two routes running northeastwards operated almost entirely in a segregated [busway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_Rapid_Transit) located in the middle of the wide [carriageway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carriageway) along [Damavand Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damavand_Street), stopping only at purpose-built stops located about every 500 metres along the routes, effectively making these routes trolleybus-BRT (but they were not called such). The other three trolleybus routes ran south and operated in mixed traffic. Both route sections were served by [limited-stop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited-stop) services and local (making all stops) services.[^cite_note-tm265-126] A 3.2-kilometer extension from Shoosh Square to Rah Ahan Square was opened in March 2010.[^cite_note-tm298-127] Visitors in 2014 found that the trolleybus system had closed, apparently sometime in 2013.[^cite_note-haseldine-128] However, it reopened in March 2016, operating on a single 1.8-km route between Meydan-e-Khorasan (Khorasan Square) and Bozorgrah-e-Be'sat.[^cite_note-tm328-129][^cite_note-trolleymotion-2016july-130] Around 30 vehicles had been refurbished and returned to service.[^cite_note-tm328-129][^cite_note-trolleymotion-2016july-130] Extensions were planned.[^cite_note-trolleymotion-2016july-130]

[Tehran's bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Bus_Rapid_Transit) (BRT) was officially inaugurated in 2008. It has 10 lines with some 215 stations in different areas of the city. As of 2011, the BRT system had a network of 100 km (62 mi), transporting 1.8 million passengers on a daily basis.

### Parks and green spaces

See also: [List of Tehran metropolis parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Tehran_metropolis_parks)

There are over 2,100 parks within the metropolis of Tehran,[^cite_note-irnamokh-142] with one of the oldest being [Jamshidie Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamshidieh_Park), which was first established as a private garden for Qajar prince Jamshid Davallu, and was then dedicated to the last empress of Iran, [Farah Pahlavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farah_Pahlavi). The total green space within Tehran stretches over 12,600 hectares, covering over 20 percent of the city's area. Tehran's Birds Garden is the largest bird park in Iran. Its main features are an open water fountain area for cooling in the hot climate, fire towers, and an [amphitheatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphitheatre).[^cite_note-145]

### Energy

See also: [Energy in Iran](10580575.md) and [Water supply and sanitation in Iran](18304723.md)

Fresh water resources of Tehran province in 2017

1. [Groundwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater) (37.0%)

2. [Sewage treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_treatment) (63.0%)

Greater Tehran is supplied by surface water from the [Lar dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lar_Dam) on the [Lar River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lar_River) in the northeast of the city, the [Latyan dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latyan_Dam) on the [Jajrood River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jajrood_River) in the north, the [Karaj River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaj_River) in the northwest, as well as by groundwater in the vicinity of the city. According to the national energy roadmap, the government plans to promote green technology to increase the nominal capacity of power plants from 74 gigawatts to over 120 gigawatts by the end of 2025.[^cite_note-147] Solar panels have been installed in [Pardisan Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardisan_Park) for green electricity production, said [Masoumeh Ebtekar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masoumeh_Ebtekar), head of the Department of Environment.

### Architecture

See also: [Architecture of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Tehran)

The oldest surviving architectural monuments are from the [Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) and [Pahlavi](338883.md) eras. In Greater Tehran, monuments dating back to the [Seljuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire) era remain as well; notably the [Toqrol Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tughrul_Tower) in Ray. [Rashkan Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashkan_Castle), dating back to the ancient [Parthian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire), of which some artifacts are housed at the [National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Iran);[^cite_note-149] and the [Bahram fire temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahram_fire_temple), which remains since the [Sassanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire). Tehran only had a small population until the late 18th century but it began to take a more considerable role in Iranian society after it was chosen as the capital city. Despite the regular occurrence of earthquakes during the Qajar period and after, some historic buildings remain from that era.[^cite_note-150] Tehran is Iran's [primate city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate_city), and is considered to have the most modernized infrastructure in the country. However, the [gentrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentrification) of old neighbourhoods and the demolition of buildings of cultural significance have caused concerns.[^cite_note-151]

### Theater

(Vahdat)

The [Roodaki Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roodaki_Hall), Tehran

Under the reign of the [Qajars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty), Tehran was home to the royal theatre of [Tekye Dowlat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tekyeh_Dowlat), located to the southeast of the [Golestan Palace](313051.md), in which traditional and religious performances were observed. It was eventually demolished and replaced with a bank building in 1947, following the reforms during the reign of [Reza Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Shah).

Before the 1979 Revolution, the Iranian national stage had become the most famous performing scene for known international artists and troupes in the Middle East,[^cite_note-157] with the Vahdat Hall, formerly but very much still known as Roodaki Hall (spelled in multiple ways in English), constructed to function as the national stage for opera and ballet. The hall was inaugurated in October 1967 and named after prominent Persian poet [Rudaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudaki). It is home to the [Tehran Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Symphony_Orchestra), the Tehran Opera Orchestra, and the [Iranian National Ballet Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_National_Ballet_Company).

The [City Theater of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Theater_of_Tehran), one of Iran's biggest theatre complexes, which contains several performance halls, was opened in 1972. It was built at the initiative and presidency of Empress Farah Pahlavi, and was designed by architect Ali Sardar Afkhami, constructed within five years.

One of the gathering centers of [cabarets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabaret) in old Tehran was [Laleh-Zar Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laleh-Zar_Street). Famous Persian cabarets were active in the city until 1979. They also introduced many domestic artists. In common language, cabaret was sometimes called "home of dance" or "dancing place".[^cite_note-158]

The annual events of [Fajr Theater Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fajr_International_Theater_Festival) and [Tehran Puppet Theater Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_International_Puppet_Theatre_Festival) take place in Tehran.

### Cinema

The first movie theater in Tehran was established by [Mirza Ebrahim Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirza_Ebrahim_Khan_Sahhafbashi) in 1904.[^cite_note-159] Until the early 1930s, there were 15 theaters in Tehran province and 11 in other provinces.[^cite_note-160] Most of the movie theatres are located downtown. The complexes of [Kourosh Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kourosh_Complex?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Mellat Gallery and Cineplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellat_Park), [Azadi Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadi_Cinema_Complex), and [Cinema Farhang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_Farhang) are among the most popular cinema complexes in Tehran. Several film festivals are held in Tehran, including [Fajr Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fajr_International_Film_Festival), [Children and Youth Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Film_Festival_for_Children_and_Youth), House of Cinema Festival, Mobile Film and Photo Festival, Nahal Festival, [Roshd Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roshd_International_Film_Festival), Tehran Animation Festival, Tehran Short Film Festival, and Urban Film Festival.

### Music and dance

There are a variety of concert halls in Tehran. An organization like the Roudaki Culture and Art Foundation has five different venues where more than 500 concerts take place this year. [Vahdat Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vahdat_Hall), [Roudaki Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roudaki_Hall), Ferdowsi Hall, Hafez Hall and Azadi Theater are the top five venues in Tehran, where classical, pop, traditional, rock or solo concerts take place.[^cite_note-161]

[Erotic dancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erotic_dance) were active and trained in Tehran until the 1979 revolution. But after this date, because of the policies of the new government, these activities were completely banned.[^cite_note-Tehran_Culture-148]

### Sports

See also: [Sport in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Iran)

Football and volleyball are the city's most popular sports, while wrestling, basketball, and futsal are also major parts of the city's sporting culture. Ice hockey and rugby are also popular. [12 ski resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ski_areas_and_resorts_in_Iran) operate in Iran, the most famous being [Tochal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochal_Complex), [Dizin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizin), and [Shemshak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shemshak_\(ski_resort\)), all within one to three hours from the city of Tehran.

Tochal's resort is the world's fifth-highest ski resort at over 3,730 m (12,240 ft) above sea level at its highest point. It is also the world's nearest ski resort to a capital city. The resort was opened in 1976, shortly before the 1979 Revolution. It is equipped with an 8 km (5 mi) long gondola lift that covers a huge vertical distance.[^cite_note-162] There are two parallel chair ski lifts in Tochal that reach 3,900 m (12,800 ft) high near Tochal's peak, rising higher than the gondola's seventh station, which is higher than any of the European ski resorts. From the Tochal peak, there are views of the Alborz range, including the 5,610-metre-high (18,406 ft) [Mount Damavand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Damavand), a dormant volcano.

&#x20;Tehran is the site of the [national stadium of Azadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadi_Stadium), the [biggest stadium by capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stadiums_by_capacity) in West Asia, where many of the top matches of Iran's Premier League are held. The stadium is a part of the [Azadi Sport Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadi_Sport_Complex), which was originally built to host the [7th Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Asian_Games) in September 1974. This was the first time the Asian Games were hosted in West Asia. Tehran played host to 3,010 athletes from 25 countries/NOCs, which was at the time the highest number of participants since the inception of the Games.[^cite_note-163] That followed hosting the [6th AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_AFC_Asian_Cup) in June 1976, and then the first [West Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asian_Games) in November 1997. The success of the games led to the creation of the West Asian Games Federation (WAGF), and the intention of hosting the games every two years.[^cite_note-164] The city had also hosted the final of the [1968 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_AFC_Asian_Cup). Several [FIVB Volleyball World League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIVB_Volleyball_World_League) courses have also been hosted in Tehran. In 2014, the city hosted the FILA Wrestling World Cup event at the Azadi Indoor Stadium.[^cite_note-165]

### Food

See also: [Iranian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_cuisine)

There are many restaurants and cafes in Tehran, both modern and classic, serving both Iranian and cosmopolitan cuisine. Pizzerias, [sandwich bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandwich_bar), and [kebab shops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kebab_shop) make up the majority of food shops in Tehran.[^cite_note-166]

### Graffiti

Main article: [Graffiti in Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graffiti_in_Tehran)

Many styles of graffiti are seen in Tehran. Some are political and revolutionary slogans painted by governmental organizations,[^cite_note-graff-167] and some are works of art by ordinary citizens, representing their views on both social and political issues. However, unsanctioned street art is forbidden in Iran,[^cite_note-graff-167] and such works are usually short-lived. During the [2009 Iranian presidential election protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Iranian_presidential_election_protests), many graffiti works were created by people supporting the [Green Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Green_Movement). They were removed from the walls by the paramilitary [Basij](2892082.md) forces.[^cite_note-168] In recent years, the Tehran Municipality has been using graffiti in order to beautify the city. Several graffiti festivals have also taken place in Tehran, including the one organized by the [Tehran University of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_University_of_Art) in October 2014.[^cite_note-169]
