The **FIBA Women's Basketball World Cup**, also known as the **Basketball World Cup for Women** or simply the **FIBA Women's World Cup**, is an international [basketball](667977.md) tournament for women's national teams held quadrennially. It was created by the [International Basketball Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA) (FIBA). Its inaugural game was in [1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) in [Chile](5489.md), three years after the first men's World Championship. For most of its early history, it was not held in the same year as the men's championship, and was not granted a consistent quadrennial cycle until [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women). After the [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) event, FIBA changed the scheduling so that the women's tournament would be held in even-numbered non-[Olympic](27566.md) years, a change that had come to the men's tournament in [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIBA_World_Championship).

Formerly known as the **FIBA World Championship for Women**, the name changed shortly after its [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) edition.[^cite_note-1] From 1986 through 2014, the tournament was held in the same year as the men's [FIBA Basketball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Basketball_World_Cup), though in different countries. After the 2014 editions of both championships, the men's event was rescheduled on a new four-year cycle (the latest in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIBA_Basketball_World_Cup)) to avoid conflict with the men's [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), but the Women's World Cup remains on the same four-year cycle, with editions held in the same years as the men's FIFA World Cup and the finals tournament played a few months after it.[^cite_note-New_calendar-2] The [2022 FIBA Women's Basketball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIBA_Women's_Basketball_World_Cup) was held in [Sydney](27862.md), Australia. The [2026 FIBA Women's Basketball World Cup](73471455.md) was held in [Berlin](3354.md), Germany.

## History

Only four nations have won titles in the history of the Women's World Cup. The first tournament was held in [1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) in [Chile](5489.md), with the participation of ten national teams. Despite losing to [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_women's_national_basketball_team) in the final round, the [United States](7129278.md) won the inaugural title. The next tournament which was held in [1957](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1957_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) in [Brazil](3383.md), marked the beginning of the long-time rivalry between the US team and [Soviet team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_women's_national_basketball_team), who debuted at the World Championship. The first official match between these teams was played on the last day of the tournament, it was also the decisive match of the championship. Soviet players led after the first half (27–24), but in the second half the Americans managed to achieve victory (51–48) and won their second World title.

The third World Championship was held in [1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) in [Moscow](19004.md), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). Due to international political tensions, caused by the [Cold War](325329.md), the [United States](3434750.md) and its allies didn't participate at this tournament with the exception of [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_women's_national_basketball_team). Thus, seven of the eight countries that played in the tournament were [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) countries. In the absence of the [US team](7129278.md), the [Soviet team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_women's_national_basketball_team) won their maiden World title while [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_women's_national_basketball_team) took silver medals and [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_women's_national_basketball_team) finished third. But at the next World Championship, which was held in [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) in [Peru](170691.md), all these [Eastern European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) teams also occupied whole podium – the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_women's_national_basketball_team) won their second title in a row, [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_women's_national_basketball_team) won silver medals, [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_women's_national_basketball_team) took bronze while [United States](7129278.md) finished only at fourth place. This tournament marked the beginning of the era of dominance of the Soviet Union in world women's basketball, which lasted for next two decades. Under leadership of head coach [Lidiya Alekseyeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidiya_Alekseyeva), Soviet team won three next World Championships held in [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) ([Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia)), [1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) ([Brazil](3383.md)) and [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) ([Colombia](5222.md)). The vast majority of medals at these tournaments were won by [Eastern European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) and [East Asian](19605700.md) teams while [United States](7129278.md) even failed to qualify into final round. In general, from 1959 to 1975 Soviet team won record five titles in a row by winning all their 40 matches with margin of 10 points and more. Therefore, it was not surprising when the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_women's_national_basketball_team) won the inaugural women's Olympic tournament which was held in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_at_the_1976_Summer_Olympics) in [Montreal](7954681.md), [Canada](5042916.md) (by winning all five matches). Despite losing to [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_women's_national_basketball_team) and the Soviet Union, the United States managed to finish second and capture silver medals while Bulgaria took Olympic bronze.

Unfortunately, the next world women's basketball major tournaments were clouded by a series of political boycotts caused by the [Cold War](325329.md). Firstly, the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and four other [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) countries withdrew from next World Championships which was held in [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) in [South Korea](27019.md). In spite of losing to the [hosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_women's_national_basketball_team) (82–94), [United States](7129278.md) won all other matches and captured a gold medal thanks to a better head-to-head point difference among the Top 3 teams. It was the third World title for the US team and the first since 1957. The next year the [United States](3434750.md) boycotted [1980 Olympic tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_at_the_1980_Summer_Olympics) which was held in [Moscow](19004.md), [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). In their absence, Soviet team captured their second Olympic title by winning all 6 matches while the [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_women's_national_basketball_team) and the [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_women's_national_basketball_team) took silver and bronze medals respectively. Therefore, the next World Championship which was held in [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) in [Brazil](3383.md) became the first international major competition for seven years with participation of all the world's top teams. The [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_women's_national_basketball_team) captured their sixth World title by winning all 10 of their matches, but this victory was the most difficult one. In the final group round, the Soviet team won the match against United States with a margin of only one point (85–84) after losing 40–49 at the end of first half. Later in the final match between these two teams the Soviets lost the first half with a score of 37–40, but also managed to achieve victory with a margin of only two points (84–82). Both of those matches had provided other teams with the knowledge that the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_women's_national_basketball_team) wasn't as overwhelming a force as it was years prior. The [China](16676096.md) won their first World Championship medal after their victory over [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_women's_national_basketball_team) in a bronze medal match (71–63). Next year the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and [Hungary](13275.md) boycotted [1984 Olympic tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_at_the_1984_Summer_Olympics) which was held in [Los Angeles](18110.md), United States thus allowing the hosts to win their maiden Olympic title. [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_women's_national_basketball_team) and [China](16676096.md) took silver and bronze Olympic medals respectively.

Fortunately, all the world's top teams were able to partake in the next World Championship, which was held in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). One month before start of tournament, [United States](7129278.md) won the basketball tournament at the [1986 Goodwill Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Goodwill_Games) in [Moscow](19004.md) by beating the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_women's_national_basketball_team) with great margin of 23 points (83–60). Both reigning Olympic Champions (United States) and World Champions (Soviet Union) won all their group and semifinal matches, and then met in the final match in [Moscow](19004.md). In this final, US team once again proved their superiority by winning with a margin of 20 points (108–88) thus ending the remarkable winning streak of the Soviet team at the World Championships which lasted 56 consecutive matches. This victory became the turning point which marked beginning of the era of dominance of the [United States](7129278.md) in world women's basketball. At the [1988 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_at_the_1988_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament) in [Seoul](19159283.md), [South Korea](27019.md), the US team defended their Olympic title by beating the [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_women's_national_basketball_team) in the final match (77–70) while the Soviet team failed to reach the final and unexpectedly finished only in third place for the first time in their history. At the next World Championship which was held in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) in [Malaysia](3607937.md), the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_women's_national_basketball_team) lost in the second group round to [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_women's_national_basketball_team) (79–82) and [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_women's_national_basketball_team) (63–64) and finished only at fifth place. It was the first and only time the Soviet women's team had failed to reach the podium at the international level competition. Like two years prior, the United States and Yugoslavia once more met in the final match, and US team achieved victory again (88–78) by winning their sixth World title. Two years later, the United States went to [1992 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_at_the_1992_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament) in [Barcelona](4443.md), [Spain](26667.md) in the status of favorites to win - especially after the Yugoslavian team was banned from competition due to [United Nations](31769.md) [sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_757). However, the former Soviet players who competed for the [Unified Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Team_at_the_1992_Summer_Olympics), as a result of Soviet Union's dissolution in December 1991, managed to achieve surprising success after a shocking victory over [United States](7129278.md) in the semifinals (79–73) and managed a victory over the [China](16676096.md) in the final match (76–66). The US team finished in third place. This was the last victory of Soviet-born players at the World level competitions, and the Unified Team ceased to exist after that.

The 12th World Championship was held in [Australia](4689264.md) in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women), after the dissolution of [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), [SFR Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) and [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia). [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_women's_national_basketball_team) became only newly formed national team who managed to qualify to this World Championship. In such situation, [United States](7129278.md) aimed to return at first position. However, they surprisingly lost semifinal match to [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_women's_national_basketball_team) (107–110) and once again finished only at the third place. In the final match, Brazil beat [China](16676096.md) (96–87) thus breaking US-Soviet duopoly on World Championship which lasted for 41 years. Two years later [United States](7129278.md) took revenge at the home [1996 Olympic tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball_at_the_1996_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament) at [Atlanta](3138.md) by beating Brazil in the final match with margin of 24 points (111–87). This victory marked beginning of US dominance at the Olympic Games – from 1996 to 2021, they won seven Olympic titles in a row with remarkable winning streak of 55 consecutive matches at Olympic tournaments.

The 13th World Championship took place in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) in [Germany](11867.md). [Russian team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_women's_national_basketball_team) participated at this tournament for first time after dissolution of [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). In the second group round, they lost to [United States](7129278.md) with great margin of 36 points (60–96) but then managed to reach final where met with US team again. In the final match, United States faced with much stronger resistance – Russia led with margin of 9 points after first half (28–37), but in the second half US team managed to reach advantage and achieved final victory (71–65). [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_women's_national_basketball_team) won World Championship bronze medals for first time in their history. The similar story took place at the next World Championship which took place in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) in [China](5405.md) – [United States](7129278.md) defeated [Russian team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_women's_national_basketball_team) at the preliminary round with margin of 34 points (89–55) but later they faced with stronger resistance from Russian side in the final. Despite this, US team won final match with margin of 5 points (79–74) and captured seventh World title while [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_women's_national_basketball_team) took bronze medals for second time in a row. At the next [2006 World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) in [Brazil](3383.md), [Russian team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_women's_national_basketball_team) lost to [United States](7129278.md) at the preliminary round (80–90) and then lost two more matches at the group stage. But after group stage, Russia achieved remarkable victory by beating US team in semifinals (75–68). It was only first defeat of [United States](7129278.md) for 12 years at the either Olympic Games or World Championships (and the last till nowadays). They finished only at third place after beating hosts in a bronze medal match (99–59). After this success, Russia was unable to show the same resistance to [Australian team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_women's_national_basketball_team) who won final match with margin of 17 points (91–74). Therefore, Australia became only fourth national team to win either Olympic or World title in the history of women's basketball.

The next World Championship took place in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) in the [Czech Republic](5321.md). Many people waited for another match between [United States](7129278.md) and [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_women's_national_basketball_team) in playoffs. Both teams won all their group matches but then Russia suffered shocking defeat to [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus_women's_national_basketball_team) in the quarterfinals (53–70). The defending champions, [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_women's_national_basketball_team) also surprisingly lost quarterfinal match to hosts - [Czech team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic_women's_national_basketball_team) (68–79). In the semifinals, Czech Republic beat Belarus in overtime (81–77) and reach final match for the first time after dissolution of Czechoslovakia. However, in the final match [United States](7129278.md) looked stronger and managed to return World title after victory with margin of 20 points (89–69). [Spain](13486344.md) won World Championship medals for first time in their history after beating Belarus in a bronze medal match (77–68). The next three edition were held without participation of Russian team who failed to qualify to [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) and [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIBA_Women's_Basketball_World_Cup) tournaments and was banned from participation in the [2022 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIBA_Women's_Basketball_World_Cup) due to the [invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md). In [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIBA_World_Championship_for_Women) US team repeated their success by beating [Spain](13486344.md) in the final match (77–64). The home team, [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_women's_national_basketball_team) managed to reach semifinals for first time in history but failed to win medals after losing to [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_women's_national_basketball_team) in a bronze medal match (44–74). At the next [2018 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIBA_Women's_Basketball_World_Cup) in [Spain](26667.md), United States captured gold again after victory over Australia in a final match (73–56). The home team, [Spain](13486344.md) reached podium for third time in a row after victory over [Belgium](40566612.md) in a bronze medal match (67–60). The number of participants of the [2022 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIBA_Women's_Basketball_World_Cup) was reduced from 16 to 12 teams. United States won gold medals for fourth time in a row by beating [China](16676096.md) in a final match (83–61). Therefore, US team won eight of last ten World tournaments, captured their 11th World title and extended own winning streak at these competitions to 30 consecutive matches. The home team, [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_women's_national_basketball_team) defeated [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_women's_national_basketball_team) in a bronze medal match (95–65) and thus reached podium for third time in a row.

For the 2026 edition of the tournament in Germany, 16 teams participated.[^cite_note-3]
