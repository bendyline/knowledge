**Boyacá** (Spanish pronunciation: [\[boʝaˈka\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)) is one of the thirty-two [departments of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Colombia), and the remnant of [Boyacá State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyac%C3%A1_State), one of the original nine states of the "[United States of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_Colombia)".

Boyacá is centrally located within Colombia, almost entirely within the [mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain) of the [Eastern Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Oriental,_Colombia) to the border with [Venezuela](32374.md), although the western end of the department extends to the [Magdalena River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_River) at the town of [Puerto Boyacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Boyac%C3%A1,_Boyac%C3%A1). Boyacá borders to the north with the Department of [Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander_Department), to the northeast with the [Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivarian_Republic_of_Venezuela) and [Norte de Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte_de_Santander), to the east with the departments of [Arauca](549644.md) and [Casanare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casanare_Department). To the south, Boyacá borders the department of [Cundinamarca](189405.md) and to the west with the Department of [Antioquia](185251.md) covering a total area of 23,189 square kilometres (8,953 sq mi).[^cite_note-Maher-Statistics-2] The capital of Boyacá is the city of [Tunja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunja).

Boyacá is known as "The Land of Freedom" because this region was the scene of a series of battles which led to Colombia's independence from [Spain](26667.md). The first one took place on 25 July 1819 in the Pantano de Vargas and the final and decisive battle known as the [Battle of Boyacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Boyac%C3%A1) was fought on 7 August 1819 at Puente de Boyacá.

Boyacá is home to three [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University): the [Universidad Pedagógica y Tecnológica de Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Pedag%C3%B3gica_y_Tecnol%C3%B3gica_de_Colombia) (UPTC), the [Universidad de Boyacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_de_Boyac%C3%A1) (UNIBOYACA), and the [Saint Thomas Aquinas University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Thomas_Aquinas_University).

## Etymology

The word *Boyacá* derived from the [Chibcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibcha_language) word "*Bojacá*" which means "Near the *cacique*", or "Region of the royal mantle".

## History



*El Libertador* [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar) crossing the *[Páramo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1ramo) de [Pisba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisba)*.

The territory of present-day Boyaca was during the [Pre-Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian) time the domain of the [Muisca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muisca_people) [indigenous peoples](4053513.md). The Muisca under the chiefdom of the *[zaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaque)* of [Hunza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunja) lived mainly by agriculture and mining [gold](12240.md) and [emeralds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald).

The first [European](9239.md) to discover the area was the Spaniard [Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzalo_Jim%C3%A9nez_de_Quesada) who conquered the northern Muisca living in the area led by last *zaque* [Aquiminzaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquiminzaque) and distributed the land in [encomiendas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encomienda) and forced the indigenous people to work for him.

In 1539, [Gonzalo Suárez Rendón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzalo_Su%C3%A1rez_Rend%C3%B3n), a Spanish [conquistador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_conquistadors_in_Colombia), founded the city of [Tunja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunja) and other sites where the indigenous people previously had their villages. Tunja became one of the main political and economic centers for the Spanish during the [Viceroyalty of New Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Granada).

During the 19th century, Boyacá was battleground for numerous confrontations between the [royalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royalist_\(Spanish_American_Revolution\)) and patriot armies led by [Simón Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar) during the [Spanish colonies' war of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American_wars_of_independence) from Spain. Two of the most decisive battles were the [Battle of Boyacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Boyac%C3%A1) and the [Battle of Vargas Swamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vargas_Swamp) (1819) won by the patriot forces against the royalists. In 1824 [Gran Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Colombia) created the [Boyacá Department (Gran Colombia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyac%C3%A1_Department_\(Gran_Colombia\)).

After the creation of the [Granadine Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granadine_Confederation) by 1858 the territory of now Boyaca became the [Sovereign State of Boyacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyac%C3%A1_State). It was later rearranged in territory and administration and renamed as "Department of Boyaca" after a series of [civil wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_wars) like the [Colombian Civil War (1860–1862)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Civil_War_\(1860%E2%80%931862\)) and the [Thousand Days War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_Days_War) that struggled over a centralist or federalist system and political instability that changed to many constitutions (such as the Constitution of 1886), Boyaca finally acquired its current definition as territory.

## Geography

Boyacá is located in the [Andean Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_Region_\(Colombia\)) in central Colombia, over the [Cordillera Oriental mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Oriental_\(Colombia\))[^cite_note-Aspectos-7] and covers a total area of 23,189 km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-Maher-Statistics-2] It borders other [Colombian departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Colombia) as follows: to the north [Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander_Department) and [Norte de Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte_de_Santander), to the east [Arauca](549644.md) and [Casanare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casanare_Department), to the south [Cundinamarca](189405.md) and a small part of [Meta](549697.md), and to the west [Antioquia](185251.md) and [Caldas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldas_Department).[^cite_note-Localizacion-1] It has a territorial dispute with Norte de Santander and Cundinamarca.

The department of Boyacá covers a small portion of the [Middle Magdalena valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Magdalena_valley?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [Magdalena River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_River) to the west, the Cordillera Oriental mountain range with altitudes of 5,380 m above sea level ([Sierra Nevada del Cocuy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada_del_Cocuy) with 25 snow peaks), flat highland plateaux, and another small portion of territory by the eastern [Llanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanos) plains. Among its most prominent geographical features are the [Range of the Zorro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_of_the_Zorro?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Serrania de las Quinchas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serrania_de_las_Quinchas) and the Andean plateaus of [Rusia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusia_Plateau?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Guantivá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantiv%C3%A1_Plateau?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Pisba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisba_National_Natural_Park), [Chontales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chontales_Plateau?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Rechiniga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rech%C3%ADniga_Plateau?action=edit\&redlink=1).

The [Altiplano Cundiboyacense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altiplano_Cundiboyacense), shared with the department of [Cundinamarca](189405.md), is densely populated with numerous valleys. The southern part is the [Bogotá savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogot%C3%A1_savanna). Boyacá is subdivided into 123 municipalities.

Many rivers originate in Boyacá, the most important are the [Chicamocha River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicamocha_River) and [Arauca River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arauca_River) and tributaries to other important rivers such as the [Magdalena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_River) and [Meta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_River).

Boyacá also has numerous lakes which include [Lake Tota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tota), [Lake Sochagota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Sochagota?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Lake Fúquene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_F%C3%BAquene), shared with the department of Cundinamarca, the artificial [Chivor Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chivor_Reservoir?action=edit\&redlink=1) and others.

## National parks

Main article: [List of national parks of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Colombia)

[El Cocuy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cocuy_National_Park) and [Pisba National Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisba_National_Park) are located in the northeast of Boyacá. Pisba National Park is shared with the department of Arauca. The flora and fauna sanctuary of [Lake Iguaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Iguaque) is situated in the centre of the department. The most beautiful [páramo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1ramo) in the world, [Ocetá Páramo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocet%C3%A1_P%C3%A1ramo), is in northeast Boyacá.

### Climate



[Guateque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guateque), in [southeastern Boyacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenza_Valley) during a rainy season

The central area of the highlands has two rainy seasons; the first between April and June, and a second between October and November with an average of 1,000 millimetres (39 in) of rainfall per year. The rest of the year is considered to be the dry seasons with intermittent rainfall.
