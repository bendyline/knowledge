**Zaporizhzhia**,[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] formerly known as **Aleksandrovsk** or **Oleksandrivsk** until 1921,[^cite_note-3] is a city in southeast [Ukraine](31750.md), situated on the banks of the [Dnieper River](380138.md). It is the [administrative centre](181337.md) of [Zaporizhzhia Oblast](494936.md).[^cite_note-4] Zaporizhzhia has a population of 710,052 (2022 estimate).[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-5]

Zaporizhzhia is known for the historic island of [Khortytsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khortytsia), multiple [power stations](212141.md) and for being an important industrial centre. Steel, aluminium, aircraft engines, automobiles, transformers for substations, and other heavy industrial goods are produced in the region.

## Names and etymology

The name *Zaporizhzhia* refers to the position of the city: "beyond the rapids"—downstream or south of the [Dnieper Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Rapids). These were previously an impediment to navigation and the site of important [portages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portage). In 1932, the rapids were flooded to become part of the reservoir of the [Dnieper Hydroelectric Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Hydroelectric_Station).[^cite_note-Pospelov-6]

Before 1921, the city was called Aleksandrovsk (or Oleksandrivsk), named after the original fortress that formed a part of the [Dnieper Defence Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro_Line) of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire).

## History

Main article: [History of Zaporizhzhia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Zaporizhzhia)

Zaporizhzhia was founded in 1770, when the Aleksandrovskaya (Александровская) Fortress was built as a part of the Dnieper Defence Line, to protect the southern territories of the Russian Empire from [Crimean Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate) invasions.[^cite_note-Novistki-191-7] Following the [Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_K%C3%BC%C3%A7%C3%BCk_Kaynarca) in 1775, the southern lands of the [East European Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_European_Plain) and the Crimean peninsula were absorbed into the Russian Empire. The Aleksandrovskaya Fortress then lost its military significance, and became a small rural town, which from 1806 to around 1930 was called Alexandrovsk.[^cite_note-Pospelov-6]



Trade school in 1911

The opening of the Kichkas Bridge at the start of the 20th century, the first rail crossing of the [Dnieper](380138.md), was followed by the industrial growth of Zaporizhzhia.[^cite_note-Bridges-8] In 1916, during World War I, the [DEKA Stock Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_Sich) transferred its aircraft engine manufacturing plant from [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md) to Zaporizhzhia.[^cite_note-meria.zp-9]

During the [Ukrainian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence) and [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War) (1917–1921), Zaporizhzhia was the scene of fierce fighting between the Red Army and the White armies of [Denikin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Denikin) and [Wrangel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Wrangel), [Petliura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symon_Petliura)'s [Ukrainian People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Army) of the [Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Republic), and German-Austrian troops. The opposing armies used the strategically important Kichkas Bridge to transfer troops, ammunition, and medical supplies. Afterwards, it became the capital of the newly formed [Zaporizhzhia Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_Governorate) of Ukraine.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

The Soviet government industrialized Zaporizhzhia still further during the 1920s and 1930s, when the Dnieper Hydroelectric Station, and the [Zaporizhzhia Steel Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhstal), and the Dnieper Aluminium Plant were built.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] In the 1930s, the American United Engineering and Foundry Company built a [strip mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strip_mill) similar to the [Ford River Rouge steel mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_River_Rouge_Complex) to produce rolling steel strip. The annual capacity of the mill reached 540,000 tonnes (600,000 short tons) of 170 cm (66 inches) wide steel.[^cite_note-Dunn-13-15]

### World War II (1941–1945)



A Red Army soldier near the [Dnieper hydro-electric dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Hydroelectric_Station)

After the outbreak of the [War between the USSR and Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_\(World_War_II\)) in June 1941, the [Soviet government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_government) began evacuating Zaporizhzhia's industries to [Siberia](29639.md).[^cite_note-zabor.zp.GPW-16] and the Soviet security forces began shooting political prisoners in the city.[^cite_note-17] On 18 August 1941, elements of the [German 1st Panzergruppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Panzer_Group) reached the outskirts of Zaporizhzhia on the right bank and seized the island of Khortytsia.[^cite_note-18]

The Red Army blew a 120-by-10-metre (394 ft × 33 ft) hole in the Dnieper hydroelectric dam on 18 August 1941, producing a flood wave that swept from Zaporizhzhia to [Nikopol](1958336.md).[^cite_note-zabor.zp.GPW-16] The flood killed local residents as well as soldiers from both armies, with historians estimating a death toll between 20,000 and 100,000.[^cite_note-19] Despite reinforcements, Zaporizhzhia was taken on 3 October 1941.[^cite_note-20] The German occupation lasted two years; during which the Germans shot over 35,000 people, and sent 58,000 people to Germany as [forced labourers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labourer).[^cite_note-zabor.zp.GPW-16]

The Germans reformed [Army Group South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Group_South) in February 1943, and put its headquarters in Zaporizhzhia.[^cite_note-21] [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md) visited the headquarters in February 1943, and again the following month, where he was briefed by Field Marshal [Eric von Manstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_von_Manstein) and his air force counterpart Field Marshal [Wolfram Freiherr von Richthofen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfram_Freiherr_von_Richthofen), and in September 1943,[^cite_note-22] the month the Army Group moved its headquarters to [Kirovohrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kropyvnytskyi).[^cite_note-Manstein301-23]

In August 1943, the Germans built the [Panther-Wotan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panther-Wotan_line) defence line along the Dnieper from [Kyiv](585629.md) to [Crimea](163045.md). They retreated back to this line in September 1943, holding the city as a bridgehead over the Dnieper with elements of 40th Panzer and 17th Corps.[^cite_note-Timeline1943-24] The Soviet [Southwestern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Front_\(Soviet_Union\)), commanded by [Army General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_General_\(Soviet_Union\)) [Rodion Malinovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodion_Malinovsky), attacked Zaporizhzhia on 10 October 1943.[^cite_note-Timeline1943-24] The defenders repelled these attacks, but the Red Army launched a surprise night attack on 13 October, which succeeded in reclaiming most parts of the city.[^cite_note-Lelyushenko-25]

### 1991–present



Modern Zaporizhzhia (2015)

In 2004, to alleviate congestion around the [Zaporizhzhia Arch Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_Arch_Bridge) area, construction began on the [New Zaporizhzhia Dniper Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zaporizhzhia_Dniper_Bridge), although construction was halted soon after it began, due to a lack of funding.[^cite_note-26]

#### Russo-Ukrainian War

During the [2014 Euromaidan regional state administration occupations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Euromaidan_regional_state_administration_occupations), during protests against President [Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych),[^cite_note-27] Zaporizhzhia's [regional state administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Ukraine) building was occupied by 4,500 protesters,[^cite_note-28] and there were clashes between Ukrainian and [pro-Russian activists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_pro-Russian_unrest_in_Ukraine) in April 2014.[^cite_note-29]

On 19 May 2016, the [Verkhovna Rada](844993.md) approved the "[Decommunisation Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decommunization_in_Ukraine)".[^cite_note-decommupbbcUZap-30] Since the introduction of the law, the [Zaporizhzhia City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_City_Council) has renamed over 50 streets and administrative areas of the city,[^cite_note-31] monuments of the [Soviet Union leaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_leaders_of_the_Soviet_Union) [Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenin) and [Felix Dzerzhinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Dzerzhinsky) have been destroyed,[^cite_note-Ukraineremoves36396854-32][^cite_note-33] and names honouring Soviet leaders in the titles of industrial plants, factories, culture centres, and the [DniproHES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DniproHES) have been removed.[^cite_note-34]

##### Russian invasion (2022)

Further information: [2022 bombing of Zaporizhzhia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_bombing_of_Zaporizhzhia)



Residential buildings in Zaporizhzhia after Russian missile strikes on 22 March 2023

Russian forces have been engaged in ongoing attacks on Zaporizhzhia since the beginning of the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md). On 27 February, fighting was reported in the southern outskirts,[^cite_note-35] and Russian forces began shelling the city later that evening.[^cite_note-36] Russia [invaded and occupied part of Zaporizhzhia Oblast](70396583.md) but failed to take Zaporizhzhia itself. On 3 March, Russian forces approached the [Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant](1763005.md), raising concerns about a potential nuclear meltdown.[^cite_note-NPR110322-37][^cite_note-38] Russian military forces fired missiles on Zaporizhzhia on the evening of 12/13 May.[^cite_note-39]

On 30 September, hours before Russia formally [annexed Southern and Eastern Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Southern_and_Eastern_Ukraine), the Russian Armed Forces [launched S-300 missiles at a civilian convoy in Zaporizhzhia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_attack_on_civilian_convoy_in_Zaporizhzhia), killing at least 30 people.[^cite_note-bbc-40] On 9 October, Russian forces [launched rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_residential_building_airstrike) at residential buildings, killing at least 17 people.[^cite_note-41]

## Geography

Zaporizhzhia is located in south-eastern [Ukraine](31750.md). The Dnieper splits the city in two; between them is [Khortytsia Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khortytsia). The city covers 334 km<sup>2</sup> (129 sq mi) at an elevation of 50 m (160 ft) above sea level.[^cite_note-42] The New and Old Dnieper flow past around Khortytsia: The [New Dnieper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Dnieper?action=edit\&redlink=1) is about 800 m (2,600 feet) wide while the [Old Dnieper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Dnieper?action=edit\&redlink=1) is about 200 m (660 feet) wide. The island size is 12 km × 2 km (7.5 mi × 1.2 mi). Smaller rivers in the city also enter the Dnieper: [Sukha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukha_Moskovka?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Mokra Moskovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokra_Moskovka?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Kushuhum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushuhum_River?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Verkhnia Khortytsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verkhnia_Khortytsia?action=edit\&redlink=1).

The flora of Khortytsia is unique and diverse, due to the dry [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe) air and a large freshwater basin, which cleans the air polluted by industry. The island is a [national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_park). The ground surface is cut by large ravines ("*balka*"), hiking routes and historical monuments. The island, which is a popular recreational area, has [sanatoriums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanatorium), resorts, health centres, and sandy beaches.[^cite_note-43]

### Industry



Industry and river port

Zaporizhzhia is an important industrial centre of Ukraine, the country's main car manufacturing company, the [Motor-Sich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor-Sich) aircraft engine manufacturer. Well supplied with electricity, Zaporizhzhia forms, together with the adjoining Donets Basin ([Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas)) and the Nikopol manganese and [Kryvyi Rih](624764.md) iron mines, one of Ukraine's leading industrial complexes.

The city is a home of Ukraine's main automobile production centre, which is based at the [Zaporizhzhia Automobile Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZAZ) (ZAZ), producing Ukrainian car brands such as *Zaporozhets* and *Tavria*.

After the end of the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution_of_1917), the city became an important industrial centre. The presence of cheap labor and the proximity of deposits of coal, iron ore, and manganese created favorable conditions for large-scale enterprises of the iron and mechanical engineering industries. Today Zaporizhzhia is an important industrial centre of the region with heavy industry (particularly [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy)), aluminium, and chemical industry. Cars, avia motors and radioelectronics are manufactured in the city. The port of Zaporizhzhia is important for transshipment for goods from the Donbas.

Zaporizhstal, Ukraine's fourth largest steelmaker, and ranking 54th in the world, is based in the city.

### Electricity generation

Zaporizhzhia is a large electricity generating hub. There are [hydroelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric) [power plant](212141.md) known as "[DniproHES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Hydroelectric_Station)" Dnieper Hydroelectric Station and the largest nuclear power plant in Europe. Prior to the 2022 invasion, the plants generated about 25% of the Ukrainian electricity supply. Located near [Enerhodar](6097193.md) and about 60 km (37 miles) from Zaporizhzhia is the [Zaporizhzhia thermal power station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_thermal_power_station) and the [Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant](1763005.md), the largest nuclear power plant in Europe.

## Culture



Magara Academic Drama Theatre

Zaporizhzhia has an orchestra, museums, theatres, and libraries. These include the Magara Academic Drama Theatre, the Municipal Theatre Lab "VIE", the Theatre for Young-Age spectators, the Theatre of Horse Riding "[Zaporizhzhian Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhian_Cossacks)", the Zaporizhzhia Regional Museum, the National Museum of the History of the Zaporizhzhian Cossacks, the Zaporizhzhia Regional [Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Museum), the Motor Sich Aviation Museum, and the [Zaporizhzhia Region Universal Scientific Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_Region_Universal_Scientific_Library).

There are a number of small amateur groups of folk music bands, [art galleries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_gallery) in Zaporizhzhia. The city regularly holds festivals, [Cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack) martial arts competitions, and art exhibitions.

Zaporizhzhia has an open-air exhibition-and-sale of [Zaporizhzhia city association of artists «Kolorit»](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolorit) near the 'Fountain of Life' at the [Mayakovskoho square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayakovsky_square_\(Zaporizhzhia\)?action=edit\&redlink=1). A daily exhibition of artists' organizations of the city is a unique place in Zaporizhzhia, where people can meet [craftsmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artisan) and artists, watch [carving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carving), [embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery), [beading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beadwork) classes, and receive advice from professional artists and designers.

## Main sights



[Khortytsya island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khortytsya)



'Fountain of Life' in Zaporizhzhia (built in 2004) with the daily exhibition of [Zaporizhzhian artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolorit).

The historical and cultural museum "Zaporizka Sich" is placed on the northern rocky part of Khotritsa Island. The museum is a reconstruction of the stronghold of the Zaporizhzhian Cossacks, and contains features of the military camp life and their lifestyle.

Each of the smaller islands between the dam and the island Khortytsia has its own legend. On one of them, *Durnya Scala* ("Rock of the Fool"), [Tzar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzar) [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) [flogged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flogged) the Cossacks for their betrayal of the Russians during the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War) between Russia and Sweden. Another small island, *Stolb* ("Pillar"), has a geological feature, which looks like a large bowl in [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) slabs, the Cossack's Bowl. It is said that in summer days, water can be boiled in this "bowl", and the Cossacks used it for cooking *galushki* (boiled dough in a spicy broth).[^cite_note-79]

## Transport links



[Zaporizhzhia International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_International_Airport) in 2019



[Zaporizhzhia-1 railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia-1_railway_station)

Zaporizhzhia is an important transportation hub in Ukraine that includes roads, as well as rail, river and air links for passenger and freight transport. [Zaporizhzhia International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_International_Airport), located to the east of the city on the left-bank of the Dnieper, serves domestic and international flights. Shyroke Airfield is to the west of the city on the right-bank of the Dnieper.

Zaporizhzhia is bypassed beyond its eastern outskirts by a major national [highway M18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_M18_\(Ukraine\)), which connects [Kharkiv](2788558.md) with [Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simferopol). The [H08](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_H08_\(Ukraine\)), which starts just outside Kyiv and travels southeast along the Dnieper through [Kremenchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremenchuk), [Kamianske](1261408.md), [Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro_\(city\)), passes through Zaporizhzhia on the way to [Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariupol). The [H15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_H15_\(Ukraine\)) from [Donetsk](562951.md) and the [H23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_H23_\(Ukraine\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) from Kropyvnytskyi via Kryvyi Rih, both end in Zaporizhzhia.

There are four road bridges and two rail bridges over the Dnieper, nearly all of which bridges cross Khortytsia Island. President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) opened the first stage of the [New Zaporizhzhia Dniper Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zaporizhzhia_Dniper_Bridge) early in 2022.

The city has two rail stations, [Zaporizhzhia-1 railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia-1_railway_station) and Zaporizhzhia-the-Second. The First is the central station, located in the southern part of the city and is a part of Simferopol-Kharkiv, the "north-south" transit route. The line of the Zaporizhzhia-the-Second station connects the Donbas coalfield with Kryvyi Rih. The city has an extensive tram network with 7 lines called the [Zaporizhzhia Tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_Tram).

The city's two river ports are part of the national water transportation infrastructure that connects Kyiv to [Kherson](1025861.md) along the Dnieper. Freight ships and [cutter boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutter_\(boat\)) travel between Zaporizhzhia and nearby villages. The island of Khortytsia splits the Dnieper into two; the main channel passes the island on its eastern side, with the *_Staryi Dnipro_* (Old Dnieper) flowing past the island on the western side.
