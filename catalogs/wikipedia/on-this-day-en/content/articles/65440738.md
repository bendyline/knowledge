The **2026 Atlantic hurricane season** is the current [Atlantic hurricane season](2930244.md) in the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere). The season officially began on June 1, 2026, and will end on November 30, 2026. These dates, adopted by convention, historically describe the period in each year when most subtropical or [tropical cyclogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclogenesis) occurs in the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) (over 97%). The first system, [Tropical Storm Arthur](83471857.md), formed on June 17 and impacted much of the northwestern coast of the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md). A little over a month later, [Tropical Storm Bertha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Bertha_\(2026\)) formed on the opposite side of the gulf, impacting nearly the entire [United States's Gulf Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Gulf_Coast) along its path. In September, [Tropical Storm Edouard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Edouard_\(2026\)) made landfall close to the [Texas–Louisiana border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabine_River_\(Texas%E2%80%93Louisiana\)).&#x20;

On September 21, the 2026 season became the first since [1914](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1914_Atlantic_hurricane_season) to not have a hurricane so late into the season. The season has been extremely inactive due to a very strong [El Niño event](83914690.md) in the Pacific.[^cite_note-1]

In advance of, and during, each hurricane season, several [forecasts of hurricane activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclone_seasonal_forecasting) are issued by national meteorological services, scientific agencies, and noted hurricane experts. These include forecasters from the United States [National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration](37876.md) (NOAA)'s [Climate Prediction Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_Prediction_Center), Tropical Storm Risk (TSR), the United Kingdom's [Met Office](319671.md) (UKMO), and [Colorado State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_State_University) (CSU). The forecasts include weekly and monthly changes in significant factors that help determine the amount of tropical storms, hurricanes, and major hurricanes within a particular season.

According to NOAA and CSU, the average hurricane season between 1991 and 2020 contained about fourteen named storms, seven hurricanes, and three major hurricanes (Category 3 and higher), as well as a median accumulated cyclone energy (ACE) index of 71.04–123.14 units.[^cite_note-Background-2] Broadly speaking, ACE is the measure of the power of a tropical or [subtropical cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_cyclone) multiplied by the length of time it existed. ACE is only calculated for full advisories on specific tropical or subtropical cyclones reaching wind speeds of 39 mph (63 km/h) or higher. NOAA typically describes a season as above-average, average, or below-average depending on the cumulative ACE index, but the number of tropical storms, hurricanes or major hurricanes can also be considered.

### Pre-season forecasts

On December 11, 2025, TSR released its first forecast for the season, expecting a near-normal season, with fourteen named storms, seven hurricanes, three major hurricanes,[^cite_note-20] and an ACE index of 125 units, close to the thirty-year average. TSR noted historically low certainty in this forecast, with the main factors being potentially moderate [El Niño](317311.md) conditions arising in mid- to late-summer 2026, along with a reasonable certainty of warmer-than-average [sea surface temperatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_surface_temperatures) (SSTs) for the season.[^cite_note-Dec21TSR-4]

On April 7, 2026, the [University of Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arizona) (UA) posted its forecast calling for an active season featuring twenty named storms, nine hurricanes, four major hurricanes, and an ACE index of 155 units.[^cite_note-Apr7UA-5] Noted in its forecast were similarities to the [2023 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Atlantic_hurricane_season), namely the possibility of [exceptionally strong El Niño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_El_Ni%C3%B1o_events) conditions for the season, along with the 2026 SST outlook being only slightly lower than that of 2023.[^cite_note-Apr7UA-5] Two days later, CSU and TSR published their April forecasts, both calling for a slightly below-normal season. CSU forecasted thirteen named storms, six hurricanes, and two major hurricanes with an ACE index of 90 units, while TSR forecasted twelve named storms, five hurricanes, and one major hurricane with an ACE index of 66 units. This is due to the likelihood of robust El Niño conditions during the season, which will likely induce above-normal [wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear) across the [Tropical Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Atlantic) and Caribbean, despite some predictions calling for slightly above-average SSTs in the Atlantic.[^cite_note-CSU-April-6][^cite_note-ha6-7] On April 16, [The Weather Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Channel) (TWC) issued its forecast, also calling for a below-average season with twelve named storms, six hurricanes, and two major hurricanes due to the predicted El Niño.[^cite_note-hs6-8] On April 21, [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) (UPenn) issued its prediction of 7–13 systems with a best estimate of 10 systems.[^cite_note-up-9] On April 22, [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University) (NCSU) released its seasonal forecast, calling for an average season of 12–15 named storms, 6–9 hurricanes, and 2–3 major hurricanes while also forecasting near-average activity in the Gulf of Mexico and below-average activity in the Caribbean Sea.[^cite_note-ns-10] Also on April 22, Mexico's [National Meteorological Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servicio_Meteorol%C3%B3gico_Nacional_\(Mexico\)) (SMN) issued its forecast for the season, predicting it to be below-to-near-average, with 11–15 named storms, 4–7 hurricanes, and 1–2 major hurricanes.[^cite_note-SMN-11]

On May 5, the [Instituto de Meteorología de Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_de_Meteorolog%C3%ADa_de_Cuba) (INSMET) issued a forecast calling for eleven tropical cyclones, five hurricanes, and two major hurricanes with one tropical cyclone occurring in the Gulf of Mexico, two in the Caribbean, and eight in the Atlantic.[^cite_note-tc-12] On May 21, forecasters at NOAA's [National Weather Service](286989.md) predicted a below-normal hurricane season with 8–14 named storms, 3–6 hurricanes, and 1–3 major hurricanes, factoring in expected development of an El Niño event, which would reduce the number of tropical systems, along with slightly above-normal Atlantic Ocean temperatures and weaker-than-average trade winds, which would favor development.[^cite_note-May21NOAA-13] On May 28, the Met Office released its prediction of nine named storms, five hurricanes, and two major hurricanes (with ranges of 6–12, 3–7, and 1–3, respectively) as well as an ACE index of 72 units.[^cite_note-UKMO-14]

### Mid-season forecasts

On June 10, CSU reduced its predicted forecast to eleven named storms, five hurricanes and only two major hurricanes with a total ACE of 70, citing the high chance of a moderate-to-strong El Niño developing by September, as well as cooler SST anomalies observed during the month of May.[^cite_note-CSU-June-15] On June 14, UA reduced its predicted hurricane activities in each category compared to its April prediction, with thirteen named storms, five hurricanes, three major hurricanes, and a total ACE of 106, primarily because the tropical Atlantic SST had not increased as much as expected from March to May.[^cite_note-Jun14UA-16] By early July, Colorado State University further reduced its prediction to nine named storms, four hurricanes, and one major hurricane, with anticipation for the ongoing moderate El Niño to intensify into a very strong El Niño by the climatological peak of hurricane season, resulting in very strong vertical wind shear across the Atlantic Basin. Additionally, the university forecast well below-average chances for a direct hurricane strike on the [Continental United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_United_States) and the Caribbean.[^cite_note-JulyCSU-17]

On August 6, NOAA further reduced their forecast amid a lull in activity approaching the typical peak of the season. Numbers were reduced slightly to seven-to-thirteen named storms, two-to-six hurricanes, and zero-to-two major hurricanes, and less than 5% chance of an above average season. These predictions aligned with expectations that El Niño conditions that had begun in June would continue to stifle tropical cyclogenesis.[^cite_note-augustnoaa-18]

## Seasonal summary



This season's ACE index, as of October 2, is approximately 10.8 units, as officially calculated by the NHC.[^cite_note-2026_AHS-21] This number represents the sum of the squares of the maximum sustained wind speeds (measured in [knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_\(unit\))) for all named storms while they are at least tropical storm intensity, divided by 10,000.

On June 17, [Tropical Storm Arthur](83471857.md) formed off the [Texan coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Coastal_Bend), bringing flooding to the [Gulf Coast of the United States](11969.md) and Mexico.[^cite_note-22] An extended hiatus occurred after Arthur's dissipation, caused by the ongoing [El Niño](317311.md) phase enhancing [wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear) across the Atlantic along with [Saharan dust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharan_dust).[^cite_note-23] Wind shear in the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md) during the month of July was the highest since records began in 1979.[^cite_note-24] A month after Arthur dissipated, [Tropical Storm Bertha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Bertha_\(2026\)) formed in the northeastern [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md).[^cite_note-td-25] Quiet conditions resumed until August 12, when Tropical Storm Cristobal formed well east-northeast of Bermuda.[^cite_note-o226-26] Later in the month, Tropical Storm Dolly formed in the [main development region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_development_region), while [Edouard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Edouard_\(2026\)) formed in the northern Gulf of Mexico.[^cite_note-naming-27][^cite_note-\:1-28] On September 19, Tropical Storm Fay formed in the subtropical Atlantic, nearly becoming the first hurricane of the season before quickly weakening due to wind shear two days later.[^cite_note-29] Consequently, the season has become the first since [1914](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1914_Atlantic_hurricane_season) to not have a hurricane so late into the season, and the third since official records began in 1851.[^cite_note-30] Gonzalo later formed on September 25 near [Cape Verde](18962637.md).[^cite_note-\:3-31]

On June 10, the National Hurricane Center began monitoring the [Bay of Campeche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Campeche) for the development of a [low-pressure area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-pressure_area),[^cite_note-32] partially associated with the remnants of [Tropical Storm Cristina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Cristina_\(2026\)) from the [Eastern Pacific basin](3092184.md).[^cite_note-33] On June 14, the low-pressure area moved inland over eastern [Mexico](3966054.md).[^cite_note-34] Conditions in the area were assessed as marginally favorable for development and, on June 16, the NHC started issuing advisories on *[Potential Tropical Cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_Tropical_Cyclone) One* at 15\:00 UTC.[^cite_note-35] The following day at 15\:00 UTC, the system acquired gale-force winds, as well as sustainable, albeit lopsided [convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convection), and was named *Tropical Storm Arthur* by the NHC as it moved offshore from the [Texas](29810.md) coast.[^cite_note-36] A poorly-organized system, Arthur reached peak intensity with 1-minute sustained winds of 40 kt (45 mph) as it underwent a center relocation, making landfall near [Galveston, Texas](53840.md) as a minimal tropical storm before degenerating into a remnant low.[^cite_note-37]

&#x20;As of July 2026, [Arthur J. Gallagher & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_J._Gallagher_&_Co.) estimated losses to be greater than US\$1 billion.[^cite_note-cr-38] Four fatalities have been attributed to Arthur.[^cite_note-ne-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] Across [Northern Mexico](48157875.md), significant rains fell, inundating roads.[^cite_note-43] River flooding occurred in [Piedras Negras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedras_Negras,_Coahuila).[^cite_note-ne-39] In the United States, [flash floods](244391.md) occurred in Texas.[^cite_note-44] Some parts of [Louisiana](18130.md) received almost 30 inches (800 mm) of rain.[^cite_note-45] The [return period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_period) for the rains is estimated to be 1-in-1,000 years.[^cite_note-cr-38] Dozens of tornadoes formed across the Gulf Coast in relation to Arthur.[^cite_note-46]

### Tropical Storm Bertha

| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |

| Duration | July 19 – July 24 |

Main article: [Tropical Storm Bertha (2026)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Bertha_\(2026\))

An [upper-level low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper-level_low) and a surface [trough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trough_\(meteorology\)) formed over the eastern Gulf of Mexico on July 17.[^cite_note-47] The following day, a broad low began to develop.[^cite_note-48] The low then became Tropical Depression Two at 15\:00 UTC on July 19 after developing a well-defined, low-level circulatio&#x6E;*.*[^cite_note-td-25] The system further organized over the next day, and the system was named *Bertha* with tropical-storm-force winds.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] Early on July 21, it attained winds of 60 miles per hour (97 km/h) and a pressure of 995 millibars (29.4 inHg).[^cite_note-51] The storm then weakened,[^cite_note-52] making landfall with winds of 45 miles per hour (72 km/h) close to the [Biloxi Wildlife Management Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biloxi_Wildlife_Management_Area) in [St. Bernard Parish, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Bernard_Parish,_Louisiana) on July 22.[^cite_note-53] On July 23, the center emerged offshore of southern Louisiana.[^cite_note-54] It remained very close to the coastline and moved back inland near the Texas-Louisiana border the same day.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] It weakened to a tropical depression on July 24,[^cite_note-57] soon degenerating to a trough over eastern Texas after losing a well-defined circulation.[^cite_note-58]

Due to Bertha's shape, heavy rains from the storm were uncommon on the mainland.[^cite_note-\:9-59] Instead, the system caused a significant amount of flooding through storm surge along with creating high surf across the [Gulf Coast of the United States](11969.md).[^cite_note-\:0-60] In Florida, dozens of turtle nests were washed out or destroyed due to Bertha.[^cite_note-61] Some roadways were inundated in Louisiana.[^cite_note-\:0-60][^cite_note-62] Per [Aon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aon_plc), Bertha caused economic losses in the tens of millions of dollars.[^cite_note-\:11-63]

On August 9, the NHC identified a trough of low pressure located well-offshore of the United States and west-northwest of [Bermuda](3460.md) as having a low chance of development into a tropical cyclone.[^cite_note-64] Over the next few days, the system gradually organized,[^cite_note-65] eventually becoming sufficiently organized to be classified as a tropical storm, receiving the name *Cristobal* on August 12.[^cite_note-o226-26] Cristobal's environment was quite unfavorable, with high wind shear, low SSTs, and dry air, causing the system to weaken to a tropical depression by August 13.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] Having lost convection, Cristobal became a [post-tropical cyclone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-tropical_cyclone) later that day.[^cite_note-68]

### Tropical Storm Dolly

| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | August 27 – August 28 |
| Peak intensity | 40 mph (65 km/h) (1-min); 1007 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

On August 24, a tropical wave exited the coast of Africa.[^cite_note-69] The disturbance gradually developed within a break in unfavorable conditions within the [Main Development Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Development_Region) (MDR) until it attained sufficient organization to be designated Tropical Storm Dolly on August 27 in the central Atlantic.[^cite_note-naming-27] Dolly failed to strengthen following its formation, entraining dry air into its circulation and slowly losing organization.[^cite_note-70] A [NOAA buoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_buoy) which Dolly passed near failed to find any westerly winds near the storm's center. Thus, at 15\:00 UTC on August 28, Dolly was designated as an open tropical wave.[^cite_note-71] The NHC continued to monitor its remnants for potential redevelopment through August 31, as they crossed the [Leeward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeward_Islands) and [Greater Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Antilles).[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

As a tropical wave, the remnants of Dolly produced heavy rainfall and thunderstorms in the [Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilles). [Fonds-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fonds-Saint-Denis), [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique) recorded 4.41 in (111.9 mm) of rain fell in six hours, including 3.76 in (95.4 mm) in three hours.[^cite_note-74] The remnants of Dolly also dropped rain over [Puerto Rico](23041.md), partially alleviating a drought.[^cite_note-75] Several reservoirs on the island exceeded typical levels of water held.[^cite_note-76] Some seas off the [Dominican Republic](8060.md) had wave heights up to 7 feet (2.1 m).[^cite_note-77]

### Tropical Storm Edouard

| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | August 31 – September 3 |
| Peak intensity | 60 mph (95 km/h) (1-min); 996 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

Main article: [Tropical Storm Edouard (2026)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Edouard_\(2026\))

On August 31, the NHC started advisories on Potential Tropical Cyclone Five.[^cite_note-78] Later that day, the system organized into a tropical depression.[^cite_note-79] At 00\:00 UTC on September 1, the depression strengthened to Tropical Storm Edouard.[^cite_note-\:1-28] Over the next few hours before landfall, Edouard gradually intensified near the coast of Louisiana. At 19\:20 UTC that day, Edouard made landfall near [Johnson Bayou, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_Bayou,_Louisiana), with peak winds of 60 mph (95 km/h) and a pressure of 996 mbar.[^cite_note-edland-80] The system then weakened to a tropical depression over southeastern Texas on September 2.[^cite_note-81] The storm continued over Texas throughout the day, remaining a tropical depression,[^cite_note-82] before eventually degenerating into a remnant low on September 3.[^cite_note-83]

While Edouard moved ashore, it produced wind gusts of 71 mph (114 km/h) in [Texas Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McFaddin_and_Texas_Point_National_Wildlife_Refuges).[^cite_note-edland-80] The storm also dropped heavy rainfall, reaching 17.71 in (450 mm) at a station along the [Little Pine Island Bayou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Island_Bayou).[^cite_note-84] Rainfall left several roads in Texas inundated.[^cite_note-85] Flooding also forced the rescue and evacuation of a couple dozen people.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] Published on September 4, an initial estimate by Aon placed losses in the tens of millions of US dollars.[^cite_note-Aon-Edouard-88]

### Tropical Storm Fay

| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | September 19 – September 29 |
| Peak intensity | 70 mph (110 km/h) (1-min); 995 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

On September 18, the NHC began tracking a trough for development in the eastern Atlantic.[^cite_note-89] On the following day, the system organized and developed into Tropical Depression Six about 525 mi (845 km) southwest of the Azores.[^cite_note-\:2-90] The area the system formed in was flanked by two regions of high wind shear.[^cite_note-91] At 09\:00 UTC on September 20, the depression strengthened to Tropical Storm Fay, with deep convection increasing significantly while moving northeastward.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] On September 21, a building [ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge_\(meteorology\)) north of Fay began turning the system towards the southwest.[^cite_note-94] Around this time, Fay also began experiencing significant wind shear and dry air intrusions, limiting possible intensification and making the system lopsided.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] By September 22, the center of circulation became exposed.[^cite_note-97] Convection collapsed later that day, leading Fay to become a remnant low.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] On September 23, convection restarted over Fay's circulation, marking its return to tropical cyclone status.[^cite_note-100] Early the following day on September 24, Fay restrengthened to a tropical storm.[^cite_note-101] Wind shear and dry air began to impact the system on September 25.[^cite_note-102] Fay then weakened to a tropical depression on September 26.[^cite_note-103] On September 29, Fay briefly restrengthened into a tropical storm once more[^cite_note-104] before weakening back into a depression,[^cite_note-105] and once again transitioning into a post-tropical cyclone later that day.[^cite_note-106]

### Tropical Storm Gonzalo

| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | September 25 – September 26 |
| Peak intensity | 50 mph (85 km/h) (1-min); 1000 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

Early on September 25, an enclosed center of circulation formed near Cape Verde, indicating that the low-pressure area had evolved into a tropical storm. The environment upon Gonzalo's formation was somewhat favorable, with moderate [humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humidity) and SSTs.[^cite_note-\:3-31] The system was sheared and asymmetrical, with most convection displaced to the eastern and southern sides of the storm.[^cite_note-107] Gonzalo was progressing north.[^cite_note-\:32-108][^cite_note-109] Early the next day, [ASCAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASCAT) indicated that Gonzalo's center became elongated and the system as a whole was weakening.[^cite_note-110] At the end of the day, the center had reorganized slightly based on [GOES-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOES-19)'s data, though ASCAT still showed weakening winds.[^cite_note-111] However, the convection continued to pull away from the center of circulation, leading to Gonzalo being declared a post-tropical cyclone.[^cite_note-112]

Across Cape Verde, stormy conditions occurred. Trees were reported to have fallen across several islands including [Sal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sal,_Cape_Verde) and [Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago,_Cape_Verde).[^cite_note-\:22-113][^cite_note-\:12-114] Power outages were also reported on those islands, particularly on northern Santiago.[^cite_note-\:22-113][^cite_note-115] Urban flooding happened in [Santa Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria,_Cape_Verde) and [Praia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praia).[^cite_note-\:12-114] Tourist infrastructure was damaged in Santa Maria while agricultural lands were damaged on [São Nicolau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Nicolau,_Cape_Verde).[^cite_note-\:12-114][^cite_note-116] Several fishing vessels were damage along Cais Beach in [Mosteiros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosteiros,_Cape_Verde_\(municipality\)) while another vessel was swept away by the sea and currently missing.[^cite_note-117] [The Gambia](65656.md) also reported rains from the precursor tropical wave that made houses inaccessible.[^cite_note-118]

### Tropical Storm Hanna

| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | September 28 – September 30 |
| Peak intensity | 45 mph (75 km/h) (1-min); 1002 [mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\)) ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\))) |

On September 26, the NHC began tracking a trough of low pressure well southeast of Bermuda.[^cite_note-119] The system gradually became more organized as it moved northeastward over the following day.[^cite_note-120] At 15\:00 UTC on September 28, the NHC named the system *Hanna* as it moved eastward.[^cite_note-121] Subjected to strong wind shear, Hanna remained a lopsided system as it turned east-southeast.[^cite_note-122] Gradual weakening followed the next day due to the unfavorable environment, and Hanna became post-tropical on September 30 as convection dissipated from its center.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]
