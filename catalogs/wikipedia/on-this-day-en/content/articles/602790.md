# Asymptomatic

Not to be confused with [Asymptotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asymptotic).



[Pulmonary contusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_contusion) due to trauma is an example of a condition that can be asymptomatic with half of people showing no signs at the initial presentation. The [CT scan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan) shows a pulmonary contusion (red arrow) accompanied by a [rib fracture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rib_fracture) (purple arrow).

**Asymptomatic** (or **clinically silent**) is an adjective categorising the medical conditions (i.e., [injuries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injuries) or [diseases](8072.md)) that patients carry but without experiencing their [symptoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signs_and_symptoms), despite an explicit diagnosis (e.g., a positive medical test).

**Pre-symptomatic** is the adjective categorising the time periods during which the medical conditions are asymptomatic.

**Subclinical** and **paucisymptomatic** are other adjectives categorising either the asymptomatic infections (i.e., [subclinical infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subclinical_infection)), or the [psychosomatic illnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychosomatic_medicine) and [mental disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_disorder) expressing a subset of symptoms but not the entire set an explicit medical diagnosis requires.&#x20;

## Examples

An example of an asymptomatic disease is [cytomegalovirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytomegalovirus) (CMV) which is a member of the herpes virus family. "It is estimated that 1% of all newborns are infected with CMV, but the majority of infections are asymptomatic." (Knox, 1983; Kumar et al. 1984)[^cite_note-1] In some diseases, the proportion of asymptomatic cases can be important. For example, in [multiple sclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_sclerosis) it is estimated that around 25% of the cases are asymptomatic, with these cases detected postmortem or just by coincidence (as [incidental findings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incidental_findings)) while treating other diseases.[^cite_note-2]

## Importance

Knowing that a condition is asymptomatic is important because:

- It may be contagious, and the contribution of asymptomatic and pre-symptomatic infections to the transmission level of a disease helps set the required control measures to keep it from spreading.

  [^cite_note-3]

- It is not required that a person undergo treatment. It does not cause later medical problems such as [high blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertension) and [hyperlipidaemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperlipidemia).

  [^cite_note-4]

- Be alert to possible problems: asymptomatic [hypothyroidism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothyroidism) makes a person vulnerable to [Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wernicke%E2%80%93Korsakoff_syndrome) or [beri-beri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiamine_deficiency) following [intravenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intravenous_therapy) glucose.

  [^cite_note-5]

- For some conditions, treatment during the asymptomatic phase is vital. If one waits until symptoms develop, it is too late for survival or to prevent damage.

## Mental health

Subclinical or subthreshold conditions are those for which the full diagnostic criteria are not met and have not been met in the past, although symptoms are present. This can mean that symptoms are not severe enough to merit a diagnosis,[^cite_note-6] or that symptoms are severe but do not meet the criteria of a condition.[^cite_note-7]
