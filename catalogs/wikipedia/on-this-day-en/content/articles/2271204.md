**Cox's Bazar**[^cite_note-2] is a city, fishing port, tourism centre, and the headquarters of the [Cox's Bazar District](452471.md) and [Cox's Bazar Sadar Upazila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar_Sadar_Upazila) in south-eastern [Bangladesh](3454.md). The city has a population of about 196,000. [Cox's Bazar Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar_Beach) is a major tourist attraction.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## Administration

The city is administered by a [Pourashava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipal_corporations_in_Bangladesh) named Cox's Bazar Municipality. It covers an area of 23.4 km<sup>2</sup> (9.0 sq mi) with 58 [mahallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahalla) and 27 wards and as of 2022 had a population of nearly 200,000.[^cite_note-citypopulation-5] Cox's Bazar is connected by road and air with the city of [Chittagong](380451.md).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

The municipality is currently in process of being ugraded into [a city corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar_City_Corporation).[^cite_note-8]

## Etymology

Cox's Bazar derives its name from Captain [Hiram Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiram_Cox), an officer of the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company) and Superintendent of Palongkee outpost. To commemorate his role in refugee rehabilitation work, a market was established and named after him.

Cox's Bazar is also known by the name *Panowa*, which translates literally as "yellow flower". An old name was "Palongkee".[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

## History

During the early 9th century, the greater Chittagong area, including Cox's Bazar, was under the rule of [Arakan](493373.md) kings until its conquest by the [Mughals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) in 1666.[^cite_note-Banglapedia-District-11] When the Mughal Prince [Shah Shuja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Shuja_\(Mughal\)) was passing through the hilly terrain of the present-day Cox's Bazar on his way to Arakan, he was attracted to its scenic and captivating beauty. He commanded his forces to camp there. His retinue of one thousand [palanquins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palanquin) stopped there for some time. A place named *[Dulahazara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulahazara_Union),* meaning "one thousand palanquins", still exists in the area. After the Mughals, the place came under the control of the Tipras and the Arakanese, followed by the Portuguese and then the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire).

The name Cox's Bazar originated from British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) (EIC) official [Hiram Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiram_Cox), who was appointed as the Superintendent of Palonki (today's Cox's Bazar) outpost. He succeeded [Warren Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Hastings), who became the [Governor of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Bengal) following the passage of the [Regulating Act 1773](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulating_Act_1773). Cox embarked upon the task of rehabilitation and settlement for the [Arakanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakanese_people) refugees in the area.[^cite_note-Haksar2009-12] He rehabilitated many refugees in the area, but died in 1799 before finishing his work. To commemorate him, a market was established and named after him, called Cox's Bazar. Cox's Bazar was first established in 1854 and became a municipality in 1869.[^cite_note-Banglapedia-District-11]

After the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857), [Company rule in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_India) came to an end and was replaced by the [British Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown). As a result, Cox's Bazar was declared a district of the [Bengal Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bengal_and_Assam) under the British Crown.

### 20th century

Just after the end of British rule in India in 1947, Cox's Bazar became part of [East Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pakistan). [Fazlul Karim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fazlul_Karim_\(lawyer\)), the first post-independence chairman of Cox's Bazar Municipality, established the Tamarisk Forest along the beach. He wanted to attract tourists as well as to protect the beach from [tsunamis](31161.md). He donated much of his father-in-law's and his own lands as sites for constructing a public library and a town hall. Karim was inspired to build Cox's Bazar as a tourist spot after seeing beaches of Bombay and Karachi, and was a resort pioneer in developing Cox's Bazar as a destination. Karim established a maternity hospital, the stadium and the drainage system by procuring grants from the [Ford Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Foundation) and [Rockefeller Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation) through correspondence. T. H. Matthews, the principal of the Dacca Engineering College (1[–](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation)49\~1954), was a friend who had helped him in these fundraising efforts. Engineer Chandi Charan Das was the government civil engineer who worked on all these projects.

In 1959 the municipality was turned into a town committee.[^cite_note-Banglapedia-District-11] In 1961 the [Geological Survey of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_Survey_of_Pakistan) initiated investigation of radioactive minerals like [monazite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monazite) around the Cox's Bazar sea-beach area.[^cite_note-Bpedia_BSHM-13]

In 1971, Cox's Bazar [wharf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wharf) was used as a naval port by the [Pakistan Navy](1183789.md)'s [gunboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunboats). This and the nearby [airstrip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airstrip) of the [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md) were the scene of intense shelling by the [Indian Navy](604522.md) during the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War). During the war, Pakistani soldiers killed many people in the town, including eminent lawyer Jnanendralal Chowdhury. The killing of two freedom fighters named Farhad and Subhash at Badar Mokam is also recorded in history.[^cite_note-Banglapedia-Upazila-14]

After Bangladesh's independence, Cox's Bazar started to receive administrative attention. In 1972 the town committee of Cox's Bazar was once again turned into a municipality. In 1975, The government of Bangladesh established a pilot plant at Kalatali.[^cite_note-Bpedia_BSHM-13] In 1984, Cox's Bazar subdivision was promoted to a district, and five years later (in 1989) the Cox's Bazar municipality was elevated to B-grade.[^cite_note-Banglapedia-District-11] In 1994 (jobs) the Marine Fisheries and Technology Station (MFTS) was established at Cox's Bazar. MFTS is a research station of [Bangladesh Fisheries Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Fisheries_Research_Institute) (BFRI) headquartered in [Mymensingh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mymensingh_District). The station covers a land area of four hectares and contains five laboratories.[^cite_note-15]

### 21st century

In September 2012 the municipality was the site of the [Cox's Bazar and Ramu riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Ramu_violence), where local Muslims attacked the Buddhist community over an alleged [Quran desecration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran_desecration) posted to Facebook.[^cite_note-16]

In 2017, hundreds of thousands of [Rohingya](1918632.md) refugees from Myanmar arrived in [Cox's Bazar District](452471.md), amounting to 725,000 in October 2018; the resulting [Kutupalong refugee camp](54548485.md) became the largest refugee camp in the world.[^cite_note-nrc-17]

In March 2021, a fire at the refugee camp left 15 dead and some 400 missing and displaced more than 45,000 mostly Rohingya refugees.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

In 2023 Bangladesh evacuated over 50,000 people in Cox's Bazar to safe shelters as [Cyclone Mocha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Mocha) approached.[^cite_note-20]

During the [July Revolution](78109234.md), around 10,000 tourists were stuck in Cox's Bazar, who were later transported by plane and army patrol.[^cite_note-21]

## Geography and climate



Cox's Bazar Map from Series U542, US Army Map Service, 1955

Cox's Bazar is located 150 km (93 mi) south of the divisional headquarter city of [Chittagong](380451.md). Cox's Bazar town has an area of 6.85 km<sup>2</sup> (2.64 sq mi), and is bounded by Bakkhali River on the north and East, [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) in the West, and Jhilwanj Union in the south.[^cite_note-22]

The [beach in Cox's Bazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_in_Cox's_Bazar) has a gentle slope and with a length of 120 km (75 mi) and is one of the longest natural sea beaches of South East Asia.

Cox's Bazar lies on a coastal plain in the southeastern corner of Bangladesh. From above, the plain appears to bulge out into the Bay of Bengal. Along the shore is an extensive area of beach and [dunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune). Most of the city is built on a [floodplain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floodplain) that is lower in elevation than the dunes, making it more susceptible to flooding due to [cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone) and [storm surges](449744.md). The Cox's Bazar coastal plain was formed after the sea reached its present level around 6,500 years ago, with the area of the current floodplain originally forming a sediment sink that has since been gradually filled in by the Bakkhali river as well as smaller streams coming down from the hills.[^cite_note-Alam_et_al-23]

The climate of Bangladesh is mostly determined by its location in the [tropical monsoon climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_monsoon_climate) region: high temperatures, heavy rainfall, and generally excessive humidity, with distinct seasonal variations.[^cite_note-24] The climate of Cox's Bazar is mostly similar to the rest of the country, but with an even wetter southwest monsoon season due to its coastal location. The annual average temperature in Cox's Bazar is a maximum of 30.1 °C (86.2 °F) and a minimum of 22.0 °C (71.6 °F). The average annual rainfall is 3,524 mm (138.7 in).

According to the [2022 Bangladesh census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Bangladeshi_census), Cox's Bazar city had 45,944 households and a population of 196,385. Cox's Bazar had a literacy rate of 81.57%: 83.49% for males and 79.36% for females, and a sex ratio of 114.06 males per 100 females. 10.30% of the population was under 5 years of age.[^cite_note-2022census-29]

According to the [2011 Bangladesh census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Bangladeshi_census), Cox's Bazar had 31,431 households and a population of 167,477. 38,042 (22.71%) were under 10 years of age. Cox's Bazar had a literacy rate (age 7 and over) of 55.72%, compared to the national average of 51.8%, and a sex ratio of 776 females per 1000 males. ## Economy



Panorama of Cox's Bazar in the early morning: clouds on a blue sky, still water and forest in the distance.

### Tourism



Cox's Bazar (Kolatoli Sea Beach) after sunset

The major economic activity in Cox's Bazar is tourism. A number of hotels, guest houses, and motels have been built in the city and coastal region, and many people are involved in the service industry.

- [Cox's Bazar Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar_Beach): The beach in Cox's Bazar is the main attraction of the town with an unbroken length of 150 km (93 mi) also termed the "longest natural unbroken sea beach" in the world.

  [^cite_note-31]

  [^cite_note-32]

- [Himchari National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himchari_National_Park):

  [^cite_note-33]

  &#x20;Himchari is located just south of the Cox's Bazar town. It consists of lush tropical rain forest, grasslands and trees, and features a number of waterfalls, the biggest of which cascades down toward the sandy, sun-drenched beach. The National Park was established in 1980 by the government of Bangladesh as a conservation area for research, education, and recreation. Once it was the stomping grounds of herds of [Asian elephant](379035.md). It is still home to a limited number of these mammals.

  [^cite_note-34]

- Aggmeda Khyang: a large Buddhist monastery, and a place revered by around 400,000 Buddhist people of Cox's Bazar; and the [Chittagong Hill Tracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Hill_Tracts). The main sanctuary is posted on a series of round timber columns. It has a prayer chamber and an assembly hall along with a repository of large and small bronze Buddha images and a number of old manuscripts.

- [Ramu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramu,_Cox's_Bazar): about 10 km (6 mi) from Cox's Bazar,

  [^cite_note-Banglapedia-35]

  &#x20;is a village with a sizeable Buddhist population. The village sells [handicrafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handicrafts) and homemade cigars. There are monasteries, khyangs and pagodas containing images of Buddha in bronze, gold and other metals with precious stones. One of the temples, on the bank of the Baghkhali River, houses relics and Burmese handicrafts, and also a large bronze statue of Buddha measuring thirteen feet high which rests on a six feet high pedestal. Weavers ply their trade in open workshops and craftsmen make handmade cigars in their pagoda like houses.

- [Dulhazra Safari Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dulhazra_Safari_Park): Dulhazra Safari Park is the first safari park in Bangladesh. It was developed on an undulating landscape covering approximately 2,224 acres (9.00 km

  2

  ) in Chakaria Upazila, Cox's Bazar District. The forest is characterized by tropical evergreen vegetation, rich in Garjan, Boilam, Telsur, and Chapalish, along with various herbs, shrubs, and creepers. The safari park is a designated protected area where animals are kept in a spacious environment that mimics their natural habitat. Visitors can easily observe the animals during their visit, whether by bus, jeep, or on foot. The park was established based on a South Asian model and serves as an extension of an animal sanctuary located along the Chittagong-Cox's Bazar road, about 50 km (30 mi) from Cox's Bazar town. The sanctuary itself protects a significant number of wild elephants native to the area. Within the safari park, domesticated elephants are available for rides. Other animal attractions include lions, Bengal tigers, crocodiles, bears, chitals, and a variety of birds and monkeys.

The only aquarium in Bangladesh is in Cox's Bazar. Attractions also include parasailing, water biking, beach biking, horse riding, Cox Carnival circus show, Daria Nagar Ecopark, numerous architectural attractions, Shishu Parks and numerous photogenic sites. The largest safari park in the country, Dulhazara Safari Park, is nearby. There is a forest reserve, [Naf Tourism Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naf_Tourism_Park), which also has a cable car planned.[^cite_note-36]

In 2013, the Bangladesh government formed the Tourist Police unit to better protect local and foreign tourists, as well as to look after the nature and wildlife in the tourist spots of Cox's Bazar.[^cite_note-37]

### Fishing

A large number of people are involved in fishing and collecting seafood and sea products for their livelihood. Cox's Bazar is one of the few major spots for aquaculture in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-38] Along with [Khulna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khulna), it is considered a major source of revenue from foreign exchanges., besides a mix of [small-scale agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small-scale_agriculture), marine and inland fishing and salt production are other industrial sources that play important roles in the national economy.

## Transportation and Development

### Transportation



Submarine [Cable Landing Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_landing_point)

#### Road



National Highway at Cox's Bazar (N1)

The [N110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N110_\(Bangladesh\)) is the national highway that connects the city with the capital and largest city [Dhaka](56656.md) as well as with many other parts of the country via [N1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N1_\(Bangladesh\)).[^cite_note-39] [Z1132](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z1132_\(Bangladesh\)) works as the alternative gateway from the Eidgaon part of the N1.[^cite_note-40]

Travelling by bus to Cox's Bazar from [Dhaka](56656.md) generally takes about 9–12 hours depending on the traffic.

In January 2024, two [open-top buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-top_bus) for tourists were launched, travelling from Laboni Point to [Sabrang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabrang_Union) in [Teknaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teknaf_Upazila) via [Marine Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar%E2%80%93Teknaf_Marine_Drive).[^cite_note-41]

#### Airport



Cox's Bazar Airport Terminal

The city of Cox's Bazar is served by [Cox's Bazar International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar_International_Airport), located northwest of the city. It is one of four Bangladesh's international airports. The main airlines serving the airport are [Biman Bangladesh Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biman_Bangladesh_Airlines), [US-Bangla Airlines](41720976.md) and [Novoair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novoair). In November 2020, Biman Bangladesh Airlines started flights between Sylhet and Cox's Bazar, which was the first direct flight between two cities without any connecting flight from Dhaka.[^cite_note-ZYL-CXB-42] Cox's Bazar Airport is in the process of being upgraded by 15 July 2025 to an [international airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_of_entry) in order to attract more tourists.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] This will make it the fourth [international airport in Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airports_in_Bangladesh).[^cite_note-46]

#### Rail



Cox's Bazar railway station

The [Cox's Bazar railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar_railway_station) is the main railway station of the district, providing trains on the [Chittagong–Cox's Bazar line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong%E2%80%93Cox's_Bazar_line) operated by the state-run [Bangladesh Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Railway). This station also provides trains that run from Dhaka, such as the [Cox's Bazaar Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar_Express). On 11 November 2023, Prime Minister [Sheikh Hasina](623439.md) inaugurated the Cox's Bazar railway station. The station was opened to the public on 1 December 2023.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

#### Education

1962: Cox's Bazar Government College is the earliest secondary and bachelor's degree offering college founded in Cox's Bazar.\
1985: Cox's Bazar Law College is the first profession-based college founded in this district. \
1991: Cox's Bazar Govt. Women's College is the first Secondary and bachelor's degree offering college in this district solely for women. \
2006: Cox's Bazar Commerce College is the First College only for [Business Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_studies), [Humanities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanities) and Business Management. \
2008: [Cox's Bazar Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar_Medical_College) is the first medical college in this district. \
2013: [Cox's Bazar International University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox's_Bazar_International_University) is founded as the first private university in the district.
