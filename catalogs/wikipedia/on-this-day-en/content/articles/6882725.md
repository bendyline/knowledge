# List of diplomatic missions of Russia



Russian diplomatic missions

This is a **list of diplomatic missions of Russia**. These missions are subordinate to the [Russian Ministry of Foreign Affairs](4432213.md). The [Russian Federation](25391.md) has one of the largest networks of embassies and consulates of any country. Russia has significant interests in Eastern Europe, the [Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East) and especially in the former states of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). It also has extensive ties to countries in the [developing world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_world), a legacy of [Cold War](325329.md) diplomatic efforts to extend the Soviet Union's influence in Africa and Asia which are now more important for commercial reasons.

Russia established several consulates in the [United States](3434750.md) and [Canada](5042916.md) to cater to Russian immigrants. In 1917, the Tsarist government vanished, with a number of consuls who maintained tsarist loyalties establishing the "Council of Ambassadors" ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Совещания послов), through which they worked as [embassies without a government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_without_a_government).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] Among these were consuls in seven U.S. cities and three Canadian cities, receiving financing from the U.S. government. The consuls stopped their services in the late 1920s; the U.S. government seized the records of the consulates. The seizure started a long dispute. The [National Archives and Records Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Archives_and_Records_Administration) received the documents in 1949. In 1980 the U.S. government loaned the documents of the Canadian consulates to the [Library and Archives Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_and_Archives_Canada). On 31 January 1990 the U.S. returned the documents to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and kept the [microfilms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microfilm) as evidence.[^cite_note-arc-3]

After 1992, due to financial reasons,[^cite_note-Kozemjakin\&Kanet1995-4] embassies in [Freetown](56661.md) ([Sierra Leone](27308.md)), [Monrovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monrovia) ([Liberia](17791.md)), [Maseru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maseru) ([Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho)), [Mogadishu](60154.md) ([Somalia](27358.md)), [Niamey](57061.md) ([Niger](21373.md)), [Ouagadougou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouagadougou) ([Burkina Faso](3470.md)), and [Port Moresby](57387.md) ([Papua New Guinea](22943.md)) were closed. In 1995 the embassy in [Paramaribo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaribo) ([Suriname](26828.md)) also suspended operations.

The Russian Federation has no diplomatic relations with [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan) and [Solomon Islands](265083.md). Since [Georgia](48768.md) and Russia severed diplomatic relations in 2008, the Swiss embassy in [Tbilisi](42298.md) hosts a Russian interests section.

In February 2022, [Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_States_of_Micronesia) and [Ukraine](31750.md) severed diplomatic relations with Russia.&#x20;

Russia reached agreements to open or reopen diplomatic missions in Burkina Faso, South Sudan, and Equatorial Guinea in July 2023, and Maldives in March 2024. The embassy in Burkina Faso was opened in December 2023, and the General Consulate in Maldives in March 2024.
