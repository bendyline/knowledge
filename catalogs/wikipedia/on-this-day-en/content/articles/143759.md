**North Yorkshire** is a [ceremonial county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England) in [Northern England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_England).[^cite_note-4] It is bordered by [County Durham](400297.md) to the north, the [North Sea](21179.md) to the east, the [East Riding of Yorkshire](153612.md) to the south-east, [South Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Yorkshire) to the south, [West Yorkshire](149139.md) to the south-west, and [Cumbria](73021.md) and [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire) to the west.

The county is the largest in England by land area, at 8,654 km<sup>2</sup> (3,341 sq mi), and had an estimated population of 1,201,415 in 2024. [Middlesbrough](334775.md) and [Redcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redcar) are located in the north-east of the county and form part of the [Teesside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesside) conurbation, which extends into County Durham. The city of [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York) is located in the south. The remainder of the county is rural, and its settlements include [Harrogate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrogate) in the south-west, the city of [Ripon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripon) near the centre, and [Scarborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough,_North_Yorkshire) on the coast in the east. For [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_England) purposes the county comprises the [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England) areas of [North Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Yorkshire_\(district\)), [Middlesbrough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Middlesbrough), [Redcar and Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redcar_and_Cleveland), [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_York), and part of [Stockton-on-Tees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton-on-Tees_Borough_Council). The local authorities of [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_York_Council) and [North Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Yorkshire_Council) form a [combined authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_authority) of [the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_and_North_Yorkshire_Combined_Authority), and the local authorities of the other three areas are part of the [Tees Valley Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Valley_Combined_Authority). The county was historically part of [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire).

The centre of the county contains a wide plain, called the [Vale of Mowbray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_of_Mowbray) in the north and [Vale of York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_of_York) in the south. The [North York Moors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_York_Moors) uplands lie to the east, and south of them the [Vale of Pickering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vale_of_Pickering) is separated from the main plain by the [Howardian Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howardian_Hills). Further east, the county has a coastline on the North Sea. The west of the county contains the [Yorkshire Dales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Dales), an extensive upland area which contains the source of the [River Ouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Ouse,_Yorkshire)/[Ure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Ure) and many of its tributaries, which together drain most of the county before reaching the [Humber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humber) estuary in the south. The Dales also contain the county's highest point, [Whernside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whernside), at 2,415 feet (736 m).[^cite_note-tall_hill2-5]

## Geography



[Rosedale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosedale,_North_Yorkshire), in the [North York Moors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_York_Moors)

The [geology of North Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Yorkshire) is closely reflected in its landscape. Within the county are the [North York Moors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_York_Moors_National_Park) and most of the [Yorkshire Dales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Dales), two of eleven areas in England and Wales to be designated [national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_parks_of_England_and_Wales). Between the North York Moors in the east and the [Pennine Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennines). The highest point is [Whernside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whernside), on the Cumbrian border, at 2,415 feet (736 m).[^cite_note-tall_hill-6] A distinctive hill to the far north east of the county is [Roseberry Topping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roseberry_Topping).

North Yorkshire contains several major rivers. The [River Tees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Tees) is the most northerly, forming part of the border between North Yorkshire and County Durham in its lower reaches and flowing east through [Teesdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesdale) before reaching the North Sea near Redcar. The Yorkshire Dales are the source of many of the county's major rivers, including the [Aire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Aire), [Lune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Lune), [Ribble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Ribble), [Swale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Swale), [Ure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Ure), and [Wharfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Wharfe).[^cite_note-7] The Aire, Swale, and Wharfe are tributaries of the Ure/Ouse, which at 208 km (129 mi) long is the sixth-longest river in the United Kingdom. The river is called the Ure until it meets Ouse Gill beck just below the village of Great Ouseburn, where it becomes the Ouse and flows south before exiting the county near Goole and entering the [Humber estuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humber_estuary).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] The North York Moors are the catchment for a number of rivers: the [Leven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Leven,_North_Yorkshire) which flows north into the Tees between Yarm and Ingleby Barwick; the [Esk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Esk,_North_Yorkshire) flows east directly into the North Sea at Whitby as well as the [Rye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Rye,_Yorkshire) (which later becomes the [Derwent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Derwent,_Yorkshire) at Malton) flows south into the River Ouse at Goole.[^cite_note-10]

The county is less than 10 miles from [Morecambe Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morecambe_Bay) at its closest point.

### Urban to rural Green belt

Further information: [South and West Yorkshire Green Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_and_West_Yorkshire_Green_Belt) and [York Green Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_Green_Belt)

North Yorkshire contains a small section of [green belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_belt_\(United_Kingdom\)) in the south of the county, which surrounds the neighbouring metropolitan area of Leeds along the North and West Yorkshire borders. It extends to the east to cover small communities such as [Huby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huby,_Harrogate), [Kirkby Overblow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkby_Overblow), and [Follifoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follifoot) before covering the gap between the towns of Harrogate and Knaresborough, helping to keep those towns separate. The belt extends into the western area of the former Selby district, reaching as far as Tadcaster and [Balne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balne,_North_Yorkshire). The belt was first drawn up from the 1950s.

The city of York has an independent surrounding belt area affording protections to several outlying settlements such as Haxby and Dunnington, and it too extends into the surrounding county.

### Protected landscapes

North Yorkshire includes all or most of four nationally protected landscapes, and smaller parts of two more protected landscapes. The [Yorkshire Dales National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Dales_National_Park) adjoins the South and West Yorkshire Green Belt in the south and extends across the county into [Cumbria](73021.md) and [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire) in the north and west. The [North York Moors National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_York_Moors_National_Park), the [Nidderdale National Landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidderdale_National_Landscape) (adjoining the green belt in the south and the Yorkshire Dales National Park in the west) and the [Howardian Hills National Landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howardian_Hills) are wholly within the county. Part of the [Forest of Bowland National Landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_of_Bowland), and a small part of the [North Pennines National Landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pennines_National_Landscape), are also within the county.

### Climate

North Yorkshire has a [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate) [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate), like most of the UK. There are large climate variations within the county. The upper [Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennines) border on a [Subarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic) climate. Overall, with the county being situated in the east, it receives below-average rainfall for the UK. Inside North Yorkshire, the upper Dales of the Pennines are one of the wettest parts of England, where in contrast the driest parts of the Vale of Mowbray are some of the driest areas in the UK.[^cite_note-auto1-11]

Summer temperatures are above average, at 22 °C. Highs can regularly reach up to 28 °C, with over 30 °C reached in [heat waves](293270.md). Winter temperatures are below average, with average lows of 1 °C. Snow and fog can be expected depending on location. The [North York Moors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_York_Moors) and [Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennines) have snow lying for an average of between 45 and 75 days per year.[^cite_note-auto1-11] Sunshine is most plentiful on the coast, receiving an average of 1,650 hours per year. It reduces further west in the county, with the Pennines receiving 1,250 hours per year.

#### History

The [Local Government Act 1888](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1888) reformed English local government by creating [administrative counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_counties_of_England), [county boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_borough), and elected county councils and county borough councils to govern them. County boroughs largely consisted of those settlements with a population over 50,000, and administrative counties covered smaller towns and rural areas.[^cite_note-26] The three ridings of Yorkshire were reconstituted as the [administrative counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_counties_of_England) of [County of York, East Riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_York,_East_Riding), [County of York, North Riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_York,_North_Riding), and [County of York, West Riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_York,_West_Riding). Within what would later become North Yorkshire, [Middlesbrough](334775.md) and [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York) were made county boroughs. In 1967, the county borough of Middlesbrough was abolished and replaced with the larger [county borough of Teesside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Borough_of_Teesside), which included [Stockton-on-Tees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton-on-Tees), [Redcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redcar), and other surrounding areas.[^cite_note-27]

The [Local Government Act 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972), which came into effect in 1974, reformed English local government again. It introduced [metropolitan counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_county), which covered the largest urban areas, and [non-metropolitan counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_county) elsewhere. Both types of county were divided into districts, with local government functions divided between a county council and district councils.[^cite_note-\:2-28][^cite_note-29] The non-metropolitan county of North Yorkshire was created from the majority of the North Riding, the northern part of the West Riding, the county borough of York, and parts of the northern and eastern edges of the East Riding including [Filey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filey) and [Norton-on-Derwent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norton-on-Derwent).[^cite_note-\:04-30] The area in the north-east around the lower reaches of the [River Tees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Tees), including Middlesbrough, became part of the new county of [Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_\(county\)). North Yorkshire was divided into eight districts: [Craven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craven_District), [Hambleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hambleton_District), [Harrogate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Harrogate), [Richmondshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmondshire), [Ryedale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryedale_District), [Scarborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Scarborough), [Selby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selby_District), and [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The provision of local government services was divided between [North Yorkshire County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Yorkshire_County_Council_election,_2009) and the district councils of the eight non-metropolitan districts.[^cite_note-\:04-30]

In 1996, Cleveland was abolished. Its districts became [unitary authority areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority_area), their councils having the powers of a non-metropolitan county council and non-metropolitan district council.[^cite_note-33] Middlesbrough, [Redcar and Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redcar_and_Cleveland), and the part of Stockton-on-Tees south of the River Tees became part of North Yorkshire for ceremonial purposes.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] In the same year, the district of [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_York) was enlarged by taking in parishes from the Harrogate, Ryedale and Selby districts, and also became a [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England) area.[^cite_note-36]

In 2023, the non-metropolitan county of North Yorkshire became a unitary authority area. The remaining seven districts were abolished and the county council took on the responsibilities of the district councils, renaming itself North Yorkshire Council as part of the restructuring process.[^cite_note-37]

### Regional

Main articles: [Tees Valley Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Valley_Combined_Authority) and [York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_and_North_Yorkshire_Combined_Authority)

[Combined authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_authorities) allow two or more councils to collaborate across local authority boundaries, and to receive a devolution deal from the [Government of the United Kingdom](25318118.md). The councils of North Yorkshire participate in two separate combined authorities.

The [Tees Valley Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Valley_Combined_Authority) was created in 2016. Its members are [Darlington Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darlington_Borough_Council), [Hartlepool Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartlepool_Borough_Council), [Middlesbrough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_Council), [Redcar and Cleveland Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redcar_and_Cleveland_Borough_Council), and [Stockton-on-Tees Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockton-on-Tees_Borough_Council).[^cite_note-38] The authority is led by the [Tees Valley Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Valley_Mayor), who since the first election to the position in 2017 has been [Ben Houchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Houchen) of the [Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)).

The [York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_and_North_Yorkshire_Combined_Authority) was created in February 2024, and its members are the [City of York Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_York_Council) and [North Yorkshire Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Yorkshire_Council).[^cite_note-39] The authority is led by the [Mayor of York and North Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_York_and_North_Yorkshire). The first election to the position [took place in 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_York_and_North_Yorkshire_mayoral_election), and resulted in [David Skaith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Skaith) of the [Labour Party](19279158.md) being elected. Both North Yorkshire Council and the combined authority are governed from [County Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Hall,_Northallerton), Northallerton.[^cite_note-40]

## Economy

In large areas of North Yorkshire, agriculture is the primary source of employment. Approximately 85% of the county is considered to be "rural or super sparse".[^cite_note-41]

Other sectors in 2019 included some manufacturing, the provision of accommodation and meals (primarily for tourists) which accounted for 19% of all jobs. Food manufacturing employed 11% of workers. A few people are involved in forestry and fishing in 2019. The average weekly earnings in 2018 were £531. Some 15% of workers declared themselves as self-employed. One report in late 2020 stated that "North Yorkshire has a relatively healthy and diverse economy which largely mirrors the national picture in terms of productivity and jobs.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]



The [Teesport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teesport) sea port handled over 53 million tonnes of cargo in 2013.



[Drax Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drax_Power_Station) has the highest generating capacity of any power station in the UK.

Mineral extraction and power generation are also sectors of the economy, as is high technology.[^cite_note-44]

Tourism is a significant contributor to the economy. A study of visitors between 2013 and 2015 indicated that the Borough of Scarborough, including Filey, Whitby and parts of the North York Moors National Park, received 1.4m trips per year on average.[^cite_note-45] A 2016 report by the National Park, states the park area gets 7.93 million visitors annually, generating £647 million and supporting 10,900 full-time equivalent jobs.[^cite_note-46]

The Yorkshire Dales have also attracted many visitors. In 2016, there were 3.8 million visits to the National Park including 0.48 million who stayed at least one night. The parks service estimates that this contributed £252 million to the economy and provided 3,583 full-time equivalent jobs. The wider Yorkshire Dales area received 9.7 million visitors who contributed £644 million to the economy.[^cite_note-47] The North York Moors and Yorkshire Dales are among England's best known destinations.[^cite_note-48]



The Shambles, a popular tourist area in York

[York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York) is a popular tourist destination. A 2014 report, based on 2012 data, stated that York alone receives 6.9 million visitors annually; they contribute £564 million to the economy and support over 19,000 jobs.[^cite_note-49] In the 2017 [Condé Nast Traveller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cond%C3%A9_Nast_Traveller) survey of readers, York rated 12th among The 15 Best Cities in the UK for visitors.[^cite_note-50] In a 2020 Condé Nast Traveller report, York rated as the sixth best among ten "urban destinations \[in the UK] that scored the highest marks when it comes to ... nightlife, restaurants and friendliness".[^cite_note-51]

During February 2020 to January 2021, the average property in North Yorkshire county sold for £240,000, up by £8100 over the previous 12 months. By comparison, the average for England and Wales was £314,000.[^cite_note-52] In certain communities of North Yorkshire, however, house prices were higher than average for the county, as of early 2021: Harrogate (average value: £376,195), Knaresborough (£375,625), Tadcaster (£314,278), Leyburn (£309,165) and Ripon (£299,998), for example.[^cite_note-53]

### Effects of the pandemic

|  | This section needs to be **updated**. Please help update this article to reflect recent events or newly available information. *(January 2023)* |
| --- | --- |

Unemployment in the county was traditionally low in recent years, but the lockdowns and travel restrictions necessitated by the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) had a negative effect on the economy during much of 2020 and into 2021.[^cite_note-59] The UK government said in early February 2021 that it was planning "unprecedented levels of support to help businesses \[in the UK] survive the crisis".[^cite_note-60]

York experienced high unemployment during lockdown periods. One analysis (by the York and North Yorkshire Local Enterprise Partnership) predicted in August 2020 that "as many as 13,835 jobs in York will be lost in the scenario considered most likely, taking the city's unemployment rate to 14.5%". Some critics suggested that part of the problem was caused by "over-reliance on the booming tourism industry at the expense of a long-term economic plan".[^cite_note-auto2-61] A report in mid June 2020 stated that unemployment had risen 114% over the previous year because of restrictions imposed as a result of the pandemic.[^cite_note-62]

Tourism in the county was expected to increase after the restrictions imposed during the pandemic were relaxed. One reason for the expected increase is the airing of *[All Creatures Great and Small](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Creatures_Great_and_Small_\(2020_TV_series\))*, a TV series about the vet [James Herriot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Herriot), based on a successful series of books; it was largely filmed within the Yorkshire Dales National Park.[^cite_note-63] The show aired in the UK in September 2020 and in the US in early 2021. One source stated that visits to Yorkshire websites had increased significantly by late September 2020.[^cite_note-64]



Image taken at 17\:29 on 7 April 2020 at Leeming in North Yorkshire, overlooking the A1(M)

The lockdowns affected traffic volumes across the regions roads, which was also mirrored in road accidents, which totalled 948 in 2020, a drop of 7% on the previous year which had 1,021 accidents.[^cite_note-65] Additionally, miles travelled by all types of vehicle dropped, with cars having the largest drop, [LCV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_commercial_vehicle) and [HGV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_goods_vehicle) less so, but pedal bicycle usage slightly increased.[^cite_note-66]

Post-pandemic health effects included children worrying more about life and whether a family member would die. A public health report detailed how 76% of children worried daily in 2014, and by 2018, this had dropped to 61%. By 2024, this had risen again to 86%.[^cite_note-67]

It was opened in 1885, closed in 1965 then repurposed and re-opened in 2000.[^cite_note-70]

### Rail

See also: [Station usage in North Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_usage_in_North_Yorkshire)









[York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_railway_station), [Harrogate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrogate_railway_station), [Middlesbrough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_railway_station)\
and [Scarborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough_railway_station) railway stations

The [East Coast Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_Main_Line) (ECML) bisects the county stopping at [Northallerton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northallerton_railway_station),[Thirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirsk_railway_station) and [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_railway_station). Passenger service companies in the area are [London North Eastern Railway](57431689.md), [Northern Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Rail), [TransPennine Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransPennine_Express) and [Grand Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_\(train_operating_company\)).

LNER and Grand Central operate services to the capital on the ECML, Leeds Branch Line and the [Northallerton–Eaglescliffe Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northallerton%E2%80%93Eaglescliffe_Line). LNER stop at York, Northallerton and on to County Durham or spur over to the [Tees Valley Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tees_Valley_Line) for [Thornaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thornaby_railway_station) and [Middlesbrough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesbrough_railway_station). The operator also branch before the county for Leeds and run to [Harrogate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrogate_railway_station) and [Skipton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skipton_railway_station). Grand Central stop at York, [Thirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirsk_railway_station) Northallerton and Eaglescliffe then over to the [Durham Coast Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham_Coast_Line) in County Durham.

[Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Trains) operates the remaining lines in the county, including commuter services on the [Harrogate Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrogate_Line), [Airedale Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airedale_Line) and York & Selby Lines, of which the former two are covered by the [Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Yorkshire_Metro) ticketing area. Remaining branch lines operated by Northern include the [Yorkshire Coast Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Coast_Line) from Scarborough to Hull, [York–Scarborough line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York%E2%80%93Scarborough_line) via [Malton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malton_railway_station), the [Hull to York Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_to_York_Line) via [Selby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selby_railway_station), the Tees Valley Line from [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darlington_railway_station) to [Saltburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltburn_railway_station) via Middlesbrough and the [Esk Valley Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esk_Valley_Line) from Middlesbrough to [Whitby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitby_railway_station). Last but certainly not least, the [Settle-Carlisle Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settle-Carlisle_Line) runs through the west of the county, with services again operated by Northern.



Current and former railway routes in eastern North Yorkshire

The county suffered badly under the [Beeching cuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beeching_cuts) of the 1960s. Places such as [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond_railway_station_\(North_Yorkshire\)), [Ripon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripon_railway_station), [Tadcaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadcaster_railway_station), [Helmsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmsley_railway_station), [Pickering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickering_railway_station) and the [Wensleydale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wensleydale) communities lost their passenger services. Notable lines closed were the [Scarborough and Whitby Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough_and_Whitby_Railway), [Malton and Driffield Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malton_and_Driffield_Railway) and the secondary main line between Northallerton and Harrogate via Ripon.

Heritage railways within North Yorkshire include: the [North Yorkshire Moors Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Yorkshire_Moors_Railway), between [Pickering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickering_railway_station) and [Grosmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grosmont_railway_station), which opened in 1973; the [Derwent Valley Light Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derwent_Valley_Light_Railway) near York; and the [Embsay and Bolton Abbey Steam Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embsay_and_Bolton_Abbey_Steam_Railway). The [Wensleydale Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wensleydale_Railway), which started operating in 2003, runs services between [Leeming Bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeming_Bar_railway_station) and [Redmire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redmire_railway_station) along a former freight-only line. The medium-term aim is to operate into Northallerton station on the ECML, once an agreement can be reached with [Network Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_Rail). In the longer term, the aim is to reinstate the full line west via [Hawes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawes_railway_station) to [Garsdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garsdale_railway_station) on the Settle-Carlisle line.

[York railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_railway_station) is the largest station in the county, with 11 platforms and is a major tourist attraction in its own right. The station is immediately adjacent to the [National Railway Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Railway_Museum).

The county receives terrestrial television from four main transmission towers. [Bilsdale Mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilsdale_TV_Mast) transmits in the county's north from near Helmsley in the county; providing [BBC North East and Cumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_North_East_and_Cumbria), [ITV Tyne Tees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Tyne_Tees) and [BBC Radio Tees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Tees).[^cite_note-73]

[Emley Moor Mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emley_Moor_transmitting_station) transmits in the county's south, between Selby and Northallerton, from West Yorkshire and [Oliver's Mount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver's_Mount) Mast transmits Scarborough and Filey providing [BBC Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Yorkshire), [ITV Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Yorkshire) and [BBC Radio York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_York).

Settle and the county's far west is served by [BBC Radio Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Lancashire), [BBC North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_North_West) and [ITV Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Granada) from [Winter Hill Mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Hill_TV_Mast), Lancashire.

## Sport

### Cricket

[Yorkshire County Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_County_Cricket_Club) play a number of fixtures at [North Marine Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Marine_Road), [Scarborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarborough,_North_Yorkshire) and some 2nd XI games in [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_North_Yorkshire). The ball game [Rock-It-Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock-It-Ball) was developed in the county.

### Racing



[York Racecourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_Racecourse)

North Yorkshire has multiple [racecourses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racecourses), at: [Catterick Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catterick_Bridge_Racecourse), [Redcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redcar_Racecourse), [Ripon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripon_Racecourse), [Thirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirsk_Racecourse) and [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_Racecourse). It also has one [motor racing](1022.md) circuit, [Croft Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croft_Circuit); the circuit holds meetings of the [British Touring Car Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Touring_Car_Championship), [British Superbike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Superbike) and [Pickup Truck Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickup_Truck_Racing) race series and one Motorcycle Racing Circuit at Oliver's Mount, Scarborough.
