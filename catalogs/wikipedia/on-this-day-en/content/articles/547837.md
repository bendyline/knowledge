**Avram David** "**Avi**" **Lewis** (born May 1967) is a Canadian politician and filmmaker who has served as [leader of the New Democratic Party](79890122.md) (NDP) since 2026.&#x20;

Raised in [a political family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_family_\(Canada\)), Lewis began his career in broadcasting, hosting several programs for [Citytv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citytv), [CBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_News) and [Al Jazeera English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English) including *[The NewMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_NewMusic),* *[CounterSpin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CounterSpin_\(TV_series\)), On the Map with Avi Lewis*, *The Big Picture with Avi Lewis*, and [*Fault Lines*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_Lines_\(TV_series\)). With his wife [Naomi Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Klein), Lewis directed the documentaries *[The Take](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Take_\(2004_film\))* and *[This Changes Everything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Changes_Everything_\(2015_film\)).* Lewis was also an associate professor at the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) and a lecturer at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University).&#x20;

Lewis first became involved in politics with the [Leap Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leap_Manifesto), published along with [Naomi Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Klein) and several other activists in 2015. He later ran as an NDP candidate in the [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_federal_election) and [2025 federal elections](68483222.md), placing third each time. He was [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_NDP_leadership_election) the party's leader in March 2026 on the first ballot, with 56 percent of the vote.

## Early life and education

Avram David Lewis was born in May 1967[^cite_note-2] in [Toronto, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto,_Ontario), where he was raised. Lewis is the great-grandson of Mojżesz Łoś (Moishe Losz), better known in Canada as [Morris Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Lewis), an outspoken [Polish-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-Jewish) member of the [Jewish Bund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Jewish_Labor_Bund), who left Europe amid shifting borders and was threatened by the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsheviks) for his political activity. Moishe arrived in [Montreal](7954681.md) in 1921, with his wife Rose and three children. Avi's grandfather [David Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lewis_\(Canadian_politician\)) was the leader of the NDP from 1971 to 1975, and his father [Stephen Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Lewis) was the leader of the [Ontario NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_New_Democratic_Party) from 1970 to 1978 and served as the [Canadian ambassador to the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Representative_of_Canada_to_the_United_Nations) from 1984 to 1988. Stephen died on March 31, 2026, two days following Avi's election as federal NDP leader.[^cite_note-3] His mother is journalist and author [Michele Landsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michele_Landsberg). Lewis has two siblings: casting director [Jenny Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_Lewis_\(casting_director\)) and human rights activist [Ilana Landsberg-Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilana_Landsberg-Lewis).[^cite_note-4]

Lewis attended [Jarvis Collegiate Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarvis_Collegiate_Institute) and [Upper Canada College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Canada_College), graduating with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) from [University College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College,_Toronto) at the [University of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Toronto) in 1988.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

## Career

### Early broadcasting career

Between 1996 and 1998, Lewis was the host of *[The NewMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_NewMusic)*, a music magazine show on [MuchMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MuchMusic) and [Citytv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citytv). He also served as MuchMusic's political specialist, doing extensive special events coverage for the channel, covering the [1993 Canadian federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Canadian_federal_election) and the [1995 Quebec referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Quebec_referendum).[^cite_note-7] Lewis won a [Gemini Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_Award) for Best Event Coverage. He also worked for [Bell Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Media)'s [CP24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP24).[^cite_note-8]

### Canadian Broadcasting Corporation

From 1998 to 2001, he hosted [CBC Newsworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_Newsworld)'s current affairs discussion show *[CounterSpin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CounterSpin_\(television_program\))*, where he presided over 500 debates. He was later the host of *Counterspin* Sunday. Lewis began hosting *The Big Picture with Avi Lewis* in the autumn of 2006 and *On the Map* in 2007. In the latter program, Lewis conducted interviews with [Ayaan Hirsi Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayaan_Hirsi_Ali) and [John Bolton](997107.md), among others.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] He was a panellist on *[Canada Reads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Reads)*, which aired from March 2 to 6, 2009, where he presented, and successfully defended, the winning book, [Lawrence Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Hill)'s *[The Book of Negroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Book_of_Negroes_\(novel\))*.[^cite_note-11]

In June 2007, CBC Newsworld debuted *On the Map with Avi Lewis*, a daily (Monday to Thursday) half-hour of international news commentary. *On the Map*'s half-hour time slot was replaced with a half-hour summary of the daily hour-long show *[Politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_News:_Politics)* by [Don Newman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Newman_\(broadcaster\)).[^cite_note-12] During the same time period on [CBC Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_Television), *[The Hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hour_\(Canadian_TV_series\))*, hosted by [George Stroumboulopoulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Stroumboulopoulos) aired.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

### Al Jazeera

*Inside USA* was first telecast on [Al Jazeera English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English) on February 8, 2008, with the episode "Politics of Race". Al Jazeera describes *Inside USA* as "an in-depth look at the real issues at stake in the [US presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_US_presidential_election)."[^cite_note-16] "Politics Of Race", released on February 22, 2008, focused on the situation in New Orleans and the disenfranchisement of Black voters.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] "Native Americans", released on March 1, 2008, focused on [Lakota Sioux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakota_Sioux) [separatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_active_separatist_movements_in_North_America) and the social and economic issues surrounding the Lakota Sioux, [Navajo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navajo_people) and the [Shoshone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoshone) peoples.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] The last few minutes were devoted to the role online videos play in US elections, including a clip from "Yes We Can".

Following the presidential election, Lewis became a co-host of *[Fault Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_Lines_\(TV_program\))* in 2009, a program known for investigative storytelling examining the United States and its role in the world.[^cite_note-21]

### Documentary films



Lewis in 2016

In 2004, Lewis and his wife [Naomi Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Klein) collaborated on *[The Take](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Take_\(2004_film\))*, a documentary that detailed the "[recovered factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recovered_factory)" movement in [Argentina](18951905.md). *The Take*, winner of the International Jury Prize at the [American Film Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Film_Institute) festival, was nominated for four Gemini Awards. Collaboration between Lewis, Klein, and Brendan Martin led to the creation of The Working World/La Bas foundation,[^cite_note-Magnani09p148-22] a [non-profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-profit) [microcredit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microcredit) aimed at [cooperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperatives) active in the United States and Argentina.[^cite_note-Magnani09p148-22]

Lewis directed the 2015 feature-length documentary *[This Changes Everything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Changes_Everything_\(2015_film\))*,[^cite_note-23] which finished second in the audience voting, for the documentary category, at the [2015 Toronto International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Toronto_International_Film_Festival).[^cite_note-24]

With his organization, The Leap, Lewis co-wrote a short film titled *A Message from the Future* with [Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria_Ocasio-Cortez) in 2019. It was nominated for Outstanding News Analysis: Editorial and Opinion at the [41st News and Documentary Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/41st_News_and_Documentary_Emmy_Awards). A sequel, titled *Message from the Future II: The Years of Repair* was also co-written by Lewis and was released in 2020 by *[The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept)*.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

### Academics and journalism

He was a journalism and media studies lecturer at [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University) in [New Jersey](21648.md) and an associate professor in the geography department of the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia), where he taught courses on social and political change, communication and documentary filmmaking.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

## Political beginnings

An active member of the NDP, he was speculated as a potential candidate in the [2017 leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_New_Democratic_Party_leadership_election), but declined to run.[^cite_note-31] Lewis supports shifting the NDP further to the left,[^cite_note-32] endorsing and campaigning for several progressive candidates in internal NDP elections, such as [Anjali Appadurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anjali_Appadurai) in the [2022 BC NDP leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_British_Columbia_New_Democratic_Party_leadership_election).[^cite_note-33]

In May 2021, Lewis was nominated as the NDP's candidate for [West Vancouver—Sunshine Coast—Sea to Sky Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Vancouver%E2%80%94Sunshine_Coast%E2%80%94Sea_to_Sky_Country), [British Columbia](3392.md) for the [2021 Canadian federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_federal_election),[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] which was subsequently called for September 20, 2021.[^cite_note-36] Lewis' run for federal parliament was endorsed by environmentalist [David Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Suzuki),[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-stefanovich-38] and actress [Jane Fonda](63520.md).[^cite_note-stefanovich-38][^cite_note-39] He received 25.63% of the vote, the NDP's best result in the riding, but still came in third behind incumbent [Liberal](149536.md) [Patrick Weiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Weiler) and [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_of_Canada) [John Weston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Weston_\(Canadian_politician\)).[^cite_note-40]

In September 2024, Lewis was acclaimed as the NDP candidate in [Vancouver Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Centre) for the [2025 Canadian federal election](68483222.md). He placed third, behind incumbent Liberal [Hedy Fry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedy_Fry) and Conservative Elaine Allan, as the NDP lost the majority of its seats in the House of Commons and official party status.[^cite_note-41]

### Leap Manifesto

In September 2015, Lewis, Naomi Klein, and others launched the [Leap Manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leap_Manifesto), which proposed broad changes to Canadian society and economics in order to respond to [climate change](5042951.md) through a policy framework that also addressed issues of wealth and income inequality, racism, and colonialism in hopes of influencing the policy debate during the [2015 Canadian federal election campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Canadian_federal_election).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] The oil and gas provisions within the manifesto were criticized by [Rachel Notley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Notley)'s [Alberta NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_NDP) government. In 2016, Lewis spearheaded a motion at the NDP's federal convention in [Edmonton, Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton,_Alberta), which would have had the party endorse the manifesto. In the end, the motion was amended to refer the manifesto to constituency associations for debate. Lewis and other manifesto signatories launched an organization to promote the manifesto's vision in 2017, called The Leap, which operated until 2021 and was a key convenor of Canada's Pact for a [Green New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_New_Deal) in 2019.[^cite_note-44]

## Leader of the New Democratic Party (2026–present)

### 2026 leadership campaign

Lewis was a candidate in the [2026 NDP leadership election](50552524.md). He launched his campaign on September 19, 2025, being the first candidate approved to run. He held his campaign launch in [Toronto](64646.md)[^cite_note-45] and used the slogan "for the many, not the money" throughout the campaign.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] His policies include establishing a Canadian [Green New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_New_Deal), supporting [Palestinian](23267.md) rights, creating public options for [groceries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_grocery_store), telecommunications, [postal banking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_savings_system), and pharmaceuticals[^cite_note-48], building a million [public housing](177912.md) units[^cite_note-49], implementing a moratorium on [AI data centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_data_centre) construction[^cite_note-50], a [wealth tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_tax) on the top 1%[^cite_note-51], and expanding healthcare access by incorporating dental, vision, and mental health services into the public system.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

By the end of December 2025, his campaign claimed to have raised nearly \$783,000.[^cite_note-54] By the end of January 2026, it was reported that his campaign had raised over \$1,000,000.[^cite_note-55] During the campaign period, Lewis was endorsed by several current and former MPs including [Libby Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libby_Davies), [Leah Gazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leah_Gazan), [Niki Ashton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niki_Ashton) and [Svend Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svend_Robinson), and provincial politicians including [Peter Tabuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Tabuns), [Amir Khadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Khadir), [Joel Harden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Harden), [Cheri DiNovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheri_DiNovo) and [Flor Marcelino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flor_Marcelino).[^cite_note-56] Before her floor-crossing, [Nunavut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunavut_\(electoral_district\)) MP [Lori Idlout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lori_Idlout) had appeared at a Lewis campaign event in Ottawa.[^cite_note-57] At the end of the campaign, Lewis had raised \$1.23 million from 10,410 donors.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

### Tenure

On March 29, 2026, at the NDP convention in [Winnipeg](100730.md), he was elected party leader on the first ballot with 39,734 votes (56.02%).[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] Lewis delivered his victory speech alongside caucus members, leadership rivals, volunteers, and [Manitoba Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_Manitoba) [Wab Kinew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wab_Kinew).[^cite_note-62] Afterwards, [Alberta NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_New_Democratic_Party) leader [Naheed Nenshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naheed_Nenshi) distanced himself and his party from the federal NDP, criticising its oil and gas policy under Lewis.[^cite_note-63] In addition, [Saskatchewan NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan_New_Democratic_Party) leader [Carla Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_Beck) released an open letter, refusing to meet with Lewis until he publicly reversed his "ideological and unrealistic" opposition to new fossil fuel developments.[^cite_note-64] Other provincial NDP leaders including [BC NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_NDP) leader [David Eby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Eby) and [Ontario NDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_New_Democratic_Party) leader [Marit Stiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marit_Stiles), congratulated Lewis on his victory.[^cite_note-65] He is the first NDP leader to have never previously held elected office.[^cite_note-66]

On April 10, 2026, Lewis shuffled the [New Democratic Party's shadow cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Democratic_Party_Shadow_Cabinet_of_the_45th_Parliament_of_Canada), which included naming [Don Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Davies) as the party's [parliamentary leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_leader_\(Canada\)), and appointing [Heather McPherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heather_McPherson_\(politician\)) as the party's [House leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_leader). In late April, the party's deputy leader and lone Quebec MP, [Alexandre Boulerice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Boulerice), announced his intention to join provincial politics and resign from the House of Commons to run provincially for the [Quebec sovereigntist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_sovereignty_movement) party [Québec solidaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qu%C3%A9bec_solidaire).[^cite_note-67] Lewis has declined to seek immediate election to the [House of Commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Commons_of_Canada) after becoming NDP leader, refusing to become a party candidate in multiple scheduled by-elections in 2026.[^cite_note-68]

## Controversies

### Israel, Gaza and antisemitism

Lewis describes himself as an [anti-Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionism) and is a member of [Independent Jewish Voices Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Jewish_Voices_Canada).[^cite_note-cjn-leastbad-69] His statements about Israel and antisemitism have repeatedly drawn criticism from Jewish community organizations, politicians and commentators.

Lewis has consistently described Israel's response to the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md) as a genocide. In his March 2026 acceptance speech as NDP leader, he said: "When Israel commits a genocide in Gaza, we call it by its name, and we do everything in our power to bring it to an end."[^cite_note-jta-70] Critics in Canada have disputed Lewis's use of the term. In the *[National Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Post)*, columnist Jesse Kline wrote that genocide under international law depends on the intent of the perpetrators. He argued that Lewis and his co-authors started from the premise that Israel was committing genocide, and so treated anyone who supported Israel's right to self-defence as complicit in it.[^cite_note-kline-71]

During the 2026 leadership campaign, Lewis said he sought to "unlearn and unpack the Zionist myths that most Canadian Jews were brought up with",[^cite_note-jta-70] and in a debate described [Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism) as "linked inextricably to the ethnic cleansing of Palestine".[^cite_note-link-72] On the eve of the leadership convention, the [Centre for Israel and Jewish Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Israel_and_Jewish_Affairs) (CIJA) and about 30 rabbis published an open letter. It said that the party's response to antisemitism had been "inconsistent, hesitant, or clouded by rhetoric", and it criticized the "repeated elevation of fringe or non-representative Jewish voices".[^cite_note-jta-70][^cite_note-cjn-elect-73] After Lewis's election, CIJA said it received the result "with a deep sense of sadness" and that "Jewish identity is not a shield against accountability". It added that in tying Zionism to ethnic cleansing, Lewis was "telling Jewish Canadians that a core part of their identity is illegitimate".[^cite_note-jta-70] Former British Columbia NDP cabinet minister [Selina Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selina_Robinson) said: "I suspect Avi will continue to demonize Jews, Zionists and the only Jewish state."[^cite_note-cjn-elect-73]

#### Views on antisemitism in Canada

In October 2025, Lewis and other members of the Jewish Faculty Network co-authored a report accusing CIJA of anti-Palestinian racism and "genocide denial". Among its claims was that CIJA promoted a "false narrative" linking the movement for Palestinian rights to the rise of antisemitism in Canada.[^cite_note-jfn-74][^cite_note-cjn-elect-73] Kline called the report an attempt to silence Zionist organizations and wrote that it "should not be mistaken for a scholarly work".[^cite_note-kline-71] In a *National Post* opinion piece, sociologist [Robert Brym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Brym?action=edit\&redlink=1) accused Lewis of "gaslighting Canadians about antisemitism". Brym argued that Lewis had cited "nearly decade-old data" to play down anti-Jewish hate crime. By Brym's calculation from government statistics, the rate of such crime in 2024 was nearly five times its 2014 level, and Jews were targeted at far higher rates than any other group.[^cite_note-brym-75] Lewis has acknowledged that antisemitism is rising in Canada.[^cite_note-hub-76]

#### Yom Kippur statement

On September 20, 2026, the eve of [Yom Kippur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur), Lewis posted on [X](9988187.md) that he was "reflecting on the fact that we failed to do our part to stop an ongoing genocide committed in our name".[^cite_note-cbc-yk-77] Later that evening, during [Kol Nidre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kol_Nidre) services, a gunman opened fire outside the Sons of Jacob Synagogue in [Belleville, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belleville,_Ontario), seriously wounding a police officer. Police later described the attack as hate-motivated and directed at the Jewish community.[^cite_note-global-78][^cite_note-jpost-79]

Liberal MPs [Anthony Housefather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Housefather) and [Ben Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Carr_\(politician\)) said the post wrongly held Jews collectively responsible for Israel's actions. Housefather called it "despicable". Carr said it was "the type of narrative that is actually contributing to the type of threats that we're seeing levied against the Jewish community".[^cite_note-cbc-yk-77] The federal government's definition of antisemitism cautions against holding Jews collectively accountable for Israel's actions.[^cite_note-cp-80] CIJA warned that "when Jewish people are collectively portrayed as complicit in atrocities, that rhetoric can become a justification for targeting Jews here at home".[^cite_note-jpost-79] In *[The Globe and Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Globe_and_Mail)*, columnist [Robyn Urback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robyn_Urback) argued that the post placed "a target on the back of any and every Jew living in Canada" and should be disqualifying for the leader of a major party.[^cite_note-urback-81] The paper's editorial board wrote that Lewis had issued a post "imputing collective guilt to Jewish Canadians for Israeli actions in Gaza".[^cite_note-globe-ed-82]

Lewis dismissed the criticism.[^cite_note-cbc-yk-77]

## Personal life

Lewis is married to journalist and author [Naomi Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Klein). The couple have one son, Toma.[^cite_note-Naomi_Klein_Facebook_Page-83][^cite_note-84]

He was featured on the November 20, 2001, *Life & Times* episode of "The Lewis Family".[^cite_note-85] Lewis' genealogical search was featured on the January 31, 2008, episode of the CBC's *[Who Do You Think You Are?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Do_You_Think_You_Are%3F_\(Canadian_TV_series\))*[^cite_note-86]
