**Naypyidaw** ([/ˈneɪpjɪdɔː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*NAYP-yid-aw*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)),[^cite_note-6] officially [romanised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Burmese) as **Nay Pyi Taw** (**NPT**),[^cite_note-7] is the [capital](181337.md) and third-largest city of [Myanmar](19457.md). The city is located at the centre of the [Naypyidaw Union Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_Union_Territory).[^cite_note-eleven-1] It is unusual among Myanmar's cities in that it is an entirely [planned city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_community) and not part of any [state or region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Myanmar). The city, previously known only as [Pyinmana District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyinmana_District), officially replaced [Yangon](57404.md) as the administrative capital of Myanmar on 6 November 2005; its official name was revealed to the public on [Armed Forces Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Day_\(Myanmar\)), 27 March 2006.[^cite_note-8]

As the seat of the government of Myanmar, Naypyidaw is the site of the [Assembly of the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_the_Union), the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Myanmar), the [Presidential Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace,_Naypyidaw), the official residences of the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Myanmar) and the headquarters of government ministries and [military](20394.md). Naypyidaw is notable for its unusual combination of large size and very low population density.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] The city hosted the 24th and 25th [ASEAN Summit](3350351.md), the 3rd [BIMSTEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Bengal_Initiative_for_Multi-Sectoral_Technical_and_Economic_Cooperation) Summit, the [Ninth East Asia Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_East_Asia_Summit), the [2013 Southeast Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Southeast_Asian_Games) and the [2014 AFC U-19 Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_AFC_U-19_Championship).

## Etymology

*Naypyidaw* is [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_language) for "abode of the king", and is generally translated as "royal capital",[^cite_note-pcgn-11] "seat of the king",[^cite_note-\:6-12] or "abode of kings".[^cite_note-pedrosa-3] Traditionally, it was used as a suffix to the names of royal capitals, such as [Mandalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandalay), which was called Yadanabon Naypyidaw (ရတနာပုံနေပြည်တော်).

## History

Naypyidaw was founded on a [greenfield site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenfield_site) near [Pyinmana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyinmana), about 320 kilometres (200 mi) north of the old capital, [Yangon](57404.md). Construction started in 2002 and was completed by 2012.[^cite_note-mcc-13] Military construction projects were overseen by the Quartermaster General [Tin Aung Myint Oo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin_Aung_Myint_Oo), while civilian construction projects were overseen by [Thein Nyunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thein_Nyunt), an army colonel.[^cite_note-\:1-14] Construction of the city was carried out by three main groups, including the military's engineering corps, contractors with familial ties to high-ranking military officials, and the military's conglomerate, [Myanma Economic Holdings Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanma_Economic_Holdings_Limited).[^cite_note-\:1-14] Construction was plagued with corruption and nepotism.[^cite_note-\:1-14] It is estimated that the planned city development reached US\$4 billion.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-\:6-12]

On 27 March 2006, more than 12,000 troops marched in the new capital in its first public event: a massive military parade to mark [Armed Forces Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Day_\(Myanmar\)), the anniversary of Burma's 1945 revolution against the [Japanese occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_Burma). Filming was restricted to the concrete parade ground, which contains enormous sculptures of Kings [Anawrahta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anawrahta), [Bayinnaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayinnaung), and [Alaungpaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaungpaya). The city was officially named "Naypyidaw"[^cite_note-geomap-16] during these ceremonies,[^cite_note-bbc4848408-17] and the official, albeit mostly administrative, capital of the country was relocated from Yangon to Naypyidaw.

### Rationale behind moving the capital

There are several assumptions why the capital was moved. Naypyidaw is more centrally located than the old capital, [Yangon](57404.md). It is also a [transportation hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_hub) located adjacent to the [Shan](493376.md), [Kayah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayah_State), and [Kayin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayin_State) states. It was felt by governmental and military leaders that a stronger military and governmental presence nearby might provide stability to those chronically turbulent regions.[^cite_note-McGeown2005-18] The military's purpose appears to have been to build an impregnable citadel, able to withstand foreign invasion or popular uprisings.[^cite_note-19] The official explanation for moving the capital was that Yangon had become too congested and crowded with little room for future expansion of government offices.[^cite_note-pedrosa-3] Historian [Michael Aung-Thwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Aung-Thwin) posits that the relocation to central Myanmar represented a historic return to the [Dry Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_Zone_\(Myanmar\)), where previous Burmese royal capitals had traditionally been established over a millennium.[^cite_note-\:5-20] The nearby town of [Pyinmana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyinmana) is a long-standing settlement and had been a pre-colonial governership that dates to the [Pagan kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagan_kingdom) era.[^cite_note-\:5-20]

### 2025 earthquake

The city suffered extensive damage in the [2025 Myanmar earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Myanmar_earthquake) on 28 March, with 80% of government buildings in the city damaged.[^cite_note-\:2-21] Naypyidaw had the second highest death toll as a result of the quake.[^cite_note-\:3-22] As of April 2025, 75 temporary relief camps had been set up to shelter 2,900 impacted families, while another 23,800 families had been relocated.[^cite_note-\:2-21] The residential areas of Zabuthiri Township and Bawgatheikdi wards, where several apartment buildings collapsed, were especially impacted by the earthquake.[^cite_note-\:4-23] The earthquake prompted Myanmar's military leader, [Min Aung Hlaing](38278640.md), to order a redrawing of the city's layout.[^cite_note-24] Following the earthquake, all government ministry websites and state-run MRTV and Myawaddy online broadcasts went offline.[^cite_note-\:4-23]

Among the significant structures that were damaged included the [Presidential Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace,_Naypyidaw), the [Pyidaungsu Hluttaw](29739324.md), the national legislature, and the mansion residences of high-ranking military officials, including [Than Shwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Than_Shwe), [Maung Aye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maung_Aye), [Thein Sein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thein_Sein), and [Tin Aye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin_Aye).[^cite_note-\:3-22] In mid-April, a Ministry of Construction assessment found that one-third of government staff buildings in nine of Naypyidaw's wards were rendered uninhabitable or destroyed.[^cite_note-25] Many government offices, including the [Central Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Myanmar), and the [ministries of foreign affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Myanmar\)), [hotels and tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Hotels_and_Tourism_\(Myanmar\)), [immigration and population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Immigration_and_Population), and [commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Commerce_\(Myanmar\)), planned to relocate to Yangon, due to damage to offices and staff housing complexes.[^cite_note-\:2-21]

Officials representing the [United Wa State Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Wa_State_Army) and the [National Democratic Alliance Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Alliance_Army) funded the reconstruction of military council housing in Naypyidaw.[^cite_note-26]

## Geography

Naypyidaw is located between the [Bago Yoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegu_Range) and [Shan Yoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shan_Hills) mountain ranges. The city covers an area of 7,054 square kilometres (2,724 sq mi) and has a population of 924,608, according to official figures.[^cite_note-pdo-27]

Chaungmagyi Dam is located a few kilometres to the north of Naypyidaw, while [Ngalaik Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngalaik_Dam) is a few kilometres to the south. The Yezin Dam is farther away in the north-east.

### Climate

Naypyidaw has a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Aw*), with a wet season from May to October. The dry season from November to April begins with very warm afternoons and comfortable mornings, but in the build-up to the wet season temperatures become sweltering. In the wet season temperatures are merely hot but much higher humidity makes for conditions equally unpleasant, although rainfall is not nearly so extreme as in coastal Myanmar cities like [Mawlamyine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawlamyine) or [Sittwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sittwe).

## Cityscape

Naypyidaw is organised into a number of zones.



Naypyidaw Aerial Image

### Residential zones



Apartment building in Naypyidaw

The residential areas are carefully organised, and apartments are allotted according to rank and marital status.[^cite_note-bp-28] The city has 1,200 four-story apartment blocks.[^cite_note-nytimes2007-29] The roofs of apartment buildings are colour-coded by the jobs of their residents: [Ministry of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Health_\(Myanmar\)) employees live in buildings with blue roofs and Ministry of Agriculture employees live in those with green roofs.[^cite_note-bp-28] High-ranking government officials live in mansions, of which there are about 50. However, in 2019, quite a few of the ministerial mansions were reported to be unused and left in a neglected state in overgrown compounds.[^cite_note-30]

### Ministry zone



[Presidential Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace,_Naypyidaw), during the meeting between [President of Myanmar](11195083.md) U. [Htin Kyaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Htin_Kyaw) and [Prime Minister of India](24452.md) [Narendra Modi](444222.md).



[Union Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_the_Union)

The city's Ministry zone contains the headquarters of Myanmar's government ministries. All the ministry buildings are identical in appearance.[^cite_note-31] A [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_the_Union) complex consisting of 31 buildings[^cite_note-reuters-32] and a 100-room [presidential palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace,_Naypyidaw) are also located there.[^cite_note-bp-28] Presidential Palace, Parliament and the city hall building were built with [Stalinist architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinist_architecture), but with a Burmese-style roof.

### Military zone

High-ranking military officers and other key officials live eleven kilometres (6.8 mi) away from regular government employees in a complex said to consist of tunnels and bunkers; this area is restricted to the public.[^cite_note-bp-28] The city also hosts a military base, which is inaccessible to citizens or other personnel without written permission.[^cite_note-mcc-13]

### Diplomatic zone

&#x20;The government has set aside two hectares (4.9 acres) of land each for foreign embassies and headquarters of [United Nations](31769.md) missions. The Chinese embassy formally opened its interim liaison office in 2017. The liaison office is the first foreign office to be permitted to open in Naypyidaw.[^cite_note-33] Bangladesh and Malaysia have also signed agreements to open embassies in Naypyidaw. The government confirms that proposals have been put forward by 11 other countries to move their embassies to Naypyidaw, namely Russia, China, the United States, India, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, the Philippines, Indonesia, Thailand, Turkey and Kuwait.[^cite_note-34] In February 2018, State Counsellor Daw [Aung San Suu Kyi](2847.md) chaired a meeting at the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Naypyidaw where she urged foreign governments to move their embassies to the capital.



Wunna Theikdi Indoor Stadium

### Hotel zone

The Hotel zone has a handful of villa-style hotels on the hilly outskirts of the city. There are twelve hotels located in or near Naypyidaw. Eight of these are located within the Naypyidaw Hotel Zone, and two are located in Laeway ([Lewe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewe)) on the Yangon-Mandalay Road.[^cite_note-YP-35] Forty villas were constructed near the Myanmar Convention Centre in preparation for the [25th ASEAN summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_ASEAN_summit) conducted in Naypyidaw in November 2014. Construction of the villas began in 2010 by the government. However, funds were limited, so the project was later put out to tender for completion by private sector investors. A total of 348 hotels and 442 inns were constructed to house the athletes and spectators of the [2013 Southeast Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Southeast_Asian_Games), which was hosted in Naypyidaw.[^cite_note-36]

### Market areas



Myoma Market

Naypyidaw Myoma Market is the commercial centre of Naypyidaw. Other shopping areas include Thapye Chaung Market, Junction Centre Naypyidaw and Naypyidaw Wholesale. Junction Centre is the city's first privately operated shopping centre.[^cite_note-pdo-27] There are also local markets and a restaurant area.

### Recreation



Safari Park

Ngalaik Lake Gardens is a small water park situated along the [Ngalaik Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngalaik_Dam), near Kyweshin Village on Ngalaik Lake (about 11 kilometres (7 mi) from Naypyidaw). Opened in 2008, facilities at the Ngalaik Lake Gardens include water slides, natural resorts, lodging and a beach. The gardens are open to the public during [Thingyan](4087333.md) holidays.[^cite_note-myanmargeneva-37]

Also opened in 2008, the 81-hectare (200-acre) [National Herbal Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Herbal_Park) has exhibits of plants having medicinal applications from all of the major regions of Myanmar. There are thousands of plants at the park, representing hundreds of different species.[^cite_note-38] Behind the city hall, there is a park with a playground and [water fountain complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_Water_Fountain_Garden), which hosts a musical light show every night.[^cite_note-bp-28]

The [Naypyidaw Zoological Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_Zoological_Gardens) opened in 2008 with 420 species and now with 1500 animals. The main attraction of the zoo is the climate-controlled penguin house. The animals were shipped to it from the old one in Yangon.[^cite_note-nyt-39][^cite_note-40] The [Naypyidaw Safari Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_Safari_Park) officially opened on 12 February 2011.[^cite_note-bw-41][^cite_note-irrawaddy20741-42]

Naypyidaw also has two golf courses, Naypyidaw City Golf Course and Yaypyar Golf Course, and a gem museum.[^cite_note-YP-35]

### Landmark

Main article: [Uppatasanti Pagoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppatasanti_Pagoda)

Similar in size and shape to the [Shwedagon Pagoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shwedagon_Pagoda) in Yangon, [Uppatasanti Pagoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppatasanti_Pagoda) was completed in 2009.[^cite_note-mizzima-43] This new pagoda is named the Uppatasanti or "Peace Pagoda". The stake-driving ceremony for the pagoda was held on 12 November 2006.[^cite_note-mrtv3-44] The invitation card for the ceremony opened with a phrase "Rajahtani Naypyidaw (the royal capital where the king resides)".[^cite_note-Steinberg2009-45] The pagoda is just 30 centimetres (12 in) shorter than the Shwedagon Pagoda.[^cite_note-irrawaddy15256-46] Uppatasanti translates roughly to "Protection against Calamity". It is the name of a [sūtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%ABtra) prepared by a monk in the early 16th century. It is to be recited in time of crisis, especially in the face of foreign invasion.[^cite_note-WENJ2006-47]

## Administration



[Pyidaungsu Hluttaw](29739324.md) Complex (Parliament)



Lobby of Zeyathiri Beikman, the government guesthouse for high level delegations

The [Naypyidaw Union Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_Union_Territory) is administered at both the [State/Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Myanmar) level, and as a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality) at the local level under two different governing bodies. Naypyidaw is one of three cities in the country to have municipal governance, the others being Yangon and Mandalay.[^cite_note-Asia-48]

[Naypyidaw Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_Council) (NPTC) is the regional administrative body of the territory, and is under the direct supervision of the [President of Myanmar](11195083.md).[^cite_note-Council-49] The council is appointed by the President, and must have no less than 5 and no more than 10 members and include both civilians and [military](20394.md) personnel. The Chairperson becomes the [ex-officio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex-officio) Mayor, and the Head of the [General Administration Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Administration_Department) (GAD) of the territory becomes the Secretary of the council.[^cite_note-Council-49][^cite_note-Constitution2008-50] Matters under the [jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction) of the Council include: general planning, coordination of [internal security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_security), [nature conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_conservation), [emergency management](1455589.md), oversight of [civil service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_service) organisations within the territory, and any other duties assigned to it by the constitution or law.[^cite_note-Council-49]

[Naypyidaw Development Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_Development_Committee) (NPTDC) is the [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government) body of the territory. The committee is appointed by the President, and must have at least 5 members, but no more than 9.[^cite_note-Committee-51] The Chairperson of the Committee becomes the Mayor of Naypyidaw, the vice-chairman becomes the Vice-Mayor, and the Head of Office becomes the Joint Secretary of the committee.[^cite_note-Committee-51] Because both laws make the chair of the Council and Committee the mayor, the same person holds both offices. Matters under the jurisdiction of the Committee include: [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning), [land-use planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-use_planning), [planning permission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planning_permission), [traffic management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_management), various [public works](323158.md) ([sewage treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_treatment), [water supply](684928.md), [sanitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitation), roads, bridges, drainage, etc.), and other local competencies.[^cite_note-Committee-51] Unlike the NPTC which must request funding for its budget from the Union government, the committee is permitted to levy taxes to fund its work.[^cite_note-Committee-51]

The current mayor is Dr. Maung Maung Naing, appointed as the Chair of Naypyidaw Council on 2 February 2021.[^cite_note-52] [Dr. Myo Aung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myo_Aung) of the [National League for Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_for_Democracy) party served as Mayor from his appointment on 1 April 2016, until his arrest on 1 February 2021, during the [2021 Myanmar coup d'état](66567997.md).[^cite_note-Councilchair-53][^cite_note-54]

### Administrative divisions

Naypyidaw Union Territory consists of 2 [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Myanmar) subdivided into 8 [townships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townships_of_Myanmar). [Pyinmana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyinmana_Township), [Lewe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewe_Township), and [Tatkone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatkone_Township) townships were all formerly part of [Yamethin District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamethin_District) in [Mandalay Region](2357817.md). Ottarathiri, Dekkhinathiri, Popphathiri, Zabuthiri, and Zeyathiri are all new townships under construction. Like the townships in the rest of the country, they are governed by a development affairs organisation (DAO) which includes a Township Administrator appointed by the [General Administration Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Administration_Department), and an elected Township Development Affairs Committee (TDAC).[^cite_note-Asia-48]

There is sometimes some controversy about land use and changes in land ownership related to the urban spread of Naypyidaw. In late 2014, for example, there were suggestions in parliament that land grabs were occurring near the Dekkhinathiri Township and that existing laws needed to be amended to provide better protection for farmers.[^cite_note-55] There has also been criticism from some members of parliament about the size of the Naypyidaw Union Territory and the fact that money is being spent on urban infrastructure (such as lighting around lakes) although the facilities do not attract much use.[^cite_note-56]

## Transport

### Roads



An empty boulevard in Naypyidaw

The four-lane, 323.2-kilometre (200.8 mi) Yangon-Naypyidaw highway links Naypyidaw with Yangon directly and is part of the 563-kilometre-long (350 mi) [Yangon-Naypyidaw-Mandalay Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangon-Naypyidaw-Mandalay_Expressway). There is a 20-lane boulevard. Like most roads in the city, it is largely empty.[^cite_note-reuters-32][^cite_note-\:0-57] Naypyidaw has four-lane roads and multilevel, flower-covered roundabouts ([traffic circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_circle)).



Yangon-Mandalay Expressway (Near Ywathit, Naypyidaw.)

### Air



[Naypyidaw International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_International_Airport)

[Nay Pyi Taw International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nay_Pyi_Taw_International_Airport), also known as Ayelar Airport, opened in December 2011 and is 16 kilometres (9.9 mi) south-east of the city, between the towns of Ela and Lewe. It has been served by all domestic airlines—[Air Bagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Bagan), [Air Mandalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Mandalay), [Myanmar National Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_National_Airlines), [FMI Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FMI_Air) and [Yangon Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangon_Airways)—with regular flights to Yangon and other cities across the country.[^cite_note-MyanmarTimes20060612-58] According to the Transport Ministry, the airport has a 3.6-kilometre (2.2 mi) long runway, a control tower 69 metres (226 ft) high and handle up to 65,000 flights annually.[^cite_note-59] Airlines from Thailand and China used to serve international flights in the past, but the airport now caters only to local destinations.

### Buses and taxis

Public transport services are limited between neighbourhoods.[^cite_note-bp-28] Government ministries run shuttle buses in the morning and evening to their respective buildings.[^cite_note-Varadarajan-60]

The city has a central bus station[^cite_note-pedrosa-3] and one taxi company, which is operated by the military.[^cite_note-bp-28] [Motorbikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorbike) are banned from some roads in Naypyidaw city limits, including sections of Taw Win Yadana Road, as a result of hundreds of traffic accident-related deaths in 2009.[^cite_note-61]

### Railways

Further information: [Myanmar Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_Railways)



Naypyidaw Central Railway Station

[Naypyidaw Central railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_Central_railway_station) was opened at milepost No. (233/0), between Ywataw station and Kyihtaunggan station on the [Yangon–Mandalay Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangon%E2%80%93Mandalay_Railway) with a station area of 2,700 m × 1,200 m (9,000 ft × 4,000 ft) and a covered area of 334.5 hectares (826.5 acres). Construction began on 8 December 2006 and Naypyidaw railway station was opened on 5 July 2009, even though the overpass, locomotive shed, concrete road leading up to the station, parking lot, passenger lounge and platform were not completed.[^cite_note-myanmargeneva20091124-62]

Before the opening of Naypyidaw railway station, Naypyidaw was served by Pyinmana and Lewe stations, though only Pyinmana station is on the main rail line (which extends from Yangon-[Bago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bago,_Myanmar)-Naypyidaw-[Thazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thazi,_Meiktila,_Mandalay_Region)-Mandalay). Lewe station is on the way from Pyinmana to [Kyaukpadaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyaukpadaung). It takes nine hours by train to get from Yangon to Pyinmana; trains leave at 12\:00 and arrive at 21\:30 local time.[^cite_note-MyanmarTimes20060529-63]

In August 2011, Russian news media announced that a Russian-based firm would be constructing a 50-kilometre (31 mi) [metro line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit), which would be the country's first underground rail system, underneath Naypyidaw.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] However, the Rail Transportation Ministry then announced that the plan had been cancelled due to lack of demand and budgetary limits.[^cite_note-66]

## Healthcare



Naypyidaw General Hospital

The city is served by five public hospitals: 1000-bed [Naypyidaw General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_General_Hospital?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Naypyidaw Women Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_Women_Hospital?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Naypyidaw ENT Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_ENT_Hospital?action=edit\&redlink=1), 100-bed [Naypyidaw Traditional Medicine Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_Traditional_Medicine_Hospital?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Naypyidaw Orthopaedic Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naypyidaw_Orthopaedic_Hospital?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] There is also a 300-bed Obstetric, Gynaecological and Children's Hospital of Defence Services, which is one of the teaching hospitals of Myanmar Defence Services Medical Academy. The nearby towns of Lewe, Pyinmana, and Tatkone each have one hospital.[^cite_note-YP-35]

## Arts and entertainment

The [Myanmar Motion Picture Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_Motion_Picture_Academy_Awards) are held annually in Naypyidaw given to the highest achievers in [Burmese cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Myanmar).[^cite_note-irrawaddy10277-75] There is a movie theater in the Junction Centre Mall as well as in the Ocean Supercenter. ## Media and communications

The headquarters of the [Ministry of Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Information_\(Myanmar\)) is located in Naypyidaw. Since 2009, Naypyidaw has had mobile phone coverage.

### Print

The *[Myanmar Alin](20812096.md)* and the *[Kyaymon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyaymon)* in Burmese and the *[New Light of Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Light_of_Myanmar)* in English are available in Naypyidaw. Since 18 November 2011, the Ministry of Information has begun publishing a weekly journal called the *Naypyidaw* *Times*, to report on government policies.[^cite_note-76]

### Radio

There are a number of AM and FM stations broadcasting in Naypyidaw. The main available channels in Naypyidaw are [Myanmar Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_Radio) (operated by [MRTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_Radio_and_Television)), Cherry FM, Mandalay FM, FM Bagan, Padamyar FM, Pyinsawaddy FM, Shwe FM and City FM.

### Television

[MRTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_Radio_and_Television), [MRTV-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRTV-4) and [MWD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myawaddy_TV) are the main channels, broadcasting Burmese-language programmes in Naypyidaw. Privately owned enterprise [Sky Net](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Net) provides more than 70 pay-TV channels of local and international origin. MRTV broadcasts 19 free-to-air channels and MWD broadcasts 8 free-to-air channels. Other TV channels such as Channel 7, 5-plus and [Myanmar International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_International), the English-language channel that targets overseas audiences are also available.

## Costs and issues

One estimate puts the cost in the range of \$3–4 billion [USD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USD), although only part of this estimate is for cash spending because non-cash investments were also reportedly involved in supporting the construction effort.[^cite_note-\:0-57]

There is now continual travel by vehicles along the 300 km (190 mi) highway from Yangon to Naypyidaw, which is four lanes for most of the way. There are relatively few vehicles for much of the time on the road. Most trucks travelling north from Yangon to Naypyidaw and Mandalay are prohibited from using the highway. The [Asian Development Bank](2512.md) has estimated that allowing more trucks onto the road would save over \$100 million per year in reduced travelling time and other savings.[^cite_note-77] There are plans to turn the road into a six-lane highway for greater safety.[^cite_note-78]

The proportion of housing that meets expatriate needs is limited because the majority of existing apartments are for government employees.[^cite_note-79]

## In popular culture

In March 2014, Naypyidaw was one of the locations featured on the British motoring TV show *[Top Gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Gear_\(2002_TV_series\))* during a two-part special event in Myanmar.[^cite_note-\:6-12]

In 2018, Naypyidaw was featured on the TV show *[Dark Tourist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Tourist_\(TV_series\))*.
