**Azraq** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الأزرق, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'blue') is a small town in [Zarqa Governorate](3969644.md) in central-eastern [Jordan](7515964.md), 100 kilometres (62 mi) east of [Amman](45007.md). The population of Azraq was 9,021 in 2004.[^cite_note-1] The [Muwaffaq Salti Air Base](32592197.md) is located in Azraq.

## History

### Prehistory

[Archaeological](18951655.md) evidence indicates that Azraq has been occupied for hundreds of thousands of years,[^cite_note-2] with the oldest known remains dating to the [Lower Palaeolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Palaeolithic), around 500–300,000 years ago.[^cite_note-3] The spring-fed oasis provided a more or less constant source of water throughout this period, and probably acted as a [refugium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugium_\(population_biology\)) for humans and other animals at times when the surrounding area dried out.[^cite_note-Cordova_et_al._2013-4] The oasis itself changed as the climate fluctuated: at times a permanent lake, a [marsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh), or a seasonal [playa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_lake).[^cite_note-Cordova_et_al._2013-4]

Animals found in Lower Palaeolithic layers at the Shishan Marsh site include a large elephant (probably the extinct *[Palaeoloxodon recki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeoloxodon_recki)*), a smaller elephant (probably *[Elephas hysudricus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephas_hysudricus),* the ancestor of the living [Asian elephant](379035.md)), the extinct [narrow-nosed rhinoceros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-nosed_rhinoceros), [camels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel), [lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion), [wild horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_horse), an [ass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asinus) (either *[Equus heimonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onager)* or *[Equus hydruntinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equus_hydruntinus)*), [gazelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazelle), [aurochs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurochs), and [wild boar](52255.md).[^cite_note-\:6-5] Protein residue analysis of tools at the site suggests that the people there butchered ducks, camels, bovines (probably [aurochs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurochs)), [equines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equus_\(genus\)) and rhinoceros (probably the narrow-nosed rhinoceros).[^cite_note-6]

During the [Epipalaeolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epipaleolithic_\(Levant\)) period the oasis was also an important focus of settlement.

### Later history

Azraq has long been an important settlement in a remote and now-arid desert area of Jordan. The strategic value of the town and its castle ([Qasr Azraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qasr_Azraq)) is that it lies in the middle of the Azraq oasis, the only permanent source of fresh water in approximately 12,000 km<sup>2</sup> (4,600 sq mi) of desert. The town is also located on a major desert route that would have facilitated trade within the region.



Azraq street view

[Nabatean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabatean) period settlement activity has also been documented in the area. [Qasr Azraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qasr_Azraq) was built by the Romans in the 3rd century AD, and was heavily modified in the Middle Ages by the [Mamluks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Empire). In the [Umayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad) period a water reservoir was constructed in southern Azraq.

During the [Arab Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Revolt) in the early 20th century, Qasr Azraq was an important headquarters for [T. E. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._E._Lawrence).[^cite_note-TE-7][^cite_note-Neil-8]



The [Azraq Wetland Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azraq_Wetland_Reserve)

The [Azraq refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azraq_refugee_camp), sheltering [refugees of the Syrian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_of_the_Syrian_Civil_War), was opened in 2014 and is located 20 km (12 mi) west of Azraq.[^cite_note-ap-apr2014-9] The site had been previously used during the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) of 1990–91 as a transit camp for displaced Iraqis and Kuwaitis.[^cite_note-jt-apr2014-10]

## Demographics



The Azraq Castle

According to the Jordan National Census of 2004, the population of Azraq was 9,021, of whom 7,625 (84.5%) were Jordanian citizens. 4,988 (55.3%) were males, and 4,033 (44.7%) females. The next census was conducted in 2014.

## Wildlife reserve



The [Shaumari Wildlife Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaumari_Wildlife_Reserve) in Azraq.

Azraq is also the site of one of Jordan's seven [protected nature reserve areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_reserves_in_Jordan) (set up by the [Royal Society for the Conservation of Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Society_for_the_Conservation_of_Nature)): the Azraq Wetlands Reserve in Azraq al-Janoubi (South Azraq).

The separate and larger [Shaumari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaumari_\(Jordan\)) reserve is about 10 kilometres (6.2 mi) south of the town.
