**Thuraya** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الثريا, [Gulf Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_Arabic) pron.: [\[ɐθ.θʊˈrɑj.jɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic); from the Arabic name for the [Pleiades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleiades), *Thurayya*)[^cite_note-1] is a [United Arab Emirates](69328.md)-based regional [mobile-satellite service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile-satellite_service) (MSS) provider. The company operates two [geosynchronous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geosynchronous) satellites and provides telecommunications coverage in about 150 countries in [Europe](9239.md), the [Middle East](19323.md), North, Central and East [Africa](5334607.md) and [Asia](689.md).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] Thuraya's [L-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_band) network delivers voice and data services.

Thuraya is the mobile satellite services subsidiary of [Yahsat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Yah_Satellite_Communications), a global satellite operator based in the United Arab Emirates, fully owned by [Mubadala Investment Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubadala_Investment_Company). The geostationary nature of the service implies high round-trip times from satellite to Earth, leading to a noticeable lag being present during voice calls.

## Services

- Voice communications with satellite phones or fixed terminals

- [Short message service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_message_service)

- 60 kbit/s downlink and 15 kbit/s uplink [GMPRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMPRS) mobile data service on Thuraya satellite phones

- 444 kbit/s high-speed data transfer via a notebook-sized terminal (ThurayaIP)

- A number of other services, such as call waiting, missed calls, voicemail, etc.

- A one-way "high-power alert" capability that notifies users of an incoming call, when the signal path to the satellite is obstructed

## Technical details of the network

### Virtual country code

Thuraya's [country calling code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_country_calling_codes) is +882 16, which is part of the [ITU-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITU-T) [International Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Networks_\(country_code\)) numbering group. Thuraya is not part of the +881 [country calling code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_country_calling_codes) numbering group, as this is allocated by ITU-T for networks in the [Global Mobile Satellite System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Mobile_Satellite_System), of which Thuraya is not a part, being a regional rather than a global system.

### Air interface

Transceivers communicate directly with the satellites using an antenna of roughly the same length as the handset and have a maximum output power of 2 [watts](21347693.md). [QPSK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QPSK) modulation is used for the [air interface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_interface). Thuraya SIM cards work in regular [GSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSM) telephones, and ordinary GSM SIM cards can be used on the satellite network as long as the SIM provider has a roaming agreement with Thuraya. As with all geosynchronous voice services, a noticeable lag is present while making a call.

Due to the relatively high gain of the antennas contained within handsets, it is necessary to roughly aim the antenna at the satellite. As the handsets contain a GPS receiver, it is possible to program the ground position of the satellites as waypoints to assist with aiming. The service operates on L-band carriers assigned in blocks to areas of coverage referred to as "spotbeams", which are Thuraya's equivalent to cells or service areas. In L-band, 34 MHz of bandwidth from 1.525 GHz to 1.559 GHz is assigned for downlink (space-to-Earth) communication, while the uplink (Earth-to-space) operates between 1.6265 GHz and 1.6605 GHz. Uplink and downlink channels are 1087 paired carrier frequencies, on a raster of 31.25 kHz. A [time-division multiple access](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time-division_multiple_access) (TDMA) time-slot architecture is employed which allocates a carrier in time slots of a fixed length.

### Use of GPS

Every Thuraya phone and standalone transceiver unit is fitted with a [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_positioning_system) receiver and transmits its location to the Thuraya gateway periodically.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] The built-in GPS capability can be used for waypoint navigation.

### Satellites



Thuraya 2 and a [nearby geostationary satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA-202), photographed on 8 December 2010 from the Netherlands

Thuraya operates two [communications satellites](45207.md) built by [Boeing](18933266.md) or [Airbus Defence and Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_Defence_and_Space).

#### Thuraya 1

The first satellite, named Thuraya 1, had deficient solar panels and could not operate properly; this satellite was positioned above Korea for testing purposes. It was launched on 21 October 2000 by [Sea Launch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Launch) on a [Zenit 3SL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenit_3SL) rocket.[^cite_note-6] At launch it weighed 5250 kg.[^cite_note-7] The satellite was used for testing and backup until May 2007, when it was moved to [junk orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junk_orbit) and declared at its end of life.[^cite_note-8]

#### Thuraya 2

Thuraya 2 was launched by Sea Launch on 10 June 2003.[^cite_note-9] It is located in [geosynchronous orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geosynchronous_orbit) at 44° E longitude with 6.3° inclination.[^cite_note-10] The satellite can handle 13,750 simultaneous voice calls. This satellite currently serves most of Europe, the Middle East, Africa and parts of Asia. The craft had a weight of 3200 kg and an expected life of 12 years. The two solar-panel wings, each containing five panels, generate 11 kW electric power. The craft has two antenna systems: a round [C-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_band_\(IEEE\)) antenna, 1.27 meters in diameter and a 12 × 16 meter AstroMesh reflector, 128 element [L-band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-band) antenna, supplied by Astro Aerospace in [Carpinteria, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpinteria,_California). These antennas support up to 351 separate [spot beams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spot_beam), each configurable to concentrate power where usage needs it.[^cite_note-11] Amateur astronomer observations suspected that the nearby MENTOR 4 [USA-202](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA-202), a satellite belonging to the US [National Reconnaissance Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Reconnaissance_Office), was eavesdropping on Thuraya 2, and this was reported to be confirmed by documents released on 9 September 2016[^cite_note-12] by *[The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept)* as part of the [Snowden files](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden).[^cite_note-13]

#### Thuraya 3

The third satellite was planned for launch by [Sea Launch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Launch) in 2007, and the start of Far East and Australia service was planned for 15 October 2007. The failure in January 2007 of the [NSS-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NSS-8) mission on another Sea Launch rocket led to a substantial delay in the launch of Thuraya-3, which was rescheduled for 14 November 2007, but the launch was postponed several times due to sea conditions.[^cite_note-14] The launch vessels set out from port again on 2 January 2008, and launch occurred successfully at 11\:49 [GMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMT) on 15 January 2008.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The Thuraya 3 satellite is technically the same as Thuraya 2, but located in [geosynchronous orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geosynchronous_orbit) at 98.5° E longitude with 6.2° inclination.

On 15 April 2024, Thuraya 3 suffered an "unexpected payload anomaly" and despite attempted recovery efforts, coverage provided by this satellite was unable to be restored.[^cite_note-17] Thuraya advised that they have suffered a sustained force majeure event and withdrew offering services to regions affected (such as Australia, New Caledonia, Papua New Guinea, Papua, East Timor, and Indonesia).

#### Thuraya 4-NGS

Thuraya 4-NGS (Next Generation Satellite)[^cite_note-18] is a satellite launched on a [SpaceX](832774.md) [Falcon 9](2647515.md) rocket on 3 January 2025.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-\:0-21] It will replace Thuraya 2. This process will span several months until Thuraya 4 reaches its operational geostationary orbit at 44° East, approximately 36,000 kilometers above Earth.[^cite_note-\:0-21]
