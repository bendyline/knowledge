**Coronaviruses** are a group of related [RNA viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_viruses) that cause diseases in [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal) and [birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird). In humans and birds, they cause [respiratory tract infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_tract_infection) that can range from mild to lethal. Mild illnesses in humans include some cases of the [common cold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_cold) (which is also caused by other viruses, predominantly [rhinoviruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinovirus)), while more lethal varieties can cause [SARS](196741.md), [MERS](39532251.md) and [COVID-19](63030231.md). In cows and pigs, they cause [diarrhea](53951.md); while in mice, they cause [hepatitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis) and [encephalomyelitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encephalomyelitis).

Coronaviruses constitute the [subfamily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subfamily) ***Orthocoronavirinae***, in the family *[Coronaviridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronaviridae)*, order *[Nidovirales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidovirales),* and realm *[Riboviria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riboviria)*.[^cite_note-OrthocoronavirinaeICTV-3][^cite_note-FanZhao2019-4] They are [enveloped viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enveloped_virus) with a [positive-sense single-stranded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-strand_RNA_virus) [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA) [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome) and a [nucleocapsid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleocapsid) of helical symmetry.[^cite_note-5] The [genome size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome_size) of coronaviruses ranges from approximately 26 to 32 [kilobases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilobase), one of the largest among RNA viruses.[^cite_note-\:1-6] They have characteristic club-shaped [spikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_protein) that project from their surface, which in [electron micrographs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_micrograph) create an image reminiscent of the [stellar corona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_corona), from which their name derives.[^cite_note-\:2-7]

## Etymology

The name "coronavirus" is derived from Latin *[corona](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/corona#Latin)*, meaning "crown" or "wreath", itself a borrowing from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) κορώνη *korṓnē*, "garland, wreath".[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] The name was coined by [June Almeida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_Almeida) and [David Tyrrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Tyrrell_\(physician\)) who first observed and studied human coronaviruses.[^cite_note-\:10-10] The word was first used in print in 1968 by an informal group of virologists in the journal *[Nature](43427.md)* to designate the new family of viruses.[^cite_note-\:2-7] The name refers to the characteristic appearance of [virions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virion) (the infective form of the virus) by [electron microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electron_microscopy), which have a fringe of large, bulbous surface projections creating an image reminiscent of the [solar corona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_corona) or halo.[^cite_note-\:2-7][^cite_note-\:10-10] This [morphology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morphology_\(biology\)) is created by the viral spike [peplomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peplomer), which are [proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proteins) on the surface of the virus.[^cite_note-11]

The scientific name *Coronavirus* was accepted as a genus name by the International Committee for the Nomenclature of Viruses (later renamed [International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Committee_on_Taxonomy_of_Viruses)) in 1971.[^cite_note-\:02-12] As the number of new species increased, the genus was split into four genera, namely *[Alphacoronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphacoronavirus)*, *[Betacoronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betacoronavirus)*, *[Deltacoronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deltacoronavirus_\(genus\))*, and *[Gammacoronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gammacoronavirus)* in 2009.[^cite_note-13] The common name coronavirus is used to refer to any member of the subfamily *Orthocoronavirinae*.[^cite_note-FanZhao2019-4] As of 2020, 45 species are officially recognised.[^cite_note-14]

## History

Main article: [History of coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_coronavirus)



Colorized [transmission electron micrograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_electron_micrograph) of coronavirus 229E

A number of 2021 studies found that the [most recent common ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_recent_common_ancestor) (MRCA) for Coronaviruses may have emerged around 21,000–25,000 years ago in [East Asia](19605700.md), during the earliest uncovered Coronavirus outbreak. Researchers found that 42 Coronavirus-specific virus-interacting proteins (CoV-VIPs) in East Asian populations were most likely selected for during this ancient outbreak, with the viruses driving an adaptive response in the ancestors of East Asians.[^cite_note-ancient_viral_epidemic-15][^cite_note-21,000_years_ago-16] The four known genera of Coronaviruses, *Alphacoronavirus*, *Betacoronavirus*, *Gammacoronavirus*, and *Deltacoronavirus* split up around 2,400 to 3,300 years ago into bat and avian coronavirus ancestors. Bat coronaviruses gave rise to the species of *Alphacoronavirus* and *Betacoronavirus* that infect mammals, while avian coronavirus produced those of *Gammacoronavirus* and *Deltacoronavirus* that infect birds.[^cite_note-Woo2012-17]

The earliest reports of a coronavirus infection in animals occurred in the late 1920s, when an acute respiratory infection of domesticated chickens emerged in North America.[^cite_note-18] Arthur Schalk and M.C. Hawn in 1931 made the first detailed report which described a new [respiratory infection of chickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avian_infectious_bronchitis) in [North Dakota](21651.md). The infection of new-born chicks was characterized by gasping and listlessness with high mortality rates of 40–90%.[^cite_note-19] Leland David Bushnell and Carl Alfred Brandly isolated the virus that caused the infection in 1933.[^cite_note-\:22-20] The virus was then known as [infectious bronchitis virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avian_coronavirus) (IBV). Charles D. Hudson and Fred Robert Beaudette cultivated the virus for the first time in 1937.[^cite_note-\:11-21] The specimen came to be known as the Beaudette strain. In the late 1940s, two more animal coronaviruses, JHM that causes brain disease (murine encephalitis) and [mouse hepatitis virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murine_coronavirus) (MHV) that causes hepatitis in mice were discovered.[^cite_note-\:3-22] It was not realized at the time that these three different viruses were related.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-\:02-12]

Human coronaviruses were discovered in the 1960s[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] using two different methods in the United Kingdom and the United States.[^cite_note-26] E.C. Kendall, Malcolm Bynoe, and [David Tyrrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Tyrrell_\(physician\)) working at the [Common Cold Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Cold_Unit) of the [British Medical Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Research_Council_\(United_Kingdom\)) collected a unique [common cold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_cold) virus designated B814 in 1961.[^cite_note-\:9-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The virus could not be cultivated using standard techniques which had successfully cultivated [rhinoviruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinovirus), [adenoviruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenoviridae) and other known common cold viruses. In 1965, Tyrrell and Bynoe successfully cultivated the novel virus by [serially passing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serial_passage) it through [organ culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_culture) of [human embryonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryo) [trachea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trachea).[^cite_note-30] The new cultivating method was introduced to the lab by Bertil Hoorn.[^cite_note-31] The isolated virus when intranasally [inoculated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inoculation) into volunteers caused a cold and was inactivated by [ether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ether) which indicated it had a [lipid envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_envelope).[^cite_note-\:9-27][^cite_note-32] Dorothy Hamre and John Procknow at the [University of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago) isolated a novel cold from medical students in 1962. They isolated and grew the virus in kidney [tissue culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_culture), designating it 229E. The novel virus caused a cold in volunteers and, like B814, was inactivated by ether.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]



Transmission electron micrograph of organ cultured coronavirus OC43

Scottish virologist [June Almeida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_Almeida) at [St Thomas' Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Thomas'_Hospital) in London, collaborating with Tyrrell, compared the structures of IBV, B814 and 229E in 1967.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] Using [electron microscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_electron_microscopy) the three viruses were shown to be morphologically related by their general shape and distinctive club-like [spikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peplomer).[^cite_note-37] A research group at the [National Institute of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institutes_of_Health) the same year was able to isolate another member of this new group of viruses using organ culture and named one of the samples OC43 (OC for organ culture).[^cite_note-38] Like B814, 229E, and IBV, the novel cold virus OC43 had distinctive club-like spikes when observed with the electron microscope.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

The IBV-like novel cold viruses were soon shown to be also morphologically related to the mouse hepatitis virus.[^cite_note-\:3-22] This new group of viruses were named coronaviruses after their distinctive morphological appearance.[^cite_note-\:2-7] [Human coronavirus 229E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_coronavirus_229E) and [human coronavirus OC43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_coronavirus_OC43) continued to be studied in subsequent decades.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-pmid23202515-42] The coronavirus strain B814 was lost. It is not known which present human coronavirus it was.[^cite_note-43] Other human coronaviruses have since been identified, including [SARS-CoV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus) in 2003, [HCoV NL63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_coronavirus_NL63) in 2003, [HCoV HKU1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_coronavirus_HKU1) in 2004, [MERS-CoV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome-related_coronavirus) in 2013, and [SARS-CoV-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2) in 2019.[^cite_note-44] There have also been a large number of animal coronaviruses identified since the 1960s.*[^cite_note-groot-45]*

## Virology

### Structure



Structure of a coronavirus

Coronaviruses are large, roughly spherical particles with unique surface projections.[^cite_note-46] Their size is highly variable with average diameters of 80 to 120 [nm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanometre). Extreme sizes are known from 50 to 200 nm in diameter.[^cite_note-\:21-47] The total [molecular mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_mass) is on average 40,000 [kDa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_\(unit\)). They are enclosed in an envelope embedded with a number of protein molecules.[^cite_note-\:20-48] The lipid bilayer envelope, membrane proteins, and nucleocapsid protect the virus when it is outside the host cell.[^cite_note-Neuman_2011-49]

The [viral envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_envelope) is made up of a [lipid bilayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid_bilayer) in which the [membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_membrane_protein) (M), [envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_envelope_protein) (E) and [spike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_protein) (S) [structural proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_structural_protein) are anchored.[^cite_note-Lai_1997-50] The molar ratio of E\:S:M in the lipid bilayer is approximately 1\:20\:300.[^cite_note-pmid1316677-51] The E and M protein are the structural proteins that combined with the lipid bilayer to shape the viral envelope and maintain its size.[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52] S proteins are needed for interaction with the host cells. But [human coronavirus NL63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_coronavirus_NL63) is peculiar in that its M protein has the binding site for the host cell, and not its S protein.[^cite_note-53] The diameter of the envelope is 85 nm. The envelope of the virus in electron micrographs appears as a distinct pair of electron-dense shells (shells that are relatively opaque to the electron beam used to scan the virus particle).[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52]

The [M protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_membrane_protein) is the main structural protein of the envelope that provides the overall shape and is a [type III membrane protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitopic_protein). It consists of 218 to 263 [amino acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acid) residues and forms a layer 7.8 nm thick.[^cite_note-\:20-48] It has three domains, a short [N-terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-terminus) [ectodomain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectodomain), a triple-spanning [transmembrane domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmembrane_domain), and a [C-terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-terminus) [endodomain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasm). The C-terminal domain forms a matrix-like lattice that adds to the extra-thickness of the envelope. Different species can have either *N*- or *O*-linked [glycans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycan) in their protein amino-terminal domain. The M protein is crucial during the assembly, [budding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budding), envelope formation, and pathogenesis stages of the virus lifecycle.[^cite_note-55]

The [E proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_envelope_protein) are minor structural proteins and highly variable in different species.[^cite_note-\:21-47] There are only about 20 copies of the E protein molecule in a coronavirus particle.[^cite_note-pmid1316677-51] They are 8.4 to 12 kDa in size and are composed of 76 to 109 amino acids.[^cite_note-\:21-47] They are integral proteins (i.e. embedded in the lipid layer) and have two domains namely a transmembrane domain and an extramembrane C-terminal domain. They are almost fully α-helical, with a single α-helical transmembrane domain, and form pentameric (five-molecular) [ion channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_channel) in the lipid bilayer. They are responsible for virion assembly, [intracellular trafficking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracellular_transport) and morphogenesis (budding).[^cite_note-\:20-48]



Diagram of the genome and functional domains of the S protein for SARS-CoV and MERS-CoV

The spikes are the most distinguishing feature of coronaviruses and are responsible for the corona- or halo-like surface. On average a coronavirus particle has 74 surface spikes.[^cite_note-Neuman_2011-49] Each [spike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peplomer) is about 20 nm long and is composed of a [trimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_trimer) of the S protein. The S protein is in turn composed of an S1 and S2 [subunit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_domain). The homotrimeric S protein is a [class I fusion protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_fusion_protein) which mediates the [receptor binding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_entry) and [membrane fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid_bilayer_fusion) between the virus and host cell. The S1 subunit forms the head of the spike and has the receptor-binding domain (RBD). The S2 subunit forms the stem which anchors the spike in the viral envelope and on protease activation enables fusion. The two subunits remain noncovalently linked as they are exposed on the viral surface until they attach to the host cell membrane.[^cite_note-\:20-48] In a functionally active state, three S1 are attached to two S2 subunits. The subunit complex is split into individual subunits when the virus binds and fuses with the host cell under the action of [proteases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proteases) such as [cathepsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathepsin) family and [transmembrane protease serine 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMPRSS2) (TMPRSS2) of the host cell.[^cite_note-56]



After binding of the ACE2 receptor, SARS-CoV spike is activated and cleaved at the S1/S2 level.

S1 proteins are the most critical components in terms of infection. They are also the most variable components as they are responsible for host cell specificity. They possess two major domains named N-terminal domain (S1-NTD) and C-terminal domain (S1-CTD), both of which serve as the receptor-binding domains. The NTDs recognize and bind sugars on the surface of the host cell. An exception is the [MHV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murine_coronavirus) NTD that binds to a protein receptor [carcinoembryonic antigen-related cell adhesion molecule 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinoembryonic_antigen-related_cell_adhesion_molecule_1) (CEACAM1). S1-CTDs are responsible for recognizing different protein receptors such as [angiotensin-converting enzyme 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiotensin-converting_enzyme_2) (ACE2), [aminopeptidase N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aminopeptidase_N) (APN), and [dipeptidyl peptidase 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipeptidyl_peptidase_4) (DPP4).[^cite_note-\:20-48]

A subset of coronaviruses (specifically the members of [betacoronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betacoronavirus) [subgroup A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embecovirus)) also has a shorter spike-like surface protein called [hemagglutinin esterase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemagglutinin_esterase) (HE).[^cite_note-groot-45] The HE proteins occur as homodimers composed of about 400 amino acid residues and are 40 to 50 kDa in size. They appear as tiny surface projections of 5 to 7 nm long embedded in between the spikes. They help in the attachment to and detachment from the host cell.[^cite_note-57]

Inside the envelope, there is the [nucleocapsid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsid), which is formed from multiple copies of the nucleocapsid (N) protein, which are bound to the positive-sense single-stranded [RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA) genome in a continuous [beads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bead)-on-a-string type conformation.[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52][^cite_note-58] [N protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_nucleocapsid_protein) is a [phosphoprotein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphoprotein) of 43 to 50 kDa in size, and is divided into three conserved domains. The majority of the protein is made up of domains 1 and 2, which are typically rich in [arginines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arginine) and [lysines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysine). Domain 3 has a short carboxy terminal end and has a net negative charge due to excess of acidic over basic amino acid residues.[^cite_note-\:21-47]

### Genome

See also: [Severe acute respiratory syndrome–related coronavirus § Genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome%E2%80%93related_coronavirus#Genome)



SARS-CoV genome and proteins

Coronaviruses contain a [positive-sense, single-stranded RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive-sense_single-stranded_RNA_virus) genome. The [genome size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome_size) for coronaviruses ranges from 26.4 to 31.7 [kilobases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilobase#Length_measurements).[^cite_note-\:1-6] The genome size is one of the largest among RNA viruses. The genome has a [5′ methylated cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-prime_cap) and a [3′ polyadenylated tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyadenylation).[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52]

The genome organization for a coronavirus is [5′-leader-UTR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_prime_untranslated_region)-replicase (ORF1ab)-spike (S)-envelope (E)-membrane (M)-nucleocapsid (N)-[3′UTR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_prime_untranslated_region)-poly (A) tail. The [open reading frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_reading_frame) 1a and 1b, which occupy the first two-thirds of the genome, encode the replicase polyprotein (pp1ab). The replicase polyprotein self cleaves to form 16 [nonstructural proteins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_nonstructural_protein) (nsp1–nsp16).[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52]

The later reading frames encode the four major structural proteins: [spike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_protein), [envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_envelope_protein), [membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_envelope_protein), and [nucleocapsid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_nucleocapsid_protein).[^cite_note-59] Interspersed between these reading frames are the reading frames for the accessory proteins. The number of accessory proteins and their function is unique depending on the specific coronavirus.[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52]

### Replication cycle

#### Cell entry



The life cycle of a coronavirus

Infection begins when the viral spike protein attaches to its complementary host cell receptor. After attachment, a [protease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protease) of the host cell [cleaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proteolysis) and activates the receptor-attached spike protein. Depending on the host cell protease available, cleavage and activation allows the [virus to enter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_entry) the host cell by [endocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocytosis) or direct fusion of the viral envelope with the [host membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid_bilayer).[^cite_note-\:6-60]

Coronaviruses can enter cells by either fusing to their lipid envelope with the cell membrane on the cell surface or by internalization via endocytosis.[^cite_note-61]

Insertion of a cleavage site can boost viral entry in different cell types by enabling promiscuous cleavage of the spike when contacting different proteases.[^cite_note-62]

#### Genome translation

On entry into the [host cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Host_\(biology\)), the virus particle is [uncoated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncoating), and its [genome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome) enters the [cell cytoplasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytoplasm). The coronavirus RNA genome has a 5′ methylated cap and a 3′ polyadenylated tail, which allows it to act like a [messenger RNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_RNA) and be directly translated by the host cell's [ribosomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosome). The host ribosomes translate the initial overlapping [open reading frames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_frame) [ORF1a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ORF1a) and [ORF1b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ORF1b) of the virus genome into two large overlapping polyproteins, pp1a and pp1ab.[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52]

The larger polyprotein pp1ab is a result of a [-1 ribosomal frameshift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribosomal_frameshift) caused by a [slippery sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slippery_sequence) (UUUAAAC) and a downstream [RNA pseudoknot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleic_acid_secondary_structure) at the end of open reading frame ORF1a.[^cite_note-\:21-47] The ribosomal frameshift allows for the continuous translation of ORF1a followed by ORF1b.[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52]

The polyproteins have their own [proteases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C30_Endopeptidase), [PLpro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papain) (nsp3) and [3CLpro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C30_Endopeptidase) (nsp5), which cleave the polyproteins at different specific sites. The cleavage of polyprotein pp1ab yields 16 nonstructural proteins (nsp1 to nsp16). Product proteins include various replication proteins such as [RNA-dependent RNA polymerase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA-dependent_RNA_polymerase) ([nsp12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nsp12)), [RNA helicase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_helicase) (nsp13), and [exoribonuclease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoribonuclease) (nsp14).[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52]

#### Replicase-transcriptase



Replicase-transcriptase complex

A number of the nonstructural proteins coalesce to form a [multi-protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_complex) replicase-transcriptase complex (RTC). The main replicase-transcriptase protein is the [RNA-dependent RNA polymerase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA-dependent_RNA_polymerase) (RdRp). It is directly involved in the [replication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_replication) and [transcription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcription_\(biology\)) of RNA from an RNA strand. The other nonstructural proteins in the complex assist in the replication and transcription process. The [exoribonuclease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoribonuclease) nonstructural protein, for instance, provides extra fidelity to replication by providing a [proofreading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proofreading_\(biology\)) function which the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase lacks.[^cite_note-SextonSmith2016-63]

*Replication* – One of the main functions of the complex is to replicate the viral genome. RdRp directly mediates the [synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_replication) of negative-sense genomic RNA from the positive-sense genomic RNA. This is followed by the replication of positive-sense genomic RNA from the negative-sense genomic RNA.[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52]



Transcription of nested mRNAs



Nested set of subgenomic mRNAs

*Transcription* – The other important function of the complex is to transcribe the viral genome. RdRp directly mediates the [synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcription_\(biology\)) of negative-sense subgenomic RNA molecules from the positive-sense genomic RNA. This process is followed by the transcription of these negative-sense subgenomic RNA molecules to their corresponding positive-sense [mRNAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_RNA).[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52] The subgenomic mRNAs form a "[nested set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subgenomic_mRNA)" which have a common 5'-head and partially duplicate 3'-end.[^cite_note-\:15-64]

*Recombination* – The replicase-transcriptase complex is also capable of [genetic recombination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_recombination) when at least two viral genomes are present in the same infected cell.[^cite_note-\:15-64] RNA recombination appears to be a major driving force in determining genetic variability within a coronavirus species, the capability of a coronavirus species to jump from one host to another and, infrequently, in determining the emergence of novel coronaviruses.[^cite_note-Su2016-65] The exact mechanism of recombination in coronaviruses is unclear, but likely involves template switching during genome replication.[^cite_note-Su2016-65]

#### Assembly and release

The replicated positive-sense genomic RNA becomes the genome of the [progeny viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_shedding). The mRNAs are gene transcripts of the last third of the virus genome after the initial overlapping reading frame. These mRNAs are translated by the host's ribosomes into the structural proteins and many accessory proteins.[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52] RNA translation occurs inside the [endoplasmic reticulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmic_reticulum). The viral structural proteins S, E, and M move along the secretory pathway into the [Golgi intermediate compartment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesicular-tubular_cluster). There, the M proteins direct most protein-protein interactions required for the assembly of viruses following its binding to the [nucleocapsid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleocapsid). Progeny viruses are then released from the host cell by [exocytosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exocytosis) through secretory vesicles. Once released the viruses can infect other host cells.[^cite_note-\:4-66]

### Transmission

Infected carriers are able to [shed viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_\(medicine\)) into the environment. The interaction of the coronavirus spike protein with its complementary [cell receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_entry) is central in determining the [tissue tropism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tissue_tropism), [infectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectivity), and [species range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Host_tropism) of the released virus.[^cite_note-\:21-47][^cite_note-67] Coronaviruses mainly target [epithelial cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithelium).*[^cite_note-groot-45]* They are transmitted from one host to another host, depending on the coronavirus species, by either an [aerosol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioaerosol), [fomite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fomite), or [fecal-oral route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fecal%E2%80%93oral_route).[^cite_note-\:12-68]

Human coronaviruses infect the epithelial cells of the [respiratory tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_tract), while animal coronaviruses generally infect the epithelial cells of the [digestive tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrointestinal_tract).*[^cite_note-groot-45]* [SARS coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome%E2%80%93related_coronavirus), for example, infects the human epithelial cells of the lungs via an aerosol route[^cite_note-\:13-69] by binding to the [angiotensin-converting enzyme 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiotensin-converting_enzyme_2) (ACE2) receptor.[^cite_note-li-70] [Transmissible gastroenteritis coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmissible_gastroenteritis_virus) (TGEV) infects the pig epithelial cells of the digestive tract via a fecal–oral route[^cite_note-\:12-68] by binding to the [alanine aminopeptidase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alanine_aminopeptidase) (APN) receptor.[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52]

## Origin



Origins of human coronaviruses with possible intermediate hosts

The [most recent common ancestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_recent_common_ancestor) (MRCA) of all coronaviruses is estimated to have existed as recently as 8000 [BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCE), although some models place the common ancestor as far back as 55 million years or more, implying long term coevolution with bat and avian species.[^cite_note-Wertheim2013-72] The most recent common ancestor of the alphacoronavirus line has been placed at about 2400 BCE, of the betacoronavirus line at 3300 BCE, of the gammacoronavirus line at 2800 BCE, and the deltacoronavirus line at about 3000 BCE. Bats and birds, as [warm-blooded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm-blooded) flying vertebrates, are an ideal [natural reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_reservoir) for the coronavirus gene pool (with [bats the reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat-borne_virus) for alphacoronaviruses and betacoronavirus – and birds the reservoir for gammacoronaviruses and deltacoronaviruses). The large number and global range of bat and avian species that host viruses have enabled extensive evolution and dissemination of coronaviruses.[^cite_note-Woo2012-17] A number of 2021 studies found that the MRCA for Coronaviruses may have emerged around 21,000–25,000 years ago in East Asia, which is significantly earlier than most prior estimates.[^cite_note-ancient_viral_epidemic-15][^cite_note-21,000_years_ago-16]

Many human coronaviruses have their origin in bats.[^cite_note-\:8-74] The human coronavirus NL63 shared a common ancestor with a bat coronavirus (ARCoV.2) between 1190 and 1449 CE.[^cite_note-Huynh2012-75] The human coronavirus 229E shared a common ancestor with a bat coronavirus (GhanaGrp1 Bt CoV) between 1686 and 1800 CE.[^cite_note-76] More recently, [alpaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpaca) coronavirus and human coronavirus 229E diverged sometime before 1960.[^cite_note-Crossley2012-77] MERS-CoV emerged in humans from bats through the intermediate host of camels.[^cite_note-\:8-74] MERS-CoV, although related to several bat coronavirus species, appears to have diverged from these several centuries ago.[^cite_note-Lau2013-78] The most closely related bat coronavirus and SARS-CoV diverged in 1986.[^cite_note-Vijaykrishna2007-79] The ancestors of SARS-CoV first infected leaf-nose bats of the genus *[Hipposideridae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hipposideridae)*; subsequently, they spread to horseshoe bats in the species *[Rhinolophidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinolophidae)*, then to [Asian palm civets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_palm_civet), and finally to humans.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-pmid18258002-81]

Unlike other betacoronaviruses, [bovine coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bovine_coronavirus) of the species *[Betacoronavirus 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betacoronavirus_1)* and subgenus *[Embecovirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embecovirus)* is thought to have originated in [rodents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodent) and not in bats.[^cite_note-\:8-74][^cite_note-82] In the 1790s, equine coronavirus diverged from the bovine coronavirus after a [cross-species jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-species_transmission).[^cite_note-\:7-83] Later in the 1890s, human coronavirus OC43 diverged from bovine coronavirus after another cross-species spillover event.[^cite_note-Vijgen2005-84][^cite_note-\:7-83] It is speculated that the [flu pandemic of 1890](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1889%E2%80%931890_flu_pandemic) may have been caused by this spillover event, and not by the [influenza virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthomyxoviridae), because of the related timing, neurological symptoms, and unknown causative agent of the pandemic.[^cite_note-Vijgen2005-84] Besides causing respiratory infections, human coronavirus OC43 is also suspected of playing a role in [neurological diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demyelinating_disease).[^cite_note-pmid29551135-85] In the 1950s, the human coronavirus OC43 began to diverge into its present [genotypes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genotype).[^cite_note-Lau2011-86] Phylogenetically, mouse hepatitis virus (*[Murine coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murine_coronavirus)*), which infects the mouse's liver and [central nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_nervous_system_viral_disease),[^cite_note-87] is related to human coronavirus OC43 and bovine coronavirus. Human coronavirus HKU1, like the aforementioned viruses, also has its origins in rodents.[^cite_note-\:8-74]

## Infection in humans



Transmission and life-cycle of SARS-CoV-2 causing [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronavirus_disease_2019)

Coronaviruses vary significantly in risk factor. Some can kill more than 30% of those infected, such as [MERS-CoV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome-related_coronavirus), and some are relatively harmless, such as the common cold.[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52] Coronaviruses can cause colds with major symptoms, such as fever, and a [sore throat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sore_throat) from swollen [adenoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenoid).[^cite_note-88] Coronaviruses can cause [pneumonia](52135.md) (either direct [viral pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_pneumonia) or secondary [bacterial pneumonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacterial_pneumonia)) and [bronchitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchitis) (either direct viral bronchitis or secondary bacterial bronchitis).[^cite_note-pmid19199189-89] The human coronavirus discovered in 2003, [SARS-CoV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS_coronavirus), which causes [severe acute respiratory syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome) (SARS), has a unique pathogenesis because it causes both [upper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_respiratory_tract_infection) and [lower respiratory tract infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_respiratory_tract_infection).[^cite_note-pmid19199189-89]

Six species of human coronaviruses are known, with one species subdivided into two different strains, making seven strains of human coronaviruses altogether.



Seasonal distribution of HCoV-NL63 in Germany shows a preferential detection from November to March.

Four human coronaviruses produce symptoms that are generally mild, even though it is contended they might have been more aggressive in the past:[^cite_note-90]

1. [Human coronavirus OC43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_coronavirus_OC43) (HCoV-OC43), [β-CoV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betacoronavirus)

2. [Human coronavirus HKU1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_coronavirus_HKU1) (HCoV-HKU1), β-CoV

3. [Human coronavirus 229E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_coronavirus_229E) (HCoV-229E), [α-CoV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphacoronavirus)

4. [Human coronavirus NL63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_coronavirus_NL63) (HCoV-NL63), α-CoV–

Three human coronaviruses produce potentially severe symptoms:

1. [Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus) (SARS-CoV), β-CoV (identified in 2003)

2. [Middle East respiratory syndrome-related coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome-related_coronavirus) (MERS-CoV), β-CoV (identified in 2012)

3. [Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome_coronavirus_2) (SARS-CoV-2), β-CoV (identified in 2019)

These cause the diseases commonly called [SARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_acute_respiratory_syndrome), [MERS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_respiratory_syndrome), and [COVID-19](63030231.md) respectively.

### Common cold

Main article: [Common cold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_cold)

Although the [common cold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_cold) is usually caused by [rhinoviruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinovirus),[^cite_note-CecilGoldman2012-91] in about 15% of cases the cause is a coronavirus.[^cite_note-92] The human coronaviruses HCoV-OC43, HCoV-HKU1, HCoV-229E, and HCoV-NL63 continually circulate in the human population in adults and children worldwide and produce the generally mild symptoms of the common cold.[^cite_note-pmid29551135-85] The four mild coronaviruses have a seasonal incidence occurring in the winter months in [temperate climates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate).[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] There is no preponderance in any season in [tropical climates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate).[^cite_note-Abdul-Rasool_2010-95]

In 2003, following the outbreak of severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS) which had begun the prior year in Asia, and secondary cases elsewhere in the world, the [World Health Organization](33583.md) (WHO) issued a press release stating that a novel coronavirus identified by several laboratories was the causative agent for SARS. The virus was officially named the SARS coronavirus (SARS-CoV). More than 8,000 people from 29 countries and territories were infected, and at least 774 died.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-li-70]

### Middle East respiratory syndrome (MERS)

Main article: [MERS](39532251.md)

In September 2012, a new type of coronavirus was identified, initially called Novel Coronavirus 2012, and now officially named Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus (MERS-CoV).[^cite_note-NPR-108][^cite_note-109] The World Health Organization issued a global alert soon after.[^cite_note-110] The WHO update on 28 September 2012 said the virus did not seem to pass easily from person to person.[^cite_note-Reuters2012-111] However, on 12 May 2013, a case of [human-to-human transmission](63917263.md) in France was confirmed by the French Ministry of Social Affairs and Health.[^cite_note-may12-112] In addition, cases of human-to-human transmission were reported by the Ministry of Health in [Tunisia](30188.md). Two confirmed cases involved people who seemed to have caught the disease from their late father, who became ill after a visit to Qatar and Saudi Arabia. Despite this, it appears the virus had trouble spreading from human to human, as most individuals who are infected do not transmit the virus.[^cite_note-113] By 30 October 2013, there were 124 cases and 52 deaths in Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-may22-114]

After the Dutch [Erasmus Medical Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_MC) sequenced the virus, the virus was given a new name, Human Coronavirus–Erasmus Medical Centre (HCoV-EMC). The final name for the virus is Middle East respiratory syndrome coronavirus (MERS-CoV). The only U.S. cases (both survived) were recorded in May 2014.[^cite_note-115]

In May 2015, an outbreak of MERS-CoV occurred in the [Republic of Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Korea), when a man who had traveled to the Middle East, visited four hospitals in the Seoul area to treat his illness. This caused one of the largest outbreaks of MERS-CoV outside the Middle East.[^cite_note-116] As of December 2019, 2,468 cases of MERS-CoV infection had been confirmed by laboratory tests, 851 of which were fatal, a [mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortality_rate) of approximately 34.5%.[^cite_note-117]

### Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19)

Main article: [COVID-19](63030231.md)

In December 2019, a pneumonia outbreak was reported in [Wuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan), China.[^cite_note-NYT-20200129-118] On 31 December 2019, the outbreak was traced to a novel strain of coronavirus,[^cite_note-WHO9Jan2020-119] which was given the interim name 2019-nCoV by the World Health Organization,[^cite_note-WHO20200110-120][^cite_note-CDC20200113-121][^cite_note-122] later renamed [SARS-CoV-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2) by the [International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Committee_on_Taxonomy_of_Viruses).

As of 10 March 2023, there were at least 6,881,955[^cite_note-JHU_ticker-102] [confirmed deaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_deaths) and more than 676,609,955[^cite_note-JHU_ticker-102] [confirmed cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_cases) in the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). The Wuhan strain has been identified as a new strain of [Betacoronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betacoronavirus) from group 2B with approximately 70% genetic similarity to the SARS-CoV.[^cite_note-123] The virus has a 96% similarity to a bat coronavirus, so it is widely suspected to originate from bats as well.[^cite_note-\:0-124][^cite_note-125]

### Coronavirus HuPn-2018

Main article: [Canine coronavirus HuPn-2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canine_coronavirus_HuPn-2018)

During a surveillance study of archived samples of Malaysian viral pneumonia patients, virologists identified a strain of [canine coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canine_coronavirus) which has infected humans in 2018.

## Infection in animals

Coronaviruses have been recognized as causing pathological conditions in [veterinary medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterinary_medicine) since the 1930s.[^cite_note-\:3-22] They infect a range of animals including swine, cattle, horses, camels, cats, dogs, rodents, birds and bats.[^cite_note-\:14-126] The majority of animal related coronaviruses infect the [intestinal tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrointestinal_tract) and are transmitted by a fecal-oral route.[^cite_note-127] Significant research efforts have been focused on elucidating the [viral pathogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_pathogenesis) of these animal coronaviruses, especially by [virologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virologist) interested in veterinary and [zoonotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoonotic) diseases.[^cite_note-pmid20627412-128]

### Farm animals

Coronaviruses infect domesticated birds.[^cite_note-\:16-129] [Infectious bronchitis virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_bronchitis_virus) (IBV), a type of coronavirus, causes [avian infectious bronchitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avian_infectious_bronchitis).[^cite_note-pmid25954763-130] The virus is of concern to the [poultry industry](12116844.md) because of the high mortality from infection, its rapid spread, and its effect on production.[^cite_note-\:14-126] The virus affects both meat production and egg production and causes substantial economic loss.[^cite_note-Cavanagh_2007-131] In chickens, infectious bronchitis virus targets not only the respiratory tract but also the [urogenital tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urogenital_tract). The virus can spread to different organs throughout the chicken.[^cite_note-pmid25954763-130] The virus is transmitted by aerosol and food contaminated by feces. Different [vaccines](32653.md) against IBV exist and have helped to limit the spread of the virus and its variants.[^cite_note-\:14-126] Infectious bronchitis virus is one of a number of strains of the species *[Avian coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avian_coronavirus)*.[^cite_note-132] Another strain of avian coronavirus is [turkey coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_coronavirus) (TCV) which causes [enteritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enteritis) in [turkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkeys).[^cite_note-\:14-126]

Coronaviruses also affect other branches of [animal husbandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_husbandry) such as [pig farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_farming) and cattle raising.[^cite_note-\:14-126] [Swine acute diarrhea syndrome coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swine_acute_diarrhea_syndrome_coronavirus) (SADS-CoV), which is related to [bat coronavirus HKU2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinolophus_bat_coronavirus_HKU2), causes [diarrhea](53951.md) in pigs.[^cite_note-pmid29618817-133] [Porcine epidemic diarrhea virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcine_epidemic_diarrhea_virus) (PEDV) is a coronavirus that has recently emerged and similarly causes diarrhea in pigs.[^cite_note-pmid32041637-134] [Transmissible gastroenteritis virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmissible_gastroenteritis_virus) (TGEV), which is a member of the species *Alphacoronavirus 1*,[^cite_note-\:17-135] is another coronavirus that causes diarrhea in young pigs.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-136][^cite_note-ReferenceB-137] In the cattle industry [bovine coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bovine_coronavirus) (BCV), which is a member of the species *[Betacoronavirus 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betacoronavirus_1)* and related to HCoV-OC43,[^cite_note-\:18-138] is responsible for severe profuse enteritis in young calves.[^cite_note-\:14-126]

### Domestic pets

Coronaviruses infect domestic pets such as cats, dogs, and ferrets.[^cite_note-\:16-129] There are two forms of [feline coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feline_coronavirus) which are both members of the species *Alphacoronavirus 1*.[^cite_note-\:17-135] Feline enteric coronavirus is a pathogen of minor clinical significance, but spontaneous [mutation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation) of this virus can result in [feline infectious peritonitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feline_infectious_peritonitis) (FIP), a disease with high mortality.[^cite_note-\:14-126] There are two different coronaviruses that infect dogs. [Canine coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canine_coronavirus) (CCoV), which is a member of the species *Alphacoronavirus 1*,[^cite_note-\:17-135] causes mild gastrointestinal disease.[^cite_note-\:14-126] [Canine respiratory coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canine_coronavirus#Canine_respiratory_coronavirus) (CRCoV), which is a member of the species *[Betacoronavirus 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betacoronavirus_1)* and related to HCoV-OC43,[^cite_note-\:18-138] cause respiratory disease.[^cite_note-\:14-126] Similarly, there are two types of coronavirus that infect ferrets.[^cite_note-139] [Ferret enteric coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferret_coronavirus) causes a gastrointestinal syndrome known as epizootic catarrhal enteritis (ECE), and a more lethal systemic version of the virus (like FIP in cats) known as ferret systemic coronavirus (FSC).[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

### Laboratory animals

Coronaviruses infect laboratory animals.[^cite_note-\:14-126] Mouse hepatitis virus (MHV), which is a member of the species *[Murine coronavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murine_coronavirus)*,[^cite_note-\:19-142] causes an epidemic [murine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murinae) illness with high mortality, especially among colonies of laboratory mice.[^cite_note-pmid16339739-143] Prior to the discovery of SARS-CoV, MHV was the best-studied coronavirus both *[in vivo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vivo)* and *[in vitro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro)* as well as at the molecular level. Some strains of MHV cause a progressive [demyelinating encephalitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demyelinating_disease) in mice which has been used as a murine model for [multiple sclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_sclerosis).[^cite_note-pmid20627412-128] [Sialodacryoadenitis virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rat_coronavirus) (SDAV), which is a strain of the species *Murine coronavirus*,[^cite_note-\:19-142] is highly infectious coronavirus of laboratory rats, which can be transmitted between individuals by direct contact and indirectly by aerosol. Rabbit enteric coronavirus causes acute gastrointestinal disease and diarrhea in young [European rabbits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_rabbits).[^cite_note-\:14-126] Mortality rates are high.[^cite_note-144]

## Prevention and treatment

A [number of vaccines](63319438.md) using different methods have been developed against human coronavirus SARS-CoV-2.[^cite_note-milken-145][^cite_note-biorender-146] [Antiviral targets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_target) against human coronaviruses have also been identified such as viral proteases, polymerases, and entry proteins. [Drugs are in development](63435931.md) which target these proteins and the different steps of viral replication.[^cite_note-pmid32147628-147][^cite_note-biorender-146]

Vaccines are available for animal coronaviruses IBV, TGEV, and Canine CoV, although their effectiveness is limited. In the case of outbreaks of highly contagious animal coronaviruses, such as PEDV, measures such as [destruction of entire herds](2980973.md) of pigs may be used to prevent transmission to other herds.[^cite_note-Fehr_2015-52]

In December 2025, the World Health Organization released the first unified strategic plan for managing coronavirus disease threats of all types, building upon its work during the COVID-19 pandemic as well as SARS, MERS, and other respiratory viruses.[^cite_note-148]
