**Nyanga** is a [township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Township_\(South_Africa\)) in the [Western Cape](313646.md), [South Africa](17416221.md). Its name in [Xhosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_language) means "[moon](19331.md)" and it is one of the oldest [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people) townships in Cape Town. In 1948,[^cite_note-Besteman2008-2] [Athlone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlone,_Cape_Town) was declared a coloured-area, resulting in the forced removal of black residents, who were forced to settle in Nyanga near [Langa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langa,_Cape_Town).

Nyanga is situated 20 km (12 mi) from Cape Town along the [N2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N2_\(South_Africa\)) highway, close to the [Cape Town International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town_International_Airport) and next to the townships of [Gugulethu](4240611.md) and [Crossroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossroads_\(Cape_Town\)).

## History

The neighbourhood was established in 1946 and was built for the black residents of [Elsie's River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elsie's_River), [Athlone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlone,_Cape_Town) and [Simon's Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon's_Town) who were forcibly removed as a result of the [1948 Group Areas Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_Areas_Act). Between 1956 and 1959, an estimate of about 21 000 residents from [Goodwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodwood,_Cape_Town), [Parow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parow,_Cape_Town), and [Bellville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellville,_Western_Cape) were forcibly relocated to Nyanga.[^cite_note-3] Later on, the government built hostels and for contract workers working in the docks in Cape Town.

### Politics

Residents of Nyanga were active in joining a national call to protest against the [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid) laws passed in 1960. Later they were active in the 1976 student uprisings, which had begun on the other side of the country on 16 June 1976 in [Soweto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soweto_uprising) against the use of [Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans) as the primary [medium of instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_of_instruction) in schools.[^cite_note-4] Nyanga and [Crossroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossroads,_Cape_Town) became notorious for black-on-black faction fighting that was allegedly perpetrated by police in the early eighties. The local authorities (izibonda) were either allied with [UDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Democratic_Front_\(South_Africa\)) or the apartheid police force.

These political differences were allegedly used by the [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police) to stir up violence, and elements of the community were infiltrated by the apartheid regime. This led to emergence of the notoriously violent group called "the witdoeke" (the white scarfs). As a result of these fights Tambo square was formed.[^cite_note-5]

Nyanga comprises nine township subdivisions: Lusaka, KTC, Old Location, Maumau, Zwelitsha, Maholweni "Hostels", White City, Barcelona, Kanana, and Europe.

## Crime



The SAPS precinct area for Nyanga in Cape Town as of 2019 after Samora Machel was separated to become its own precinct area.

The high rate of violent crime in Nyanga had earned the area the moniker "murder capital of South Africa" with 289 recorded homicides in 2018/19,[^cite_note-6] until it was overtaken by another precinct area in 2022.[^cite_note-7] In the five year period before 2018 a total of 1,473 homicides were recorded in the precinct area.[^cite_note-8] The [South African Police Service](1228194.md) (SAPS) Nyanga precinct area includes half of the Nyanga neighborhood as well as the adjoining communities of [New Crossroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossroads,_Western_Cape) and [Brown's Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown's_Farm). Prior to December 2018 the precinct area also included [Samora Machel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samora_Machel,_Western_Cape?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-9]

By 2021/22 the number of recorded homicides had declined to 203.[^cite_note-10] In November 2023 [Google Maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Maps) announced that it would no longer recommend routes through the area following a number of violent incidents targeting motorists that included the death of motorists during the [2023 taxi strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cape_Town_taxi_strike).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]
