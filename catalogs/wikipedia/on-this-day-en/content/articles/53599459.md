The **Ukrainian Air Defence Forces** (**UADF**; [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Війська протиповітряної оборони України, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Vijśka protypovitrianoï oborony Ukraïny*) were an [anti-aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-aircraft) military service of [Ukraine](31750.md), active from 1992 to 2004.

They were established on the basis of the former Soviet [8th Air Defence Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Air_Defence_Army), and the last commander of that army, Lieutenant General [Mykhailo Lopatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykhailo_Lopatin), became the first commander of the Ukrainian Air Defence Forces.

## History

From January 24, 1992, after the collapse of the USSR, [28th Air Defense Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28th_Air_Defense_Corps?action=edit\&redlink=1), previously subordinate to [2nd Air Defence Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Air_Defence_Army) was transferred under the [8th Air Defence Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Air_Defence_Army) of Ukraine.[^cite_note-1] Units stationed in Moldova were transferred to the [Moldovan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Moldova) (275th Guards Anti-Aircraft Rocket Brigade, battalions and companies from the 14th Radio-Technical Brigade).

There were approximately 67,000 air defense troops in Ukraine in 1992. The new Air Defence Forces headquarters was formed on the basis of the HQ [8th Air Defence Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Air_Defence_Army). There were three air defence corps: the 28th ([Lviv](49968.md)), [49th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_Air_Defence_Corps?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Odesa), and 60th ([Dnipropetrovsk](989795.md)). All three air defence corps were taken over by Ukraine on 1 February 1992. The 28th ADC became the Western AD Region on 1 June 1992.

The Military Balance 95/96 said that six fighter regiments had been disbanded. (p. 71)

The Air Defence Forces expanded in July 1996 through the transfer of the air defence brigades and nondivisional air defence regiments of the [Ukrainian Ground Forces](7433327.md) in order to centralize the control of air defense assets. This shift transitioned away from the Soviet practice of placing some air defence units under ground forces control. These units included the 68th and 77th Separate Radio-Technical Brigades, the 138th, 223rd, 46th, 156th, [108th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/108th_Anti-Aircraft_Missile_Regiment_\(Ukraine\)) and 55th Anti-Aircraft Missile Brigades, the 1046th, 1067th Anti-Aircraft Missile Regiments, and the 1094th Anti-Aircraft Artillery Regiment.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETkachukPopkoKharukSlobodanyuk202248-2]

After the accidental shooting down of [Siberia Airlines Flight 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberia_Airlines_Flight_1812) in October 2001, the commander of the Ukrainian Air Defence Forces, [Colonel general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_general) [Volodymyr Tkachov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volodymyr_Tkachov?action=edit\&redlink=1), first offered to resign and then was dismissed from his post.[^cite_note-3] An anti-aircraft exercise being run from a training area in the [Crimea](163045.md) had gone wrong, and a [surface-to-air missile](182664.md) destroyed the plane.

In 2004, the Air Defence Forces were amalgamated with the [Ukrainian Air Force](1135769.md), becoming the *Anti-Air Defence Missile Artillery of the Ukrainian Air Force* ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Зенітні ракетні війська протиповітряної оборони ПC, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Zenitni raketni viysʹka protypovitryanoyi oborony PS*). The merger of the services thus enabled the Armed Forces of Ukraine to adopt the tri-service structure, common to most modern armed forces in the world, in a historic break with the Soviet precedence. In that capacity as a speciality of the force, Ukrainian Air Defense became involved in the long [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md) from the 2010s and onward. The modern day AADMA-AF reports as a operating service arm under the office of the Commander of the Air Force with units operating all over the country.

## Units in the early 1990s

The first issue of the [International Institute for Strategic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Strategic_Studies)' *Military Balance* after the Soviet collapse, 1992–93, listed one Air Defence army, 270 combat aircraft, and seven regiments of Su-15s (80), MiG-23s (110) and MiG-25s (80).[^cite_note-4] By March 1994 *Air Forces Monthly* reported three air defence regions:

- the Southern with the 62nd and 737th Fighter Aviation Regiments

- the Western with the 92nd (transferred from [14th Air Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Air_Army) and based at [Mukachevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukachevo_\(air_base\))), 179th, and [894th Fighter Aviation Regiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/894th_Fighter_Aviation_Regiment) (from 28th AD Corps/[2nd Air Defence Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Air_Defence_Army))

- the Central with the 146th (Vasylkiv), 636th ([Kramatorsk](2864798.md), disbanded in 1996 and its Su-15s broken up for scrap),
