The **Antonov An-32** ([NATO reporting name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_reporting_name): **Cline**) is a [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop) twin-engined [military transport aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_transport_aircraft). Its first flight was in July 1976 and was displayed at the 1977 [Paris Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Air_Show). A variant of the [An-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-24), it was given upgraded equipment and more powerful [Ivchenko AI-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivchenko_AI-20) engines, and is oriented towards flying in adverse weather conditions. The aircraft was produced from 1980 to 2012, and remains in service. It is the fourth member of the Antonov An-24 family, succeeding the An-24, [An-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-30) and [An-26](908173.md), and coming before the cancelled [An-132](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov/Taqnia_An-132).

## Design and development

The An-32 is essentially a re-engined [An-26](908173.md). It is designed to withstand adverse weather conditions better than the standard An-26. Announced at the May 1977 Paris Air Show, the An-32 is distinguished from its predecessor by engines raised 1.5 m above the wing in order to avoid foreign object damage on rough, unprepared airstrips.

The type features high-lift wings with automatic leading-edge slats, large triple-slotted trailing edge flaps and an enlarged tailplane and a very large increase in power, giving improved take-off performance and service ceiling. The high placement of the engine [nacelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacelle) above the wing allowed for larger diameter propellers, which are driven by 5,100 hp rated [Ivchenko AI-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivchenko_AI-20) turboprop engines, providing almost twice the power of the An-26's AI-24 powerplants.[^cite_note-AirInternational-2][^cite_note-warfiles-3]

Production from the Government Aircraft Factory in Kyiv, has included 123 aircraft for the [Indian Air Force](403355.md), which ordered the aircraft under strong foreign relations between then USSR leader [Leonid Brezhnev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Brezhnev) and then India leader [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi).[^cite_note-AirInternational-2][^cite_note-warfiles-3]

The majority of production has been for the Russian and Ukrainian Air Forces, with around 40 per year being built during the late 1980s to early 1990s.[^cite_note-AirInternational-2] The estimated price for a modernised An-32 version is 15 million US dollars.[^cite_note-warfiles-3]

The last An-32 was produced in 2012.

## Operational history

The An-32 has outstanding takeoff characteristics in hot and high conditions, up to 55 °C (131 °F) and 4,500 m (14,800 ft) elevation, and is suitable for use as a medium tactical military transport roles as well as commercial roles. Operating as a cargo transport over the short and medium range air routes, the An-32 is suitable for air-dropping cargo, passenger carrying, medevac, firefighting, skydiving or paratrooping roles.

The An-32 entered service in the 1980s and served with the air forces of Ukraine, Russia, Bangladesh Air Force, the Indian Air Force, the Iraqi Air Force, the Mexican Air Force and the Sudanese Air Force.

In 2014, the [Iraqi Air Force](1817852.md) used its An-32B as bombers against the ISIS in response to the [Islamic State invasion of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_invasion_of_Iraq).[^cite_note-4]

## Variants



A [State Emergency Service of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Emergency_Service_of_Ukraine) An-32 [firefighting aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firefighting_aircraft) dumps water on a forest fire.

- **An-32** : Twin-engined transport aircraft

- **An-32A** : The first civil variant, the majority of the 36 aircraft built were delivered to various government factory enterprises, for use in transporting assemblies between plants.

- **An-32B** : Improved version

- **An-32B-100** : Modernised version of the An-32B. [Maximum takeoff weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_takeoff_weight) (MTOW) increased to 28.5 tons, payload increased to 7.5 tons.

  [^cite_note-An-32-5]

- **An-32B-110** : New avionics allowing aircraft to be operated by two crew members. Metric (Russian) avionics variant.

  [^cite_note-aviant110-6]

- **An-32B-120** : Imperial (non-Russian) avionics variant of An-32B-110.

  [^cite_note-aviant110-6]

- **An-32B-300** : Version fitted with [Rolls-Royce AE 2100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolls-Royce_AE_2100) turboprop engines, providing 4,600 hp each.

  [^cite_note-7]

- **An-32LL** (*Letayushchaya Laboratoriya* flying laboratory): The An-32 first prototype was equipped with a large SV-36P eight-bladed propeller and D-236 engine on the port side for testing, in place of the standard engine and propeller. The increased noise produced by the experimental installation (115-120 dB) outweighed the modest gains in performance.

  [^cite_note-8]

- **An-32MP** : Marine Patrol version.

  [^cite_note-AN-32P-9]

- **An-32P Firekiller** : Aerial firefighting version. Special category type certificate granted on 10 March 1995. A total of eight tons of liquid can be discharged from the two external tanks simultaneously or one after the other. Drops are conducted at 40–50 m above ground level and 240 to 260 km/h. Can be used as a cargo aircraft when not fighting fires.

  [^cite_note-AN-32P-9]

- **An-32V-200** : A tactical transport/cargo aircraft outgrowth from the An-32B-100, with more modern avionics allowing two crew operation. Intended for export; despite reasonable interest few have been sold.

- **An-32 RE** : Modernised version of the An-32B. MTOW increased to 28.5 tons, payload increased to 7.5 tons.

  [^cite_note-An-32-5]

### Civil operators

In July 2016, a total of 25 Antonov An-32 aircraft remained in [airline](1942.md) service. The largest operator was Aero Transporte S.A (ATSA) of Peru with four aircraft. Some 16 other airlines operated smaller numbers of the type.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

## Accidents and incidents

- On 25 March 1986, an Indian Air Force An-32 disappeared over the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) on a delivery flight from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) (via [Muscat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscat_International_Airport), [Oman](22316.md).) No trace was ever found of the aircraft or its three crew and four passengers.

  [^cite_note-Muscat-30]

- On 15 July 1990, an [Indian Air Force](403355.md) An-32 crashed in the [Ponmudi Mountain Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponmudi) while en route from [Tambaram Air Force Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambaram_Air_Force_Station) to [Thiruvananthapuram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiruvananthapuram) in India, killing all 5 on board.

  [^cite_note-31]

- On 8 January 1996, an [An-32 freighter crashed into a crowded marketplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Air_Africa_crash) in [Kinshasa](45773.md), [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire), resulting in the deaths of approximately 237 people on the ground. The crew attempted to abort the takeoff at [Kinshasa-N'Dolo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Dolo_Airport) after the aircraft failed to gain height. Four of the six crew members survived. Overloading was cited as a possible cause.

  [^cite_note-32]

- On 12 February 1998, a [Sudanese Air Force An-32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Sudanese_Air_Force_Antonov_An-32_crash) carrying 51 on board, overrun the runway after landing, crashing on river, killing 26 of the 57 on board, among them several of the country's most senior government leaders, including the [Vice-President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_Sudan) [Zubair Mohamed Salih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zubair_Mohamed_Salih).

- On 28 March 1998, a [Peruvian Air Force](1040948.md) An-32 carrying the dual civil/military registration OB-1389/FAP-388 and inbound from [Tumbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumbes,_Peru) evacuating 50 people stranded by [El Niño](317311.md)-driven floods had an engine failure while approaching [Piura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap._FAP_Guillermo_Concha_Iberico_International_Airport). As the aircraft was overloaded, the pilot could not maintain height and the An-32 struck three houses of a nearby shantytown and crashed into a canal. While the crew of five survived, 21 passengers died plus one person on the ground.

  [^cite_note-33]

- On 26 August 2007, a [Great Lakes Business Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Business_Company) An-32B carrying nine tons of minerals, 12 passengers, and a crew of three experienced engine trouble after takeoff from [Kongolo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongolo_Airport), [Kongolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongolo,_Katanga), [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md), and crashed short of the runway while attempting to return to the airport, killing 14 of the 15 people on board.

  [^cite_note-34]

- On 11 April 2008, [Kata Air Transport Flight 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kata_Air_Transport_Flight_007), a An-32, crashed during return the airport caused engine failure, killing all 8 on board.

- On 10 June 2009, an Indian Air Force An-32 carrying 13 people crashed shortly after it took off from [Mechuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechuka#Mechuka_Airport) in [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh), a state bordering China.

  [^cite_note-35]

  &#x20;All 13 people on board were reported to have been killed. Soon after the crash, India agreed a \$US400 million deal with [Ukraine](31750.md) for an An-32 fleet upgrade. This upgrade as reported will extend the life of these transport aircraft by nearly 15 years.

  [^cite_note-36]

- On 12 December 2014, a Sri Lanka Air Force An-32 carrying five people crashed on approach to land at [Ratmalana Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratmalana_Airport) after taking off from [Katunayaka Bandaranayake International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandaranayake_International_Airport). The pilot, co-pilot and two of the aircrew were killed in the crash and the fifth crew member suffered critical injuries and died after six days from the accident due to his injuries.

  [^cite_note-37]

  [^cite_note-38]

- On 22 July 2016, an [Indian Air Force An-32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Indian_Air_Force_An-32_disappearance) (Registration - K2743) travelling from [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai) to [Port Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Blair) went missing above the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) with 29 people on board. It left the [Tambaram Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambaram_Air_Base) near Chennai at 8.30 am and disappeared from radar tracking at 9.12 am, 280 km east of Chennai.

  [^cite_note-39]

  &#x20;On 12 January 2024, the [Ministry of Defence (India)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(India\)) announced that the wreckage of the missing aircraft had been found in a recent search operation in collaboration with the [National Institute of Ocean Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Ocean_Technology). The wreckage was found at a depth of 3,400 metres (11,200 ft) and at a distance of 140 nautical miles (310 km) from the coast of [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai).

  [^cite_note-40]

  &#x20;It was found by a ship on a research mission in the nearby areas and stumbled upon during calibration of Instruments.

  [^cite_note-41]

- On 3 October 2018 a [Sudan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_Air_Force) An-32 collided with an [Antonov An-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-30) (also of the Sudan Air Force) at [Khartoum International Airport](4306874.md).

  [^cite_note-42]

- On 3 June 2019, [an Indian Air Force An-32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Indian_Air_Force_An-32_crash) went missing near [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh) shortly after taking off from [Jorhat Air Base](7492180.md) in [Assam](186162.md) in northeastern India. The wreckage of the aircraft was found in Arunachal Pradesh eight days later with the 13 on board found dead.

  [^cite_note-43]

  [^cite_note-44]

- On 30 March 2026, an An-32 of the [Iraqi Air Force](1817852.md) was destroyed at [Baghdad International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad_International_Airport) by an Iranian-backed Iraqi militia, the [Popular Mobilization Forces](45671374.md), using Shaheb-12 missiles.

  [^cite_note-45]

- On 13 June 2026, an An-32 of the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) crashed while landing at [Jorhat](42478747.md), Assam, India, killing 5 IAF personnel with one survivor.
