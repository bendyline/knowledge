**Indian diaspora** ([ISO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_15919): *Bhāratīya Pravāsī*), officially **Non-Resident Indians** (**NRIs**) and **People of Indian Origin** (**PIOs**), are people of Indian descent who reside or originate outside of [India](14533.md), forming a part of the larger [South Asian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_diaspora). According to the [Government of India](553883.md), *Non-Resident Indians* are citizens of India who currently are not living in India, while the term *People of Indian Origin* refers to people of Indian birth or ancestry who are citizens of countries other than India (with some exceptions). [Overseas Citizenship of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Citizenship_of_India) (OCI) is given to *People of Indian Origin* and to persons who are not *People of Indian Origin* but married to an *Indian citizen* or *Person of Indian Origin*. Persons with OCI status are known as **Overseas Citizens of India** (**OCIs**).[^cite_note-31] The OCI status is a permanent visa for visiting India with a foreign passport.

According to the [Ministry of External Affairs](10302410.md) report updated on 26 November 2024, there are 35.4 million non-resident Indians (NRIs) and People of Indian Origins (PIOs) (including OCIs) residing outside India. The Indian diaspora comprise the world's largest overseas diaspora.[^cite_note-POI-2] Every year, 2.5 million (25 [lakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakh)) Indians immigrate overseas, making India the nation with the highest annual number of emigrants in the world.[^cite_note-32]

## Legal framework

### Non-resident Indian (NRI)

Strictly, the term *[non-resident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_\(law\)) Indian* refers only to the [tax status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax) of an Indian citizen who, as per section 6 of [The Income-tax Act, 1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income-tax_Act,_1961), has not resided in India for a specified period for the purposes of the Income Tax Act.[^cite_note-itact-33] The rates of income tax are different for persons who are "resident in India" and for NRIs. For the purposes of the Income Tax Act, "residence in India" requires stay in India of at least 182 days in a financial year or 365 days spread out over four consecutive years and at least 60 days in that year. According to the act, any Indian citizen who does not meet the criteria as a "resident of India" is a non-resident of India and is treated as NRI for paying income tax.

Seafarers are not considered NRIs. However, as they work out of India, often for more than 182 days, their income is taxed as that of NRIs while they enjoy all the other rights of a citizen.

### Person of Indian Origin (PIO)

A **Person of Indian Origin** (**PIO**)[^cite_note-34] means a foreign citizen (except a national of [Pakistan](23235.md), [Afghanistan](737.md), [Bangladesh](3454.md), [China](5405.md), [Iran](14653.md), [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan), [Sri Lanka](26750.md) and/or [Nepal](171166.md)), who:

- previously held an Indian [passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passport),

- either of whose parents/grandparents/great-grandparents were born and permanently resided in India as defined in [Government of India Act, 1935](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India_Act_1935) and other territories that became part of India thereafter provided neither was at any time a citizen of any of the aforesaid countries (as referred above), or

- is a spouse of a citizen of India or of a PIO.

### Overseas Citizenship of India (OCI)

Main article: [Overseas Citizenship of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Citizenship_of_India)

After multiple efforts by leaders across the Indian political spectrum, a long term visa scheme was established. It is entitled the "Overseas Citizenship of India", and is commonly referred to as the *OCI card*. The name is itself misleading, as it doesn't offer Indian citizenship. The [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India) does not permit full dual citizenship. The OCI card is effectively a long-term visa, with restrictions on voting rights and government jobs. The card is available to certain Overseas ex-Indians, and while it affords holders residency and other rights, it does have restrictions, and is not considered to be any type of Indian citizenship from a constitutional perspective.

[Prime Minister](24452.md) [Narendra Modi](444222.md) announced on 28 September 2014 that PIO and OCI cards would be merged.[^cite_note-35] On 9 January 2015, the Person of Indian Origin Card scheme was withdrawn by the Government of India and was merged with the [Overseas Citizen of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Citizen_of_India) card scheme. PIO cardholders must apply to convert their existing cards into OCI cards. The [Bureau of Immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Immigration_\(India\)) stated that it would continue to accept the old PIO cards as valid travel documents until 31 December 2023.[^cite_note-36]

### Central Asia

Narimsimhan et al. (2019)[^cite_note-38] have found that there was an "Indus periphery" population living in [Central Asia](6742.md) during the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age). They had migrated from the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation) and had settled down in [BMAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bactria%E2%80%93Margiana_Archaeological_Complex) settlements to trade, this is corroborated by the discovery of Indus Valley seals in Central Asia.[^cite_note-39]

The modern Indian merchant diaspora in [Central Asia](6742.md) and [Arabia](47858.md) emerged in the mid-16th century and remained active for over four centuries.

Multani people from [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan), [Shikarpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikarpur,_Sindh) and [Mawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawar) of both Hindu and Muslim background acted as bankers and merchants in [Safavid Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_Iran). Hindu merchants in [Hamadan](43610555.md) were massacred by [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) as stated by an [Armenian](140376.md), with the Indian merchant community plummeting due to the [Ottoman and Afghan wars in Iran (1722–27)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Hotaki_War_\(1726%E2%80%931727\)).[^cite_note-40] In [Kerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerman), traders of Hindu background had a [caravanserai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravanserai).[^cite_note-41] Traders of Indian background were mentioned by [Jean Chardin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Chardin), [Jean de Thévenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_de_Th%C3%A9venot), [Adam Olearius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Olearius) and F. A. Kotov in the [Safavid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) in Persia where they lived along with Jews and Armenians. Traders from India of Sikh and Hindu background lived in the [Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) and [Zand dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zand_dynasty) in Persia after a clampdown by [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah) and the Afghan [Ghilzar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghilji) wars in Iran.[^cite_note-42]

[Sarmarqandi](51491.md) and [Bukharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukhara) traders bought Indian [indigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigo_dye) from merchants of Hindu origin in Kandahar in 1783 according to [George Forester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Forester). The tallest houses were owned by Hindus according to Elphinstone in 1815. Lumsden recorded 350 stores owned by Hindus in [Kandahar](17260.md). Finance, precious metals, and textiles were all dealt with by Sikhs and Hindus in Kandahar.[^cite_note-43]

A Hindu worked for [Timur Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur_Shah_Durrani) in Afghanistan. [Peshawar](66230.md) Hindus were in Kabul by 1783. [Money lending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan) was the main occupation of Hindus in Kabul. Armenians and Hindus lived in [Kabul](16826.md) according to an 1876 survey.[^cite_note-44] Jews and Hindus lived in Herat in the 1800s.[^cite_note-45] Sindhi Shikarpur Hindus, Jews, and Arabs lived in [Balkh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkh) in 1886.[^cite_note-46] Sindhi and Punjabi were the languages used by Indians in Afghanistan. Some Afghan cities including Kabul have places of worship for Hindus and Sikhs.[^cite_note-47] Local citizenship has been obtained in Afghanistan by Hindu and Sikh traders.[^cite_note-48]

Peshawari and [Shikarpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikarpur,_Sindh) Indian traders were involved in Central Asia. The Shikarpuri invested in grain in the [Emirate of Bukhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Bukhara) as well as [Fergana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fergana) cotton. They also engaged in legal money lending in Bukhara, which they could not legally do in [Russian Turkestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Turkestan).[^cite_note-49] Jews, Hindus, [Baloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people), Persians, and Arabs lived in Samarkand, and Hindus and [Baháʼís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith) live in Baluchistan and [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorasan) in Iran.[^cite_note-50]

Uyghur merchants would harass Hindu usurers by screaming at them asking them if they ate beef or hanging cow skins on their quarters. [Uyghur](31783.md) men also rioted and attacked Hindus for marrying Uyghur women in 1907 in [Poskam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poskam_County,_Xinjiang) and [Yarkand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarkant_County) like Ditta Ram calling for their beheading and stoning Indians to death as they engaged in [anti-Hindu violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Hindu_sentiment).[^cite_note-51] Hindu Indian usurers engaging in a religious procession led to violence against them by Muslim Uyghurs.[^cite_note-52] In 1896 two Uyghur Turkis attacked a Hindu merchant and the British consul [Macartney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macartney_Embassy) demanded the Uyghurs be punished by flogging.[^cite_note-53]

The money lenders and merchants of Hindu background from British India in [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) were guaranteed by the British Consul-General.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] Russian refugees, missionaries, and [British-Indian merchants and money lenders of Hindu background](#Central_Asia) were potential targets of gangs of [Kashgaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashgar) so the Consulate-General of Britain was a potential shelter.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] [The killings of two Hindus at the hands of Uighurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_East_Turkestan_Republic#Christians_and_Hindus) took place in the Shamba Bazaar[^cite_note-58] in a most brutal fashion.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] The plundering of the valuables of slaughtered British Indian Hindus happened in Posgam on 25 March 1933, and on the previous day in [Kargilik Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargilik_Town) at the hands of [Uyghurs](31783.md).[^cite_note-62] Killings of Hindus took place in [Khotan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotan) at the hands of the Bughra Amirs.[^cite_note-63] [Antagonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antagonism_\(phytopathology\)) against both the British and Hindus ran high among the Muslim Turki [Uyghur](31783.md) rebels in Xinjiang's southern area. Muslims plundered the possessions in [Karghalik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargilik_Town) of Rai Sahib Dip Chand, who was the [aksakal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqsaqal) of Britain, and his fellow Hindus on 24 March 1933, and in [Keryia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keriya_Town) they slaughtered British Indian Hindus.[^cite_note-64] Sind's [Shikarpur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikarpur_District) was the origin of the Hindu diaspora there. The slaughter of the Hindus from British India was called the "Karghalik Outrage". The Muslims had killed nine of them.[^cite_note-65] The forced removal of the Swedes was accompanied by the slaughter of the Hindus in [Khotan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Khotan) by the Islamic Turkic rebels.[^cite_note-66] The Emirs of Khotan slaughtered the Hindus as they forced the Swedes out and declared [sharia](28840.md) in Khotan on 16 March 1933.[^cite_note-67]

### Southeast Asia

See also: [Hinduism in Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Southeast_Asia), [Buddhism in Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Southeast_Asia), and [Chitty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitty)

A major emigration from the Indian subcontinent was to [Southeast Asia](28741.md). There is a possibility that the first wave of Indian migration towards Southeast Asia occurred when [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka) invaded [Kalinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_\(region\)) and following [Samudragupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudragupta)'s expedition towards the South.[^cite_note-68] This was followed by early interaction of Indian traders with South Asians and, after the mid-first millennium CE, by the emigration of members of the [Brahmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmin) social [caste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste). This resulted in the establishment of the [Indianised kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_India) in Southeast Asia. The [Chola rulers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola_dynasty), who were known for their naval power, conquered [Sumatra](28679.md) and the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula).



Indian trader's family in [Bagamoyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagamoyo), [German East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_East_Africa), around 1906/18

Another early diaspora, of which little is known, was a reported Indian "Shendu" community that was recorded when [Yunnan](166410.md) was annexed by the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) in the 1st century by the Chinese authorities.[^cite_note-69]

During the mid-19th century right after the [British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidencies_and_provinces_of_British_India) ended, much of the [migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_migration) that occurred was of pioneering [Girmityas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girmitiyas) indentured workers – mostly [Bhojpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_language)-speaking people from the [Bhojpuri region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_region) of [Bihar](442482.md) and [Awadhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awadhi_language)-speaking people from the [Awadh region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awadh) of [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md) to other [British colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_the_British_Empire) under the [Indian indenture system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_indenture_system). The major destinations were [Mauritius](19201.md), [Guyana](17238691.md), [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md), [Suriname](26828.md), other parts of the [Caribbean](18956035.md) (e.g. [Jamaica](15660.md), [Guadeloupe](12343.md), [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique), [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize), [Barbados](3455.md), [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada), [Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](27228.md), [Saint Lucia](27208.md)), [Fiji](10707.md), [Réunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9union), [Seychelles](27288.md), [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula) (e.g. [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [Singapore](27318.md)), [East Africa](260987.md) (e.g. [Kenya](188171.md), [Somalia](27358.md), [Tanzania](30118.md), [Uganda](31816.md)) and [Natal province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natal_\(province\)) of [South Africa](17416221.md).

[Gujarati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_people) and [Sindhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhi_people) merchants and traders settled in the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md), [Aden](38050.md), [Oman](22316.md), [Bahrain](18933277.md), [Dubai](211583.md), South Africa and East African countries, most of which were ruled by the British. The [Indian rupee](490783.md) was the legal currency in many countries of Arabian peninsula. [Punjabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabis), [Rajasthani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthani_peoples), [Sindhis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhis), [Baloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people) and [Kashmiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiris) Camel drivers were brought to Australia.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

Individuals of Indian origin have achieved a high [demographic profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_profile) in [metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) worldwide, including [India Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Square) (*Little [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay)*

) in [Jersey City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_City,_New_Jersey#Demographics), [New Jersey](21648.md), United States, home to the highest concentration of Asian Indians in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md).

After gaining independence from the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), unlike internal migration, senior government leaders have historically not vocalized opinions on international emigration. As a result, it remains a political issue only in states with major emigrant populations, such as [Kerala](4349459.md), [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India), [Tamil Nadu](29918.md) and to a lesser degree [Gujarat](53707.md), [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md) and [Goa](40010153.md). However, the phenomenon continues to be a major force in India's economic ([foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment)), social and political relations with nations having significant Indian populace. For example, the 2008 signing of the [India–United States Civil Nuclear Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93United_States_Civil_Nuclear_Agreement) was helped by intense lobbying from [Indian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Americans).[^cite_note-77]

## Overseas experience

### Love for India

Main article: [Indomania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indomania)

[Indomania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indomania) or Indomania is love, admiration or special interest for India or its people and culture.[^cite_note-78] An [Indophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indomania) is someone who loves [India](14533.md), [Indian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_India), [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cuisine), [religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_religions), [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_India) or its [people](7564733.md).

### Overseas discrimination

Main article: [Anti-Indian sentiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Indian_sentiment)

See also: [Persecution of Hindus in Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Hindus_in_Malaysia) and [Expulsion of Asians from Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Asians_from_Uganda)



Indians expelled from [Uganda](31816.md) arriving at [Amsterdam Airport Schiphol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Airport_Schiphol), November 1972

Indian diaspora communities have experienced [discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discrimination) and anti-Indian sentiment in a number of countries, particularly in the context of colonial and postcolonial racial and nationalist policies. In [South Africa](17416221.md), Indians faced extensive legal and racial discrimination under colonial rule and later under [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid), including restrictions on residence, movement, political participation and economic activity.[^cite_note-79]

In [East Africa](260987.md), South Asian communities also faced political and economic discrimination in some states after independence. This contributed to substantial emigration from countries such as [Kenya](188171.md) and [Tanzania](30118.md) and culminated in the expulsion of most of [Uganda](31816.md)'s South Asian population by President [Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin) in 1972. Much of the property and businesses left behind by those expelled were subsequently taken over by the Ugandan state.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

In [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar), South Asians were among the groups affected by violence, deportations and property confiscations during and after the [Zanzibar Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar_Revolution) of 1964. The Asian population subsequently declined substantially through emigration.[^cite_note-82]



The burnt-out remains of Govinda's Indian Restaurant in Suva following the [2000 coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Fijian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), during which [Indo-Fijian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Fijians)-owned businesses were targeted in the city's central business district.

In [Malaysia](3607937.md), Indians experienced socioeconomic marginalisation under postcolonial policies favouring the [Bumiputera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumiputera_\(Malaysia\)) population, although these policies were not directed exclusively at Indians. Concerns over discrimination contributed to the emergence of the [Hindu Rights Action Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Rights_Action_Force) (HINDRAF), which organised a major protest in 2007.[^cite_note-83]

In [Fiji](10707.md), Indo-Fijians faced political and institutional discrimination and ethnic tensions over political representation and land. These tensions contributed to political instability surrounding the [military coups of 1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Fijian_coups_d'%C3%A9tat) and [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Fijian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), after which substantial numbers of Indo-Fijians emigrated.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

In [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma), the government of [Ne Win](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ne_Win) introduced policies of nationalisation and economic [Burmanisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmanisation?action=edit\&redlink=1) after the [1962 Burmese coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Burmese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat). These policies had a particularly severe impact on the country's Indian business community, while restrictions on citizenship and employment and the nationalisation of businesses and property contributed to a large-scale exodus of people of Indian origin during the 1960s.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

## Diaspora by host country

### Africa

#### Mauritius

Main article: [Mauritians of Indian origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritians_of_Indian_origin)

The people are known as [Indo-Mauritians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritians_of_Indian_origin), and form about 65.8% of the population. The majority of them are [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) (73.7%) and a significant group are [Muslims](19541.md) (26.3%). Mauritius is the only Hindu majority (48.5%) country of [Africa](5334607.md) according to the 2011 census. There are also a relatively small number of [Baháʼís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith) and [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs). The [mother tongue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_tongue) of Indo-Mauritians is [Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritian_Creole), as well as French and English in general fields, however various Indian languages are still spoken, especially [Bhojpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_language), [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language), [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi), [Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_language), [Odia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language), [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language), and [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) as they are used in religious activities.

Mauritius hosts the [Aapravasi Ghat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aapravasi_Ghat), the only site of UNESCO in the world, to pay homage to the memory of [indenture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indenture). The Indian Festivals of [Maha Shivaratri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maha_Shivaratri), [Diwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diwali), [Thaipusam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaipusam), [Pongal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pongal_\(festival\)), [Ganesh Chaturthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesh_Chaturthi) and [Ugadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugadi) are all National Holidays as well as the Annual Commemoration of the Arrival of Indian Indentured Labourers in Mauritius.

#### Réunion

Main articles: [Indians in Réunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_R%C3%A9union) and [Malbars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malbars)

Indians make up a quarter of Réunion's population. Most originally came as [indentured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indentured) workers from [Tamil Nadu](29918.md).

#### South Africa

[Navanethem Pillay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navanethem_Pillay), an [Indian South African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_South_African) descent who served as the [U.N High Commissioner for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_High_Commissioner_for_Human_Rights).

Most Asians in South Africa are descended from indentured Indian labourers who were brought by the British from India in the 19th century, mostly to work on the [Sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane) [plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation) of what is now the province of [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md) (KZN).[^cite_note-181] The majority are of [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_South_Africans) speaking heritage along with people that speak [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) or [Bhojpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_language), mostly descending from Bihar and Uttar Pradesh. There are also smaller numbers of [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language) speaking communities while a minority are descended from Indian traders who migrated to South Africa at around the same time, many from Gujarat. The city of [Durban](313650.md) has the highest number of Asians in sub-Saharan Africa,[^cite_note-182] and the Indian independence leader [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) worked as a lawyer in the country in the early 1900s. South Africa has one of the highest number of people of Indian descent outside of India in the world, i.e. born in South Africa and not migrant. Most of them are fourth or fifth-generation descendants. Most Indian South Africans do not speak any Indian languages, as they were 'lost' over the generations, although some do enjoy watching Indian movies and listening to [Indian music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_India), and they maintain (and have had imposed upon them) a strong *Indian* [racial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(human_categorization\)) identity as a consequence of the legacy of [Apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid).[^cite_note-183]

#### East Africa

Main article: [Indian diaspora in Southeast Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_diaspora_in_Southeast_Africa)



[Sir Ben Kingsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Kingsley) of [Indo-Kenyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_diaspora_in_Southeast_Africa) descent is a notable Oscar-winning actor



Farrokh Bulsara, better known as [Freddie Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freddie_Mercury), lead singer and co-founder of the immensely successful rock band *[Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_\(band\))*, was of [Parsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsi) descent born in [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar).

Before the larger wave of migration during the British colonial era, a significant group of South Asians, especially from the west coast ([Sindh](28972.md), [Surat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surat), [Konkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkan) and [Malabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_Coast)) travelled regularly to South East Africa, especially Zanzibar. It is believed that they travelled in Arab *[dhows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhow)*, [Maratha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) Navy ships (under [Kanhoji Angre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanhoji_Angre)), and possibly [Chinese junks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junk_\(ship\)) and Portuguese vessels. Some of these people settled in South-East Africa and later spread to places like present day Uganda, and Mozambique. Later they mingled with the much larger wave of South Asians who came with the British.

Indian migration to the modern countries of Kenya, Uganda, Mauritius, South Africa, and Tanzania began nearly a century ago when these parts of the continent were under British and French colonial rule. Most of these migrants were of Gujarati or [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_people) origin. There are almost three million Indians living in South-East Africa. Indian-led businesses were (or are) the backbone of the economies of these countries. These ranged in the past from small rural grocery stores to [Sugarcane mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane_mill). In addition, Indian professionals, such as doctors, teachers, engineers, also played an important part in the development of these countries.



[Prateek Suri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prateek_Suri), an Indian‑origin entrepreneur active in African markets with residence in Africa.&#x20;

Over the decades, South Asian communities also became closely linked to commercial activity in the region. Many established trading networks that connected coastal ports with inland markets, while others developed small and medium‑sized enterprises that supported local economies. Their involvement expanded into areas such as retail, transport, manufacturing, and later investment, contributing to the growth of both urban and rural economic sectors. This commercial presence has continued into the modern era, with Indian‑origin businesses remaining active across Africa and participating in a wide range of economic activities.[^cite_note-184]

#### Singapore

Main article: [Indian Singaporeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Singaporeans)



[V. Sundramoorthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._Sundramoorthy) is a former [Singapore international](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_national_football_team) [footballer](10568.md) and currently the head coach of [S.League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.League) club [Tampines Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampines_Rovers).

Indian Singaporeans – defined as persons of [South Asian](21566765.md) paternal [ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestry) – form 9% of the country's citizens and permanent residents,[^cite_note-194] making them [Singapore](27318.md)'s third largest ethnic group. Among cities, Singapore has one of the largest overseas Indian populations.

Although contact with ancient India left a deep cultural impact on Singapore's indigenous [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_in_Singapore) society, the mass migration of ethnic Indians to the island only began with the [founding of modern Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_of_modern_Singapore) by the British in 1819. Initially, the Indian population was transient, mainly comprising young men who came as workers, soldiers and convicts. By the mid-20th century, a settled community had emerged, with a more balanced [gender ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio) and a better [spread of age groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_profile). Tamil is one among the four official languages of Singapore alongside English, Chinese and Malay.

Singapore's Indian population is notable for its [class stratification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_stratification), with disproportionately large [elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite) and [lower income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_class) groups. This long-standing problem has grown more visible since the 1990s with an influx of both well-educated and unskilled migrants from India, and as part of growing [income inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_inequality) in Singapore. Indians earn [higher incomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_income_in_Singapore) than Malays, the other major minority group. Indians are also significantly more likely to hold university degrees than these groups. However, the mainly locally born Indian students in public primary and secondary schools under-perform the national average at major examinations.

Singapore Indians are linguistically and religiously diverse, with South Indians and Hindus forming majorities. Indian culture has endured and evolved over almost 200 years. By the mid to late 20th century, it had become somewhat distinct from contemporary South Asian cultures, even as Indian elements became diffused within a broader [Singaporean culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Singapore). Since the 1990s, new Indian immigrants have increased the size and complexity of the local Indian population. Together with modern communications like [cable television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television) and the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet), this has connected Singapore with an emerging global Indian culture.

Prominent Indian individuals have long made a mark in Singapore as leaders of various fields in national life. Indians are also collectively well-represented, and sometimes over-represented, in areas such as [politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Singapore), [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Singapore), [diplomacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Singapore) and the [law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_in_Singapore).

There is also a small community of Indian origin, the [Chitty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitty), who are the descendants of Tamil traders who had emigrated before 1500 CE. Considering themselves Tamil, speaking Tamil, and practice Hinduism, the Chittys number about 2,000 today.

#### Israel

Main articles: [Indians in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_Israel), [Bene Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bene_Israel), and [Bnei Menashe](1674436.md)

The [Bene Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bene_Israel) ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): בני ישראל, "Sons of Israel") are an ancient group of [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) who migrated in the 18th century from villages in the Konkan area to nearby Indian cities, primarily [Mumbai](19189.md), but also to [Pune](164634.md), and [Ahmedabad](206337.md). In the second half of the 20th century, most of them [emigrated to Israel](351513.md), where they now number about 85,000. The native language of the Bene Israel is [Judæo-Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jud%C3%A6o-Marathi), a form of Marathi.

Another prominent community that migrated to Israel after its creation were the Jews of [Cochin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochin), in Kerala ([Cochin Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochin_Jews)) – a community with a very long history. They are known to have been granted protection by the king of the [Princely State of Cochin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_State_of_Cochin). The earliest Jews in this region, as per local tradition, date to as early as 379 CE. The community was a mix of native Jews (called "Black Jews"), and European Jews (called "White Jews") who had emigrated to Cochin after the successive European conquests of Cochin. The Jewish community of Cochin spoke a variant of [Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalam), called [Judeo-Malayalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judeo-Malayalam). The community, after the creation of Israel, saw a mass exodus from Cochin, and is presently facing extinction in India.

Still another group of Indians to arrive in Israel belong to the [Bnei Menashe](1674436.md) ("Children of [Menasseh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Manasseh)", Hebrew בני מנשה) a group of more than 10,000 people from India's [North-Eastern border states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Sister_States) of [Manipur](422642.md) and [Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram), who claim descent from one of the [Lost Tribes of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ten_Lost_Tribes), and of whom about 3,700 now live in Israel (some of them in [Israeli settlements](15123.md) on the [West Bank](33209.md)). Linguistically, Bnei Menashe are [Tibeto-Burmans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibeto-Burman_languages) and belong to the [Mizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizo_people), [Kuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuki_people) and [Chin peoples](1828130.md) (the terms are virtually interchangeable).[^cite_note-196] The move to convert them to Judaism and bring them to Israel is politically controversial in both India and Israel.[^cite_note-197]

#### Persian Gulf

Indians command a dominant majority of the population [Persian Gulf countries](3088555.md). After the 1970s oil boom in the [Middle East](19323.md), numerous Indians from [Kerala](4349459.md) emigrated, taking advantage of close historical ties with the 'Gulf' as well as the lack of ample skilled labour from nearby [Africa](5334607.md) and the [Middle East](19323.md). Major urban centres such as [Dubai](211583.md), [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md), [Doha](26214389.md), [Riyadh](51554.md), [Muscat](57053.md), [Baghdad](4492.md), [Kuwait](57021.md), and [Manama](20481.md) were experiencing a development boom and thousands of Indians laboured in construction industries.

This work was done on a contractual basis rather than permanently, and working age men continued to return home every few years. This has remained the dominant pattern as the countries in the Persian Gulf, especially [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), [Bahrain](18933277.md), [Qatar](8238258.md) and [Kuwait](7515890.md) have a common policy of not naturalising non-Arabs, even if they are born there.

The Persian Gulf region has provided incomes many times over for the same type of job in India and has geographical proximity to India, and these incomes are free of taxation. The NRIs make up a good proportion of the working class in the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md) (GCC). NRI population in these [GCC](220950.md) countries is estimated to be around 20 million, of which a quarter is resident in the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) (UAE).[^cite_note-198] In 2005, about 75% of the population in the UAE was of Indian descent. The majority originate from Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh, Odisha, Karnataka, and Goa. Similarly, Indians are the single largest nationality in Qatar, representing around 85% of the total population as of 2014.[^cite_note-199] They also form majorities in Bahrain, Kuwait, and Oman.

Since the early 2000s, significant number of Indians have reached the region, taking up high skill jobs in business and industry. Major Indian corporations maintain solid regional presence there while some are headquartered there.

There is a huge population of NRIs in West Asia, most coming from [Kerala](4349459.md) and [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md). They work as engineers, doctors, lawyers, labourers and in clerical jobs. Unlike in Europe and America, most of the countries in West Asia do not grant [citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship) or permanent residency to these Indians, however long they might live there. They have a minority in Saudi Arabia. The NRI population tends to save and remit considerable amounts to their dependents in India. It is estimated such remittances may be over US\$10 billion per annum (including remittances by formal and informal channels in 2007–2008). The relative ease with which people can travel to their home country means that many NRIs in the Gulf and West Asia maintain close links to Indian culture, with people often travelling twice or thrice a year, especially during holiday period, while some live in India for several months each year. Satellite television allows many NRIs to consume Indian media and entertainment, and there are TV soaps aimed at the NRI community in the Gulf countries. Live performances and cultural events, such as [Tiarts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiatr) for Goans living in UAE, occur quite often and are staged by community groups.

### Europe

#### Netherlands and Suriname

Main articles: [Indians in the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_the_Netherlands) and [Indo-Surinamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Surinamese)

There are around 120,000 people of Indian origin in the Netherlands, 90% of whom migrated from the former Dutch colony of Suriname, where their forefathers were brought as workers to farm and tend to crops in the former Dutch colonies.

Indo-Surinamese are nationals of Suriname of Indian or other South Asian ancestry. After the Dutch government signed a treaty with the United Kingdom on the recruitment of contract workers, Indians began migrating to Suriname in 1873 from what was then British India as indentured labourers, many from the modern-day Indian states of Uttar Pradesh, Bihar and the surrounding regions. Just before and just after the independence of Suriname on 25 November 1975 many Indo-Surinamese emigrated to the Netherlands.

During the heyday of British rule in India, many people from India were sent to other British colonies for work. In the Dutch colony of Suriname, the Dutch were allowed by the British Raj to recruit labourers in certain parts of the North-Indian United Provinces. Today, Europe's largest Hindu temple is currently situated in [The Hague](30269.md).[^cite_note-211]

#### United Kingdom

Main articles: [British Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indians) and [Indian community of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_community_of_London)



[Madhur Jaffrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhur_Jaffrey) is a notable Indian-born [British Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian) actress, food and travel writer, and television personality.



[Rishi Sunak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishi_Sunak), the first British Indian (non-white) [Leader of the Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) and [Prime Minister of the United Kingdom](24150.md) (2022–2024)

The Indian emigrant community in the United Kingdom is now in its third generation. Indians in the UK are the largest community outside of [Asia](689.md) proportionally, and the second largest in terms of population, only surpassed by the United States, and closely followed by Canada. The first wave of Indians in the United Kingdom worked as manual labourers and were not respected within society. However, this has changed considerably. On the whole, third and fourth generation immigrants are proving to be very successful, especially in the fields of law, business and medicine.

Indian culture has been constantly referenced within the wider British culture, at first as an "exotic" influence in films like *[My Beautiful Laundrette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Beautiful_Laundrette)*, but now increasingly as a familiar feature in films like *[Bend It Like Beckham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bend_It_Like_Beckham)*.

The [United Kingdom Census 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Census_2011) recorded 1,451,862 people of Indian ethnicity resident in the UK (not including those who categorised themselves as of mixed ethnicity).[^cite_note-UK_Census_2011-145] The main ethnic groups are [Gujaratis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujaratis), [Punjabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabis), [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis), [Hindi-speaking people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi_Belt), [Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamils), [Telugus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_people), [Malayalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayali), [Goan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goans)-[Konkanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkani_people), [Sindhis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhis), [Marathis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_people), and [Anglo-Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Indian).[^cite_note-Chanda-212] Hindus comprise 49% of the British Indian population, Sikhs 22.1%, Muslims 13.9%, Christians nearly 10%, with the remainder made up of Jains (15,000), Parsis (Zoroastrians), and Buddhists.[^cite_note-213]

There are 2,360,000 people currently speaking Indian languages in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-214] [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) is now the second most widely spoken language in the United Kingdom,[^cite_note-215] and the most frequently spoken language among school pupils who do not have English as a first language.

[Rishi Sunak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishi_Sunak) became the first British Indian (non-white) [Prime Minister of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Ministers_of_the_United_Kingdom) in October 2022.[^cite_note-auto-216]

#### Ukraine

[Ukraine](31750.md) has become a significant destination for Indian students, particularly in the field of medicine. As of early 2022, over 18,000 Indian students were enrolled in Ukrainian universities, making up one of the largest international student communities in the country. The [COVID-19](62750956.md) pandemic and the subsequent [Russia Ukraine conflict in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) brought global attention to this population, culminating in India’s evacuation mission known as **[Operation Ganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ganga)**,[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218] which successfully repatriated thousands of stranded Indian nationals. The student diaspora in Ukraine has also found reflection in contemporary literature. One such example is *The Life of Tolka*,[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220] a memoir by Indian doctor and author Dr. Nitin Chopra,[^cite_note-221] which captures the emotional, academic, and cultural journey of Indian medical students navigating life in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe).

#### Canada

Main article: [Indo-Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Canadians)

See also: [South Asian Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Canadians), [South Asian Canadians in British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Canadians_in_British_Columbia), [South Asian Canadians in the Greater Toronto Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Canadians_in_the_Greater_Toronto_Area), and [South Asian Canadians in Greater Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Canadians_in_Greater_Vancouver)



[Harjit Sajjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harjit_Sajjan), is an [Indian Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Canadian) politician and former [lieutenant colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_Colonel_\(Canada\)) with the Canadian Armed Forces. He served as the [Minister of National Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_National_Defence_\(Canada\)) from 2015 to 2021.



Canada's [Lilly Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilly_Singh), known by her [YouTube](3524766.md) username "IISuperwomanII", is a popular [YouTube personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_YouTubers) of Indian origin.

[^cite_note-222]



Canada's 11th [Minister of Innovation, Science and Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Innovation,_Science_and_Industry), from 2015 to 2021, [Navdeep Bains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navdeep_Bains) is one of the most successful [Indo-Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Canadians) politicians

According to [Statistics Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Canada), via the [2021 Canadian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_census), 1,858,755 persons classified themselves as being of Indian origin, comprising approximately 5.1% of the total Canadian population.[^cite_note-NoteCanadaPopulation2021-11] Unlike in India, however, representation of various minority religious groups is much higher amongst the Indo-Canadian population. For instance in India, Sikhs comprise 2% and Christians 2.2% of the population of India, Hindus 80% and Muslims 14%. In 2011, [Sikhs](27964.md) represented 35%, [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) represented 28%, [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) 17%, [Christians](5211.md) 16% of the total people of Indian origin in Canada.[^cite_note-224]

A Punjabi community has existed in British Columbia, Canada, for over 120 years. The first known Indian settlers in Canada were [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) soldiers who had passed through Canada in 1897 on their way home from attending [Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Victoria)'s Diamond Jubilee celebration in London, England. Some are believed to have remained in [British Columbia](3392.md) and others returned there later. Punjabi Indians were attracted to the possibilities for farming and forestry. They were mainly male [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs) who were seeking work opportunities. [Indo-Caribbeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Caribbean), descendants of the Indian indentured workers who had gone to the Caribbean since 1838, made an early appearance in Canada with the arrival of the [Trinidadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidadian) medical student Kenneth Mahabir and the Demerara (now Guyana) clerk M N Santoo, both in 1908.

The first Indian immigrants in British Columbia allegedly faced widespread [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism) from the majority [Anglo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo) community. [Race riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_riots) targeted these immigrants, as well as new Chinese immigrants. Most decided to return to India, while a few stayed behind. The [Canadian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_government) prevented these men from bringing their wives and children until 1919, another reason why many of them chose to leave. Quotas were established to prevent many Indians from moving to Canada in the early 20th century. These quotas allowed fewer than 100 people from India a year until 1957, when the number was increased to 300. In 1967, all quotas were scrapped. Immigration was then based on a point system, thus allowing many more Indians to enter. Since this open-door policy was adopted, Indians continue to come in large numbers, and roughly 25,000-30,000 arrive each year, which now makes Indians the second highest group immigrating to Canada each year, after the Chinese.

Most Indians choose to emigrate to larger urban centres like Toronto and Vancouver, where more than 60% live. Smaller communities are also growing in Calgary, Edmonton, Montreal, and Winnipeg. A place called [Little India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Market,_Vancouver) exists in South Vancouver and a section of [Gerrard Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerrard_Street_\(Toronto\)) in Toronto as well. Indians in Vancouver live mainly in the suburb of [Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey,_British_Columbia), or nearby [Abbotsford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbotsford,_British_Columbia) but are also found in other parts of Vancouver. The vast majority of Vancouver Indians are of Punjabi Sikh origin and have taken significant roles in politics and other professions, with several [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_British_Columbia) [justices](44719.md), three [attorneys general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General) and one provincial premier hailing from the community. Both [Gurmant Grewal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurmant_Grewal) and his wife [Nina Grewal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Grewal) were the first married couple in Canada to be concurrently elected as [Member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament) in 2004. The most read newspaper in the Indian community is *The Asian Star* and *The Punjabi Star* based in Vancouver started by an immigrant from Mumbai-Shamir Doshi.

The [Greater Toronto Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Toronto_Area) contains the second largest population of Indian descent in North America, enumerating 572,250 residents of Indian origin as of 2011, surpassed only by the 592,888 estimate by the 2011 American Community Survey[^cite_note-225][^cite_note-226] (and 659,784 in 2013[^cite_note-227]) for the New York City [Combined Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Statistical_Area). Note, however, that the Toronto count (but not the New York count) includes individuals of [West Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indian)/Indo-Caribbean descent. Compared to the Vancouver area, Toronto's Indian community is much more linguistically and religiously diverse with large communities of [Gujaratis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujaratis), [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis), [Malayalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malayalis), and [Tamils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamils), including Tamil ethnic minority from Sri Lanka, as well as more Indians who are [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu), [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) and [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) than Vancouver. From Toronto, Canadian carrier [Air Canada](145623.md) operates non-stop flights to Delhi and Mumbai.[^cite_note-228]

The United States has the largest Indian population in the world outside Asia. Indian immigration to North America started as early as the 1890s. Emigration to the United States also started in the late 19th and early 20th century, when [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs) arriving in Vancouver found that the fact that they were subjects of the British Empire did not mean anything in Canada itself, and they were blatantly discriminated against.[^cite_note-229] Some of these pioneers entered the US or landed in [Seattle](11388236.md) and [San Francisco](49728.md) as the ships that carried them from Asia often stopped at these ports. Most of these immigrants were Sikhs from the [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab) region.

Asian women were restricted from immigrating because the US government passed laws in 1917, at the behest of California and other states in the west, which had experienced a large influx of Chinese, Japanese, and Indian immigrants during and after the gold rush. As a result, many of the South Asian men in California married Mexican women. A fair number of these families settled in the Central Valley in California as farmers, and continue to this day. These early immigrants were denied voting rights, family re-unification and citizenship. In 1923 the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md), in *[United States v. Bhagat Singh Thind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_v._Bhagat_Singh_Thind)*, ruled that people from India (at the time, British India, e.g. South Asians) were ineligible for citizenship. [Bhagat Singh Thind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagat_Singh_Thind) was a [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) from India who settled in Oregon; he had applied earlier for citizenship and was rejected there.[^cite_note-230] Thind became a citizen a few years later in New York.

After World War II, US immigration policy changed, after almost a half century, to allow family re-unification for people of non-white origin. In addition, Asians were allowed to become citizens and to vote. Many men who arrived before the 1940s were finally able to bring their families to the US; most of them in this earlier era settled in California and other west coast states.

Another wave of Indian immigrants entered the US after independence of India. A large proportion of them were Sikhs joining their family members under the newly more (though not completely) colour-blind immigration laws, then Malayali immigrants from Middle East, Kerala, etc. and professionals or students came from all over India. The Cold War created a need for engineers in the defence and aerospace industries, some of whom came from India. By the late 1980s and early 1990s, large numbers of [Gujarati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_people), [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_people), and [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_people) people had settled in the US. The most recent and probably the largest wave of immigration to date occurred in the late 1990s and early 2000s during the [internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) boom. As a result, Indians in the US are now one of the largest among the groups of immigrants with an estimated population of about 3.2 million, or \~1.0% of the US population according to [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey) of 2010 data.[^cite_note-231] The demographics of Indian Americans have accordingly changed from majority Sikh to majority Hindu, with Sikhs only comprising 10% to 20% of Indian Americans today. This is much smaller than the proportion of Sikhs amongst the Indian populations in the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand, but larger than in India. In 2018, with 25% of the population of all non-resident migrants in the US, Indians made up the highest number of non-resident migrants (those without US citizenship or [green card](300237.md)).[^cite_note-232] The [US Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Census_Bureau) uses the term Asian Indian to avoid confusion with the [indigenous peoples of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) commonly referred to as American Indians.



Percent of population claiming Asian Indian ethnicity by state in 2010

In contrast to the earliest groups of Indians who entered the US workforce as taxi drivers, labourers, farmers, or small business owners, the later arrivals often came as professionals or completed graduate studies in the US and moved into professional occupations. They have become very successful financially thanks to highly technical industries, and are thus probably the most well-off community of immigrants. They are well represented in all walks of life, but particularly so in academia, information technology, and medicine.[^cite_note-233] There were over 4,000 PIO professors and 84,000 Indian‑born students in American universities in 2007–08. The American Association of Physicians of Indian Origin has a membership of 35,000. In 2000, *[Fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_\(magazine\))* magazine estimated the wealth generated by Indian [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley) entrepreneurs at around \$250 billion. Many IT companies like [Google](1092923.md), [Microsoft](19001.md), [Adobe](1955.md) and [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) have CEOs of Indian origin.[^cite_note-234]



Aerial view of [exurban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exurb) [Monroe Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroe_Township,_Middlesex_County,_New_Jersey), [Middlesex County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_India,_Edison/Iselin), [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_New_Jersey) housing tracts in 2010. Since then, significant new housing construction is rendering an increasingly [affluent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affluent) and [suburban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburb) environment to Monroe Township, while maintaining the proximity to [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_the_New_York_metropolitan_area) sought by Indians in this township with the fastest-growing Indian population in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md).

[Patel Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patel_Brothers) is the world's large supermarket chain serving the Indian diaspora, with 57 locations in 19 U.S. states—primarily located in the [New Jersey/New York Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_the_New_York_City_metropolitan_area), due to its large [Indian population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indians_in_the_New_York_City_metropolitan_area), and with the [East Windsor/Monroe Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Windsor,_New_Jersey), [New Jersey](21648.md) location representing the world's largest and busiest Indian grocery store outside India.

The [New York City Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Metropolitan_Area), including [Manhattan](45470.md), [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens), and [Nassau County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau_County,_New_York) in [New York State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State), and most of [New Jersey](21648.md), is home to, by far, the largest Indian population in the United States,[^cite_note-235] estimated at 679,173 as of 2014.[^cite_note-236] Though the Indian diaspora in the US is largely concentrated in metropolitan areas surrounding cities such as New York City, Washington D.C., Boston, Philadelphia, Atlanta, Chicago, Detroit, Dallas, Houston, Los Angeles, and San Francisco – almost every metropolitan area in the United States has a community of Indians.

### Oceania

#### Australia

Main article: [Indian Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Australians)

At the 2016 Australian census, 619,164 people stated that they had Indian ancestry, of which 455,389 were born in India, with people from India making up the third largest immigrant population in the country and the second most popular country of origin for new migrants from 2016.[^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238] Before roads and road transport were developed, many Indians had come to Australia to run [camel trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel_train). They would transport goods and mail via camels in the desert. Some of the earliest Punjabi arrivals in Australia included Kareem Bux, who came as a hawker to [Bendigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bendigo) in 1893, Sardar Beer Singh Johal, who came in 1895 and Sardar Narain Singh Heyer, who arrived in 1898. Many Punjabis took part in the rush for gold on the Victorian fields.

Indians also entered Australia in the first half of the 20th century when both Australia and India were both British colonies. Indian Sikhs came to work on the banana plantations in Southern Queensland. Today many of them live in the town of [Woolgoolga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolgoolga) (a town lying roughly halfway between Sydney and Brisbane). Some of these Indians, the descendants of Sikh plantation workers, now own banana farms in the area. There are two Sikh temples in Woolgoolga, one of which has a museum dedicated to Sikhism. Many Britons and [Anglo-Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Indian) born in India migrated to Australia after 1947. These British citizens decided to settle in Australia in large numbers but are still counted as Indian Nationals in the census. The third wave of Indians entered the country in the 1970s and 1980s after the abolition of the [White Australia policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Australia_policy) in 1973 with many Indian teachers, doctors and other professional public service occupations settling in Australia accompanied by many IT professionals.[^cite_note-239]

After successive [military coups in Fiji of 1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Fijian_coups_d'%C3%A9tat) and [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Fijian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), a significant number of Fijian-Indians migrated to Australia; as such there is a large Fijian-Indian population in Australia. Fijian-Indians have significantly changed the character of the Indian community in Australia. While most earlier Indian migration was by educated professionals, the Fijian-Indian community was also largely by professionals but also brought many small business owners and entrepreneurs.

The current wave of Indian migration is that of engineers, toolmakers, Gujarati business families from East Africa and relatives of settled Indians. Starved of government funding, Australian education institutes are recruiting full fee paying overseas students. Many universities have permanent representatives stationed in India and other Asian countries. Their efforts have been rewarded with a new influx of Indian students entering Australia. The total number of student visas granted to Indian students for 2006–2007 was 34,136;[^cite_note-240] a significant rise from 2002 to 2003, when 7,603 student visas were granted to Indian students.[^cite_note-241] According to the Australian Bureau of Statistics, 87% of Indians residing in Australia are under the age of 50, and over 83% are proficient in English.

#### Fiji

Main articles: [Indo-Fijians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Fijians) and [Girmitiyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girmitiyas)

Indo-Fijians are Fijians whose ancestors came mainly from [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md) and [Bihar](442482.md), while a very small minority hailed from [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md) and [Tamil Nadu](29918.md). Later on, a small population of [Gujaratis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujaratis), [Punjabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabis) and [Bengalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengalis) emigrated to [Fiji](10707.md). They number 313,798 (37.6%) (2007 census) out of a total of 827,900 people living in Fiji.[^cite_note-242] They are mostly descended from indentured labourers, *girmitiyas* or *girmit*, brought to the islands by the British colonial government of Fiji between 1879 and 1916 to work on Fiji's sugar cane plantations. Music has featured prominently in Indo-Fijian culture, with a distinctive genre emerging in the first decades of the 20th century that some claim influenced early jazz musicians. One of the Indo-Fijian jazz pioneers in the early evolution of this distinct ethnic art-form, Ravinda Banjeeri, likened the struggle to be heard through music as "like a bear emerging from a dark wood, listening to twigs snapping in an otherwise silent forest". The Indo-Fijians have fought for equal rights, although with only limited success. Many have left Fiji in search of better living conditions and social justice and this exodus has gained pace with the series of coups starting in the late 1980s.

#### New Zealand

Main category: [New Zealand people of Indian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:New_Zealand_people_of_Indian_descent)



The former [Governor General of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_General_of_New_Zealand), [Anand Satyanand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anand_Satyanand), is of Indian descent.

Indians began to arrive in New Zealand in the late eighteenth century, mostly as crews on [Royal Navy](26061.md) warships. The earliest known Indians to set foot in Aotearoa New Zealand were Muslim lascars who arrived in December 1769 on the ship *Saint Jean Baptiste* captained by Frenchman Jean François Marie de Surville sailing from Pondicherry, India.[^cite_note-Sadeque-243] Their arrival marks the beginning of Indian presence in New Zealand, in which hundreds of unnamed South Asian lascars visited New Zealand on European ships in order to procure timber and seal skins.[^cite_note-Sadeque-243] The period of Indian settlement begins with the earliest known Indian resident of New Zealand, a lascar of Bengali descent from the visiting ship *City of Edinburgh* who jumped ship in 1809 in the [Bay of Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Islands,_New_Zealand) to live with a [Māori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_people) wife.[^cite_note-244] Numbers slowly increased through the 19th and 20th centuries, despite a law change in 1899 that was designed to keep out people who were not of "British birth and parentage".[^cite_note-245] As in many other countries, Indians in New Zealand, also called "Indo-Kiwis", dispersed throughout the country and had a high rate of small business ownership, particularly fruit and vegetable shops and convenience stores. At this stage most Indian New Zealanders originated from Gujarat and the Punjab. Changes in immigration policy in the 1980s allowed many more Indians, Pakistanis and Bangladeshis into the country. Today, South Asians from all over the subcontinent live and work in New Zealand, with small numbers involved in both local and national politics.[^cite_note-246] Notable Indian New Zealanders include former [Dunedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin) mayor [Sukhi Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhi_Turner), cricketers [Dipak Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipak_Patel_\(cricketer,_born_1958\)) and [Jeetan Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeetan_Patel), singer [Aaradhna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaradhna), Minister [Priyanca Radhakrishnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priyanca_Radhakrishnan) and former [Governor General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_General_of_New_Zealand) [Anand Satyanand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anand_Satyanand).

## Impact



Overseas Citizen of India (**OCI**) Card

### Influence in India

#### Overseas Indians' Day

Since 2003, the *[Pravasi Bharatiya Divas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pravasi_Bharatiya_Divas)* (Overseas Indians' Day) sponsored by [Ministry of Overseas Indian Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Overseas_Indian_Affairs), is celebrated in India on 9 January each year, to "mark the contributions of the Overseas Indian community in the development of India". The day commemorates the arrival of [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) in India from South Africa, and during a three-day convention held around the day, a forum for issues concerning the Indian diaspora is held and the annual *Pravasi Bharatiya Samman* Awards are bestowed.[^cite_note-248] As of December 2005,[^cite_note-249] the Indian government has introduced the "[Overseas Citizenship of India (OCI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Citizenship_of_India)" scheme to allow a limited form of [dual citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_citizenship) to Indians, NRIs, and PIOs for the first time since independence in 1947. The PIO Card scheme is expected to be phased out in coming years in favour of the OCI programme.

#### Expansion of Indian soft power



Indian Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) and UK Prime Minister [David Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Cameron) interacting with children from the Indian diaspora at Wembley Stadium, London, 13 November 2015

Generations of diaspora have enhanced India's soft power through proliferation of elements of Indian culture. With expansion of [Indosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indosphere) cultural influence of [Greater India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_India),[^cite_note-253] through transmission of [Hinduism in Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Southeast_Asia)[^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255][^cite_note-256] and the [Silk Road transmission of Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road_transmission_of_Buddhism)[^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258] leading to [Indianization of Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianization_of_Southeast_Asia) through formation of non-Indian southeast Asian native [Indianized kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indianized_kingdom)[^cite_note-259] which adopted [sanskritized language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskritization)[^cite_note-260] and other Indian elements[^cite_note-261] such as the [honorific titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_honorifics), [naming of people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_name#Global_Indian_influence_in_names), [naming of places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_names_in_India#Global_Indian_influence_in_place_name), mottos of organisations and educational institutes as well as adoption of [Indian architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_temple_architecture#Southeast_Asia_as_part_of_Greater_India), [martial arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_martial_arts#Influence), [Indian music and dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_India#Globalization_of_Indian_music), [traditional Indian clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothing_in_India), [traditional Indian games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_games_of_India),[^cite_note-262][^cite_note-263][^cite_note-264] and [Indian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cuisine#Outside_India), a process which has also been aided by the ongoing historic expansion of Indian diaspora.[^cite_note-265]

In the United States, [Indian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Americans) are the third largest Asian American ethnic group behind [Chinese Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_American) and [Filipino Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_American),[^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276][^cite_note-277] by far the richest and most educated ethnic group in the USA compared to all other ethnic groups, earning \$101,591 median income per year compared to \$51,000 and \$56,000 for overall immigrant and native-born households in 2015,[^cite_note-278] with the lowest poverty rate compared to other foreign-born and U.S. born ethnic groups.[^cite_note-279] Overall, Indians are also more educated than other ethnic groups with an average of 32% and 40% of Indians holding a bachelor's degree and postgraduate degree respectively, compared to the 30% and 21% average of all Asians in the United States, and the 19% and 11% average of Americans overall.[^cite_note-280] 15.5% of all Silicon Valley startups by 2006 were founded by Indian immigrants,[^cite_note-281][^cite_note-282] and Indian migrants have founded more engineering and technology companies from 1995 to 2005 than immigrants from the UK, China, Taiwan and Japan combined.[^cite_note-283] Over 80% of all [H-1B visas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-1B_visas) are granted to Indian IT professionals and 23% of all Indian business school graduates in USA take up a job in United States.[^cite_note-284][^cite_note-285]

#### Caste and social discrimination

See also: [Caste discrimination in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste_discrimination_in_the_United_States)

Caste identities and [caste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste_system_in_India)-based social divisions have also persisted among parts of the Indian diaspora. Research has documented caste-based discrimination and social divisions among Indian and South Asian communities in countries including the United States, the United Kingdom and Australia, although the extent and visibility of such practices vary between communities.[^cite_note-Shailendra-286][^cite_note-Priyanka-287][^cite_note-288]

In the United States, scholars have examined caste discrimination in employment and other community settings. [California Department of Fair Employment and Housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Department_of_Fair_Employment_and_Housing) filed a lawsuit against [Cisco Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisco_Systems) in 2020, alleging discrimination against a [Dalit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalits) engineer by higher-caste supervisors, brought the issue to wider public attention.[^cite_note-Shailendra-286]

In the United Kingdom, caste discrimination has been the subject of research, campaigning and debate over whether specific legal protections are necessary. Research and individual cases have documented allegations of discrimination in social, educational and employment settings, while some Hindu and Indian community organisations have disputed the extent of the problem and opposed caste-specific legislation.[^cite_note-Priyanka-287]
