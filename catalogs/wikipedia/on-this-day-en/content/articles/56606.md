**Bamako**[^cite_note-10] is the [capital](181337.md) and largest city of [Mali](19127.md) with a 2022 population of 4,227,569. It is located on the [Niger River](21437.md), near the rapids that divide the upper and middle [Niger](21373.md) valleys in the southwestern part of the country, and the city is surrounded on all sides by the [Koulikoro Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koulikoro_Region).&#x20;

Bamako is the nation's administrative centre. The city is administered as separate capital district (distinct from any [region of Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_of_Mali)) equivalent to a [cercle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercle_\(Mali\)), and is divided into seven [communes](16112417.md). Bamako's [river port](1341936.md) is located in nearby [Koulikoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koulikoro), along with a major regional trade and conference centre. It is the seventh-largest West African urban centre after [Lagos](85232.md), [Abidjan](21371767.md), [Kano](508782.md), [Ibadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadan), [Dakar](44251.md), and [Accra](56824.md). Locally manufactured goods include textiles, processed meat, and metal goods as well as mining. Commercial fishing occurs on the Niger River.

In recent years, Bamako has seen significant urban development, with the construction of modern buildings, shopping malls, and infrastructure projects aimed at improving the quality of life for its residents. The city is home to many notable institutions, such as the [University of Bamako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bamako), the [National Museum of Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Mali), the Mali National Zoo, and the [Grand Mosque of Bamako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Mosque_of_Bamako). It is also the location for the [Modibbo Keita International Airport](4587990.md). The buildings of Bamako have a unique architectural style.

## History

See also: [Timeline of Bamako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Bamako)

The area of the city has evidence of settlements since the [Palaeolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) era. The fertile lands of the Niger River Valley provided the people with an abundant food supply and early chiefdoms in the area grew wealthy as they established trade routes linking across west Africa, the [Sahara](325363.md), and leading to northern Africa and Europe as early as 600s BCE. The early inhabitants traded [gold](12240.md), [ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory), [kola nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kola_nut), and salt. By the 11th century, the [Empire of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Ghana) became the first kingdom to dominate the area, later succeeded by the [Mali Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_Empire).[^cite_note-AFN-11]



A view of the [tata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_\(fortification\)) of Bamako in 1880 from the Niger

The *[kafu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafo)* of Bamako was founded around 1650 by the Niare family, perhaps associated with a [Soninke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soninke_people) man named Bamba Sanogo. The Toure and Drave families, mostly clerics and merchants, were also prominent in early Bamako.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPerinbam19994-12][^cite_note-Soninkara-13] It was a client state of the [Segou Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segou_Empire).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPerinbam19992-14] The Scottish explorer [Mungo Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mungo_Park_\(explorer\)) visited Bamako in 1806 during his exploration of the Niger River. He estimated that the city at the time held 6000 inhabitants, similar to many other commercial settlements across West Africa at the time.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEImperato199645-15][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPerinbam199919-16]



French Fort of Bammakou, built in 1883

In February 1882, [Samory Toure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samory_Toure) defeated the French at the Battle of Samaya outside [Kinieran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinieran). Faced with Toure's expanding [Wassoulou Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassoulou_Empire), some of the leaders of the Dyula community in Bamako began making overtures to join the anti-French alliance. The French commander [Gustave Borgnis-Desbordes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Borgnis-Desbordes), eager to possess this key strategic location on the Niger, rushed a force to establish a fort there on 1 February 1883.[^cite_note-Fofana-17]<sup>: 76 </sup> Kebe Brema, Samory's brother, led a force to Bamako to lure the French out of their defenses. They fought two [ battles at Woyo Wayanko creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Woyowoyanko) in early April, with Kebe Brema winning the first but eventually being forced to retreat.[^cite_note-18]

In 1904 a railroad was built connecting Bamako to [Kayes](1079700.md), and the city began growing quickly upon being named the capital of [French Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Sudan) in 1908.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEImperato199645-15] The [cercle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercles_of_Mali) of Mali at this time had around 160 000 inhabitants living in 4-500 villages.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPerinbam199917-19] [A railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar-Niger_railroad) connecting Bamako to [Dakar](44251.md) was completed in 1923.[^cite_note-decline-20]

Mali gained independence from France in April 1960, and the Republic of Mali was later established. At this time, Bamako had a population of around 160,000. During the 1960s, the country became socialist and Bamako was subject to [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) investment and influence.[^cite_note-AFN-11] However, the economy declined as state enterprises collapsed and unrest was widespread.[^cite_note-AFN-11] Eventually, [Moussa Traoré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Traor%C3%A9) led a successful coup and ruled Mali for 23 years. However his rule was characterised by severe droughts and poor government management and problems of food shortages.[^cite_note-AFN-11]

In the late 1980s the people of Bamako and Mali campaigned for a free-market economy and multiparty democracy. In 1990, the [National Congress for Democratic Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_for_Democratic_Initiative) (*Congrès National d'Initiative démocratique*, CNID) was set up by the lawyer [Mountaga Tall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountaga_Tall), and the [Alliance for Democracy in Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_Democracy_in_Mali) (*Alliance pour la démocratie au Mali*, ADEMA) by Abdramane Baba and historian [Alpha Oumar Konaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Oumar_Konar%C3%A9). These with the *Association des élèves et étudiants du Mali* (AEEM) and the [Association Malienne des Droits de l'Homme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_Malienne_des_Droits_de_l'Homme) (AMDH) aimed to oust Moussa Traoré. Under the old constitution, all labor unions had to belong to one confederation, the [National Union of Malian Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Union_of_Malian_Workers) (UNTM). When the leadership of the UNTM broke from the government in 1990, the opposition grew. Groups were driven by paycuts and layoffs in the government sector, and the Malian government acceding to pressure from international donors to privatise large swathes of the economy that had remained in public hands even after the overthrow of the socialist government in 1968. Students, even children, played an increasing role in the protest marches in Bamako, and homes and businesses of those associated with the regime were ransacked by crowds.

On 22 March 1991, a large-scale protest march in central Bamako was violently suppressed, with estimates of those killed reaching 300. Four days later, a military coup deposed Traoré. The *Comité de Transition pour le Salut du Peuple* was set up, headed by General [Amadou Toumani Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amadou_Toumani_Tour%C3%A9).[^cite_note-21] Alpha Oumar Konari officially became president on 26 April 1992.[^cite_note-AFN-11]

Bamako had been the target of numerous [jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist) [terrorist attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_attack) during the [Islamist insurgency in the Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamist_insurgency_in_the_Sahel).[^cite_note-ShiftingStrategy-22] On 20 November 2015, two gunmen [took 170 people hostage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Bamako_hotel_attack) in the [Radisson Blu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radisson_Blu) hotel. Twenty-one people were killed along with the two gunmen during the seven-hour siege.[^cite_note-23] In a [series of attacks on 17 September 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bamako_attacks), a group of gunmen from [Jama'at Nasr al-Islam wal-Muslimin](53401088.md) (JNIM), an [al-Qaeda](1921.md) affiliate, attacked a military training school, killing many [gendarme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarme) and trainees, and set aircraft ablaze at the [international airport](4587990.md).[^cite_note-ShiftingStrategy-22][^cite_note-PeltierTriebert-24] At least 77 people were killed.[^cite_note-ShiftingStrategy-22] This was the first jihadist attack on Bamako since 2016.[^cite_note-PeltierTriebert-24] JNIM is a successor to some of the organizations responsible for the 2015 attack.[^cite_note-ShiftingStrategy-22] The 2024 attack was downplayed by the Malian military junta (led by [Assimi Goïta](65000306.md)), which seized control over the country in a [2021 coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Malian_coup_d'%C3%A9tat).[^cite_note-ShiftingStrategy-22][^cite_note-PeltierTriebert-24] The [fuel blockade targeting Bamako](81764054.md)[^cite_note-25] and the [2026 offensive in Mali](83033878.md)[^cite_note-26] have also impacted Bamako.

## Geography



Pont des Martyrs

Bamako is situated on the Niger River floodplain, which hampers development along the riverfront and the Niger's tributaries. Bamako is relatively flat, except to the immediate north where an [escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escarpment) is found, being what remains of an extinct volcano. The Presidential Palace and main hospital are located here.

Road in Bamako. Kuluba hill, with the Presidential Palace, is in the background.

Originally, the city developed on the northern side of the river, but as it grew, bridges were developed to connect the north with the south. The first of these was the [Pont des Martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyrs_Bridge_\(Bamako\)) (2-lane with two pedestrian sections) and the [King Fahd Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Fahd_Bridge_\(Bamako\)) (four-lane with two motorcycle and two pedestrian sections). Additionally, a seasonal causeway between the eastern neighborhoods of Sotuba and Misabugu was inherited from colonial times (alternated traffic on one lane with five crossing sections). The [Sotuba Causeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotuba_Causeway?action=edit\&redlink=1) (*Chaussée submersible de Sotuba* in French, and *Babilikoroni* in [Bamanankan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_language)) is typically under water from July to January. A third bridge (1.4 km long, 24 m \[26 yd; 79 ft] wide, four-lane with two motorcycle and two pedestrian sections) was built at the same location[^cite_note-27] in 2011 in partnership with the Chinese government[^cite_note-28] to reduce downtown congestion, notably by trucks.

### Climate

Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), Bamako features a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Aw*). Located in the [Sudano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanian_Savanna)-[Sahelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) zone, Bamako is very hot on average all year round with the hottest months being between March and May. The mildest months are between November and February. During the dry season, rainfall is scarce: virtually none falls between November and April due to the dominance of the Saharan anticyclone and the dry [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_winds). The rainy season occurs in the summer with the peak occurring with a few storms beginning in May, then transitioning to the monsoon from June to October.

## Environment

### Waste Management Controversy

In 2015, the Bamako city government privatized waste collection in the city. Before the introduction of this program there was a long standing informal waste collection system carried out by "Economic interest groups."[^cite_note-35] Before the privatization these independent groups collected waste throughout the city. After privatization, not only did many waste collectors become unemployed but the corporation that they enlisted to do the job was collecting only 30% of Bamako's waste. The garbage build up creates toxic living conditions and it only worsens when it rains. Due to the prevalence of waste in Bamako's streets, including informal dumping sites near a school, citizens have taken to protesting, marching and it has even escalated to the point of property damage.[^cite_note-36]

## Administration

The District of Bamako has been divided into six communes (distinguished by numbers, and not named) since Ordinance No. 78-34/CNLM of 18 August 1978, and amended by a law in February 1982 establishing the new boundaries of Communes III and IV.[^cite_note-37] Each commune is administered by the municipal council and a mayor elected from among its members. The last elections were held on 26 April 2009 and the [Alliance for Democracy in Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_Democracy_in_Mali) hold the majority of representatives for the communes.

### Communes and neighborhoods

[Commune I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commune_I,_Bamako) has a population of 335,407 people (2009) and covers 35 square kilometres (14 sq mi). It is bounded to the north by the rural commune of [Djalakorodji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djalakorodji?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([Kati Cercle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kati_Cercle)), west by the Commune II, north-east by the rural commune of [Sangarebougou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangarebougou) (Kati Cercle), on the east by the rural commune of [Gabakourou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabakourou?action=edit\&redlink=1) and south by the Niger River. Nine neighborhoods comprise this commune: Banconi, Boulkassombougou, Djelibougou, Doumanzana Fadjiguila, Sotuba Korofina North, and South Korofina Sikoroni.[^cite_note-38]

[Commune II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commune_II,_Bamako) has a population of 159,805 people (2009) and covers 18.3 square kilometres (7.1 sq mi). It is bounded to the east by the backwater of Korofina at the west foot of the Point G hill, and to the south by [Niger River](21437.md). The municipality has eleven neighborhoods: Niaréla (the oldest), Bagadadji, Medina-Coura, Bozola, Missira, Hippodrome, Quinzambougou, Bakaribougou, TSF, Industrial Area, and Bougouba. The new [Cité du Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cit%C3%A9_Niger) island is also located in the Commune II.[^cite_note-39] The area is the most important in the industry sector in Bamako.

[Commune III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commune_III,_Bamako) has a population of 128,872 people (2009) and covers 20.7 square kilometres (8.0 sq mi). It is bounded on the north by the [Kati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kati), east by the Boulevard du Peuple, which separates it from the Commune II, south by the portion of the Niger River, between the Pont des Martyrs and the Motel de Bamako, and west by the [Farako River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farako_River?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Avenue Cheick Zayed El Mahyan Ben Sultan with the neighborhood of ACI-2000. Commune III is the administrative and commercial center of Bamako. It accommodates in particular the two largest markets in the capital, the Grand Market and Dibida. Twenty neighborhoods make up this commune and the villages of Koulouninko and Sirakorodounfing were attached to the Commune III.[^cite_note-40]

[Commune IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commune_IV,_Bamako) has a population of 300,085 people (2009) and covers 42 square kilometres (16 sq mi). It is bounded to the east by Commune III, north, west by Kati Cercle and south by the left bank of the Niger River. Commune IV consists of eight neighborhoods: Taliko, Lassa, Sibiribougou, Djikoroni Para, Sébénikoro, Hamdallaye, Lafiabougou and Kalabambougou.[^cite_note-41]

[Commune V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commune_V,_Bamako) has a population of 414,668 people (2009) and covers 42 square kilometres (16 sq mi).[^cite_note-42] It is bounded to the north by the Niger River, south by the airport and the commune of [Kalabancoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalabancoro), and to the east by the Commune VI and Niger. It consists of eight neighborhoods: Badalabougou, Sema I, Quartier Mali, Torokorobougou, Baco-Djicoroni, Sabalibougou, Daoudabougou and Kalaban-Coura.



Aerial view of neighbourhood ACI 2000

[Commune VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commune_VI,_Bamako) has a population of 470,269 people (2009) and covers 87 square kilometres (34 sq mi). This is the largest of the communes that make up Bamako. It consists of ten neighborhoods: Banankabougou, Djanékéla, Faladié, Magnambougou, Missabougou, Niamakoro, Sénou, Sogoniko, Sokorodji and Yrimadio.

Bamako contains the following neighborhoods (quartiers): ACI-2000, Badalabugu, Bajalan I, Bajalan II, Bako Jikoroni, Bagadaji, Bamako Kura, Bankoni, Bolibana, Bozola, Bugudani, Bulkasumbugu, Dar Salam, N'tomikorobougou, Dawdabugu, Dravela, Fajigila, Falaje, Garantigibugu, Jalakoroji, Janekela, Janjigila, Jelibugu, Jikoroni Para, Jumanzana, Hamdallaye, [Hippodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippodrome,_Bamako), Kalaban Koro, Kalaban Kura, Korofina, Kuluba, Kulubleni, Lafiabugu, Madina Kura, Magnambugu (Magnambugu Faso Kanu), Misabugu, Misira, Niarela, Ntomikorobugu, Point G, Quartier du Fleuve, Quartier Mali, Quinzanbugu, Sabalibugu I, Sabalibugu II, Safo, Same, Sangarebugu, Saranbugu, Sebeninkoro, Sikoroni, Sirakoro, Senu, Sibiribugu, Sokoniko, Sokoroji, Sotuba, Titibugu, Torokorobugu, TSF-Sans Fil, Wolofobugu, Yirimanjo, Zone Industrielle

## Economy



Craft sellers set up their wares at the *zone artisanal* in Bamako city centre.

### Industry

The District of Bamako concentrates 70% of industrial activity in the country.[^cite_note-43] [^cite_note-44] The service sector is the most developed, and the city thrives in crafts and trade.

The traditional commercial center of Bamako was located to the north of the river, and contained within a triangle bounded by Avenue du Fleuve, Rue Baba Diarra, and Boulevard du Peuple. This area contains the Marché Rose and Street Market.

The downtown area is highly congested, polluted, and expensive, and urbanization is sprawling at a rapid pace within a radius of 30 kilometres (19 mi). The largest urbanized area now lies on the southern bank of the Niger River. A modern central business district is rapidly developing immediately west of the downtown area in the ACI-2000 district, taking advantage of a well-designed geometric layout, legacy of the old airport runways and taxiways. A large administrative area is being developed at the junction between ACI-2000 and the King Fadh Bridge, containing most of the state departments (ministries) and administrative services in a central location. Bamako is also the headquarters of many large companies and administrative institutions. [Air Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Mali_\(2005\)) (formerly Compagnie Aérienne du Mali) has its head office in Bamako.[^cite_note-45] Bamako received much investment by [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) for decades which saw a number of important structures being built. In recent years, China has become an important investor in Bamako, developing its infrastructure and facilities.



Cattle crossing a road in Bamako

Agriculture is active in Bamako, with the presence of [Bozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozo_people) fisherman, and it is common to see cattle crossing the streets.

Much of the electricity is generated from the hydroelectric [Sélingué Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9lingu%C3%A9_Dam). The drinking water supply in Bamako and [Kati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kati) is also ensured by a pumping station on the Niger River. ## Architecture



Ministry buildings

The [BCEAO Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCEAO_Tower_\(Bamako\)) at 20 stories is the tallest building in the West African nation. It sits on the north ("left") bank of the River Niger in the city centre.[^cite_note-Velton2006-46] The BCEAO Tower is the Malian headquarters of the [Central Bank of West African States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_West_African_States), which provides development banking and government financial and currency services in several [Francophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francophone) [West African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_African) nations. Classified as [Neo-Sudanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Sudanic?action=edit\&redlink=1) architecture, it is modeled on the [Sudano-Sahelian architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudano-Sahelian_architecture) of the famous mosques of [Djenne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djenne) and [Timbuktu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu). The building is located in the busy [Commune III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commune_III_\(Bamako\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) neighbourhood, where "Avenue Moussa Tavele" meets the waterside boulevard between the two main Bamako bridges: [King Fahd Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Fahd_Bridge) a block west and [Martyrs Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyrs_Bridge_\(Bamako\)) three blocks east. Just to the east of the BCEAO complex, a park and formal garden mark where the diagonally running "Boulevard du Peuple" reaches the river. By contrast, small market gardens and launching points or river canoes lie along the river front.[^cite_note-Velton2006-46]

The [Cité Administrative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cit%C3%A9_Administrative_\(Bamako\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Administrative City) is a complex of buildings located just west of the northern end of the King Fahd Bridge. The complex was begun in 2003 by then President [Konaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Oumar_Konar%C3%A9) with the help of funding from the government of Libya. The 10-hectare (25-acre) Cité Administrative was completed in 2010 and houses many of the offices of the government.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

Bamako has experienced staggering population growth. In 1884, it had only 2,500 inhabitants, 8,000 in 1908, 37,000 in 1945, and 100,000 in 1960. Today, the population is over 42 times what it was in 1960, with a population of 4,227,569 recorded at the 2022 census, and continues to attract a rural population in search of work.[^cite_note-51] This uncontrolled growth has caused significant difficulties in terms of traffic, sanitation (including access to safe water), and pollution. Bamako hosts a diverse population composed of different ethnic groups in Mali and from neighboring countries.[^cite_note-censusculture-50]

The city is dominated by the Bambara, the largest ethnicity in Mali, who make up over 40% of the population. The Bambara language is the dominant language of the city, spoken by nearly two-thirds of the population.[^cite_note-censusculture-50]

## Culture

The [National Library of Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_Mali) was first created by the [Institut Français d'Afrique Noire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Fran%C3%A7ais_d'Afrique_Noire), an arm of the French colonial government, in 1944. Following Mali's 1960 independence, this library became the Government Library; it would later be renamed again as the National Library of Mali. In 1968, the library was transferred from its initial home in Koulouba to Ouolofobougou, a section of Bamako. The library holds more than 60,000 works, including books, periodicals, audio documents, videos, and software. These materials are available free to the public, though a small subscription fee is required for borrowing privileges. The library also hosts some of the exhibits for [African Photography Encounters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Photography_Encounters), a biannual Bamako photography festival.[^cite_note-Kent-52]



The [National Museum of Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Mali) buildings, designed by architect [Jean-Loup Pivin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Loup_Pivin?action=edit\&redlink=1)

The [National Museum of Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Mali) is an archeological and anthropological museum, presenting permanent and temporary exhibits on the prehistory of Mali, as well as the musical instruments, dress, and ritual objects associated with Mali's various [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_group). The National Museum began under French rule as the Sudanese Museum, part of the [Institut Français d'Afrique Noire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_Fran%C3%A7ais_d'Afrique_Noire) (IFAN) under [Théodore Monod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%A9odore_Monod). It was opened on 14 February 1953, under the direction of [Ukrainian](31750.md) archeologist [Yuriy Shumovskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuriy_Shumovskyi). Shumovskyi had worked in the museum for nine years, gathering half (nearly 3,000) of today's finds. The museum also houses indoor and outdoor exhibitions of works by local artists. With the independence of the Republic of Mali in 1960, the Sudanese Museum became the National Museum of Mali, with the new objectives of promoting national unity and celebrating Malian traditional culture. However, lack of financial means and absence of qualified personnel caused some deterioration in the museum's collections. On 30 March 1956, the National Museum moved into a new [cemented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cement) structure, created by architect [Jean-Loup Pivin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Loup_Pivin?action=edit\&redlink=1) from traditional Malian designs. Since the 1996 election of former archaeologist [Alpha Oumar Konaré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Oumar_Konar%C3%A9) to Mali's presidency, the museum's funding has increased considerably, becoming among the best in West Africa. The museum often hosts part of the biannual [African Photography Encounters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Photography_Encounters), photography festival held in Bamako since 1994.

Also of note is the [Muso Kunda Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muso_Kunda_Museum), the [Bamako Regional Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamako_Regional_Museum?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Bamako Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamako_Zoo?action=edit\&redlink=1), the [Bamako Botanical Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamako_Botanical_Gardens?action=edit\&redlink=1), the National Conference Center Tower (NCC), the Souvenir Pyramid, the Independence Monument, Al Quoods Monument, the triangular *Monument de la paix*, the Hamdallaye obelisk, the Modibo Keita Memorial and many other monuments, the [Palais de la Culture Amadou Hampaté Ba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_de_la_Culture_Amadou_Hampat%C3%A9_Ba) and the [Point G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_G?action=edit\&redlink=1) hill, containing caves with [rock paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_art).

In 1988, Bamako was the location of a [WHO](33583.md) conference known as the [Bamako Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamako_Initiative) that helped reshape health policy of [sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md). The yearly held [Budapest-Bamako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest-Bamako) rally has the endpoint in Bamako, with the [Dakar Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar_Rally) often passing through Bamako.

### Music

A music boom in Bamako took off in the 1990s, when vocalist [Salif Keita](734004.md) and singer-guitarist [Ali Farka Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Farka_Tour%C3%A9) achieved international fame.[^cite_note-NYT_06-53] It attracted a number of tourists, record producers, and aspiring musicians to the city to try to follow in their footsteps. It is common to see musicians in the streets with *[djembes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djembe)* and percussion bands playing traditional Bamana rhythms.[^cite_note-NYT_06-53]

## Transport



This is a [sotrama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_taxi#Sotrama) stand. The sotrama (taxi van) is what is used as public transportation, many being owned independently.

The [Dakar-Niger Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar-Niger_Railway) links Bamako to [Dakar](44251.md) via [Kati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kati), [Négala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9gala?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Kita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kita,_Mali), and [Kayes](1079700.md).

In 2015, a rail link to [San-Pédro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San-P%C3%A9dro,_Ivory_Coast) in [Ivory Coast](57744.md) is proposed.[^cite_note-56]

The road network links Bamako to [Koulikoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koulikoro), Kati, [Kolokani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolokani), [Ségou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9gou), and [Sikasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikasso).



Bamako-Sénou International Airport

The [Bamako-Sénou International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamako-S%C3%A9nou_International_Airport) is 15 kilometres (9 mi) from the city and opened to passengers in 1974. Passenger traffic steadily increased in the early 2000s. Government figures revealed 403,380 passengers in 1999, 423,506 in 2003, 486,526 in 2004, and 516,000 in 2005, and is predicted to reach over 900,000 by 2015 under a low (4%) yearly growth-rate scenario.[^cite_note-mcamali2006-57] To date this growth rate has been surpassed. Total air traffic the airport increased by 12.4% in 2007 and 14% in 2008. Most of this increase came in passenger transport, with the number of passengers served increasing by 20% in 2007 and 17% in 2008. Twenty-seven airline carriers operated weekly or better at Bamako-Sénou International Airport in the 2007–2008 period. This continued growth was offset by cargo flights' decline of 16.75% in 2007, and 3.93% in 2008.[^cite_note-pana0109-58] The highest frequency route is on the Bamako-Dakar sector with 29 weekly non-stop connections. Domestic flights also serve Mali's regional capitals [Kayes](1079700.md), [Mopti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mopti), [Timbuktu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu), [Sikasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikasso), [Gao](1255721.md), and [Kidal](1463133.md). Bamako Senou International Airport is managed by [Aéroports du Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9roports_du_Mali) (ADM).[^cite_note-mcamali2006-57] Its operations are overseen by the Malian Ministry of Equipment and Transports.[^cite_note-pana0109-58]

Today, with the new administrative arrangement,[^cite_note-59] the territory of Mali will henceforth comprise twenty (20) Regions instead of eight (08), plus the District of Bamako which will henceforth comprise ten (10) urban communes instead of six (06).[^cite_note-60]

Much of the transportation is either by the Niger River, or by paved roads linking Bamako to other major urban areas. Navigating the river is possible from Koulikoro to Mopti and Gao. The bush taxi is one of the main modes of transport.



Bridge of Friendship between China and Mali.

Bamako is situated on both sides of the Niger River and three bridges connect the two banks: the Bridge of Martyrs completed in 1960 and renamed in memory of protesters killed in March 1991 by the regime of [Moussa Traoré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Traor%C3%A9), the [King Fahd Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Fahd_Bridge_\(Bamako\)), named after the Saudi Arabian donor, and a third bridge, the *Pont de l'amitié sino-malienne* funded by the People's Republic of China. Located in Sotuba area, it has the objective to decongest traffic in the city.[^cite_note-61]

## Healthcare

The Point G hospital, built between 1906 and 1913, covers 25 hectares (62 acres). A former military hospital, it became a civilian hospital shortly before the independence of Mali, and is situated on a hill overlooking Bamako.[^cite_note-62]

The second hospital of Bamako is the Gabriel Touré Hospital named after a young doctor and humanist Gabriel Touré who was born in 1910 in [Ouagadougou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouagadougou) and died in 1935 after having been contaminated by a patient with the [pneumonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumonic_plague). The hospital was established in 1959.[^cite_note-63]

The contract for the building of a new hospital in Bamako, to relieve pressure on the other hospital resources was signed on 27 December 2008. Located in the district of [Yirimadio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yirimadio?action=edit\&redlink=1), the department will include a pediatric and obstetrics-gynecology facilities, a department of internal medicine, medical imagery facilities and hospital care with 150 beds to support the emergency services and intensive care. This hospital, like many recent developments in Bamako is financed and equipped with Chinese investment.[^cite_note-64]

## In popular culture

Bamako has provided the backdrop or been the subject of books and films such as *[Bamako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamako_\(film\))*, directed by [Abderrahmane Sissako](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abderrahmane_Sissako). The film depicts a trial taking place in Bamako, amid the daily life that is going on in the city. In the midst of that trial, two sides argue whether the [World Bank](36752.md) and [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), or perhaps corruption, are guilty of the financial state of many poverty-stricken African countries. The film was first released at the Cannes Film Festival on 21 May 2006 and in [Manhattan](45470.md) by [New Yorker Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Yorker_Films) on 14 February 2007 and was the recipient of the first [Film Award of the Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_Award_of_the_Council_of_Europe) given at the [Istanbul International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_International_Film_Festival) in April 2007.[^cite_note-65]
