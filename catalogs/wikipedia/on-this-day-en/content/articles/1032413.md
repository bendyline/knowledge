The **Uzbekistan national football team** ([Uzbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_language): *Oʻzbekiston milliy futbol terma jamoasi*, Ўзбекистон миллий футбол терма жамоаси) represents [Uzbekistan](31853.md) in men's international [football](10568.md) and is controlled by the [Uzbekistan Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan_Football_Association), the governing body for [football in Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Uzbekistan).

Uzbekistan holds the highest competitive results among teams from [Central Asia](6742.md). At the [2011 Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup_2011), Uzbekistan reached the semi-finals of the tournament for the first time. At other competitions such as the [Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Asian_Games), Uzbekistan won the gold medal in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1994_Asian_Games) in [Japan](15573.md), while finishing as the runners-up at the [Afro-Asian Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Asian_Cup_of_Nations) in 1995. They qualified for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) for the first time in [2026](36581929.md), becoming the first [Central Asian](6742.md) country and the third post-Soviet state after Russia and Ukraine to qualify for the tournament.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

### Early history

Since 1992, Uzbekistan gained independence and a new Uzbek national team was organized. The national team held its first match in 1992, a game against [Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajikistan_national_football_team), in the framework of the [1992 Central Asian Cup](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9A%D1%83%D0%B1%D0%BE%D0%BA%20%D0%A6%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B9%20%D0%90%D0%B7%D0%B8%D0%B8%20%D0%BF%D0%BE%20%D1%84%D1%83%D1%82%D0%B1%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%83%201992) (the tournament was held once, initiated by [FIFA](11049.md)). In 1992, Uzbekistan was also a member of the [CIS national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIS_national_football_team) and instead participated in [Euro 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1992). The Uzbek national team did not play the following year.

Most of the republics became members of [UEFA](32332.md) ([Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_national_football_team), [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus_national_football_team), [Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova_national_football_team), [Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia_national_football_team), [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania_national_football_team), [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia_national_football_team), [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_national_football_team), [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia_national_football_team) and [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_national_football_team)). While Uzbekistan also wanted to become a member of UEFA, the rest of [Central Asia](6742.md) ([Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan_national_football_team), [Kyrgyzstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyzstan_national_football_team), [Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajikistan_national_football_team) and [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmenistan_national_football_team)), chose the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md). In 2002, [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan_Football_Federation) became a member of UEFA for football development, but Uzbekistan chose to remain in the AFC.

In 1994, the [Uzbekistan Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekistan_Football_Federation) was officially adopted by the AFC and [FIFA](11049.md). In the same year Uzbekistan won the [Uzbekistan Independence Cup](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9A%D1%83%D0%B1%D0%BE%D0%BA%20%D0%9D%D0%B5%D0%B7%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%81%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B8%20%D0%A3%D0%B7%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BA%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B0%20%D0%BF%D0%BE%20%D1%84%D1%83%D1%82%D0%B1%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%83%201994).

In the [1994 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1994_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament), Uzbekistan were grouped with powerhouse [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team), [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_national_football_team) and [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_national_football_team), alongside [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_national_football_team). After a 4–1 victory over the Saudis, Uzbekistan followed up with a 5–0 victory over Malaysia and a 1–0 win over Hong Kong before winning the group with a 5–4 win against Thailand. In the quarter-finals, Uzbekistan defeated [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmenistan_national_football_team) 3–0 to advance to the semi-finals where they faced [South Korea](1018627.md). The Korean side included nine players that had already participated in the [1994 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup); despite this, Uzbekistan shocked South Korea with a 1–0 victory to advance to the final, its first ever final since gaining independence. In their final game against [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_football_team), Uzbekistan defeated the Chinese 4–2 to capture its first and only Asian honor two years after its creation.[^cite_note-futbolgrad-4]

In the [1996 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_AFC_Asian_Cup), Uzbekistan was grouped with Japan, China and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_national_football_team), and were eliminated in the group stage. Uzbekistan's first-ever attempt to qualify for the World Cup was in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)). Grouped with [Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_national_football_team), [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia_national_football_team) and [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia_national_football_team) in the first stage, Uzbekistan won every match except for the away fixture against Indonesia. Shortly after, the Uzbeks played [South Korea](1018627.md), [Japan](993546.md) and the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team), and failed to qualify for the tournament.

### 2000s

The [2000 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_AFC_Asian_Cup) saw the Uzbeks with Japan, Saudi Arabia and [Qatar](1111350.md), gaining only a point against the latter with a draw. Uzbekistan participated in the [first round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_first_round) in order to qualify for the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) in Japan and South Korea, and eventually made the [second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round). The group contained [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_football_team), the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team), [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_national_football_team) and [Qatar](1111350.md), and the Uzbeks were unable to make any major breakthrough in the second round, losing twice to the UAE, and suffering two away defeats to China and Oman. An away draw to Qatar confirmed Uzbekistan's failure to qualify, despite a late win over already qualified China in the final match.

Uzbekistan failed to make further impact on the continental stage until they reached the last eight of the [2004 Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_AFC_Asian_Cup), topping their group after winning all matches, where they were beaten by [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team) after a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md). The country would eventually defeat [Iraq](1039652.md) in the [second qualifying round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round#Group_2) for the [2006 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) in [Germany](11867.md), with goals from [Maksim Shatskikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maksim_Shatskikh) and [Alexander Geynrikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Geynrikh) sending them through to the last eight. They were knocked out in the [final stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round) of the [Asian qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)) to the 2006 World Cup after losing on the [away goals rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Away_goals_rule) to [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team). The result was subject to controversy as three games were played; the first, a 1–0 win for Uzbekistan was wiped out after FIFA declared the result void after a mistake by [Toshimitsu Yoshida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshimitsu_Yoshida), a Japanese referee.[^cite_note-5] The replay ended 1–1, and after the return finished 0–0, Uzbekistan were eliminated.

In the [2007 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup), Uzbekistan advanced from the [group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup_Group_C) by beating [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_national_football_team) 5–0 and [China PR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_PR_national_football_team) 3–0. However, Uzbekistan was knocked out of the tournament in the [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage#Saudi_Arabia_vs_Uzbekistan) by losing to [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) 2–1.

### 2010s



Uzbekistan national team supporters during a qualification match for the [2010 World Cup](168079.md) against Japan at [Pakhtakor Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakhtakor_Stadium) in Tashkent

After having three foreign coaches (German [Hans-Jürgen Gede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-J%C3%BCrgen_Gede), Englishman [Bob Houghton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Houghton) and Russian [Valeri Nepomniachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeri_Nepomniachi)) in three years, the country turned to former Uzbekistan Olympic team coach [Rauf Inileev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rauf_Inileev). During [qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) for the [2010 World Cup](168079.md), Uzbekistan advanced to the [fourth round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round) of the [Asian qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)) after winning their first four matches, but finished last in [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round#Group_A) of the final round behind favorites Australia, Japan, Bahrain and Qatar, with four points from eight matches.



Uzbekistan at the [2011 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup)

In the [2011 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup), Uzbekistan ended in fourth place, their all-time best result in the tournament. After getting past the [group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup_Group_A) and [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage#Uzbekistan_vs_Jordan), the Uzbek team lost their first opportunity to reach the Asian Cup final when [Australia](4792249.md) thrashed them 6–0 in the [semi-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage#Uzbekistan_vs_Australia) game. Several days later, they were defeated again by [South Korea](1018627.md) in the [third place match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage#Third_place_match) 3–2.[^cite_note-6]

In [qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) for the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md), Uzbekistan advanced to Asia's [fourth round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round) after winning their group in the [third round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round#Group_C) over Japan. Uzbekistan finished with 16 points (five wins and one draw), more than any other team in the third round, including an impressive 1–0 away win against Japan. In the fourth round, Uzbekistan finished third in [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round#Group_A) behind [Iran](272865.md) and South Korea, and lost to South Korea (14 points) on goal difference, and would eventually play [Jordan](1090065.md) in the [fifth round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fifth_round) to determine the AFC participant in the [intercontinental play-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(inter-confederation_play-offs\)). On 10 September 2013, with the two teams drawn at the end of the second leg, Jordan eventually progressed to the intercontinental play-off after winning 9–8 on [penalties](511302.md).

In the [2015 Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup), Uzbekistan advanced to the [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage) after finishing as runners-up in [Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_Group_B), won by China. The team was knocked out of the tournament in the [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage#South_Korea_vs_Uzbekistan) after losing 2–0 in [extra time](633242.md) to South Korea.

Uzbekistan continued their quest to head to the World Cup during [2018 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)), but their campaign had been shattered with a 4–2 defeat to [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team). However, the Uzbeks soon bounced back and won the last remaining matches to top the group and qualified to the [2019 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup) as well as the last round. Once again, Uzbekistan in the last round, missed an opportunity when they finished fourth, behind Iran, South Korea and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_national_football_team), when Uzbekistan could only manage a 0–0 draw to the South Koreans in the last match.[^cite_note-espn-7]



Uzbekistan at the [2019 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup)

Uzbekistan started their [2019 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup) campaign with a 2–1 victory over Oman and continued with a 4–0 win over Turkmenistan, which guaranteed Uzbekistan to progress from the group stage for the fifth consecutive time, despite ending with a 1–2 defeat to Japan in the last match. Facing Australia, defending Asian champions, Uzbekistan drew 0–0 after 120 minutes. In the penalty shootout, Australia prevailed with a 4–2 win.

### 2020s



Uzbekistan national football team with President of Uzbekistan after World Cup qualifying match against Qatar on 10 June 2025

In the [second qualifying round for the 2022 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round), Uzbekistan suffered another disappointment by failing to qualify for the [final round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round). The *White Wolves* accumulated five wins and three defeats. Moreover, Uzbekistan's failure was also compounded by the fact that [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team), originally in Group H of the same round, surprisingly withdrew due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), in which FIFA and the AFC then ruled North Korea's results as null and void. This caused [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_national_football_team) to qualify ahead of Uzbekistan, since Lebanon's original results against North Korea were a draw and a loss.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-AFC_May_update-10]



Uzbekistan squad against [Iran](272865.md) at the [third round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round) of the [2026 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)).

Uzbekistan took part in the [third qualifying round for the 2023 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_AFC_Asian_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_third_round). Designated as the host country of Group C (due to the [COVID-19 pandemic in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Asia)), the Central Asians won all three games and finished top of their group without conceding a goal, confirming their qualification for the [tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_AFC_Asian_Cup). Uzbekistan was placed in Group B with Australia, Syria and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_national_football_team). Prior to the tournament, Uzbek all-time top goalscorer Eldor Shomurodov sustained a leg injury, ruling him out of the squad. Uzbekistan drew 0–0 in their opening game against [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_national_football_team) and won against India 3–0. In their last match against Australia, Uzbekistan drew 1–1 to seal a place in the round of 16 as the second-placed team in their group. They faced Thailand, whom they managed to beat 2–1. In the quarter-finals, they faced hosts Qatar. With the match drawn 1–1 after extra time, Uzbekistan lost 3–2 in the penalty shoot-out.[^cite_note-11]

Uzbekistan were unbeaten throughout their entire [second qualifying round for the 2026 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round), winning four matches and drawing two, and entered the [third qualifying round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round) as second place in the group. In the [third round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round), the team qualified for the [World Cup](36581929.md) for the first time in history, with one match to spare after an away goalless draw with the [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team).[^cite_note-12] This makes Uzbekistan the first [Central Asian](6742.md) nation, as well as the third former [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team) after [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team) and [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_national_football_team), to qualify for the World Cup.[^cite_note-13]

Uzbekistan entered the [2025 CAFA Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_CAFA_Nations_Cup), where the team won the title after a draw against [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_national_football_team) and victories over [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmenistan_national_football_team), [Kyrgyzstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyzstan_national_football_team) and a last-minute extra-time victory over [Iran](272865.md) 1–0 in the final.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

### Nicknames



Uzbekistan vs. Bahrain at Pakhtakor Central Stadium in 2009

The Uzbekistan national team has received several nicknames by supporters and media. The most common one used is "The White Wolves" ([Uzbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_language): *Oq boʻrilar* */ Оқ бўрилар*),[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] while also being called "Asian Italy"[^cite_note-20] (Uzbek: *Osiyo Italiyasi* */ Осиё Италияси*) due to the similarity of colors (white and blue) of [Italy](362466.md) and Uzbekistan, as well as similar tactics (defensive football) of these teams.[^cite_note-21]

Uzbekistan is also called the "[Huma birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huma_bird)" (Uzbek: *Humo qushlari* */ Ҳумо қушлари*); the mythical Huma bird is the national bird of Uzbekistan, and is depicted on the [state emblem of Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblem_of_Uzbekistan). The Huma bird is depicted on the emblem of the [National Olympic Committee of the Republic of Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Olympic_Committee_of_the_Republic_of_Uzbekistan). Another nickname is the "Turanians"[^cite_note-22] (Uzbek: *Turonliklar* */ Туронликлар*), as Uzbekistan is located in the center of the ancient region of [Turan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turan).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

### Rivalries



Uzbekistani fans at the [2019 Asian Cup in UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup)

[Kazakhstan national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan_national_football_team) is considered to be the main rival of the Uzbekistan national team. This rivalry dates back to the time of the Soviet Union, when both countries were part of the [15 republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_the_Soviet_Union) that made up the Union. The rivalry between Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan was evident at all levels, from grandiose construction projects to football. The two countries' top football clubs, [Pakhtakor Tashkent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakhtakor_FC) and [Kairat Almaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Kairat), competed for the title of the strongest club in [Central Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Central_Asia). In the 1990s, both countries were part of the AFC, and Uzbekistan performed better than Kazakhstan. In 2002, Kazakhstan joined the UEFA, and the intensity of the rivalry decreased as the national teams and clubs primarily played friendly matches. However, fans and the football community in Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan continue to compare their national teams and clubs. Objectively, Uzbekistan has surpassed Kazakhstan in terms of football development, becoming the main football country in the region. During the Soviet era, Uzbekistan's national team also had some rivalry with [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_national_football_team) and [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_national_football_team).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

In addition to Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan competes with the national team of [Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajikistan_national_football_team) from the [Central Asian](6742.md) countries, as well as to a lesser extent with the national teams of [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmenistan_national_football_team) and [Kyrgyzstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyzstan_national_football_team). Over the past 15 years, rivalry with the [Iran national team](272865.md) has become especially important, and the Iran national team can be considered Uzbekistan's second main rival. The matches against [South Korea](1018627.md), [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team), [Qatar](1111350.md) and the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE_national_football_team) are also of great importance.[^cite_note-27]

## Home stadium



[Milliy Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milliy_Stadium)



[Pakhtakor Central Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakhtakor_Central_Stadium)

From its formation in 1992 until the end of 2012, the main home stadium of the Uzbekistan national football team was the [Pakhtakor Central Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakhtakor_Central_Stadium) in [Tashkent](57533.md), built and opened in 1956. This stadium is also the venue for [Pakhtakor Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakhtakor_Tashkent_FK). During the Soviet Union, this stadium was home for the Uzbekistan SSR national team. It was reconstructed in 1996, 2008 and 2012 and currently holds 35,000 spectators, previously at 55,000 spectators. The team also uses [Milliy Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milliy_Stadium) (formerly known as Bunyodkor Stadium), built in 2012 and accommodating 34,000 spectators. This stadium is also a home for the [Bunyodkor Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bunyodkor).
