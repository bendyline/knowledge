[Donald Trump](4848272.md), the 45th and 47th [president of the United States](24113.md), developed a social and professional relationship with financier and child sex offender [Jeffrey Epstein](6253522.md) that began in the late 1980s and continued into at least the early 2000s.[^cite_note-Reinhard-2019-1][^cite_note-Epstein-2025-2][^cite_note-Broadwater-2025-3][^cite_note-Gabbatt-2025-4] During Trump's prior careers as a [businessman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_career_of_Donald_Trump) and [media personality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_career_of_Donald_Trump) before [entering politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_career_of_Donald_Trump) in 2015, he and Epstein visited each other's real estate properties regularly. Trump and Epstein socialized frequently throughout the 1990s and early 2000s, including attending parties at Trump's [Mar-a-Lago](1452754.md) resort in Florida and Epstein's residence. Flight logs released during an associate's trial confirm that Trump flew on Epstein's private jet multiple times in the 1990s, and according to Epstein, Trump first had sex with his future wife [Melania Knauss](1209075.md) in his private jet. Trump had a falling out with Epstein around 2004 and ceased contact. After Epstein was said to have sexually harassed a teenage daughter of another Mar-a-Lago member in 2007, Trump banned him from the club. Epstein said, in a 2019 email attributed by [NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News) and *[Axios](52436164.md)* to the event, that he had never been a member of the club.

Epstein was convicted of procuring a person under the age of 18 for prostitution in 2008. Since Epstein's 2019 arrest for [sex trafficking](2101268.md) of minors and his [death in prison](61499571.md) shortly thereafter, their former relationship has come under further scrutiny, particularly during and after Trump's [re-election as president](59568546.md) in 2024. Trump has denied any knowledge of Epstein's criminal activities and distanced himself from the deceased financier in the years before Epstein's arrest and death. Trump promoted unsubstantiated conspiracy theories about the circumstances and suggested Epstein was murdered. Media attention and public pressure mounted in 2025, as the [Trump administration](78298108.md) had not released [files relating to Epstein](80415168.md), despite Trump promising to do so during [his 2024 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_2024_presidential_campaign).

On November 12, 2025, Democrats on the [House Oversight Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Oversight_Committee) released thousands of emails authored by Epstein and his associates. Epstein had claimed in a number of private email exchanges that he had damaging information on Trump. In a 2011 email to his accomplice [Ghislaine Maxwell](32018562.md), Epstein said that Trump had spent hours at his house with [Virginia Giuffre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Giuffre) and that Trump was "the dog that didn't bark".[^cite_note-Gold-2025-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Although Giuffre revealed that Epstein and Maxwell had recruited her from Mar-a-Lago into a [sex trafficking](2101268.md) ring, she did not accuse Trump himself of wrongdoing. Maxwell claimed in 2025 that Trump was not a "close friend" of Epstein, contradicting Epstein's previous statements, and that she never witnessed Trump behave inappropriately. No criminal wrongdoing has ever been established against Trump in connection with Epstein's crimes. A [sexually suggestive birthday note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Epstein's_birthday_book), allegedly written by Trump to Epstein in 2003, included a crude drawing; Trump has denied its legitimacy.

The two chambers of the [U.S. Congress](31756.md) passed the [Epstein Files Transparency Act](81594904.md) on November 18, 2025, forcing the [Department of Justice](52563.md) to release eligible documents related to Epstein and his associates. Trump had opposed the bill for months, but expressed support for it on the day before Congress approved it, and then he signed it. The White House stated that the release of these emails was only to create "[fake news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_news)" against Trump and was actually a reaction from Democrats to the government shutdown. Democratic Representative [Ro Khanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ro_Khanna) from California stated that the purpose of releasing the files was not to attack Trump.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-Macaya_11/12/2025-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-Collins-2025-11] In June 2025, Trump associate [Elon Musk](909036.md) also claimed that the reason for not releasing the Epstein files was because Trump's name was in the documents, a claim that the Department of Justice denied.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] On January 31, 2026, Trump said he would sue writer [Michael Wolff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Wolff_\(journalist\)) and "maybe [the Epstein estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estate_of_Jeffrey_Epstein), I guess" for allegedly conspiring against Trump's political aspirations, again claiming that Epstein was "not a friend", after *[The New York Times](30680.md)* had asserted a month earlier that they were once best friends.[^cite_note-Galbraith_2/1/2026-14][^cite_note-Confessore_Tate_12/18/2025-15]

## History

Since the 1970s, at least 28 women have [accused Donald Trump of sexual misconduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump_sexual_misconduct_allegations),[^cite_note-16] for acts that have included rape, kissing and groping without consent; looking under women's skirts; and walking in on naked teenage pageant contestants. Trump has denied all of the allegations. He has a history of insulting and [belittling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimisation_\(psychology\)) women when speaking to the media and on social media,[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-Shear-2018-18] and has made [lewd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lascivious_behavior) comments about women, disparaged their physical appearance, and referred to them using derogatory epithets.[^cite_note-Shear-2018-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

[Jeffrey Epstein](6253522.md) cultivated an [elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite) social circle[^cite_note-21] and [procured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procuring_\(prostitution\)) many women and children whom he and his associates sexually abused.[^cite_note-22] In addition to his own sexual abuse of the victims, Epstein directed other persons to abuse the girls sexually. Epstein used paid employees to find and bring minor girls to him. Epstein worked in concert with others to obtain minors not only for his own sexual gratification, but also for the sexual gratification of others. His close associate [Ghislaine Maxwell](32018562.md) was later implicated as [procuring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procuring_\(prostitution\)) or recruiting underage girls in addition to being, for an extended period, Epstein's chief girlfriend.

### Early years (1980s–1992)



Exterior of Mar-a-Lago in 1999

Trump said that his friendship with Epstein started in the late 1980s. The two men were neighbors and would visit each other's properties.[^cite_note-Staff-2025-23][^cite_note-Reinhard-2019-1] On the July 16, 2025, episode of *[Erin Burnett Outfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Burnett_Outfront)*, Jack O'Donnell, who served as COO of Trump Plaza and Casino from 1987 to 1990, claimed in an interview with CNN reporter [Erin Burnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Burnett) that, among other things, "In my mind, \[Epstein] was his best friend, you know, \[throughout] the time I was there for four years."[^cite_note-CNN-2025-24][^cite_note-Kile-2025-25] They would often visit the casino floor together, with O'Donnell also recounting one occasion where he reprimanded both Trump and Epstein when they brought in some girls who were under the New Jersey legal gambling age of 21.[^cite_note-CNN-2025-24][^cite_note-Kile-2025-25] O'Donnell previously wrote about this incident in his 1991 book *Trumped!*, though Epstein's name would be omitted by the book's publisher.[^cite_note-CNN-2025-24]

In 1990, Epstein bought a mansion two miles north of [Mar-a-Lago](1452754.md), which Trump had purchased five years earlier.[^cite_note-Feuer-2025-26] In 1992, Trump invited [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) to film a party he threw for himself and Epstein at Mar-a-Lago, where they joined various NFL cheerleaders. In the NBC video, Trump was filmed at a Mar-a-Lago party whispering in Epstein's ear, prompting Epstein to start laughing. NBC News revealed footage of the party in July 2019, showing Trump, Epstein and the cheerleaders. At one point during the video, Trump appears to tell Epstein: "Look at her, back there ... She's hot."[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-Feuer-2025-26]

Florida businessman George Houraney and [Jill Harth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jill_Harth), a long-term couple, ran a pin-up girl beauty competition called American Dream Calendar Girls. In December 1992, they sought out Trump as an investor and met with him in Manhattan. Trump openly expressed his sexual interest in Harth, allegedly groping her and telling Houraney "I'm going to go after her". (At this time, Houraney and Harth had been a couple for over a decade.) Harth also recalled Trump saying: "I'd like to see the quality of the girls" in the competition. The following month, they reached a deal, and Trump invited them to Mar-a-Lago with a number of the models, where he allegedly groped Harth again. Houraney said he brought 28 people he described as "girls" to the property at Trump's request in order to host a private "calendar girl" event exclusively for Trump and Epstein.[^cite_note-Gabbatt-2025-4][^cite_note-Stringer-2025-30][^cite_note-31] The American Dream competition was held at [Trump Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Nugget_Atlantic_City) in Atlantic City in November 1993. Trump refused to sign a contract beforehand; afterward, the venue claimed it had lost money and that it would no longer host the event. Houraney sued Trump in 1995 over the business deal, claiming he had been cheated out of a \$250,000 fee as well as potential millions of dollars in future business. In 1997, Harth (who was by then married to Houraney) sued Trump for sexual misconduct. Later in 1997, Houraney settled with Trump; as part of their settlement, Harth dropped her lawsuit. Houraney and Harth then divorced, and Trump invited Houraney to his Mar-a-Lago Christmas party that year.[^cite_note-Feuer-2025-26]

In January 2020, an assistant U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York wrote that "Donald Trump traveled on [Epstein's private jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein's_private_jet) ... on at least eight flights between 1993 and 1996."[^cite_note-35] Flight logs released in 2021 showed seven flights that Trump took on the plane between 1993 and 1997.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-Hill-2025-36][^cite_note-ToI_staff-2024-39][^cite_note-Broadwater-2025-3] According to Epstein, Trump first had sex with [Melania Knauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melania_Knauss) in his private jet.[^cite_note-Gabbatt-2025-4]

An anonymous affidavit by "Tiffany Doe" said that Epstein had paid her directly from 1991 to 2000 "to attract adolescent women" to parties at his mansion ([Herbert N. Straus House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_N._Straus_House)). In the summer of 1994, she lured a minor who expressed interest in modeling. In her affidavit, she said that both "Mr. Trump and Mr. Epstein were advised that she was 13 years old. I personally witnessed four sexual encounters" between Trump and the 13-year-old. In one of the alleged incidents, a 12-year-old girl was simultaneously victimized, and in another, Trump continued assaulting the 13-year-old "despite her pleas to stop". She signed the affidavit in 2016.[^cite_note-40]

[Maria Farmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Farmer), the first person to report Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell to law enforcement, encountered Trump late at night in Epstein's Manhattan office in 1995. Trump was not accused of any crime in any of the documents.[^cite_note-Staff-2025-23]

&#x20;As a teenager, [Virginia Giuffre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Giuffre) worked as a locker room attendant in the spa at Mar-a-Lago. There, she met Maxwell, who recruited her as Epstein's masseuse.[^cite_note-Broadwater-2025-3][^cite_note-43] Trump told reporters on July 28, 2025:

### Falling out (mid-2000s)



View of the Maison de L'Amitie&#x20;

In 2004, Trump and Epstein competed to buy the [Maison de L'Amitie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maison_de_L'Amitie) at its [foreclosure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreclosure) auction. Trump outbid Epstein, renovating the house and selling it four years later at a profit.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] Some media reports have described the outbidding as the end of the Trump–Epstein friendship, with phone records indicating that the two ceased communicating after the fact.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-Broadwater-2025-3][^cite_note-Gabbatt-2025-4] (Nonetheless, shortly after the [2016 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election), Trump may have called Epstein to chat about his victory, according to Epstein's brother, Mark.)[^cite_note-Dicker-2025-58] As of Epstein's 2019 arrest, Trump had not confirmed that the bidding caused the end of his friendship with Epstein.[^cite_note-Reinhard-2019-1][^cite_note-62]

Epstein was investigated by Palm Beach police in April 2005 for soliciting an underage girl for sexual purposes, leading to a June 2006 indictment by a grand jury of one count of solicitation of prostitution. The FBI began investigating in July 2006, and the U.S. Attorney's Office indicted him in June 2007.[^cite_note-63] [Michael Reiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Reiter), the Palm Beach police chief at the time, said during a 2019 FBI interview that Trump called him in July 2006 and thanked him for "stopping \[Epstein]", as "everyone has known he's been doing this". According to Reiter, Trump said that Maxwell was Epstein's "operative" and "evil", and suggested that he investigate her. Reiter's account contradicts Trump's July 2019 claims that he did not know Epstein was abusing girls.[^cite_note-64]

### 2007 ban from Mar-a-Lago

According to a 2020 book, after Epstein was said to have sexually harassed a teenage daughter of another Mar-a-Lago member in 2007, Trump revoked his Mar-a-Lago membership.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] The 2016 book *Filthy Rich* detailed that the teen had told her father "while relaxing at Mar-a-Lago, she had been approached and invited out to Epstein's house. The girl said that she had gone and that Epstein had tried to get her to undress. The girl's father had gone directly to Trump, who—in no uncertain terms—told Epstein that he was barred from Mar-a-Lago."[^cite_note-67] Mar-a-Lago's registry shows Epstein was a member until October 2007, according to the 2020 book *The Grifter's Club*.[^cite_note-68] *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reports that Trump gave the same explanation to associates, and that in 2009, Brad Edwards, an attorney for a number of Epstein's victims, was told something similar by Trump.[^cite_note-Feuer-2025-26]

Citing conversations with former Mar-a-Lago and Epstein employees, *The Wall Street Journal* reported in 2025 that Mar-a-Lago had dispatched spa employees—most commonly young women—to Epstein's nearby mansion for various spa services and did so for several years. In that time, according to former Mar-a-Lago employees, Epstein exposed himself and was otherwise sexually suggestive towards the employees. These spa employees said they actively warned each other about Epstein, but these house calls did not cease until 2003; according to these accounts, Trump agreed to remove Epstein from Mar-a-Lago after an 18-year-old beautician told her managers that Epstein had pressured her for sex.[^cite_note-69] Trump's second presidential administration denied Trump as ever sending teenage workers to Epstein's mansion after being asked by [TMZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMZ). White House Press Secretary [Karoline Leavitt](71739774.md) accused the original *Wall Street Journal* story of being full of "fallacies and innuendo."[^cite_note-70]

Another alternative account came from Representative [Jamie Raskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Raskin) in 2026 after he was shown unredacted Epstein documents. According to Raskin, he found a document in which Trump admitted he had "never asked \[Epstein] to leave" Mar-a-Lago, seeming to contradict Trump's assertion that he had removed Epstein from the club.[^cite_note-Cameron-2026-71][^cite_note-72] Raskin found that, in the publicly released documents, that information was redacted. In response to a request for comment from [Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek), Trump's White House insisted that the redactions had to do with "personally identifiable information" for certain individuals, such as Epstein's victims.[^cite_note-Cameron-2026-71]

## 2025 congressional investigation

### Birthday book revelations

Trump and Mandelson (pictured) met a number of times in 2025. Trump said in September that he did not know him.

In September 2025, the [House Oversight Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Oversight_Committee) released a 2003 birthday book created by Maxwell for Epstein's 50th birthday, titled *The First Fifty Years*.[^cite_note-98] The album included a note allegedly signed by Trump, featuring a drawing and a typewritten message. Trump denied writing or signing the note and his legal team has disputed its authenticity citing supposed signature irregularities,[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] but a *New York Times* comparison with contemporary first name signatures of Trump suggested strong similarities.[^cite_note-101] Coverage of the release described the disputed note as adding renewed public and media attention to Trump's past association with Epstein.[^cite_note-102]

The book led to the sacking of British Ambassador to the United States [Peter Mandelson](216970.md) following widespread criticism over his own association with Epstein. As the scandal began shortly before the [state visit by Trump to the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_state_visit_by_Donald_Trump_to_the_United_Kingdom), it created a diplomatic predicament for Trump, and his team were reportedly concerned about the timing, as Mandelson's dismissal over his links to Epstein could've shone an unwelcome spotlight on Trump's own past association with him. A source with knowledge of the White House's discussions said that Trump's team was "nervy" about anything that could resurface the Epstein scandal, and they were concerned Mandelson's dismissal will overshadow the UK state visit. *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* noted that with Trump having his own well-documented links to Epstein, "there is no subject he wants to revisit less" than the scandal surrounding Mandelson's ties to him. For Trump, whose priority was to avoid distractions during the state visit, the controversy arrived at an "especially awkward" time.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]

During a September 18, 2025, press conference in the UK, Trump claimed he did not know who Mandelson was, despite having met him multiple times. Trump made the statement when asked if he had any sympathy for Mandelson after he was dismissed from his role as ambassador. In response, Trump stated: "I don't know him, actually." He added that it was a choice made by the Prime Minister and suggested [Keir Starmer](18681491.md) was better placed to speak on the matter. Despite Trump's claim of not knowing Mandelson, multiple sources and photographic evidence show they have met on several occasions. In May 2025, Mandelson stood with Trump in the Oval Office during the announcement of a US-UK trade framework. Photos captured the two shaking hands and smiling together. According to Mandelson, Trump commented on his "beautiful accent" during one Oval Office meeting. ## Political ramifications

Trump's relationship with Epstein received significant media attention in 2025 due to the unwillingness of the [Trump administration](78298108.md) to release [files relating to Epstein](80415168.md), despite Trump's earlier promises to do so during the 2024 campaign.[^cite_note-136] Trump received significant amounts of blowback from the media, the public, and even many of his supporters for this decision. While Trump has attempted to distance himself from Epstein and downplay their association, some of the MAGA movement's most fervent supporters grew increasingly vocal in demanding the release of Epstein-related files, leading to visible fractures within his support base.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

After U.S. attorney general [Pam Bondi](4550623.md) and other Trump officials had for months teased the imminent release of incendiary information (the "[Jeffrey Epstein client list](80415168.md)") from FBI records of the investigation into Epstein's sex trafficking operation, Bondi stated in a memo released in July 2025 that there was no evidence that Epstein had such a list or that he had blackmailed prominent individuals. The memo also confirmed that Epstein had committed suicide while in custody.[^cite_note-140] The announcement caused an uproar among some of Trump's most fervent supporters who had bought into the conspiracy theory that Epstein was at the center of "a cabal of powerful men and celebrities, largely Democrats" and that the government had covered it up.[^cite_note-Feuer-2025-26] In social media posts, Trump said the continuing demands for release of the files were a hoax perpetrated by Democrats, and that his supporters pressing for release were "stupid", "foolish", and "past supporters".[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

Deputy Attorney General [Todd Blanche](74006584.md) met with Maxwell on July 24 and 25, 2025 at the U.S. attorney's office in Tallahassee.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] Maxwell told Blanche (according to the transcript released a month later)[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146] that Trump and Epstein were not "close friends", contradicting Epstein previously stating he was Trump's closest friend.[^cite_note-147]

On September 23, 2025, a statue of Trump and Epstein titled *[Best Friends Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Friends_Forever_\(sculpture\))*, also known as *Why Can't We Be Friends?*,[^cite_note-Gelt-2025-193] was briefly installed at the [National Mall](167796.md) in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) Created by an anonymous art group aliased "The Secret Handshake", the sculpture was created in protest of the relationship of Trump and Epstein and the closely associated sexual misconduct allegations against Trump. The sculpture had permits to stay on display until September 28, but was dismantled by the [U.S. Park Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Park_Police) within 24 hours of installation. On October 2, it was reported that the statue had been temporarily reinstalled on the National Mall.[^cite_note-Gelt-2025-193]

A *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* sketch aired on November 15, 2025, a few days after the release of Epstein's emails, starred [James Austin Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Austin_Johnson) as Trump and [Ashley Padilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashley_Padilla) as White House press secretary [Karoline Leavitt](71739774.md). In the show, Leavitt's character argued that Trump had not done anything illegal, saying that "If anything, his crime was loving too much, and possibly too young." The sketch also references Mark Epstein's email on Trump supposedly "blowing Bubba". It also depicts Trump saying: "Jeffrey Epstein, I barely knew the guy, OK? As evidenced by the thousands of pictures of us together, dancing and grinding our teeth at various parties."[^cite_note-196] In January 2026, leading up to Epstein's birthday on January 20, and Trump's first inauguration anniversary of his [second presidency](78298108.md), activists displayed on the [National Mall](167796.md) a ten-foot tall (3.0 m) replica of the birthday card Trump sent to Epstein.[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198][^cite_note-199]
