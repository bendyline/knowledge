The **Main Directorate of Intelligence of the Ministry of Defence of Ukraine** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Головне управління розвідки Міністерства оборони України, ГУР МОУ, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Holovne upravlinnia rozvidky Ministerstva oborony Ukrainy, **GUR** / **HUR [MO](14284651.md)***),[^cite_note-3] also known in English as the **Defense Intelligence of Ukraine** (**DIU**), is the [military intelligence](146678.md) service of the [Ukrainian government](1372585.md). It is an agency of the [Ministry of Defence](14284651.md), not the [General Staff of the Armed Forces of Ukraine](17313362.md).

Similar to the [GRU](261749.md), both its predecessor and Russian counterpart, the HUR is not restricted to intelligence-gathering but also carries out military operations and contains [Spetsnaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spetsnaz) units under its operational control.

The agency was established from the existing intelligence assets of the [Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev_Military_District), [Odesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa_Military_District) and [Carpathian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Military_District) [military districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_districts_of_the_Soviet_Union)[^cite_note-History-2] of the [Soviet Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Armed_Forces) and its [Main Intelligence Directorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRU_\(Soviet_Union\)) (GRU), following the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) and the [independence of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_Ukraine).

The Intelligence Directorate of the General Headquarters of the [Armed Forces of Ukraine](42799055.md) was established in February 1992. Based on a presidential decree issued on 7 September 1992, the Strategic Military Intelligence Directorate of the [Ministry of Defence](14284651.md) was created. The existence of two separate agencies with similar responsibilities, and reporting to different authorities, hampered the development of an effective military intelligence system.[^cite_note-History-2][^cite_note-USCC-4]

On 6 July 1993, the Decree of the President of Ukraine ordered the merging of the two agencies to form the Main Military Intelligence Directorate of the Ministry of Defence. It was renamed the Main Intelligence Directorate of the Ministry of Defense of Ukraine, or Defence Intelligence of Ukraine (DIU) for short, on 4 April 1994.[^cite_note-USCC-4][^cite_note-History-2]

On 22 March 2001, the [Ukrainian parliament (Rada)](844993.md) ratified the Law of Ukraine "On Intelligence Agencies of Ukraine". The HUR was given the status of special government authority.[^cite_note-History-2]



HUR special forces at the [2021 Kabul airlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Kabul_airlift)

On 18 August 2021, HUR special forces moved on [Il-76MDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il-76MD) from the [25th Transport Aviation Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25th_Transport_Aviation_Brigade) of the [Ukrainian Air Force](1135769.md) and flew to [Kabul](16826.md) with the mission of evacuating citizens of Ukraine and foreigners during the [2021 Kabul airlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Kabul_airlift). In total, the aircraft of the brigade made six flights and transported more than 700 citizens of different countries. Special forces went to Kabul three times a day to find and bring citizens who were to be evacuated to the [Kabul airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_airport).[^cite_note-5]

In the February of 2022, the ongoing conflict of Ukraine escalated to a full-scale war, leading to Russia's invasion on the 24th of the month. The DIU played an indirect role in events surrounding this invasion by issuing pre-invasion warnings, strengthening defence by coordinating western allies, carrying out covert missions, and influencing the leadership of Ukraine to respond decisively. The DIU prevented the fall of the Ukrainian cities by providing defence over Kyiv and launching counter attacks in Kharkiv.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

On 20 September 2023, CNN reported[^cite_note-8] that [Ukrainian special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces_of_Ukraine) were likely behind multiple attacks on [Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner_Group)-backed [Rapid Support Forces](43053874.md) (RSF) forces in Sudan, using [DJI Mavic 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJI_Mavic#Mavic_3,_3_Cine,_3_Classic,_3_Pro,_3_Pro_Cine,_3_Enterprise,_3_Thermal_and_3_Multispectral) and [first-person view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_view_\(radio_control\)) (FPV) drones. In May 2023, HUR Chief Kyrylo Budanov vowed[^cite_note-9] to "destroy Russian war criminals anywhere in the world, wherever they are." In March 2024, reports confirmed that HUR operators from the "Timur" unit were actively fighting Wagner and RSF in Sudan.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

On 30 January 2024, the DIU received a Snatch Land Rover SUV for its personnel from volunteers of the British-Ukrainian society "Driving Ukraine".[^cite_note-12]

On 2 May 2025, a Ukrainian HUR [MAGURA V5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAGURA_V5) naval drone shot down a Russian [Su-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su-30) using a R-73 missile, 50 km west of Novorossiysk. The crew of two ended up in the Black Sea, where a civilian ship later recovered them. HUR claimed it was the first time a maritime drone shot down an aircraft.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

In April 2026, HUR announced that it had performed two space-launch capability tests during the period under Budanov's leadership, with the first test reaching an altitude of 100 km, and the second launch reaching 204 km.[^cite_note-16]

In June 2026 [Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes) reported that following Russia's [2014 invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Russian_aggression_against_Ukraine) began close cooperation with the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md), which helped reform the institution weakened by corruption, Russian infiltration and its Soviet-era legacy. The cooperation is reported to be ongoing as of mid-2026, with Ukraine providing its US counterpart with insights into Russia.[^cite_note-17]

## Emblem

In 2016, the HUR emblem was changed to an illustration of an owl plunging a sword into Russia. The owl has been said to be a tongue-in-cheek reference to the emblem of the [Spetsnaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spetsnaz_GRU) special forces of the Russian [GRU](261749.md), which depicts a bat - a natural prey of the owl.[^cite_note-18] In addition, the motto "The wise will rule over the stars" ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Sapiens Dominabitur Astris*) ostensibly alludes to the GRU's motto "Only the stars are above us" ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Выше нас только звeзды, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *Vyshe nas tolko zvyezdy*). The new emblem was met with outrage from Russian journalists and politicians, including [Dmitry Rogozin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Rogozin), and with some calling it a provocation.[^cite_note-19]

## Spheres of activity

The Main Directorate of Intelligence conducts its activity in the military, political, technical, economic, signals, informational and environmental spheres.

The major missions of the directorate are:[^cite_note-20]

- Extracting, analyzing, processing and disseminating information in accordance with the law "On Intelligence"

  [^cite_note-21]

- Conducting measures aimed at promoting the realization of the national interests of Ukraine

- Identifying external threats to the national security of Ukraine, and conducting measures against such threats

- Participating in the fight against terrorism and other criminal activities that pose a threat to the national security of Ukraine

- Cooperating with foreign intelligence bodies

- Carrying out other activities defined by law to ensure the national security of Ukraine

## Legal basis

The Main Directorate of Intelligence is legally allowed to operate based upon the following documents:[^cite_note-59]

- The [Constitution of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ukraine)

- [Law of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Ukraine) "On the National Security of Ukraine", 1991

- Law of Ukraine "On the Defense of Ukraine", 2018

- Law of Ukraine "On Intelligence", 2020

- Law of Ukraine "On Military Duty and Military Service", 1992

- Law of Ukraine "On Central Bodies of Executive Power", 2011

- Law of Ukraine "On Civil Service", 2015

- Law of Ukraine "On International Treaties of Ukraine", 2004

- Law of Ukraine "On the Fight Against Terrorism", 2003

- Law of Ukraine "On Counter-Intelligence Activities", 2002

- Law of Ukraine "On Operational and Investigative Activities", 1992

- Law of Ukraine "On State Secrets", 1994

- Law of Ukraine "On the Social and Legal Protection of Servicemen and Their Families", 1991

- Presidential [Decree](322949.md) "On the National Security Strategy of Ukraine", 2020

- Presidential Decree "On the Military Security Strategy of Ukraine", 2021

- Presidential Decree "On Additional Measures to Further Democratize Society and Strengthen Civil Control Over the Activities of Law Enforcement and Intelligence Agencies of Ukraine", 2004

- Presidential Decree "On Ensuring Control over the Activities of the Armed Forces of Ukraine and Other Military Formations", 2010

- Presidential Decree "On Strengthening Control over the Activities of the Armed Forces of Ukraine and Other Military Formations", 2015

- Presidential Decree "On the Inter-Agency Commission on the Policy of Military-Technical Cooperation and Export Control", 2007

- Presidential Decree "On Certain Issues of Leadership in the Spheres of National Security and Defense", 2008

- Presidential Decree "On the Regulation of Military Service in the Armed Forces of Ukraine by Citizens of Ukraine", 2008
