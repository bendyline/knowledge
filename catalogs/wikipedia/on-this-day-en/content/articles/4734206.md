**White South Africans** are [South Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africans) of [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe) [descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Africans_of_European_ancestry). In [linguistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language), cultural, and historical terms, they are generally divided into the [Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans)-speaking descendants of the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company)'s original colonists, known as [Afrikaners](18947965.md), and the [Anglophone descendants](1472509.md#South_Africa) of predominantly [British](19097669.md) colonists of [South Africa](17416221.md). White South Africans are by far the largest population of [White Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Africans). *White* was a legally defined [racial classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_classification) under [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid).[^cite_note-Posel2001-5]

White settlement in South Africa began with [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonial_empire) colonisation in 1652, followed by [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) colonisation in the 19th century, which led to tensions and further expansion inland by [Boer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boer) settlers. Throughout the 19th and 20th centuries, waves of immigrants from [Europe](9239.md) contributed to the growth of the white population, which peaked in the mid-1990s. Under apartheid, strict racial classifications enforced a legal and economic order that privileged the white minority. Post-apartheid reforms such as [Black Economic Empowerment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Economic_Empowerment) had the goal of redistributing business opportunities and market access to previously disadvantaged groups, prompting reports of newfound economic vulnerability among some white South Africans as material advantages and disadvantages were beginning to be brought to light. Since the early 1990s, a large number of white South Africans have [emigrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emigrated), due to concerns over [crime](6551455.md) and employment prospects, with a number returning in subsequent years. The white population in South Africa peaked between 1989 and 1995 at around 5.2 to 5.6 million due to high birth rates and immigration, then declined until the mid-2000s before experiencing a modest increase from 2006 to 2013. It has been declining since.

As of the 2022 census, white South Africans make up 7.3% of the population, predominantly speak Afrikaans (61%) or [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) (36%), mostly identify as [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian) (87%), and are unevenly distributed with the highest concentrations in [Western Cape](313646.md) and [Gauteng](309661.md) provinces. Former South African leaders have made controversial statements about Afrikaners’ identity and race relations, while apartheid enforced white minority rule and granted “honorary white” status to certain [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_people) immigrants and some [African Americans](2154.md). In South Africa, the legacy of apartheid continues to shape racial and economic dynamics.

The majority of Afrikaans-speaking (Afrikaners) and English-speaking White South Africans trace their ancestry to the 17th and 18th-century Dutch colonists or the [1820 British colonists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1820_Settlers). Other colonists included [Huguenots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenots) who emigrated from [France](5843419.md), and [Walloons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walloons) who emigrated from present-day [Belgium](3343.md). The remainder of the White South African population consists of later immigrants from Lebanon, and Europe such as [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_South_Africans) and [Norwegians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_South_Africans). [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) immigrants arrived after the collapse of the [Portuguese colonial administrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) in [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Angola) and [Mozambique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Mozambique), although many also originate from [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## History

[Powder Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder_magazine) at [Stellenbosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellenbosch) built by the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) between 1776 and 1777



[Lizzie van Zyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizzie_van_Zyl), a [Boer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boer) child, in a [British concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War_concentration_camps) in 1901

The history of white settlement in South Africa started in 1652 with the settlement of the [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope) by the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) (VOC) under [Jan van Riebeeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Riebeeck).[^cite_note-Hunt1-10] Despite the preponderance of officials and colonists from the [Netherlands](21148.md), there were also a number of French [Huguenots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenot) fleeing [religious persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Fontainebleau) at home and German soldiers or sailors returning from service in Asia.[^cite_note-Keegan-11] The [Cape Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Cape_Colony) remained under Dutch rule for two more centuries, after which it was annexed by the United Kingdom around 1806.[^cite_note-Lloyd1-12] At that time, South Africa was home to about 26,000 [people of European ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_emigration), a relative majority of whom were still of Dutch origin.[^cite_note-Lloyd1-12] However, the Dutch settlers grew into conflict with the British government over the [abolition of the Cape Colony slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_Trade_Act_1807) and limits on colonial expansion into African lands. In order to prevent a frontier war, the [British Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Parliament) decided to send British settlers to start farms on the eastern frontier.[^cite_note-\:0-13] Beginning in 1818 thousands of [British settlers](19097669.md) arrived in the growing [Cape Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Colony), intending to join the local workforce or settle directly on the frontier.[^cite_note-Lloyd1-12] Ironically most of the farms failed due to the difficult terrain, forcing the British settlers to encroach on African land in order to practise [pastoralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoralism).[^cite_note-\:0-13] About a fifth of the Cape's original [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language)-speaking white population migrated eastwards during the [Great Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Trek) in the 1830s and established their own autonomous [Boer republics](364762.md) further inland.[^cite_note-Greaves-14] Nevertheless, the population of white ancestry (mostly European origin) continued increasing in the Cape as a result of settlement, and by 1865 had reached 181,592 people.[^cite_note-census1865-15] Between 1880 and 1910, there was an influx of [Jews](25955086.md) (mainly via [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Jews)) and immigrants from [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Lebanon_under_Ottoman_rule) and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria) arriving in South Africa. Recent immigrants from the [Levant region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) of Western Asia were originally classified as [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(South_Africa\)), and thus "non-white", but, in order to have the right to purchase land, they successfully argued that they were "white". The main reason being that they were Caucasian and from the lands in which [Christianity](5211.md) and [Judaism](15624.md) originated, and that the race laws did not target Jews, who were also a [Semitic people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_people). Therefore, arguing that if the laws targeted other people from the Levant, it should also affect the Jews.[^cite_note-Shimoni-16][^cite_note-ownership-17]



[Boer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boer) guerrillas during the [Second Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Boer_War).



Density of White South Africans by district in 1922. These were concentrated primarily in [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria) (former capital of the [Transvaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvaal_Republic)), [Cape Town](6653.md) (former capital of the [Cape Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Colony)) and [Port Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Elizabeth,_South_Africa), as well as in the surrounding regions.

The first nationwide census in South Africa was held in 1911 and indicated a white population of 1,276,242. By 1936, there were an estimated 2,003,857 white South Africans, and by 1946 the number had reached 2,372,690.[^cite_note-Shimoni-16] The country began receiving tens of thousands of European immigrants, namely from [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans_in_South_Africa), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_South_Africans), [the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Netherlands), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_in_South_Africa), and the territories of the [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) during the mid- to late twentieth century.[^cite_note-Afrikaans1-18] South Africa's white population increased to over 3,408,000 by 1965, reached 4,050,000 in 1973, and peaked at 5,244,000 in 1994–95.[^cite_note-ZAS-19]

The number of white South Africans resident in their home country began gradually declining between 1990 and the mid-2000s as a result of increased emigration.[^cite_note-ZAS-19]

### Apartheid era

See also: [Population Registration Act, 1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_Registration_Act,_1950)



White South African folk dancers at [Utrecht](50333.md), [Netherlands](21148.md) in 1953

Under the [Population Registration Act of 1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_Registration_Act,_1950), each inhabitant of South Africa was classified into one of several different race groups, of which White was one. The Office for Race Classification defined a white person as one who "in appearance obviously is, or who is generally accepted as a white person, but does not include a person who, although in appearance obviously a white person, is generally accepted as a coloured person." Many criteria, both physical (e.g. examination of head and body hair) and social (e.g. eating and drinking habits, a native speaker of English, Afrikaans or [another European language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Europe)) were used when the board decided to classify someone as white or coloured.[^cite_note-Posel2001-5] The Act was repealed on 17 June 1991.

### Post–apartheid era



Flag of South Africa used during the Apartheid era (1928–1994)



Flag of South Africa used after the Apartheid era as the Rainbow Nation (since 1994)

[Black Economic Empowerment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Economic_Empowerment) legislation further empowers blacks as the government considers ownership, employment, training and social responsibility initiatives, which empower black South Africans, as important criteria when awarding [tenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procurement); [private enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_enterprises) also must adhere to this legislation.[^cite_note-20] Some reports indicate a growing number of whites in poverty compared to the pre-apartheid years and attribute this to such laws – a 2006 article in [The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian) stated that over 350,000 Afrikaners may be classified as poor, and alluded to research claiming that up to 150,000 were struggling for survival.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

As a consequence of Apartheid policies, Whites are still widely regarded as being one of 4 defined race groups in South Africa. These groups (blacks, whites, Coloureds and Indians) still tend to have strong racial identities, and to identify themselves, and others, as members of these race groups[^cite_note-Pillay2019-23][^cite_note-Posel2001-5] and the classification continues to persist in government policy due to attempts at redress like Black Economic Empowerment and Employment Equity.[^cite_note-Posel2001-5]

### Diaspora and emigration

See also: [Brain drain of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_South_Africa#Knowledge)



The Afrikaner diaspora went mainly to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Americans), [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Argentines), [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Canadians) and [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Australians)

Since the 1990s, there has been a significant emigration of whites from South Africa. Between 1995 and 2005, more than one million South Africans emigrated, citing violence as the main reason, as well as the lack of employment opportunities for whites.[^cite_note-24]

### Current trends

See also: [White South African refugee program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_South_African_refugee_program)

In recent decades, there has been a steady proportional decline in South Africa's white community, due to [higher birthrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_territories_by_fertility_rate) among other South African ethnic groups, as well as a high rate of emigration. In 1977, there were 4.3 million whites, constituting 16.4% of the population at the time. As of 2008, it was estimated that at least 800,000 white South Africans had emigrated since 1995.[^cite_note-25]

Like many other communities strongly affiliated with the [West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture) and Europe's [colonial legacy](7299.md) in Africa, white South Africans were in the past often economically better off than their black African neighbours and have surrendered political dominance to majority rule. There were also some white Africans in South Africa who lived in poverty—especially during the 1930s and increasingly since the end of minority rule. Current estimates of white poverty in South Africa run as high as 12%, though [fact-checking](595273.md) website Africa Check described these figures as "grossly inflated" and suggested that a more accurate estimate was that "only a tiny fraction of the white population – as few as 7,754 households – are affected."[^cite_note-26]



White South Africans protest against [gender based violence](3634424.md) and femicide outside [Houses of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houses_of_Parliament,_Cape_Town) in Cape Town

The new phenomenon of white poverty is mostly blamed on the government's [affirmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action) employment legislation, which reserves 80% of new jobs for black people[^cite_note-27] and favours companies owned by black people (see [Black Economic Empowerment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Economic_Empowerment)). In 2010, [Reuters](18998750.md) stated that 450,000 whites live below the poverty line according to [Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_\(South_African_trade_union\)) and civil organisations,[^cite_note-28] with some research saying that up to 150,000 are struggling for survival.[^cite_note-29] However, the proportion of white South Africans living in poverty is still much lower than for other groups in the country, since approximately 50% of the general population fall below the upper-bound [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line).[^cite_note-30]

A further concern has been [crime](6551455.md). Some white South Africans living in affluent white suburbs, such as [Sandton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandton), have been affected by the 2008 13.5% rise in house robberies and associated crime.[^cite_note-31] In a study, [Johan Burger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Burger), senior researcher at the [Institute for Security Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Security_Studies) (ISS), said that criminals were specifically targeting wealthier suburbs. Burger explained that several affluent suburbs are surrounded by poorer residential areas and that inhabitants in the latter often target inhabitants in the former. The report also found that residents in wealthy suburbs in [Gauteng](309661.md) were not only at more risk of being targeted but also faced an inflated chance of being murdered during the robbery.[^cite_note-32]

The [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis) slowed the high rates of white people emigrating overseas and has led to increasing numbers of white emigrants returning to live in South Africa. Charles Luyckx, CEO of Elliot International and a board member of the Professional Movers Association, stated in December 2008 that emigration numbers had dropped by 10% in the six months prior. Meanwhile, "people imports" had increased by 50%.[^cite_note-33]



Afrikaners in [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria)

In May 2014, Homecoming Revolution estimated that around 340,000 white South Africans had returned to South Africa in the preceding decade.[^cite_note-BBCFlanagan-34]

Furthermore, immigration from Europe has also supplemented the white population. The 2011 census found that 63,479 white people living in South Africa were born in Europe; of these, 28,653 had moved to South Africa since 2001.[^cite_note-superwebmigr-35]

At the end of apartheid in 1994, 85% of South Africa's [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land) was owned by whites.[^cite_note-36] The [land reform program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform_in_South_Africa) introduced after the end of apartheid intended that, within 20 years, 30% of white-owned [commercial farm land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intensive_farming) should be transferred to black owners. Thus, in 2011, the farmers' association, Agri South Africa, coordinated efforts to resettle farmers throughout the African continent. The initiative offered millions of hectares from 22 African countries that hoped to spur development of efficient commercial farming.[^cite_note-37] The 30 percent target was not close to being met by the 2014 deadline.[^cite_note-38] According to a 2017 government audit, 72% of the nation's private farmland is owned by white people.[^cite_note-39] In February 2018, the [Parliament of South Africa](376182.md) passed a motion to review the property ownership clause of the constitution, to allow for the [expropriation of land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform_in_South_Africa), in the public interest, without compensation,[^cite_note-40] which was supported within South Africa's ruling [African National Congress](2503.md) on the grounds that the land was originally seized by whites without just compensation.[^cite_note-41] In August 2018, the South African government began the process of taking two white-owned farmlands.[^cite_note-42] Western Cape ANC secretary [Faiez Jacobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faiez_Jacobs) referred to the property clause amendment as a "stick" to force dialogue about the transfer of land ownership, with the hope of accomplishing the transfer "in a way that is orderly and doesn't create a 'them' and 'us' \[situation]."[^cite_note-43]

In 2025, 59 white South Africans [arrived in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_South_African_refugee_program) after U.S. president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) granted them refugee status.[^cite_note-44]

Between October 2025 and March 2026, 4,499 refugees were admitted into the United States; all but three were white South Africans, with over half arriving in February and March.[^cite_note-45]

According to the [2022 South African census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_South_African_census), white South Africans make 7.3% of South Africa's population.[^cite_note-auto-47] The province with the highest percentage of white population is [Western Cape](313646.md) at 16.4%, while the white population is below 5% in [Limpopo](365944.md), [Mpumalanga](313647.md), [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md) and [North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_\(South_African_province\)).[^cite_note-auto-47]

The [Statistics South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_South_Africa) [Census 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_National_Census_of_2011) showed that there were about 4,586,838 white people in South Africa, amounting to 8.9% of the country's population.[^cite_note-midyear-48] This was a 6.8% increase since the 2001 census. According to the Census 2011, [Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans) was the [first language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) of 61% of White South Africans, while [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_English) was the first language of 36%.[^cite_note-cib2011-49] The majority of white South Africans identify themselves as primarily *South African*, regardless of their first language or ancestry.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

Approximately 87% of white South Africans are [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_South_Africa), 9% are [irreligious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion_in_South_Africa), and 1% are [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_South_Africa). The largest [Christian denomination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denomination) is the [Dutch Reformed Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Reformed_Church_in_South_Africa_\(NGK\)) (NGK), with 23% of the white population being members. Other significant denominations are the [Methodist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_Church_of_Southern_Africa) (8%), the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism_in_South_Africa) (7%), and the [Anglican Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Church_of_Southern_Africa) (6%).[^cite_note-52]

### Migrations

Meanwhile, many white South Africans have also emigrated to Western countries over the past two decades, mainly to [English-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-speaking) countries such as the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africans_in_the_United_Kingdom), [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Australians) and [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_New_Zealanders). However, the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis) slowed the rate of emigration and in May 2014, the Homecoming Revolution estimated that around 340,000 white South Africans had returned in the preceding decade.[^cite_note-BBCFlanagan-34]

### 2022 census accuracy controversy

After the publication of the [2022 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_South_African_census) results, it was reported that the undercount rate was 31%. The high undercount rate was reported as an issue of concern as it raised questions about the accuracy of the number of white, Indian, foreign-born and [homeless people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homelessness_in_South_Africa) recorded in the census.[^cite_note-\:2-56]

### Statistics

#### Historical population

Statistics for the white population in South Africa vary greatly. Most sources show that the white population peaked in the period between 1989 and 1995 at around 5.2 to 5.6 million. Up to that point, the white population largely increased due to high birth rates and immigration. Subsequently, between the mid-1990s and the mid-2000s, the white population decreased overall. However, from 2006 to 2013, the white population increased.

## Politics



Painting of an account of the arrival of [Jan van Riebeeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Riebeeck), founder of [Cape Town](6653.md)

The majority of White South Africans vote for the Liberal [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(South_Africa\)) (DA) and a smaller minority vote for the more conservative [Freedom Front Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Front_Plus) (FF+).

Former South African President [Jacob Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma) commented in 2009 on Afrikaners being "the only white tribe in a black continent or outside of [Europe](9239.md) which is truly African", and said that "of all the white groups that are in South Africa, it is only the Afrikaners that are truly South Africans in the true sense of the wor&#x64;*.*"[^cite_note-75] These remarks have led to the [Centre for Constitutional Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Constitutional_Rights) (CCR) laying a complaint with the [Human Rights Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Human_Rights_Commission) against Zuma. According to the CCR's spokesman, Zuma's remarks constituted "unfair discrimination against non-Afrikaans-speaking, white South Africans....."[^cite_note-76]

In 2015, a complaint was investigated for [hate speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hate_speech) against Jacob Zuma who said "You must remember that a man called [Jan van Riebeeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_van_Riebeeck) arrived here on 6 April 1652, and that was the start of the trouble in this country."[^cite_note-77]

Former South African President [Thabo Mbeki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thabo_Mbeki) stated in one of his speeches to the nation that: "South Africa belongs to everyone who lives in it. Black and White."[^cite_note-78]

Prior to 1994, a white minority held complete political power under a system of [racial segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation) called [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid). During [apartheid era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid_era), immigrants from [Taiwan](25734.md), [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans_in_South_Africa), and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_people_in_South_Africa) were considered *[honorary whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_whites)* in the country, as the government had maintained diplomatic relations with these countries. These were granted the same privileges as white people, at least for purposes of residence.[^cite_note-79] Some [African Americans](2154.md) such as [Max Yergan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Yergan) were granted an "honorary white" status as well.[^cite_note-80]

## Discrimination

Further information: [Anti-white racism § South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-white_racism#South_Africa), and [Racism in South Africa § Racism against white Africans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism_in_South_Africa#Racism_against_white_Africans)

[Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(South_Africa\)) MP [Gwen Ngwenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwen_Ngwenya) states that racism aimed at white people in South Africa is often overlooked compared to racism aimed at [black people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_South_Africans), noting that racism aimed at white people elicits little reaction from the populace.[^cite_note-81]

In 2001, former South African president [Nelson Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela) criticised the growing racial intolerance of black South Africans in their attitudes toward South Africans of other racial groups.[^cite_note-82]

After a black person was allegedly killed by two white people, businesses and properties owned by white people and other minorities in [Coligny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coligny,_South_Africa) were targeted for destruction by members of the black community.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

According to Sharlene Swartz, a research specialist of the [Human Sciences Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Sciences_Research_Council) (HSRC), discourses expressing violent resentment towards white populations in South Africa are widespread, citing as an example student protests during which the inscription "Fuck White People" appeared on t-shirts and walls.[^cite_note-swartz-85]
