**Charlotte**[^cite_note-12] is the [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_North_Carolina) in the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [North Carolina](21650.md). With a population of 874,579 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census),[^cite_note-QuickFacts-13] it is the [14th-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population) in the U.S., seventh-most populous city in [the South](179553.md), and second-most populous city in [the Southeast](816925.md) (after [Jacksonville, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville,_Florida)). The [Charlotte metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_metropolitan_area), with an estimated 2.88 million residents, is the [21st-largest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) in the U.S.[^cite_note-PopEstCBSA-10] The [Charlotte metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_metropolitan_area) is part of an 18-county [combined statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_statistical_area) with an estimated population of 3.47 million as of 2024.[^cite_note-PopEstCBSA-10] It is the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [Mecklenburg County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg_County,_North_Carolina).

Between 2004 and 2014, Charlotte was among the country's fastest-growing metropolitan areas, with 888,000 new residents.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Based on U.S. census data from 2005 to 2015, Charlotte tops the U.S. in millennial population growth.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Throughout the 2020s, it has remained one of the fastest-growing major cities in the United States.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Residents of Charlotte are referred to as "[Charlotteans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_people_from_Charlotte,_North_Carolina)".[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

Charlotte is home to the corporate headquarters of [Bank of America](347756.md), [Honeywell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeywell), [Truist Financial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truist_Financial), and the [East Coast](89126.md) headquarters of [Wells Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo), which, when combined with other Charlotte-based financial institutions, makes the city the second-largest banking center in the nation.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

Charlotte's notable attractions include three [major league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada) professional sports teams, the [Carolina Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Panthers) of the [NFL](21211.md), the [Charlotte Hornets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Hornets) of the [NBA](22093.md), and [Charlotte FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_FC) of [MLS](71802.md). The city is also home to the [NASCAR Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Hall_of_Fame), [Opera Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_Carolina), [Charlotte Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Symphony), [Charlotte Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Ballet), Children's Theatre of Charlotte, [Mint Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mint_Museum), [Harvey B. Gantt Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_B._Gantt_Center), [Bechtler Museum of Modern Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bechtler_Museum_of_Modern_Art), the [Billy Graham Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Graham_Library), [Levine Museum of the New South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levine_Museum_of_the_New_South), [Charlotte Museum of History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Museum_of_History), [Carowinds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carowinds) amusement park, and [U.S. National Whitewater Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._National_Whitewater_Center).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

Charlotte has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate). It is located several miles east of the [Catawba River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catawba_River) and southeast of [Lake Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Norman), the largest human-made lake in North Carolina.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] [Lake Wylie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Wylie) and [Mountain Island Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Island_Lake) are two smaller human-made lakes located near the city.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] As of 2024, 66% of the city's area is occupied by green spaces.[^cite_note-43] The city ranks as the [greenest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_forest) in [North America](21139.md) and 28th in the world.[^cite_note-44]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Charlotte, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Charlotte,_North_Carolina).

### Early history

The [Catawba Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catawba_people) are the earliest known inhabitants of the Charlotte area. They were first encountered in the region in 1567 by the Spanish Conquistador [Juan Pardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Pardo_\(explorer\)).[^cite_note-45]

### 18th century

The area that is now Charlotte was first settled by European colonists around 1755 when Thomas Spratt and his family settled near what is now the [Elizabeth neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_\(Charlotte_neighborhood\)). [Thomas Polk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Polk) (great-uncle of [President](24113.md) [James K. Polk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_K._Polk)), who later married Thomas Spratt's daughter, built his house by the intersection of two [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) trading paths between the [Yadkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yadkin_River) and [Catawba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catawba_River) rivers.[^cite_note-Story1-46] One path ran north–south and was part of the [Great Wagon Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wagon_Road); the second path ran east–west along what is now Trade Street.

By 1759, half the Catawba tribe had died from [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox), an endemic disease among European colonists, against which the Catawba had no natural [immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunity_\(medical\)). At its peak, the Catawba population was 10,000. But by 1826, the Catawba population dropped to 110.[^cite_note-47]

The city of Charlotte was developed first by a wave of migration of [Scots-Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotch-Irish_Americans) [Presbyterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism), or [Ulster-Scot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster-Scot) settlers from [Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster), who dominated the culture of the Southern Piedmont Region and made up the principal founding population in the backcountry. [German immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans) also settled in the area before the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), but in smaller numbers. They still contributed greatly to the early foundations of the region.

Mecklenburg County was initially part of [Bath County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bath_County,_North_Carolina) (1696 to 1729) of the New Hanover Precinct, which became [New Hanover County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hanover_County) in 1729. The western portion of New Hanover split into [Bladen County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bladen_County,_North_Carolina) in 1734, and its western portion split into [Anson County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anson_County) in 1750. Mecklenburg County was formed from Anson County in 1762. Further apportionment was made in 1792, after the American Revolutionary War, with [Cabarrus County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabarrus_County) formed from Mecklenburg.[^cite_note-48]

Nicknamed the "Queen City",[^cite_note-caucus-49] like its county a few years earlier, Charlotte was named in honor of [Charlotte of Mecklenburg-Strelitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_of_Mecklenburg-Strelitz), who had become the [queen consort of Great Britain and Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_consort_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) in 1761, seven years before the town's incorporation.[^cite_note-50] A second nickname derives from the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), when British commander General [Charles Cornwallis, 1st Marquess Cornwallis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Cornwallis,_1st_Marquess_Cornwallis) occupied the city but was driven out by hostile residents. He wrote that Charlotte was "a hornet's nest of rebellion", leading to the historical nickname "[The Hornet's Nest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hornet's_Nest_\(novel\))".[^cite_note-51]

Within decades of Polk's settling, the area grew to become the Town of Charlotte, [incorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_incorporation) in 1768.[^cite_note-charter-52] Though chartered as Charlotte, the name appears as a form of "Charlottesburgh" on many maps until around 1800.[^cite_note-burgh-53] A form of "Charlottetown" also appears on maps of British origin depicting General Cornwallis' route of invasion.[^cite_note-town-54] The crossroads in Piedmont became the heart of [Uptown Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uptown_Charlotte). In 1770, surveyors marked the streets in a [grid pattern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_pattern) for future development. The east–west trading path became Trade Street, and the Great Wagon Road became Tryon Street, in honor of [William Tryon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Tryon), a royal governor of colonial North Carolina.[^cite_note-Story2-55] The [intersection of Trade and Tryon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_and_Tryon) is commonly known today as "Trade and Tryon", or simply "The Square",[^cite_note-Story1-46] and formally as "Independence Square".[^cite_note-56]

While surveying the boundary between the Carolinas in 1772, [William Moultrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Moultrie) stopped in Charlotte, whose five or six houses were "very ordinary built of logs".[^cite_note-57]

Local leaders came together in 1775 and signed the [Mecklenburg Resolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg_Resolves), more popularly known as the [Mecklenburg Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg_Declaration_of_Independence). While not a true declaration of independence from [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) rule, it is among the first such declarations that eventually led to the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution). May 20, the traditional date of the signing of the declaration, is celebrated annually in Charlotte as "MecDec", with musket and cannon fire by reenactors in Independence Square. North Carolina's [state flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_North_Carolina) and [state seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seal_of_North_Carolina) also bear the date.

In 1799, in nearby Cabarrus County, 12-year-old [Conrad Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrad_Reed) found a 17- pound rock, which his family used as a doorstop. Three years later, a jeweler determined it was nearly solid gold, paying the family a paltry \$3.50.[^cite_note-58] The first documented gold find in the United States of any consequence set off the nation's first [gold rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_rush). Many veins of gold were found in the area throughout the 19th and early 20th centuries, leading to the 1837 founding of the [Charlotte Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Mint).[^cite_note-59] North Carolina was the chief producer of gold in the United States, until the Sierra Nevada was found in 1848,[^cite_note-60] although the volume mined in the Charlotte area was dwarfed by subsequent rushes.

### 19th century

In 1842, [Union County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_County,_North_Carolina) formed from Mecklenburg's southeastern portion and a western portion of Anson County. These areas were all part of one of the original six judicial/military districts of North Carolina known as the [Salisbury District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury_District,_North_Carolina).[^cite_note-61]

Charlotte is traditionally considered the home of Southern [Presbyterianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism), but in the 19th century, numerous churches, including Presbyterian, [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist), [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist), [Episcopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_\(United_States\)), [Lutheran](23371382.md), and [Roman Catholic](606848.md) formed, eventually giving Charlotte the nickname, "[The City of Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Churches)".[^cite_note-Story40-62]

Some groups still [pan for gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placer_mining) occasionally in local streams and creeks. The [Reed Gold Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_Gold_Mine) operated until 1912.[^cite_note-63] The [Charlotte Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Mint) was active until 1861 when [Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) forces seized it at the outbreak of the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War). The mint was not reopened at the war's end, but the building, albeit in a different location, now houses the [Mint Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mint_Museum_of_Art).

The city's first boom came after the Civil War, as Charlotte became a cotton processing center and railroad hub. By the 1880s, Charlotte sat astride the Southern Railway mainline from [Atlanta](3138.md) to [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) Farmers from miles around would bring cotton to the railroad platform in Uptown. Local promotors began building textile factories, starting with the 1881 Charlotte Cotton Mill that still stands at Graham and 5th streets.[^cite_note-64]

Charlotte's city population at the [1890 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1890_United_States_census) grew to 11,557.[^cite_note-Story-65]

### 20th century



Aerial view of Charlotte in 1932

In 1910, Charlotte surpassed [Wilmington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington,_North_Carolina) to become North Carolina's largest city with 34,014 residents.[^cite_note-66]

The population grew again during [World War I](4764461.md), when the U.S. government established [Camp Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Greene), north of present-day Wilkinson Boulevard. The camp supported 40,000 soldiers, with many troops and suppliers staying after the war, launching urbanization that eventually overtook older cities along the [Piedmont Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_Crescent). In the [1920 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_United_States_census), Charlotte fell to being the state's second largest city, [Winston-Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston-Salem) with 48,395 people, had two thousand more people than Charlotte. Charlotte would pass Winston-Salem in population by the [1930 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_United_States_census), and has remained North Carolina's largest city since.[^cite_note-67]

Until 1958, the [Seaboard Air Line Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaboard_Air_Line_Railroad) operated a daily passenger train from its [own station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_station_\(Seaboard_Air_Line_Railroad\)) (which had opened in 1896) to [Wilmington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington,_North_Carolina).[^cite_note-aboard-68][^cite_note-69]

The city's modern-day banking industry achieved prominence in the 1970s and 1980s, largely under the leadership of [financier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financier) [Hugh McColl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_McColl). McColl transformed [North Carolina National Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_National_Bank) (NCNB) into a formidable national bank that through aggressive acquisitions eventually merged with BankAmerica to become [Bank of America](347756.md). [First Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Union), later [Wachovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wachovia) in 2001, experienced similar growth before it was acquired by [San Francisco](49728.md)–based [Wells Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo) in 2008. Measured by control of assets, Charlotte became the second largest banking headquarters in the United States after [New York City](645042.md).[^cite_note-Story13-70]

On September 22, 1989, the city was hit by [Hurricane Hugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Hugo). With sustained winds of 69 mph (111 km/h) and gusts of 87 mph (140 km/h),[^cite_note-71] Hugo caused massive property damage, destroyed 80,000 trees, and knocked out electrical power to most of the population. Residents were without power for weeks, schools were closed for a week or more, and the cleanup took months. The city was caught unprepared; Charlotte is 200 miles (320 km) inland, and residents from coastal areas in both Carolinas often wait out hurricanes in Charlotte.[^cite_note-72]

### 21st century

In December 2002, Charlotte and much of central North Carolina were hit by an [ice storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_ice_storm_of_2002) that resulted in more than 1.3 million people losing power.[^cite_note-73] During an abnormally cold December, many were without power for weeks. Many of the city's [Bradford pear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford_pear) trees split apart under the weight of the ice.

In August 2015 and September 2016, the city experienced several days of protests related to the [police shootings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_killings_by_law_enforcement_officers_in_the_United_States) of [Jonathan Ferrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_of_Jonathan_Ferrell) and [Keith Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_of_Keith_Lamont_Scott).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

## Geography

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has a total area of 312.00 square miles (808.1 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 310.02 square miles (802.9 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 1.98 square miles (5.1 km<sup>2</sup>) (0.63%) is water.[^cite_note-TigerWebMapServer-8] Charlotte is the [twenty-sixth-most expansive city in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_area) and lies at an elevation of 751 feet (229 m). Charlotte constitutes most of [Mecklenburg County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecklenburg_County,_North_Carolina) in the Carolina [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(United_States\)).[^cite_note-76] [Uptown Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uptown_Charlotte) sits atop a long rise between two creeks, Sugar Creek and Irwin Creek, and was built on the [gunnies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunnies) of the St. Catherine's and Rudisill [gold](12240.md) [mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining).[^cite_note-77] Charlotte is 25 miles (40 km) southwest of [Concord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concord,_North_Carolina);[^cite_note-78] 26 miles (42 km) northeast of [Rock Hill, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Hill,_South_Carolina);[^cite_note-79] 83 miles (134 km) southwest of [Greensboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensboro,_North_Carolina);[^cite_note-80] 135 miles (217 km) west of [Fayetteville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayetteville,_North_Carolina);[^cite_note-81] and 165 miles (266 km) southwest of [Raleigh](57845.md), the [state capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals).[^cite_note-82]

Though the Catawba River and its lakes lie several miles west, there are no significant bodies of water or other geological features near the city center.[^cite_note-83] Consequently, development has neither been constrained nor helped by waterways or ports that have contributed to many cities of similar size. The lack of these obstructions has contributed to Charlotte's growth as a highway, rail, and air transportation hub.[^cite_note-84]

Charlotte has 199 neighborhoods radiating in all directions from [Uptown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uptown_Charlotte).[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] [Biddleville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biddleville_\(Charlotte_neighborhood\)), the primary historic center of Charlotte's [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) community, is west of Uptown, starting at the [Johnson C. Smith University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_C._Smith_University) campus and extending to the airport.[^cite_note-Biddle-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] East of The Plaza and north of Central Avenue, [Plaza Midwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Midwood) is known for its international population, including Eastern Europeans, [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Americans), [Middle-Easterners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Eastern_Americans), and [Hispanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] North Tryon and the Sugar Creek area include several [Asian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans) communities.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] [NoDa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NoDa_\(Charlotte_neighborhood\)) (North Davidson), north of Uptown, is an emerging center for arts and entertainment.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] [Myers Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myers_Park_\(Charlotte_neighborhood\)), [Dilworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilworth_\(Charlotte_neighborhood\)), and [Eastover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastover_\(Charlotte_neighborhood\)) are home to some of Charlotte's most affluent, oldest and largest houses, on tree-lined boulevards, with [Freedom Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Park_\(Charlotte,_North_Carolina\)) nearby.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

The [SouthPark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SouthPark_\(Charlotte_neighborhood\)) area offers shopping, dining, and multifamily housing.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] Far South Boulevard is home to a large Hispanic community.[^cite_note-105] Many students, researchers, and affiliated professionals live near [UNC Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNC_Charlotte) in the northeast area known as [University City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_City,_North_Carolina).[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108]

The large area known as Southeast Charlotte is home to many golf communities, luxury developments, churches, the Jewish community center, and private schools. As undeveloped land within Mecklenburg has become scarce, many of these communities have expanded into [Weddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weddington,_North_Carolina) and [Waxhaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waxhaw,_North_Carolina) in [Union County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_County,_North_Carolina).[^cite_note-109] [Ballantyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballantyne_\(neighborhood\)), in the south of Charlotte, and nearly every area on the I‑485 perimeter, has experienced rapid growth over the past ten years.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] The [Steele Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steele_Creek,_North_Carolina) neighborhood which is primarily in Mecklenburg county is located in Southwest Charlotte and is known for its outdoor recreation and shopping opportunities.

Since the 1980s in particular, Uptown Charlotte has undergone massive construction of buildings, housing [Bank of America](347756.md), [Wells Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo), [Hearst Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearst_Corporation), and [Duke Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Energy), several hotels, and multiple condominium developments.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

### Parks and green space



[Little Sugar Creek Greenway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Sugar_Creek_Greenway) at East 4th Street overpass



A view of [Romare Bearden Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romare_Bearden_Park), also located in Uptown Charlotte

[Latta Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latta_Park) was created in 1891 as an amusement park.[^cite_note-118]

[Bryant Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryant_Park_\(Charlotte,_North_Carolina\)) was established in the 1930s and is one of the earliest small-scale public parks in Charlotte. It is the only green space remaining in West Morehead Street's industrial sector.[^cite_note-119]

The 120-acre (49 ha) [Park Road Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Road_Park_\(Charlotte,_North_Carolina\)) is a prominent landmark near the SouthPark area.[^cite_note-southpark-120] Park Road Park features eight basketball courts, two horseshoe pits, six baseball fields, five picnic shelters, volleyball courts, playgrounds, trails, tennis courts, and an 11-acre (4.5 ha) lake.[^cite_note-121] The Charlotte-Mecklenburg Parks & Recreation Department operates 36 tennis facilities and the 12 lighted tennis courts at the park.[^cite_note-122] In September 2013, the 5.4-acre (2.2 ha) [Romare Bearden Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romare_Bearden_Park) opened to the public.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

The urban section of [Little Sugar Creek Greenway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Sugar_Creek_Greenway) was completed in 2012. Inspired in part by the [San Antonio River Walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_River_Walk), and integral to Charlotte's extensive urban park system, it is "a huge milestone" according to Gwen Cook, greenway planner for Mecklenburg County Park and Recreation.[^cite_note-125] However, the Little Sugar Creek Greenway bears no relation to the San Antonio River Walk.

The city of Charlotte and Mecklenburg County began purchasing flood-prone homes in the 1990s. Voluntary buyouts of 700 households have created around 200 acres (81 ha)s of open land that can flood safely, thereby saving an estimated \$28 million in flood damage and emergency rescues.[^cite_note-126]

[McAlpine Creek Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McAlpine_Creek_Park) and integrated McAlpine Creek Greenway constructed in 1978 was the first greenway built in the western piedmont of North Carolina.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

### Climate

Like much of the Piedmont region of the southeastern United States, Charlotte has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa*), with four distinct seasons. Charlotte is part of USDA [hardiness zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zone) 8a, transitioning to 7b in the suburbs in all directions except the south.[^cite_note-129]

Winters are short and fairly mild, with a normal January daily high temperature of 52.3 °F (11.3 °C) and daily mean temperature of 42.1 °F (5.6 °C). On occasion temperatures can fall below 20 °F (−6.7 °C), but Charlotte also enjoys multiple warm winter days with highs in excess of 65 °F (18.3 °C).[^cite_note-NWS_Greenville-Spartanburg,_SC_(GSP)-130] On average, there are 59 nights per year that drop to or below freezing, and only 1.5 days that fail to rise above freezing.[^cite_note-NWS_Greenville-Spartanburg,_SC_(GSP)-130] Summers are hot and humid, with a normal July daily high temperature of 90.3 °F (32.4 °C) and daily mean temperature of 80.1 °F (26.7 °C).[^cite_note-NCDC_txt_KCLT-131] Hot and humid days can arrive as early as May and last until the end of September. There is an average of 44 days per year with highs at or above 90 °F (32 °C).[^cite_note-NWS_Greenville-Spartanburg,_SC_(GSP)-130] Official record temperatures range from 104 °F (40 °C) recorded six times on 6 September 1954, 9–10 August 2007 and [June 29 to July 1, 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_2012_North_American_heat_wave), down to −5 °F (−21 °C) recorded on [December 30, 1880](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Winter_of_1880%E2%80%9381), [February 14, 1899](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Blizzard_of_1899) and [January 21, 1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_1985_Arctic_outbreak).[^cite_note-132] The record cold daily maximum is 14 °F (−10 °C) on [February 12 and 13, 1899](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Blizzard_of_1899).[^cite_note-NWS_Greenville-Spartanburg,_SC_(GSP)-130] The record warm daily minimum is 82 °F (28 °C) on August 13, 1881.[^cite_note-133] The average window for freezing temperatures is November 5 to March 30, allowing a growing season of 220 days.[^cite_note-NWS_Greenville-Spartanburg,_SC_(GSP)-130]

Charlotte is directly in the path of subtropical moisture from the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md) as it heads up the eastern seaboard, thus the city receives ample precipitation throughout the year but also many clear, sunny days. Precipitation is generally slightly less frequent in autumn than in spring.[^cite_note-NWS_Greenville-Spartanburg,_SC_(GSP)-130] On average, Charlotte receives 43.60 inches (1,110 mm) of precipitation annually, evenly distributed throughout the year. Annual precipitation has historically ranged from 26.23 in (666 mm) in 2001 to 68.44 in (1,738 mm) in 1884.[^cite_note-NWS_Greenville-Spartanburg,_SC_(GSP)-130]

There is an average of 3.5 inches (8.9 cm) of snow, mainly in January and February and rarely December or March, with more frequent ice storms and sleet mixed in with rain. Seasonal snowfall has historically ranged from no snowfall in 2022-23 and 2023-24[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] to 22.6 in (57 cm) in 1959–60.[^cite_note-NWS_Greenville-Spartanburg,_SC_(GSP)-130] Snow and ice storms can have a major impact on the area, as they often pull tree limbs down onto power lines and make driving hazardous. Snow has been recorded a small number of times in April, most recently on April 2, 2019.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]

As of 2020, the Charlotte metropolitan area as a whole is noted for having one of the worst [weather radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_radar) gaps among any major [U.S. East Coast city](89126.md), with little to no coverage in a roughly quadrilateral area spanning [Concord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concord,_North_Carolina), [Salisbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury,_North_Carolina) and much of [Statesville](127986.md).[^cite_note-WaPo_CLT_radar-138] As the nearest [NWS](286989.md)-owned [NEXRAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEXRAD) is located in [Greer, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greer,_South_Carolina), more than 80 mi (130 km) to the west-southwest of Charlotte, this deficit is particularly problematic during [severe thunderstorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_thunderstorm) or [tornado](37530.md) episodes.[^cite_note-WaPo_CLT_radar-138] The current lowest angle of the radar, based in Greer, is quite far above the surface over Charlotte, so the [velocities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_radar#Velocity) measurement for detecting [rotations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_vortex_signature) cannot be below mid-level in potential tornado-forming storms and thus cannot indicate whether said rotation extends closer to the ground (below 5,000 ft, 1,500 m).[^cite_note-WaPo_CLT_radar-138]

In the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), there were 874,579 people, 342,448 households, and 195,614 families living in the city. In 2019, the U.S. Census Bureau estimates showed 885,708 residents living within Charlotte's city limits[^cite_note-PopEstCities-148] and 1,093,901 in Mecklenburg County.[^cite_note-Bureau-149] The [combined statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Statistical_Area), or trade area, of [Charlotte–Concord–Gastonia, NC–SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte-Concord-Gastonia,_NC-SC_Metropolitan_Statistical_Area) had an estimated population of 3,387,115 in 2023.[^cite_note-PopEstCBSA-10] Figures from the more comprehensive 2010 census show Charlotte's [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 2,457 per square mile (949/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 319,918 housing units at an average density of 1,074.6 per square mile (414.9/km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-CharlotteCensusData-150]

In 1970, the U.S. Census Bureau reported Charlotte's population as 30.2% Black and 68.9% White.[^cite_note-151] In 2020, 39.72% of the population was non-Hispanic white, 32.5% Black or African American, 0.25% Native American, 7.02% Asian, 0.05% Pacific Islander, 4.15% other or mixed, and 16.32% Hispanic or Latin American of any race. This reflected the national demographic shift as Hispanic or Latinos and Asians increased in population.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]

In 2020, the median income for a household in Charlotte was \$48,670. The median income for a family was \$59,452. Males had a median income of \$38,767 versus \$29,218 for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for Charlotte was \$29,825. The percentage of the population living at or below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line) was 10.6%, with 7.8% of families living at or below the poverty line. Out of the total population, 13.8% of those under the age of 18 and 9.7% of those 65 and older were living below the poverty line.

### Religion



[Billy Graham Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Graham_Library)

Charlotte has been historically [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) and remains predominantly Protestant today. It is the birthplace of [Billy Graham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Graham), and is also the historic seat of [Southern Presbyterianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_in_the_United_States). The changing demographics of the city's increasing population have brought scores of new denominations and faiths.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158] The [Billy Graham Evangelistic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Graham_Evangelistic_Association), [Wycliffe Bible Translators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wycliffe_USA)' [JAARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JAARS) Center, [SIM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serving_In_Mission) Missions Organization, and The Christian Research Institute make their homes in the Charlotte general area.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] In 2018, Charlotte proper had over 700 places of worship.[^cite_note-162]

As of 2013, 51.91% of people in Charlotte practice religion on a regular basis, making it the second most religious city in North Carolina after [Winston-Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston-Salem,_North_Carolina). The largest religion in Charlotte is [Christianity](5211.md), with [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists) (13.26%) having the largest number of adherents. The second largest Christian group are the [Roman Catholics](606848.md) (9.43%), followed by [Methodists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist) (8.02%) and [Presbyterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian) (5.25%). Other Christian affiliates include [Pentecostals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostal) (2.50%), [Lutherans](23371382.md) (1.30%), [Episcopalians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_\(United_States\)) (1.20%), [Latter-Day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter-Day_Saints) (0.84%), and other Christian (8.87%) churches, including the [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox) and [non-denominational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondenominational_Christianity) congregations. [Judaism](15624.md) (0.57%) is the second largest religion after Christianity, followed by [Eastern religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_religions) (0.34%) and [Islam](6037917.md) (0.32%).[^cite_note-163]

According to the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center), 73% of adults in the Charlotte Metro area identified as Christians in 2023–24.[^cite_note-164] The four largest Christian denominations are [Evangelical Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Protestants) (33%), [Mainline Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainline_Protestants) (15%), Historically Black Protestants (15%) and Catholics (7%). Within the Evangelical Protestants, the largest grouping are the [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists). The [Baptist Peace Fellowship of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist_Peace_Fellowship_of_North_America) is headquartered in Charlotte. The largest [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) church in Charlotte, by attendance, is [Elevation Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevation_Church), a Southern Baptist church founded by lead pastor [Steven Furtick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Furtick). The church has over 15,000 congregants at nine Charlotte locations.[^cite_note-165]

Most African Americans in Charlotte are Baptists affiliated with the [National Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Baptist_Convention,_USA,_Inc.), the largest predominantly African American denomination in the United States. African American Methodists are largely affiliated with either the [African Methodist Episcopal Zion Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Methodist_Episcopal_Zion_Church), headquartered in Charlotte, or the [African Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Methodist_Episcopal_Church). African American Pentecostals are represented by several organizations such as the [United House of Prayer for All People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_House_of_Prayer_for_All_People), [Church of God in Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_God_in_Christ), and the [United Holy Church of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Holy_Church_of_America).

The [Presbyterian Church (USA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_\(USA\)) ([Mainline Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainline_Protestant)) is now the fourth largest denomination in Charlotte, with 68,000 members and 206 congregations. The second largest Presbyterian denomination, the [Presbyterian Church in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_in_America) ([Evangelical Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Protestants)) has 43 churches and 12,000 members, followed by the [Associate Reformed Presbyterian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_Reformed_Presbyterian_Church) ([Evangelical Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Protestants)) with 63 churches and 9,500 members.[^cite_note-thearda.com-166]

The [Advent Christian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advent_Christian_Church) is headquartered in Charlotte. The [Western North Carolina Annual Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_North_Carolina_Annual_Conference) of the [United Methodist Church](32304.md) is also headquartered in Charlotte.[^cite_note-167] Both [Reformed Theological Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Theological_Seminary) and [Gordon-Conwell Theological Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon-Conwell_Theological_Seminary) have campuses there; more recently, the [religious studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_studies) academic departments of Charlotte's local colleges and universities have also grown considerably.[^cite_note-168]



[St. Peter's Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Catholic_Church_\(Charlotte,_North_Carolina\)), the city's oldest [Catholic Church](606848.md)

The [Roman Catholic Diocese of Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Charlotte) was formally established in 1972. It covers the 46 counties in the western half of North Carolina, with more than 565,000 Catholics including an array of peoples, rites and languages. It operates 93 parishes and missions, 20 schools, and more than 50 ministries and programs.[^cite_note-169] Metro Charlotte is home to around 120,000 Catholics making it the third largest Christian denomination.[^cite_note-thearda.com-166] Charlotte's [Cathedral of Saint Patrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Patrick_in_Charlotte) is the seat of [Bishop of Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Charlotte) [Michael T. Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_T._Martin).[^cite_note-170] [St. Matthew Parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Matthew_Catholic_Church_\(Charlotte,_North_Carolina\)), located in the [Ballantyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballantyne_\(Charlotte_neighborhood\)) neighborhood, is the largest Catholic parish with over 30,000 parishioners.[^cite_note-171] [St. Peter's Catholic Church (Charlotte, North Carolina)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Catholic_Church_\(Charlotte,_North_Carolina\)), located in [Uptown Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uptown_Charlotte) is the oldest Catholic church in Charlotte. It was established in 1851 and until 1940 was the only Catholic church in the city.

The [Greek Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church)'s cathedral for North Carolina, [Holy Trinity Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Trinity_Greek_Orthodox_Cathedral_\(Charlotte,_North_Carolina\)), is located in Charlotte.[^cite_note-172]

Charlotte has the largest [Jewish](25955086.md) population in the Carolinas.[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174] Shalom Park in south Charlotte is the hub of the Jewish community, featuring two synagogues, [Temple Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Israel_\(Charlotte,_North_Carolina\))[^cite_note-175] and Temple Beth El, as well as a community center, the Charlotte Jewish Day School for grades K–5, and the headquarters of the *[Charlotte Jewish News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Jewish_News)*.[^cite_note-176]

### Festivals and events

The Charlotte region is home to many annual festivals and special events. [The Carolina Renaissance Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Renaissance_Festival) operates on Saturdays and Sundays each October and November. Located near the intersection of [NC 73](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Highway_73) and Poplar Tent Road, the Carolina Renaissance Festival is one of the largest renaissance themed events in the country. It features 11 stages of outdoor variety entertainment, a 22-acre village marketplace, an interactive circus, an arts and crafts fair, a jousting tournament, and a feast, all rolled into one non-stop, day-long family adventure.[^cite_note-308]

The Yiasou Greek Festival is a Greek Festival. It began in 1978 and since then has become one of Charlotte's largest cultural events.[^cite_note-309] The Yiasou (the Greek word for Hello, Goodbye and Cheers) Greek Festival features Hellenic cultural exhibits, authentic Greek cuisine and homemade pastries, entertainment, live music and dancing, wine tastings, art, shopping and more.[^cite_note-310]

Taste of Charlotte is a three-day festival offering samples from area restaurants, live entertainment and children's activities. Located on Tryon Street, Taste of Charlotte spans six city blocks from Stonewall to 5th Street.[^cite_note-311][^cite_note-312]

Moo and Brew Fest is an annual craft beer and burger festival that is the largest in North Carolina, held each April and includes various national musical acts.[^cite_note-313][^cite_note-314]

Breakaway Music Festival is a music festival which takes place at the NC Music Factory and consists of hip hop and electronic music artists and DJs.[^cite_note-315]

[Heroes Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heroes_Convention) is an annual comic book convention held in June at the Charlotte Convention Center. Founded in 1982, it is one of the oldest and largest independent comic book conventions in the United States.[^cite_note-316]

Charlotte [Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_Pride) is an annual [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) event held in August. In 2019, the event attracted 200,000 people to Uptown Charlotte.[^cite_note-317] The event's parade became Charlotte's largest annual parade in 2017.[^cite_note-318]

Charlotte Turkey Trot is an annual 5k & 8k running marathon hosted every [Thanksgiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanksgiving_\(United_States\)), it is also the largest running event in the state of North Carolina.[^cite_note-319][^cite_note-320][^cite_note-321]

Charlotte also has the [Charlotte Regional Farmers Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Regional_Farmers_Market) where local farmers sell their [produce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Produce).[^cite_note-Daniel_2011_p._101-322]

Three annual arts and cultural festivals celebrating visual and performing arts are hosted throughout the Charlotte region: BOOM Charlotte[^cite_note-323] hosted in the Camp North End area, the Charlotte International Arts Festival[^cite_note-324] hosted in Ballantyne, and Charlotte SHOUT\![^cite_note-325] hosted in Uptown.

### Zoos and aquariums

Charlotte is "... the largest metropolitan area in the United States without a zoo".[^cite_note-326] The Charlotte Zoo initiative is a proposal to allocate 250 acres (101 ha) of natural North Carolina land to be dedicated to the zoological foundation, which was incorporated in 2008. On August 18, 2012, Channel 14 News says that the initiative is "... still a few years away" and the plot of land is "... just seven miles from the center of uptown". According to the news channel, "... the zoo will cost roughly \$300 million, and will be completely privately-funded."[^cite_note-327] The Charlotte Observer references two other zoos, the [Riverbanks Zoo and Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverbanks_Zoo_and_Garden) and the [North Carolina Zoological Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Zoological_Park) as two "great zoos" that are accessible from the Charlotte-Mecklenburg area, both roughly more than 70 miles away.[^cite_note-328]

Charlotte is also served by the [Sea Life Charlotte-Concord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Life_Charlotte-Concord) Aquarium in the nearby city of [Concord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concord,_North_Carolina). The aquarium is 30,000 square feet in size, and is part of the [Concord Mills mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concord_Mills). The aquarium opened on February 20, 2014.[^cite_note-329][^cite_note-330]

### Libraries



[ImaginOn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ImaginOn) Children's Theater and Library

The [Charlotte Mecklenburg Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Mecklenburg_Library) serves the Charlotte area with a large collection (more than 1.5 million) of books, CDs and DVDs at 15 locations in the city of Charlotte, with branches in the surrounding towns of [Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthews,_North_Carolina), [Mint Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mint_Hill,_North_Carolina), [Huntersville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntersville,_North_Carolina), [Cornelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelius,_North_Carolina) and [Davidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidson,_North_Carolina). All locations provide free access to Internet-enabled computers and WiFi, and a library card from one location is accepted at all 20 locations.[^cite_note-Charlotte_Mecklenburg_Library_Info-331]

Although the library's roots go back to the *Charlotte Literary and Library Association,* founded on January 16, 1891,[^cite_note-cmstory.org_Web_Site-332] the state-chartered [Carnegie Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Library), which opened on the current North Tryon site of the Main Library, was the first non-subscription library opened to members of the public in the city of Charlotte. The philanthropist [Andrew Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Carnegie) donated \$25,000 for a library building, on the condition that the city of Charlotte donate a site and \$2,500 per year for books and salaries,[^cite_note-Public_Library_of_Charlotte_and_Mec-333] and that the state grant a charter for the library. All conditions were met, and the Charlotte Carnegie Library opened in an imposing classical building on July 2, 1903.

The 1903 state charter also required that a library be opened for the disenfranchised African-American population of Charlotte. This was completed in 1905 with the opening of the *Brevard Street Library for Negroes*, an independent library in Brooklyn, a historically black area of Charlotte, on the corner of Brevard and East Second Streets (now Martin Luther King Boulevard).[^cite_note-cmstory.org-334] The Brevard Street Library was the first library for African Americans in the state of North Carolina,[^cite_note-cmstory.org-334] and some sources say in the southeast.[^cite_note-charmeck.org_Web_Site-335] The library was closed in 1961 when the Brooklyn neighborhood in Second Ward was redeveloped, but its role as a cultural center for African-Americans in Charlotte is continued by the Beatties Ford and West Boulevard branches of the library system, as well as by Charlotte's African-American Cultural Center.

## Government



Charlotte-Mecklenburg Government Center

Charlotte has a [council–manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council%E2%80%93manager) form of government. The [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Charlotte,_North_Carolina) and [Charlotte City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_City_Council) are elected every two years, with no [term limits](505825.md). The mayor is *ex officio* chair of the City Council, and only votes in case of a tie. Unlike other mayors in council–manager systems, Charlotte's mayor has the power to [veto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veto_power_in_the_United_States#In_local_government) [ordinances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_ordinance) passed by the council; the council can override a mayoral veto by a vote of seven of its ten members.[^cite_note-384] The Council appoints a [city manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_manager) to serve as chief administrative officer.[^cite_note-385]

Unlike some other cities and towns in North Carolina, elections are held on a partisan basis. [Vi Lyles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi_Lyles), a [Democrat](5043544.md) [elected in 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Charlotte_mayoral_election), became the 59th mayor of Charlotte, North Carolina. She was elected to her third term in 2022.[^cite_note-386]

[Patrick Cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Cannon), a Democrat, was sworn in as mayor on December 2, 2013.[^cite_note-387] On March 26, 2014, Cannon was arrested on public corruption charges. Later the same day, he resigned as mayor.[^cite_note-388] Mayor Pro Tem [Michael Barnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Barnes_\(North_Carolina_politician\)) served as Acting Mayor until April 7, when the City Council selected State Senator [Dan Clodfelter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Clodfelter), also a Democrat, to serve the remainder of Cannon's term.[^cite_note-389] Former Mecklenburg County Commission chairwoman [Jennifer Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Roberts_\(politician\)) defeated Clodfelter in the 2015 Democratic primary and went on to win the general election, becoming the first Democratic woman to be elected to the post.[^cite_note-390] She was ousted in the 2017 Democratic primary by Mayor Pro Tem [Vi Lyles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi_Lyles), who later defeated Republican City Councilman Kenny Smith in the general election to become Mayor of Charlotte.[^cite_note-391]

Historically, voters have been friendly to moderates of both parties. However, in recent years, Charlotte has swung heavily to the Democrats. Republican strength is concentrated in the southeastern portion of the city, while Democratic strength is concentrated in the south-central, eastern, and northern areas.[^cite_note-392] The city had a Republican mayor from 1987 to 2009.[^cite_note-393][^cite_note-394][^cite_note-395]

The Charlotte City Council has 11 members (7 from districts and 4 [at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-large)). Democrats control the council with a 9-to-2 advantage, winning all 4 of the at-large seats in the November 2013, 2015, and 2017 municipal elections. While the City Council is responsible for passing ordinances, the city's budget, and other policies, all decisions can be overridden by the [North Carolina General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_General_Assembly), since North Carolina municipalities do not have [home rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_home_rule). While municipal powers have been broadly construed since the 1960s, the General Assembly still retains considerable authority over local matters.[^cite_note-396]

Charlotte is split between three [congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_district) on the federal level the [8th district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina's_8th_congressional_district), represented by Republican [Mark Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Harris_\(North_Carolina_politician\)), the [12th district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina's_12th_congressional_district), represented by Democrat [Alma Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma_Adams), and the [14th district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina's_14th_congressional_district), represented by Republican [Tim Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Moore_\(North_Carolina_politician\)). Charlotte is represented by ten members of the [North Carolina House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_House_of_Representatives), [Mary Gardner Belk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Gardner_Belk) (D-88th), [Terry M. Smith University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_C._Smith_University), [Johnson & Wales University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson_&_Wales_University), [Queens University of Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens_University_of_Charlotte), and the [University of North Carolina at Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Charlotte).[^cite_note-420][^cite_note-421][^cite_note-422][^cite_note-423] Several notable colleges are located in the metropolitan suburbs. Located in nearby [Davidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidson,_North_Carolina), North Carolina is [Davidson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davidson_College).[^cite_note-424] Additional colleges in the area include [Belmont Abbey College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont_Abbey_College) in the suburb of [Belmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont,_North_Carolina), North Carolina, [Gaston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaston_College) with its main campus in the suburb of [Dallas, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas,_North_Carolina) and [Wingate University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wingate_University) in the suburb of [Wingate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wingate,_North_Carolina), North Carolina.[^cite_note-425][^cite_note-426] Also nearby are [Winthrop University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winthrop_University), Clinton Junior College, [York Technical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_Technical_College) in [Rock Hill, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Hill,_South_Carolina), and [Gardner-Webb University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardner-Webb_University) in [Boiling Springs, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boiling_Springs,_North_Carolina) in the westernmost part of the [Charlotte area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_metropolitan_area).[^cite_note-427][^cite_note-428][^cite_note-429]

UNC Charlotte is the city's largest university. It is located in [University City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_City,_Charlotte,_North_Carolina), the northeastern portion of Charlotte, which is also home to [University Research Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Research_Park), a 3,200 acres (13 km<sup>2</sup>) research and corporate park. With more than 30,000 students, UNC Charlotte is the second largest university in the state system.[^cite_note-430]

Central Piedmont Community College is the largest [community college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_college) in [the Carolinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Carolinas), with more than 70,000 students each year and 6 campuses throughout the Charlotte-Mecklenburg region.[^cite_note-431] CPCC is part of the statewide [North Carolina Community College System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Community_College_System).

The Charlotte School of Law opened its doors in Charlotte in 2006 and was fully accredited by the American Bar Association in 2011. The law school offered the [Juris Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juris_Doctor) degree but the Bar association rescinded the accreditation in 2017. Charlotte School of Law, once the largest law school in the Carolinas, has ceased to operate.[^cite_note-432]

[Pfeiffer University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfeiffer_University_at_Charlotte) has a satellite campus in Charlotte.[^cite_note-433][^cite_note-434] [Wake Forest University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_University), with its main campus in [Winston-Salem, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston-Salem,_North_Carolina), also operates a satellite campus of its Babcock Graduate School of Management in the Uptown area.[^cite_note-435] On March 24, 2021, it was announced [Wake Forest School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake_Forest_School_of_Medicine) would expand a 20-acre campus in Charlotte by 2024.[^cite_note-436] The [Connecticut School of Broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_School_of_Broadcasting), [DeVry University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeVry_University), and [ECPI University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECPI_University) all have branches in Charlotte.[^cite_note-437][^cite_note-438][^cite_note-439] The [Universal Technical Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Technical_Institute) has the NASCAR Technical Institute in nearby [Mooresville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mooresville,_North_Carolina), serving the Charlotte area.[^cite_note-440] [Montreat College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreat_College) (Charlotte) maintains a School of Professional and Adult Studies in the city. Additionally, [Union Presbyterian Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Presbyterian_Seminary) has a non-residential campus offering the Master of Arts in Christian Education, and the [Master of Divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Divinity) in Charlotte near the Beverley Woods area.[^cite_note-441]

The [North Carolina Research Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Research_Campus), a 350-acre [biotechnology](4502.md) hub located northeast of Charlotte in the city of [Kannapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannapolis), is a public-private venture including eight universities, one community college, the [David H. Murdock Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_H._Murdock_Research_Institute) (DHMRI), the [U.S. Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Agriculture) (USDA) and corporate entities that collaborate to advance the fields of human health, nutrition and agriculture. Partnering educational organizations include UNC Charlotte and [Rowan-Cabarrus Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowan-Cabarrus_Community_College), from the Charlotte region, as well as [Appalachian State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_State_University), [University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Chapel_Hill), [Duke University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_University), [University of North Carolina at Greensboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Carolina_at_Greensboro), [North Carolina A\&T State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_A\&T_State_University), [Shaw University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaw_University), [North Carolina Central University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Central_University) and [North Carolina State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_University).[^cite_note-N.C._Research_Campus_Partners_and_Research-442] The research campus is part of a larger effort by leaders in the Charlotte area to attract energy, health, and other knowledge-based industries that contribute to North Carolina's strength in biotechnology.[^cite_note-443]

## Media

Main article: [Media in Charlotte, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_in_Charlotte,_North_Carolina)

### Newspapers

Charlotte has one major daily newspaper, *[The Charlotte Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Charlotte_Observer)*.[^cite_note-444] It boasts the largest circulation in North Carolina[^cite_note-445] and South Carolina. A local news website, *The Charlotte Ledger*, is also published in the city.[^cite_note-446]

### Radio

Charlotte is the 24th largest radio market in the nation, according to [Nielsen Audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Audio). While major groups like [iHeartMedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHeartMedia) and [Urban One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_One) have stations serving Charlotte, several smaller groups also own and operate stations in the area.[^cite_note-447][^cite_note-448] The local [National Public Radio](29697232.md) news affiliate is [WFAE News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WFAE), which sponsors a number of podcasts and radio shows.[^cite_note-449]

### Television

According to [Nielsen Media Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_Media_Research), Charlotte is the 22nd largest television market in the nation (as of the 2016–2017 season) and the largest in the state of North Carolina.[^cite_note-450] Major television stations located in Charlotte include [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) affiliate [WBTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBTV) 3 (the oldest television station in the Carolinas),[^cite_note-451] [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) affiliate [WSOC-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WSOC-TV) 9,[^cite_note-452] [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) affiliate [WCNC-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WCNC-TV) 36,[^cite_note-453] [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_station_\(North_America\)) station [WCCB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WCCB) 18,[^cite_note-454] and [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS) member station [WTVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTVI) 42.[^cite_note-455] One cable sports network is headquartered in Charlotte: the [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN)-controlled [SEC Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEC_Network).[^cite_note-456] [Raycom Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raycom_Sports) is also headquartered in Charlotte.[^cite_note-457][^cite_note-458]

Other stations serving the Charlotte market include [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company) affiliate [WJZY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WJZY) 46 in [Belmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont,_North_Carolina),[^cite_note-459] [UNC-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS_North_Carolina)/[PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS) member station WUNG-TV 58 in [Concord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concord,_North_Carolina), independent station [WAXN-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WAXN-TV) 64 (a sister to WSOC-TV) in [Kannapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannapolis,_North_Carolina), and two stations in [Rock Hill, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Hill,_South_Carolina): [CW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_CW) owned-and-operated station [WMYT-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WMYT-TV) 55 (a sister to WJZY)[^cite_note-460] and [PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS) member station [WNSC-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNSC-TV) 30. ## Infrastructure

### City services

#### Emergency medical services

[Emergency medical services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_medical_services) for the city of Charlotte are provided by Mecklenburg EMS Agency (MEDIC). MEDIC received over 160,000 calls in 2022 and transported over 107,000 patients in Mecklenburg County.[^cite_note-464] The agency employs over 600 paramedics, EMTs, EMDs and admin staff.[^cite_note-465]

In addition to dispatching MEDIC's EMS calls, the agency also dispatches all county fire calls outside of the city of Charlotte.[^cite_note-medic911.com-466]

#### Hospitals



[Carolinas Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolinas_Medical_Center) is the flagship hospital of [Atrium Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrium_Health)&#x20;

Hospitals in Charlotte include [Atrium Health Mercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrium_Health_Mercy),[^cite_note-467] [Atrium Health Pineville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrium_Health_Pineville),[^cite_note-468] [Atrium Health University City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrium_Health_University_City),[^cite_note-469] Carolinas ContinueCare Pineville,[^cite_note-470] Atrium Health [Carolinas Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolinas_Medical_Center)[^cite_note-471] / [Levine Children's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levine_Children's_Hospital), Novant Health Charlotte Orthopedic Hospital,[^cite_note-472] Novant Health Hemby Children's Hospital,[^cite_note-473] and Novant Health Presbyterian Medical Center.[^cite_note-474] Charlotte-Mecklenburg Hospital Authority (dba [Atrium Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrium_Health)) is the public hospital authority of Mecklenburg County.[^cite_note-475]

#### Fire department

The [Charlotte Fire Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Fire_Department) provides fire suppression, emergency medical services, public education, hazardous materials (HAZMAT) mitigation, technical rescues, and fire prevention and inspection with 1,164 personnel. Forty-three fire stations are strategically scattered throughout Charlotte to provide a reasonable response time to emergencies in the city limits.[^cite_note-476]

#### Law enforcement and crime

See also: [Charlotte-Mecklenburg Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte-Mecklenburg_Police_Department)

The Charlotte-Mecklenburg Police Department (CMPD) is a combined jurisdiction agency. The CMPD has law enforcement jurisdiction in both the city of Charlotte and the few unincorporated areas left in Mecklenburg County. The other small towns maintain their own law enforcement agencies for their own jurisdictions. The department consists of approximately 1,700 sworn law enforcement officers, 550 civilian personnel, and more than 400 volunteers.[^cite_note-477]

An average of 4,939 vehicles are stolen every year in Charlotte.[^cite_note-478][^cite_note-479][^cite_note-480][^cite_note-481][^cite_note-482]

According to the Congressional Quarterly Press; '2008 City Crime Rankings: Crime in Metropolitan America,' Charlotte, North Carolina ranks as the 62nd most dangerous city larger than 75,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-483] However, the entire Charlotte-Gastonia Metropolitan Statistical Area ranked as 27th most dangerous out of 338 metro areas.[^cite_note-484][^cite_note-485]

#### Waste treatment

Charlotte has a municipal waste system consisting of trash pickup, water distribution, and waste treatment. There are five waste water treatment plants operated by Charlotte Water (previously Charlotte-Mecklenburg Utility Department).[^cite_note-486] Charlotte has a [biosolids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosolids) program.[^cite_note-Charlotte_Water-487] Some Chester residents spoke out against the program on February 26, 2013.[^cite_note-488] Charlotte's sludge is handled, transported, and spread on farm fields in Chester by a company called Synagro, a wholly owned subsidiary of the [Carlyle Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlyle_Group)[^cite_note-489] Charlotte's sludge is of the "CLASS B" variety, which means it still contains detectable levels of [pathogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathogen).[^cite_note-Charlotte_Water-487][^cite_note-490]

### Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in Charlotte, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Charlotte,_North_Carolina)

#### Car Ownership

The city of Charlotte has a lower than average percentage of households without a car. In 2015, 7.4 percent of Charlotte households lacked a car, and decreased to 6 percent in 2016. The national average was 8.7 percent in 2016. Charlotte averaged 1.65 cars per household in 2016, compared to a national average of 1.8.[^cite_note-491][^cite_note-492][^cite_note-493] This may be caused by Charlotte's low rankings in public transportation and walkability compared to other US cities.[^cite_note-494][^cite_note-495]

#### Mass transit

Main article: [Charlotte Area Transit System](2173439.md)



The [Blue Line's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynx_Blue_Line) Bland Street Station in Charlotte's South End neighborhood

The [Charlotte Area Transit System](2173439.md) (CATS) is the agency responsible for operating mass transit in the Charlotte metropolitan area, carrying over 16 million riders annually. Established in 1999 and administered as a department of the city of Charlotte, CATS operates [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) transit, [streetcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streetcar), [express buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter_bus), [local buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_bus), and special bus services serving Charlotte and the surrounding area in addition to other programs such as [vanpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanpool).[^cite_note-496][^cite_note-497]

CATS' rail arm, LYNX Rapid Transit Services, comprises two lines as of fall 2020. The Blue Line is an 18.9‑mile line north–south light rail line running through [South End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_End_\(Charlotte_neighborhood\)), [Center City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uptown_Charlotte), [NoDa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NoDa), and [University City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_City_\(Charlotte_neighborhood\)).[^cite_note-498] The [CityLYNX Gold Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CityLYNX_Gold_Line) streetcar, Phase 1 of which opened in 2015, is under Phase 2 construction as of fall 2020. When completed, the Gold Line will link the Beatties Ford neighborhood through Uptown and then south and east to the Elizabeth neighborhood.[^cite_note-499] The [LYNX Silver Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LYNX_Silver_Line), a light rail line in the pre-project development phase as of fall 2020, will link the southeastern suburbs of [Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthews,_North_Carolina), [Stallings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stallings,_North_Carolina), and [Indian Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Trail,_North_Carolina) with [Uptown Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uptown_Charlotte) and the future Charlotte Gateway Station before extending west to [Charlotte Douglas International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Douglas_International_Airport) and across the Catawba River to [Belmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont,_North_Carolina) in Gaston County.[^cite_note-500]

The bulk of CATS ridership is derived from its extensive bus network, which has its main hub at the Charlotte Transportation Center in Uptown, which also connects to the Blue and Gold lines. Other bus hubs are located at community transit centers in SouthPark, Eastland, and at Rosa Parks Place. CATS operates express buses to outlying parts of the city and some commuter bus to the northern suburbs in the Lake Norman area under the MetroRAPID umbrella.[^cite_note-501][^cite_note-502]

#### Roads and highways



I-85/I-485 [turbine interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interchange_\(road\)#Turbine_interchange) under construction just north of Charlotte, 2013

Charlotte's central location between the population centers of the [Northeast](431669.md) and [Southeast](816925.md) has made it a transportation focal point and primary distribution center, with two major interstate highways, [I-85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_85_in_North_Carolina) and [I-77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_77_in_North_Carolina), intersecting near the city's center. The latter highway also connects to the population centers of the [Rust Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_Belt).[^cite_note-503][^cite_note-504]

Charlotte's [beltway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_road), designated [I-485](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_485) and simply called "485" by local residents, was under construction for over 20 years, but funding problems slowed its progress.[^cite_note-505] The final segment was finished in mid-2015.[^cite_note-506] I-485 has a total circumference of approximately 67 mi (108 km). Within the city, the [I-277](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_277_\(North_Carolina\)) loop freeway encircles Charlotte's uptown (usually referred to by its two separate sections, the [John Belk Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_M._Belk) and the Brookshire Freeway) while [Route 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Route_4) links major roads in a loop between I-277 and I-485. [Independence Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_74#North_Carolina), which carries [US 74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_74#North_Carolina) and links downtown with the Matthews area, is undergoing an expansion and widening in the eastern part of the city.[^cite_note-507]

#### Air



[Charlotte Douglas International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Douglas_International_Airport) with the Uptown Charlotte skyline in the background

In 2011, [Charlotte Douglas International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Douglas_International_Airport) was the sixth-busiest airport in both the U.S. and the world overall as measured by traffic (aircraft movements).[^cite_note-508][^cite_note-509] The airport handled just over 50 million travellers in 2019, as well as many domestic and international carriers including [Air Canada](145623.md), [Lufthansa](97733.md), and [Volaris](3717400.md). It is a major hub for [American Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Airlines), having historically been a hub for its predecessors [US Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Airways) and [Piedmont Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_Airlines).[^cite_note-510][^cite_note-511][^cite_note-512] Nonstop flights are available to many destinations across the United States, [Canada](5042916.md), [Central America](6121.md), the [Caribbean](18956035.md), [Europe](9239.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), and [South America](26769.md).[^cite_note-513][^cite_note-514] The [145th Airlift Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/145th_Airlift_Wing) of [North Carolina Air National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Air_National_Guard) is also located east of the airport.[^cite_note-515][^cite_note-516]

#### Intercity transportation

See also: [Charlotte station (Amtrak)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_station_\(Amtrak\))

Charlotte is served daily by three [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) routes with ten daily trips from a station on North Tryon Street, just outside downtown.

- The *[Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crescent_\(Amtrak\))* connects Charlotte with [New York](8210131.md), [Philadelphia](50585.md), [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore,_Maryland), [Washington, D.C.](108956.md); [Charlottesville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville,_Virginia), and [Greensboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensboro,_North_Carolina) to the north, and [Greenville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenville,_South_Carolina), [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta,_Georgia), [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Alabama), [Meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian,_Mississippi) and [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans,_Louisiana) to the south. It arrives overnight once in each direction.

  [^cite_note-517]

- The *[Carolinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolinian_\(train\))* connects Charlotte with New York; Philadelphia; Baltimore; Washington, D.C.; [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia); [Raleigh](57845.md); [Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_North_Carolina); and Greensboro. Charlotte is the southern terminus, with the northbound train leaving just before the morning rush and the southbound train arriving in the evening.

  [^cite_note-518]

- The *[Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(train\))*, a regional companion of the *Carolinian*, connects Charlotte with Greensboro, Durham and Raleigh with four daily round trips. Charlotte is the southern terminus.

  [^cite_note-519]

Charlotte is also served by both [Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines) and low-cost curbside carrier [Megabus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabus_\(North_America\)). Charlotte is a service stop for Greyhound routes running to Atlanta, Detroit, [Jacksonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville,_Florida), New York and Philadelphia. It is also a stop for buses running out of Megabus' hub in Atlanta, with connections to Megabus' northeastern routes out of New York.[^cite_note-520]

The city is planning a new centralized downtown multimodal station called [Gateway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Gateway_Station). It is expected to house Amtrak, Greyhound and the future LYNX Red Line.[^cite_note-521] It is under construction at the former site of the Greyhound station; Greyhound is currently operating from a temporary station nearby.[^cite_note-522]
