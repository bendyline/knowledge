The **Montréal Victoire** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Victoire de Montréal*) are a professional [ice hockey](14790.md) team based in the [Greater Montreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Montreal) area that competes in the [Professional Women's Hockey League](74699992.md) (PWHL). They are one of the six charter franchises of the league. The team plays its home games at [Place Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_Bell) in [Laval, Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laval,_Quebec). The Victoire are the current [Walter Cup](76537110.md) champions, having won the championship in [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Walter_Cup_Finals).[^cite_note-3]

## History

### Founding and inaugural season (2023–24)

On August 29, 2023, it was announced that one of the PWHL's first six franchises would be located in Montreal.[^cite_note-4] On September 1, [Danièle Sauvageau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dani%C3%A8le_Sauvageau), the one-time coach for [Canada's national team](1329942.md), was announced as the team's general manager.[^cite_note-5] [Kori Cheverie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kori_Cheverie), a former assistant coach with the Canadian national team and the first woman to have coached a Canadian men's hockey team, was named the team's inaugural coach on September 13.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Montreal's first three players—Canadian national team members [Marie-Philip Poulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-Philip_Poulin), [Laura Stacey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Stacey), and [Ann-Renée Desbiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann-Ren%C3%A9e_Desbiens)—were signed on September 5, 2023.[^cite_note-8] At the [2023 PWHL Draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_PWHL_Draft) on September 18, the team selected 15 players; their first pick, at sixth overall, was Canadian national team member [Erin Ambrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Ambrose).[^cite_note-9]

The team colours were officially announced on November 14, 2023, as burgundy, "sand", and "storm".[^cite_note-10] Later that month, it was revealed that the team would host the majority of its games at the [Verdun Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verdun_Auditorium), with four games at [Place Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_Bell).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] On December 29, the team announced that Poulin would serve as team captain, with Stacey and [Erin Ambrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Ambrose) serving as assistant captains.[^cite_note-13]



Montreal celebrating a victory over PWHL Minnesota in 2024.

Montreal's first ever game took place on January 2, 2024, against [PWHL Ottawa](74756360.md); the 8,318 fans in attendance at Ottawa's [TD Place Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TD_Place_Arena) set a new attendance record for professional women's hockey.[^cite_note-\:0-14] Claire Dalton scored the first goal in franchise history. Although Montreal twice trailed in the game, [Ann-Sophie Bettez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann-Sophie_Bettez) scored in overtime to secure the team's first win by a score of 3–2.[^cite_note-\:0-14] The team hosted its first home game on January 13, with [PWHL Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Fleet) securing a 3–2 overtime win in front of a sold-out crowd at Verdun Auditorium.[^cite_note-15]

On February 16, 2024, Montreal played a match hosted by [PWHL Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Sceptres) at [Scotiabank Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotiabank_Arena) in a game dubbed "The Battle on Bay Street".[^cite_note-16] The game set a league and women's hockey attendance record with a sell-out crowd of 19,285, beating the previous record of 18,013 at the [2013 IIHF Women's World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_IIHF_Women's_World_Championship).[^cite_note-RecordBreak-17] Two months later, on April 20, Montréal hosted Toronto in its first-ever home match at the [Bell Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Centre), selling out the arena and setting another new attendance record at 21,105.[^cite_note-18] This game was known as "The Duel at the Top", with the two teams vying for first place in the standings.[^cite_note-19] Montréal managed to draw large crowds throughout the season.[^cite_note-20]

Montreal clinched a berth in the inaugural PWHL playoffs on March on April 24 with a 5–2 win over [PWHL New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Sirens).[^cite_note-\:02-21] They faced Boston in the first round of the playoffs, and lost three straight games in overtime—including a triple-overtime affair in the second game of the series—to get eliminated from contention.[^cite_note-\:1-22][^cite_note-23] After the season, [Erin Ambrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Ambrose) was named the league's top defender for its inaugural year.[^cite_note-24]

### Regular season titles and Walter Cup (2024–present)

In September 2024, the team announced that Place Bell would serve as its primary home venue for the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_PWHL_season).[^cite_note-25] The Victoire enjoyed success in the regular season, finishing first in the league.[^cite_note-26] Team captain [Marie-Philip Poulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-Philip_Poulin) led the league in goals,[^cite_note-GoalsAward25-27] and subsequently became the first Victoire player to receive the PWHL's [Billie Jean King Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Jean_King_Most_Valuable_Player) honour.[^cite_note-28] As well, the [International Ice Hockey Federation](16760449.md) named her the [IIHF Female Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_Female_Player_of_the_Year) for her feats with both the Victoire and the Canadian national team.[^cite_note-29] The Victoire faced the Ottawa Charge in the semi-finals of the Walter Cup playoffs, but were ousted in four games.[^cite_note-30]

With the arrival of the PWHL's first [expansion teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_team), the [Seattle Torrent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Torrent) and [Vancouver Goldeneyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Goldeneyes), the Victoire lost four players in the [2025 expansion draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_PWHL_Draft), including defender [Cayla Barnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayla_Barnes) to the Torrent.[^cite_note-31] The [2025–26 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_PWHL_season) would see further success, despite the loss of Poulin to injury for much of March and April.[^cite_note-32] The Victoire were notably dominant on home ice, with an 11–2–1–1 record at Place Bell and the Bell Centre,[^cite_note-33] and finished with a 17–5–2–6 record overall. They tied the [Boston Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Fleet) to lead the league with 62 points, winning their second consecutive regular season title on the tiebreaker after a shootout victory over the Torrent in the final game of the season.[^cite_note-34] The Victoire achieved their first postseason series victory, defeating the Frost in five games to reach the [Walter Cup Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Walter_Cup_Finals) for the first time.[^cite_note-35] Facing the Ottawa Charge in the finals, the Victoire prevailed in a four-game series, becoming the first Canadian team to hoist the Walter Cup.[^cite_note-36]

## Team identity



Inaugural season logo for PWHL Montreal.

Montreal operated without league branding during the PWHL's inaugural season, along with the other charter franchises. The team was known as PWHL Montreal and wore a league-wide jersey template that featured the city's name diagonally on the front.[^cite_note-37] The team did have its own colour scheme, featuring burgundy, black, and beige. In October 2023, the league registered a trademark for the name Montreal Echo.[^cite_note-38] Ultimately, when the league unveiled franchise names in September 2024, the team was given the name Victoire; it is a nod to the city's history of success in ice hockey.[^cite_note-\:03-39] According to a report from *[The Hockey News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hockey_News)*, other names in contention for the team included the Lumieres and Metro.[^cite_note-40] In addition to the Victoire name, the team's logo was unveiled, featuring "MV" initials and a [fleur-de-lis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleur-de-lis), Quebec's national symbol.[^cite_note-\:03-39] The team retained its colour scheme.
