**Petr Macinka** (born 18 August 1978) is a Czech politician and former presidential spokesperson who has served as [Deputy Prime Minister of the Czech Republic](81315178.md) and [Minister of Foreign Affairs](16070604.md) since December 2025, in the [third cabinet of Andrej Babiš](81315178.md).[^cite_note-1] He has been the leader of the Czech political party [Motorists for Themselves](76614519.md) since 2022, and was elected as an MP in the [2025 Czech parliamentary election](65703300.md). Macinka served as the [Minister of the Environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Environment_\(Czech_Republic\)) from December 2025 to February 2026. A long-time ally of former Czech President [Václav Klaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Klaus), he previously served as a spokesman at the [Václav Klaus Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Klaus_Institute) and as deputy director of the Presidential Press Department.

## Early life and career

Macinka was born in [Hodonín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodon%C3%ADn) in 1978.[^cite_note-2] He studied political science and international relations at the private [CEVRO Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEVRO_Institute), obtaining a master's degree in 2013.[^cite_note-3] From 2008 to 2013, he worked as deputy director of the press department in the office of the president under [Václav Klaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Klaus).[^cite_note-4]

## Political career

In 2022, Macinka took over a dormant minor party and rebranded it as *Motoristé sobě* (English: Motorists for Themselves). The party first ran in the 2022 Prague municipal elections on a platform opposing bicycle lanes and promoting free public transport for motorists. Although it failed to pass the electoral threshold in Prague, it gained media attention for its [anti-green](8149492.md) and populist rhetoric.[^cite_note-5]

In 2024, Macinka led the party into [a coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C5%99%C3%ADsaha_and_Motorists) with [Přísaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C5%99%C3%ADsaha) for the [2024 European Parliament election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Parliament_election_in_the_Czech_Republic). The coalition won 10.26% of the vote and gained two seats, one of which went to the Motorists' election leader [Filip Turek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filip_Turek). Following the election, the party grew in national profile. At its first congress in late 2024, Macinka was re-elected leader, and Turek was appointed honorary president.[^cite_note-seznam-turek-6]

Ahead of the [2025 Czech parliamentary election](65703300.md), Macinka declared that the party aimed to enter the national parliament and overtake the Civic Democratic Party (ODS). He expressed openness to forming a coalition with [ANO 2011](40808475.md), while rejecting any cooperation with left-wing parties.[^cite_note-7] In the election, the Motorists received 6.8% of the vote and won seats in the Chamber of Deputies for the first time, with Macinka among the MPs elected in the [South Moravian Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Moravian_Region).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

### Minister of Foreign Affairs

In January 2026, Macinka reaffirmed support for [Ukraine](31750.md) during a visit to Kyiv, stating that the Czech Republic advocates for [diplomatic efforts to end the war](70255183.md) while supporting continued ammunition supplies.[^cite_note-10]

During talks with Israeli Foreign Minister [Gideon Sa'ar](485357.md) on 20 January 2026, Macinka expressed support for a [Gaza ceasefire](81215079.md).[^cite_note-11]

## Views

The Motorists have faced criticism over alleged ties to far-right figures and rhetoric.[^cite_note-12] In 2024, [Filip Turek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filip_Turek), the party's top European election candidate, was photographed wearing a helmet with neo-Nazi symbolism and appeared to give a Nazi salute. Macinka defended Turek, calling the criticism a "pseudo-problem" and part of a political smear campaign.[^cite_note-seznam-turek-6] In October 2025, Macinka once again defended Turek after an archive of his deleted social media posts and comments was published by the newspaper *[Deník N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den%C3%ADk_N)*. The posts included openly racist, sexist, and homophobic statements and numerous allusions to Hitler and Mussolini.[^cite_note-dennikposts-13][^cite_note-noska-14] Macinka expressed full support for Turek, who denied authorship of the posts and said the case was a deliberate attempt to discredit him.[^cite_note-noska-14]

In 2025 Macinka expressed that he was opposed to a referendum on Czech withdrawal from the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-15] He also opposes adoption of the Euro, [same-sex marriage](92656.md), progressive taxation, and the EU's 2035 [phase-out of fossil fuel vehicles](55503807.md), which he called "industrial self-destruction".[^cite_note-16]

His potential nomination to the ministry of environment sparked several protests in Prague and other cities in the Czech Republic.[^cite_note-17] On 15 December 2025, after he was inaugurated as the interim head of the environment ministry, Macinka said: "I would like to reassure the Czech public and all ordinary citizens and to tell them that the [climate crisis](17987598.md) in the Czech Republic is over as of today".[^cite_note-18]

In April 2026, Macinka wrote to the Czech News Agency that he considered the nonprofit environmental organization [Hnutí DUHA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hnut%C3%AD_DUHA), without exaggeration, to be a [terrorist](30636.md) organization.[^cite_note-19] In response, the organization called on Macinka to publicly apologise and sent a pre-trial notice, saying it would sue if he did not.[^cite_note-20] On 24 June 2026, the group filed a lawsuit, as Macinka had not apologised.[^cite_note-21]

Macinka expressed support for relocating the Czech embassy in [Israel](9282173.md) from Tel Aviv to [Jerusalem](16043.md), stating it should have been done earlier. Speaking at a [Hanukkah](7388.md) event in Prague's Palach Square, he called for greater Czech solidarity with Israel and [Jewish](25955086.md) communities worldwide, as well as condemning the recent anti-Semitic [mass shooting at Bondi Beach](81839099.md) in Sydney.[^cite_note-22]

Following the [2026 United States intervention in Venezuela](82021123.md), Macinka called for the de-escalation of regional tensions through diplomatic channels. He advocated for a negotiated settlement that included the [Venezuelan opposition](75340539.md) to ensure national stability.[^cite_note-23]
