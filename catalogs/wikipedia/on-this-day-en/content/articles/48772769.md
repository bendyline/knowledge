**Guy Bernard Parmelin** (French pronunciation: [\[ɡi bɛʁnaʁ paʁməlɛ̃\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); born 9 November 1959) is a Swiss politician and viticulturist who has been a member of the [Federal Council](218299.md) since 2016. A member of the [Swiss People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_People's_Party) (SVP/UDC), he is currently the [president of Switzerland](33730924.md) for 2026, having previously served as president in 2021 and as [vice president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_Switzerland) in 2020 and 2025.[^cite_note-Parliament-1][^cite_note-2] Parmelin has also led the [Department of Economic Affairs, Education and Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Department_of_Economic_Affairs,_Education_and_Research) since 2019. He previously led the [Department of Defence, Civil Protection and Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Department_of_Defence,_Civil_Protection_and_Sport) between 2016 and 2018.

On 2 October 2026 he announced to retire at the end of 2026.[^cite_note-3]

## Early life and education

Parmelin was born 9 November 1959 in [Bursins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursins), the oldest of three children, to Richard Parmelin, a farmer and winegrower, and Jeannine Parmelin (née Favre; 1939–2020). His siblings are Christophe Parmelin and Valérie Cottet ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9e) Parmelin).

He completed his Gymnasium [Matura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matura) in 1977 and then completed a diploma in agriculture at the Cantonal Agricultural College Marcelin in [Morges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morges). In 1985, he completed a [master's degree](170346.md) in [viticulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viticulture).[^cite_note-HDS-4]

## Biography

### Early political career

A master [wine grower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viticulture) by trade, he was elected to the [Grand Council of Vaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Council_of_Vaud) from 1994 until 2003, when he was [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Swiss_federal_election) to the [National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_\(Switzerland\)) for the [canton of Vaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaud).[^cite_note-Parliament-1] From 2000 to 2004, Parmelin was also president of the [Swiss People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_People's_Party) of the canton of Vaud. On 9 December 2015, he was elected by the [Federal Assembly](1797312.md) to the [Federal Council](218299.md) in replacement of [Eveline Widmer-Schlumpf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eveline_Widmer-Schlumpf).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

### Member of the Federal Council



Parmelin (seated centre) in the official [Federal Council](218299.md) group photo for 2026, designed according to his wishes

After the [Swiss People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_People's_Party) won a record vote of over 29% in the [2015 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Swiss_federal_election), Federal Councillor [Eveline Widmer-Schlumpf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eveline_Widmer-Schlumpf) announced she would not run for reelection.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] She had been expelled from the SVP/UDC shortly after her election in 2007, whereupon she founded the splinter [Conservative Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland) (BDP/PBD). The SVP/UDC was expected to take Widmer-Schlumpf's seat; it put forward three candidates, including Parmelin, who was ultimately [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Swiss_Federal_Council_election).[^cite_note-Gains-9]

Parmelin became the first SVP/UDC Federal Councillor from the [French-speaking part of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romandy).[^cite_note-Gains-9][^cite_note-10] He was selected to become head of the [Federal Department of Defence, Civil Protection and Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Department_of_Defence,_Civil_Protection_and_Sports), replacing fellow party member [Ueli Maurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ueli_Maurer), who became head of the [Federal Department of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Department_of_Finance).[^cite_note-11] Starting in 2019, Parmelin became the head of the [Federal Department of Economic Affairs, Education and Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Department_of_Economic_Affairs,_Education_and_Research). He served as Vice President of Switzerland in 2020. He assumed the presidency on 1 January 2021 alongside Vice President [Ignazio Cassis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignazio_Cassis).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]



Parmelin with Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) in front of the [Villa La Grange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_La_Grange) on 16 June 2021

On 16 June 2021, as President of Switzerland, Parmelin hosted the [2021 Russia–United States summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russia%E2%80%93United_States_summit) at [Villa La Grange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_La_Grange) in Geneva between [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) and [Joe Biden](145422.md). He wished them "a fruitful dialogue, in the interests of \[their] two countries, and the world".[^cite_note-16]

On 10 December 2025, Parmelin was elected Federal President for the year 2026 with 203 valid votes in the [Federal Assembly](1797312.md) (of a total of 246).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

On 9 January 2026, Parmelin addressed the nation in an open letter following the lethal New Year's Eve [fire at the Le Constellation bar](82004998.md) in [Crans-Montana](2283558.md), which killed 40 people, many teenagers, and injured over 110, pledging solidarity and demanding investigations into safety breaches.[^cite_note-19]

## Personal life

In 1995, Parmelin married Caroline Merotto, the daughter of Aldo Merotto, an engineer of Italian origin, and Maria Merotto (née Moosrainer). They have no children.[^cite_note-HDS-4]
