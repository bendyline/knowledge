# Auto rickshaw

"Tuk tuk" redirects here. For the film, see [*Tuk Tuk* (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuk_Tuk_\(film\)).



A typical auto rickshaw in Sri Lanka

An **auto rickshaw** is a motorized version of the [pulled rickshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulled_rickshaw) or [cycle rickshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_rickshaw).[^cite_note-1] Often called a **tuk tuk**, it is usually a three-wheeled vehicle, powered by an engine.[^cite_note-2] They use a variety of fuels, with the most common being [petrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrol), [compressed natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_natural_gas), [liquefied petroleum gas](361384.md), and [electricity](9550.md). They are known by many names across many different countries.

The auto rickshaw is a common form of transport around the world (especially in South Asian countries like India and Pakistan) both as a [vehicle for hire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_for_hire) and private use. They are especially common in countries with [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate) or subtropical climate since they are usually not fully enclosed, and in many [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries) because they are relatively inexpensive to own and operate.&#x20;

There are different auto rickshaw designs. The most common type used for passenger transport is characterized by a sheet-metal body resting on three wheels with a canvas roof and drop-down side curtains. The driver is seated in a small cabin at the front and operates [handlebar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcycle_handlebar) controls with a space for carrying up to three passengers in the back.[^cite_note-3] The cargo versions might have an open space at the rear. A simpler version might have an expanded sidecar mounted on a three wheeled motorcycle.

As of 2023, India is the largest market for electric auto rickshaws, surpassing China.[^cite_note-4] As of 2024, [Bajaj Auto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajaj_Auto) of India is the world's largest auto rickshaw manufacturer.[^cite_note-5]

## Origin



A 1952 Lambretta FD sold as open top model



[Daihatsu Midget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daihatsu_Midget) Model DKA



1960 Lambretta Li 175 Series 2



RAR soldier helps push-start a Lambretta at Nui Dat

In the 1930s Japan, which was the most industrialized country in Asia at the time, encouraged the development of motorized vehicles including less expensive three-wheeled vehicles based on motorcycles. The [Mazda-Go](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda-Go), a 3-wheel open "truck" released in 1931,[^cite_note-MZhist-6] is often considered the first of what became auto rickshaws. Later that decade the Japanese [Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Posts_and_Telecommunications_\(Japan\)) distributed about 20,000 used three-wheelers to Southeast Asia as part of efforts to expand its influence in the region.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] They became popular in some areas, especially Thailand, which developed local manufacturing and design after the Japanese government abolished the three-wheeler license in Japan in 1965.[^cite_note-11]

Production in Southeast Asia started from the [knockdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knock-down_kit) production of the [Daihatsu Midget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daihatsu_Midget), which was introduced in 1959.[^cite_note-12] An exception is the indigenously modified Philippine tricycle, which originates from the [Rikuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikuo_Motorcycle) [Type 97 motorcycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_97_motorcycle) with a sidecar, introduced to the islands in 1941 by the [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army) during World War II.[^cite_note-brits-13]

In Asia, [Corradino D'Ascanio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corradino_D'Ascanio), aircraft designer at [Piaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio) and inventor of the [Vespa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio_Vespa), came up with the idea of building a light three-wheeled commercial vehicle to power Italy's post-war [economic reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_reconstruction). The [Piaggio Ape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio_Ape) followed suit in 1947. [Innocenti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innocenti), another leading Scooter manufacturer, also came up with their [Lambretta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambretta) line of three wheelers in a cargo version, later adopted as a passenger version by its Indian colloborator [Automobile Products of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile_Products_of_India).

## Regional variations

### Africa and the Middle East

#### Egypt

Locally named the "tuktuk", the rickshaw is used as a means of transportation in most parts of [Egypt](8087628.md). It is generally rare to find in some affluent and newer parts of cities such as [New Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Cairo) and [Heliopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heliopolis,_Cairo); and on highways due to police control and enforcement.

#### Gaza

Together with the [2010 boom of recreational facilities in Gaza](22098325.md#Tourism) for the local residents, [donkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donkey) carts have all but been displaced by tuk-tuks in 2010. Due to the ban by [Egypt](8087628.md) and Israel on the import of most motorised vehicles, the tuk-tuks have had to be smuggled in parts through the [tunnel network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip_smuggling_tunnels) connecting Gaza with [Egypt](8087628.md).[^cite_note-14]

#### Iraq

Due to extreme congestion in [Baghdad](4492.md) and other Iraqi cities combined with the insensible cost of vehicles in relation to frequent violence, rickshaws have been imported from India in large numbers to provide taxi service and other purposes, in stark contrast to previous attitudes of the [pre-U.S. 2003 invasion](201936.md) eras with rickshaws being disdained and sedans being held in high regard as a status symbol. Rickshaws have been noted for being instrumental in political protest revolts.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

#### Kenya



Tuk tuk in Mombasa, Kenya

Locally named 'Tuktuk', the rickshaw is used as a means of transportation in some parts of [Kenya](188171.md).

#### Madagascar

In [Madagascar](18964.md), man-powered rickshaws are a common form of transportation in a number of cities, especially [Antsirabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antsirabe). They are known as "posy" from *pousse-pousse*, meaning *push-push*. Cycle rickshaws took off since 2006 in a number of flat cities like [Toamasina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toamasina) and replaced the major part of the posy, and are now threatened by the auto rickshaws, introduced in 2009. Provincial capitals like Toamasina, [Mahajanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanga), [Toliara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toliara), and [Antsiranana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antsiranana) are taking to them rapidly. They are known as "bajaji" in the north and "tuk-tuk" or "tik-tik" in the east, and are now licensed to operate as taxis. They are not yet allowed an operating licence in the congested, and more polluted national capital, [Antananarivo](56597.md).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

#### Morocco

In [Morocco](19291.md), there are Auto-rickshaws in Rabat, Casablanca and Marrakesh.

#### Nigeria

#### Bangladesh

See also: [Rickshaw art in Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickshaw_painting_of_Bangladesh)



"CNGs" in [Dhaka](56656.md), Bangladesh

Auto rickshaws (locally called "baby taxis" or "CNGs" for those running on [compressed natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_natural_gas)) are one of the more popular modes of transport in Bangladesh mainly due to their size and speed. They are best suited to narrow, crowded streets, and are thus the principal means of covering longer distances within urban areas.[^cite_note-32]

[Two-stroke engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-stroke_engines) had been identified as one of the leading sources of [air pollution](10934212.md) in [Dhaka](56656.md). Thus, since January 2003, traditional auto rickshaws were banned from the capital; only the new natural gas-powered models (CNG) were permitted to operate within the city limits. All CNGs are painted green to signify that the vehicles are [eco-friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco-friendly) and that each one has a meter built-in.[^cite_note-33]

As of 2025, auto rickshaws in Bangladesh are predominantly electric. There were around 4 million unregistered electric auto rickshaws circulating in [Bangladesh](3454.md) by 2025, up from 200,000 in 2016. They constitute "perhaps the world’s biggest informal [EV](279350.md) fleet."[^cite_note-auto1-34]

#### India



Auto rickshaws in [Pune](164634.md), India



API 175 Lambretta Auto sold from 50's till the late 90's

Most cities offer auto rickshaw services, although [cycle rickshaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_rickshaw) and hand-[pulled rickshaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulled_rickshaw) are also available but rarely in certain remote areas, as all other cities began using auto rickshaws. [^cite_note-FrommersIndia-2010-35]<sup>: 15, 57, 156 </sup> Many state governments have launched an initiative of women-friendly rickshaw service called the [Pink Rickshaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Rickshaw) driven by women.[^cite_note-36] The drivers are known as the "Rickshaw-wallah", *auto-[wallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallah)*, *tuktuk-wallah* or *auto-kaaran* in places like Tamil Nadu/Kerala. Auto-rickshaws are also known as tempos in some parts of India.[^cite_note-37]

Auto rickshaws are used in cities and towns for short distances; they are less suited to long distances because they are slow and the carriages are open to air pollution.[^cite_note-FrommersIndia-2010-35]<sup>: 57, 58, 110 </sup> Auto rickshaws (often called "autos") provide cheap and efficient transportation. Modern auto rickshaws run on electricity as the government pushes for e-mobility through its FAME-II scheme, [compressed natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_natural_gas) (CNG) and [liquified petroleum gas](361384.md) (LPG) due to [government](553883.md) regulations and are environmentally friendly compared to full-sized cars.[^cite_note-38]

To augment the speedy movement of traffic, auto rickshaws are not allowed in the southern part of the [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai_City_district).[^cite_note-39]

India is the location of the annual [Rickshaw Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickshaw_Run).

There are two types of auto rickshaws in India. In older versions the engines were below the driver's seat, while in newer versions engines are in the rear. They normally run on [petrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrol), CNG, or [diesel](81761.md). The [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) of a normal rickshaw is four, including the driver's seat. Six-seater rickshaws exist in different parts of the country, but the model was officially banned in the city of [Pune](164634.md) on 10 January 2003 by the [Regional Transport Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Transport_Office) (RTA).[^cite_note-40]

Apart from this, modern electric auto rickshaws, which operate on electric motors and offer high torque and loading capacity with better speed, are also gaining popularity in India. Many auto drivers have switched to electric three-wheelers due to the high prices of CNG and diesel, making traditional auto rickshaws much more expensive compared to their electric counterparts. The government is also taking steps to convert existing CNG and diesel rickshaws to electric models.[^cite_note-41]

CNG autos in many cities (e.g. Delhi, Agra) are distinguishable from the earlier petrol-powered autos by a green and yellow livery, as opposed to the earlier black and yellow appearance. In other cities (such as Mumbai) the only distinguishing feature is the 'CNG' print found on the back or side of the auto. Some local governments are considering [four-stroke engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-stroke_engine) instead of two-stroke versions.

Notable auto rickshaw manufacturers in India include [Bajaj Auto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajaj_Auto), [Mahindra & Mahindra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahindra_&_Mahindra), [Piaggio Ape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio_Ape), [Atul Auto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atul_Auto), [Kerala Automobiles Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerala_Automobiles_Limited), [TVS Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVS_Motors) and [Force Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Motors).

In Delhi there also used to be a variant powered by a [Harley-Davidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley-Davidson) engine called the *phat-phati*, because of the sound it made. The story goes that shortly after the [Independence of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_India), a stock of Harley-Davidson motorbikes that had been used by British troops during [World War II](32927.md) were found to be left behind in a military storage house in Delhi. Drivers purchased these bikes, added on a gear box (probably from a [Willys jeep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willys_MB)), welded on a passenger compartment that was good for four to six passengers, and put the unconventional vehicles onto the roads. A 1998 ruling of the [Supreme Court](395852.md) against the use of polluting vehicles finally signed the death warrant of Delhi's phat-phatis.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

As of 2022, India has about 2.4 million battery-powered, three-wheeled rickshaws on its roads. Some 11,000 new ones hit the streets each month, creating a US\$3.1 billion market. Manufacturers include Mahindra & Mahindra Ltd. and [Kinetic Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_Engineering_Limited). A prerequisite for the adoption to electric vehicles is the availability of charging stations; as of early 2024, India had 12,146 public EV charging stations operational across the country.[^cite_note-44]

#### Nepal

Auto rickshaws were a popular mode of transport in [Nepal](171166.md) during the 1980s and 1990s, until the government banned the movement of 600 such vehicles in the early 2000s.[^cite_note-51] The earliest auto rickshaws running in [Kathmandu](17168.md) were manufactured by Bajaj Auto.

Nepal has been a popular destination for the [Rickshaw Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickshaw_Run). The 2009 Fall Run took place in [Goa](40010153.md), India and ended in [Pokhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhara), Nepal.[^cite_note-52]

#### Pakistan

Auto rickshaws are a popular mode of transport in Pakistani towns[^cite_note-ABC_News_Promote_Peace-53] and are mainly used for travelling short distances within cities. One of the major manufacturers of auto rickshaws is [Piaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio). The government is taking measures to convert all gasoline powered auto rickshaws to cleaner CNG rickshaws by 2015. In relation to that, [Environment Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_Canada) is implementing pilot projects in [Lahore](125315.md), [Karachi](17123.md), and [Quetta](93764.md) with engine technology developed in [Mississauga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississauga), Ontario, Canada that uses CNG instead of gasoline in two-stroke engines, in an effort to combat [environmental pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_pollution) and noise levels. However, as CNG rickshaws became less popular, more expensive and inefficient, the government started promoting [electric rickshaws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_rickshaw) instead by issuing easy loans through commercial banks in all the major cities of Pakistan.

In many cities in Pakistan, there are also motorcycle rickshaws, usually called "chand gari" (moon car) or "chingchi", after the Chinese company [Jinan Qingqi Motorcycle Co. Ltd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_Qingqi) who first introduced these to the market.

There are many rickshaw manufacturers in Pakistan. Lahore is the hub of CNG auto rickshaw manufacturing. Manufacturers include: New Asia automobile Pvt, Ltd; AECO Export Company; STAHLCO Motors; Global Sources; Parhiyar Automobiles; Global Ledsys Technologies; Siwa Industries; Prime Punjab Automobiles; Murshid Farm Industries; Sazgar Automobiles; NTN Enterprises; and Imperial Engineering Company.

#### Sri Lanka



Trishaw in [Polonnaruwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polonnaruwa), Sri Lanka

Auto rickshaws, commonly known as three-wheelers, tuk-tuks ([Sinhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhala_language): ටුක් ටුක්, pronounced [\[ṭuk ṭuk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA)), autos, or trishaws can be found on all roads in [Sri Lanka](26750.md) transporting people or freight. Sri Lankan three-wheelers are of the style of the light Phnom Penh-type. Most of the three-wheelers in Sri Lanka are a slightly modified Indian [Bajaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajaj_Auto) model, imported from India. Although there are a few models which are manufactured locally, imports from other countries in the region including other brands of three-wheelers, such as the [Piaggio Ape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio_Ape), are increasing. Three-wheelers were introduced to Sri Lanka for the first time around 1979 by [Richard Pieris & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Pieris_&_Company). As of mid-2018, a new gasoline powered tuk-tuk typically costs around US\$4,300, while a newly introduced Chinese electric model cost around US\$5,900.[^cite_note-\:0-54] Since 2008, the Sri Lankan government has banned the import of all 2-stroke gasoline engines due to environmental concerns.[^cite_note-\:0-54] Models imported to the island are now four-stroke engines.

Most three-wheelers are available as hired vehicles, with some being used for hauling goods, as advertising vehicles, or for private company usage. Bajaj enjoys a virtual monopoly in the island, with its agent being David Pieries Motor Co, Ltd.[^cite_note-55] A few three-wheelers in Sri Lanka have [distance meters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taximeter), with it becoming more and more common in the [capital city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Jayawardenepura_Kotte). However, the vast majority of fares are negotiated between the passenger and driver. There are 1.2 million trishaws in Sri Lanka and most are on financial loans. The selling of bakery items from tuk-tuks announcing their presence with mobile phone ringtones played over loudspeakers is known as *[choon paan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choon_paan)* in Sri Lanka.

#### Cambodia

&#x20;In [Cambodia](334751.md), a passenger-carrying three-wheeled vehicle is known as រ៉ឺម៉ក *rœmâk* from the French *remorque*. #### Indonesia

In [Indonesia](14579.md), auto rickshaws are popular in many areas such as in [Jakarta](16275.md), [Yogyakarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogyakarta), [Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan), [Makassar](389464.md), and other places in the country. Auto rickshaws are known as *Bentor* in regions such as Jogja, Medan and Gorontalo while in Jakarta, they are known as *Bajay.* The term *Bajay* or *Bajaj* stems from their manufacturer, Bajaj Auto, which came from India.[^cite_note-57]They are available in blue (for the ones which use [compressed natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_natural_gas)) and orange (for normal gasoline fuel).[^cite_note-Republika-58] The blue ones are imported from India with the brand of [Bajaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajaj_Auto) and [TVS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVS_Motor) and the orange ones are the old design from 1977. The orange ones uses two-stroke engines as their prime mover, while the blue ones use four stroke engines. The orange *bajaj* has been banned since 2017 due to emission regulations.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-Republika-58] The *Bajaj* is one of the most popular modes of transportation in the city. Outside of Jakarta, the *bentor*-style auto rickshaw is ubiquitous, with the passenger cabin mounted as a sidecar (like in Medan) or in-front (like the ones in some parts of Sulawesi) to a motorcycle.

In the [Philippines](23440.md), a similar mode of [public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_the_Philippines) is the [tricycle](29843385.md) ([Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *traysikel*; [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language): *traysikol*).[^cite_note-PItrykes-60] Unlike auto rickshaws, Philippine tricycles use a [motorcycle](19876.md) with a [sidecar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidecar) configuration. The exact date of their appearance in the Philippines is unknown, but they began appearing after World War II, roughly at the same time as the [jeepney](884965.md). One hypothesis is that they were derived from the [Rikuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rikuo_Motorcycle) [Type 97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_97_motorcycle) military motorcycle used by the [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army) in the Philippines during World War II. The motorcycle was essentially a licensed copy of a [Harley-Davidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley-Davidson) with a sidecar.[^cite_note-brits-13] Another hypothesis places their origin in the similarly configured [pedicab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedicab), a human-powered [cycle rickshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycle_rickshaw), although the provenance of the trisikad is also unknown.

The design and configuration of tricycles vary widely between localities, although there is a tendency toward standardization within individual municipalities. The usual design consists of a passenger or cargo sidecar attached to a motorcycle, typically on the right side. Left-side sidecars are uncommon.

Tricycles can carry passengers in the sidecar and on the motorcycle, as well as cargo. The number of passengers varies according to the design and local regulations. Some tricycles also have roof racks for carrying goods. Tricycles are a significant source of air pollution in the Philippines,[^cite_note-cleanair-61][^cite_note-cnnphil-62] and two-stroke engines have been identified as a source of [volatile organic compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volatile_organic_compound) emissions.[^cite_note-gulfnews-63] Some local governments have implemented programs to replace two-stroke tricycles with cleaner four-stroke models.[^cite_note-cleanair-61][^cite_note-cleanairmanda-64]

Three-wheeled vehicles distinct from conventional motorized tricycles, known elsewhere as tuk-tuks, are also operated in the Philippines. In parts of [Batangas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batangas) and [Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite), such vehicles are known as *bukyo*.[^cite_note-65] Electric versions, commonly known as [e-trikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_rickshaw), have also been introduced in several cities.[^cite_note-66] In Philippine usage, electric three-wheeled vehicles are also commonly referred to as [e-bikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_bicycle). In January 2026, the LTO announced a ban on e-trikes on major roads in Metro Manila, effective January 2.[^cite_note-67]

#### Thailand

The auto rickshaw, called tuk tuk ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): ตุ๊ก ๆ, pronounced [\[túk túk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Thai)) in Thailand, is a widely used form of urban transport in [Bangkok](56615.md) and other Thai cities. The name is [onomatopoeic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onomatopoeic), mimicking the sound of a small (often two-cycle) engine. It is particularly popular where [traffic congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion) is a major problem, such as in Bangkok and [Nakhon Ratchasima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhon_Ratchasima). In Bangkok in the 1960s, these were called *samlo* (สามล้อ, lit. 'three-wheeler'), and they are still popularly called that today.

Bangkok and other cities in Thailand have many tuk-tuks which are a more open variation on the Indian auto rickshaw. About 20,000 tuk-tuks were registered as taxis in Thailand in 2017.[^cite_note-68] Bangkok alone is reported to have 9,000 tuk-tuks.[^cite_note-69]

Tuk tuk hua kob (ตุ๊ก ๆ หัวกบ, pronounced [\[túk túk hua̯ kop̚\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Thai), lit. 'frog-headed tuk tuk') is a unique tuk tuk with a cab looking like a frog's head. Only [Phra Nakhon Si Ayutthaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phra_Nakhon_Si_Ayutthaya_District) and [Trang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mueang_Trang_District) have vehicles like this.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

in 2018, [MuvMi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MuvMi), an electric tuk-tuk [ride hailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridesharing_company) service launched in Bangkok.[^cite_note-72]

### East Asia

#### China



Auto rickshaw Haikou

&#x20;Various types of auto rickshaw are used around China, where they are called sān lún chē (三轮车) and sometimes sān bèng zǐ (三蹦子), meaning three wheeler or tricycle. They may be used to transport cargo or passengers in the more rural areas. However, in many urban areas the auto rickshaws for passengers are often operated illegally as they are considered unsafe and an eyesore.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] They are permitted in some towns and cities, however. The Southeast Asian word tuk tuk is transliterated as dū dū chē (嘟嘟车, or beep beep car) in Chinese.[^cite_note-75]

### Europe

#### France

A number of tuk-tuks (250 in 2013 according to the Paris Prefecture) are used as an alternative tourist transport system in Paris, some of them being pedal-operated with electric motor assist. They are not yet fully licensed to operate and await customers on the streets. Vélotaxis were common during the Occupation years in Paris due to fuel restrictions.[^cite_note-76]

#### Italy



An Ape C (1956–1967)

Auto rickshaws have been commonly used in Italy since the late 1940s, providing a low-cost means of transportation in the post–World War II years when the country was short of economic resources. The [Piaggio Ape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio_Ape) (Tukxi), designed by [Vespa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vespa) creator [Corradino D'Ascanio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corradino_D'Ascanio) and first manufactured in 1947 by the Italian company [Piaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piaggio), though primarily designed for carrying freight has also been widely used as an auto rickshaw. It is still extremely popular throughout the country, being particularly useful in the narrow streets found in the center of many little towns in central and southern Italy. Though it no longer has a key role in transportation, Piaggio Ape is still used as a minitaxi in some areas such as the islands of [Ischia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ischia) and [Stromboli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stromboli) (on Stromboli no cars are allowed). It has recently been re-launched as a trendy-ecological means of transportation, or, relying on the role the Ape played in the history of Italian design, as a promotional tool.

#### Portugal



Tuk Tuk Taxi in Albufeira Portugal

Tuk Tuks are used in the main touristic cities and regions of the country, specially in [Lisbon](18091.md) and the sunny region of [Algarve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algarve), as a novel form of transport for visitors during the tourist season.

#### United Kingdom

In 2006 a British travel writer –  [Antonia Bolingbroke-Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonia_Bolingbroke-Kent) –  and her friend Jo Huxster travelled 12,561 miles (20,215 km) with an auto rickshaw from [Bangkok](56615.md) to [Brighton](49796.md). With this 98 days' trip they set a [Guinness World Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Record) for the longest journey ever with an auto rickshaw. ### Central and North America

#### El Salvador

The *mototaxi* or *moto* is the El Salvadoran version of the auto rickshaw. These are most commonly made from the front end and engine of a motorcycle attached to a two-wheeled passenger area in back. Commercially produced models, such as the Indian [Bajaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajaj_Auto) brand, are also employed.

#### Guatemala

In Guatemala tuk-tuks operate, both as taxis and private vehicles, in [Guatemala City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala_City), around the island town of [Flores](11426.md), [Peten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet%C3%A9n_\(department\)), in the mountain city of [Antigua Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigua_Guatemala), and in many small towns in the mountains. From 2005 to present the tuk-tuks have been prevalent in [Lago de Atitlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lago_de_Atitl%C3%A1n) towns such as [Panajachel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panajachel) and [Santiago Atitlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Atitl%C3%A1n). While tuk-tuks continue to serve as a prevalent form of transportation in Antigua and Lake Atitlan their use throughout the country as a whole has declined.

#### United States

In the 1950s and 1960s, the [United States Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Post_Office_Department) (replaced in 1971 by the [United States Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service)) used the [WestCoaster Mailster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westcoaster_Mailster), a close relative of the tuk-tuk.[^cite_note-82] Similar vehicles remain in limited use for parking enforcement, mall security, and other niche applications. After a short time on the market (Mid-2000s to 2008) in the United States,[^cite_note-83] the vehicles failed to gain popularity in the United States, and as a result, are no longer available. The Manufacturer [Bajaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajaj_Auto) cited the manual transmissions aboard the three-wheelers as the reason for poor sales. As a result of modifications that made the machines [EPA](58666.md) and [DOT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Transportation_\(U.S.\)) compliant, the vehicles that were sold are still street-legal.[^cite_note-84]



Westcoaster Mailster

Auto rickshaws are rarely seen in the United States, however there are companies that operate them as taxis, affordable transportation services, or rentals, usually in urban areas like Tuk Tuk Chicago in [Chicago](6886.md), Capital Tuk-Tuk in [Sacramento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento), eTuk Ride Denver in [Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver), the Boston rickshaw company in [Boston](24437894.md) and several more.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

The [New York Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Police_Department) (NYPD) operates auto rickshaws that they call “three-wheel patrol scooters”. The patrol scooters are used for parking and traffic enforcement on city streets and to patrol places that most cars can't – like the narrow paths in [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park). The NYPD patrol scooters started being replaced in 2016 with [Smart Fortwos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Fortwo). The NYPD believes that the Smart Fourtwos are safer, more comfortable, and more affordable, than the three-wheel patrol scooters due to the Smart Fortwos coming with features that the patrol scooters lack like air conditioning, and airbags, while also costing about \$6,000 less. The Smart Fortwos can also be driven on highways if needed. The Smart Fortwos are also said to be more “approachable” and “friendlier looking” which helps with [public relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_relations).[^cite_note-97]

## Fuel efficiency and pollution

In July 1998, the [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) ordered the government of Delhi to implement [CNG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_natural_gas) or [LPG](361384.md) ([Autogas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autogas)) [fuel](21188370.md) for all autos and for the entire bus fleet in and around the city. Delhi's air quality has improved with the switch to CNG. Initially, auto rickshaw drivers in Delhi had to wait in long queues for CNG refueling, but the situation improved following an increase in the number of CNG stations. Gradually, many state governments passed similar laws, thus shifting to CNG or LPG vehicles in most large cities to improve air quality and reduce pollution. Certain local governments are pushing for [four-stroke engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-stroke_engine) instead of two-stroke ones. Typical [mileage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_economy_in_automobiles) for an Indian-made auto rickshaw is around 35 kilometres per litre (99 mpg<sub>‑imp</sub>; 82 mpg<sub>‑US</sub>) of petrol. Pakistan has passed a similar law prohibiting auto rickshaws in certain areas. CNG auto rickshaws have started to appear in huge numbers in many Pakistani cities.

In January 2007 the Sri Lankan government also banned two-stroke trishaws to reduce air pollution. In the Philippines[^cite_note-116] there are projects to convert carbureted two-stroke engines to direct-injected via [Envirofit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envirofit_International#Two-stroke_engine_direct_injection_retrofit) technology. Research has shown LPG or CNG gas direct-injection can be retrofitted to existing engines, in similar fashion to the Envirofit system.[^cite_note-117] In [Vigan City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigan_City) majority of tricycles-for-hire as of 2008 are powered by motorcycles with four-stroke engines, as [tricycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricycles) with two-stroke motorcycles are prevented from receiving operating permits. Direct injection is standard equipment on new machines in India.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119]

In March 2009 an international consortium coordinated by the [International Centre for Hydrogen Energy Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_Hydrogen_Energy_Technologies) initiated a two-year public-private partnership of local and international stakeholders aiming at operating a fleet of 15 hydrogen-fueled three-wheeled vehicles in New Delhi's [Pragati Maidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragati_Maidan) complex.[^cite_note-120] As of January 2011, the project was nearing completion.

[Hydrogen internal combustion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_internal_combustion_engine) (HICV) use in three-wheelers has only recently begun to be looked into, mainly by developing countries, to decrease local pollution at an affordable cost.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] At some point, [Bajaj Auto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajaj_Auto) made a HICV auto rickshaw together with the company "[Energy Conversion Devices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Conversion_Devices)".[^cite_note-thecityfix.com-123] They made a report on it called "Clean Hydrogen Technology for 3-Wheel Transportation in India" and it stated that the performance was comparable with CNG autos. In 2012, Mahindra & Mahindra showcased their first HICV auto rickshaw, called the Mahindra HyAlfa.[^cite_note-thecityfix.com-123] The development of the hydrogen-powered rickshaw happened with support from the [International Centre for Hydrogen Energy Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_Hydrogen_Energy_Technologies).

## World records

On 16 September 2022, at 11\:04 a.m. (Indian Standard Time), a Canadian team (Greg Harris and Priya Singh) and a Swiss team (Michele Daryanani and Nevena Lazarevic) set the world record for the highest altitude at which an auto rickshaw has ever been driven. The world record was officially recognized and certified by [Guinness World Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Records) on October 10, 2024.[^cite_note-124] The two teams set the record by driving to the summit of [Umling La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umling_La) Pass at an altitude of 5,798 meters (19,022 feet).[^cite_note-125]

The two teams were participating in the [Rickshaw Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rickshaw_Run) (Himalayan Edition), an event promoted by The Adventurists, where teams drive auto rickshaws from the [Thar desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thar_Desert) town of [Jaisalmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaisalmer) in Rajasthan to the [Himalayan](37966.md) town of [Leh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leh) in [Ladakh](303611.md). Rickshaw Run teams are given the start and finish lines, but are otherwise unsupported and left to their own navigational choices in completing the approximately 2,300 km journey.

The road at Umling La Pass was constructed by India's Border Roads Organization and completed in 2017. Guinness World Records certified the road as the highest motorable road in the world.[^cite_note-126]
