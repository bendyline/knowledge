Early presidential elections were held in the disputed territory[^cite_note-recognition-1] of [South Ossetia](318381.md) on 18 September 2026.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] The election was called following the resignation of President [Alan Gagloyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Gagloyev), who stepped down on 23 June 2026 to accept an advisor position under [Russian](25391.md) [president](239646.md) [Vladimir Putin](32817.md).[^cite_note-PresidentResigns-4] A month prior, Gagloyev had signed a cooperation treaty with Russia that allowed Russian citizens to hold office in South Ossetia.[^cite_note-JAM_treaty-5] As the president had resigned early, the 55th article of the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_South_Ossetia) mandated that elections must be held no later than 90 days following the resignation of the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_South_Ossetia).[^cite_note-ConstitutionSouthOssetia-6] The final turnout of the election was reported to be 82.65%.[^cite_note-DeclaredResults-7]

Similar to previous elections in South Ossetia, the election had the characteristics of a [managed democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managed_Democracy) and the consolidation of state support behind a favoured candidate.[^cite_note-KambolovOutsider-8] The [European Union](9317.md) refused to recognise the election as legitimate, stating that the process was a continuation of steps toward a [future Russian annexation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposed_Russian_annexation_of_South_Ossetia) of South Ossetia.[^cite_note-EUReaction-9] [Georgia](48768.md) also similarly condemned the election as illegal, stating that the electoral process violated [international law](8195726.md) by intruding on Georgia's territorial integrity.[^cite_note-GeorgiaReaction-10] Authorities in South Ossetia sent designated journalists to cover the election,[^cite_note-11] while also utilizing multiple [Russophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russophile) bloggers as election observers.[^cite_note-OssetiaObservers1-12][^cite_note-OssetiaObservers2-13]

The election was also marked by the rapid rise of [Marat Kambolov](83485725.md), who became acting president in the aftermath of Gagloyev's resignation on 23 June after having been recently appointed [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_South_Ossetia) by the [South Ossetian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Ossetian_Parliament) on 16 June.[^cite_note-PresidentResigns-4][^cite_note-OC_1-14] Kambolov became president-elect of South Ossetia on 19 September after the CEC declared that he won at least 85% of the vote.[^cite_note-15] During the registration period, Kambolov was backed by Putin and received endorsements from both the sitting government and the opposition.[^cite_note-j2-16]

The only serious challenger to Kambolov, [Ira Farn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ira_Farn) leader [Hoh Chochiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoh_Chochiev?action=edit\&redlink=1), was rejected from the ballot on the grounds of not being fluent in Russian and his signature sheets being illegal.[^cite_note-ChochievRejected-17] Chochiev criticised Kambolov's candidacy in response, stating that Kambolov did not meet the 10-year residency requirement for candidates running for the presidency of South Ossetia.[^cite_note-Chochiev2-18] [Iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_\(political_party,_2023\)) leader [Georgiy Kabisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgiy_Kabisov) was unable to run due to being a convicted felon and endorsed Eduard Gabarayev, who was also rejected from the ballot by the CEC for discrepancies in personal information.[^cite_note-j3-19][^cite_note-GabarayevRejected-20] [Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_Party_\(South_Ossetia\)) candidate Fatima Khugayeva, the only female candidate, was rejected for her nominating committee being lower than the minimum 100 members requirement.[^cite_note-KhugayevaRejected-21] In addition to Kambolov, [Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_Party_\(South_Ossetia\)) leader [Zurab Kokoyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurab_Kokoyev), along with former KGB officer Kazbek Kodoyev and [Patriots of Alanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriots_of_Alanya) candidate Murat Valiyev, were also registered for the ballot.[^cite_note-RegisteredComplete-22]

The campaign was more defined by televised debates, as no campaign banners were present during the campaign period.[^cite_note-ElectionMood-23] In the first debate about social policy on 28 August, technical difficulties involving interrupting sounds and freezing images were observed.[^cite_note-24] These technical difficulties relating to outdated equipment in the first debate caused Kambolov to skip the second debate about economic policy that was held on 8 September.[^cite_note-CK_1-25][^cite_note-26] The third debate on 11 September about security policy was only attended by Kodoyev.[^cite_note-Debate3-27] By the fourth debate between Valiev and Kodoyev, the debates had reduced interest and increased unpopularity, with views of the debate being low.[^cite_note-Debate4-28] Notable campaign issues included [brain drain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_drain), demand for higher wages, border queues, environmental policies, and lack of public transportation.[^cite_note-29]

## Background

See also: [2022 South Ossetian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_South_Ossetian_presidential_election), [Alan Gagloyev § Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Gagloyev#Presidency), and [Marat Kambolov § Rapid political rise](83485725.md#Rapid_political_rise)

The build-up to the [2024 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_South_Ossetian_parliamentary_election) was viewed by observers as preparation work for Presidential runs as the office of President holds most of the political power in South Ossetia.[^cite_note-RFE-30] This consisted of both the consolidation and creation of new political parties, in order to gain a national presence before the Presidential election.[^cite_note-RFE-30] *[Radio Free Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Free_Europe)* reported that the merger of [Dzambolat Tedeev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzambolat_Tedeev)'s [Patriots of Alania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriots_of_Alania) into the [People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_of_South_Ossetia) was in preparation for Tedeev to become the party's Presidential nominee.[^cite_note-RFE-30] This merger would fall apart after the party failed to significantly increase its vote share in the parliamentary election.[^cite_note-Patriots-31] On March 15, 2024, Tedeev criticized the 10-year residency qualification for candidates to hold the office of President stating it makes North Ossetians feel ostracized from "their native land" and urged for a constitutional amendment to remove the requirement as it prevents him from pursuing the office.[^cite_note-32]

Beginning in 2025 there had been speculation that then incumbent President [Alan Gagloyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Gagloyev) would not complete his term due to mounting corruption scandals, mostly centered around Russian financial aid disappearing into construction companies owned by Gagloyev and his Prime Minister [Konstantin Dzhussoev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Dzhussoev)'s friends and families, let alone stand for re-election.[^cite_note-Gogicheva-33][^cite_note-Dzhussoev-34] Gagloyev would sack Dzhussoev and his cabinet on January 21, 2026.[^cite_note-Dzhussoev-34] Elements within Gagloyev's own [Nykhaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nykhaz) party began exploring alternative candidates such as [Nelli Gogicheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelli_Gogicheva?action=edit\&redlink=1), the editor-in-chief of the state-owned magazine *[Fidiuæg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidiu%C3%A6g?action=edit\&redlink=1)* who initially announced her candidacy before quickly withdrawing it just hours later citing pressure from "destructive forces."[^cite_note-Gogicheva-33]

On May 25, 2026, Gagloyev signed a friendship treaty with Russia that allows any Russian citizen to run for and hold public office in South Ossetia.[^cite_note-JAM_treaty-5] The next day the Russian newspaper *Kommersant* reported that the relatively unknown North Ossetian director of the [Kurchatov Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurchatov_Institute), [Marat Kambolov](83485725.md), was Putin's top choice to become the new President of South Ossetia.[^cite_note-Kambolov-35] On May 27 Gagloyev would name Kambolov his adviser on 'the implementation of the integration deal between South Ossetia and Russia.[^cite_note-OC_1-14] On 8 June 2026 Dzhussoev's successor [Dzambolyt Tadtayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzambolyt_Tadtayev) resigned and with Gagloyev's endorsement Kambolov was named Prime Minister on 16 June by the [South Ossetian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Ossetian_Parliament).[^cite_note-OC_1-14] Gagloyev resigned as President on 23 June, with Kambolov becoming acting President until snap elections could be held within 90 days.[^cite_note-ConstitutionSouthOssetia-6][^cite_note-PresidentResigns-4]

## Campaign

Three of the four parties in Parliament—Nykhas, United Ossetia, and the [Communist Party of South Ossetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_South_Ossetia)—endorsed Kambolov, while the People's Party did not put forward or endorse a candidate; this left Kambolov as the only major candidate.[^cite_note-j2-16] Six candidates, either independents or representing minor extra-parliamentary parties, applied to run against Kambolov.[^cite_note-j3-19] Kambolov has vowed to undo allowing ethnic Georgians transit through [Akhalgori Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhalgori_Municipality), which Gagloyev campaigned on and implemented, while also promising to ease off the South Ossetian policy of creeping their border fences deeper into Georgian territory.[^cite_note-36] On July 13, Russian President Vladimir Putin told Kambolov "We will do everything to support you."[^cite_note-sova-37] According to the state-owned [Russian Public Opinion Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Public_Opinion_Research_Center) Kambolov has a 79% approval rating in South Ossetia.[^cite_note-sova-37]

Although Kambolov himself did not meet the residency requirements to run, this had not stopped him from using it as justification for blocking the candidacy of Tedeev, a sitting member of the [Parliament of North Ossetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_North_Ossetia), from running who instead announced that his freshly re-formed Patriots of Alania would be standing Murat Valiyev, an unknown former KGB agent, as a "technical candidate" to represent Tedeev in the election.[^cite_note-Patriots-31] Despite this, Tedeev also simultaneously urged his followers to vote for Kambolov.[^cite_note-j3-19] Valiyev ran an [agrarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrarianism) campaign, vowing to increasing farming subsidies and introduce a state-run advertising program for South Ossetian produce.[^cite_note-CK_1-25]

Supporters of former President [Leonid Tibilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Tibilov) who are generally skeptical of annexation, and instead promote [autarky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autarky), coalesced around Tibilov's former bodyguard Hoh Chochiev who was running under the [Ira Farn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ira_Farn) banner with an endorsement from [Irystony Nog Faltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irystony_Nog_Faltar?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-j-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] Chochiev had founded Ira Farn ahead of the [2024 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_South_Ossetian_parliamentary_election), where the party won 0 seats, as a [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossetian_nationalism) and [Assianist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assianism) party that advocates for [Ossetian unification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposed_Russian_annexation_of_South_Ossetia) albeit not within Russia.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Chochiev was described by regional media as Kambolov's only serious challenger.[^cite_note-j3-19] Chochiev's candidacy would be blocked by the Central Election Commission citing his lack of knowledge in the Russian language, despite his fluency in Ossetian, and that his signature sheets weren't legal.[^cite_note-ChochievRejected-17][^cite_note-RegisteredComplete-22] Chochiev would then denounce the election as a sham and a violation of "the trust of citizens" while highlighting Kambolov's own candidacy was illegal per the 10-year residency requirement.[^cite_note-Chochiev2-18]

[Zurab Kokoyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurab_Kokoyev), a former Prime Minister and the founder and longtime leader of the [Unity Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_Party_\(South_Ossetia\)), announced his intention to run despite previously backing Kambolov.[^cite_note-j3-19] He had been described as someone "whose role is largely to fill out the ballot rather than mount a serious challenge."[^cite_note-j3-19]

Fatima Khugayeva was the only female candidate. A retired lieutenant colonel in [Russia's Interior Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia's_Interior_Ministry) born in [Moscow](19004.md), she was nominated by the [Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_Party_\(South_Ossetia\)), which primarily represents the interests of small business owners.[^cite_note-j3-19] She previously ran for the Russian Duma in the [Lermontovsky constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lermontovsky_constituency) in 2020 for the [Communists of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communists_of_Russia) before moving to the South Ossetian village of [Kvaisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kvaisa).[^cite_note-44] Her candidacy was rejected as her nominating committee only had 78 members, while 100 are needed.[^cite_note-KhugayevaRejected-21]

Two members of the [Iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_\(political_party,_2023\)) party ran. The first was [Georgiy Kabisov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgiy_Kabisov), the party's founder and leader, who was previously the minister of Information and Press. The other candidate was Eduard Gabarayev, a former Lt. Colonel of the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(South_Ossetia\)) who gained prominence in 2010 for translating the Ministry of Defence's charter into Ossetian.[^cite_note-Gabarayev-45][^cite_note-j3-19] Gabarayev was not endorsed by the party, instead becoming a candidate through a citizens petition, and as such ran as an independent.[^cite_note-46] Gabarayev had previously run for President in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_South_Ossetian_presidential_election), but dropped out two days before the election and endorsed Kabisov, who was also running at the time.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-Gabarayev-45] This time, Kabisov ended his campaign early and endorsed Gabarayev; Kabisov is a felon and would have had his candidacy rejected by the Central Election Commission.[^cite_note-j3-19] Regardless, Gabarayev's candidacy was rejected by the Central Election Commission for "inconsistencies" in his personal information.[^cite_note-GabarayevRejected-20]

Former KGB agent Kazbek Kodoyev also ran as an independent.[^cite_note-j3-19] Kodoyev was born in [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg) but moved to Tskhinvali at a young age and graduated with a degree in French Language from the [North Ossetian State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Ossetian_State_University) before earning a masters-equivalent in [Jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisprudence) from the [South Ossetian State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Ossetian_State_University).[^cite_note-CK_1-25] Kodoyev also served as the rector to the South Ossetian State University on security.[^cite_note-CK_1-25]

Instead of campaign banners, the campaigns of Kokoyev, Kodoyev, and Valiyev were limited to television debates.[^cite_note-ElectionMood-23] Debates were conducted by the state television between late August and mid September.[^cite_note-DebateSchedule-48] The first debate discussing social policies was held on 28 August with Kambolov, Kodoyev, and Valiev attending.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] A second debate relating to economic policies was held on 8 September, with only Kodoyev and Valiev taking part with Kambolov skipping citing his dismay with technical difficulties during the first debate.[^cite_note-CK_1-25][^cite_note-51] The third debate centered on security and foreign relations, which took place on 11 September, was only attended by Kodoev.[^cite_note-Debate3-27][^cite_note-52] The last debate happened on 16 September,[^cite_note-DebateSchedule-48] which was attended by Kodoyev and Valiyev, where both candidates discussed administration and environmental policies.[^cite_note-Debate4-28]

## Electoral system

The election is held using the [two-round system](25932.md); a candidate would be declared the winner if they received over 50% of the vote in the first round. If no candidate passes the 50% threshold, a run-off election will be held.[^cite_note-53]

According to the 48th article of the constitution, a citizen of South Ossetia running for president must be at least 35 years old, must be fluent in the state languages ([Ossetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossetian_language) and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language)), and must have also permanently resided in South Ossetia for the last ten years.[^cite_note-ConstitutionSouthOssetia-6] Following election, the president serves a five-year term, and the same person may renew his or her presidency for a second consecutive term.[^cite_note-ConstitutionSouthOssetia-6]

On 25 May 2026, Russia and South Ossetia signed a treaty on deeper integration, which included a provision that any Russian citizen can hold elected office in South Ossetia, and vice versa. South Ossetia has not updated article 48 to reflect this. Regardless, North Ossetian Russian national [Marat Kambolov](83485725.md) was named the Prime Minister, and later the President, by former President [Alan Gagloyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Gagloyev), despite not meeting these requirements.[^cite_note-OC-54]

Due to the sudden nature of the election, the terms for members of the [Central Election Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Election_Commission) were set to expire before the election took place. However, Parliament passed a law to extend all members terms by two years to prevent this on 29 July.[^cite_note-55]

## Conduct

A day of [electoral silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_silence) was observed on 17 September.[^cite_note-67] A total of 75 polling stations were open on 18 September between 8\:00 am and 8\:00 pm [MSK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Time), with 70 stations being located in South Ossetia. The other five stations were located in Russia, with four stations operating in [North Ossetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Ossetia) and one station operating in Moscow. Concurrently, 11 polling stations also operated in South Ossetia between 18 and 20 September for Russian citizens voting in the [2026 elections](68779631.md) for the [State Duma](1010187.md).[^cite_note-68] 60 observers monitored the election, which included persons from Russia, Belarus, Abkhazia, and Venezuela.[^cite_note-69] The South Ossetian government also had a number of independent, unqualified, observers, mostly [Russophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russophile) bloggers such as [Germany](11867.md)'s Christian Wagner and [Serbia](29265.md)'s Srdjan Perisic.[^cite_note-OssetiaObservers1-12][^cite_note-OssetiaObservers2-13] The CEC stated that 26,639 citizens were on the voter lists, but added that the figure was not final.[^cite_note-70] The *[State Information Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Information_Agency)* highlighted support for Kambolov among South Ossetians fighting in volunteer units as part of the [Russian Invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Invasion_of_Ukraine), stating "fascism must be defeated" and urging a final resolution to the "[events of August 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Georgian_war)".[^cite_note-71]

## Results

Partial results from 30 of 75 polling stations were reported by the CEC on 18 September, stating that [Marat Kambolov](83485725.md) was leading with 88.4% of the vote, while [Patriots of Alanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriots_of_Alanya) candidate Murat Valiyev came second with 4.3% of the vote. An initial preliminary turnout of 73.2% was also declared.[^cite_note-Preliminary1-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

Further preliminary results that were 99% reporting on 19 September later showed Kambolov winning with 85.12% of the vote, while Valiev was in second place with 3.75% of the vote. Kodoev was in third place with 2.40%, while Kokoev came last with 1.35%. Nearly five percent of all voters cast their ballot against all candidates. The preliminary turnout reported at the time was 77.91%.[^cite_note-Preliminary2-75] Official results were released later on the same day, declaring a final turnout of 82.65%.[^cite_note-DeclaredResults-7]

## Aftermath

Following the closure of the polls, a concert and lottery were held in the city centre of [Tskhinvali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tskhinvali) to celebrate the end of the election period. Some of the items that were handed out in the lottery included household appliances, phones, TVs, bicycles, vacuums, and a [Niva Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lada_Niva) car made from Russia as a grand prize.[^cite_note-76]

President-elect [Marat Kambolov](83485725.md) thanked voters for their turnout in the election, while also encouraging Russian citizens to vote in the State Duma elections that were being held concurrently.[^cite_note-77] [Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_Party_\(South_Ossetia\)) candidate [Zurab Kokoyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurab_Kokoyev) similarly noted the turnout and congratulated Kambolov on his victory.[^cite_note-78] [Patriots of Alanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriots_of_Alanya) candidate Murat Valiyev stated that the results were clear, being satisfied with the campaign while desiring a rapid consolidation of administration.[^cite_note-79] Kazbek Kodoyev thanked election organisers, state bodies, and security agencies for an orderly process at the polls.[^cite_note-80] *[Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Welle)* described Kambolov's victory as a foregone conclusion and noted that Kambolov had, in the days leading up to the election, initiated a purge of supporters of former President Gagloyev, including the sacking of Deputy Head of the KGB [Alexander Tuayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Tuayev?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Minister of Education [Lolayev Kambolov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lolayev_Kambolov?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-Deutsche_Welle-81] Marat Kambolov was sworn in as president on 30 September,[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] while the government simultaneously resigned since Kambolov was no longer prime minister.[^cite_note-84]

The [European Union](9317.md)'s High Representative [Kaja Kallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaja_Kallas) stated that the outcome of the elections should be considered null and void, highlighting continued Russian attempts in consolidating further influence in South Ossetia.[^cite_note-Deutsche_Welle-81][^cite_note-EUReaction-9] The [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_of_Georgia) of [Georgia](48768.md) also similarly condemned the election, stating that the polling stations held in South Ossetia were a violation of Georgia's sovereignty and territorial integrity.[^cite_note-GeorgiaReaction-10][^cite_note-Deutsche_Welle-81]
