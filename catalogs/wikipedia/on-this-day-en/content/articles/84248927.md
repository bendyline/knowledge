In the early morning of 13 September 2026, prosecutors and courts in Turkey started a crackdown on [LGBTQ+ advocacy groups](4750738.md) and [HIV advocacy groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS_activism), arresting 162 people,[^cite_note-GU1-1] which then evolved into attacks on journalists and protesters. The government's justification for the crackdowns refers to a controversial policy called "My Family is Safe".[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

## Background

Turkish president [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan), who in 2002 spoke out favorably on LGBTQ+ rights,[^cite_note-4] has soured on pro-LGBTQ+ statements for some time. Erdoğan called LGBTQ+ rights a "tool of global dictatorship and against Islam" in 2022,[^cite_note-5] and continued in 2023 that LGBTQ+ rights were "the biggest threat to Turkish families".[^cite_note-6] In January 2025, amid declining birth rates, he declared 2025 the "Year of the Family", which sought to promote traditional Turkish family values and provide financial incentives to families and newlyweds. Erdoğan said that a global [LGBTQ+ movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_movements) was a threat to traditional family values, arguing that "the target of gender-neutralisation policies, in which LGBT is used as a battering ram, is the family. Criticism of LGBT is immediately silenced; anyone who defends nature and the family is subject to heavy oppression."[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

In May 2025, as part of the Year of the Family, Erdoğan declared a national "Decade of Family and Population" beginning in 2026. During a speech announcing the initiative, he identified the LGBTQ+ movement as "a full-fledged fascism" that "tolerates no differing opinion", and a "threat" to "not only the family but also women, children, and human dignity". He stated that "the fight against LGBT perversion is also a fight for freedom, dignity, and the future of humanity."[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Prosecutors stated that an investigation had found evidence of minors being "inappropriately influenced" on LGBTQ+ issues.[^cite_note-13]

These initiatives have been used as a legal basis for crackdowns against LGBTQ+ organizations and media, usually via accusations that such materials violate [public morality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_morality), or are [obscene](22036467.md).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Obscenity is not clearly defined in [law](2963429.md) and same-sex relations are not a crime in Turkey.[^cite_note-17]

## Crackdowns

On the morning of 13 September 2026, police started raiding LGBTQ+ clubs and advocacy groups as well as the homes and workplaces of activists and journalists.[^cite_note-GU1-1] At least 162 individuals were arrested,[^cite_note-\:2-18] and more than 100 detained in multiple locations including [Istanbul](3391396.md), [Ankara](802.md) and [İzmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmir). Amongst those raided was Turkey's longest-running LGBTQ+ advocacy group [KAOS GL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAOS_GL), and police detained the members of its board of directors.[^cite_note-GU1-1] Justice Minister [Akın Gürlek](82385461.md) announced on social media that 13 businesses, 9 associations, and over a hundred suspects had been detained during "'My Family Is Safe' operations" in order to "protect children, the family, and public order".[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Gürlek further stated that the [Financial Crimes Investigation Board (MASAK)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Crimes_Investigation_Board_\(Turkey\)) was looking into financial transactions involving organizations that promote "LGBT issues and immorality".[^cite_note-\:2-18]

Footage released by [Turkey's Interior Ministry](10283846.md) showed police searching filing cabinets, confiscating [rainbow flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_flag) and escorting people away in handcuffs. The operation also involved raids on nightclubs and the homes of activists. Istanbul prosecutors said authorities seized digital devices, drugs and a firearm during the raids and arrested dozens of people.[^cite_note-GU1-1]

Along with the raids that were taking place, Turkish courts issued rulings to [block the social media accounts](9790999.md) of advocacy groups, human rights organizations, newspapers, and journalists,[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] including groups such as [Amnesty International](18947898.md), along with women's advocacy groups.[^cite_note-25] [Emma Sinclair-Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Sinclair-Webb) of [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) accused authorities of attempting to smear and criminalise LGBTQ+ advocacy, saying that "we believe (the people arrested) include others not associated with LGBTQ+ groups (detained) for drug and prostitution offences."[^cite_note-GU1-1]

## Protests

On 15 September, demonstrations and [protests](266137.md) started outside the [Istanbul Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul_Courthouse), with heavy police forces surrounding them. Shortly after the rally started to grow, Turkish police intervened in the public rally and detained many pro-LGBTQ+ groups and activists.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] On 16 September, Turkish prosecutors and police started making arrests in several cities, such as [Mersin](1146241.md) and [Istanbul](3391396.md), under the "My Family is Safe" operations.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] In [İzmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0zmir), police [teargassed](1202646.md) hundreds of protesters.[^cite_note-GU1-1] On September 19, 2026, many protesters started demonstrations outside of [Turkish embassies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_of_Turkey) across Europe and the United States in solidarity with the LGBTQ+ community.[^cite_note-31]

## Reactions

Kaos GL called the crackdown "state-led discrimination, politics of fear and the dismantling of civil society".[^cite_note-32] Human rights organisations condemned the raids.[^cite_note-33] The [Turkish Medical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Medical_Association) condemned the restrictions on organizations working on [HIV/AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS).[^cite_note-34] [New Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Party_\(Turkey,_2026\)) leader [Özgür Özel](49050282.md) called the operations "unconstitutional" and said that the [Ministry of Justice](10283737.md) should not "disregard the Constitution."[^cite_note-35] The [Left Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Party_\(Turkey\)) opposed the raids, and said that "None of us are safe".[^cite_note-36] [DEM Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoples'_Equality_and_Democracy_Party) co-chairman [Tülay Hatimoğulları](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BClay_Hatimo%C4%9Fullar%C4%B1) has said that "neither families nor others are safe".[^cite_note-37]

The European Union said that they found the raids concerning.[^cite_note-\:1-38][^cite_note-39] [Nacho Sánchez Amor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nacho_S%C3%A1nchez_Amor), the [European Parliament](9581.md)'s Turkey rapporteur has said that the raids were pushing Turkey to a "darker place".[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] Christian Wigand, a spokesman for the [European Commission](9974.md), commented on [Turkey's candidacy to join the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_enlargement_of_the_European_Union#Turkey), stating that Turkey "should promote and ensure respect for the human rights and dignity of all individuals, irrespective of their gender identity or sexual orientation ... stigmatization and discrimination are contrary to the values of the Union."[^cite_note-\:1-38] Germany, France, Sweden, Finland, the Netherlands and Iceland issued a joint statement of solidarity with the LGBTQ+ community in Turkey.[^cite_note-42]
