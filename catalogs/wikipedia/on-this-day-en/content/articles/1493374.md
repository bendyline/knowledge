The **Mil Mi-17** ([NATO reporting name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_reporting_name): **Hip**) is a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet)-designed Russian [military helicopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_helicopter) family introduced in 1975 (Mi-8M), continuing in production as of 2024 at two factories in Russia, in [Kazan](202295.md) and [Ulan-Ude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulan-Ude). It is known as the **Mi-8M** series in Russian service. The helicopter is mostly used as a medium twin-turbine [transport helicopter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_helicopter), as well as an armed [gunship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_gunship) version.

## Development

Developed from the basic [Mi-8](957233.md) airframe, the Mi-17 was fitted with the larger [Klimov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klimov) TV3-117MT engines, rotors, and transmission developed for the [Mi-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-14), along with fuselage improvements for heavier loads. Optional engines for "[hot and high](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_and_high)" conditions are the 1,545 kW (2,072 shp) Isotov TV3-117VM. Recent exports to China and [Venezuela](32374.md) for use in high mountains have the new [Klimov VK-2500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klimov_VK-2500) version of the [Klimov TV3-117](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klimov_TV3-117) engine with [FADEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FADEC) control.

The designation Mi-17 is for export; the Russian armed forces call it the Mi-8MT. The Mi-17 is recognisable by the tail rotor on the port side instead of the starboard side, and dust shields in front of the engine intakes. Engine cowls are shorter than on the TV2-powered Mi-8, not extending as far over the cockpit, and an opening for a [bleed air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleed_air) valve outlet is present forward of the exhaust.

Actual model numbers vary by builder, engine type, and other options. As an example, the sixteen new Ulan Ude-built machines delivered to the [Czech Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Air_Force) in 2005 with -VM model engines were designated as Mi-171Sh, a development of the Mi-8AMTSh. Modifications include a new large door on the right side, improved [Czech](5321.md)-built [APU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auxiliary_power_unit), and [Kevlar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevlar) armour plates around the cockpit area and engines. Eight have a loading ramp in place of the usual clamshell doors and can load a vehicle up to the size of an [SUV](47696.md).

Licensed production of the Mi-17 started in China, with production being led by Mil Moscow Helicopter Plant JSC and the Sichuan Lantian Helicopter Company Limited in Chengdu, Sichuan province. The plant built 20 helicopters in 2008, using Russian Ulan-Ude-supplied kits; annual production was expected to increase to 80 helicopters. The variants planned to be built by Lantian include the Mi-171, Mi-17V-5, and Mi-17V-7.[^cite_note-1] In 2021 it was reported that China was replacing the Russian-made Mi-17 with their own [Z-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin_Z-20), except possibly for the Mi-171Sh assault helicopter; the last Mi-17 order was in 2014.[^cite_note-2]

In 2021 the website of [Russian Helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Helicopters), the manufacturer, said that the Mi-8/17 was "the most widely operated helicopter in history."[^cite_note-3]

## Operational history



An [Army of North Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_North_Macedonia) Mi-17 performing a tight low-level right turn

Royal Cambodian Air Force Mi-17s were used during the [Cambodian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_government)'s offensives; by 1994 ten operational airframes were in service, with five converted to [helicopter gunships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_gunship) equipped with 57 mm S-5 [rocket pods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_pod), providing [air support](600792.md) for ground forces attacking the [Khmer Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Rouge) positions. In 1996 the Government launched an offensive during the dry season at the Khmer Rouge stronghold of [Anlong Veng and Pailin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pailin), using five Mi-17 gunships and eight Mi-17-Mi-8 troops transports.[^cite_note-4]

In May 1999, during [Operation Safed Sagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Safed_Sagar), the Mi-17 was used in the first air phase of the [Kargil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_War) by 129HU of the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) (IAF) against the [Pakistan Army](1965925.md). One IAF Mi-17 and one IAF [MiG-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-21) escort were shot down by [Pakistan Army Air Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Army_Air_Defence_Corps) [Anza-II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anza_\(missile\)) [shoulder-fired missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoulder-fired_missile). This led to the withdrawal of armed helicopters and attacks by [fixed-wing aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-wing_aircraft) such as the [Mirage 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Mirage_2000) armed with [LGBs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser-guided_bomb) in the Kargil theatre.[^cite_note-acig-5]

The Mi-17 was used extensively by the [Sri Lanka Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Air_Force) in [Sri Lanka's war on terrorism by the LTTE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_civil_war). Seven of them were lost in combat and attacks on airports.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-6]

Freelance pilot [Neall Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neall_Ellis) operated an Mi-17 in support of the [Sierra Leone](27308.md) [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone/Government) in the [Sierra Leone Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Leone_Civil_War), ferrying ammunition and other supplies to government troops.[^cite_note-7]



US special forces help [Northern Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Alliance) troops from a CIA-operated MI-17 at [Bagram Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagram_Airbase) during [Operation Anaconda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Anaconda), 2002.

[US Army Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Special_Forces) in Afghanistan extensively used [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA)-operated Mi-17s during the initial stages of [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom).[^cite_note-8]

The Mi-17 is used for passenger transport by [Air Koryo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Koryo), national airline of [North Korea](21255.md). Previous flights include those between [Pyongyang](47948.md) and [Kaesong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaesong) and Pyongyang and [Haeju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haeju).

The [Mexican Navy](1225277.md) uses its Mi-17s for anti-narcotic operations such as locating [marijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marijuana) fields and dispatching [marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Naval_Infantry) to eradicate the [plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marijuana_cultivation).[^cite_note-9]

The [Slovak Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_Air_Force) and [Croatian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Air_Force) operated Mi-17s in [Kosovo](17391.md) as part of [KFOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Force).

Both the [pro-Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Air_Force_\(1951%E2%80%932011\)) and [anti-Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Libyan_Air_Force) forces in the [2011 Libyan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Libyan_civil_war) have operated Mi-17s.



[Afghan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Air_Force) Mi-17 showing the clamshell cargo door arrangement

Mi-17s are operated by the [Afghan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Air_Force). In July 2010 two Mi-17 were flown by a mixed crew of [United States Air Force](32090.md) and Afghan Air Force personnel in a 13-hour mission that rescued 2,080 civilians from flood waters. This was the largest rescue by two helicopters in USAF history. USAF pilot [Lt Col](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel) Gregory Roberts received the [Distinguished Flying Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distinguished_Flying_Cross_\(United_States\)) for the mission.[^cite_note-10]

During the [Tham Luang cave rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tham_Luang_cave_rescue) in July 2018, the [Royal Thai Army](1230371.md) used the Mi-17 helicopter for searching operations, and evacuating the first batch of survivors from Tham Luang to Chiang Rai hospital;[^cite_note-11] on 10 July 2018 a Mil Mi-17 helicopter took the last evacuated boy to the hospital.[^cite_note-12]

During the 19 November 2023 [Houthi](17240332.md) [hijacking of the *Galaxy Leader*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_hijacking_of_the_Galaxy_Leader), one Mi-17 helicopter was used to land the hijacking team on the deck of the *Galaxy Leader*.[^cite_note-13]

In August 2025, the [Indian Air Force](403355.md), from its Northern Sector, deployed five of its Mi-17 helicopters along with a [Chinook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CH-47) and [C-130J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130J) transport aircraft each for the [flood relief operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relief_operations) following the [Uttarakhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Uttarakhand_flash_flood) and [Kishtwar district flash floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Kishtwar_district_flash_flood).[^cite_note-14] IAF Mi-17s equipped with [Bambi Buckets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_bucket) were also deployed to stop the spread of forest fires in [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh) and [Nagaland](408677.md) in early 2026, dropping nearly 12,000 tonnes of water and [fire retardant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_retardant) from low altitudes.[^cite_note-15] The IAF is also refitting certain Mi-17V5 units to carry the [Spike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spike_\(missile\)) missile.[^cite_note-16]

On 29 April 2026, the [Unmanned Systems Forces (Ukraine)](76045069.md) destroyed four helicopters, two [Mi-28s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi-28) and two Mi-17s, while refuelling in Voronezh Oblast using drones.[^cite_note-17]

## Orders (2000 onwards)



Iranian Mi-171Sh armed with unidentified cruise missiles

In October 2007, the [Saudi Arabian](349303.md) Government cancelled the purchase of 64 [NHIndustries NH90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHIndustries_NH90) helicopters and agreed to buy 150 Russian-made Mil Mi-17 and [Mi-35](384591.md) helicopters instead.[^cite_note-18]

On 28 October 2008, the Royal Thai Army announced a deal to buy six Mi-17s to meet its requirement for a medium-lift helicopter. This is the first time the [Thai armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_armed_forces) have acquired Russian aircraft instead of American aircraft.[^cite_note-19] *[Flight International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_International)* quotes the Thai Army's rationale: "We are buying three Mi-17 helicopters for the price of one [Black Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UH-60_Black_Hawk). The Mi-17 can also carry more than 30 troops, while the Black Hawk could carry only 13 soldiers. These were the key factors behind the decision."[^cite_note-20]

On 15 December 2008, it was reported that [India](14533.md) ordered 80 Mi-17V-5 helicopters worth \$1.375 billion, which would be delivered to the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) between 2011 and 2014 to replace aging Mi-8s.[^cite_note-21] In August 2010, it was reported that India planned to order another 59 Mi-17s.[^cite_note-22] The first Mi-17V-5s entered service with India in February 2012.[^cite_note-23] In December 2012, India signed a contract for 71 aircraft at a reported cost of US\$1.3 billion.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] In December 2014 it was reported that India is in agreement with the Russian Federation to produce on its territory Mi-17s and Ka-226Ts.[^cite_note-26] All 151 helicopters were delivered as of February 2016.[^cite_note-27]

On 11 June 2009, it was announced that the [United States](3434750.md) had handed over four Mi-17 cargo helicopters to the [Pakistan Army](1965925.md) to facilitate its [counter-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-terrorism) operations. This followed an urgent request for helicopters by [Pakistan's Chief of Army Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Army_Staff_\(Pakistan\)) General [Ashfaq Parvez Kayani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashfaq_Parvez_Kayani) in a [leaked US embassy cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_diplomatic_cables_leak).[^cite_note-28]

On 16 September 2009, the [United States Navy](20518076.md) delivered the last two of four Mi-17s to the [Afghan National Army Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_National_Army_Air_Corps).[^cite_note-navy-29] On 19 June 2010, it was announced that the US government would buy and refurbish 31 more Mi-17 helicopters from Russia to supply the Afghan Air Force.[^cite_note-30]



[Afghan Commandos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_National_Army_Commando_Corps) exiting a Mi-17 of the Special Missions Wing of the Afghan Air Force during a training exercise

The US was reportedly considering adding the helicopter to the US military for [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces) use in order to obscure troop movements.[^cite_note-Russian_copters-31] The US has used some Mi-8s and Mi-17s for training,[^cite_note-Harding-32] and has purchased units for allies in Iraq, Afghanistan and Pakistan.[^cite_note-Russian_copters-31]

In August 2010 a contract was signed by the [Argentine Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Air_Force) for two Mi-17Es, plus an option on another three, to support [Antarctic bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Antarctica).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

In September 2010, the Polish Defense Minister announced that his country would buy five new Mi-17s from Russia, to support Polish operations in Afghanistan. All five Mi-17-1Vs were delivered by 2011.[^cite_note-35]

In 2011, Chief of Staff of the [Afghan National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_National_Army) [Abdul Wahab Wardak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Wahab_Wardak) announced that the [US government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_government) would buy Mi-17s for use by Afghanistan's troops. He explained the choice of the Russian helicopter over the American [Chinook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinook_helicopter) was due to the familiarity of the Afghan technical and pilot staff with the helicopter type and that it is better suited for [Afghanistan](737.md)'s environment. The United States continued to purchase the helicopters for Afghanistan in 2013, despite a congressional prohibition.[^cite_note-36] Overall, 63 Mi-17s were acquired through the 2011 contract at a cost of US\$16.4 to US\$18.4 million each, or US\$4 to US\$6 million more each than a refurbished American Chinook.[^cite_note-37]

China signed two contracts with Rosoboronexport in 2009 and 2012 for 32 and 52 Mi-171E, respectively.[^cite_note-38]

In 2014 and 2015, Bangladesh ordered a total of 11 Mi-171Sh helicopters. 5 more ordered in 2017.[^cite_note-39]

The Helicopters of Russia has concluded a contract with the Defense Ministry of Belarus for the supply of twelve Mi-8MTV-5 military transport helicopters in 2016–2017. The Belarusian military will get the helicopters possessing the same parameters as those used by the Russian military.[^cite_note-40]

In the course of the Army-2017 International Military Technical Forum signed a contract for the supply of helicopters to Russian state special purpose aviation. Three Mi-8AMTSh military transport helicopters were produced and three more were ordered later.[^cite_note-41]

Royal Thai Army ordered 2 Mi-17V-5s in September 2017 which received in December 2018 plus 3 more received in March 2021 for a total of 10 delivered since 2011. RF National Guard ordered two Mi-8AMTSHs in April 2018.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

Russia supplied seven Mi-35 and three Mi-17 helicopters to Serbia.[^cite_note-45]

A contract was signed on 18 January 2019 between Russian Helicopters, Kazakhstan Engineering, and Kazakh firm Aircraft Repair Plant No 405 (ARP 405) that will see 45 kit versions of the Mil Mi-8AMT and Mi-171 helicopters delivered to Kazakhstan until 2025 for local assembly.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

In 2019, China ordered 100 Mi-171 (including 18 combat-transport Mi-171Sh) and 21 [Kazan Ansat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazan_Ansat) helicopters.[^cite_note-49] A contract with the civilian airline QINGDAO for the supply of six Mi-171 helicopters with VK-2500-03 engines was signed in December 2019.[^cite_note-50]

In 2019, the [Philippine Air Force](27918720.md) reportedly expressed its interest in acquiring 16 Mi-171 helicopters for its heavy-lift helicopter requirement, with a possible option to add one Mi-171 that is fitted for VVIP transport, in a deal worth P12.5 billion to be signed during the official visit to [Russia](25391.md) by President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md). From 3 to 7 March 2020, a delegation composed of Philippine military and defense officials, and officials of the Embassy of the Philippines in Russia met with representatives from Sovtechnoexport and visited the [Ulan-Ude Aviation Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulan-Ude_Aviation_Plant). Local defense blog Maxdefense Philippines reported that a Notice of Award (NOA) was issued during the third quarter of 2020, although it was unclear if it was awarded to Sovtechnoexport or [Rosoboronexport](3094695.md).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] On 27 July 2022, the Philippine government canceled the deal to purchase 16 Mi-17 helicopters due to fears of possible US sanctions.[^cite_note-59]

In 2021 Russia offered the [Argentine Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Air_Force) Mil Mi-17 helicopters as part of a bigger arms deals.[^cite_note-60]

Russia in October 2021 delivered to Mali 2 Mi-171Sh and 2 Mi-17V-5 helicopters in the framework of a contract signed in December 2020.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

Bangladesh and Peru each ordered two Mi-171A2s in 2021.[^cite_note-63]

On 13 April 2022, US President [Joe Biden](145422.md) announced that the US Government would supply 11 Mi-17 helicopters to the Ukrainian government to aid in its defense during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-64] The US supplied 16 Mi-17s including five that were undergoing maintenance in Ukraine when Russia invaded. These helicopters belonged to the [former Afghan government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Afghanistan), but were paid for by the US under the Afghanistan Security Forces Fund. Therefore, the Pentagon treated these aircraft as its property after that government [fell](68481047.md) in September 2021.[^cite_note-65]

[Iran](14653.md) reportedly ordered 12 search and rescue helicopters of the Mi-8/17/171 family in early 2024.[^cite_note-66] Iran reportedly ordered an additional 21 Mi-171 transport helicopters in mid-2026.[^cite_note-67]

## Accidents and notable incidents

- On 16 September 2000, a Mi-17 helicopter of the [Sri Lanka Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Air_Force) [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_SLAF_Mi-17_Crash) near Aranayake in Kegalle District, [Sri Lanka](26750.md) with Minister of Shipping, Ports and Rehabilitation [M. H. M. Ashraff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._H._M._Ashraff) on board. Besides the minister, there were 14 others on board – nine party officials, three bodyguards and two crew members. The authorities initially claimed that engine failure had caused the crash. The government immediately ordered an inquiry into the crash and in January 2001. President Kumaratunga appointed a Presidential Commission to inquire into the crash. However, neither found any conclusive evidence for the crash's cause.

  [^cite_note-129]

- On 30 July 2005, a Mi-17 presidential helicopter crashed in mountain ranges in [South Sudan](32350676.md) due to poor visibility claiming the life of then President of South Sudan [John Garang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Garang), six of his colleagues and seven Ugandan crew members. He was returning from a private visit in [Rwakitura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwakitura) meeting President [Yoweri Museveni](293409.md) of Uganda.

  [^cite_note-130]

  [^cite_note-131]

- On 12 January 2008, a Mi-17 of the [Macedonian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_Republic_of_Macedonia) [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Macedonian_Armed_Forces_Mil_Mi-17_crash), killing all three crew members and eight passengers.

- On 3 March 2008, an [Iraqi Air Force](1817852.md) Mi-17 (Mi-8AMT) crashed near Baiji while ferrying troops from Tal Afar to the capital Baghdad. All eight people on board perished in the accident.

  [^cite_note-132]

- On 31 May 2008, a [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) Mi-171 transport crashed in southwest [Sichuan](65185.md) province, killing 5 crew and 13 passengers on board. It was on a rescue mission during the [2008 Sichuan earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Sichuan_earthquake).

  [^cite_note-133]

- On 14 January 2009, an [Afghan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Air_Force) Mi-17 crashed in Herat while en route to Farah province. All 13 on board were killed, including Maj. Gen. Fazl Ahmad Sayar, one of Afghanistan's four regional commanders.

  [^cite_note-134]

- On 3 May 2009 a [Venezuelan Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venezuelan_Army) Mi-17V-5 [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1chira_helicopter_crash) in the state of [Táchira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1chira) killing all 17 people on board.

  [^cite_note-135]

  [^cite_note-137]

  [^cite_note-139]

## Specifications (Mil-171A2)



[Klimov VK-2500PS-03](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klimov_VK-2500PS-03) on display at [Bangladesh Military Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Military_Museum), [Bangladesh](3454.md)&#x20;

*Data from* *Russian Helicopters*[^cite_note-211]

**General characteristics**

- **Crew:** 3 (pilot, copilot, engineer)

- **Capacity:** 24 troops / 12 stretchers / 4,000 kg (8,818 lb) cargo internally / 5,000 kg (11,023 lb) externally slung.

- **Length:** 18.465 m (60 ft 7 in)

- **Height:** 5.65 m (18 ft 6 in)

- **Empty weight:** 7,489 kg (16,510 lb)

- **Gross weight:** 11,100 kg (24,471 lb)

- **Max takeoff weight:** 13,000 kg (28,660 lb) normal

13,500 kg (29,762 lb) with under-slung load

- **Powerplant:** 2 × [Klimov VK-2500PS-03](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klimov_VK-2500PS-03) [turboshaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboshaft) engines, 1,800 kW (2,400 hp) each for takeoff

2,000 kW (2,700 hp) emergency rating

- **Main rotor diameter:** 21.25 m (69 ft 9 in)

- **Main rotor area:** 354.7 m

  2

  &#x20;(3,818 sq ft) \***Blade section:** [NACA 23012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NACA_airfoil)

  [^cite_note-Selig-212]

**Performance**

- **Maximum speed:** 280 km/h (170 mph, 150 kn)

- **Cruise speed:** 260 km/h (160 mph, 140 kn)

- **Range:** 800 km (500 mi, 430 nmi)

- **Service ceiling:** 6,000 m (20,000 ft) \***Hover ceiling OGE:** 4,000 m (13,000 ft)

- **Rate of climb:** 8 m/s (1,600 ft/min)

**Armament**\


- Some variants such as Mi-8AMTSh and Mi-171Sh have disposable stores on six hardpoints, including rockets such as [S-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-8_\(rocket\)) and [gun pods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_pod) such as [UPK-23-250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gryazev-Shipunov_GSh-23). Some variants such as Mi-171SH-HV and Mi-171SH-VN can be armed with [anti-tank guided missiles](37517.md) such as [Ataka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9M120_Ataka) and [precision-guided munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition) along with other armaments. Have one or two window-mounted [machine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_gun) such as the [PK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PK_machine_gun).

- Other: [hardpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardpoint) which can carry [drop tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drop_tanks), to increase the range, or up to 4,000 kg (8,800 lb) of [bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_bomb).
