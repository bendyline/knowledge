The **Union of European Football Associations** (**UEFA**)[^cite_note-7] is the governing body of [football](10568.md) in Europe and one of [FIFA](11049.md)'s six continental confederations for association football consisting of 55 national associations. It runs national and club competitions for men and women, such as the [Champions League](44220.md), the [Women's Champions League](1985338.md), the [European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship), and the [Women's European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Championship) as well as the [Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League).

## History and membership

UEFA was officially inaugurated on 15 June 1954 in [Basel](4911.md), Switzerland, after consultation between the Italian, French, and Belgian associations.[^cite_note-8] At the founding meeting, 25 members were present. However, six other associations which were not present were still recognised as founding members, bringing the total of founding associations to 31.[^cite_note-9] UEFA grew to more than 50 members by the mid-1990s, as new associations were born out of the fragmentation of the Soviet Union, Yugoslavia and Czechoslovakia into their constituent states. UEFA's main headquarters after its foundation were located in Paris, but moved to [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern) in 1960. Finally, they moved to [Nyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyon), Switzerland, in 1995, opening the organisation's current headquarters in 1999.[^cite_note-10]

### Current members

UEFA membership coincides for the most part with recognition as a [sovereign country](23604120.md) in Europe (48 out of 55 members are sovereign UN member states), although there are some exceptions. One UN member state (Monaco) and one UN General Assembly non-member observer state (Vatican City) are not members. Some UEFA members are not sovereign states, but form part of a larger recognised sovereign state in the context of international law. These include England, Northern Ireland, Scotland, and Wales ([UK constituent countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_countries_of_the_United_Kingdom)); Gibraltar ([UK overseas territory](207114.md)); Faroe Islands ([Danish Realm](11746442.md)); and Kosovo ([state with limited recognition](15808914.md)). In the context of these countries, sports governance tends to be carried out at the territorial level coterminous with the UEFA member entity. However, UEFA has declined membership to other [polities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polity) like [Jersey](15693.md).[^cite_note-11]

Some UEFA members are transcontinental states ([Azerbaijan](746.md), [Georgia](48768.md), [Kazakhstan](16642.md), [Russia](25391.md), and [Turkey](11125639.md)), and others are considered part of Europe both culturally and politically (Turkey, [Cyprus](5593.md), and [Armenia](10918072.md)). Countries which had been members of the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) (AFC) were also admitted to the European Football Association, such as [Israel](9282173.md) (because it had been banned from the AFC group in 1974) and Kazakhstan. Involving clubs, some UEFA member associations allow teams from outside their association's main territory to take part in their "domestic" competition, for example, [AS Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC) in the French League, various Welsh clubs are in the English leagues, English-based [The New Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Saints) in the Welsh League, or [Derry City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derry_City_F.C.), situated in Northern Ireland, plays in the Ireland-based [League of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Ireland).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

On 28 February 2022, due to the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) and in accordance with a recommendation by the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) (IOC), UEFA suspended the participation of Russia.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-fifa-uefa-15] The [Russian Football Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Football_Union) unsuccessfully appealed the UEFA ban to the [Court of Arbitration for Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Arbitration_for_Sport), which upheld the ban.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] On 26 September 2023 the ban was lifted for the [Russia U-17 team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_under-17_football_team) enabling them to complete in the [2024 Euro U-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_UEFA_European_Under-17_Championship) with UEFA saying "by banning children from our competitions, we not only fail to recognise and uphold a fundamental right for their holistic development but we directly discriminate against them". The lifting of the ban also applied to all teams, men and women, of underage players.[^cite_note-18] This was rejected by the FAs of Ukraine, England, and Sweden, with all three threatening to boycott matches against Russia.[^cite_note-19]

## Executive committee

The UEFA executive committee comprises the UEFA president and up to 19 other members: 16 elected by the UEFA Congress (including at least two female members), two by [European Football Clubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Football_Clubs) and one by the [European Leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Leagues). UEFA runs official international competitions in Europe and some countries of [Northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Asia), [Southwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Asia) and [Central](6742.md) Asia for national teams and professional clubs, known as *[UEFA competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_competitions)*, some of which are regarded as the world's most prestigious tournaments.

UEFA is the organiser of two of the most prestigious competitions in international football: The [UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) and the [UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League). The main competition for men's national teams is the [UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) (also known as the Euro), which started in 1958, with the first finals in [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_European_Nations'_Cup), and was known as the European Nations Cup until 1964. The [UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League) is the second tournament of UEFA and was introduced in 2018. The tournament largely replaced the international friendly matches previously played on the FIFA International Match Calendar. It will be played every two years.

UEFA also runs national competitions at [Under-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Under-21_Championship), [Under-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Under-19_Championship) and [Under-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Under-17_Championship) levels. For women's national teams, UEFA operates the [UEFA Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Championship) for senior national sides as well as [Women's Under-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Under-19_Championship) and [Women's Under-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Women's_Under-17_Championship) Championships.

Beside continental European competitions for national and their junior teams, the UEFA organises various qualification male and female tournaments among European national and their junior teams for World Cups (organised by FIFA) and Olympics (organised by IOC).

UEFA also organised the [UEFA–CAF Meridian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA%E2%80%93CAF_Meridian_Cup) with [CAF](715265.md) for youth teams in an effort to boost youth football. UEFA launched the [UEFA Regions' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Regions'_Cup), for semi-professional teams representing their local region, in 1999. In [futsal](188197.md) there is the [UEFA Futsal Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Futsal_Championship) and [UEFA Under-19 Futsal Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Under-19_Futsal_Championship). Despite the existence of UEFA's Futsal *and* Beach soccer committee, UEFA does not organise any beach soccer competitions. International and club beach soccer competitions for UEFA members are organised externally by [Beach Soccer Worldwide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_Soccer_Worldwide).

The [Italian](362466.md), [German](250204.md), [Spanish](609825.md), [French](250197.md) and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team)[^cite_note-42] men's [national teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_sports_team) are the only teams to have won the European football championship in all categories.

### Club

Further information: [List of UEFA club competition winners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_UEFA_club_competition_winners)



UEFA member countries by club competition entry entitlements, 2009/10

The top-ranked UEFA competition is the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md), which started in 1955 as the European Champion Clubs' Cup (or simply the European Cup) and initially only gathered the top team of each country; this competition has since been expanded to gather the top 1–4 teams of each country's league (the number of teams depend on that country's ranking and can be upgraded or downgraded).

A second, lower-ranked competition is the [UEFA Europa League](232175.md). This competition, for national knockout cup winners and high-placed league teams, was launched by UEFA in 1971 as a successor of both the former UEFA Cup and the [Inter-Cities Fairs Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Cities_Fairs_Cup) (also began in 1955). A third competition, the [UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup), which started in 1960, was absorbed into the UEFA Cup (now UEFA Europa League) in 1999.

In December 2018, UEFA announced the creation of a third club competition, later named the [UEFA Europa Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Europa_Conference_League). The competition features 32 teams in 8 groups of 4, with a knockout round between the second placed teams in Europa Conference League and the third placed teams in the Europa League, leading to a final 16 knockout stage featuring the eight group winners. The first edition of the competition was played in 2021–2022.[^cite_note-43]

In women's football UEFA also conducts the [UEFA Women's Champions League](1985338.md) for club teams. The competition was first held in 2001, and was known as the UEFA Women's Cup until 2009.

The [UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Super_Cup) pits the winners of the Champions League against the winners of the Europa League (previously the winners of the Cup Winners' Cup), and came into being in 1973.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

The [UEFA Intertoto Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Intertoto_Cup) was a summer competition, previously operated by several Central European football associations, which was relaunched and recognised as [official UEFA club competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_club_competition_records) by UEFA in 1995.[^cite_note-47] The last Intertoto Cup took place in 2008.

The [European/South American Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_Cup_\(1960%E2%80%932004\)) was jointly organised with [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) between the [Champions League](44220.md) and the [Copa Libertadores](299730.md) winners.[^cite_note-48]

Only [five teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_UEFA_club_competition_winners)[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] ([Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_FC), [Ajax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Ajax), [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.), [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) and [Chelsea](7473.md)[^cite_note-51]) have [won each of the three main competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_club_competition_records#List_of_teams_to_have_won_the_three_main_European_club_competitions) (European Cup/UEFA Champions League, European Cup Winners' Cup/UEFA Cup Winners' Cup and UEFA Cup/UEFA Europa League),[^cite_note-52] a feat that is no longer possible for any team that did not win the Cup Winners' Cup. There are currently eight teams throughout Europe that have won two of the three trophies; all but one have won the Cup Winners' Cup, four require a win in the Champions League and four require a UEFA Europa League win.

Until the first staging of the [UEFA Europa Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_UEFA_Europa_Conference_League) in 2022, Juventus of Italy was the only team in Europe to win all [UEFA's official championships and cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_club_competition_records)[^cite_note-official-53] and, in commemoration of achieving that feat, have received *[The UEFA Plaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_UEFA_Plaque)* by the Union of European Football Associations on 12 July 1988.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-UEFA_Plaque-55]

UEFA's premier [futsal](188197.md) competition is the [UEFA Futsal Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Futsal_Cup), a tournament started in 2001 which replaced the former [Futsal European Clubs Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futsal_European_Clubs_Championship). This event, despite enjoying a long and well-established tradition in the European futsal community, dating back to 1984, was never recognised as official by UEFA.

There was an attempt to create a Europa League-style second tier women's club competition, which had been in discussion since 2021.[^cite_note-56] In December 2023, the attempt came into a fruition, with the first edition of the competition has been played in 2025–26.[^cite_note-57] In December 2024, the name of the competition, "UEFA Women's Europa Cup", was announced.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

- **&#x20;4th&#x20;**&#x20;– Fourth place

- **QF** – Quarter-finals (1934–1938, 1954–1970, and 1986–present: knockout round of 8)

- **R3** – Round 3 (2026–present: knockout round of 16)

- **R2** – Round 2 (1974–1978: second group stage, top 8; 1982: second group stage, top 12; 1986–2022: knockout round of 16; 2026–present: knockout round of 32)

- **R1** – Round 1 (1930, 1950–1970 and 1986–present: group stage; 1934–1938: knockout round of 16; 1974–1982: first group stage)

- *Q* — Qualified for upcoming tournament

- &#x20;**••**  – Qualified but withdrew

- &#x20;**•**  – Did not qualify

- &#x20;×  – Did not enter / Withdrew / Banned

- &#x20;   – Hosts

## Sanctions

### Against associations

- [Lithuania](17675.md) [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_Football_Federation), in 1990 sanctions were imposed due to the secession of the Lithuanian Football Federation from the Football Federation of the Soviet Union

- [Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) [FR Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Association_of_Serbia_and_Montenegro), in 1992–1998 sanctions were imposed due to the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War) (as part of the [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars))

- [Russia](25391.md) [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Football_Union), in 2022 sanctions were imposed due to the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).

- [Belarus](3457.md) [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Federation_of_Belarus), in 2022 sanctions were imposed due to [supporting Russia in its invasion of Ukraine](70175917.md).

### Against clubs

- [Albania](738.md) [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Football_Association), in 1967 special sanctions were imposed against [1966–67 Albanian Superliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966%E2%80%9367_Albanian_Superliga) due to its political background

- [England](9316.md) [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association), in 1985–1991 sanctions were imposed against English association football clubs due to the [Heysel Stadium disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heysel_Stadium_disaster) by suspending their participation in continental competitions for five years

- [Italy](14532.md) [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIGC), in 1974–1975 sanctions were imposed against [SS Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Lazio) due to its fans, Italy was restricted from the European Cup to which Lazio qualified

- [Netherlands](21148.md) [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Football_Association), in 1990–1991 sanctions were imposed against [AFC Ajax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Ajax) due to its fans, the Netherlands were restricted from the European Cup to which Ajax qualified

## Corruption and controversy

Dissatisfied fans across Europe have referred to the organisation as *UEFA [mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia)*, including in Russia's top league,[^cite_note-90] in Bulgaria's top league,[^cite_note-91] and in a Champions League group stage match held in Sweden.[^cite_note-92] The term has also been covered for its use outside of stadiums, for example during a protest in Kosovo outside an EU building following the [Serbia v Albania (UEFA Euro 2016 qualifying)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_v_Albania_\(UEFA_Euro_2016_qualifying\)) match.[^cite_note-93] [F.C. Copenhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F.C._Copenhagen) supporters displayed banners around the city, with slogans such as "UEFA MAFIA – THE PANDEMIC OF FOOTBALL", when UEFA ordered their [2019–20 Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_UEFA_Europa_League) round of 16 return leg be played behind closed doors, despite reduced capacity being allowed by the Danish government.[^cite_note-94]

Following the [2015 FIFA corruption case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_corruption_case), the then-president of UEFA, [Michel Platini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Platini), was also involved in the case. Swiss prosecutors accused FIFA president Sepp Blatter of making a "disloyal payment" of \$2m (£1.6m) to Mr Platini. Swiss attorney general, [Michael Lauber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Lauber?action=edit\&redlink=1), stated: "We didn't interview Mr Platini as a witness, that's not true. We investigated against him in between as a witness and an accused person".[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] Both Platini and [Sepp Blatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepp_Blatter) were banned from football-related activity. Platini appealed to [Court of Arbitration for Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Arbitration_for_Sport), which lowered the six-year ban to four years. He further appealed to Swiss courts and the [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights) but the courts rejected his appeals.[^cite_note-97]

In 2019 UEFA's decision to host Europa League Cup final in Baku, Azerbaijan left one of the finalists, Arsenal, with a decision to withdraw their Armenian player [Henrikh Mkhitaryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrikh_Mkhitaryan) out of the competition due to safety concerns,[^cite_note-98] and there has been long-standing debates about the extent to which the elite clubs or UEFA itself should exert the most influence on the game.[^cite_note-99] UEFA's decision to partner with blockchain company [Chiliz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiliz) in February 2022 was criticised and described as 'incomprehensible' by fan groups across Europe.[^cite_note-100]

In July 2025, UEFA sanctioned [Crystal Palace F.C.](385313.md) and [Olympique Lyonnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais) for breaching its [multi-club ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-club_ownership_\(association_football\)) rules, due to overlapping ownership by John Textor's Eagle Football Group. Palace, who had qualified for the [UEFA Europa League](232175.md) as FA Cup winners, were instead placed in the [UEFA Europa Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Europa_Conference_League), while Lyon retained the Europa League spot. The [Court of Arbitration for Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Arbitration_for_Sport) rejected Palace's appeal in August 2025.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

In July 2026, UEFA made multiple public statements regarding [FIFA](11049.md) President [Gianni Infantino](9286117.md)'s proposal to create a \$20 billion commercial subsidiary, the [FIFA Forward Enterprise](83820435.md), which would be involved with the running of FIFA's major tournaments such as the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md), [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup), and other tournaments.[^cite_note-103] UEFA initially rejected it completely, calling upon FIFA to stop these plans and to not inject private money into its competitions. The situation escalated on 30 July, when UEFA released a statement in conjunction with all 55 member associations after a vote to boycott all FIFA competitions indefinitely until the commercialisation plans were stopped and assurances were made that they would never be brought up in the future.[^cite_note-104]

In August 2026, UEFA began preparing a criminal complaint against Infantino in Switzerland for financial mismanagement. Requests for discovery were also filed in [Manhattan](45470.md) for potential evidence from [Thrive Capital Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrive_Capital_Management), the would-be primary investors, and southern [Florida](18933066.md), where FIFA has offices.[^cite_note-105]
