**Mazar-i-Sharif** or **Mazar-e-Sharif**[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Afghanistan) in northern [Afghanistan](737.md), serving as the [capital](181337.md) and largest city of [Balkh Province](457218.md). It has 12 city districts (*[nahias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)*) and an estimated population of 568,013 people.[^cite_note-population-4][^cite_note-2_Mazar-i-Sharif_districts_get_office_complexes-7] [Hafiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafiz_\(Quran\)) Abdul Rahman Himat is the current [mayor](101146.md) of the city.[^cite_note-Municipality_of_Mazar_Sharif-1] His predecessor was Mohammad Kazim Tariq.[^cite_note-8]

Mazar-i-Sharif is home to the [Mausoleum of Imam Ali](17910844.md). The city has long been a tourist destination because of its famous shrines as well as the [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_architecture) and [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period) archeological sites. The city is also home to the [Mawlana Jalaluddin Mohammad Balkhi International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawlana_Jalaluddin_Mohammad_Balkhi_International_Airport) and [Balkh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkh_University). It is linked by a [road network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_Ring_Road) with [Hairatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hairatan) to the north, [Kunduz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunduz) to the east, [Aybak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aybak) to the southeast, [Bamyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamyan) to the south, and [Sheberghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheberghan) to the west. It is about 55 km (34 mi) to the south from the [Afghanistan–Uzbekistan border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan%E2%80%93Uzbekistan_border). The ancient city of [Balkh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkh) is about 25 km (16 mi) slightly to the northwest of Mazar-i-Sharif.

The region around Mazar-i-Sharif has been historically part of [Greater Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khorasan) and was controlled by the [Tahirids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahirid_dynasty) followed by the [Saffarids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffarid_dynasty), [Samanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samanid_Empire), [Ghaznavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavids), [Ghurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghurid_dynasty), [Ilkhanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanids), [Timurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_dynasty), and [Khanate of Bukhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Bukhara) until 1751 when it became part of the [Durrani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire).

Mazar-i-Sharif is the regional hub of northern Afghanistan, located in close proximity to [Termez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termez) in [Uzbekistan](31853.md) and the [Khatlon Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khatlon_Region) of [Tajikistan](30108.md). It has the highest percentage of built-up land (91%) of all the Afghan provincial capitals,[^cite_note-Volume-II-3] and it has additional built-up area extending beyond the municipal boundary but forming a part of the larger [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area). It is also the lowest-lying major city in the country, about 357 m (1,171 ft) above [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level). Although the city was mostly spared from the devastation that occurred in Afghanistan's other major cities during the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War) and subsequent [civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Civil_War_\(1992%E2%80%931996\)), it was regarded in 2020 as one of the dangerous cities in the country.[^cite_note-9]

On 14 August 2021, Mazar-i-Sharif was seized by [Taliban](30635.md) fighters, becoming the 25th provincial capital to be captured by the Taliban as part of the wider [2021 Taliban offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive).

## Etymology

The name *Mazar-i-Sharif* means "tomb of the saint", a reference to the purported tomb of [Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali), cousin, son-in-law and companion of the [Islamic](6037917.md) prophet [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad). The tomb is housed in the large, blue-tiled sanctuary and mosque in the center of the city known as the [Shrine of Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazrat_Ali_Mazar) or the Blue Mosque.[^cite_note-10]

## History

Further information: [History of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Afghanistan)

### Ancient period

The [Achaemenids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) controlled the region from the sixth century BCE. [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) conquered the area but it was then incorporated into the [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire) after his death. The decline of the Seleucids consequently led to the emergence of the [Greco-Bactrian kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Bactrian_Kingdom). Around 130 BCE, the [Sakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka) occupied the region and the Greco-Bactrian kingdom fell. The [Yuezhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuezhi) took Mazar-i-Sharif and the surrounding area which led to the creation of the [Kushan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire). The [Sasanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) subsequently controlled the area after the fall of the Kushans. The Islamic conquests reached Mazar-i-Sharif in 651 CE.[^cite_note-11]

### 9th century until 1919

The region around Mazar-i-Sharif has been historically part of [Greater Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khorasan) and was controlled by the [Tahirids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahirid_dynasty) followed by the [Saffarids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffarid_dynasty), [Samanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samanids), [Ghaznavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavids), [Ghurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghurid_dynasty), [Ilkhanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanids), [Timurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_dynasty), and [Khanate of Bukhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Bukhara).



Mazar-i-Sharif & surroundings from [ISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISS), 2016

The poet [Jalal al-Din Rumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumi) was born somewhere in this area. His father Baha' Walad was descended from the first caliph [Abu Bakr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr).

The [Seljuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Seljuq_Empire) sultan [Ahmed Sanjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Sanjar) ordered a city and shrine to be built on the location, which was later destroyed by [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan) and his Mongol army in the 13th century, and then rebuilt. During the nineteenth century, due to the absence of drainage systems and the weak economy of the region, the excess water of this area flooded many acres of the land in the vicinity of residential areas causing a malaria epidemic in the region. The ruler of North Central Afghanistan decided to move the capital to Mazar-i-Sharif.[^cite_note-12]

The city along with the region south of the [Amu Darya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amu_Darya) became part of the [Durrani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire) in around 1751. For the most part the region was controlled by autonomous Uzbek rulers. After the Bukharan-Durrani war of 1788–1790, Qilich Ali Beg of [Khulm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kholm,_Afghanistan) formed a mini-empire stretching from Balkh to [Aybak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aybak,_Samangan), [Saighan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayghan_District), [Kahmard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahmard), [Darra-i Suf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darah_Sof_District), and [Qunduz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunduz_Province).[^cite_note-\:34-13] When he died in 1817, the [Balkh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkh) and Mazar-i Sharif region became an independent city state with Aqcha as its dependency. In November 1837 the Bukharans conquered the city but Balkh was still able to retain autonomy.[^cite_note-\:342-14][^cite_note-\:62-15] In 1849 the city was conquered and annexed into Afghanistan.

### Late 20th century

During the 1980s [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), Mazar-i-Sharif was a strategic base for the [Soviet Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Ground_Forces) as they used its airport to launch air strikes on [mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen) rebels. Mazar-i-Sharif was also the main city that linked to Soviet territory in the north, especially the roads leading to the [Uzbek Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). As a garrison for the Soviet-backed [Afghan Army](212681.md), the city was under the command of General [Abdul Rashid Dostum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Rashid_Dostum). Mujahideen militias [Hezbe Wahdat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezbe_Wahdat) and [Jamiat-e Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamiat-e_Islami) both attempted to contest the city but were repelled by the Army. Dostum mutinied against [Mohammad Najibullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Najibullah)'s government on March 19, 1992, shortly before its collapse, and formed his new party and militia, [Junbish-e Milli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junbish-e_Milli). The party took over the city the next day. Afterwards Mazar-i-Sharif became the *[de facto](43594.md)* capital of a relatively stable and secular [proto-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-state) in northern Afghanistan under the rule of Dostum. The city remained peaceful and prosperous, whilst rest of the nation disintegrated and was slowly taken over by fundamentalist [Taliban](30635.md) forces.[^cite_note-16] The city was called at the time a "glittering jewel in Afghanistan's battered crown". Money rolled in from foreign donors [Russia](25391.md), [Turkey](11125639.md), newly independent [Uzbekistan](31853.md) and others, with whom Dostum had established close relations.[^cite_note-17] He printed his own currency for the region and established his own airline. The city remained relatively liberal as [Kabul](16826.md) previously was, where activities such as coeducational schools and betting was legal as opposed to the Taliban dominated regions in the south of the country.[^cite_note-18]

This peace was shattered in May 1997 when he was betrayed by one of his generals, warlord [Abdul Malik Pahlawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Malik_Pahlawan) who allied himself with the Taliban, forcing him to flee from Mazar-i-Sharif as the Taliban were getting ready to take the city through Pahlawan. Afterwards Pahlawan himself mutinied the Taliban on the deal and it was reported that between May and July 1997 that Pahlawan executed thousands of Taliban members, that he personally did many of the killings by slaughtering the prisoners as a revenge for the 1995 death of [Abdul Ali Mazari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Ali_Mazari). "He is widely believed to have been responsible for the brutal massacre of up to 3,000 Taliban prisoners after inviting them into Mazar-i-Sharif."[^cite_note-19] Several of the Taliban escaped the slaughtering and reported what had happened. Meanwhile, Dostum came back and took the city again from Pahlawan.

However the Taliban retaliated in 1998 [attacking the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Mazar-i-Sharif_\(1997%E2%80%931998\)) and killing an estimated 8,000 [non-combatants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-combatant). At 10 am on 8 August 1998, the Taliban entered the city and for the next two days drove their pickup trucks "up and down the narrow streets of Mazar-i-Sharif shooting to the left and right and killing everything that moved—shop owners, cart pullers, women and children shoppers and even goats and donkeys."[^cite_note-rashid-73-20] More than 8000 noncombatants were reported killed in Mazar-i-Sharif and later in [Bamiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamiyan).[^cite_note-21] In addition, the Taliban were criticized for forbidding anyone from burying the corpses for the first six days (contrary to the injunctions of Islam, which demands immediate burial) while the remains rotted in the summer heat and were eaten by dogs.[^cite_note-22] The Taliban also reportedly sought out and massacred members of the [Hazara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazara_people), while in control of Mazar.[^cite_note-rashid-73-20]

### Since 2001

Further information: [Fall of Mazar-i-Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Mazar-i-Sharif), [War in Afghanistan (2001–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_\(2001%E2%80%93present\)), [International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force), and [2021 Taliban offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive)

Following the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) in 2001, Mazar-i-Sharif was the first Afghan city to fall to the U.S.-backed Afghan military alliance, [Northern Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Alliance) (United Front). The Taliban's defeat in Mazar quickly turned into a rout from the rest of the north and west of Afghanistan. After the [Battle of Mazar-i-Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mazar-i-Sharif) in November 2001, the city was officially captured by forces of the Northern Alliance. They were joined by the [United States Special Operations Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Special_Operations_Forces) and supported by [U.S. Air Force](32090.md) aircraft. After this battle, the Northern Alliance advanced towards the city of [Kunduz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunduz), which was the last remaining Taliban stronghold in northern Afghanistan. The [siege of the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Kunduz) lasted two weeks with the city being captured on November 25.[^cite_note-23] Around 8,000 Taliban fighters were captured. They were taken to Mazar-i-Sharif and then to [Sheberghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheberghan) prison in [Jowzjan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jowzjan_Province). Between 400 and 3,000 prisoners were reportedly [massacred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasht-i-Leili_massacre) by the Northern Alliance during the journey and buried in mass graves in the Dasht-e Leili desert west of Sheberghan.[^cite_note-24]



[Camp Marmal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Marmal), located south of the city next to [Mazar-i-Sharif Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazar-i-Sharif_Airport)

The city slowly came under the control of the [Karzai administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Hamid_Karzai) after 2002, which is led by [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Afghanistan) [Hamid Karzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Karzai). The [209th Corps (Shaheen)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/209th_Corps_\(Afghanistan\)) of the [Afghan National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_National_Army) is based at Mazar-i-Sharif, which provides [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Afghanistan) assistance to northern Afghanistan. The [Afghan Border Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Border_Police) headquarters for the Northern Zone is also located in the city. Despite the security put in place, there are reports of Taliban activities and [assassinations](2963.md) of tribal elders. Officials in Mazar-i-Sharif reported that between 20 and 30 Afghan tribal elders have been assassinated in [Balkh Province](457218.md) in the last several years. There is no conclusive evidence as to who is behind it but majority of the victims are said to have been associated with the [Hezb-i Islami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezb-i_Islami) political party.[^cite_note-PAN-assassinations-25]

A carpet seller in Mazar

Small-scale clashes between militias belonging to different commanders persisted throughout 2002, and were the focus of intensive UN peace-brokering and small arms disarmament programme. After some pressure, an office of the Afghan Independent Human Rights Commission opened an office in Mazar in April 2003. There were reports about northern [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_people) civilians being ethnically cleansed by the other groups, mainly by ethnic Tajiks, Hazaras and Uzbeks.[^cite_note-USAToday-26]

[NATO-led peacekeeping forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force) in and around the city provided assistance to the Afghan government. [ISAF Regional Command North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force#Structure), led by [Germany](11867.md), is stationed at [Camp Marmal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Marmal) which lies next to [Mazar-i-Sharif Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazar-i-Sharif_Airport). Since 2006, [Provincial Reconstruction Team Mazar-i-Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PRT_Mazar-i-Sharif) had unit commanders from Sweden on loan to ISAF. The unit is stationed at [Camp Northern Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Northern_Lights?action=edit\&redlink=1) which is located ten km (six mi) west of Camp Marmal. [Camp Nidaros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Nidaros), located within Camp Marmal, has soldiers from [Latvia](17514.md) and Norway and is led by an ISAF-officer from Norway.

In 2006, the discovery of new [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic) remains was announced.[^cite_note-27]

On April 1, 2011, ten foreign employees working for [United Nations Assistance Mission in Afghanistan](1234277.md) ([UNAMA](1234277.md)) [were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Mazar-i-Sharif_attack) by angry demonstrators in the city. The demonstration was organized in retaliation to [pastors](215153.md) [Terry Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Jones_\(pastor\)) and Wayne Sapp's [March 21 Qur'an-burning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Qur'an-burning_controversy) in [Florida](18933066.md), United States.[^cite_note-28] Among the dead were five [Nepalis](10886000.md), a [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegians), [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians) and [Swedish](29380512.md) nationals, two of them were said to be [decapitated](70802.md).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] Terry Jones, the American pastor who was going to burn [Islam](6037917.md)'s [Holy Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qur'an), denied his responsibility for incitement.[^cite_note-32] President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) strongly condemned both the Quran burning, calling it an act of "extreme intolerance and bigotry", and the "outrageous" attacks by protesters, referring to them as "an affront to human decency and dignity." "No religion tolerates the slaughter and beheading of innocent people, and there is no justification for such a dishonorable and deplorable act."[^cite_note-33] U.S. legislators, including Senate Majority Leader [Harry Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Reid), also condemned both the burning and the violence in reaction to it.[^cite_note-34]

By July 2011 violence grew to a record high in the [insurgency](589841.md).[^cite_note-35] In late July 2011, NATO troops also handed control of Mazar-i-Sharif to local forces amid rising security fears just days after it was hit by a deadly bombing. Mazar-i-Sharif is the sixth of seven areas to transition to Afghan control, but critics say the timing is political and there is skepticism over Afghan abilities to combat the [Taliban insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban_insurgency).

On 10 November 2016, a [suicide attacker](18835454.md) [rammed a truck bomb into the wall of the German consulate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_bombing_of_the_German_consulate_in_Mazar-i-Sharif) in Mazar-i-Sharif. Eight people were killed and more than a hundred others were injured.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-yahoo-37]

On 21 April 2017, a [coordinated Taliban attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Camp_Shaheen_attack) killed more than 100 people at [Camp Shaheen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/209th_Corps_\(Afghanistan\)), the Afghan Army base in Mazar-i-Sharif.[^cite_note-38]

In November 2018, *[Voice of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_of_America)* reported that 40 houses in Qazil Abad, an immediate [suburb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburb) of Mazar-i-Sharif, used [unexploded](406804.md) [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) [Grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grad_missile) [surface-to-surface rockets](1483499.md) as construction materials. As a result, several people were killed and wounded from explosions over the years. These rockets, left behind by the [Soviet Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Ground_Forces) in 1989 at the end of the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), were used as cheap building materials by the poor residents of the village. It was estimated that over 400 rockets were incorporated into the village as wall and ceiling beams, door-stoppers, and even footbridges used by children. When the rest of the world discovered this fact, the [Danish demining group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_demining_group) of the [Danish Refugee Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Refugee_Council) visited the village and, after asking the residents, began [demining](2206580.md) and rebuilding the village, safely removing and disposing of the rockets through [controlled detonation](2216689.md) at the [border with Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan%E2%80%93Uzbekistan_border).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

President [Ashraf Ghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashraf_Ghani) visited the city on 11 August 2021 to rally local warlords to fight the Taliban.[^cite_note-42] On 14 August, the Taliban captured Mazar-i-Sharif along with [Sharana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharana) and [Asadabad](815053.md), the provincial capitals of [Paktika](457294.md) and [Kunar](2159600.md) provinces respectively.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] Local government forces and regional leaders [Abdul Rashid Dostum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Rashid_Dostum) and [Atta Mohammad Noor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atta_Mohammad_Noor) fled to neighboring [Uzbekistan](31853.md).[^cite_note-46]

On 21 April 2022, [Islamic State – Khorasan Province](48029263.md) [killed 31 people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mazar-i-Sharif_mosque_bombing) by bombing a [Shia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam_in_Afghanistan) [mosque](19894.md). A week later, 11 people [were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28_April_2022_Mazar-i-Sharif_bombings) in a double bombing. On March 9, 2023, the Taliban-appointed Governor of Balkh, [Daud Muzamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daud_Muzamil), was killed in a bomb blast.[^cite_note-47]

Mazar-i-Sharif is also known for the Afghan song *Bia ke berem ba Mazar* (*Come let's go to Mazar*) by [Sarban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Rahim_Sarban).[^cite_note-48]

## Geography

Further information: [Geography of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Afghanistan)

Mazar-i-Sharif is located in northern Afghanistan, serving as the capital and largest city of Balkh Province. It is situated at an elevation of approximately 357 m (1,171 ft) above [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level),[^cite_note-Volume-II-3] and connected by a [road network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_Ring_Road) with [Hairatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hairatan) to the north, [Kunduz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunduz) to the east, [Kabul](16826.md) to the southeast, [Bamyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamyan) to the south, and [Herat](14128.md) to the southwest. Mazar-i-Sharif is administratively divided into 12 city districts (*[nahias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)*),[^cite_note-2_Mazar-i-Sharif_districts_get_office_complexes-7][^cite_note-49] covering a land area of 83 km<sup>2</sup> (32 sq mi) or 8,304 ha (20,520 acres).[^cite_note-Volume-I-2][^cite_note-Volume-II-3]

### Climate

Mazar-i-Sharif has a [cold steppe climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BSk*) with hot summers and cold winters. Precipitation is low and mostly falls between December and April. The climate in Mazar-i-Sharif is very hot during the summer with daily temperatures of over 40 °C (104 °F) from June to August. The winters are cold with temperatures falling below freezing; it may snow from November through March.[^cite_note-50]

## Demographics

Further information: [Demographics of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Afghanistan)



Locals of Mazar-i-Sharif enjoying rides at a small family [amusement park](137327.md) in 2012

Mazar-i-Sharif has an estimated population of 568,013 people.[^cite_note-population-4] In 2015 there were 77,615 [dwelling units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwelling_unit) in the city.[^cite_note-Volume-I-2][^cite_note-Volume-II-3]

The November 2003 issue of *[National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_\(magazine\))* magazine indicated the ethnic composition as [Pashtuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns) 15%, [Hazaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazaras) 12%, [Tajiks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajiks) 53%, [Turkmens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmens) 10%, and [Uzbeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbeks) 20%.[^cite_note-52] Occasional [ethnic violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_violence_in_Afghanistan) has been reported in the region in the last decades, mainly between Pashtuns and the other groups.[^cite_note-USAToday-26][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] In 2011 news reports mentioned assassinations taking place in the area but with no evidence as to who is behind them.[^cite_note-PAN-assassinations-25]

The dominant language in Mazar-i-Sharif is an eastern dialect of Persian known as [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari), followed by [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto), and [Uzbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_language).

Research shows that exposure to war and armed violence in Mazar-e-Sharif has led to the development of mental disorders, including [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_\(mood\)), [anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety), [post-traumatic stress disorder](82974.md) (PTSD), and [substance use disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_use_disorder). Studies conducted in Mazar-e-Sharif, which were conducted after the fall of the Taliban and under conditions of relative security, show that despite a relative decrease in direct experience of war among the younger generation, the psychological effects of war continue to be severe in the form of daily stress and psychological problems, especially in men. These stresses and mental disorders are caused by past war events and socio-cultural problems that continue to have an impact on the mental health of individuals.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

## Economy

Further information: [Economy of Afghanistan](9895.md)

Mazar-i-Sharif serves as the major trading center in northern Afghanistan. The local economy is dominated by trade, agriculture and [Karakul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karakul_sheep) [sheep farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep_farming). Small-scale oil and gas exploration have also boosted the city's prospects. It is also the location of consulates of [India](14533.md), [Iran](14653.md), [Pakistan](23235.md), [Russia](25391.md), [Tajikistan](30108.md), [Turkey](11125639.md), [Turkmenistan](198149.md) and [Uzbekistan](31853.md) for trading and political linking purposes.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

## Main sights

The modern city of Mazar-i Sharif is centred around the Shrine of Ali. Much restored, it is one of Afghanistan's most glorious monuments. Outside Mazar-i Sharif lies the ancient city of [Balkh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkh). The city is a centre for the traditional [buzkashi](169471.md) sport, and the [Blue Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazrat_Ali_Mazar) is the focus of northern Afghanistan's [Nowruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowruz) celebration. Although most Muslims believe that the real grave of [Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali) is found within [Imam Ali Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Ali_Shrine) in [Najaf](200512.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), others still come to Mazar-i-Sharif to pay respect.

## Infrastructure

### Transportation

Further information: [Transport in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Afghanistan)

#### Rail



Railway terminal

It became the first city in Afghanistan to connect itself by rail with a neighboring country. [Rail service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Afghanistan) from Mazar-i-Sharif to [Uzbekistan](31853.md) began in December 2011 and cargo on [freight trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight_rail_transport) arrive at a station near [Mazar-i-Sharif Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazar-i-Sharif_Airport),[^cite_note-61] where the goods are reloaded onto trucks or airplanes and sent to their last destinations across Afghanistan.

#### Air

As of June 2016 [Mazar-i-Sharif Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazar-i-Sharif_Airport) has direct air connections to [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_International_Airport), [Mashad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mashhad_Airport), [Tehran](57654.md), and [Istanbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istanbul-Atat%C3%BCrk).

#### Road

Highway [AH76](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH76) links Mazar-i-Sharif to [Sheberghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheberghan) in the west, and [Pul-e Khomri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pul-e_Khomri) and [Kabul](16826.md) to the south-east. Roads to the east link it to [Kunduz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunduz). Roads to the north link it to the [Uzbek](31853.md) border town [Termez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termez), where it becomes highway M39 going north to [Samarkand](51491.md) and [Tashkent](57533.md). Roads to the south link it to [Bamiyan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamiyan_Province) and the mountainous range of central Afghanistan.
