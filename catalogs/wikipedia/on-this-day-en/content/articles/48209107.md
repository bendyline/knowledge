The border between [Morocco](19291.md) and [Spain](26667.md) consists of three non-contiguous lines totalling 18.5 km (11.5 miles) around the Spanish territories of [Ceuta](6443.md) (8 km; 5 miles), [Peñón de Vélez de la Gomera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1%C3%B3n_de_V%C3%A9lez_de_la_Gomera) (85 metres; 279 feet) and [Melilla](20226.md) (10.5 km; 6½ miles). Spanish islets such as the [Chafarinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chafarinas) or the [Alhucemas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhucemas_Islands) are located off the Moroccan coast.

## History



Spanish-Moroccan border in 2004

The sites of present-day [Ceuta](6443.md) and [Melilla](20226.md) became increasingly integrated into the Roman sphere during the first century BC. Following the Roman annexation of the client [Kingdom of Mauretania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mauretania), whose last king, [Ptolemy of Mauretania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_of_Mauretania)—the son of the Numidian king [Juba II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juba_II) and [Cleopatra Selene II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleopatra_Selene_II), daughter of [Cleopatra VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleopatra_VII) and [Mark Antony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Antony)—was executed in AD 40, both became part of the Roman province of [Mauretania Tingitana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauretania_Tingitana), remaining within the Roman world for several centuries.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] Following the administrative reforms of [Diocletian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian) in the late third century, the province was incorporated into the [Diocese of Hispania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Hispania), with the capital in [Emerita Augusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusta_Emerita) (present day [Mérida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_Spain)).[^cite_note-3]

In [late antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_antiquity), Ceuta became a stronghold of the Germanic [Vandal Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandal_Kingdom) and was subsequently conquered by the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire).[^cite_note-4] Ceuta remained under Byzantine control until 711.

Following the [Arab conquest of the Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Maghreb), the territories that later became the [plazas de soberanía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plazas_de_soberan%C3%ADa) were ruled by various Muslim polities of north-west Africa,[^cite_note-O'Reilly-5] including the [Idrisid state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idrisid_dynasty), centred first at [Walili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volubilis) and later [Fez](39760328.md), the [Almoravid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravid_dynasty), the [Almohad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad_Caliphate), both based at [Marrakesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marrakesh), and the [Marinid Sultanate of Fez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinid_dynasty), with Ceuta and Melilla at times under different rulers, as political authority in the region fragmented between competing states and regional polities.

[Ceuta](6443.md) was conquered by [Portugal](23033.md) in 1415.[^cite_note-Payne-6] Following the [Reconquista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconquista) of the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula), Spain looked south to the North African coast, capturing [Melilla](20226.md) from the [Sultanate of Fez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Fez) in 1497, with Portugal's blessing.[^cite_note-O'Reilly-5] Spain took the then-island of [Peñón de Vélez de la Gomera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1%C3%B3n_de_V%C3%A9lez_de_la_Gomera) by force in 1508, with Portugal recognising this claim the following year with the Treaty of Sintra.[^cite_note-O'Reilly-5] Retaken by the Kingdom of Fez in 1554, Spain reconquered it in 1564, and it has remained under Spanish control since then.

During the period of [Iberian Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Union) (1580-1640) Ceuta attracted many Spanish settlers; as a result, when Portugal regained its independence from Spain in 1640 Ceuta opted to remain with Spain, a situation Portugal acquiesced to in 1668 with the [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon_\(1668\)).[^cite_note-O'Reilly-5][^cite_note-CIA_–_Spanish_boundaries-7] A treaty outlining Ceuta's boundary with the [Alawi Sultanate of Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alawi_dynasty) was signed 7 October 1844 and confirmed by another on 6 May 1845.[^cite_note-CIA_–_Spanish_boundaries-7][^cite_note-O'Reilly-5] A treaty outlining the Melilla-Morocco boundary, replete with a parallel 'neutral zone', was signed 24 August 1859.[^cite_note-CIA_–_Spanish_boundaries-7][^cite_note-Rézette-8]

Repeated Moroccan attempts to gain control of the two exclaves by force during the 18th-19th centuries failed, culminating in the [Hispano–Moroccan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispano%E2%80%93Moroccan_War_\(1859%E2%80%931860\)) of 1859–60, which resulted in a Spanish victory. The subsequent [Treaty of Wad-Ras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Wad_Ras) (a.k.a. the Treaty of Tétouan) of 26 April 1860 expanded the border of Ceuta to its present limit.[^cite_note-CIA_–_Spanish_boundaries-7] Melilla's border was supposed to be fixed by 'the range of a piece of cannon', however this proved impractical, and a further treaty was therefore signed on 30 October 1861 and confirmed on 26 June 1862 which outlined the modern boundary line.[^cite_note-Rézette-8][^cite_note-CIA_–_Spanish_boundaries-7][^cite_note-O'Reilly-5][^cite_note-9] Localised disputes in Melilla continued however, and further treaties were signed in 1894–95.[^cite_note-O'Reilly-5]

By a [Franco-Spanish treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_Between_France_and_Spain_Regarding_Morocco) of 27 November 1912, Spain was granted a protectorate over Morocco's Mediterranean littoral, referred to as [Spanish Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Morocco).[^cite_note-AJIL1-10] Ceuta, [Peñón de Vélez de la Gomera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1%C3%B3n_de_V%C3%A9lez_de_la_Gomera) and Melilla thereafter were absorbed into the administration of this entity. When Morocco gained independence from [France](5843419.md) in 1956, Spain relinquished its protectorate in northern Morocco under the Hispano-Moroccan Joint Declaration of 7 April 1956.[^cite_note-O'Reilly-5] Spain retained the [plazas de soberanía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plazas_de_soberan%C3%ADa), which had not formed part of the protectorate established in 1912. Upon independence, Morocco laid claim to the Spanish-held territories, while Spain maintained that they were integral parts of Spanish territory.[^cite_note-O'Reilly-5][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

In 1934, a huge storm created a [tombolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tombolo) between [Peñón de Vélez de la Gomera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1%C3%B3n_de_V%C3%A9lez_de_la_Gomera) and the Moroccan mainland, turning the island into a peninsula with an 85-metre (279-foot) international border.[^cite_note-ABC-13]

Since Moroccan independence the dispute over the *_plazas de soberanía_* has flared up from time-to-time, most notably in 1975 when it was feared Morocco would attempt an invasion of the territories similar to the [Green March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_March) invasion of [Western Sahara](33220.md) conducted that same year.[^cite_note-O'Reilly-5] At present Spain remains in control of the *_plazas_* and refuses to discuss the issue of their sovereignty with [Morocco](19291.md).[^cite_note-O'Reilly-5]

In 1993, Spain began building the Ceuta and [Melilla border fences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melilla_border_fence), which were further strengthened in 1995.[^cite_note-Saddiki-14] In response to an increase in the number of migrants' attempts to breach the fence, both were significantly fortified in 2005, creating a doubled-fence system replete with barbed wire and surveillance equipment.[^cite_note-Jaume_Castan_Pinos-15] Since then there have been numerous attempts to cross the fences, resulting in several fatalities.[^cite_note-16]

On 17 May 2021, more than 6,000 migrants [crossed the border into Ceuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Morocco%E2%80%93Spain_border_incident). It was widely speculated that Morocco permitted the sudden influx to punish Spain for allowing [Polisario Front](201574.md) leader [Brahim Ghali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahim_Ghali) to be treated at a Spanish hospital.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-\:0-18] The next day, Morocco closed the border again.[^cite_note-19] Most of the arrivals returned to Morocco.[^cite_note-\:0-18][^cite_note-20]

On [24 June 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Melilla_incident), around 2,000 migrants and asylum seekers gathered in the early hours of the day to cross the Melilla border fence:[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] 23 persons were killed after a [crowd crush](69280791.md) during a conflict with Moroccan and Spanish security forces.[^cite_note-23]

In July 2026, the Spanish Interior Ministry announced that roughly [80,000 migrants had crossed the border](83824713.md)[^cite_note-24] and entered Ceuta, with 141 migrants dying[^cite_note-25]. Many of the migrants subsequently returned to Morocco.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

## Maritime borders

[Morocco](19291.md) and [Spain](26667.md) also share a [maritime territorial border](496983.md) along the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar). At its narrowest point, the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar) is 14.3 [kilometres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilometre) (8.9 mi; 7.7 nmi) wide.[^cite_note-28]

The territorial seas are generally limited by the median-line rule under international law, unless specifically agreed.[^cite_note-29]

The territorial seas between the [Canary Islands](5717.md) and the Moroccan mainland do not meet, the shortest distance being approximately 96 kilometres (60 mi; 52 nmi), between [Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura) and the coast near [Cape Juby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Juby). This exceeds the combined 24 nautical miles (44 km; 28 mi) maximum breadth of two territorial seas.[^cite_note-30] Their potential [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) entitlements nevertheless overlap, although no bilateral EEZ boundary has been agreed.[^cite_note-31]

Spain and Morocco have likewise not concluded a comprehensive maritime-delimitation agreement in the [Alboran Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alboran_Sea).
