The **Liberal Party** ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Partido Liberal*, **PL**) is a [far-right](221220.md)[^cite_note-farright-9] political party in [Brazil](3383.md). From its foundation in 2006 until 2019, it was called the **Party of the Republic** (Portuguese: *Partido da República*, **PR**).

The party was founded in 2006 as a merger of the 1985 [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_\(Brazil,_1985\)) and the [Party of the Reconstruction of the National Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_the_Reconstruction_of_the_National_Order) (PRONA),[^cite_note-\:0-11] as a [big tent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_tent), [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right) party,[^cite_note-\:4-12][^cite_note-Reuters-13][^cite_note-14] and was considered part of the *[Centrão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centr%C3%A3o)*, a bloc of parties without consistent ideological orientation that support different sides of the political spectrum in order to gain political privileges.[^cite_note-\:4-12][^cite_note-\:2-15][^cite_note-\:3-16] As such, it supported the government of [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](99328.md) and [Dilma Rousseff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilma_Rousseff)— members of the center-left [Workers' Party](224622.md) (PT)—and [Michel Temer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Temer).[^cite_note-17]

In 2021, it became the base of the then-[president of Brazil](273638.md) [Jair Bolsonaro](46603496.md) for the [2022 Brazilian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Brazilian_general_election). This led to many of his supporters joining the party, which thereby became the largest bloc in the [National Congress of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_of_Brazil),[^cite_note-18] and the Liberal Party took a general shift to the [far-right](221220.md).[^cite_note-anti-lgbt-19]

## History

The Party of the Republic was founded on 26 October 2006, by the merger of the old [Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_\(Brazil,_1985\)) — which initially started as a [classical liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_liberal) party,[^cite_note-\:0-11] but slowly shifted towards [social conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatism) after it became influenced by [evangelicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism)[^cite_note-\:0-11] — and the [Party of the Reconstruction of the National Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_the_Reconstruction_of_the_National_Order) (*Partido da Reedificação da Ordem Nacional*, PRONA) — a [far-right](221220.md) [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist) party.[^cite_note-CPDOC-20][^cite_note-21] The merger was performed in order to surpass the [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) of 5%,[^cite_note-\:0-11][^cite_note-23] but also as a rebranding as the Liberal Party was heavily implicated in the [Mensalão scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mensal%C3%A3o_scandal).[^cite_note-\:0-11][^cite_note-\:1-24]

Historically, the party was a pragmatic party of business interests, supporting the candidacies of [Lula](99328.md) and [Dilma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilma_Rousseff) from the [Workers' Party](224622.md) (PT) for the sake of moderating their presidencies. It generally supported a form of [Lulism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lulism), which had less economic regulation. As such, the Party of the Republic was considered part of the *[Centrão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centr%C3%A3o)*.[^cite_note-\:2-15][^cite_note-\:3-16] PR's predecessor, the Liberal Party, was heavily involved in the Mensalão — a [vote-buying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_buying) scheme done by the [Workers' Party](224622.md) in order to gain support in the [National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_of_Brazil),[^cite_note-\:1-24] and Lula's [Vice President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_Brazil) [José Alencar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Alencar) was a member of the old PL.

During the 2010 elections, the Party of the Republic focused on the parliamentary elections; it won 41 of the 513 seats in the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_of_Brazil) and 4 of the 81 [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Brazil) seats. One of PR's elected politicians was professional humorist and professional clown [Tiririca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiririca), who became the [State of São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo)'s most voted representative with more than one million votes, and due to Brazil's [proportional voting system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_vote), Tiririca thus supported PR in electing a sizeable amount of representatives.[^cite_note-25]

Sergio Victor Tamer, founder of the Party of the Republic, was the party's president from 2006 to 2014. [Alfredo Nascimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Nascimento_\(politician\)) succeeded Tamer as president of the PR until April 2016, when he resigned due to party leadership not supporting the [impeachment of Dilma Rousseff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_process_against_Dilma_Rousseff). However, 26 of the PR's MPs did vote for her impeachment.[^cite_note-26]

After that move by its MPs, the party took a more rightward turn away from its bipartisan past and supported [Geraldo Alckmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraldo_Alckmin)'s failed campaign in the [2018 Brazilian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Brazilian_general_election).

On 7 May 2019, the Superior Electoral Court (TSE) voted to approve a motion of the party to change its name back to **Liberal Party** (PL).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] According to party leadership, the change was done in order to return to the party's roots as body defending [economic liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism), the [free market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market) and low [intervention of the state in the economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intervention_of_the_state). The social positions of the party remained socially conservative, however.[^cite_note-\:5-29] Other specialists point it out as part of a national tendency of parties in Brazil rebranding in order to get better perception from the electorate due a process of loss of trust caused by the Brazilian political crisis,[^cite_note-30] and also riding a wave of pro-liberalism sentiment in Brazil.[^cite_note-\:5-29]

The Liberal Party provokes controversy in 2020 by nominating an openly [neo-Nazi](54361.md) activist as a municipal candidate in the town of [Pomerode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomerode).[^cite_note-31] Following the controversy, the candidate was expelled from the party, which claimed to be unaware of the candidate's ideology.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] The candidacy was subsequently withdrawn.[^cite_note-35]

On 30 November 2021, [President of Brazil](273638.md) [Jair Bolsonaro](46603496.md) and his son Senator [Flávio Bolsonaro](58640941.md) — who were previously affiliated with the [Social Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Liberal_Party_\(Brazil\)) (PSL) and left it, attempting to create the [Alliance for Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_Brazil) party with no avail[^cite_note-36] — joined the PL in preparation for the [2022 Brazilian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Brazilian_general_election) (as presidential candidates must be affiliated with a political party). He had previously considered returning to the [Progressists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressistas) (PP),[^cite_note-economist.com-37] the [Social Christian Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Christian_Party_\(Brazil\)) (PSC), [Brazilian Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Labour_Party_\(current\)) (PTB), as well negotiation with number of other smaller and/or right-wing to far-right parties.[^cite_note-38] Bolsonaro's affiliation to the PL has been pointed out by analysts as a consolidation of an alliance with the *[Centrão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centr%C3%A3o)*.[^cite_note-39]

In the 2022 general election, the party had formed a presidential ticket and many gubernatorial tickets with a hard right coalition of the [Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republicans_\(Brazil\)) and the [Progressists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressistas) (PP). The election was a great success to the party, resulting in PL becoming the largest bloc in the [National Congress of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_of_Brazil) with 99 seats and the [Federal Senate](6132308.md) with 13 seats.[^cite_note-40] According to some analysts, the party had been divided between two wide factions: one with traditional *Centrão* politicians loyal to party president [Valdemar Costa Neto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdemar_Costa_Neto), and a Bolsonarist one, composing about two-thirds of the PL's elected bench, with Bolsonaro’s followers from the PSL.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-\:6-42] In an interview, Neto revealed he feared that in case [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](99328.md) is elected president, there would be a split in the party as the traditional faction might want to align themselves with a possible PT government, while the Bolsonarist branch would form an opposition.[^cite_note-\:6-42]

The Liberal Party is a [big tent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_tent) conservative party. The Liberal Party used to be described as [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right)[^cite_note-\:4-12][^cite_note-44] but since Bolsonaro's incorporation the party has moved to the [far-right](221220.md) of the political spectrum.[^cite_note-anti-lgbt-19]

Though previously a party of [national liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_liberalism), before its merger with [PRONA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_the_Reconstruction_of_the_National_Order), the party has increasingly been affiliated with the far-right in Brazil. This has come as a result of the party's joining around the political philosophy of Jair Bolsonaro, who was initially affiliated with the [PSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Liberal_Party_\(Brazil\)) and other socially conservative parties. With the questioning of democracy, foreign policy, and the anti-democratic statements of Bolsonaro, the party seems to have re-embraced some of the tendencies of the head of PRONA [Eneas Carneiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/En%C3%A9as_Carneiro), a noted supporter of [LaRoucheism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaRouche_movement), the [previous military dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_dictatorship_in_Brazil), and a right-wing opposition to [neoliberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism).[^cite_note-economist.com-37]

Generally the party is right-wing populist, economically liberal, but socially anti-liberal and pro-[Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_Brazil), aligning with the ideology of Bolsonaro. The party is pro-[agrobusiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrobusiness), pro-military, and [pro-life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-abortion_movements). The party promotes a generally more economically open form of [Brazilian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_nationalism) than Carneiro.[^cite_note-45] In a letter to members in 2021, PL president Valdemar Costa Neto defined the values of [freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom), [Christian faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_faith), [family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family), and [economic liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism) as ideological commitments. He also maintained that the party will act in the interests of agribusiness and the advancement of anti-drug public policies.[^cite_note-46] The party also has defended the [right to bear arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_bear_arms) and advocates reducing the [age of criminal responsibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_criminal_responsibility) from 18 to 16 years.[^cite_note-47]

The party has frequently supported Bolsonaro's attacks on the media and the electoral system in Brazil.[^cite_note-af-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]
