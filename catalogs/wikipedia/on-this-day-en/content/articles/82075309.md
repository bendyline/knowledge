On 7 January 2026, the [Yemeni government](70488867.md) (Presidential Leadership Council, PLC) [forces](34252.md) captured the [Southern Transitional Council](54251566.md)'s *de facto* capital, [Aden](38050.md), resulting in the STC's reported dissolution and the collapse of the group's forces in the area.[^cite_note-\:0-1] The city's capture was the culmination of a government [counteroffensive](81757459.md) against the STC that had previously captured [Hadhramaut](3604476.md) and [al-Mahrah Governorates](2488921.md) amid Saudi airstrikes against STC positions.[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-2] Following Aden's capture, the secretary-general of the STC announced its dissolution and its leader [Aidarus al-Zoubaidi](52145652.md) fled to the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md).[^cite_note-\:4-3]

## Background

Aden has changed hands several times over the course of the current [civil war](46215853.md). In 2015, it was designated as the "temporary capital" of Yemen following the [Houthi](17240332.md) [takeover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sanaa_\(2014\)) of [Sanaa](18691665.md).[^cite_note-4] A few weeks later, the [Battle of Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Aden_\(2015\)) broke out between the Houthis and PLC.[^cite_note-\:1-5] Despite [Abdrabbuh Mansour Hadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdrabbuh_Mansour_Hadi) fleeing Aden, the government forces successfully recaptured the city and drove the Houthis out of the surrounding area.[^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-\:2-6] In 2018, the STC [gained control of Aden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Aden_\(2018\)) and has held the city since.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-\:2-6]

### 2025–2026 offensive

A [2025 offensive](81757459.md) by the STC gained large swathes of territory in southern and eastern Yemen. In early 2026, the Saudi-backed PLC launched a counteroffensive that took back much of the land in eastern Yemen with the assistance of Saudi airstrikes.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] On 7 January, the Saudi-backed forces began advancing towards Aden, launching airstrikes against STC forces in the region.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

## Battle

On 7 January 2026, Saudi Arabia launched airstrikes against the UAE-backed STC forces around Aden. The government army first entered the area at [Al-Alam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-%CA%BDAlam) and moved into the city from there.[^cite_note-\:5-11] PLC forces captured the city's [international airport](9421129.md) and the [presidential palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Maashiq_Palace). The PLC claimed total control over the city and all districts of the governorate, but the STC denied these claims, saying that they still held control over some areas.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

## Aftermath

After Aden's fall to the PLC, Zoubaidi went missing for a brief period of time.[^cite_note-\:0-1] He had been expected to fly to [Riyadh](51554.md) for peace talks but did not arrive to board his plane.[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-\:3-12] The Yemeni government charged Zoubaidi with [high treason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_treason) on 7 January.[^cite_note-\:4-3][^cite_note-\:7-13] On 8 January, Saudi Arabia claimed that Zoubaidi had fled Aden by boat during the night to [Somaliland](51425.md), where he was then flown to the UAE via [Mogadishu](60154.md).[^cite_note-\:3-12][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The STC maintains that Zoubaidi remains in Aden.[^cite_note-\:5-11]

A curfew was imposed on the city on 7 January after the battle, and was lifted on 9 January.[^cite_note-\:6-16][^cite_note-17] The [flag of Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Yemen) was raised over government buildings in the city by PLC troops.[^cite_note-\:7-13]

On 9 January, the STC was reportedly dissolved by its secretary-general. However, others in the group's leadership have refuted this, claiming that the STC is still active.[^cite_note-\:6-16]
