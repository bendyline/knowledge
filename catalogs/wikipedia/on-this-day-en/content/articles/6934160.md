The **Department for Infrastructure** (**DfI**, [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language): *An Roinn Bonneagair*;[^cite_note-5] [Ulster Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulster_Scots_language): *Depairtment fur Infrastructure*) is a [devolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolved) [Northern Ireland](21265.md) government department in the [Northern Ireland Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Executive).

Up until May 2016, the department was called the **Department for Regional Development**.

## Aim

DfI's overall aim is to "improve quality of life by securing transport and water infrastructure and shaping the region's long-term strategic development".[^cite_note-6]

## Responsibilities

The department's main responsibilities include [^cite_note-7]

- regional strategic planning and development;

- transport strategy and [sustainable transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_transport);

- public roads;

- public transport;

- air and sea ports;

- water and sewerage services;

- Driver licensing and Vehicle Testing.

Two transport matters are [reserved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserved_and_excepted_matters#List_of_key_reserved_matters) to Westminster and are therefore not devolved: [^cite_note-8]

- [navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation) (including [merchant shipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant_shipping))

- [civil aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_aviation)

DfI's main counterparts in the [United Kingdom Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Government) are:

- the [Department for Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Transport);

In the [Irish Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Government), its main counterparts are:

- the [Department of Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Transport_\(Ireland\));

## Agencies

- [DfI Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DfI_Roads): Responsible for ensuring that measures are taken to implement the roads aspects of the Regional Transportation Strategy for Northern Ireland 2002–12 and that the public road network is managed, maintained and developed. DFI Roads is responsible for just over 25,000 kilometres of public roads, approximately 9,000 kilometres of footways, 5,800 bridges, 257,700 streetlights and 370 public car parks.

- [Driver and Vehicle Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driver_and_Vehicle_Agency): Responsible for vehicle MOT testing, driver testing and driver licensing, including [licensing for taxi drivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxis_of_Northern_Ireland?action=edit\&redlink=1).

- [DfI Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DfI_Rivers)

## History

The [Ministry of Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Home_Affairs) was established on the formation of Northern Ireland in June 1921 and was responsible for a range of non-economic domestic matters, including local government. A separate [Ministry of Health and Local Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Health_and_Local_Government) was formed in 1944 and was subsequently split in 1965, to create the [Ministry of Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Development_\(Northern_Ireland\)). An environment ministry existed in the 1974 [Northern Ireland Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Executive_\(1974\)) and the ministry was known as the **Department of the Environment** under direct rule.

**The DoE** is still a phrase used in everyday language in Northern Ireland to describe DFI Roads, which was once run by the department but is currently an agency of the Department for Infrastructure.

Following a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Northern_Irish_Belfast_Agreement_referendum) on the [Belfast Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfast_Agreement) on 23 May 1998 and the granting of [royal assent](153067.md) to the [Northern Ireland Act 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Act_1998) on 19 November 1998, a [Northern Ireland Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Assembly) and [Northern Ireland Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Executive) were established by the [United Kingdom Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Government) under Prime Minister [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair). The process was known as [devolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution) and was set up to return devolved legislative powers to Northern Ireland. DRD was one of five new devolved Northern Ireland departments created in December 1999 by the [Northern Ireland Act 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Act_1998) and [The Departments (Northern Ireland) Order 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Departments_\(Northern_Ireland\)_Order_1999).

A devolved minister first took office on 2 December 1999. Devolution was suspended for six periods, during which the department came under the responsibility of [direct rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_rule_over_Northern_Ireland) ministers from the [Northern Ireland Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Office):
