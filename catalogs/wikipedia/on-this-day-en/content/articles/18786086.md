Denmark and France have shared [current and historical relations](429154.md). Denmark has an embassy in Paris and France has an embassy in [Copenhagen](5166.md). Both countries are full members of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe), the [European Union](9317.md) and [NATO](21133.md).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

## History

The relations between Denmark and France date back to the Dark Ages, when Danish Vikings pillaged the Northern part of the country, including Paris which Ragnar Lodbrok and his army sacked in the 845. Paris was again attacked in 885 and 886 by Vikings. They settled Northern France, becoming the Normans, who conquered England in 1066. When Scandinavia Christianized, French monks lived in Denmark, and Danish students in Paris. In the 17th century, there were many Danish students in medicine, law, philosophy and theology in France, while in Denmark there were many French tutors.[^cite_note-3]

[Ingeborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingeborg_of_Denmark,_Queen_of_France), daughter of [Valdemar I of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdemar_I_of_Denmark), was [queen consort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_consort) of France (1193 & 1200–1223).

In 1570, France, along with [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) and the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), helped conclude the peace between Denmark and Sweden at [Szczecin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szczecin) ending the [Northern Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Seven_Years'_War) in 1570.[^cite_note-4]

### Scanian War

Embassy of France in Copenhagen

Main article: [Scanian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scanian_War)

The Scanian War (1675–1679) was a part of the [Northern Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Wars) involving the union of [Denmark–Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark%E2%80%93Norway), [Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Brandenburg) and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire). It was fought mainly on [Scanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scania) soil, in the former Danish provinces along the border with Sweden and in Northern Germany. While the latter battles are regarded a theater of the Scanian war in English, Danish and Swedish historiography, they are seen as a separate war in German historiography, called the **Swedish-Brandenburgian War**.

The war was prompted by the Swedish involvement in the [Franco-Dutch War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Dutch_War). Sweden had allied with France against several European countries. The [United Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic), under attack by France, sought support from [Denmark–Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark%E2%80%93Norway). After some hesitation, King [Christian V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_V_of_Denmark) started the invasion of the [Scania (Skåneland)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sk%C3%A5neland) in 1675, while the Swedish were occupied with a war against [Brandenburg](3765.md). The invasion of Scania was combined with a simultaneous Norwegian front called the **Gyldenløve War**, forcing the defending Swedes to fight a two-front war in addition to their entanglements in the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire).

The Danish objective was to retrieve the [Scanian lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sk%C3%A5neland) that had been ceded to Sweden in the [Treaty of Roskilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Roskilde), after the [Northern Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Wars). Although the Danish offensive was initially a great success, Swedish counter-offensives led by the 19-year-old [Charles XI of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_XI_of_Sweden) nullified much of the gain.

It was a war with no definite victor; the Swedish navy lost at sea, the Danish army was defeated in Scania by the Swedes, who in turn were defeated in Northern Germany by the Brandenburgers. The war and the hostilities ended when Denmark's ally the [United Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) settled with Sweden's stronger ally [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Modern_France) and the Swedish king [Charles XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_XI) married Danish princess [Ulrike Eleonora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrike_Eleonora_of_Denmark), sister of [Christian V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_V). Peace was made on behalf of France with the treaties of [Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Fontainebleau_\(1679\)) and [Lund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lund) and [Saint Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint-Germain-en-Laye_\(1679\)), restoring most of the lost territories to Sweden.[^cite_note-5]

### War of the Sixth Coalition

Main article: [War of the Sixth Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Sixth_Coalition)

In the War of the Sixth Coalition (1812–1814), a coalition of [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire), [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_under_the_Restoration), Sweden, the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland), and a number of [German States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederation_of_the_Rhine) finally defeated [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) and drove [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) Bonaparte into exile on [Elba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elba). After Napoleon's disastrous [invasion of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon's_invasion_of_Russia), the continental powers joined Russia, Britain, [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal) and the rebels in [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_under_the_Restoration). With their armies reorganized along more Napoleonic lines, they drove Napoleon out of Germany in 1813 and invaded France in 1814, forcing Napoleon to abdicate and [restoring the Bourbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon_Restoration_in_France).

### 21st century

The last French state visit to Denmark took place in August 2018 (the previous one dated back to 1982). President [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) met with his counterpart [Lars Løkke Rasmussen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_L%C3%B8kke_Rasmussen) as well as the royal family and numerous figures from the Danish political scene. The main issues discussed were security, Europe, and climate change. Meetings also focused on gender equality and empathy. The French delegation included business leaders such as [TotalEnergies](804161.md)'s CEO [Patrick Pouyanné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Pouyann%C3%A9), [Ponant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compagnie_du_Ponant) cruise line president Jean-Emmanuel Sauvée, and Danish-French speaker and coach [Malene Rydahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malene_Rydahl).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

The most recent Danish state visit to France was in April 2025, when King [Frederik X](339155.md) of Denmark and [Queen Mary of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_of_Denmark) travelled to France. This visit led to the conclusion of a new bilateral strategic partnership covering, among other areas, health, defense, ecological and energy transition, culture, and heritage.[^cite_note-8]

## Economic relations

French exports to Denmark were 2.19 billion [euros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euros) in 2009. Imports from Denmark were 2.35 billion euros in 2009.[^cite_note-relations-9]

## Resident diplomatic missions

- Denmark has an embassy in Paris.

- France has an embassy in [Copenhagen](5166.md).
