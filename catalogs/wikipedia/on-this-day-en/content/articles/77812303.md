The **House of Lords (Hereditary Peers) Act 2026** (c. 12), sometimes referred to during passage as the **Hereditary Peers Bill**,[^cite_note-3] is an [act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Parliament_\(United_Kingdom\)) of the [Parliament of the United Kingdom](13964.md) which removed all remaining [hereditary peers](604698.md) from the [House of Lords](13658.md) on 29 April 2026.&#x20;

Hereditary peers who also held a [life peerage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_peer) had their membership in the House of Lords continue uninterrupted, and those excluded may rejoin the Lords should they be subsequently granted a life peerage. The [earl marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_marshal) and [lord great chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_great_chamberlain), offices held by hereditary peers, lost their *ex officio* seats but continue to carry out their ceremonial duties.

For much of its history, most hereditary peers were entitled to sit in the House of Lords. This was abolished in the [House of Lords Act 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords_Act_1999), retaining 92 excepted peers until further legislation could be enacted. [House of Lords reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords_reform) was proposed at the [2024 United Kingdom general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election) in the [Labour Party manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change_\(manifesto\)), which included an age cap for life peers and the removal of hereditary peers entirely.[^cite_note-4] Following 18 months of consideration before Parliament, the Commons and Lords agreed on the final text of the bill on 10 March 2026,[^cite_note-BBC-5] with royal assent being granted to the bill on 18 March 2026.[^cite_note-ra-6] It fully came into force on 29 April 2026.[^cite_note-2026_reform_bbc-7] As part of negotiations to obtain passage of the bill, an unspecified number of new life peerages were to be created to allow some hereditary peers to continue to be members of the House of Lords.[^cite_note-BBC-5] In June 2026, approximately one-third of those peers excluded by the act were created life peers and returned to Parliament after a short hiatus.

## Background

Further information: [History of reform of the House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_reform_of_the_House_of_Lords)

Reform of the House of Lords has been a part of successive [Government](25318118.md) policy since the early 19th century.[^cite_note-lr-8] The last major change was made in the [House of Lords Act 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords_Act_1999) under the [first Blair ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Blair_ministry), which provided that:[^cite_note-lr-8]

> No-one shall be a member of the House of Lords by virtue of a hereditary peerage.

— House of Lords Act 1999, section 1, Exclusion of hereditary peers.

The act then provided several exceptions, allowing 90 [hereditary peers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By-elections_to_the_House_of_Lords), as well as the [Lord Great Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Great_Chamberlain) and the [Earl Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earl_Marshal), to remain in the House of Lords pending further reform. The act originally intended to exclude hereditary peers in their entirety, but the exceptions made (section 2 of the act) were reached as part of a compromise agreed between the [Houses of Lords](13658.md) and the [Commons](13828.md) during the bill's passage through Parliament.[^cite_note-9]

## Provisions

The act excluded all the remaining 92 hereditary peers from [Parliament](13964.md), and they are no longer entitled to a [seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_seat) in the [chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_chamber) unless they are created [life peers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_peer).[^cite_note-10] The 26 [Lords Spiritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lords_Spiritual) and a variable number of [life peers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_peer) will remain sitting in the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house). Although no longer members of the House of Lords, the [Earl Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Fitzalan-Howard,_18th_Duke_of_Norfolk) and [Lord Great Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Carington,_7th_Baron_Carrington) will continue to carry out their [ceremonial duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Officers_of_State_\(United_Kingdom\)).[^cite_note-11]

The act also amends the [House of Lords Reform Act 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords_Reform_Act_2014) to allow peers to resign in situations where they have lost mental capacity.[^cite_note-12]

The sections of the act as adopted are listed below:[^cite_note-13]

- Section 1: Exclusion of remaining hereditary peers. This section repeals section 2 of the House of Lords Act 1999, removing the exceptions for hereditary peers remaining in the House of Lords;

- Section 2: Retirement. This section allows a resignation notice to be signed and given on behalf of a peer who lacks capacity;

- Section 3: Claims to hereditary peerages. This section removes the House of Lords' jurisdiction over claims to hereditary peerages;

- Section 4: Consequential amendments;

- Section 5: Extent and commencement;

- Section 6: Short title.

Sections 2, 5 and 6 came into force upon royal assent on 18 March 2026, while sections 1, 3 and 4 came into force when the first session of the 59th Parliament was [prorogued](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prorogation) on 29 April 2026.

## Reactions

### Opposition

The bill received criticism including from former [Leader of the House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_House_of_Lords) and hereditary peer [Lord Strathclyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Galbraith,_2nd_Baron_Strathclyde), who suggested that so as to reduce the size of the House of Lords, peers who infrequently attend debates ought to be removed instead of the remaining hereditary peers, who have been very active.[^cite_note-14]

Numerous [members of the House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Members_of_the_House_of_Lords) have said that other elements of Lords reform should be prioritised, such as the abolition of the [Lords Spiritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lords_Spiritual),[^cite_note-15] pointing out that the only other sovereign state where [clerics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleric) are automatically granted a legislative seat is [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-16] [Baroness Harman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Harman) subsequently introduced an amendment that would mandate the government to introduce proposals to remove the right of the 26 Church of England bishops to sit *ex officio* as Lords Spiritual, in line with Labour's election manifesto commitment to consult on wider reforms of the upper house;[^cite_note-17] she withdrew her amendment before debate.[^cite_note-18] [Lord Birt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Birt,_Baron_Birt) also introduced an amendment to require proposals to remove the Lords Spiritual, but he withdrew his amendment before debate.[^cite_note-19] [Viscount Hailsham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Hogg) introduced an amendment to remove the Lords Spiritual by phasing them out through retirement;[^cite_note-20] he did not move his amendment at the [committee stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_bill_committee) [debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debate_\(parliamentary_procedure\)).[^cite_note-21]

### Support

[Minister of State for the Constitution and European Union Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_the_Constitution_and_European_Union_Relations), [Labour](19279158.md) MP [Nick Thomas-Symonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Thomas-Symonds), said that "The second chamber plays a vital role in our constitution and people should not be voting on our laws in parliament by an accident of birth".

[University College London](52029.md) [Constitution Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Unit) asserted that the only other country with a hereditary element in its legislature were the hereditary chiefs in [Lesotho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesotho)'s [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Lesotho),[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] though other countries have hereditary elements as well, such as the 18 chiefs in [Zimbabwe](34399.md)'s [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Zimbabwe), [Tonga](30158.md)'s [9 internally elected nobles](30928502.md) in the [Legislative Assembly](4493518.md), and [Samoa](27238.md)'s requirement to hold [*matai*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fa%CA%BBamatai#Modern_politics) status to stand for election to the [Legislative Assembly of Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_Samoa).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

## Passage

The [bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_\(law\)) was formally introduced to Parliament by the [chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_the_Duchy_of_Lancaster), [Pat McFadden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_McFadden), receiving its [first reading](4062253.md) on Thursday, 5 September 2024,[^cite_note-pb-28] with its second reading on 15 October.&#x20;

The bill proceeded to Committee Stage, where, due to its [constitutional significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_law), it became subject to a [Committee of the Whole House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_the_Whole_House_\(United_Kingdom\)). The committee, and then the bill's third reading, took place on 12 November 2024 with the bill passing the House of Commons by a vote of 435–73.[^cite_note-pb-28][^cite_note-29]

## Impacts

At the time Section 1 of the act came into force on 29 April 2026, there were 80 excepted hereditary peers in the House of Lords: of the 92 total seats provided for in legislation, 11 had become vacant by resignation, death or disqualification since May 2024 and [not refilled due to a suspension of by-elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/By-elections_to_the_House_of_Lords#End_of_by-elections), while the *ex officio* seat of the Lord Great Chamberlain was held by [Lord Carrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Carington,_7th_Baron_Carrington) who held a "regular" excepted hereditary peer seat and thereby reduced the count by one. Three of the 80 sitting excepted hereditary peers (the [Earl of Kinnoull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Hay,_16th_Earl_of_Kinnoull), [Lord Addington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Hubbard,_6th_Baron_Addington) and [Earl Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Russell,_7th_Earl_Russell)) [held concurrent life peerages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hereditary_peers_in_the_House_of_Lords_by_virtue_of_a_life_peerage) and continued in the House uninterrupted, while the remaining 77 peers were excluded.

In May 2026 it was announced that [26 of the 77 excluded peers were to be given life peerages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_political_peerages), which were created between 1 June and 17 June 2026.[^cite_note-44] This returned approximately one-third of the excluded peers to Parliament after a short hiatus. As their hereditary peerages were created many decades—in some cases centuries—before their life peerages, they are considered "higher titles" in the [Peerage of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peerage_of_the_United_Kingdom); under [Standing Order 7 of the House of Lords](https://www.parliament.uk/business/publications/house-of-lords-publications/rules-and-guides-for-business/the-standing-orders-of-the-house-of-lords-relating-to-public-business/#lords-manner-of-introduction) they continue to be referred to in Parliament by their hereditary titles despite being in the House by virtue of their life peerage.[^cite_note-45]

## Future reform

The 2024 Labour Party manifesto provided a commitment to introduce an age limit for [members of the House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Members_of_the_House_of_Lords): "At the end of the Parliament in which a member reaches 80 years of age, they will be required to retire from the House of Lords. A Labour government would ensure all peers met high standards, would introduce a new participation requirement, and would strengthen the circumstances in which disgraced members could be removed. It would also reform the appointments process, to ensure quality, and would seek to improve the national and regional balance of the chamber."[^cite_note-Cp108-46]

There is some support amongst [peers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Members_of_the_House_of_Lords) for a measure to strengthen the powers of the [House of Lords Appointments Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Lords_Appointments_Commission).[^cite_note-47]

The Labour Party also conceives a longer term plan (beyond 2029) to replace the upper house with an "alternative" second chamber.[^cite_note-Cp108-46][^cite_note-48]
