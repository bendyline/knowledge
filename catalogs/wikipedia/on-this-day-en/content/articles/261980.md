**Negros Oriental** (*Eastern [Negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros)*; [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language): *Sidlakang Negros*; [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Silangang Negros*), officially the **Province of Negros Oriental** ([Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language): *Lalawigan sa Sidlakang Negros*; [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Lalawigan ng Silangang Negros*), is a [province](165100.md) in the [Philippines](23440.md) located in the [Negros Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros). Its capital is the city of [Dumaguete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumaguete), one of the two regional centers of Negros Island Region, with the other being [Bacolod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacolod). It occupies the southeastern half of the large island of [Negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros), and borders [Negros Occidental](266813.md), which comprises the northwestern half. It also includes [Apo Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apo_Island), a popular [dive site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dive_site) for both local and foreign tourists.

Negros Oriental faces [Cebu](160278.md) to the east across the [Tañon Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta%C3%B1on_Strait) and [Siquijor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siquijor) to the southeast. The primary spoken language is [Binisaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisaya_language), and the predominant religious denomination is [Roman Catholicism](606848.md). [Dumaguete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumaguete) is the capital, seat of government, and most populous city of the province. According to the 2020 census, it had a population of 1,432,990 people,[^cite_note-PSA20–NIR-4] making it the second most-populous province in the region after Negros Occidental, the fifth most-populous province in the [Visayas](72418.md), and the 19th most-populous province of the [Philippines](23440.md).

## History

See also: [Negros Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Revolution)



The Dumaguete Church in circa 1891 with its belfry to warn townsfolk of attacks by marauding pirates.

### Early history

[Negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Island), the largest island in the [Visayas](72418.md), is believed to have once been part of a larger landmass that was cut off by rising waters at the end of the last [ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_age).[^cite_note-panay-5] Among the early inhabitants of the island were the [Negritos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negritos) and the [Austronesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples), and later the [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), who are mainly merchants.[^cite_note-wow-6] They called the island "Buglas", a native word which is believed to mean "cut off".[^cite_note-panay-5]

### Spanish colonial era

Spanish explorers on the expedition of [Miguel Lopez de Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Lopez_de_Legazpi) first came to the island in April 1565. Legazpi dropped anchor in [Bohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol) and sent his men to scout the island.[^cite_note-wow-6] Because of the strong currents of the [Tañon Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta%C3%B1on_Strait) between Cebu and Negros, they were carried for several days and forced to land on the western side of the island. They reported seeing many dark-skinned inhabitants, and they called the island "Negros" (*Negro* means "black" in [Spanish](26825.md)). The island was sparsely settled at the time, except for a few coastal settlements including [Ilog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilog,_Negros_Occidental) and [Binalbagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binalbagan,_Negros_Occidental). In 1571, Legaspi assigned [encomiendas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encomienda) on the island to 13 of his men.[^cite_note-wow-6] [Augustinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Augustine) friars began the [Christianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization) of the island the next year. The island was administered as part of the jurisdiction of [Oton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oton,_Iloilo) until 1734 when it became a military district, and [Ilog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilog,_Negros_Occidental) became the capital of the island. By 1818, Negros Oriental had large numbers of Spanish-Filipino families settling in it.[^cite_note-Dic-7]<sup>: 359 </sup> With [Amlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amlan) having 155 Spanish-Filipino families[^cite_note-Dic-7] and [Dumaguete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumaguete_City) having 25 Spanish-Filipino families.[^cite_note-Dic-7] The capital was transferred to [Himamaylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himamaylan) in 1795. Negros [became a politico-military province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_\(historical_province\)) in 1865 and the capital was transferred to [Bacolod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacolod).

Due to its proximity to Mindanao, the southeastern coasts of Negros were in constant threat from [Moro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people) marauders looking for slaves, so [watchtowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchtower_\(fortification\)) were built to protect the Christian villages. The Moro raids and Negros Oriental's distance from the Negrense capital of Bacolod, induced 13 Recollectionist priests to petition for the division of the island in July 1876.[^cite_note-wow-6] The island of Negros was then divided into the provinces of Negros Oriental and [Negros Occidental](266813.md) by a royal decree executed by Governor General [Valeriano Weyler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeriano_Weyler) on January 1, 1890. [Dumaguete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumaguete) was made the first and only capital of Negros Oriental. In 1892, [Siquijor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siquijor) became a part of Negros Oriental, having previously been administered by Spain under the politico-military province of [Bohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol).

The [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution) reached Negros in 1898, disrupting government functions but without extreme violence and bloodshed. Revolutionary troops in the island were composed mostly of farm labourers and other prominent people of the province of Negros Oriental, who were organized and led by Don Diego de la Viña. The Spanish colonial government in Dumaguete and the rest of the island was overthrown on November 24, 1898. Later, the Negros Occidental area under the leadership of Gen. Araneta, along with the Negros Oriental area under the leadership of Don Diego de la Viña, merged to form the [Cantonal Republic of Negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonal_Republic_of_Negros), a separate government from the more familiar [Malolos Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malolos_Republic) established in [Luzon](69375.md).[^cite_note-WorldStatesmenOrg-8]

### American occupation era



[Demetrio Larena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demetrio_Larena) the first [Governor of Negros Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Negros_Oriental).



[Silliman University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silliman_University)

In 1901, the Negros Oriental province was reorganized by the [United States](3434750.md) and a civil government was established, with [Demetrio Larena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demetrio_Larena) as governor. The American government made Siquijor a "sub-province" of Negros Oriental. Negros Oriental became a province under the American civil government on March 10, 1917, through *Act 2711*.[^cite_note-GovPH-Act2711-9] In 1934, Negros Oriental became a *corregimiento*, a separate military district. Under the American colonial government, transportation infrastructure was developed with improvements of roads and new bridges.[^cite_note-AsiaPlanetNet-10]

### Japanese occupation era

During [World War II](32927.md), both Negros provinces were invaded by [Imperial Japanese forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan), causing many residents to flee to the inland mountains.[^cite_note-Mills-11] Negros Island was liberated by combined Philippine and American troops with the local Negrense guerillas attacking the Japanese on August 6, 1945. The 7th, 73rd, 74th, and 75th Infantry Divisions of the Philippine Commonwealth Army were established from January 3, 1942, to June 30, 1946, and the 7th Constabulary Regiment of the Philippine Constabulary was active from October 28, 1944, to June 30, 1946, at the Military General Headquarters in Negros Oriental. They started the engagements of the Anti-Japanese Imperial Military Operations in Negros from 1942 to 1945 against the Japanese Imperial forces.

On September 17, 1971, [Siquijor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siquijor) became an independent province by virtue of *Republic Act No. 6396*.[^cite_note-ChanRobles-RA6398-12]

### Martial law era

Main articles: [Martial law under Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos) and [Human rights abuses of the Marcos dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship)

The beginning months of the 1970s had marked a period of turmoil and change in the Philippines, as well as in Negros Oriental.[^cite_note-Robles2016-13][^cite_note-GazetteHistoryProtest-14] During his [bid to be the first Philippine president to be re-elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos%E2%80%99_1969_reelection_campaign) for a second term, Ferdinand Marcos launched an unprecedented number of foreign debt-funded public works projects. This caused[^cite_note-Balbosas1992-15][^cite_note-Balisacan\&Hill2003-16] the Philippine economy to take a sudden downwards turn known as the [1969 Philippine balance of payments crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Philippine_balance_of_payments_crisis), which led to a period of economic difficulty and a significant rise of social unrest.[^cite_note-Cororaton1997-17][^cite_note-Celoza1997-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-Kessler1989-20] <sup>: "43" </sup> With only a year left in his last constitutionally allowed term as president, Ferdinand Marcos placed the Philippines under [Martial Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_Law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos) in September 1972 and thus retained the position for fourteen more years.[^cite_note-Kasaysayan9ch10-21] This period in Philippine history is remembered for the Marcos administration's record of [human rights abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship),[^cite_note-McCoy199909202-22][^cite_note-Abinales\&Amoroso20052-23] particularly targeting political opponents, student activists, journalists, religious workers, farmers, and others who fought against the Marcos dictatorship.[^cite_note-Rappler-24]

Dumaguete was one of the first cities in the country to learn about [Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos)' declaration of [martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_Law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos) on September 23, 1972. Local news station DYSR was able to pick up the news from an Australian broadcast. Elsewhere in the country, media outlets such as newspapers and broadcast stations had already been shut down, but DSYR was able to make the announcement before Information Secretary [Francisco Tatad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Tatad) did at around noon. DYSR itself would be shut down later that day.[^cite_note-CrisMasML-25] [Silliman University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silliman_University) was one of the last four universities in the Philippines to be allowed to reopen for classes, with Marcos himself complaining about instances where members of the political opposition such as Senators [Jovito Salonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jovito_Salonga) and [Juan Liwag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Liwag) were invited to speak at the university.[^cite_note-CrisMasML-25]

In the mid-1980s, the [crony capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_cronies) which characterized the Marcos administration had a major effect on the island of Negros in which Dumaguete is located.[^cite_note-ChicagoTribuneNegrosFamine-26][^cite_note-CañaEsquire-27] A [sugar hoarding scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASUTRA_monopoly) by [National Sugar Trading Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Sugar_Trading_Corporation) (NASUTRA) of [Roberto Benedicto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Benedicto) backfired,[^cite_note-CañaEsquire-27] resulting in the mass-firing of sugar workers in Negros Oriental and Negros Occidental. Worsened by the economic nosedive which had begun in 1983, it eventually became known as the 1985 [Negros famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_famine).[^cite_note-CañaEsquire-27]

### Contemporary

See also: [Negros Island Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Island_Region) and [Negros Island killings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Island_killings)

The municipality of [Guihulngan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guihulngan) became a component city by virtue of *Republic Act No. 9409* which sought to convert the municipality into a city. The law was ratified on June 14, 2007. However, the cityhood status was lost twice in the years 2008 and 2010 after the [LCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Cities_of_the_Philippines) questioned the validity of the cityhood law. The cityhood status was reaffirmed after the [court](1335534.md) finalized its ruling on February 15, 2011 which declared the cityhood law constitutional.

On May 29, 2015, the [Negros Island Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Island_Region) was formed when President [Benigno Aquino III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benigno_Aquino_III) signed *Executive Order No. 183, s. 2015*.[^cite_note-GovPH-EO183-28] Negros Oriental was separated from the [Central Visayas](47863267.md) region and transferred to the new region along with [Negros Occidental](266813.md) and [Bacolod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacolod). However, on August 9, 2017, President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) dissolved the Negros Island Region, revoking *Executive Order No. 183, s. 2015* through the signing of *Executive Order No. 38*, citing a lack of funds to fully establish the region according to [Benjamin Diokno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Diokno), the [Secretary of Budget and Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_Budget_and_Management_\(Philippines\)). This returned Negros Oriental to the Central Visayas region.[^cite_note-29]

In 2018, with the [Duterte administration](7407293.md#Presidency_\(2016%E2%80%932022\)) promoting [federalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_the_Philippines), the idea of Negros Oriental and Negros Occidental reunified into one federal region was discussed with local provincial politicians, with some additional support from the known native [Negrenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negrense). There is also a suggestion, jointly approved by the provincial governors, that Negros Oriental along with Negros Occidental, be renamed with their pre-colonial names as "*Buglas Sidlakan*" and "*Buglas Nakatundan*" respectively, with Negros, as a federal state, be named as "*Negrosanon Federated Region*", due to the negative [racial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ati_people) connotation associated with the name "*Negros*".[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

On June 13, 2024, Negros Oriental was transferred from Central Visayas to the Negros Island Region after President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) signed *Republic Act 12000*.[^cite_note-34]

## Geography



Rock formations at [Apo Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apo_Island)

Negros Oriental occupies the southeastern half of the island of [Negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_\(island\)), with [Negros Occidental](266813.md) comprising the northwestern half. It has a total land area of 5,385.53 km<sup>2</sup> (2,079.36 mi<sup>2</sup>). A chain of rugged mountains separates Negros Oriental from Negros Occidental. Negros Oriental faces [Cebu](160278.md) to the east across the [Tañon Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta%C3%B1on_Strait) and [Siquijor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siquijor) to the southeast. The [Sulu Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sea) borders it to the south to southwest. Negros is primarily volcanic, making its soil ideal for agriculture. Eighty percent of all arable land in the island region is cultivated.

### Topography



Mount Talinis, located southwest of [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia,_Negros_Oriental), is the second highest volcanic mountain in Negros

The province's topography is characterized by low, grooved mountain ranges, some of which lie close to the shoreline. At the southern end of the province is [Mount Talinis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Talinis), also known as *Cuernos de Negros* ("Horns of Negros"), which is a dormant [complex volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_volcano) which rises to a height of 2,000 metres (6,600 ft). At the northern end of the province is the active [Kanlaon Volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanlaon_Volcano), the highest peak of the island region with a height of 2,465 metres (8,087 ft). There are a few flatlands and plateaus in the interior to the southwest of the province, which includes the Tablas Plateau.[^cite_note-AFMISDAGovPH-37]

One of the landmarks of Dumaguete is the Dumaguete Bell Tower which stands next to the [Saint Catherine of Alexandria Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumaguete_Cathedral).[^cite_note-38] It was once used to warn the city of impending pirate attacks.[^cite_note-39]

### Climate

Negros Oriental has a [tropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate). Because of the mountain range running from the north to the south, the province has two types of [climatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate) conditions.[^cite_note-aboc-40] The eastern part of the province is characterized as having a modestly distinct wet season, and a short dry season lasting from one to three months. The western half of the province is characterized by a distinct [wet season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season) and [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season).[^cite_note-AFMISDAGovPH-37]

Political divisions of Negros Oriental

[Dumaguete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumaguete) is the provincial capital and seat of government. It is also the province's most populous city, despite having the smallest land area among all component cities and municipalities of Negros Oriental.

The population of Negros Oriental in the 2020 census was 1,432,990 people,[^cite_note-PSA20–NIR-4] with a density of 250/km<sup>2</sup> (650/sq mi). In 2010, its registered voting population was 606,634.[^cite_note-NSCB-NIR-46] 34.5% of the population are concentrated in the six most populous component cities of [Dumaguete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumaguete), [Bayawan](949970.md), [Guihulngan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guihulngan), [Tanjay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjay), [Bais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bais,_Negros_Oriental) and [Canlaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canlaon). Population growth per year is about 0.99% between 2010 and 2015, lower than the national average of 1.72%.[^cite_note-PSA15–NIR-43]

Residents of the whole Negros island are generally called "[Negrenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negrense)" (and less often "Negrosanons") while residents of Negros Oriental sometimes refer to themselves specifically as "NegOrenses" to distinguish themselves from residents of Negros Occidental. Many NegOrenses are of either pure/mixed [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_people) heritage, with foreign ancestry (i.e. [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Filipino) and/or [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Filipino)) as minorities.

### Languages

Negros Oriental is predominantly a [Cebuano-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language) province due to its close proximity to [Cebu](160278.md), with 72% of residents reporting it as a first language. [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language) is spoken by the remaining 28% and is common in areas close to the border with [Negros Occidental](266813.md). [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_English), while seldom used as first languages, are generally understood and used for official, literary, and educational purposes. The indigenous [Minagahat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magahat_language) and [Karul-an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karolanos_language) languages are spoken by the [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_the_Philippines) highlander peoples of Negros. The province is also said to be home of the last living remnants of the [Inatá language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inat%C3%A1_language?action=edit\&redlink=1) speakers.

### Religion

[Christianity](8276776.md) is the predominant religion in the province with [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_the_Philippines) (75%) as the largest single denomination.[^cite_note-47] However, there is a strong and growing presence of [mainline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainline_Protestant) and [evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) which forms about 9% of the province population. The [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md) forms 3–5% of the total population,[^cite_note-48] the [Seventh-day Adventists](28632.md), [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) and the [Aglipayan Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Independent_Church), also known as the [Philippine Independent Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Independent_Church), also have some presence. Adherents of [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Philippines) and [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_the_Philippines) constitute a minority of the population. Some Muslim places of worship are the Imam Khomeini Islamic Center and the [Bagacay Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagacay_Mosque?action=edit\&redlink=1), which is officially known as Masjidus Salam.[^cite_note-49]

A [geothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_electricity) power station in [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia,_Negros_Oriental)

With its vast fertile land resources, Negros Oriental's other major industry is [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture). The primary crops are [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), [sweetcorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweetcorn), [coconut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut) and [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice).[^cite_note-AFMISDAGovPH-37] In the coastal areas, [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing) is the main source of income. People are also involved in [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle) ranches, [fish ponds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_pond) and [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber) plantations, especially in [Bayawan](949970.md). There are also mineral deposits like [gold](12240.md), [silver](27119.md), and [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) found throughout the inner areas of the province.



The Forest Camp Resort in Valencia

The province is already emerging as a major technological center in Visayas, with its growing [business process outsourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process_outsourcing) (BPO) that has started to penetrate the province's secondary cities and other technology-related industries. Vehicle assembly is a growing industry in Amlan. Construction of mass housing and subdivisions is very evident in the periphery of Dumaguete and is expected to spill over into the province's secondary cities and fast-growing towns.

Other industries include water bottling and warehousing, as well as cold and dry storing. Retailing has penetrated other urban areas outside Dumaguete, with the entry of supermarkets and shopping malls in cities such as Bayawan, Tanjay, and Bais. The town of Bacong, which borders Dumaguete in the south, hosts many industrial plants geared for the local and export markets, which can bolster economic growth. Negros Oriental is also a notable tourist destination in the [Visayas](72418.md).

## Transportation



A [motorized tricycle](29843385.md) in Dumaguete

Negros Oriental has a network of roads, including a national road that spans the circumference of Negros Island. National and provincial roads in the province total more than 900 kilometers, though only about half of these are paved.[^cite_note-abot-59]

Many residents do not own private vehicles and rely solely on public transport. Buses and [jeepneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeepneys) link the cities and municipalities of the province. For short distances within a town, [motorized tricycles](29843385.md) ("tricycles" for short) are available. Moreover, [motorcycle taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcycle_taxi), known locally as *habal-habal*, are the primary mode of transportation in places that cannot be reached with other types of vehicles.



[Sibulan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibulan_Airport) terminal in 2007

[Sibulan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibulan_Airport), located in [Sibulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibulan), is the province's only commercial airport.[^cite_note-abot-59] It is a [domestic airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_airport) with multiple daily flights to and from [Manila](184334.md), served by [Philippine Airlines](296889.md) and [Cebu Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebu_Pacific). The airport also serves flights to and from [Cebu](160278.md) and [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro). Based on 2002 statistics, an average of 5,800 outgoing passengers and 5,700 incoming passengers pass through the airport every month. In March 2021, upgrade works were made to the current Sibulan Airport which included pavement reconstruction, expansion of the terminal building, and expansion of [CAAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Aviation_Authority_of_the_Philippines) administrative buildings.[^cite_note-60] The airport is due for transfer to [Bacong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacong) because of congestion in its current location and has been proposed since 2014 and is still pending final approval as of 2022.[^cite_note-abot-59][^cite_note-61]

The primary [seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport) of the province is the [Port of Dumaguete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Dumaguete). Additionally, there are five other seaports in the province classified as tertiary.[^cite_note-DTIGovPH-NegrosOriental-62]

## Culture



Buglasan Festival at the Ninoy Aquino Memorial Freedom Park in Dumaguete

Each town in Negros Oriental celebrates an annual [town fiesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiesta_patronal), usually dedicated to a [patron saint](68055.md) of a particular town or city. In some of the larger towns, there are particular fiestas for specific neighborhoods or [barangays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay).

1. Jimalalud: January 15 - [Sr. Sto. Niño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Ni%C3%B1o_de_Ceb%C3%BA)

2. Canlaon: March 19 - [Sr. San Jose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph)

3. Ayungon: May 15 - [St. Isidore the Farmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isidore_the_Laborer)

4. Sibulan: June 13 - [St. Anthony of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Anthony_of_Padua)

5. Tayasan: June 13 - [St. Anthony of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Anthony_of_Padua)

6. Tanjay City: July 25 - [St. James the Greater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._James_the_Greater)

7. Bacong: August 28 - [St. Augustine of Hippo](2030.md)

8. Bais: September 10 - [St. Nicholas of Tolentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Nicholas_of_Tolentino)

9. Dauin: September 10 - [St. Nicholas of Tolentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Nicholas_of_Tolentino)

10. Manjuyod: October 4 - [St. Francis of Assisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Francis_of_Assisi)

11. Valencia: October 12 - Our Lady of the Abandoned

12. Dumaguete: November 25 - [St. Catherine of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Catherine_of_Alexandria)

13. Amlan: November 30 - [St. Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Andrew)

14. Siaton: December 6 - [St. Nicholas of Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Nicholas)

Additionally, the Buglasan Festival is celebrated annually in October in the provincial capital of Dumaguete and is hailed as Negros Oriental's "festival of festivals".[^cite_note-Inquirer-BuglasanFest-63] It is a week-long celebration where unique booths of each town and city in Negros Oriental feature their native products and tourist attractions. The highlights of the occasion are the float parade and street dancing competition.[^cite_note-SunStar-BuglasanFest-64] Through *Presidential Proclamation 695*, October 25, 2024 was declared a [special non-working day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_Philippines) in celebration of its Buglasan Festival.[^cite_note-65]



Landscape of a beach resort in [Dauin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dauin), the province's resort capital. Tourism is one of the major industries in the province of Negros Oriental.

## Media

There are at least seven local media publications in general circulation around the province. These publications include *Dumaguete MetroPost*,[^cite_note-DumagueteMetroPost-66] *The Negros Chronicle*,[^cite_note-TheNegrosChronicle-67] *Dumaguete Star Informer*, *Times Focus*, and *Island News*.[^cite_note-TheVisayanDailyStar-68] [PLDT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLDT), [Globe Telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe_Telecom), [DITO Telecommunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dito_Telecommunity) and their subsidiaries are major providers of a network connection within the province. Major providers, in TV and radio are [Advanced Media Broadcasting System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Media_Broadcasting_System) operating AllTV Channel 12 Dumaguete acquired from a defunct [ABS-CBN Dumaguete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABS-CBN) station, [People's Television Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Television_Network), [GMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMA_Network), [GTV (Philippine TV network)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GTV_\(Philippine_TV_network\)), and [TV5 Dumaguete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV5_Network). Cable TV and pay TV providers like [G Sat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_Sat), [Cignal TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cignal_TV) and [SatLite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SatLite) provides access to [Kapamilya Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapamilya_Channel), [BBC](19344654.md), [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN), and other international programs. The province is mainly served before by one regional newscast: [TV Patrol Central Visayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Patrol_Central_Visayas) (shared with [ABS-CBN Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DYCB-TV)).
