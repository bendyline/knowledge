**Czech Radio** ([Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language): *Český rozhlas*, ČRo) is the [public](178808.md) radio broadcaster of the [Czech Republic](5321.md) operating continuously since 1923. It is the oldest national radio broadcaster in [continental Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Europe) and the second-oldest in [Europe](9239.md) after the [BBC](19344654.md).[^cite_note-history-1] Czech Radio was established in 1992 by the Czech Radio Act, which sets out the framework for its operation and finance. It acts as the successor to the previous state-owned Czechoslovak Radio which ceased to exist by 1992.&#x20;

The service broadcasts throughout the Czech Republic nationally and locally. Its four national services are [Radiožurnál](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CRo_Radio%C5%BEurn%C3%A1l), [Dvojka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CRo_Dvojka), [Vltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CRo_Vltava) and [Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CRo_Plus). Czech Radio operates twelve nationwide stations and another fourteen regional stations. All ČRo stations broadcast via internet stream, digital via [DAB+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAB+) and [DVB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVB), and part analog via terrestrial transmitters. It is based in [Prague](23844.md) in a building in Vinohradská třída.

## History

### Czechoslovak era

*_Český rozhlas_*, then *_Československý rozhlas_*, was established on 18 May 1923, making its first broadcast from a [scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scouting) tent in the [Kbely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kbely) district of [Prague](23844.md), under the name *Radiojournal*.[^cite_note-01074898.pdf-2] The premises of the station changed numerous times, firstly moving to the district of [Hloubětín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hloub%C4%9Bt%C3%ADn), before later using locations in the *Poštovní nákupny* building, the *Orbis* building and the *Národní dům na Vinohradech* building, all in Prague.[^cite_note-01074898.pdf-2] Since the moves, Czech Radio has been located in Prague in the building Vinohradská 12 since 1933.

The first regular announcer of the station, who prepared and presented the news from the daily papers, was [Adolf Dobrovolný](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Dobrovoln%C3%BD). He took up the position on 17 January 1924, becoming the station's first professional radio announcer and his position was made permanent on 1 January 1925.[^cite_note-01074898.pdf-2] He held the position until his death in 1934.

A message broadcast on Czech Radio on 5 May 1945 brought about the start of the [Prague uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_uprising).[^cite_note-pp2013-3] In the same year, regional studios in the cities of [Plzeň](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plze%C5%88), [České Budějovice](149384.md), [Hradec Králové](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hradec_Kr%C3%A1lov%C3%A9) and [Ústí nad Labem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Ast%C3%AD_nad_Labem) were launched.[^cite_note-history-1]

The station was taken over by Soviet forces, after [short fighting with unarmed civilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_for_Czechoslovak_Radio), in August 1968, in the first day of the [Soviet invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact_invasion_of_Czechoslovakia), although broadcasting managed to continue from alternative locations.[^cite_note-pp2013-3]

### Czech era



Logo used between 1996 and 2013

In 1991, the Czech Radio Group changed its status and became an independent organisation, despite being publicly funded as of 2008.[^cite_note-history-1][^cite_note-pp2008-4] Czech Radio (ČRo) was established by Act of the Czech National Council (No. 484/1991 Coll.) on Czech Radio.&#x20;

On 1 January 1992, Czech Radio was established as a public radio with property transferred from Czechoslovak Radio.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKöpplová2003103-104-5][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHanáčková202319-6] The headquarters were set up at Vinohradská 12 in Prague, where the old Czechoslovak Radio was based at.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJešutová2003422-7] Operation of regional stations in the Czech Republic was also transferred. On 1 January 1993, Czech Radio became a member of the [European Broadcasting Union](10380.md) (EBU).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJešutová2003603-8] In 1999, Czech Radio launched an experimental digital radio broadcast in Prague.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKöpplová2003149-9]

An envisaged new premises for Czech Radio, a 30-storey building in the district of [Pankrác](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pankr%C3%A1c) which took 22 years to build at a cost of 1.35 billion [Czech koruna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_koruna), was sold after the construction phrase in 1996 as it was deemed too big for the station's requirements.[^cite_note-pp1996-10]

In 2002, [Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Free_Europe/Radio_Liberty) station stopped broadcasting in the [Czech Republic](5321.md), with the broadcast rebranded as *Czech Radio 6* under the Czech Radio group.[^cite_note-pp2002-11] In October 2005, digital television broadcasting was launched on [DVB-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVB-T), with some of the ČRo's circuits being included in the first multiplex.[^cite_note-12]

A large expansion of the number of ČRo stations took place in 2005. At that time, the D-dur, Rádio Česko and Leonardo circuits began broadcasting, and in 2006, [ČRo Radio Wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CRo_Radio_Wave) station was added.[^cite_note-13]

Czech Radio launched a new logo in 2013, featuring the letter *R* with stripes, at a cost of 2.2 million Czech koruna.[^cite_note-2013logo-14] The organisation marked 90 years of existence in 2013, celebrating the occasion with a 48-hour broadcast including 90 interviews interspersed with news reports every half-hour.[^cite_note-pp2013-3] The event, which took place on [Wenceslas Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenceslas_Square), set a new national record for the longest uninterrupted radio broadcast.[^cite_note-pp2013-3] In the same year, [ČRo 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CRo_6), ČRo Radio Czech Republic and ČRo Leonardo were cancelled and replaced by the new station [ČRo Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CRo_Plus).&#x20;

At the end of 2021, the broadcasting of the stations [Radiožurnál](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CRo_Radio%C5%BEurn%C3%A1l), [Dvojka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CRo_Dvojka) and [Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CRo_Plus) on the medium and long wave bands was terminated, and thus the broadcasting of Czech Radio via AM was terminated altogether.[^cite_note-15] In 2024, the regional station Český rozhlas Region was renamed Český rozhlas Střední Čechy.[^cite_note-16]

## Organization

### Finance

The majority of Czech Radio's income comes from radio licence fees, which have been set at CZK 45 per month since 2005.[^cite_note-26] ČRo's annual income from these fees in 2019 amounted to CZK 2.09 billion, or 91% of Czech Radio's total income of CZK 2.29 billion that year. The remaining few percent of the income is made up of revenues from Czech Radio's own business activities, which include, for example, the sale of limited advertising time, sponsorship revenues and licensing of recordings.

According to Act No. 231/2001 Coll., on the Operation of Radio and Television Broadcasting and on Amendments to Other Acts, the time reserved for advertising and teleshopping may not exceed 3 minutes per day on each Czech Radio station, and a maximum of 5 minutes per day for local stations. Commercial messages may not be included in news, journalistic and educational programmes, with the exception of cultural and sports broadcasts, if advertising is part of them in terms of the conditions of the broadcasting rights.[^cite_note-27]

In April 2026, Culture Minister [Oto Klempíř](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oto_Klemp%C3%AD%C5%99) unveiled details of a planned overhaul of public media funding which would abolish license fees in favour of state funding. The proposal has raised an outcry from the opposition parties, who see it as an attack on media independence.[^cite_note-28]

### Logos

The original logo of Czech Radio was used between 1992 and 1995. Since 1996, the radio has had a logo created by the Men On The Moon agency, which won the Logo of the Year award in 1997. Since 2013, Czech Radio has used the logo designed by Studio Marvil.[^cite_note-29]
