**Ahvaz** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): اهواز; [\[ʔæhˈvɒːz\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Persian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/99/Ahvaz-fa.ogg/Ahvaz-fa.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/99/Ahvaz-fa.ogg/Ahvaz-fa.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>)[^cite_note-3] is a city in the [Central District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_District_\(Ahvaz_County\)) of [Ahvaz County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahvaz_County), [Khuzestan](448079.md) province, [Iran](14653.md), serving as capital of the province, the county, and the district.[^cite_note-Khuzestan_Province_Structure-4]

Positioned near the border with [Iraq](7515928.md), along the banks of the [Karun River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun_River), Ahvaz serves as a key industrial and transportation hub, with a diverse population and a history shaped by its proximity to [Iraq](7515928.md) and the [Persian Gulf](24761.md).[^cite_note-5]

Ahvaz is home to over 1.3 million people within its metropolitan area. Census results suggest a steady increase in population.[^cite_note-6] It is home to [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians), [Arabs](2185.md) and other groups such as [Qashqai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qashqai_people) and [Kurds](17068.md).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Languages spoken in the area include [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language), [Khuzestāni Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khuzestani_Arabic), [Southern Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Kurdish), [Neo-Mandaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Mandaic), and dialects such as [Bakhtiari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhtiari_dialect), [Dezfuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dezfuli_dialect) and [Shushtari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shushtari_dialect).[^cite_note-9]

The [Karun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun), the largest river in Iran by volume,[^cite_note-10] flows through the center of the city. It is one of two navigable rivers in Iran, alongside the [Arvand Rud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvand_Rud).[^cite_note-11]

Ahvaz has earned the reputation of being the City of Bridges due to its numerous big bridges built on the [Karun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun) to facilitate better communication between the east and west parts of the city,[^cite_note-12] although since the beginning of the 20th century, this city has always been known as the Oil Capital of [Iran](14653.md), along with other cities of [Khuzestan](448079.md) province.[^cite_note-13]

This city has a long history, dating back to the [Achaemenid period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire). In ancient times, the city was one of the main centers of the [Academy of Gondishapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Gondishapur).

## Etymology

The city known today as Ahvaz was first recognized as an urban settlement in ancient times under the name [Taryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taryana).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-\:3-15] This name clearly reflects “Dāryāna”[^cite_note-\:1-16] derived from “Dāryān” the legendary name of [Darius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great), and also refers to a powerful dynasty of Persian kings that ruled after the Achaemenid period.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-\:2-18]

Taryana was located along a section of the [Royal Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Road) that stretched from [Persepolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persepolis) to [Sardis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardis), with part of the royal route crossing the [Karun River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun) in Taryana (present-day Ahvaz). Later, during the reign of [Ardashir I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardashir_I) (*Ardashir Babakan*), Taryana expanded and was renamed “Hormazd-Ardeshir” ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): هُرمَزد اردشیر) in [New Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Persian), being reestablished on both sides of the river. The first part of the name honored "God," while the second part was named after the king himself. The name was later abbreviated to “Darawashir”,[^cite_note-\:1-16][^cite_note-\:2-18] but it is unclear if this change was made by [Ardashir I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardashir_I) in 230 (cf. *[Encyclopædia Iranica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Iranica)*, [al-Maqdisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Maqdisi), et al.)[^cite_note-19] or by his grandson [Hormizd I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormizd_I) according to the Middle Persian [Šahrestānīhā ī Ērānšahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0ahrest%C4%81n%C4%ABh%C4%81_%C4%AB_%C4%92r%C4%81n%C5%A1ahr). The king built a dam over the Karun River in the city, which increased its significance, and soon Darawashir became the capital of the province of [Susiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susiana) (*modern-day [Khuzestan](448079.md)*).[^cite_note-\:1-16][^cite_note-20]

The commercial center of [Susiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susiana), located on one side of the river in Darawashir (Darāvāshīr, east of the city, spanning the area from present-day [Khorram Kushk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorram_Kushk) to the present-day “Chaharrah Zand”), was known as "Hūǰestān-vāčār" (“*the market of the Huzestan*”). The other side of the river, serving as the administrative and aristocratic center (west of Ahvaz, covering the area of present-day Amaniyeh, Lashkar, and Lashkarābād), was called Horamshir.[^cite_note-\:1-16]

According to the research of numerous historians, including [Clifford Bosworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Edmund_Bosworth) in the *[Encyclopædia Iranica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Iranica)[^cite_note-\:4-21]*, [Vladimir Minorsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Minorsky),[^cite_note-22] [Svat Soucek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svat_Soucek),[^cite_note-\:5-23] and [Abdolmajid Arfaei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Majid_Arfaei), the names Ahvaz and [Khuzestan](448079.md) share a common origin and are both derived from the name of the ancient people who inhabited this region, the **Uzi** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): *Uxii*).[^cite_note-\:4-21][^cite_note-\:6-24]

**Uji** (also spelled Uzi) was given to a warrior tribe in the province of [Susiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susiana). It is recorded in Old Persian as '**Uvaja**' ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): اووَجَه / اووَژه, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *uvaja/uvja*) and in Assyrian cuneiform inscriptions as '**Huzi**'. Later, Greek historians recorded it as 'Ouxioi' ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): اوکسی), and Arab writers recorded it as 'Khūzī' years later.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-Bosworth3-26][^cite_note-hamedani2-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-lockhart-30][^cite_note-\:7-31]

The name of today's [Khuzestan](448079.md) province is derived from their ethnonym through phonetic transformation and Persianization (Huzistan, “the land of [the Huzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huzi_people)”). In [Syriac Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Christianity) writings, the name of the city appears as [Beth Huzaye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Huzaye_\(East_Syriac_ecclesiastical_province\)).[^cite_note-\:6-24][^cite_note-\:5-23]

When the Muslims defeated the [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) in the 7th century CE, the second part of the city (Horamshir) was destroyed. The Arabs translated the name of the remaining part of the city, the Hūǰestān-vāčār as Sūq al-Ahvāz.[^cite_note-\:4-21][^cite_note-\:1-16]

The name of “Ahvaz” is the Arabicized plural form of the word "Huzi", following the *af‘āl* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): افعال) pattern. Thus, “Hūzīs” became “Ahvāz”. The Huzis, who were likely descendants of the [Elamites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elam), were the indigenous inhabitants of the [Khuzestan region](448079.md). Their language, known as the [Huzi language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huzi_language), remained in use in Khuzestan until the [Sasanian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) and even for centuries after the [Muslim conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia) and subsequent rule in [Iran](14653.md). Over time, due to varying pronunciations across different languages and dialects, the name of the province evolved from Hūzistān to Khuzestān.[^cite_note-\:1-16]

During the [Qajar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran), under [Naseroddin Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naser_al-Din_Shah_Qajar), the name of Ahvaz was changed to 'Nāseriyeh'. However, in September 1935, by approval of the Council of Ministers, the city was once again officially named Ahvaz.[^cite_note-\:3-15]

## History

See also: [Khuzestan Province § History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khuzestan_Province#History)

### 20th century

In the 19th century, "Ahvaz was no more than a small [borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough) inhabited mainly by [Sabeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabians) (1500 to 2000 inhabitants according to Ainsworth in 1835; 700 according to Curzon in 1890)."[^cite_note-32]

In the 1880s, in late [Qajar Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran), the [Karun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun) was dredged and reopened to commerce. A newly built railway crossed the Karun at Ahvaz. The city again became a commercial crossroads, linking river and rail traffic. The construction of the [Suez Canal](29323.md) further stimulated trade. A port city was built near the old village of Ahvaz and named *Bandar-e-Naseri* in honour of [Naser al-Din Shah Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naser_al-Din_Shah_Qajar).

Oil was found near Ahvaz in the early 20th century, and the city once again grew and prospered as a result of this newfound wealth. From 1897 to 1925, the city of Ahvaz was under the control of a Qajar prince, who served as governor, and Reza-Gholi Khan Sarhang, the commander of the Khuzestan army. [Khaz'al al-Ka'bi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaz'al_al-Ka'bi) was recognized by [Mozaffar ad-Din Shah Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozaffar_ad-Din_Shah_Qajar) as hereditary ruler of the [Emirate of Muhammara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Muhammara), [Sardar Asad Bakhtiari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali-Qoli_Khan_Bakhtiari), as the most powerful leader of the [Bakhtiari people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhtiari_people). He had power and authority over most regions of Khuzestan, such as Dezful, Shushtar, Izeh, even Ahvaz and Amir Mojahede Bakhtiari in [Ramhormoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramhormoz) and Behbahan. At this time, the newly founded Ahvaz was named *Nâseri* in honour of its founder Nasser ed-Din Shah Qajar. Afterwards, during [Pahlavi Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavi_Iran), it resumed its old name, *Ahvaz*. The government of [Khuzestan province](448079.md) was transferred there from [Shushtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shushtar) in 1926. The [Trans-Iranian Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Iranian_Railway) reached Ahvaz in 1929 and by [World War II](32927.md), Ahvaz had become the principal built-up area of the interior of Khūzestān. Professional segregation remained well marked between various groups in that period still feebly integrated: [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people), sub-groupings of Persians and [Arabs](2185.md). Natives of [Isfahan Province](448061.md) held an important place in retail trade, as owners of cafes and hotels, and as craftsmen.[^cite_note-33]

During World War II, in July 1942, a camp for Polish refugees was established in Ahvaz (see also *[Iran–Poland relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Poland_relations)*).[^cite_note-jw-34] Some 4,000 Poles were housed there as of May 1943.[^cite_note-jw-34]

[Iraq](7515928.md) attempted to annex Khuzestan and Ahvaz in 1980, resulting in the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) (1980–1988). Ahvaz was close to the front lines and suffered severely during the war. Iraq had hoped to exacerbate ethnic tensions and win over popular support for the invaders. Most accounts say that the [Arabs of Khuzestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabs_of_Khuzestan) resisted the Iraqis rather than welcome them as liberators. However, some Iranian Arabs claim that as a minority, they face discrimination from the central government; they agitate for the right to preserve their cultural and linguistic distinction and more provincial autonomy. See [Politics of Khuzestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Khuzestan).

In 1989, the Foolad Ahvaz steel facility was built near the town. This company is best known for its company-sponsored [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_\(soccer\)) club, [Foolad F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foolad_F.C.), which was the champion of [Iran's Premier Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran's_Premier_Football_League) in 2005.

In 2005, the city witnessed [a series of bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahvaz_Bombings). Many government sources relate these events to developments in Iraq, accusing foreign governments of organizing and funding Arab separatist groups. The [Arab Struggle Movement for the Liberation of Ahvaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Struggle_Movement_for_the_Liberation_of_Ahvaz) claimed credit for several of the bombings, including four bombs on 12 June 2005, that killed 8 people.[^cite_note-35] Gunmen killed at least 29 people at the [Ahvaz military parade attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahvaz_military_parade_attack) on 22 September 2018.[^cite_note-36]

### 2025–2026 Iranian protests

During the [2025–2026 Iranian protests](81976180.md), the city of Ahvaz also witnessed protest gatherings and clashes between protesters and security forces.[^cite_note-\:0-37] On 2 January 2026, Iraqi militias affiliated with the Iranian government had recruited forces to assist Iranian security forces in [suppressing protests](82036668.md) in Iran.[^cite_note-Iran_International-38] According to the report, the troops were assembled at a base in Ahvaz before being deployed to various regions to support protest suppression efforts.[^cite_note-Iran_International-38]

On 10 January, protests and demonstrations were held in Ahvaz. According to reports, some streets were blocked during the protests, and following clashes between protesters and security forces, the Ahvaz governorate building caught fire.[^cite_note-\:0-37]

On 16 January, scattered protests and clashes were reported in various neighborhoods and areas of the city. On 17 January, a group of shopkeepers and citizens held a protest gathering in the Grand Bazaar of Ahvaz, after which a number of shops were closed.[^cite_note-\:0-37]

On 18 January, a protest gathering took place in the Naderi area and was dispersed by security forces using tear gas. Clashes were also reported on 19 January, in the Golestan area. According to reports, security forces opened fire in order to prevent protesters from approaching certain government buildings.[^cite_note-\:0-37]

In addition, reports indicated that plainclothes forces confronted protest gatherings in the "Zeitoun Karmandi" and central bazaar areas, leading to the arrest of several protesters. During these events, law enforcement forces reportedly retreated to the "Boostan" intersection in the "Kuy-e Saadi" neighborhood. The use of drones by security forces to monitor and control protests was also reported.[^cite_note-\:0-37]

On 21 and 22 January, protests and clashes continued in parts of the city, and several protesters were arrested. On Monday night, nighttime protests accompanied by clashes were reported on Naderi Street, 24-Metri Street, and in the "Abdolhamid Bazaar", where security forces blocked several roads using concrete barriers.[^cite_note-\:0-37]

On 23 January, nighttime protests in the "Golestan", "Saadi", and "Goldasteh" neighborhoods were met with security force intervention. According to reports, live ammunition and pellet guns were used during the clashes, resulting in the deaths and injuries of a number of protesters.[^cite_note-\:0-37]

### Languages

Main article: [Khuzestan province § Languages](448079.md#Languages)

Persian serves as the official language and is widely spoken across Ahvaz.[^cite_note-en.mehrnews.com-40] Many Ahvazis are [bilingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilingual), speaking both [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) and one of the following languages/dialects. The Arabic spoken in Ahvaz is a variety of [Khuzestani Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khuzestani_Arabic).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] Another part of Ahvazis speak the [Bakhtiari dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhtiari_dialect).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] [Neo-Mandaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Mandaic), one of the [Neo-Aramaic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Aramaic_languages), is also spoken among the [Mandaeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaeans) of Ahvaz. It is a descendant of the [Mandaic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandaic_language) partially influenced by Khuzestani Persian.

## Bridges

There are 9 bridges over the [Karun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun) river.



Pol Sefid



One of the attractions of Ahvaz is its beautiful urban sunset

### Pol Siah

The [Pol Siah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pol_Siah) (Black Bridge), also known as the Victory Bridge, was the first modern bridge over the Karun River. The bridge was built during [World War II](32927.md) and used to supply the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and had a major impact on Allied victory.[^cite_note-50]

### Pol Sefid

[Pol Sefid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pol_Sefid_\(Ahvaz\)) (White Bridge) is an [arch bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch_bridge) completed on 21 September 1936 and inaugurated on 6 November 1936. The bridge remains a symbol of the city still today. The White Bridge has two suspension spans with lengths of 136 and 130 meters and three arched spans with a length of 49 meters, which makes the total length of the bridge 501 meters. The bridge was built by the Swedish company [Skanska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skanska).

### Pol Hashtom

[Pol Hashtom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pol_Hashtom) is the eighth intra-urban bridge in Ahvaz.[^cite_note-51] It is the largest cable bridge in the [Middle East](19323.md).[^cite_note-52] Construction started in February 2006 and ended in March 2012.[^cite_note-53] The bridge connects the Amaniyeh area and Takht-e Soleyman street, which are located west of the [Karun river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun), with Zand street, which is located east of this river.[^cite_note-54]

### Other

The other 6 bridges are third bridge, Naderi bridge, Fifth bridge, Sixth bridge, Seventh bridge (also named *Dialogue among civilizations bridge*), Cable bridge, and Ninth bridge.

## Geography



Ahvaz and the [Karun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun) during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) lockdown

Ahvaz lies on the [Khuzestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khuzestan) plain at 31°30′ north latitude and 48°40′ east longitude, at an elevation of 12 m (39 ft) above sea level. Much of [Khuzestan province](448079.md) consists of such plains.[^cite_note-scu-55]



Kianabad, 2012

The city has a [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BWh*), with long, hot summers and short, mild winters. Summer temperatures regularly reach at least 45 °C (113 °F) and sometimes exceed 50 °C (122 °F),[^cite_note-hottest-56] and sandstorms and duststorms are common. The highest reading on record, 53.7 °C (128.7 °F), was set on 29 June 2017; the [dew point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dew_point) that day peaked at 23 °C (73 °F), unusually humid for the city's typically dry heat.[^cite_note-57] In July 2022, [NASA](18426568.md) listed Ahvaz among the hottest cities in the world.[^cite_note-58]



A [flare stack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_flare) in Ahvaz. Incomplete combustion releases [soot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soot), a known [carcinogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogen).

[^cite_note-59]

Winter minima fall to around 5 °C (41 °F), though frost has been recorded in every winter month as well as in early spring and late autumn.[^cite_note-irimo-lows-60] The record low of −7.0 °C (19.4 °F) was set during the exceptionally cold winter of 1963–64, when freezing temperatures persisted for several nights and frost reached many normally hot Iranian cities.[^cite_note-irimo-lows-60][^cite_note-61] Average annual precipitation is about 219 mm (8.6 in).[^cite_note-noaa-normals-62]

### Pollution

In 2011, the [World Health Organization](33583.md) ranked Ahvaz as the world's most air-polluted city.[^cite_note-WHO-70] The reason Ahvaz is so polluted is because of its oil industry. The [pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution) can be very dangerous, causing different types of diseases, and can be harmful to plants.[^cite_note-71]

## Transportation

### Roads

Ahvaz, the largest city in the province, is composed of two distinct districts. The newer district, which functions as the administrative and industrial centre, is situated on the right bank of the [Karun River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun), while residential areas are located in the older section of the city, which is positioned on the left bank. The city can be accessed via the following routes, in addition to the single-runway airport:

- [](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_39_\(Iran\)) Ahvaz-[Abadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abadan,_Iran) expressway (145 km)

- [](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeway_5_\(Iran\)) Ahvaz-[Bandar Imam Khomeini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar_Imam_Khomeini) freeway (175 km)

## Sport

Traditionally, Khuzestan province has been a major soccer hub in Iran. The city has two existing sport complexes: [Takhti Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takhti_Stadium_\(Ahvaz\)) and the newly constructed [Ghadir Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghadir_Stadium). There are several other smaller complexes for martial arts, swimming pools and gymnasiums. Also, a new privately owned [stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Foolad_Stadium) is currently under construction by [Foolad F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foolad_F.C.) in Ahvaz.

The city has significant sports facilities, including three major stadiums:

- [Takhti Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takhti_Stadium_\(Ahvaz\))

- [Ghadir Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghadir_Stadium)

- [Foolad Khouzestan Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foolad_Arena) (Foolad Arena)

### Football



[Takhti Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takhti_Stadium_\(Ahvaz\)), June 1973, girls' running race

[Football](10568.md) is a major part of the city's culture. The abundant enthusiasm has made Ahvaz home to three Iranian major [Football](10568.md) clubs: [Foolad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foolad_F.C.), [Esteghlal Khuzestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esteghlal_Meli-Sanati_Khuzestan_F.C.) are currently playing in the [Persian Gulf Pro League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_Pro_League), and [Esteghlal Ahvaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esteghlal_Ahvaz_F.C.) is playing in [Azadegan League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadegan_League).

Foolad have won the league on two occasions, the 2013–2014 season and the 2004–2005 season. Esteghlal Ahvaz finished runners–up in the league in the 2006–2007 season. In 2016, [Esteghlal Khuzestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esteghlal_Khuzestan_F.C.) won the league for the first time.

A number of other teams such as [Foolad B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foolad_Khuzestan_B_F.C.) the second team of Foolad and [Karun Khuzestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun_Khuzestan_F.C.) play in the [2nd Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_Football's_2nd_Division).

## Colleges and universities



[Shahid Chamran University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahid_Chamran_University_of_Ahvaz) (formerly Jundishapur)

Ahvaz occupies a prominent place in Iranian higher education owing to its large and well-regarded universities. Gondishapur University was founded on 24 September 1955 and was renamed Jundishapur shortly afterwards, keeping that name until the [Islamic Revolution](347268.md) of 1979.[^cite_note-74] The faculty of agriculture was the university's first faculty; agriculture, literature, mathematics and medicine were among the earliest subjects offered. On 15 June 1958 the first cohort of 38 graduates of the agriculture faculty received their certificates at a ceremony. The faculties of engineering and theology began operating around the start of the Islamic Revolution and the [Dezful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dezful) branch in 1987-88, while the remaining faculties had opened before the revolution. Given the industrial character of [Khuzestan](448079.md), research at Shahid Chamran University carries particular weight; its academics have produced dozens of books and translations and hundreds of research projects.[^cite_note-hamshahri-75]



The former building of the Faculty of Literature and Humanities, Shahid Chamran University

The university today has 15 faculties and a university campus; its medical departments were spun off in the 1980s and have since formed the independent [Ahvaz Jundishapur University of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahvaz_Jundishapur_University_of_Medical_Sciences).[^cite_note-hamshahri-75] Bachelor's and master's programmes are offered in most subjects, with doctoral programmes additionally available in agriculture, educational sciences and psychology, mathematics, veterinary medicine and chemistry.[^cite_note-scu-intro-76]

[Shahid Chamran University of Ahvaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahid_Chamran_University_of_Ahvaz) is the oldest institution of higher education in the city. It lies in western Ahvaz overlooking the bank of the [Karun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun). Its faculty of literature and foreign languages is separate from the main campus and is regarded as one of the city's landmarks. According to the university, more than 14,000 students are enrolled and over 700 academic staff are employed there.[^cite_note-scu-intro-76]

[Ahvaz Jundishapur University of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahvaz_Jundishapur_University_of_Medical_Sciences) also traces its origins to the 1955 foundation. The medical faculty, together with the other health-science departments, was separated from Jundishapur University in the mid-1980s and placed under the Ministry of Health.[^cite_note-77]

[Azad University, Ahvaz Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Azad_University,_Ahvaz_Branch) was established on 22 December 1982 and was the first branch of the [Azad University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Azad_University) in Khuzestan province, as well as one of its first five branches nationwide. Teaching began in 1983-84 with a mathematics programme, which was discontinued after a short time; instruction resumed in January-February 1985 with civil engineering and agriculture. In 2014 the branch was merged with the Science and Research Branch of Khuzestan.[^cite_note-78]
