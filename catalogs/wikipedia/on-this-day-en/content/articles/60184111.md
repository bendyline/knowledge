**Lucas Pinheiro Braathen** ([/ˈluːkəs piˈɲeɾu brah-ten/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/ab/PT-P-LPB2026.ogg/PT-P-LPB2026.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/ab/PT-P-LPB2026.ogg/PT-P-LPB2026.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, born 19 April 2000) is a Norwegian and Brazilian [alpine ski racer](39576.md) who specializes in the technical events of [slalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slalom_skiing) and [giant slalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_slalom). He is the [men's giant slalom](82277242.md) champion at the [2026 Winter Olympics](38416091.md), becoming the first [Brazilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_at_the_Olympics) and South American in history to win a medal at the Winter Olympics, as well as the first athlete representing any tropical nation to win a Winter Olympic medal.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

Until his retirement from World Cup racing in October 2023, he represented [Norway](21241.md) in international events. In March 2024, Pinheiro Braathen announced that he would return to the alpine skiing circuit, representing [Brazil](3383.md) instead.

## Early life

Pinheiro Braathen was born in Oslo to a Norwegian father and a Brazilian mother on 19 April 2000.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] His parents divorced when he was three years old and he went to Brazil with his mother to live in the city of [Campinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campinas). Later in his childhood, he moved back to Norway to live with his father while regularly visiting Brazil.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-6] He grew up speaking Norwegian and Portuguese.[^cite_note-7]

Pinheiro Braathen first skied when he was three or four years old, but he did not start skiing again until he was nine years old.[^cite_note-8] Braathen also played football as a child, though he preferred skiing as it is an individual sport.[^cite_note-9]

## Career

From [Hokksund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokksund), Pinheiro Braathen raced for Norwegian club [Bærums SK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A6rums_SK).[^cite_note-10] At the [Junior World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Junior_Alpine_Skiing_Championships) in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Junior_Alpine_Skiing_Championships_2019), he finished fourth and eleventh, followed by a silver medal in super-G, and a bronze medal in the combined event. He made his [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_Alpine_Ski_World_Cup) debut in December [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_FIS_Alpine_Ski_World_Cup) in [Val d'Isere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val_d'Isere), and collected his first points (five) with a 26th-place finish.[^cite_note-Bio-11]



Pinheiro Braathen in December 2022 at [Canalone Miramonti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canalone_Miramonti), [Madonna di Campiglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_di_Campiglio)

Pinheiro Braathen recorded his first victory (and podium) in October 2020 at the opener of the [2020–21 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_FIS_Alpine_Ski_World_Cup), a giant slalom in [Sölden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B6lden). In [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_FIS_Alpine_Ski_World_Cup), he won his first slalom at the [Lauberhorn race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauberhorn_ski_races) in [Wengen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wengen), going from 29th place after the first run to first place after the second run, the largest jump to victory at that time.[^cite_note-12]



Pinheiro Braathen with the Small Crystal Globe of the [2023 Men's Slalom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Alpine_Skiing_World_Cup_%E2%80%93_Men's_slalom)

Pinheiro Braathen announced his retirement from World Cup racing on 27 October 2023, a day before the new season's opening event in Sölden.[^cite_note-13] However, on 7 March 2024, he announced his return to alpine ski racing as a competitor for Brazil instead of Norway.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] On 27 October 2024, exactly a year after he had announced his retirement, he returned at the World Cup giant slalom [season opener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_FIS_Alpine_Ski_World_Cup) in Sölden, narrowly finishing in 4th place behind three of his former teammates from Norway and scoring the first ever world cup points for Brazil in alpine skiing.

On 6 February 2026, Pinheiro Braathen participated in the opening ceremony of the [2026 Winter Olympics](75430691.md) as the [flag bearer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_bearer) of Brazil, alongside [Nicole Silveira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Silveira).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] On 14 February, Braathen arrived in the Milano Cortina 2026 as the second in the slalom, giant slalom and overall rankings after a successful [2025–26 FIS Alpine Ski World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_FIS_Alpine_Ski_World_Cup), behind only skiing superstar Swiss [Marco Odermatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Odermatt). In the [giant slalom](82277242.md) Braathen put himself in pole position for gold with a leading time of 1\:13.92 in the first run, almost a full second faster than second-place Odermatt. Braathen's second run saw him maintain his lead to secure the gold medal in a combined time of 2\:25.00, with Odermatt (2\:25.58) and [Loïc Meillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lo%C3%AFc_Meillard) (2\:26.17) of [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics) taking silver and bronze, respectively.

Braathen's historic victory made him the first [Latin American](18524.md) and Brazilian, as well as the first athlete from any [tropical nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_nations_at_the_Winter_Olympics), to win a medal in the Winter Olympics.[^cite_note-18]

After the Olympics, Pinheiro Braathen competed in the penultimate World Cup event of the season in [Kranjska Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podkoren_3), Slovenia. He topped the field in the giant slalom, winning a title in that discipline for the first time while representing Brazil.[^cite_note-19] On the next day he attained another podium with a third-place finish in the slalom.[^cite_note-20]

Next, Pinheiro Braathen competed in the last World Cup event of the season in [Lillehammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lillehammer), [Norway](21241.md). He topped the field in the giant slalom with a total time of 2\:20.65, winning the title in that discipline and securing that season's crystal globe on the giant slalom, the first of his career and the first ever for a Brazilian.[^cite_note-21]

## Personal life

Pinheiro Braathen speaks Norwegian, [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language), English, and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) fluently, is a fan of [Brazilian musical genres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Brazil) such as [bossa nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossa_nova), and is a supporter of [São Paulo Futebol Clube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_FC).[^cite_note-22] He currently has a home base established inside of Milan Italy, and he says "I owe it all to the winter Olympics for helping me find this new home." Part of his family resides in [Paulínia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul%C3%ADnia), northwest of the state of [São Paulo](229422.md), while his mother lives in New Zealand.[^cite_note-23]

In June 2025, he made his romantic relationship with Brazilian actress [Isadora Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isadora_Cruz) public.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]
