**Dipaculao** [\[ˌdiːpɐˈkuːlaʊ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Tagalog), officially the **Municipality of Dipaculao** ([Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Bayan ng Dipaculao*; [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language): *Ili ti Dipaculao*), is a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_of_the_Philippines) in the [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Province) of [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_\(province\)), [Philippines](23440.md). According to the 2024 census, it has a population of 33,597 people.[^cite_note-5]

The municipality is home to the [Dinadiawan River Protected Landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinadiawan_River_Protected_Landscape). This municipality is also notable for the location where both [Ateneo Blue Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ateneo_Blue_Eagles) [basketball players Chukwuemeka Divine Adili and Rene Clert Noja Baterbonia died from drowning](83420041.md) during a [team building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_building) activity at a beach resort there on June 8, 2026.[^cite_note-rb11-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-sunstar-firm-9][^cite_note-pep-correct-10][^cite_note-rb2-11][^cite_note-rb27-12]

## History

### Early history and etymology

The name "Dipaculao" was first documented in Spanish records in 1719, when the [Misión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_mission) de San Miguel de Dipaculao was established by the [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscans) priest Sebastián de la Madre de Dios.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-Andree-15] The [mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_mission) was aimed at converting the indigenous [Ilongot people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilongot_people) who lived on the preexisting settlement variously recorded as either "*Ipaculao*" or "*Dipaculao*." The mission was headed by a series of Franciscan missionaries originating from [Baler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baler,_Aurora), though they were all largely unsuccessful at converting the Ilongot and the nearby [Casiguran Dumagat Agta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casiguran_Dumagat_Agta).[^cite_note-Andree-15]

By 1870, the settlement was recorded as being depopulated and largely abandoned by the Ilongot, who had moved to *rancherías* (indigenous villages) closer to the Spanish settlement of Casignan (also spelled "*Casecnan*" or "*Casiguan*", now [Maria Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Aurora,_Aurora)) to grow rice and various crops for trade in local markets.[^cite_note-Andree-15] The few remaining inhabitants of the settlement that arrived with the Spanish are believed to have primarily consisted of [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people) migrants, since the mission was recorded as Ilocano-speaking in Spanish publications from as early as 1865 and 1869.[^cite_note-Andree-15][^cite_note-Apuntes-16]

The mission and the original native settlement was bounded by [Casiguran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casiguran,_Aurora) to the far north, Baler to the south, and Casignan to the northwest. It was situated on an island bordered by the saltwater Dipaculao River, which merges with the Casignan River (also known as the Bicanili and Cabatangan) to the north, and an unnamed river to the southeast of Baler.[^cite_note-de_Huerta-17][^cite_note-De_La_Cavada-18]

It is unknown if the settlement was named after the Dipaculao River or vice versa. The location of the Dipaculao River is also now unclear due to changes in river topography. It is likely the same river that now flows through Lobbot, Lipit, and Ipil. The prefix "*di-*" ("of the" or "coming from"), is a common prefix in the place names of the Ilongot people; the meaning of the other component is unknown.[^cite_note-19]

### American colonial period

In 1921, more migrants from [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) (mostly [Ilocanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people) from neighboring [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija), [La Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Union), and [Pangasinan](1691800.md)) settled in Dipaculao.[^cite_note-Dipaculao-20][^cite_note-Aurorapeople-21] [Igorots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot_people) from the [Cordilleras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central,_Luzon) also came down to live alongside Ilocanos and the aboriginal [Ilongot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilongot_people).[^cite_note-Aurorapeople-21][^cite_note-22] Other ethnic groups who came to and stayed in Dipaculao include Christianized [Gaddang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaddang_people) and [Isinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isinai_language) settlers who settled the surrounding lowlands of [Baler Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baler_Bay), Gaddang from Nueva Vizcaya and [Mountain Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Province), and Isinai from Nueva Vizcaya.[^cite_note-23] Later that year, the large strip of land where the municipality is located was returned to [Tayabas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tayabas_province) (present-day Quezon Province).[^cite_note-24]

### Modern era

On November 27, 1950, Dipaculao was converted from a *[barrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrio)* of [Baler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baler,_Aurora) into an independent municipality of [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon) via Executive Order No. 375 by [President](271126.md) [Elpidio Quirino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elpidio_Quirino), with 16 other *[barrios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrio)* of Baler were annexed to the new municipality.[^cite_note-25] Anacleto Mijares was the first Municipal Mayor, and oversaw the transition.[^cite_note-26] It later became part of Quezon's [sub-province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-provinces_of_the_Philippines) of Aurora beginning in 1951.[^cite_note-27]

On June 21, 1957, the *[sitios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitio)* of Dimabuno, Laboy, Dinadiawan and Puangi were converted into *barrio*s.[^cite_note-PhilippineLawInfo-RA1761-28][^cite_note-PhilippineLawInfo-RA1762-29][^cite_note-PhilippineLawInfo-RA1763-30][^cite_note-PhilippineLawInfo-RA1764-31] Several more *sitios* were converted into *barrios* on June 18, 1966.[^cite_note-ChanRobles-RA4809-32]

The municipality later separated from Quezon and became part of the new province of [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_\(province\)) on August 13, 1979.[^cite_note-ChanRobles-BatasPamb7-33]

## Geography

According to the [Philippine Statistics Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Statistics_Authority), the municipality has a land area of 361.64 square kilometers (139.63 sq mi) [^cite_note-nscb-area-34] constituting 11.49% of the 3,147.32-square-kilometer (1,215.19 sq mi) total area of Aurora.

Dipaculao is situated 12.54 kilometers (7.79 mi) from the provincial capital [Baler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baler,_Aurora), and 264.85 kilometers (164.57 mi) from the country's capital city of [Manila](184334.md).
