The ***Legal Consequences arising from the Policies and Practices of Israel in the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including East Jerusalem (Request for Advisory Opinion)*** was a proceeding before the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) (ICJ), the highest legal body of the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN), stemming from a resolution adopted by the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) (UNGA) in December 2022 requesting the Court to render an [advisory opinion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advisory_opinion) relating to the [legality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legality) of the [Israeli occupation](2781576.md) of the [Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories).[^cite_note-1]

[Israel](9282173.md) has occupied the Palestinian territories, which comprise the [West Bank](33209.md) (including [East Jerusalem](343508.md)) and the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), since 1967, making it the longest military occupation in modern history.[^cite_note-AIA-2] In 2004, the ICJ [delivered an advisory opinion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_Consequences_of_the_Construction_of_a_Wall_in_the_Occupied_Palestinian_Territory) on the [Israeli West Bank barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_West_Bank_barrier), deciding that it contravened international law and should be removed. In January 2023, the ICJ acknowledged the UNGA's request for an advisory opinion on the legal consequences arising from Israel's policies and practices in the occupied Palestinian territories.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

Public hearings opened on 19 February 2024 at the ICJ's seat in [The Hague](30269.md),[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] with 52 states and three international organizations presenting legal arguments—the largest number of parties to participate in any single case in the ICJ's history.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-AIA-2][^cite_note-AJ1-9][^cite_note-10] The court's advisory opinion was delivered on 19 July 2024, holding that the Palestinian territories constitute one political unit and that Israel's occupation since 1967, and the subsequent creation of [Israeli settlements](15123.md) and exploitation of natural resources, are illegal under international law. The court also held that Israel should pay full [reparations](29967971.md) to the [Palestinian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) for the damage the occupation has caused,[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] and held that its policies violate the [International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Convention_on_the_Elimination_of_All_Forms_of_Racial_Discrimination).[^cite_note-Haaretz-13]

The [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) welcomed the decision as historic,[^cite_note-ha1-14] while the Israeli government formally rejected it, stating a political settlement can only be attained through negotiations; Israeli leaders and politicians further decried the opinion as [antisemitic](1078.md).[^cite_note-ha1-14] The court's opinion was backed by the [European Union](9317.md)[^cite_note-mee1-15] but criticized by the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-\:0-16] On 18 September 2024, the UNGA adopted a resolution calling for the end, within a year, to Israel's "unlawful presence" in the West Bank and Gaza.[^cite_note-17]

## Background

A draft motion prepared by the [State of Palestine](241405.md) was approved by the [Special Political and Decolonization Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Fourth_Committee) (Fourth Committee) on 11 November 2022.[^cite_note-18] It was passed by a vote of 98 to 17, with 52 abstentions, and was sent to the [General Assembly](31957.md).[^cite_note-19] Nicaragua presented the draft resolution because Palestine is not a full member of the UN.[^cite_note-20]

On 30 December 2022, the UN General Assembly adopted resolution A/RES/77/247 with 87 votes in favor, 26 against, and 53 abstentions.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

On 20 January 2023, the ICJ confirmed "The request was transferred to the ICJ through a letter sent by the Secretary-General of the United Nations, Antonio Guterres, on January 17, and the request was registered yesterday, Thursday."[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

## UNGA resolution

Paragraph 18 of the resolution[^cite_note-26] requests the Court to render an advisory opinion on the following questions:

> (a) What are the legal consequences arising from the ongoing violation by Israel of the right of the Palestinian people to self-determination, from its prolonged occupation, settlement and annexation of the Palestinian territory occupied since 1967, including measures aimed at altering the demographic composition, character and status of the Holy City of Jerusalem, and from its adoption of related discriminatory legislation and measures?
>
> (b) How do the policies and practices of Israel referred to in paragraph 18 (a) above affect the legal status of the occupation, and what are the legal consequences that arise for all States and the United Nations from this status?

## Reactions to the UNGA resolution

[Riyad al-Maliki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyad_al-Maliki), [Minister of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates of the State of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_and_Expatriates_\(Palestine\)), hailed the resolution as "a diplomatic and legal success and achievement".[^cite_note-27] Al-Maliki expressed gratitude to the nations that supported the resolution by sponsoring it and voting in favor of it. He urged countries that did not support the resolution to "adhere to international law and avoid standing on the wrong side of history." Days prior to the final vote, in an attempt to impede the referral of the conflict to The Hague, then-Israeli Prime Minister [Yair Lapid](2448072.md) wrote to 50 countries urging them not to support it in the General Assembly.[^cite_note-28]

The [Organization of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md) (OIC) welcomed the resolution's approval by the General Assembly, "praising the positions of the countries that supported it, which confirms their commitment to international law and is consistent with their historical support for the Palestinian cause".[^cite_note-29]

## Submissions to the Court

The court fixed 25 July 2023 as the deadline for presentation of written statements and 25 October 2023 as the deadline for written comments on the statements made by other States or organizations.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

On 7 August the Court announced that 57 written statements had been filed in the Court's registry, including three from international organizations—[League of Arab States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Arab_States), [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md), and [African Union](10634933.md)—with the remainder from Palestine and UN member states.[^cite_note-32] According to *[Le Monde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Monde)*, 57 written submissions is a record (more than have been received on any other case) since the Court's creation in 1945, which the newspaper regards as already a small victory for the Palestinians.[^cite_note-Le-Monde-Sept-2023-33]

The written statements will not be published by the Court until the start of public hearings, but according to *Le Monde*, a large majority of the 57 submissions recognize the jurisdiction of the Court to render an opinion on the issues raised; only around 10 or so submissions contest the referral to the Court.[^cite_note-Le-Monde-Sept-2023-33]

On 14 November, the court announced that 15 comments on the written statements were accepted by the Court.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

### Canada

On 14 July 2023, the Government of Canada argued that the Court should exercise its discretion to decline to render an advisory opinion, because Israel has not accepted the jurisdiction of the Court in this matter, and the UN Security Council has established a framework for the parties to resolve the dispute through negotiations.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-Cotler-and-Kittrie-38]

### France

Although France abstained in the General Assembly on the resolution which requested the advisory opinion from the Court, France submitted a statement to the Court of around 20 pages, in which it reaffirmed the illegal nature of colonization, recounts the legal obligations of the occupier in the occupied territories, including East Jerusalem, and notes the risk of an annexation by fait accompli.[^cite_note-Le-Monde-Sept-2023-33]

### Israel

The Government of Israel reportedly made submissions to the Court arguing that the Court lacks the authority to adjudicate the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, and that it should instead be resolved by direct negotiations between Israel and Palestinian Authority.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

### Palestine

On 24 July 2023, during a meeting at the Hague, Palestine Minister of Foreign Affairs and Expatriates, Riyad Al-Malki, delivered the Palestinian submission.[^cite_note-41]

### United Kingdom

The UK submission opposed the hearing of the case in the ICJ.[^cite_note-42]

## Opinion

The ICJ delivered its opinion on 19 July 2024.[^cite_note-ICJ2024Opinion-76]

### Jurisdiction and discretion

The ICJ is the principal judicial organ of the United Nations. Article 96(1) of the [Charter of the United Nations](88805.md) authorizes the General Assembly to request an advisory opinion from the ICJ on any legal question, and Article 65(1) of the [Statute of the International Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_the_International_Court_of_Justice) authorizes the Court to give one. The Court found that the questions submitted by the General Assembly were legal questions and that it had jurisdiction. It declined to treat Israel's lack of consent as a reason not to give the opinion, finding that the request did not concern only a bilateral dispute and addressed a matter of particular concern to the United Nations.[^cite_note-ICJ2024Jurisdiction-77]

The Court separately considered whether it should exercise its discretion to decline to give the opinion. It found no compelling reason to do so, rejecting objections concerning Israel's lack of consent, the existing negotiation framework, the framing of the questions, and the adequacy of the factual record.[^cite_note-ICJ2024Discretion-78][^cite_note-ICJAdvisoryJurisdiction-79]

### Principal conclusions

By a majority of eleven to four, the Court found that Israel's continued presence in the [occupied Palestinian territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories) was unlawful. It declared that Israel was required to end that presence "as rapidly as possible", immediately cease all new settlement activity, evacuate all settlers, and make reparation for the damage caused by its internationally wrongful acts.[^cite_note-ICJ2024Opinion-76][^cite_note-80]

In relation to the prohibition on racial segregation and apartheid in the [International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Convention_on_the_Elimination_of_All_Forms_of_Racial_Discrimination) (CERD), the Court found that Israeli laws "implement a separation" between Palestinians and settlers in the occupied territories in physical and juridical senses, "breach\[ing]" Article 3 of CERD.[^cite_note-Amnesty_ICJ_vindication-81][^cite_note-ICJ_pdf-82]<sup>: 65 </sup> It also found that all states, the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md), and the [Security Council](31956.md) were under obligations not to recognize as legal the situation arising from Israel's unlawful presence and not to render aid or assistance in maintaining it.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

The Court identified breaches of international law including [forcible evictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_displacement_in_East_Jerusalem), [pervasive house demolitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_demolition_of_Palestinian_property), [restrictions on residence and movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_freedom_of_movement), [the transfer and retention of settlers into East Jerusalem and the West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settler_colonialism), the failure to protect Palestinians from [settler violence](47246246.md), [restrictions on access to water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_supply_and_sanitation_in_the_State_of_Palestine), the [extension of Israeli law into the West Bank and East Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_law_in_the_West_Bank_settlements), and [the exploitation of natural resources](59076253.md#Resource_extraction).[^cite_note-Siddique-85] Although it addressed the Israeli [separation policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafrada) and distinguishing segregation from apartheid, it did not find that the policy constituted apartheid.[^cite_note-Haaretz-13]

The Court wrote:[^cite_note-ICJ_pdf-82]

> The Court considers that the violations by Israel of the prohibition of the acquisition of territory by force and of the Palestinian people's right to self-determination have a direct impact on the legality of the continued presence of Israel, as an occupying Power, in the Occupied Palestinian Territory. The sustained abuse by Israel of its position as an occupying Power, through annexation and an assertion of permanent control over the Occupied Palestinian Territory and continued frustration of the right of the Palestinian people to self-determination, violates fundamental principles of international law and renders Israel's presence in the Occupied Palestinian Territory unlawful.

The Court found that the [Israeli–Palestinian peace process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_peace_process) did not prevent international law from applying to the situation.[^cite_note-Guardian_why_ICJ_hard_ignore-86] It declared that the General Assembly and Security Council should consider the measures required to end Israel's unlawful presence, and that all states were required to cooperate with the United Nations to put the identified obligations into effect.[^cite_note-Reuters_top_UN_court_says_illegal-87]

### Dissents

Vice-President [Julia Sebutinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Sebutinde) dissented from the principal conclusions. She would have declined to give the opinion because she considered the record inadequate and one-sided, the proceeding an improper circumvention of Israel's lack of consent and the existing negotiation framework, and the direction that Israel end its presence "as rapidly as possible" impracticable without attention to security guarantees and competing sovereignty claims. She also maintained that full reparation was inappropriate and that the Court had misapplied the law of occupation.[^cite_note-Sebutinde2024-88]

Judges [Peter Tomka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Tomka), [Ronny Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronny_Abraham), and [Bogdan Aurescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogdan_Aurescu) dissented from the conclusion that Israel's continued presence had become unlawful. They argued that illegal annexation and violations of the law of occupation did not make the occupation itself unlawful. They also maintained that the opinion improperly included Gaza and failed to account sufficiently for the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords) and the rights of Israel and Palestine to security.[^cite_note-TomkaAbrahamAurescu2024-89]

## Implementation by the United Nations

### General Assembly

The UN confirmed that the matter would be submitted to the General Assembly to determine what action would follow.[^cite_note-90]

On 18 September 2024, the General Assembly adopted a non-binding resolution demanding that Israel end its unlawful presence in the occupied Palestinian territory within 12 months and comply with the obligations identified in the advisory opinion. The resolution passed by 124 votes to 14, with 43 abstentions.[^cite_note-\:2-91][^cite_note-92]

The resolution also called on member states to stop importing products from Israeli settlements in the occupied Palestinian territory and to stop supplying arms, munitions, and related equipment to Israel "in all cases where there are reasonable grounds to suspect that they may be used in the Occupied Palestinian Territory".[^cite_note-\:2-91][^cite_note-\:4-93][^cite_note-94]

The resolution directed Switzerland to convene a Conference of High Contracting Parties to the Fourth Geneva Convention concerning measures to enforce the Convention in the occupied Palestinian territory, including East Jerusalem, within six months.[^cite_note-\:3-95][^cite_note-\:4-93]

### United Nations Security Council

Members of the Security Council discussed the opinion and called on the Council to consider measures for its implementation.[^cite_note-UNSCDiscussion2024-96] The Council did not agree on an implementation measure; a 2025 General Assembly committee report described the relevant Council discussions as "deadlocked".[^cite_note-UNSCDeadlock2025-97]

## Reactions

### Political responses in Israel

Israeli leaders and politicians decried the ICJ opinion as [antisemitic](1078.md):[^cite_note-ha1-14]

- [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) called the opinion "a decision of lies", since "the Jewish people are not occupiers in their own land."

  [^cite_note-Berg-98]

- Knesset MP [Yuli Edelstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuli_Edelstein) stated that the International Court of Justice has been "hijacked by Islamists and their supporters".

  [^cite_note-JP-99]

- National Security Minister [Itamar Ben-Gvir](54504115.md) stated that the decision "proves for the umpteenth time that this is a distinctly antisemitic and political organization."

  [^cite_note-Ynet-100]

- The Israeli Minister for Finance [Bezalel Smotrich](46196834.md) called for an immediate annexation of the West Bank, stating: "The answer to The Hague – sovereignty now."

  [^cite_note-Ynet-100]

- [Benny Gantz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Gantz) stated that the decision was a testament to outside meddling that was "counterproductive to regional security" but also further evidence for the 'judicialization' of a political conflict.

  [^cite_note-101]

- [Avigdor Lieberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avigdor_Lieberman) stated that the judgment was an "antisemitic act," and also accused the court of "launching a war" against Israel, which is "already fighting six others."

  [^cite_note-Shpigel-102]

- [Yariv Levin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yariv_Levin), Israel's [Deputy Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_of_the_Prime_Minister_of_Israel), stated: "No lies \[by the World Court] will change the simple fact: the Land of Israel belongs to the people of Israel."

  [^cite_note-Shpigel-102]

- [Israel Katz](19041378.md), Israel's Foreign Minister, stated that the judgment was 'dangerous' in that it plays into the hands of the extremists and "encourages the Palestinian Authority to continue its path of slander."

  [^cite_note-Shpigel-102]

- [Yair Lapid](2448072.md), the opposition leader, stated that the decision was "disconnected, one-sided, tainted by antisemitism," and "serves only Islamic terrorism."

  [^cite_note-Shpigel-102]

- [Ahmad Tibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Tibi) stated that the verdict "accurately captured reality, determining that the state of segregation and the application of Israeli law in the occupied territories is apartheid."

  [^cite_note-Shpigel-102]

- The Israeli human rights NGO [B'tselem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B'tselem) declared, "No more excuses. The international community must force Israel to end the occupation."

  [^cite_note-103]

### Political responses in Palestine

- The office of Palestinian President [Mahmoud Abbas](232595.md) welcomed the opinion and called the decision historic.

  [^cite_note-\:0-16]

- Secretary General of the Executive Committee of the Palestine Liberation Organization [Hussein al-Sheikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_al-Sheikh) hailed the verdict as a victory for the Palestinians' right to self-determination, and considered that the judgment amounted to the "collapse and defeat of the Judaization project through confiscation, settlement, displacement, and racist practices against a people under occupation."

  [^cite_note-Berg-98]

### Political responses in the US

- The [United States Department of State](31975.md) criticized the ICJ opinion, claiming that it would make future negotiations for a Palestinian state more difficult.

  [^cite_note-104]

### Political responses in the UK

- The newly formed Labour government said it "respects the independence of the ICJ" and is considering the opinion before making an official response. The Foreign Office added the UK is "strongly opposed to the expansion of illegal settlements and rising settler violence".

  [^cite_note-\:1-105]

### Political responses in the EU

- The [European Union](9317.md) backed the ICJ's advisory opinion and stated that the opinion aligns with the bloc's own positions, with its foreign policy chief [Josep Borrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josep_Borrell) saying the opinion would be analyzed on its implication on the EU's policy.

  [^cite_note-mee1-15]

- [Belgium](3343.md) Minister of Foreign Affairs, [Hadja Lahbib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadja_Lahbib) said in a post on X: "The International Court of Justice has ruled that Israel's occupation of the Palestinian territory does not comply with international law and that settlement activity must cease. Belgium will always stand up for the respect of international law."

  [^cite_note-\:1-105]

- [Spain](26667.md) said the opinion "includes important pronouncements … on the illegality of the Israeli occupation of the Palestinian territories and on settlements, among other aspects" and that "The government urges the UN and the international community to take into consideration the conclusions of the report and to adopt appropriate measures in this regard".

  [^cite_note-\:1-105]

  [^cite_note-106]

- The Ministry of Foreign Affairs of [Slovenia](27338.md) called on Israel "to comply with its duties and obligations under international law" as laid out in the ICJ's opinion.

  [^cite_note-\:1-105]

- [Norway](21241.md) Minister of Foreign Affairs, [Espen Barth Eide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espen_Barth_Eide), said in a post on X: "The ICJ is crystal clear: Israeli policies and practices are to be considered annexation of large parts of the Occupied Palestinian Territory, and are in violation of international law. This is an unequivocal message to Israel, which we expect them to comply with immediately."

  [^cite_note-\:1-105]

### Political responses in Africa

- [South Africa](17416221.md) Minister of International Relations and Cooperation [Ronald Lamola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Lamola) said the opinion "affirms South Africa's long-standing position that the occupation by Israel of Palestinian territory remains unlawful under international law" and that "There is now an additional legal obligation for all states to end complicity in Israel's illegal actions and to act to ensure respect for international law," he said in a statement.

  [^cite_note-\:1-105]

### Analyses

- [Daniel Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Levy_\(political_analyst\)), a political analyst, stated the ICJ case would cause third parties to reckon with the consequences of being "complicit in this violation of international law and in guaranteeing impunity for the violating party, namely Israel."

  [^cite_note-107]

- [Ynet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ynet) journalist Itamar Eichner stated that though the ICJ decision represented the worst-case scenario Israeli politicians feared, the very 'brutality' of the opinion may act in Israel's favour. In his view, it is likely that Israeli leaders are hoping that [Donald Trump](4848272.md) will win the U.S. elections, and then pass laws against the ICJ and ICC, and impose sanctions on the judges responsible for these verdicts.

  [^cite_note-108]
