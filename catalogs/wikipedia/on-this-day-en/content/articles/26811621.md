**Oregon** ([/ˈɒrɪɡən, -ɡɒn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e1/En-us-Oregon.ogg/En-us-Oregon.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e1/En-us-Oregon.ogg/En-us-Oregon.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*ORR-ih-ghən*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), [*-⁠gon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key))[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] is a [state](18618239.md) in the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest) region of the United States. It is a part of the [Western United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States), with the [Columbia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River) delineating much of Oregon's northern boundary with [Washington](13015878.md), while the [Snake River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_River) delineates much of its eastern boundary with [Idaho](14607.md). The [42° north parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_parallel_north) delineates the southern boundary with [California](5407.md) and [Nevada](21216.md). The western boundary is formed by the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md).

Oregon has been home to many [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) nations for thousands of years. The first European traders, explorers, and settlers began exploring what is now Oregon's Pacific coast in the early to mid-16th century. As early as 1564, the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_expeditions_to_the_Pacific_Northwest) began sending vessels northeast from the [Philippines](23440.md), riding the [Kuroshio Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuroshio_Current) in a sweeping circular route across the northern part of the Pacific. In 1592, [Juan de Fuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Fuca) undertook detailed mapping and studies of ocean currents in the Pacific Northwest, including the Oregon coast as well as the [strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Juan_de_Fuca) now bearing his name. The [Lewis and Clark Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition) traversed Oregon in the early 19th century, and the first permanent European settlements in Oregon were established soon afterward by [trappers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapping) and [fur traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade). The United States received joint occupation rights to the region from the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) through the [Treaty of 1818](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_1818). The [Oregon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Treaty) of 1846 formally brought Oregon under American sovereignty, and the [Oregon Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Territory) was created two years later. Oregon was admitted to the United States on February 14, 1859, becoming the 33rd state.

Today, with 4.2 million people over 98,000 square miles (250,000 km<sup>2</sup>), Oregon is the [ninth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area) and [27th-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population) U.S. state. The capital, [Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem,_Oregon), is the third-most populous city in Oregon, with 175,535 residents.[^cite_note-Census2021Salem-11] [Portland](23503.md), with 652,503, ranks as the 26th among U.S. cities. The [Portland metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_metropolitan_area), which includes neighboring counties in Washington, is the 26th largest [metro area in the nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area), with a population of 2,512,859. Oregon is also one of the most geographically diverse states in the U.S.,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJewellMcRae20144-12] marked by volcanoes, abundant bodies of water, dense evergreen and mixed forests, as well as [high deserts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Desert_\(Oregon\)) and semi-arid [shrublands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrubland). At 11,249 feet (3,429 m), [Mount Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hood) is the state's highest point. Oregon's only national park, [Crater Lake National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crater_Lake_National_Park), comprises the [caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera) surrounding [Crater Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crater_Lake), the deepest lake in the U.S. The state is also home to the single [largest organism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_organisms#Fungi) in the world, *[Armillaria ostoyae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armillaria_ostoyae)*, a fungus that runs beneath 2,200 acres (8.9 km<sup>2</sup>) of the [Malheur National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malheur_National_Forest).[^cite_note-ABCfungus-13]

Oregon's economy has historically been powered by various forms of agriculture, fishing, logging, and [hydroelectric](381399.md) power. Oregon is the top [lumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumber) producer of the [contiguous U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contiguous_U.S.), with the lumber industry dominating the state's economy during the 20th century.[^cite_note-Forest_Land_Protection_Program-14] Technology is another one of Oregon's major economic forces, beginning in the 1970s with the establishment of the [Silicon Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Forest) and the expansion of [Tektronix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tektronix) and [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel). Sportswear company [Nike, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.), headquartered in [Beaverton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaverton,_Oregon), is the state's largest public corporation with an annual revenue of \$46.7 billion.[^cite_note-15]

## Etymology

Main article: [Etymology of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etymology_of_Oregon)



An Oregon border [welcome sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_sign) at [Denio, Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denio,_Nevada)

The name *Oregon* is of obscure origin. It appeared for the first time in 1765, in a petition written by [Robert Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Rogers_\(British_Army_officer\)), a [Massachusetts](1645518.md)-born officer in the British Army. The petition, addressed to [George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_III), sought royal patronage for an expedition to "the River called by the Indians Ouragon", which supposedly lay in the far west of North America. In subsequent writings, Rogers used the spellings *Ourigan*, *Ourgan*, and *Ouregan*.[^cite_note-goddard-16] It is not clear where Rogers drew the name from. One theory is that it came from a misreading of [Baron de Lahontan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis-Armand_de_Lom_d'Arce_de_Lahontan,_Baron_de_Lahontan)'s map of North America, which labels the [Wisconsin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_River) as the *Ouaricon-sint* (a corrupt form of *Ouisconsing*, the French name for the river).[^cite_note-17] Another is that it was coined by Rogers himself using an [Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_languages) word meaning "good" or "beautiful" (cf. [Wauregan, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wauregan,_Connecticut), which takes its name from the same word).[^cite_note-goddard-16] Theories tracing the name to the Spanish words *orégano* and *oreja*, meaning "[marjoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_marjoram)" and "ear" respectively, are regarded as implausible.[^cite_note-18]

The first work to use the modern spelling was [Jonathan Carver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Carver)'s *Travels Through America* (1778), which identifies the "Oregon or \[...] River of the West" as one of the "four great rivers" of North America. Carver's source for this information was evidently Rogers, with whom he was personally acquainted.[^cite_note-19] The "River of the West" would subsequently come to be known as the [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River), leaving the name *Oregon* to fall into obscurity. It was revived by the poet [William Cullen Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Cullen_Bryant), who used it in his celebrated work "[Thanatopsis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanatopsis)" (1817). The first person to apply the name to a territory rather than a river was [Hall J. Kelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hall_J._Kelley), a prominent advocate of the United States' claim to the disputed Columbia region, which he referred to as the "[Oregon Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Country)". Finally, in 1848, that part of the region lying south of the [49th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_parallel_north) was organized as the "[Territory of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Oregon)", receiving statehood in 1859.[^cite_note-20]

## History

Main article: [History of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Oregon)

### Earliest inhabitants

See also: [Native American peoples of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_peoples_of_Oregon) and [Kennewick Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennewick_Man)



Paul Shoaway of the [Umatilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umatilla_people) tribe, 1899

While there is considerable evidence that [Paleo-Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians) inhabited the region, the oldest evidence of habitation in Oregon was found at [Fort Rock Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Rock_Cave) and the [Paisley Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paisley_Caves) in [Lake County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_County,_Oregon). Archaeologist [Luther Cressman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luther_Cressman) dated material from Fort Rock to 13,200 years ago,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobbins2005-21] and there is evidence supporting inhabitants in the region at least 15,000 years ago.[^cite_note-22] By 8000 BC, there were settlements throughout the state, with populations concentrated along the lower Columbia River, in the western valleys, and around coastal estuaries.

During the [prehistoric period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_period), the [Willamette Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willamette_Valley) region was flooded after the collapse of glacial dams from [Lake Missoula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Missoula), located in what is now [Montana](19978.md). These massive floods occurred during the [last glacial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_glacial_period) and filled the valley with 300 to 400 feet (91 to 122 m) of water.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllenBurnsSargent2009175–189-23]

By the 16th century, Oregon was home to many Native American groups, including the [Chinook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinookan_peoples), [Coquille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coquille_people) (Ko-Kwell), [Bannock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannock_people), [Kalapuya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalapuya), [Klamath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klamath_people), [Klickitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klickitat_people), [Molala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molala), [Nez Perce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nez_Perce), [Shasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shasta_people), [Takelma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takelma), [Umatilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umatilla_people), and [Umpqua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umpqua_people).[^cite_note-BBGreatBasin-24][^cite_note-BBNWCoast-25][^cite_note-BBColumbiaPlateau-26][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarey192247-27]

### European and pioneer settlement

Main articles: [Oregon Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Country), [Oregon pioneer history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_pioneer_history), [Columbia District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_District), [Provisional Government of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Government_of_Oregon), [Organic act § List of organic acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_act#List_of_organic_acts), and [Spanish expeditions to the Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_expeditions_to_the_Pacific_Northwest)

The first Europeans to visit Oregon were Spanish explorers led by [Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Cabrillo), who sighted southern Oregon off the Pacific coast in 1543.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHemming2008140–141-28] Sailing from Central America on the *[Golden Hind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Hind)* in 1579 in search of the [Strait of Anian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Anian) during [his circumnavigation of the Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Drake's_circumnavigation), the English explorer and privateer Sir [Francis Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Drake) briefly anchored at [South Cove, Cape Arago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Arago_State_Park), just south of [Coos Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coos_Bay,_Oregon), before sailing for what is now California.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson190439-30] [Martín de Aguilar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mart%C3%ADn_de_Aguilar), continuing separately from [Sebastián Vizcaíno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Vizca%C3%ADno)'s scouting of California, reached as far north as [Cape Blanco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Blanco_\(Oregon\)) and possibly to Coos Bay in 1603.[^cite_note-Cogswell-31][^cite_note-OE-32] Exploration continued routinely in 1774, starting with the expedition of the frigate *Santiago* by [Juan José Pérez Hernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Jos%C3%A9_P%C3%A9rez_Hern%C3%A1ndez), and the coast of Oregon became a valuable trade route to Asia. In 1778, British captain [James Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cook) also explored the coast.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson190464–65-33]

[French Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Canadians), [Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_people), [Métis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tis), and other continental natives (e.g. [Iroquois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iroquois)) trappers arrived in the late 18th and early 19th centuries, soon to be followed by Catholic clergy. Some traveled as members of the [Lewis and Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition) and Astor Expeditions. Few stayed permanently such as Étienne Lussier, often referred to as the first "European" farmer in the state of Oregon. Evidence of the French Canadian presence can be found in numerous names of French origin such as [Malheur Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malheur_Lake), the [Malheur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malheur_River), [Grande Ronde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Ronde_River), and [Deschutes Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deschutes_River_\(Oregon\)), and the city of [La Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Grande,_Oregon). Furthermore, many of the early pioneers first came out West with the [North West Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Company) and the [Hudson's Bay Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_Company) before heading South of the Columbia for better farmland as the fur trade declined. [French Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Prairie) by the [Willamette River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willamette_River) and [French Settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Settlement,_Oregon) by the [Umpqua River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umpqua_River) are known as early mixed ancestry settlements.



[Fort Astoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Astoria), as established by [John Jacob Astor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Jacob_Astor) in 1813

The Lewis and Clark Expedition traveled through northern Oregon also in search of the [Northwest Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Passage). They built their winter fort in 1805–1806 at [Fort Clatsop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Clatsop), near the mouth of the Columbia River, staying at the encampment from December until March.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAmbrose1997326-34]

British explorer [David Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Thompson_\(explorer\)) also conducted overland exploration. In 1811, while working for the North West Company, Thompson became the first European to navigate the entire Columbia River.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson1904145–146-35] Stopping on the way, at the junction of the Snake River, he posted a claim to the region for [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) and the North West Company. Upon returning to [Montreal](7954681.md), he publicized the abundance of fur-bearing animals in the area.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson1904146-36]

Also in 1811, New Yorker [John Jacob Astor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Jacob_Astor) financed the establishment of [Fort Astoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Astoria) at the mouth of the Columbia River as a western outpost to his [Pacific Fur Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Fur_Company);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELoyAllanBuckleyMeacham200112–13-37] this was the first permanent European settlement in Oregon.

In the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812), the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) gained control of all Pacific Fur Company posts. The [Treaty of 1818](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_1818) established joint British and American occupancy of the region west of the Rocky Mountains to the Pacific Ocean. By the 1820s and 1830s, the Hudson's Bay Company dominated the Pacific Northwest from its [Columbia District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_District) headquarters at [Fort Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Vancouver) (built in 1825 by the district's chief factor, [John McLoughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McLoughlin), across the Columbia from present-day [Portland](23503.md)).

In 1841, the expert trapper and entrepreneur [Ewing Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewing_Young) died leaving considerable wealth and no apparent heir, and no system to [probate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probate) his estate. A meeting followed Young's funeral, at which a probate government was proposed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson1904221-38] Doctor [Ira Babcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ira_Babcock) of [Jason Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Lee_\(missionary\))'s [Methodist Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_Mission) was elected supreme judge.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson1904207-39] Babcock chaired two meetings in 1842 at [Champoeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champoeg,_Oregon), (halfway between Lee's mission and [Oregon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_City)), to discuss [wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_wolf) and other animals of contemporary concern. These meetings were precursors to an [all-citizen meeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champoeg_Meetings) in 1843, which instituted a provisional government headed by an [executive committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Committee_\(Oregon_Country\)) made up of [David Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hill_\(Oregon_politician\)), [Alanson Beers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alanson_Beers), and [Joseph Gale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Gale).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson1904226-40] This government was the first acting public government of the [Oregon Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Country) before annexation by the government of the United States. It was succeeded by a Second Executive Committee, made up of [Peter G. Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_G._Stewart), [Osborne Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osborne_Russell), and [William J. Bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_J._Bailey), and this committee was itself succeeded by [George Abernethy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Abernethy), who was the first and only Governor of Oregon under the provisional government.

Also in 1841, Sir [George Simpson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Simpson_\(HBC_administrator\)), governor of the Hudson's Bay Company, reversed the Hudson's Bay Company's long-standing policy of discouraging settlement because it interfered with the lucrative fur trade.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson1904215-41] He directed that some 200 [Red River Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_River_Colony) settlers be relocated to HBC farms near Fort Vancouver, (the [James Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Sinclair_\(fur_trapper\)) expedition), in an attempt to hold Columbia District.

Starting in 1842–1843, the [Oregon Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Trail) brought many new American settlers to the Oregon Country. [Oregon's boundaries were disputed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_boundary_dispute) for a time, contributing to tensions between the U.K. and the U.S., but the border was defined peacefully in the 1846 [Oregon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Treaty). The border between the U.S. and [British North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_North_America) was set at the [49th parallel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_parallel_north).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson1904273-42] The [Oregon Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Territory) was officially organized on August 13, 1848.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson1904285-43]

Settlement increased with the [Donation Land Claim Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donation_Land_Claim_Act) of 1850 and the [forced relocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_transfer) of the native population to [Indian reservations in Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_reservations_in_Oregon).

The first Oregon proposition for a railroad in Oregon was made in 1850 by H. M. Knighton, the original owner of the townsite of [St. Helens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Helens,_Oregon). Knighton asserted that this would fulfill his township's belief that it should be the supreme metropolitan seaport in that area upon the Columbia River, as opposed to Portland. He suggested building a railroad in 1851 from St. Helens, through the Cornelius pass and across [Washington County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_County,_Oregon) to the city of Lafayette, which was at the time the big town of the Willamette Valley.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

### Black exclusion laws

In December 1844, Oregon passed its first [black exclusion law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_black_exclusion_laws), which prohibited [African Americans](2154.md) from entering the territory while simultaneously prohibiting [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States). Slave owners who brought their slaves with them were given three years before they were forced to free them. Any African Americans in the region after the law was passed were forced to leave, and those who did not comply were arrested and beaten. They received no less than twenty and no more than thirty-nine stripes across the back if they still did not leave. This process could be repeated every six months.[^cite_note-46]

### Statehood

Slavery played a major part in Oregon's history and even influenced its path to statehood. The territory's request for [statehood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)) was delayed several times, as members of Congress argued among themselves whether the territory should be admitted as a "free" or "slave" state. Eventually politicians from the South agreed to allow Oregon to enter as a "free" state, in exchange for opening slavery to the Southwestern U.S.[^cite_note-47]

Oregon was admitted to the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)) on February 14, 1859, though no one in Oregon knew it until March 15.[^cite_note-Brother_Jonathan_(ship)-48] Founded as a refuge from disputes over slavery, Oregon had a "whites only" clause in its original state Constitution.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcLagan198028-49][^cite_note-50] At the outbreak of the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), regular U.S. troops were withdrawn and sent east [to aid the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_in_the_American_Civil_War). Volunteer cavalry recruited in California were sent north to Oregon to keep peace and protect the populace. The [First Oregon Cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Oregon_Cavalry) served until June 1865.

### Post-Reconstruction

In the 1880s, the growth of railroads expanded the state's [lumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumber), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), and other agricultural markets, and the rapid growth of its cities.[^cite_note-engemanohs-51] Due to the abundance of timber and waterway access via the Willamette River, Portland became a major force in the lumber industry of the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest), and quickly became the state's largest city. It would earn the nickname "Stumptown",[^cite_note-52] and would later become recognized as one of the most dangerous port cities in the United States due to racketeering and illegal activities at the turn of the 20th century.[^cite_note-kennedy-53] In 1902, Oregon introduced [direct legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_Legislation_League) by the state's citizens through [initiatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Initiatives) and [referendums](51582.md), known as the [Oregon System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_Legislation_League).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEvans1966156-54]

On May 5, 1945, six civilians were killed by a [Japanese balloon bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu-Go_balloon_bomb) that exploded on [Gearhart Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gearhart_Mountain) near [Bly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bly,_Oregon).[^cite_note-findingdulcinea.com-55][^cite_note-Mitchell_Monument-56] They remained the only people on American soil whose deaths were attributed to an enemy balloon bomb explosion during [World War II](32927.md). The bombing site is now located in the [Mitchell Recreation Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchell_Recreation_Area).

Industrial expansion began in earnest following the 1933–1937 construction of the [Bonneville Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonneville_Dam) on the Columbia River. [Hydroelectric power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_power), food, and lumber provided by Oregon helped fuel the development of the West, although the periodic fluctuations in the U.S. building industry have hurt the state's economy on multiple occasions. Portland, in particular, experienced a population boom between 1900 and 1930, tripling in size; the arrival of World War II also provided the northwest region of the state with an industrial boom, where [Liberty ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_ship) and aircraft carriers were constructed.[^cite_note-ohs2003toll-57]

During the 1970s, the Pacific Northwest was particularly affected by the [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis), with Oregon suffering a substantial shortage.[^cite_note-58]

In 1971, the Oregon Beverage Container Act of 1971,[^cite_note-59] popularly called the Bottle Bill, became the first law of its kind in the United States. The Bottle Bill system in Oregon was created to control litter. In practice, the system promotes recycling, not reusing, and the collected containers are generally destroyed and made into new containers. Ten states[^cite_note-60] currently have similar laws.

In 1994, Oregon became the first U.S. state to legalize [physician-assisted suicide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_suicide) through the [Oregon Death with Dignity Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Death_with_Dignity_Act). A measure to legalize recreational use of [marijuana](1481886.md) in Oregon was approved on November 4, 2014, making Oregon only the second state at the time to have legalized [gay marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_marriage), physician-assisted suicide, and recreational marijuana.[^cite_note-61]

### Gasoline pump law

Self service gasoline was banned in Oregon from 1951 until August 2023.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] Although self-serve is now allowed in Oregon, gas stations are not required to offer it and many currently do not.[^cite_note-64]

[New Jersey](21648.md) is the only state remaining where self serve gas stations are not allowed.[^cite_note-65]

## Geography

See also: [List of regions of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regions_of_Oregon)



[Crater Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crater_Lake)

Oregon is 295 miles (475 km) north to south at longest distance, and 395 miles (636 km) east to west. With an area of 98,381 square miles (254,810 km<sup>2</sup>), Oregon is slightly larger than the [United Kingdom](31717.md). It is the ninth largest state in the U.S.[^cite_note-66] Oregon's highest point is the summit of [Mount Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hood), at 11,249 feet (3,429 m), and its lowest point is the sea level of the Pacific Ocean along the Oregon Coast.[^cite_note-usgs-67] Oregon's mean elevation is 3,300 feet (1,006 m). [Crater Lake National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crater_Lake_National_Park), the state's only national park, is the site of the deepest lake in the U.S. at 1,943 feet (592 m).[^cite_note-68]

Oregon claims the [D River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D_River) as the shortest river in the world,[^cite_note-driver-69] though the state of Montana makes the same claim of its [Roe River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe_River).[^cite_note-roeriver-70] Oregon is split into eight geographical regions. In [Western Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Oregon): [Oregon Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Coast) (west of the [Coast Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Coast_Range)), the Willamette Valley, [Rogue Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_Valley), [Cascade Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_Range) and [Klamath Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klamath_Mountains); and in [Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Oregon) and [Eastern Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Oregon): the [Columbia Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Plateau), the [High Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Desert_\(Oregon\)), and the [Blue Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Mountains_\(ecoregion\)).

Oregon lies in two [time zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_zone). Most of [Malheur County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malheur_County,_Oregon) is in the [Mountain Time Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Time_Zone), while the rest of the state lies in the [Pacific Time Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Time_Zone).

### Geology and terrain

See also: [Geology of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_Oregon), [List of rivers in Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_in_Oregon), [List of Oregon mountain ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Oregon_mountain_ranges), and [List of Oregon state parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Oregon_state_parks)



[Mount Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hood) is the highest peak in Oregon.

Western Oregon's mountainous regions, home to three of the [most prominent mountain peaks of the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_most_prominent_summits_of_the_United_States) including Mount Hood, were formed by the [volcanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic) activity of the [Juan de Fuca Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_de_Fuca_Plate), a [tectonic plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics) that poses a continued threat of volcanic activity and earthquakes in the region. The most recent major activity was the [1700 Cascadia earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1700_Cascadia_earthquake).[^cite_note-71] [Washington](13015878.md)'s [Mount St. Helens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_St._Helens) [erupted in 1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_eruption_of_Mount_St._Helens), an event visible from northern Oregon and affecting some areas there.[^cite_note-72]

The Columbia River, which forms much of Oregon's northern border, also played a major role in the region's geological evolution, as well as its economic and cultural development. The Columbia is one of North America's largest rivers, and one of two rivers to cut through the [Cascades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_Range). The [Klamath River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klamath_River) in southern Oregon is the other. About 15,000 years ago, the Columbia repeatedly flooded much of Oregon during the [Missoula Floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missoula_Floods). The modern fertility of the Willamette Valley is largely the result. Plentiful [salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon) made parts of the river, such as [Celilo Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celilo_Falls), hubs of economic activity for thousands of years.

Today, Oregon's landscape varies from [rain forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_forest) in the Coast Range to barren desert in the southeast, which still meets the technical definition of a [frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier). Oregon's [geographical center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centroid) is further west than any of the other 48 contiguous states, although the westernmost point of the lower 48 states is in Washington. [Central Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Oregon)'s geographical features range from [high desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Desert_\(Oregon\)) and volcanic rock formations resulting from [lava beds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava_bed). The [Oregon Badlands Wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Badlands_Wilderness) is in this region of the state.[^cite_note-73]

### Flora and fauna

Main article: [Fauna of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_of_Oregon)

Typical of a western state, Oregon is home to a unique and diverse array of wildlife. Roughly 60 percent of Oregon is covered in forest.[^cite_note-forestfacts-74] The areas west of the Cascades are more densely populated by forest, making up around 80 percent of the landscape. Some 60 percent of Oregon's forests are within federal land.[^cite_note-forestfacts-74] Oregon is the top timber producer of the lower 48 states.[^cite_note-Forest_Land_Protection_Program-14][^cite_note-Oregon_is_top_timber_producer_in_worst_year-75]

  [^cite_note-Oregon_Wildlife_Species-80]

[Moose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moose) have not always inhabited the state but came to Oregon in the 1960s; the [Wallowa Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallowa_Valley) herd numbered about 60 as of 2013.[^cite_note-Oregon's_only_moose_herd_thriving-81] [Gray wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_wolves) were extirpated from Oregon around 1930 but have since found their way back; most reside in northeast Oregon, with two packs living in the south-central part.[^cite_note-Wolves_in_Oregon-82] Although their existence in Oregon is unconfirmed, reports of [grizzly bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grizzly_bear) still turn up, and it is probable some still move into eastern Oregon from Idaho.[^cite_note-Moose_enter_Oregon,_so_are_grizzlies_next?-83]

Oregon is home to what is considered the largest single organism in the world, an *[Armillaria solidipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armillaria_solidipes)* [fungus](19178965.md) beneath the [Malheur National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malheur_National_Forest) of eastern Oregon.[^cite_note-ABCfungus-13]

Oregon has several [National Park System sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_areas_in_the_United_States_National_Park_System), including [Crater Lake National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crater_Lake_National_Park) in the southern part of the Cascades, [John Day Fossil Beds National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Day_Fossil_Beds_National_Monument) east of the Cascades, [Lewis and Clark National Historical Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_National_Historical_Park) on the north coast, and [Oregon Caves National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Caves_National_Monument) near the south coast. Other areas that were considered for potential national park status in the 20th century include the southern Oregon Coast, Mount Hood, and Hells Canyon to the east.[^cite_note-84]

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Oregon)



[Köppen climate types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) in Oregon

Most of Oregon has a generally mild climate, though there is significant variation given the variety of landscapes across the state.[^cite_note-85] Oregon's western region, west of the [Cascade Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_Range), has an [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate), populated by dense [evergreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_rainforest) mixed forests. Western Oregon's climate is heavily influenced by the Pacific Ocean. The western third of Oregon is very wet in the winter, moderately to very wet during the spring and fall, and dry during the summer. The [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity) of Western Oregon is high except during summer days, which are semi-dry to semi-humid. Eastern Oregon typically sees low humidity year-round.[^cite_note-dri-86]

The state's southwestern portion, particularly the [Rogue Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_Valley), has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) with drier and sunnier winters and hotter summers, similar to [Northern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_California).[^cite_note-87]

Oregon's northeastern portion has a [steppe climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe_climate), and its high terrain regions have a [subarctic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate). Like [Western Europe](33800.md), Oregon, and the Pacific Northwest in general, is considered warm for its latitude, and the state has far milder winters at a given elevation than comparable latitudes elsewhere in North America, such as the [Upper Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Midwest), [Ontario](22218.md), [Quebec](7954867.md) and [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England).[^cite_note-dri-86] However, the state ranks fifth for coolest summer temperatures of any state in the country, after Maine, Idaho, Wyoming, and Alaska.[^cite_note-88]

The eastern two thirds of Oregon, which largely comprise high [desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid), have cold, snowy winters and very dry summers. Much of the east is semiarid to arid like the rest of the [Great Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Basin), though the [Blue Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Mountains_\(Pacific_Northwest\)) are wet enough to support extensive forests. Most of Oregon receives significant snowfall. The Willamette Valley, where 60 percent of the population lives,[^cite_note-89] has considerably milder winters for its latitude and typically sees only light snowfall.[^cite_note-dri-86]

Oregon's highest recorded temperature is 119 °F (48 °C), which was set at [Prineville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prineville,_Oregon) on July 29, 1898, and tied at [Pendleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendleton,_Oregon) on August 10, 1898, and [Pelton Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelton_Dam) on June 29, 2021.[^cite_note-90] The lowest recorded temperature is −54 °F (−48 °C) at [Seneca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seneca,_Oregon) on February 10, 1933.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoone20049-91]

### Cities and towns

Further information: [List of cities and unincorporated communities in Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_unincorporated_communities_in_Oregon)

Oregon's population is largely concentrated in the [Willamette Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willamette_Valley). It stretches from [Eugene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon) in the south, home of the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon), to [Corvallis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvallis,_Oregon), home of [Oregon State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_University), and [Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem,_Oregon), the capital, to Portland, Oregon's largest city.[^cite_note-2010_US_Census-92]

[Astoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astoria,_Oregon), at the mouth of the Columbia River, was the first permanent English-speaking settlement west of the [Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains) in what is now the U.S. [Oregon City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_City,_Oregon), at the end of the Oregon Trail, was the Oregon Territory's first incorporated city, and its first capital from 1848 until 1852, when the capital was moved to Salem. [Bend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bend,_Oregon), near the geographic center of the state, is one of the ten fastest-growing metropolitan areas in the U.S.[^cite_note-93]

In southern Oregon, [Medford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medford,_Oregon) is a rapidly growing metro area and is home to the [Rogue Valley International–Medford Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_Valley_International%E2%80%93Medford_Airport), the state's third-busiest airport. To the south, near the California border, is the city of [Ashland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashland,_Oregon). [Eastern Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Oregon) is sparsely populated, but is home to [Hermiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermiston,_Oregon), with a population of 18,000 and the largest and fastest-growing city in the region.[^cite_note-94]

As of the 2020 census, the population of Oregon was 4,237,256. The gender makeup of the state was 49.5% male and 50.5% female. 20.5% of the population were under the age of 18; 60.8% were between the ages of 18 and 64; and 18.8% were 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-2020DP1-104]

According to the 2020 census, 13.9% of Oregon's population was of [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) origin (of any race) and 71.7% [non-Hispanic White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_whites), 2.0% [African American](2154.md), 1.5% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States), 4.6% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans), 1.5% [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_Americans), and 10.5% two or more races.[^cite_note-114] According to the 2016 [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey), 12.4% of Oregon's population were of Hispanic or Latino origin (of any race): [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Americans) (10.4%), [Puerto Rican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Ricans) (0.3%), [Cuban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Americans) (0.1%), and other Hispanic or Latino origin (1.5%).[^cite_note-ACS2016DEMO-115] The five largest ancestry groups for White Oregonians were: [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans) (19.1%), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans) (11.7%), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Americans) (11.3%), [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_ancestry) (5.3%), and [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Americans) (3.8%).[^cite_note-116]

The state's most populous ethnic group, [non-Hispanic Whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_whites), decreased from 95.8% of the total population in 1970 to 71.7% in 2020, though it increased in absolute numbers.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

As of 2011, 38.7% of Oregon's children under one year of age belonged to [minority groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_and_ethnicity_in_the_United_States), meaning they had at least one parent who was not a non-Hispanic White.[^cite_note-119] Of the state's total population, 22.6% was under the age 18, and 77.4% were 18 or older.

The [center of population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_of_population) of Oregon is located in [Linn County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linn_County,_Oregon), in the city of [Lyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyons,_Oregon).[^cite_note-120] Around 60% of Oregon's population resides within the [Portland metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_metropolitan_area).[^cite_note-121]

As of 2009, Oregon's population comprised 361,393 foreign-born residents.[^cite_note-cnu-122] Of the foreign-born residents, the three largest groups are originally from countries in: Latin America (47.8%), Asia (27.4%), and Europe (16.5%).[^cite_note-cnu-122] [Mexico](3966054.md), [Vietnam](202354.md), [China](5405.md), [India](14533.md), and the [Philippines](23440.md) were the top countries of origin for Oregon's immigrants in 2018.[^cite_note-123]

The [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) first reached Oregon in the 1890s. There is a substantial Roma population in Willamette Valley and around Portland.[^cite_note-124]

Oregon has frequently been cited by statistical agencies for having a smaller percentage of religious communities than other U.S. states.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130] According to a 2009 [Gallup poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup_poll), Oregon was paired with [Vermont](32578.md) as the two "least religious" states in the U.S.[^cite_note-131]

In the same 2009 Gallup poll, 69% of Oregonians identified themselves as being [Christian](5211.md).[^cite_note-132] The largest Christian denominations in Oregon by number of adherents in 2010 were the [Roman Catholic Church](410868.md) with 398,738; [The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) with 147,965; and the [Assemblies of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assemblies_of_God_USA) with 45,492.\[^cite_note-[www.thearda.com-133](http://www.thearda.com-133/)] Oregon also contains the largest community of Russian [Old Believers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Believers) to be found in the U.S.[^cite_note-134] Judaism is the largest non-Christian religion in Oregon with more than 50,000 adherents, 47,000 of whom live in the Portland area.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] Recently, new kosher food and Jewish educational offerings have led to a rapid increase in Portland's Orthodox Jewish population.[^cite_note-137] The Northwest Tibetan Cultural Association is headquartered in Portland. There are an estimated 6,000 to 10,000 [Muslims](19541.md) in Oregon, most of whom live in and around Portland.[^cite_note-138]

Most of the remainder of the population had no religious affiliation; the 2008 [American Religious Identification Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Religious_Identification_Survey) placed Oregon as tied with Nevada in fifth place of U.S. states having the highest percentage of residents identifying themselves as "non-religious", at 24 percent.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] Secular organizations include the [Center for Inquiry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Inquiry), the Humanists of Greater Portland, and the United States Atheists.

During much of the 1990s, a group of conservative Christians formed the [Oregon Citizens Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Citizens_Alliance), and unsuccessfully tried to pass legislation to prevent "gay sensitivity training" in public schools and legal benefits for homosexual couples.[^cite_note-141]

### Future projections

Projections from the U.S. Census Bureau show Oregon's population increasing to 4,833,918 by 2030, an increase of 41.3% compared to the state's population of 3,421,399 in 2000.[^cite_note-154] The state's own projections forecast a total population of 5,425,408 in 2040.[^cite_note-155]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Oregon)

See also: [Oregon locations by per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_locations_by_per_capita_income)

In 2025, the gross domestic product (GDP) of Oregon was \$342.8 billion and the state's [per capita personal income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_personal_income_in_the_United_States) was \$73,678.[^cite_note-156] As of 2015, Oregon ranks as the 17th highest in [median household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_income_in_the_United_States#Income_by_state) at \$60,834.[^cite_note-kff-157] In 2025, small businesses made up 99.4% of the businesses in the state, and employed 53.4% of its work force.[^cite_note-158]

Oregon's unemployment rate was 4.8% in May 2025,[^cite_note-159] while the U.S. unemployment rate was 4.2 that month.[^cite_note-160] In August, 2013, Oregon had the third largest amount of food stamp users in the nation (21% of the population).[^cite_note-161]

### Agriculture



Teenagers harvesting berries in [Boring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boring,_Oregon), 1946

Oregon's diverse landscapes provide ideal environments for various types of farming. Land in the Willamette Valley owes its fertility to the [Missoula Floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missoula_Floods), which deposited lake sediment from [Glacial Lake Missoula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_Lake_Missoula) in western Montana onto the valley floor.[^cite_note-chapter24-162] In 2016, the Willamette Valley region produced over 100 million pounds (45 kt) of [blueberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blueberries).[^cite_note-163] The industry is governed and represented by the [Oregon Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Department_of_Agriculture).[^cite_note-DOA-164]

Oregon is also one of four major world hazelnut (*[Corylus avellana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corylus_avellana)*) growing regions, and produces 95% of the domestic hazelnuts in the United States. While the history of [wine production in Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_wine) can be traced to before [Prohibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition), it became a significant industry beginning in the 1970s. In 2005, Oregon ranked third among U.S. states with 303 wineries.[^cite_note-165] Due to regional similarities in climate and [soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil), the grapes planted in Oregon are often the same varieties found in the French regions of [Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace_wine) and [Burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgundy_wine). In 2014, 71 wineries opened in the state. The total is currently 676, which represents a growth of 12% over 2013.[^cite_note-166]

In the southern Oregon coast, commercially cultivated cranberries account for about 7 percent of U.S. production, and the cranberry ranks 23rd among Oregon's top 50 agricultural commodities. Cranberry cultivation in Oregon uses about 27,000 acres (110 square kilometers) in southern [Coos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coos_County,_Oregon) and northern [Curry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry_County,_Oregon) counties, centered around the coastal city of [Bandon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandon,_Oregon). In the northeastern region of the state, particularly around [Pendleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pendleton,_Oregon), both irrigated and dry land wheat is grown.[^cite_note-167] Oregon [farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmer) and ranchers also produce [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle), [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep), dairy products, eggs and [poultry](23197.md).

[Caneberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caneberries) (*Rubus*) are farmed here.[^cite_note-Ellis-et-al-eds-1991-168]<sup>: 25 </sup> [Stamen blight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamen_blight) (*Hapalosphaeria deformans*) is significant here and throughout the [PNW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest).[^cite_note-Ellis-et-al-eds-1991-168]<sup>: 25 </sup> Here it especially hinders commercial [dewberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewberries).[^cite_note-Ellis-et-al-eds-1991-168]<sup>: 25 </sup>

*[Phytophthora ramorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytophthora_ramorum)* was first discovered in the 1990s on the [California Central Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Central_Coast)[^cite_note-Anderson-et-al-2004-169] and was quickly found here as well.[^cite_note-Rizzo-et-al-2005-170] *P. ramorum* is of economic concern due to its infestation of *Rubus* and *[Vaccinium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccinium)* spp. (including [cranberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranberry) and [blueberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blueberry)).[^cite_note-Rizzo-et-al-2005-170]

Peaches grown in the [Willamette Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willamette_Valley) are mostly sold directly and do not enter the more distant markets.[^cite_note-Olsen-2002-171] [OSU Extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_University_Extension_Service) recommended several [peach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peach_cultivar) and [nectarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nectarine_cultivar) cultivars for Willamette.[^cite_note-Olsen-2002-171]

Approximately 1.3 million acres of agricultural land in Oregon is owned by foreigners, with nearly half being held by [Canadians](5042916.md).[^cite_note-172]

### Forestry and fisheries

See also: [List of freshwater fishes of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_freshwater_fishes_of_Oregon)



[Fish ladder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_ladder) at [Bonneville Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonneville_Dam), [Multnomah County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multnomah_County,_Oregon)



Historic Lumber Sled at Camp 18 in [Elsie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elsie,_Oregon)

Vast forests have historically made Oregon one of the nation's major [timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumber)-producing and logging states, but forest fires (such as the [Tillamook Burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tillamook_Burn)), over-harvesting, and lawsuits over the proper management of the extensive federal forest holdings have reduced the timber produced. Between 1989 and 2011, the amount of timber harvested from federal lands in Oregon dropped about 90%, although harvest levels on private land have remained relatively constant.[^cite_note-173]

Even the shift in recent years towards finished goods such as paper and building materials has not slowed the decline of the timber industry in the state. The effects of this decline have included [Weyerhaeuser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weyerhaeuser)'s acquisition of Portland-based [Willamette Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willamette_Industries) in January 2002, the relocation of [Louisiana-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana-Pacific)'s corporate headquarters from Portland to [Nashville](22018.md), and the decline of former lumber [company towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_town) such as [Gilchrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilchrist,_Oregon). Despite these changes, Oregon still leads the U.S. in [softwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softwood) lumber production; in 2011, 4,134 million board feet (9,760,000 m<sup>3</sup>) was produced in Oregon, compared with 3,685 million board feet (8,700,000 m<sup>3</sup>) in Washington, 1,914 million board feet (4,520,000 m<sup>3</sup>) in [Georgia](48830.md), and 1,708 million board feet (4,030,000 m<sup>3</sup>) in [Mississippi](16949861.md).[^cite_note-174] The slowing of the timber and [lumber industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_industry) has caused high unemployment rates in rural areas.[^cite_note-referencedesk-175]

Oregon has one of the largest [salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon)-fishing industries in the world, although ocean [fisheries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery) have reduced the river fisheries in recent years.[^cite_note-176] Because of the abundance of waterways in the state, it is also a major producer of hydroelectric energy.[^cite_note-177]

On June 30, 2022, an [emerald ash borer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald_ash_borer) infestation was found in [Forest Grove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Grove,_Oregon); the first for [Western North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_North_America).[^cite_note-ODF-EAB-bulletin-178][^cite_note-response-plan-EAB-Oregon-179][^cite_note-survey-DOA-EAB-180][^cite_note-2022-06-30-EAB-181]

### Tourism and entertainment



Elizabethan stage at the [Oregon Shakespeare Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Shakespeare_Festival) in [Ashland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashland,_Oregon)

Tourism is also a strong industry in the state. Tourism is centered on the state's natural features – mountains, forests, waterfalls, rivers, beaches and lakes, including [Crater Lake National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crater_Lake_National_Park), [Multnomah Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multnomah_Falls), the [Painted Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painted_Hills), the Deschutes River, and the [Oregon Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Caves_National_Monument_and_Preserve). Mount Hood and [Mount Bachelor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Bachelor) also draw visitors year-round for [skiing](28478.md) and other snow activities.[^cite_note-182]

Oregon is home to [many breweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewing_in_Oregon), and Portland has the largest number of breweries of any city in the world.[^cite_note-183]



Hells Canyon is one of the largest canyons in the United States.

The state's coastal region produces significant tourism as well.[^cite_note-184] The [Oregon Coast Aquarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Coast_Aquarium) comprises 23 acres (9.3 ha) along [Yaquina Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaquina_Bay) in [Newport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport,_Oregon), and was also home to [Keiko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keiko_\(orca\)) the orca whale.[^cite_note-185] It has been noted as one of the top ten aquariums in North America.[^cite_note-186] [Fort Clatsop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Clatsop) in [Warrenton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrenton,_Oregon) features a replica of [Lewis and Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition)'s encampment at the mouth of the Columbia River in 1805. The [Sea Lion Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Lion_Caves) in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence,_Oregon) are the largest system of sea caverns in the U.S., and also attract many visitors.[^cite_note-187]



[Oceanarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanarium) at the [Oregon Coast Aquarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Coast_Aquarium)

Oregon has also historically been a popular region for [film shoots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography) due to its diverse landscapes, as well as its proximity to [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles).[^cite_note-ORfilm-188] [Movies filmed in Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_shot_in_Oregon) include: *[Animal House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lampoon's_Animal_House)*, *[Free Willy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Willy)*, *[The General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_General_\(1927_film\))*, *[The Goonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Goonies)*, *[Kindergarten Cop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindergarten_Cop)*, *[One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Flew_Over_the_Cuckoo's_Nest_\(film\))*, and *[Stand By Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_by_Me_\(film\))*. Oregon native [Matt Groening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Groening), creator of *[The Simpsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons)*, has incorporated many references from his hometown of Portland into the TV series.[^cite_note-189] The [Oregon Film Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Film_Museum) is located in the old Clatsop County Jail in [Astoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astoria,_Oregon). Additionally, the [last remaining Blockbuster store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockbuster_\(Bend,_Oregon\)) is located in Bend.[^cite_note-190]

### Technology

High technology industries located in [Silicon Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Forest) have been a major employer since the 1970s. [Tektronix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tektronix) was the largest private employer in Oregon until the late 1980s. [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_Corporation)'s creation and expansion of several facilities in eastern [Washington County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_County,_Oregon) continued the growth that Tektronix had started. Intel, the state's largest for-profit private employer,[^cite_note-oreg-2013jul17-191][^cite_note-OPB-Intel-2022-192] operates four large facilities, with Ronler Acres, Jones Farm and Hawthorn Farm all located in [Hillsboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsboro,_Oregon).[^cite_note-193]

The spinoffs and startups that were produced by these two companies led to the establishment of the so-called Silicon Forest. The recession and [dot-com bust of 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dot-com_bubble) hit the region hard; many high technology employers reduced the number of their employees or went out of business. [Open Source Development Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Source_Development_Labs) made news in 2004 when they hired [Linus Torvalds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Torvalds), developer of the [Linux kernel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linux_kernel). In 2010, [biotechnology](4502.md) giant [Genentech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genentech) opened a \$400 million facility in [Hillsboro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsboro,_Oregon) to expand its production capabilities.[^cite_note-genentech_expansion-194] Oregon is home to several large [datacenters](579730.md) that take advantage of cheap power and a climate conducive to reducing cooling costs. [Google](1092923.md) operates a large datacenter in [The Dalles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dalles,_Oregon), and [Facebook](7529378.md) built a large datacenter near [Prineville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prineville,_Oregon) in 2010. [Amazon](90451.md) opened a datacenter near [Boardman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boardman,_Oregon) in 2011, and a fulfillment center in [Troutdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troutdale,_Oregon) in 2018.[^cite_note-amazon_data_center-195][^cite_note-196]

### Taxes and budgets

Oregon's [biennial](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Biennial) state budget, \$2.6 billion in 2017, comprises [General Funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fund_accounting), [Federal Funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_funds), Lottery Funds, and Other Funds.[^cite_note-202]

Oregon is one of [only five states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_taxes_in_the_United_States) that have no [sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax).[^cite_note-203] Oregon voters have been resolute in their opposition to a sales tax, voting proposals down each of the nine times they have been presented.[^cite_note-204] The last vote, for 1993's Measure 1, was defeated by a 75–25% margin.[^cite_note-205]

The state also has a minimum corporate tax of only \$150 a year,[^cite_note-206] amounting to 5.6% of the General Fund in the 2005–07 biennium; data about which businesses pay the minimum is not available to the public.[^cite_note-207] As a result, the state relies on [property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) and income taxes for its revenue. Oregon has the fifth highest personal income tax in the nation. According to the U.S. Census Bureau, Oregon ranked 41st out of the 50 states in taxes per capita in 2005 with an average amount paid of 1,791.45.[^cite_note-percapita-208]

A few local governments levy sales taxes on services: the city of Ashland, for example, collects a 5% sales tax on prepared food.[^cite_note-209]

The City of Portland imposes an Arts Education and Access Income Tax on residents over 18—a flat tax of \$35 collected from individuals earning \$1,000 or more per year and residing in a household with an annual income exceeding the federal poverty level. The tax funds Portland school teachers, and art focused non-profit organizations in Portland.[^cite_note-210]

The State of Oregon also allows transit districts to levy an income tax on employers and the self-employed. The State currently collects the tax for [TriMet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TriMet) and the [Lane Transit District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lane_Transit_District).[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212]

Oregon is one of six states with a revenue limit.[^cite_note-213] The "[kicker law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kicker_\(Oregon_tax_rebate\))" stipulates that when income tax collections exceed state economists' estimates by two percent or more, any excess must be returned to taxpayers.[^cite_note-214] Since the enactment of the law in 1979, refunds have been issued for seven of the eleven biennia.[^cite_note-215] In 2000, Ballot Measure 86 converted the "kicker" law from statute to the [Oregon Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Constitution), and changed some of its provisions.

Federal payments to county governments that were granted to replace timber revenue when logging in National Forests was restricted in the 1990s, have been under threat of suspension for several years. This issue dominates the future revenue of rural counties, which have come to rely on the payments in providing essential services.[^cite_note-216]

55% of state revenues are spent on public education, 23% on human services (child protective services, Medicaid, and senior services), 17% on public safety, and 5% on other services.[^cite_note-217]

Oregon has had a \$15 bicycle tax for each new bicycles over \$200 since 2018. Oregon is the only state in the nation with a bicycle excise tax.[^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219]

### Healthcare

Main article: [List of hospitals in Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_Oregon)

For health insurance, as of 2018 [Cambia Health Solutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambia_Health_Solutions) has the highest market share at 21%, followed by [Providence Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Health_&_Services).[^cite_note-ama-assn.org-220] In the Portland region, [Kaiser Permanente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Permanente) leads.[^cite_note-ama-assn.org-220] Providence and Kaiser are vertically [integrated delivery systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_delivery_system) which operate hospitals and offer insurance plans.[^cite_note-221] Aside from Providence and Kaiser, hospital systems which are primarily Oregon-based include [Legacy Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legacy_Health) mostly covering Portland, [Samaritan Health Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritan_Health_Services) with five hospitals in various areas across the state, and [Tuality Healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuality_Healthcare) in the western Portland metropolitan area. In [Southern Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Oregon), Asante runs several hospitals, including [Rogue Regional Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_Regional_Medical_Center). Some hospitals are operated by multi-state organizations such as [PeaceHealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PeaceHealth) and [CommonSpirit Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CommonSpirit_Health). Some hospitals such [Salem Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem_Hospital_\(Oregon\)) operate independently of larger systems.

[Oregon Health & Science University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Health_&_Science_University) is a Portland-based medical school that operates two hospitals and clinics.

The [Oregon Health Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Health_Plan) is the state's [Medicaid managed care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicaid_managed_care) plan, and it is known for innovations.[^cite_note-\:0-222] The Portland area is a mature [managed care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managed_care) and two-thirds of Medicare enrollees are in [Medicare Advantage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_Advantage) plans.[^cite_note-\:0-222]

## Education

### Elementary, middle, and high school

See also: [List of school districts in Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_school_districts_in_Oregon) and [List of high schools in Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_schools_in_Oregon)

In the 2013–2014 school year, the state had 567,000 students in public schools.[^cite_note-facts-223] There were 197 public school districts, served by 19 [education service districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_service_district_\(Oregon\)).[^cite_note-facts-223]

In 2016, the largest school districts in the state were:[^cite_note-224] [Portland Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Public_Schools_\(Oregon\)), comprising 47,323 students; [Salem-Keizer School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem-Keizer_School_District), comprising 40,565 students; [Beaverton School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaverton_School_District), comprising 39,625 students; [Hillsboro School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsboro_School_District), comprising 21,118 students; and [North Clackamas School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Clackamas_School_District), comprising 17,053 students.

Approximately 90.5% of Oregon high school students graduate, improving on the national average of 88.3% as measured from the [2010 U.S. census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_U.S._Census).[^cite_note-225]

On May 8, 2019, educators across the state protested to demand smaller class sizes, hiring more support staff, such as school counselors, librarians, and nurses, and the restoration of art, music, and physical education classes. The protests caused two dozen school districts to close, which equals to about 600 schools across the state.[^cite_note-226]

### Colleges and universities

See also: [List of colleges and universities in Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_Oregon) and [Oregon Office of University Coordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Office_of_University_Coordination)



The Memorial Union at [Oregon State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_University)

Especially since the 1990 passage of Measure 5, which set limits on property tax levels, Oregon has struggled to fund higher education. Since then, Oregon has cut its higher education budget and now ranks 46th in the country in state spending per student. However, 2007 legislation funded the university system far beyond the governor's requested budget though still capping tuition increases at 3% per year.[^cite_note-227] Oregon supports a total of seven public universities and one affiliate. It is home to three public research universities: The University of Oregon (UO) in Eugene and Oregon State University (OSU) in [Corvallis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvallis,_Oregon), both [classified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Classification_of_Institutions_of_Higher_Education) as research universities with very high research activity, and [Portland State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_State_University) which is classified as a research university with high research activity.[^cite_note-228]



Johnson Hall at the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon)

UO is the state's highest nationally ranked and most selective[^cite_note-229] public university by *[U.S. News & World Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report)* and *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_Magazine's_List_of_America's_Best_Colleges)*.[^cite_note-230] OSU is the state's only land-grant university, has the state's largest enrollment for fall 2014,[^cite_note-231] and is the state's highest ranking university according to *[Academic Ranking of World Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Ranking_of_World_Universities)*, *[Washington Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Monthly)*, and *[QS World University Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QS_World_University_Rankings)*.[^cite_note-232] OSU receives more annual funding for research than all other public higher education institutions in Oregon combined.[^cite_note-233] The state's urban Portland State University has Oregon's second largest enrollment.

The state has three regional universities: [Western Oregon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Oregon_University) in [Monmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monmouth,_Oregon), [Southern Oregon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Oregon_University) in Ashland, and [Eastern Oregon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Oregon_University) in [La Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Grande,_Oregon). The [Oregon Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Institute_of_Technology) has its campus in [Klamath Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klamath_Falls,_Oregon). The quasi-public [Oregon Health & Science University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Health_&_Science_University) (OHSU) includes medical, dental, and nursing schools, and graduate programs in biomedical sciences in Portland and a science and engineering school in Hillsboro. The state also supports [17 community colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_community_colleges_in_Oregon).



Eliot Hall at [Reed College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_College)

Oregon is home to a wide variety of private colleges, the majority of which are located in the Portland area. The [University of Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Portland), a Catholic university, is affiliated with the [Congregation of Holy Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_of_Holy_Cross). [Reed College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_College), a rigorous liberal arts college in Portland, was ranked by *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* as the 52nd best college in the country in 2015.[^cite_note-234]

Other private institutions in Portland include [Lewis & Clark College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_&_Clark_College); [Multnomah University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multnomah_University); [Portland Bible College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Bible_College); [Warner Pacific College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Pacific_College); [Cascade College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_College); the [National University of Natural Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Natural_Medicine); and [Western Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Seminary), a theological graduate school. [Pacific University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_University) is in the Portland suburb of [Forest Grove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Grove,_Oregon). There are also private colleges further south in the Willamette Valley. McMinnville is home to [Linfield College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linfield_College), while nearby Newberg is home to [George Fox University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Fox_University). Salem is home to two private schools: [Willamette University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willamette_University) (the state's oldest, established during the provisional period) and [Corban University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corban_University). Also located near Salem is [Mount Angel Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Angel_Abbey), one of America's largest Roman Catholic seminaries. The state's second medical school, the [College of Osteopathic Medicine of the Pacific, Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Osteopathic_Medicine_of_the_Pacific,_Northwest), is located in Lebanon. Eugene is home to three private colleges: [Bushnell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bushnell_University), [New Hope Christian College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hope_Christian_College), and [Gutenberg College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutenberg_College).

## Law and government

See also: [Government of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Oregon)



The Golden Pioneer atop the [Oregon State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_Capitol)

A writer in the [Oregon Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Country) book *A Pacific Republic*, written in 1839, predicted the territory was to become an independent republic. In 1843, settlers of the Willamette Valley voted in majority for a republican form of government.[^cite_note-235] The Oregon Country functioned in this way until August 13, 1848, when Oregon was annexed by the U.S. and a territorial government was established. Oregon maintained a territorial government until February 14, 1859, when it was granted [statehood](18618239.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson1904296-236]

### Structure

Oregon state government has a [separation of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers) similar to the [federal government](195149.md), with three branches:

- A [legislative branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) (the [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) [Oregon Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Legislative_Assembly))

- An [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) which includes an "administrative department" and [Oregon's governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Oregon) as chief executive

- A [judicial branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary), headed by the [Chief Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_justice) of the [Oregon Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Supreme_Court).

Governors in Oregon serve four-year terms and are limited to two consecutive terms, but an unlimited number of total terms. Oregon has no [lieutenant governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_governor_\(United_States\)); in case the office of governor is vacated, Article V, Section 8a of the [Oregon Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Constitution) specifies that the [Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Secretary_of_State) is first in line for succession.[^cite_note-articlev-237] The other statewide officers are [Treasurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_Treasurer), [Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Attorney_General), and [Labor Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Bureau_of_Labor_and_Industries).

The [biennial](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Biennial) [Oregon Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Legislative_Assembly) consists of a thirty-member [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_Senate) and a sixty-member [House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_House_of_Representatives). A debate over whether to move to annual sessions is a long-standing battle in Oregon politics, but the voters have resisted the move from citizen legislators to professional lawmakers. Because Oregon's state budget is written in two-year increments and, there being no sales tax, state revenue is based largely on income taxes, it is often significantly over or under budget.&#x20;

Recent legislatures have had to be called into special sessions repeatedly to address revenue shortfalls resulting from economic downturns, bringing to a head the need for more frequent legislative sessions. Oregon Initiative 71, passed in 2010, mandates the legislature to begin meeting every year, for 160 days in odd-numbered years, and 35 days in even-numbered years.

The state supreme court has seven elected justices, currently including the only two openly gay state supreme court justices in the nation. They choose one of their own to serve a six-year term as Chief Justice.

### Ballot measures

Oregon's constitution provides for [ballot initiatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballot_measure) voted upon by the electorate in general. In the 2002 general election, Oregon voters approved a [ballot measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Ballot_Measure_25_\(2002\)) to increase the state minimum wage automatically each year according to inflationary changes, which are measured by the [consumer price index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_price_index) (CPI).[^cite_note-238] In the 2004 general election, Oregon voters passed ballot measures [banning same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Ballot_Measure_36_\(2004\))[^cite_note-239] and [restricting land use regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Ballot_Measure_37_\(2004\)).[^cite_note-240] In the [2006 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon's_statewide_elections,_2006), voters restricted the use of [eminent domain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Ballot_Measure_39_\(2006\)) and extended the state's discount prescription drug coverage.[^cite_note-241]

In the 2020 general election, Oregon voters approved a [ballot measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Oregon_Ballot_Measure_110) decriminalizing the possession of small quantities of street drugs such as cocaine and heroin, becoming the first state in the country to do so after the drugs were originally made illegal.[^cite_note-242] The initiative has been described as a mixed success after three years of implementation, and calls for change arose.[^cite_note-243][^cite_note-244]

[Drug overdose deaths continued to rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opioid_epidemic_in_the_United_States), in line with other states. Funds allocated to treatment and other services have apparently not increased the success of these alternate outcomes.[^cite_note-245][^cite_note-246] In 2024, Governor Kotek signed a bill reversing the decriminalization component of the ballot measure while also expanding funding for drug treatment.[^cite_note-247]

In 2020 the state also approved [a ballot measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Oregon_Ballot_Measure_109) to create a legal means of administering [psilocybin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psilocybin) for medicinal use, making it the first state in the country to legalize the drug.[^cite_note-248]

### Federal representation

Like all U.S. states, Oregon is represented by two [senators](24909346.md) and currently has six [representatives](19468510.md). After Oregon was admitted to the Union, it began with a single member in the House of Representatives, [La Fayette Grover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Fayette_Grover), who served in the [35th U.S. Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35th_U.S._Congress) for less than a month. [Congressional apportionment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_congressional_apportionment) increased the size of the delegation following the censuses of [1890](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1890_United_States_census), [1910](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1910_United_States_census), [1940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1940_United_States_census), and [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_United_States_census). Following the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), Oregon gained a sixth congressional seat, filled in the [2022 congressional elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections).[^cite_note-249] A [detailed list of the past and present congressional delegations from Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon's_congressional_delegations) is available.

The [U.S. District Court for the District of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._District_Court_for_the_District_of_Oregon) hears federal cases in the state. The court has courthouses in [Portland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_O._Hatfield_United_States_Courthouse), [Eugene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_L._Morse_United_States_Courthouse), Medford, and Pendleton. Also in Portland is the federal bankruptcy court, with a second branch in Eugene.[^cite_note-250] Oregon, among other western states and territories, is in the [9th Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Ninth_Circuit). One of the court's meeting places is at the [Pioneer Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_Courthouse) in downtown Portland, a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark) built in 1869.

### Politics

Political opinions in Oregon are geographically split by the [Cascade Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_Range), with [Western Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Oregon) being more [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_liberalism_in_the_United_States) and [Eastern Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Oregon) being [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-251] In a 2008 analysis of the 2004 presidential election, a political analyst found that according to the application of a [Likert scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likert_scale), Oregon had the most liberal Kerry voters and the most conservative Bush voters, making it the most politically polarized state in the country.[^cite_note-252]

The base of Democratic support is largely concentrated in the urban centers of the Willamette Valley. The eastern two-thirds of the state beyond the Cascade Mountains typically votes Republican; in 2000 and [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_presidential_election), [George W. Bush](3414021.md) carried every county east of the Cascades. The region's sparse population means the more populous counties in the Willamette Valley usually outweigh the eastern counties in statewide elections.&#x20;

In 2008, for instance, Republican Senate incumbent [Gordon H. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_H._Smith) lost his bid for a third term, even though he [carried all but eight counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_Senate_election_in_Oregon). His Democratic challenger, Jeff Merkley, won Multnomah County by 142,000 votes, more than double the overall margin of victory. Oregonians have voted for the [Democratic](5043544.md) presidential candidate in every election since 1988. In 2004 and 2006, Democrats won control of the State Senate, and then the House.&#x20;

Since 2023, Oregon has been represented by four Democrats and two [Republicans](32070.md) in the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md). Since 2009, the state has had two Democratic U.S. senators, [Ron Wyden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Wyden) and [Jeff Merkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Merkley). Oregon voters have elected Democratic governors in every election since 1986, most recently electing [Tina Kotek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Kotek) over Republican [Christine Drazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Drazan) and Independent [Betsy Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betsy_Johnson) in the 2022 gubernatorial election.

During Oregon's history, it has adopted many electoral reforms proposed during the [Progressive Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Era), through the efforts of [William S. U'Ren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Simon_U'Ren) and his [Direct Legislation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_Legislation_League). Under his leadership, the state overwhelmingly approved a ballot measure in 1902 that created the [initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_initiative) and [referendum](51582.md) for citizens to introduce or approve proposed laws or amendments to the state constitution directly, making Oregon the first state to adopt such a system. Today, roughly half of U.S. states do so.[^cite_note-253]

In 1904, the [primary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partisan_primary) to select [party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) candidates was adopted. In 1908, the Oregon Constitution was amended to include recall of public officials. More recent amendments include the nation's first doctor-assisted suicide law,[^cite_note-254] called the [Death with Dignity Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Oregon_Ballot_Measure_16) (which was challenged, unsuccessfully, in 2005 by the [Bush administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_W._Bush) in a case heard by the [U.S. Supreme Court](31737.md)), [legalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Oregon_Ballot_Measure_67) of [medical cannabis](175440.md), and among the nation's strongest [anti-urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_growth) and pro-environment laws. More recently, [2004's Measure 37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Ballot_Measure_37_\(2004\)) reflects a backlash against such land-use laws. A further ballot measure in 2007, [Measure 49](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Ballot_Measure_37_\(2004\)#Measure_49), curtailed many of the provisions of 37.

Of the [measures placed on the ballot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Oregon_ballot_measures) since 1902, the people have passed 99 of the 288 initiatives and 25 of the 61 referendums on the ballot, though not all of them survived challenges in courts (see *[Pierce v. Society of Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierce_v._Society_of_Sisters)*, for an example). During the same period, the legislature has referred 363 measures to the people, of which 206 have passed.

Oregon pioneered the American use of [postal voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_voting), beginning with experimentation approved by the [Oregon Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Legislative_Assembly) in 1981 and culminating with a [1998 ballot measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Ballot_Measure_60_\(1998\)) mandating that all counties conduct elections by mail. It remains one of just two states, the other being Washington, where [voting by mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absentee_ballot) is the only method of voting.

In 1994, Oregon adopted the [Oregon Health Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Health_Plan), which made health care available to most of its citizens without private health insurance.[^cite_note-255]

Oregon is the only state that does not have a mechanism to impeach executive officeholders, including the governor.[^cite_note-256] Removing an executive office holder would require a recall election. It is one of four states that requires two-thirds of members of the House and Senate be present to establish a quorum.[^cite_note-257] It is one of a minority of states that does not have a lieutenant governor.[^cite_note-258] The Secretary of State is the first in line of succession to replace the governor in event of a vacancy. This last occurred in 2015, when Gov. [John Kitzhaber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kitzhaber) resigned amid allegation of influence peddling and Secretary of State [Kate Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Brown) became governor. Brown won a special election in 2016 to retain the position, and won a full four-year term in 2018.

In the [U.S. Electoral College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_College_\(United_States\)), Oregon cast seven votes through the 2020 presidential election. Under apportionment of Congress under the 2020 U.S. census, Oregon added a sixth congressional seat. Under the Electoral College formula of votes equaling the number of U.S. House seats plus the two U.S. Senators, Oregon cast eight votes in the 2024 election. Oregon has supported Democratic candidates in the last ten elections. Democratic incumbent [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) won the state by a margin of twelve percentage points, with over 54% of the popular vote in 2012. In the 2016 election, [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) won Oregon by 11 percentage points.[^cite_note-259] [Joe Biden](145422.md) won Oregon by 16 percentage points in the [2020 election](48410011.md), and [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris) won Oregon by 14 percentage points in the [2024 election](59568546.md).[^cite_note-Leip,_David-260]

In a 2020 study, Oregon was ranked as the easiest state for citizens to vote in.[^cite_note-261]

Oregon [retains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Oregon) the [death penalty](5902.md), though there is currently a gubernatorial hold on executions.[^cite_note-262]

## Sports

See also: [Sports in Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Portland,_Oregon)



The [Moda Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Garden_\(arena\)) (formerly the Rose Garden) during a [Portland Trail Blazers](72897.md) game

Oregon is home to four major professional sports teams: the [Portland Trail Blazers](72897.md) of the [NBA](22093.md), the [Portland Thorns FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Thorns_FC) of the [NWSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women's_Soccer_League), the [Portland Timbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Timbers) of [MLS](71802.md), and the [Portland Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Fire) of the [WNBA](57721.md).[^cite_note-timbers-263][^cite_note-264]

Until 2011, the only major professional sports team in Oregon was the Portland Trail Blazers of the National Basketball Association. From the 1970s to the 1990s, the Blazers were one of the most successful teams in the NBA in terms of both win–loss record and attendance, including winning the [1977 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_NBA_Finals) behind star [Bill Walton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Walton) and reaching the [1992 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_NBA_Finals) with star [Clyde Drexler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clyde_Drexler).[^cite_note-265] In the early 21st century, the team's popularity declined due to personnel and financial issues (an era when the team was derisively referred to as the [Jail Blazers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Portland_Trail_Blazers#The_Jailblazers:_2000-2003)), but it was revived after the departure of controversial players and the acquisition of new stars such as [Brandon Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Roy), [LaMarcus Aldridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaMarcus_Aldridge), and [Damian Lillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damian_Lillard).[^cite_note-samsmith-266][^cite_note-mejia-267] The Blazers play in the [Moda Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moda_Center) in Portland's Lloyd District, which, as of 2026, also is home to the Portland Fire. The [Portland Winterhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Winterhawks) of the [junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_ice_hockey) [Western Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hockey_League) also play at the Moda Center.[^cite_note-rosequarter-268]

The Portland Timbers play at [Providence Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Park), just west of downtown Portland. The Timbers have a strong following, with the team regularly selling out its games.[^cite_note-Wahl-269] The Timbers repurposed the formerly multi-use stadium into a [soccer-specific stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer-specific_stadium) in fall 2010, increasing the seating in the process.[^cite_note-pgepark-270] The Timbers operate Portland Thorns FC, a women's soccer team that has played in the [National Women's Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women's_Soccer_League) since the league's first season in 2013. The Thorns, who also play at Providence Park, have won three league championships: in the inaugural [2013 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_National_Women's_Soccer_League_season), in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_National_Women's_Soccer_League_season), and in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_National_Women's_Soccer_League_season). The team has been by far the NWSL's attendance leader in each of the league's seasons.



Providence Park during a [Portland Thorns FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Thorns_FC) match

Eugene and Hillsboro have minor-league baseball teams: the [Eugene Emeralds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Emeralds) and the [Hillsboro Hops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsboro_Hops) both play in the [High-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-A) [Northwest League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_League).[^cite_note-High_A-271] Portland has had minor-league baseball teams in the past, including the [Portland Beavers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Beavers) and [Portland Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Rockies), who played most recently at Providence Park when it was known as PGE Park. Salem also previously had a [Class A Short Season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_A_Short_Season) Northwest League team, the [Salem-Keizer Volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem-Keizer_Volcanoes) that was not included in the [2021 Minor League Baseball reorganization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_League_Baseball#Reorganization_of_2021). The Volcanoes ownership later formed the amateur [Mavericks Independent Baseball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mavericks_Independent_Baseball_League), which is fully based in Salem.[^cite_note-mavsleague-272]

The [Oregon State Beavers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_State_Beavers) and the University of Oregon Ducks football teams of the [Pac-12 Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pac-12_Conference) meet annually in the [Oregon–Oregon State football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon%E2%80%93Oregon_State_football_rivalry). Both schools have had recent success in other sports as well: Oregon State won back-to-back [college baseball championships](536107.md) in 2006 and 2007,[^cite_note-273] winning a third in 2018;[^cite_note-274] and the University of Oregon won back-to-back [NCAA men's cross country championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Men's_Cross_Country_Championship) in 2007 and 2008.[^cite_note-275]
