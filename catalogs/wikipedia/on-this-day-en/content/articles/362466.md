The **Italy national football team** ([Italian](14708.md): *Nazionale di calcio dell'Italia*) has represented [Italy](14532.md) in men's international [football](10568.md) since [its first match in 1910](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1910_Italy_v_France_football_match). The team is controlled by the [Italian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Football_Federation) (FIGC), the governing body for [football in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Italy) and a founding member of [UEFA](32332.md). Italy plays its home matches at various stadiums across the country, while its [training center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_ground_\(association_football\)) and technical headquarters, the [Centro Tecnico Federale di Coverciano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centro_Tecnico_Federale_di_Coverciano), is located in [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence).

Italy is one of the most successful national teams in international football. It has won the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) four times ([1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup), [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup), [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup), and [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup)), becoming the first team to successfully defend the title, and has also finished as runners-up in [1970](157227.md) and [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup). Italy has won the [UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) twice ([1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1968) and [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020)), reached the final in [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000) and [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012), and won the [Olympic football tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Summer_Olympics) in [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics). Italy also finished third in the [FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup) ([2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup)) and the [UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League) ([2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Nations_League_Finals) and [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UEFA_Nations_League_Finals)).

The team is known as *gli Azzurri* (the Blues), after [Savoy blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy_blue), the traditional colour of the [House of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Savoy). Italy has long-standing rivalries with [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil%E2%80%93Italy_football_rivalry), [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France%E2%80%93Italy_football_rivalry), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany%E2%80%93Italy_football_rivalry), and to a lesser extent with [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy%E2%80%93Spain_football_rivalry) and [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_football_team). The team has reached the top of the [FIFA World Rankings](719095.md) in 1993 and 2007. Between 2018 and 2021, Italy set a record unbeaten run of 37 competitive men's international matches,[^cite_note-2] which was surpassed by Spain in 2026.[^cite_note-3] Italy has failed to qualify for the last three World Cups (2018, 2022 and 2026), marking its longest absence in the competition.

## History

Main article: [History of the Italy national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Italy_national_football_team)

### Origins and first two World Cup titles in 1934 and 1938



The squad celebrating its first [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup)

An early attempt to create an Italian national team occurred on 30 April 1899, when an Italian selection played a Swiss eleven, losing 0–2 in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin).[^cite_note-4] The [team's first official match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1910_Italy_v_France_football_match) was held in [Milan](36511.md) on 15 May 1910. Italy defeated [France](250197.md) by a score of 6–2, with Italy's first goal scored by [Pietro Lana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Lana).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-magliarossonera-6][^cite_note-7] The Italian team played with a (2–3–5) system and consisted of De Simoni, Varisco, [Calì](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Cal%C3%AC), Trerè, Fossati, [Capello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_Capello), Debernardi, Rizzi, [Cevenini I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_Cevenini), Lana, [Boiocchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arturo_Boiocchi), with Calì as the team's first captain.[^cite_note-8]

The first success in an official tournament was the bronze medal at the [1928 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1928_Summer_Olympics) in [Amsterdam](844.md). After losing the semi-final against [Uruguay](679782.md), an 11–3 victory against [Egypt](1022953.md) secured third place in the competition. In the [1927–30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1927%E2%80%9330_Central_European_International_Cup) and [1933–35 Central European International Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933%E2%80%9335_Central_European_International_Cup), Italy achieved first place out of five [Central European](5188.md) teams, topping the group with 11 points in both editions of the tournament.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Italy would also later win the gold medal at the [1936 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics) with a 2–1 victory in extra time in the gold medal match over [Austria](695226.md) on 15 August 1936.[^cite_note-SR-11]

After declining to participate in the inaugural World Cup ([1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), in Uruguay) the Italy national team won two consecutive editions of the tournament in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup), under the direction of coach [Vittorio Pozzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittorio_Pozzo) and the performance of [Giuseppe Meazza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Meazza), who is considered one of the best Italian football players of all time by some.[^cite_note-Giuseppe_Meazza_La_favola_di_Peppin_il_folbèr-12][^cite_note-The_inimitable_Giuseppe_Meazza-13] Italy hosted the 1934 World Cup, and played their first ever World Cup match in a 7–1 win over the [United States](582488.md) in [Rome](25458.md). Italy defeated [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team) 2–1 in [extra time](633242.md) in the final in Rome, with goals by [Raimundo Orsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raimundo_Orsi) and [Angelo Schiavio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelo_Schiavio) to achieve their first World Cup title. They achieved their second title in 1938 in a 4–2 defeat of [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team), with two goals by [Gino Colaussi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gino_Colaussi) and two goals by [Silvio Piola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Piola) in the World Cup that followed. It is rumored that before the 1938 finals [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist) Italian Prime Minister [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini) sent a [telegram](3485470.md) to the team, saying "Vincere o morire!" (literally translated as "Win or die!"). However, no record remains of such a telegram, and World Cup player [Pietro Rava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Rava) said when interviewed: "No, no, no, that's not true. He sent a telegram wishing us well, but no never 'win or die'."[^cite_note-14]

### Reconstruction in the 1950s and 1960s



The Italy national team in 1965

In 1949, 10 of the 11 players in the team's initial line-up were killed in a [plane crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superga_air_disaster) that affected [Torino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino_FC), winners of the previous five [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A) titles. Italy did not advance further than the first round of the [1950 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup), as they were weakened severely due to the air disaster. The team had travelled by boat rather than by plane, fearing another accident.[^cite_note-lisi_47-15]

In the [1954](60523.md) and [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup) World Cups, Italy failed to progress past the first round,[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] and did not qualify for the [1958 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup) due to a 2–1 defeat to [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_national_football_team) in the last match of the [qualifying round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)).[^cite_note-18] Italy did not take part in the first edition of the [European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Football_Championship) in [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_European_Nations'_Cup) (then known as the European Nations Cup),[^cite_note-19] and was knocked out by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team) in the first round of the [1964 European Nations' Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_European_Nations'_Cup_qualifying).[^cite_note-20]

Their participation in the [1966 World Cup](61629.md) was ended by a 0–1 defeat at the hands of [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team). Despite being the tournament favourites, the *Azzurri*, whose 1966 squad included [Gianni Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianni_Rivera) and [Giacomo Bulgarelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacomo_Bulgarelli), were eliminated in the first round by the semi-professional North Koreans. The Italian team was bitterly condemned upon their return home, while North Korean scorer [Pak Doo-ik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pak_Doo-ik) was celebrated as the [David who killed Goliath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goliath). Upon Italy's return home, furious fans threw fruit and rotten tomatoes at their transport bus at the airport.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

### European champions and World Cup runners-up (1968–1974)



Captain [Giacinto Facchetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacinto_Facchetti) celebrates Italy's [UEFA Euro 1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1968) victory.

In 1968, Italy hosted the [European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1968) and won the tournament in its first participation, beating [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) in Rome and winning their first major competition since the 1938 World Cup. The [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1968_Final) ended in a 1–1 draw after extra time, and the rules of the time required the match to be replayed a few days later. This would be the only time the final of the European Championship or World Cup was replayed.[^cite_note-23] On 10 June 1968, Italy won the replay 2–0 (with goals from [Gigi Riva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigi_Riva) and [Pietro Anastasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Anastasi)) to take the trophy.

In the [1970 World Cup](157227.md), exploiting the performances of European champions' players like [Giacinto Facchetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giacinto_Facchetti), Gianni Rivera and Gigi Riva and with a new centre-forward [Roberto Boninsegna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Boninsegna), the team were able to return to the World Cup final match after 32 years. They reached this result after one of the most famous matches in football history—the "[Game of the Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_v_West_Germany_\(1970_FIFA_World_Cup\))", the 1970 World Cup semifinal between Italy and West Germany that Italy won 4–3 in extra time, with five of the seven goals coming in extra time.[^cite_note-24] They were later defeated by [Brazil](149286.md) in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) 4–1.

After losing to [Belgium](679716.md) in the quarter-finals to qualify for the [1972 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1972), this generation's cycle ended at the [1974 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup), where the team was eliminated in the group stage after a 2–1 loss against [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team) in the last match of the group.

### Third World Cup title generation (1978–1986)

Under the initial guide of [Fulvio Bernardini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulvio_Bernardini) and later that of head coach [Enzo Bearzot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Bearzot), a new generation of Italian players came to the international stage in the second half of the 1970s. At the [1978 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup), Italy was the only team in the tournament to beat the eventual champions and host team [Argentina](454699.md), and the *Azzurri* made it to the third-place final, where they were defeated by Brazil 2–1. In the second round group stage match against the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), which prevented Italy from reaching the final, Italian goalkeeper [Dino Zoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Zoff) was beaten by a long-distance shot from [Arie Haan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arie_Haan), and Zoff was criticised for the defeat.[^cite_note-25] Italy hosted the [1980 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1980), the first edition to be held between eight teams instead of four,[^cite_note-26] automatically qualifying for the finals as hosts. After two draws with [Spain](609825.md) and Belgium and a narrow 1–0 win over [England](9904.md), Italy were beaten by Czechoslovakia in the third-place match on penalties 9–8 after [Fulvio Collovati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulvio_Collovati) missed his kick.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]



Italy's starting line-up, before the match against [Argentina](454699.md) in a group stage game at the [1982 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup)



One of the widely remembered pictures of the 1982 World Cup, Italian President [Sandro Pertini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandro_Pertini) playing *[scopone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scopa)* with [Dino Zoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Zoff), [Franco Causio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Causio) and coach Bearzot.

After a [scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totonero_1980) in [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A), where some national team players such as [Paolo Rossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Rossi)[^cite_note-29] were prosecuted and suspended for match fixing and illegal betting, the *Azzurri* qualified for the second round of the [1982 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup) after three uninspiring draws against [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team), [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_national_football_team), and [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team). Having been loudly criticised, the Italian team decided on a press black-out from then on, with only coach Enzo Bearzot and captain Dino Zoff appointed to speak to the press. Italy's second group was a [group of death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_death) with Argentina and Brazil. In the opener, Italy prevailed 2–1 over Argentina, with Italy's goals, both left-footed strikes, being scored by [Marco Tardelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Tardelli) and [Antonio Cabrini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Cabrini). After Brazil defeated Argentina 3–1, Italy needed to win in order to advance to the semi-finals. Italy took the lead twice with Paolo Rossi's goals, and twice Brazil came back. When [Falcão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Roberto_Falc%C3%A3o) scored to equalise at 2–2, Brazil would have been through on goal difference, but in the 74th minute Rossi scored the winning goal, for a [hat-trick](411541.md), in a crowded penalty area to send Italy to the semifinals after one of the greatest [games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_v_Italy_\(1982_FIFA_World_Cup\)) in World Cup history.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] Italy then progressed to the semi-final where they defeated Poland with two goals from Rossi.

In the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup_final) on 11 July 1982, Italy met West Germany in [Madrid](41188263.md). The first half ended scoreless, after Antonio Cabrini missed a penalty awarded for a [Hans-Peter Briegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Peter_Briegel) foul on [Bruno Conti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Conti). In the second half Paolo Rossi again scored the first goal, and while the Germans were pushing forward in search of an equaliser, Marco Tardelli and substitute [Alessandro Altobelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Altobelli) finalised two [*contropiede*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_tactics_and_skills) counterattacks to make it 3–0. [Paul Breitner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Breitner) scored West Germany's consolation goal seven minutes before the end.[^cite_note-33] Tardelli's screaming celebration after his goal was one of the defining images of Italy's 1982 World Cup triumph.[^cite_note-34] Paolo Rossi won the [Golden Boot](3482503.md#Golden_Boot) with six goals as well as the [Golden Ball Award](3482503.md#Golden_Ball) for the best player of the tournament,[^cite_note-storiedicalcio.altervista.org-35] and 40-year-old captain-goalkeeper Dino Zoff became the oldest player to win the World Cup.[^cite_note-36]

Italy subsequently failed to qualify for [UEFA Euro 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1984),[^cite_note-euro1984-37][^cite_note-38] and then entered as reigning champions in the [1986 World Cup](157231.md),[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] but were eliminated by reigning European Champions, France, in the round of 16.[^cite_note-42]

### Vicini years and World Cup runners-up with Sacchi (1986–1994)

In 1986, [Azeglio Vicini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azeglio_Vicini) was appointed as new head coach, replacing Bearzot.[^cite_note-43] He granted a central role to players such as [Walter Zenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Zenga) and [Gianluca Vialli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianluca_Vialli), and conceded a chance to young players coming from the U21 team;[^cite_note-44] Vialli scored goals that gave Italy a [1988 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1988) pass,[^cite_note-45] and he was shown like Altobelli's successor, having his same goal attitude.[^cite_note-46] Both forwards stroke the target in Germany, where the Soviet Union defeated the *Azzurri* in the semi-finals.[^cite_note-47]



[Roberto Baggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Baggio) in 1990

Italy hosted the World Cup for the second time in [1990](157232.md). The Italian attack featured talented forwards [Salvatore Schillaci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Schillaci) and a young [Roberto Baggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Baggio). Italy played nearly all of their matches in Rome and did not concede a single goal in their first five matches; however, they lost the semi-final in Naples to defending champion Argentina. Argentinian player [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona), who played for [Napoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSC_Napoli), made comments prior to the game pertaining to the North–South inequality in Italy and the *[Risorgimento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unification_of_Italy)*, asking Neapolitans to root for Argentina in the game.[^cite_note-48] Italy lost 4–3 on penalty kicks following a 1–1 draw after extra time. Schillaci's first-half opener was equalised in the second half by [Claudio Caniggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Caniggia)'s header for Argentina. [Aldo Serena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_Serena) missed the final penalty kick with [Roberto Donadoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Donadoni) also having his penalty saved by goalkeeper [Sergio Goycochea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Goycochea). Italy went on to defeat England 2–1 in the third-place match in Bari, with Schillaci scoring the winning goal on a penalty to become the tournament's top scorer with six goals.

After failing to qualify for [UEFA Euro 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1992), Vicini was replaced by former [AC Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Milan) coach [Arrigo Sacchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrigo_Sacchi), who brought a new style of play. In November 1993, FIFA ranked Italy No. 1 since the [ranking system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings) was introduced in December 1992.[^cite_note-first-49]

At the [1994 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) in the United States, Italy lost the opening match against [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team) 0–1 at [Giants Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giants_Stadium) in [New Jersey](21648.md). After a 1–0 win against [Norway](203277.md) in [East Rutherford](136878.md) and a 1–1 draw with [Mexico](808402.md) at the [RFK Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RFK_Stadium) in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), Italy advanced from [Group E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup#Group_E) based on goals scored among the four teams tied on points. During their round of 16 match at [Foxboro Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxboro_Stadium) near [Boston](24437894.md), Italy was down 0–1 late against [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team), but Baggio equalised in the 88th minute and a penalty in extra time to take the win.[^cite_note-50] Baggio scored another late goal against Spain at their quarter-final match in Boston to seal a 2–1 win and two goals against [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team) in their semi-final match in New York for another 2–1 win.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] In the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup_Final), which took place in [Los Angeles](18110.md)'s [Rose Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_\(stadium\)) stadium 2,700 miles (4,320 km) and [three time zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_the_United_States) away from the [Northeastern United States](431669.md) where they had played all their previous matches, Italy, who had 24 hours less rest than Brazil, played 120 minutes of scoreless football, taking the match to a [penalty shootout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_shootout_\(football\)), the first time a World Cup final was settled on penalties.[^cite_note-53] Italy lost the subsequent shootout 3–2 after Baggio, who had been playing with the aid of a pain-killer injection[^cite_note-54] and a heavily bandaged hamstring,[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] missed the final penalty kick of the match, shooting over the crossbar.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

### Euro 2000 runners-up (1996–2000)

Italy, still led by Sacchi, qualified for [Euro 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996) in England, but did not progress beyond the group stage. Having defeated Russia 2–1 and losing by the same score against the [Czech Republic](729739.md), Italy required a victory in their final group match against Germany to progress to the quarter-finals. However, [Gianfranco Zola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianfranco_Zola) failed to convert a decisive penalty in a 0–0 draw against the Germans, who eventually won the tournament.[^cite_note-UEFA.com-59]

Finishing second behind England in the [qualification campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_2) for the [1998 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), Italy booked a place at the final tournament after defeating Russia in a [play-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA_play-off\)), with [Pierluigi Casiraghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierluigi_Casiraghi) scoring the winning goal in a 2–1 aggregate victory on 15 November 1997.[^cite_note-bbc_2017-11-60] After finishing first in their [group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_B) and overcoming [Norway](203277.md) in the second round, Italy faced a penalty shoot-out in the [quarterfinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Italy_vs_France), for the third World Cup in a row.[^cite_note-61] The Italian side, where [Alessandro Del Piero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Del_Piero) and Baggio renewed the controversial *staffetta* (lit. 'relay') between Mazzola and Rivera from 1970, held the eventual world champions and host team, [France](250197.md), to a 0–0 draw after extra time, but lost 4–3 in the shoot-out. With two goals scored in this tournament, Baggio remains the only Italian player to have scored in three different FIFA World Cups.[^cite_note-Mondial-62]



Italy (right) line-up ahead of the [UEFA Euro 2000 Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000_Final) against France

&#x20;Two years later, at the [Euro 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000), with four consecutive victories the team led by former captain [Dino Zoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Zoff) made it all the way to the semifinals, facing another penalty shoot-out but emerging victorious over the co-hosts, the Netherlands.[^cite_note-63] Italian goalkeeper [Francesco Toldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Toldo) saved one penalty during the match and two in the shootout, while striker [Francesco Totti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Totti) scored his penalty with a *cucchiaio* (lit. 'spoon') chip.[^cite_note-64] Italy finished the tournament as runners-up, losing the final 2–1 against France (to a [golden goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_goal) in extra time) after conceding an equalising goal just 30 seconds before the expected end of injury time.[^cite_note-65] After the defeat, coach Dino Zoff resigned in protest after being criticised by AC Milan club president and politician [Silvio Berlusconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Berlusconi).[^cite_note-66]

### Trapattoni years (2000–2004)

Giovanni Trapattoni took charge of the team in July 2000 following the resignation of Dino Zoff.[^cite_note-67] Playing in [Group 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_8) of the [2002 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)) process, Italy finished undefeated after facing [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_football_team), [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_national_football_team), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) and [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania_national_football_team). In the [final tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), a 2–0 victory against [Ecuador](1016797.md) with a double from [Christian Vieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Vieri) was followed by a series of controversial matches. During the match against [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team), English referee [Graham Poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Poll) incorrectly disallowed two regular goals resulting in a 2–1 defeat to Italy.[^cite_note-68] Despite two goals being ruled for offsides, a late headed goal from Alessandro Del Piero helped Italy to a 1–1 draw with Mexico, proving enough to advance to the knockout stages.[^cite_note-69]

Co-host country [South Korea](1018627.md) eliminated Italy in the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#South_Korea_vs_Italy) by a score of [2–1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_v_Italy_\(2002_FIFA_World_Cup\)). The match proved controversial with members of the Italian team, most notably striker Francesco Totti and coach [Giovanni Trapattoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Trapattoni), suggesting a conspiracy to eliminate Italy from the competition.[^cite_note-70] Trapattoni even obliquely accused FIFA of ordering the official to ensure a Korean victory so that one of the two host nations would remain in the tournament.[^cite_note-71] The most contentious decisions by the game referee [Byron Moreno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byron_Moreno) were an early penalty awarded to South Korea (saved by Buffon), a [golden goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_goal) by [Damiano Tommasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damiano_Tommasi) incorrectly ruled [offside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offside_\(association_football\)), and the sending off of Totti after being presented with a second yellow card for an alleged [dive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_\(association_football\)) in the penalty area.[^cite_note-72] FIFA president [Sepp Blatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepp_Blatter) stated that the linesmen had been a "disaster" and admitted that Italy suffered from bad offside calls during the group matches, but he denied conspiracy allegations. While questioning Totti's sending off by Moreno, Blatter refused to blame Italy's loss entirely on the referees, stating: "Italy's elimination is not only down to referees and linesmen who made human not premeditated errors. Italy made mistakes both in defense and in attack."[^cite_note-autogenerated1-73]

Trapattoni stayed on and guided the team at [Euro 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004) in Portugal, where after draws against [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) along with a victory over [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team) in [Group C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004_Group_C), Italy were eliminated following a three-way five point tie based on the number of goals scored in matches among the tied teams.[^cite_note-74] Goalkeeper [Gianluigi Buffon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianluigi_Buffon) and then Italian football federation president [Franco Carraro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Carraro) accused the Swedish and Danish teams of fixing the result of their final match (2-2 was the result which consented both teams to advance).[^cite_note-75] Despite calls, then-UEFA spokesperson Robert Faulkner said the organization would not investigate the result.[^cite_note-76]

### 2006 World Cup victory



The crowd at [Circus Maximus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circus_Maximus) in [Rome](25458.md), after Italy scored against France



Italian President [Giorgio Napolitano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Napolitano) congratulates coach [Lippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcello_Lippi) and captain [Cannavaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Cannavaro) after the final match against France.

The Italian Football Federation replaced Trapattoni with [Marcello Lippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcello_Lippi).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] With [controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Serie_A_scandal) plaguing the [domestic league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005-06_Serie_A), Italy entered the [2006 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) as one of the [eight seeded teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup#Seeds).[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] Italy finished first in Group E with wins against Ghana and the Czech Republic and a draw with the United States.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] In the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup#Round_of_16), Italy secured a 1–0 victory over [Australia](4792249.md) with Francesco Totti scoring a penalty.[^cite_note-85] Italy overcame [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_national_football_team), 3–0, after taking an early lead through [Gianluca Zambrotta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianluca_Zambrotta) and additional goals coming from [Luca Toni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luca_Toni).[^cite_note-86] In the semi-finals, Italy beat hosts Germany 2–0 with goals [Fabio Grosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Grosso) and [Alessandro Del Piero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Del_Piero) in the last minutes of extra time.[^cite_note-87]

On 9 July 2006, the *Azzurri* won their fourth World Cup title after defeating France in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_final). French captain [Zinedine Zidane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinedine_Zidane) opened the scoring from the penalty spot in the seventh minute before [Marco Materazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Materazzi) scored from a corner kick, twelve minutes later. The score remained level and during extra-time and Zidane was sent off for [headbutting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headbutt) Materazzi.[^cite_note-88] Italy went on to win the penalty shootout 5–3, with all Italian players scoring their kicks. The decisive penalty goal was scored by Grosso.[^cite_note-89]

FIFA named seven Italian players — [Gianluigi Buffon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianluigi_Buffon), [Fabio Cannavaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Cannavaro), Gianluca Zambrotta, [Andrea Pirlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Pirlo), [Gennaro Gattuso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gennaro_Gattuso), [Francesco Totti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Totti) and Luca Toni — to the 23-man tournament [All Star Team](3482503.md#All-Star_Team).[^cite_note-90] Buffon also won the [Lev Yashin Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yashin_Award), given to the best goalkeeper of the tournament; he conceded only two goals in the tournament's seven matches, the first an own goal by [Zaccardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Zaccardo) and the second from Zidane's penalty kick in the final, and remained unbeaten for 460 consecutive minutes.[^cite_note-91] In honour of Italy winning a fourth World Cup, members of the squad were awarded the [Italian Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit_of_the_Italian_Republic) of *Cavaliere*.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

### The decline of the world champions (2006–2010)

Marcello Lippi, who had announced his resignation three days after the World Cup triumph, was replaced by [Roberto Donadoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Donadoni).[^cite_note-94] Italy qualified for Euro 2008 by winning their [group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008_qualifying_Group_B) ahead of France. On 14 February 2007, Italy climbed to first in the [FIFA World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings) for the second time.[^cite_note-first-49] At [Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008), the *Azzurri* lost 3–0 to the Netherlands in the opening match of the group stage. The following game against [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_football_team) ended 1–1 thanks to a penalty save from [Gianluigi Buffon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianluigi_Buffon).[^cite_note-zambrotta-95] Italy would win their final group game against France 2–0, a rematch of the 2006 World Cup final. The *Azzurri* were eliminated in the quarter-finals on penalties to eventual champions Spain. Within a week of the game, Roberto Donadoni's contract was terminated and Marcello Lippi was rehired as coach.[^cite_note-Lippireturns-96]

Italy qualified for their first ever [FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup) held in South Africa in June 2009 by virtue of winning the 2006 World Cup. They won their opening match of the [tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) against the United States, but subsequent defeats to [Egypt](1022953.md) and Brazil meant that they finished third in the group on goals scored (points level with the US and Egypt), and were eliminated.[^cite_note-97]

At the [2010 World Cup](168079.md) in South Africa, reigning champions Italy were unexpectedly eliminated in the [first round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_F), finishing last place in their group. After being held to 1–1 draws with [Paraguay](804025.md) and [New Zealand](1023035.md) needing equalizers in both games, they suffered a 3–2 loss to [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_national_football_team) having trailed 2-0 and 3-1.[^cite_note-98] It was the first time Italy failed to win a single game at a World Cup tournament, and in doing so became only the third nation to be eliminated in the first round while being reigning World Cup champions.[^cite_note-99] Marcello Lippi stepped down after Italy's World Cup campaign and was replaced by [Cesare Prandelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Prandelli), although Lippi's successor had already been announced before the tournament.[^cite_note-100]

### Euro 2012 runners-up and fluctuating results (2012–2018)



The national football team of Italy before the UEFA Euro 2012 final, [Olympic Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olimpiyskiy_National_Sports_Complex), Kyiv, 1 July 2012

At [UEFA Euro 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012), Italy finished second in their group behind Spain, which earned them a quarter-final tie against England. After a mostly one-sided affair in which Italy failed to take their chances, they managed to beat England on penalties.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] In the semi-final against Germany, two first-half goals by [Mario Balotelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Balotelli) saw the Italians through to the final. In the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_Final), Italy fell to a 4–0 defeat to Spain.[^cite_note-103]

During the [2013 Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_FIFA_Confederations_Cup) in Brazil, Italy reached the semi-finals, losing 7–6 on penalties to Spain.[^cite_note-104] Italy did manage to beat Uruguay in the third place play-off. At the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md), Italy defeated England 2–1 in their first match before succumbing to underdogs [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team) 1–0 in the second group stage match.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106] In Italy's last group match, they were knocked out by Uruguay 1–0, in a controversial match, where Italian player [Claudio Marchisio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Marchisio) was controversially sent off whilst Uruguay's [Luis Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Su%C3%A1rez_\(Uruguayan_footballer\)) bit Italy's [Giorgio Chiellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Chiellini) without any sanction.[^cite_note-marchisio-107][^cite_note-108] Shortly after this loss, coach Cesare Prandelli resigned.[^cite_note-109]

Former [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_FC) manager [Antonio Conte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Conte) was selected to replace Prandelli. On 10 October 2015, Italy qualified for [Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016), courtesy of a 3–1 win over [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_national_football_team);[^cite_note-110] the result meant that Italy had gone 50 games unbeaten in European qualifiers.[^cite_note-111] On 4 April 2016, it was announced that Antonio Conte would step down as Italy coach after Euro 2016 to become head coach of [Chelsea](7473.md).[^cite_note-112] The 23-man squad was initially criticised by many fans and members of the media for its lack of quality,[^cite_note-never_underestimate-113] which saw notable absences, such as Andrea Pirlo and [Sebastian Giovinco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Giovinco) being controversially left out.[^cite_note-114] Italy opened Euro 2016 with a 2–0 victory over Belgium and qualified thanks to a win against Sweden in the second match, which made their defeat 1–0 to Ireland in the final group stage match irrelevant for access to the round of 16.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] Italy subsequently defeated reigning European champions Spain 2–0 in the round of 16.[^cite_note-117] However, Italy were defeated by reigning [world champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_final) Germany in the quarter-finals on penalties, after a 1–1 draw.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-stride-119]

#### Failure to qualify for 2018 World Cup

After Conte's planned departure following Euro 2016, [Gian Piero Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gian_Piero_Ventura) took over as manager for the team.[^cite_note-120] During [qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) for the 2018 World Cup, Italy finished second in Group G, five points behind Spain.[^cite_note-bbc_2017-11-60][^cite_note-121] Italy would compete in the [play-off round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round) against Sweden, where they lost 1–0 on aggregate and therefore eliminated; the first time that Italy had failed to qualify for the World Cup since [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-122] Following the match, veterans [Andrea Barzagli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Barzagli), [Daniele De Rossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniele_De_Rossi) and captain [Gianluigi Buffon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianluigi_Buffon) all declared their retirement from the national team.[^cite_note-123] On 15 November 2017, Ventura was dismissed as head coach,[^cite_note-124] and on 20 November 2017, [Carlo Tavecchio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Tavecchio) resigned as president of the Italian Football Federation.[^cite_note-125] [Luigi Di Biagio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Di_Biagio) was called as [caretaker manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caretaker_manager) and led the team in subsequent friendlies in March 2018, including the last of Buffon's 176 appearances in a friendly against Argentina.[^cite_note-126]

### Resurgence and second European title (2018–2021)

On 14 May 2018, [Roberto Mancini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Mancini) was announced as the new manager.[^cite_note-127] On 16 August 2018, in the first FIFA World Rankings update that followed the World Cup, Italy dropped to their lowest-ever ranking of 21st.[^cite_note-128] On 18 November 2019, Italy finished [Euro 2020 qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_qualifying) with ten wins in all ten matches, becoming only the sixth side to qualify for a European Championship with a perfect record.[^cite_note-129] On 17 March 2020, UEFA confirmed that [Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020) would be postponed by one year due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_association_football).[^cite_note-130]



President of Italy [Sergio Mattarella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Mattarella) (right) congratulates captain [Chiellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Chiellini) in Rome, the day after Italy's triumph at [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020)

At the delayed Euro 2020, Italy finished top of Group A, ahead of [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team), [Switzerland](681862.md), and [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_football_team). Being one of the host nations, Italy played all three group games at Rome's [Stadio Olimpico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Olimpico), and it became the first team in European Championship history to win each group stage match without conceding.[^cite_note-131] In the round of 16, Italy defeated [Austria](695226.md) 2–1 at [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md) after extra time.[^cite_note-132] In the quarter-finals, Italy secured a 2–1 victory over [Belgium](679716.md), before beating [Spain](609825.md) on penalties in the semi-finals.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

In the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_final), on 11 July 2021, Italy won the European Championship defeating hosts [England](9904.md) at Wembley Stadium on penalties after a 1–1 draw,[^cite_note-135] for their second European title and their first since [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1968). Goalkeeper [Gianluigi Donnarumma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianluigi_Donnarumma) also won the *Player of the Tournament* award, given to the best player of the tournament.[^cite_note-136] On 16 July, all members of the European Championship-winning squad were awarded the [Italian Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Merit_of_the_Italian_Republic) of *Cavaliere*.[^cite_note-137]

In October 2021, Italy participated in the [UEFA Nations League Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Nations_League_Finals) as hosts, and lost the semi-final against Spain, 2–1 at the [San Siro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Siro).[^cite_note-138] This match meant the end of the record 37-game unbeaten run and the first defeat for Italy in more than three years. Four days later, Italy won the third-place play-off, 2–1 against Belgium.[^cite_note-139]

### Italian football crisis (2022–present)

#### Failure to qualify for 2022 World Cup and disappointment at Euro 2024

On 15 November 2021, Italy drew 0–0 with [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_national_football_team) in their final [2022 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_C) match and finished in second place, two points behind Switzerland.[^cite_note-\:0-140] On 24 March 2022, Italy lost 1–0 in the semi-final of the [play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round) against [North Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Macedonia_national_football_team), their first-ever World Cup qualifying home defeat, therefore, failing to qualify for the World Cup for a second consecutive time.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

On 1 June 2022, Italy took part in the [CONMEBOL–UEFA Cup of Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL%E2%80%93UEFA_Cup_of_Champions) match, rebranded as the [2022 Finalissima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Finalissima), losing 3–0 against defending [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) champion [Argentina](454699.md).[^cite_note-143] In September, Italy qualified for the [2023 UEFA Nations League Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UEFA_Nations_League_Finals) after beating [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) 2–0 in [Budapest](36787.md).[^cite_note-144] On 15 June 2023, Italy played the semi-final against Spain, losing 2–1.[^cite_note-145] Three days later, Italy won the third-place match 3–2 against the Netherlands.[^cite_note-146]

In August 2023, after five years as Italy's coach, Mancini resigned.[^cite_note-147] [Luciano Spalletti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Spalletti) was chosen as the new coach,[^cite_note-148] and led the team in the last six games of [Euro 2024 qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_qualifying), managing to achieve direct qualification to the [European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024).[^cite_note-149] Italy were eliminated from the tournament in the round of 16 following a 2–0 loss to [Switzerland](681862.md),[^cite_note-150] leading the minister for Sport [Andrea Abodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Abodi) to call the Euro campaign a "failure".[^cite_note-151]

#### Failure to qualify for 2026 World Cup



The Italy XI that were defeated by Norway in June 2025

In June 2025, after a 3–0 defeat against [Norway](203277.md) in the first match of the [2026 FIFA World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)) in [Oslo](22309.md), Spalletti was relieved from his duties,[^cite_note-152] and former [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) champion [Gennaro Gattuso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gennaro_Gattuso) took his place.[^cite_note-153] Gattuso's debut as Italy's *commissario tecnico* came on 5 September in a 5–0 win against [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia_national_football_team).[^cite_note-154] In November of the same year, Italy qualified for the [play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round) for the third time in a row following a 4–1 home defeat to Norway.[^cite_note-155] Italy defeated [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_national_football_team) 2–0 in the semi-final,[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157] but on 31 March 2026, Italy failed to qualify for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) after losing away to [Bosnia and Herzegovina](1008974.md) on penalties following a 1–1 draw, marking the third straight World Cup for which they failed to qualify.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] Two days later, [Gabriele Gravina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriele_Gravina) resigned as President of the Italian Federation,[^cite_note-160] and [Buffon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianluigi_Buffon) and Gattuso resigned as head of delegation and head coach, respectively.[^cite_note-161]

On 10 April, Italy U21 coach [Silvio Baldini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Baldini) was named caretaker manager for Italy's friendlies in June,[^cite_note-162] both won despite having called up only Under-21 players with the exception of captain Donnarumma.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164] On 28 July, newly elected [FIGC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Football_Federation) president [Giovanni Malagò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Malag%C3%B2) announced [Roberto Mancini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Mancini)’s return as head coach of the national team and that [Claudio Ranieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Ranieri) would take on the role of technical director of the Italian Federation.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166]

## Rivalries

- **[Italy vs. Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil%E2%80%93Italy_football_rivalry)**: matches between the nations are known as the World Derby ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Clássico Mundial*).

  [^cite_note-167]

  &#x20;The most successful football nations in the world, they have achieved nine [World Cups](11370.md) between one another. Since their first match at the 1938 World Cup, they have played against each other a total of five times in the World Cup, most notably in the [1970 World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_final) and the [1994 World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup_final) in which Brazil won 4–1 and 3–2 on [penalties](511302.md) after a goalless draw respectively.

  [^cite_note-notablematches2-168]

- **[Italy vs. France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France%E2%80%93Italy_football_rivalry)**: matches between the two nations officially began on 15 May 1910, Italy's first recorded match ending in a 6–2 victory.

  [^cite_note-ci_risiamo2-169]

  [^cite_note-storia_recente2-170]

  &#x20;Notable matches in the [World Cup](11370.md) and the [European Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) include the [2006 World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_final), when the Italians defeated the French 5–3 in the penalty shoot-out, after a 1–1 draw, and the [2000 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000), won by France with an extra-time [golden goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_goal) by [David Trezeguet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Trezeguet).

  [^cite_note-171]

- **[Italy vs. Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany%E2%80%93Italy_football_rivalry)**: matches between the two nations have cumulated in five matches in the World Cup, including the "[Game of the Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_v_West_Germany_\(1970_FIFA_World_Cup\))", the 1970 World Cup semifinal between the two countries that Italy won 4–3 in extra time, with five of the seven goals coming in extra time.

  [^cite_note-172]

  &#x20;Italy defeated West Germany 3–1 in the [1982 FIFA World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup_final). Germany has also won three [European Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) while Italy has won it twice. The two countries have faced each other four times in the European Championship, with three draws (one German penalty shoot-out victory) and one Italian victory.

  [^cite_note-173]

  &#x20;Germany had never defeated Italy in a major tournament match until their victory in the Euro 2016 quarterfinals, on penalties (though statistically considered a draw), with all Germany's other wins over Italy being in [friendly competitions](977090.md#International_football).

  [^cite_note-stride-119]

- **[Italy vs. Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy%E2%80%93Spain_football_rivalry)**: this is a less heated and less heartfelt rivalry for the Italians, especially when compared to those with Germany and France. Matches between Italy and Spain are known as the Mediterranean Derby ([Spanish](26825.md): *Rivalidad futbolística Italia-España)*, named after the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) that separates the two nations.

  [^cite_note-med212-174]

  &#x20;Matches between them have been contested since 1920, and although they are not immediate geographical neighbours, their rivalry at international level is enhanced by the strong performances of their representative clubs in UEFA competitions.

  [^cite_note-175]

  [^cite_note-176]

  &#x20;Since the quarterfinal match between them at [Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008) (won by Spain in the penalty shoot-out), the rivalry has renewed, as Italy and Spain played against each other also in the [UEFA Euro 2012 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_final) (which Spain won 4–0) in the Round of 16 of [UEFA Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016) (which Italy won 2-0) and in the [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020) semifinal (won by Italy in the penalty shoot-out).

  [^cite_note-177]

  [^cite_note-bbc_report2-178]

- **Italy vs. England**: this rivalry has been renewed in recent times.

  [^cite_note-179]

  [^cite_note-180]

  [^cite_note-181]

  &#x20;Italy and England played against each other at [UEFA Euro 1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1980), at the [1990 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_1990), in the quarter-finals of [UEFA Euro 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012), at the [2014 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_2014) and most notably in the [Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_2020) final.

Italy's classic kit, worn by Giacinto Facchetti in 1968

The first shirt worn by the Italy national team, in its debut against France on 15 May 1910, was white. The choice of colour was due to the fact that a decision about the appearance of the kit had not yet been made, so it was decided not to have a colour, which was why white was chosen.[^cite_note-passionemaglie.it-182] After two games, for a friendly against Hungary in Milan on 6 January 1911, the white shirt was replaced by a blue jersey (specifically [Savoy azure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy_azure))—blue being the border colour of the royal [House of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Savoy) crest used on the flag of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) (1861–1946); the shirt was accompanied by white shorts and black socks (which later became blue).[^cite_note-passionemaglie.it-182] The team later became known as *gli Azzurri* (the Blues).[^cite_note-passionemaglie.it-182][^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185]

In the 1930s, Italy wore a black kit, ordered by [the fascist regime of Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_under_Fascism_\(1922%E2%80%931943\)). The black kit debuted on 17 February 1935 in a friendly against France at the [Stadio Nazionale PNF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Nazionale_PNF) in Rome.[^cite_note-186] A blue shirt, white shorts and black socks were worn at the [1936 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Summer_Olympics) in Berlin the following year. At the [1938 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup) in France, the all-black kit was worn once in the match against France.[^cite_note-187]

After World War II, the fascist regime fell and the [monarchy was abolished in 1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_Italian_institutional_referendum). The same year saw the [birth of the Italian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_of_the_Italian_Republic), and the blue-and-white kit was reinstated. The cross of the former Royal House of Savoy was removed from the [flag of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Italy), and consequently from the national team's badge, now consisting solely of the *[Tricolore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Italy)*. For the [1954 FIFA World Cup](60523.md), the country's name in Italian, *ITALIA*, was placed above the tricolour shield, and for the [1982 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup), *FIGC*, the abbreviation of the [Italian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Football_Federation), was incorporated into the badge.[^cite_note-passionemaglie.it-182]

In 1983, to celebrate the previous year's World Cup win, three gold [stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_\(football_badge\)) replaced *ITALIA* above the tricolour, representing their three World Cup victories. In 1984, a round emblem was launched, featuring the three stars, the inscriptions *ITALIA* and *FIGC*, and the tricolour.[^cite_note-passionemaglie.it-182]

The first known kit manufacturer was [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) in 1974. From 2003 to 2022, the kit was made by [Puma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puma_SE).[^cite_note-passionemaglie.it-182] Since the 2000s, an all-blue uniform including blue shorts has occasionally been used, particularity in international tournaments.[^cite_note-passionemaglie.it-182] After Italy's 2006 World Cup victory, a fourth star was added to the tricolour badge. In March 2022, after almost 20 years with Puma, it was announced that Adidas will be Italy's kit manufacturer from 2023 as part of a four-year deal.[^cite_note-188]

During the earliest days of Italian nation football, it was common for a Technical Commission to be appointed. The Commission took the role that a standard coach would currently play. Ever since 1967, the national team has been controlled only by the coach. ## Head-to-head record

Main article: [Italy national football team head-to-head record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_football_team_head-to-head_record)

As of 2 October 2026, the complete official match record of the Italian national team comprises [902 matches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_national_football_team_head-to-head_record): 482 wins, 243 draws and 177 losses.[^cite_note-210] During these matches, the team scored 1,575 times and conceded 890 goals. Italy's highest winning margin is nine goals, which was achieved against the [United States](582488.md) in 1948 (9–0). Their longest winning streak is 13 wins,[^cite_note-32unbeaten13consecutive-211] and their unbeaten record is 37 consecutive official matches.[^cite_note-212]
