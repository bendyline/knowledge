# Sports in the United States

Not to be confused with [Sports in North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_North_America).





















From top, left to right: [U.S. baseball team](4119270.md), [U.S. men's basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_men's_national_basketball_team), [Danica Patrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danica_Patrick) driving at the [IndyCar Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndyCar_Series), [Sydney McLaughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_McLaughlin), [American football](18951490.md) [Army–Navy Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army%E2%80%93Navy_Game), the [skateboarder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skateboarder) [Tony Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Hawk), the 1980 [Miracle on Ice](295721.md) hockey game, [Christian Pulisic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Pulisic) as part of the [U.S. men's soccer team](582488.md), the start of the 2015 [Daytona 500](39021.md), and golfer [Michelle Wie West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Wie_West).

**Sports in the United States** are a significant aspect of the [nation's culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_United_States). Historically, the most popular sport has been [baseball](3850.md); however, since the mid-20th century [American football](18951490.md) has slowly become the most popular spectator sport. Additionally, [basketball](3921.md) and [ice hockey](14790.md) saw a significant growth during this period due to the advent of television. [Soccer](10568.md) has emerged as the fourth-most-popular sport in the 21st century, surpassing ice hockey.

In the first half of the 20th century [boxing](4243.md), [college rowing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_rowing_in_the_United_States),[^cite_note-1] and [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football) were among the most popular sports after baseball. [Golf](19568112.md), [tennis](29773.md), and [college basketball](768856.md) are other spectator sports with longstanding popularity. Tennis is currently considered to be the sixth most popular sport in the United States. Most recently, [mixed martial arts](228344.md) has been breaking records in attendance and broadcast viewership for all [combat sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_sports).

Roughly 27% of United States-based sports fans show an interest in soccer, compared to a global average of 40%. By contrast, American football is the most popular sport in the United States with 52%. Basketball with 43% and baseball with 37% are further behind.[^cite_note-2] Approximately 17% of the people in the United States follow ice hockey.[^cite_note-3]

The [major professional sports leagues in the United States and Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada) are [Major League Baseball](38776.md) (MLB), the [National Basketball Association](22093.md) (NBA), the [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL), the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL), and [Major League Soccer](71802.md) (MLS).

Just in the year 2024, the NFL generated over \$23 billion, making them the most valued professional sports league in the United States and the world.[^cite_note-4] With the addition of multiple TV/Streaming contracts that have been recently signed, the revenue is only expected to increase.[^cite_note-5]

The market for professional sports in the United States is about \$69 billion as of 2013, roughly 50% larger than that of all of Europe, the Middle East, and Africa combined.[^cite_note-6] All these leagues enjoy wide-ranging domestic media coverage and, except for MLS, all are considered the preeminent leagues in their respective sports in the world. Although American football does not have a substantial following in other nations, the NFL does have the highest average attendance (67,254) of any professional sports league in the world. MLB has the second highest average attendance of any sports league in the United States (29,293),[^cite_note-7] followed by MLS, the NBA, and the NHL. Of these five American-based leagues, all but the NFL have at least one team in [Canada](5042916.md).

Professional teams in all major sports in the United States operate as [franchises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_sports_league_organization) within a league, meaning that a team may move to a different city if the team's owners believe there would be a financial benefit, but franchise moves are usually subject to some form of league-level approval.[^cite_note-8] All major sports leagues use a similar type of regular-season schedule with a post-season [playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playoff) tournament, instead of crowning as champions the team with the best won/loss record against all the other teams. Significantly, in all the major sports leagues in the United States (MLB, NBA, NFL, NHL, and MLS), the teams which set the best regular season record in the history of their respective leagues did not go on to win the championship, even as many low-seeded teams in the playoffs have in fact gone on to win the title. These are factors which are cited as adding to the excitement and relevance of the post-season, and justifying all teams' attempts throughout the regular season to at least qualify for the postseason even as wild cards.

In addition to the major league–level organizations, several sports also have professional [minor leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_league), active in smaller cities across the country. But as in Canada and [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Australia), sports leagues in the United States do not practice [promotion and relegation](648049.md), unlike most sports leagues in Europe.

Another notable distinction is that most sports fans in the United States tend to follow more than one team sport, depending on the time of year, unlike the case in many parts of the world where fans might avidly follow only one team sport such as soccer or baseball. Thus, it is possible for an American sports fan who follows multiple sports to spend practically every single day of the year watching professional sports, since there is no time of year when all the major professional leagues would be off-season.

Furthermore, sports are particularly associated with [education in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_United_States), with most [high schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_schools_in_the_United_States) and [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University) having organized sports, while [college sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_athletics_in_the_United_States) competitions play a very important role in the American sporting culture. In fact, college basketball and college football are more popular than professional sports in some parts of the country, a unique sporting feature in the United States. The major sanctioning body for college sports is the [National Collegiate Athletic Association](60706.md) (NCAA). Colleges collectively receive billions of dollars from TV deals, sponsorships, and ticket sales. In 2019, the total revenue generated by NCAA athletic departments added up to \$18.9 billion.[^cite_note-9]

Based on the [Olympic Games](22576.md), [world championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_championship), and other major competitions in respective sports, the United States is the most successful nation in the world in baseball, basketball, athletics, swimming, lacrosse, beach volleyball, figure skating, tennis, golf, boxing, diving, shooting, rowing and snowboarding, and is one of the top five most successful nations in ice hockey, wrestling, gymnastics, volleyball, speed skating, alpine skiing, bobsleigh, equestrian, sailing, cycling, weightlifting and archery, among others. This makes the United States the most successful sports nation in the world. The United States has been referred to by some as "the hegemon of world sports".[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The United States has placed first in the Summer Olympic medal table [19 times out of 30 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Olympic_teams_by_medals_won#List_by_team) and 29 appearances. Unlike most other nations, the United States government does not provide funding for sports nor for the [United States Olympic & Paralympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Olympic_&_Paralympic_Committee). The United States Olympic contingent is additionally the only Olympic contingent in the world to receive no government funding; neither training and development costs nor prize money are provided by the U.S. national government.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-nationalreview\.com-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-\:0-21]

With American sporting achievements including winning the Summer Olympic medal table 4 straight times (2012, 2016, 2020 and 2024) and in 7 of the 8 Summer Olympics between 1996-2024, winning the 2015 and 2019 FIFA Women's World Cups and making the 2011 final, making the semifinals in 8 of the 9 FIFA Women's World Cups, the finals in 5 of the 9 Women's World Cups, winning 4 of them, and winning 5 of the 8 women's Olympic soccer tournaments (where full teams compete), winning the last 5 straight men's Olympic basketball tournaments, from 2008-2024, and the last 8 straight women's Olympic basketball tournaments from 1996-2024, winning the men's and women's olympic ice hockey gold medals in 2026, winning a record amount of gold medals (12) in the 2026 Winter Olympics, winning the last 6 straight all-around gymnastic Olympic gold medals from 2004-2024, winning the most gold medals in swimming in each of the last 9 Summer Olympics (1992-present), winning the most gold medals in athletics in each of the last 11 Summer Olympics (1984-present), winning 11 of the 14 World Lacrosse Championships, winning the most all time Olympic gold medals in figure skating (19), winning the most all time Olympic gold medals in snowboarding (17), and the most golds in 6 of the 7 Olympics it was held, winning 3 women's tennis Grand Slam singles titles since 2023 (2023 US Open, 2025 Australian Open, 2025 French Open), and making 2 of the last 3 US Open men's tennis finals (2024 and 2026), winning 27 of the last 39 men's major golf championships since 2017, while having a sizable portion of their athletic pool playing American football, there is a question about whether the United States is currently in a sporting golden age, or if American dominance in sports is the norm.&#x20;

According to the [American Time Use Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Time_Use_Survey), 23.4 percent of the civilian population age 15 and older participated in sports, exercise, or recreation on an average day in 2025. The rate was 25.7 percent for men and 21.1 percent for women, and participants spent an average of 1.47 hours on those activities on days when they participated.[^cite_note-22]

A [Grand entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalcade) at the 2018 Boswell FFA Rodeo in [Boswell, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boswell,_Oklahoma).

Lacrosse developed among the [Indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) of the [Eastern Woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Woodlands) and the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) region as far back as 1100 AD, making it the first sport played on the American continent.[^cite_note-23] [American football](18951490.md), [arena football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_football), [baseball](3850.md), [softball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softball), and [indoor soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_soccer) evolved out of older British ([Rugby football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_football), [British baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_baseball), [rounders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rounders), and [association football](10568.md)) sports.[^cite_note-The_Sports_Rules_Book-24] [Basketball](3921.md), [volleyball](32558.md), [beach volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beach_volleyball), [racquetball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racquetball), [pickleball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickleball), [skateboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skateboarding), [snowboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowboarding), [ultimate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_\(sport\)), [windsurfing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windsurfing), and [water skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_skiing) are fully American inventions,[^cite_note-The_Sports_Rules_Book-24] some of which have become popular in other countries and worldwide.[^cite_note-25]

## Olympics

Main article: [United States at the Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_at_the_Olympics)



[Michael Phelps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Phelps) celebrates after winning his eighth gold medal in the [2008 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics)



[Francis Olympic Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Olympic_Field) of [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis), site of the [1904 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_Summer_Olympics)

American athletes have won a total of 2,764 medals (1,105 of them gold) at the [Summer Olympic Games](27566.md) and another 330 (114 of them gold) at the [Winter Olympic Games](34069.md), making the United States the most prolific medal-winning nation in the history of the Olympics. The U.S. is ranked first in the all-time medal table even if [all the incarnations of Russia and Germany are combined](3729318.md#Total,_all_Olympics_\(including_precursors\)), leading the second-placed Russians by 430 gold and 957 total medals. These achievements are even more impressive considering the fact that the American Olympic team remains the only in the world to receive no government funding.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-nationalreview\.com-19][^cite_note-\:0-21]

The United States hosted both Summer and Winter Games in 1932, and has hosted more Games than any other country – eight times, four times each for the Summer and Winter Games:

- The [1904 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_Summer_Olympics) in St. Louis, [1932 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_Summer_Olympics) and [1984 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Summer_Olympics) in Los Angeles; and the [1996 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Summer_Olympics) in Atlanta;

- The [1932 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_Winter_Olympics) and [1980 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Winter_Olympics) in Lake Placid, New York; the [1960 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Winter_Olympics) in Squaw Valley, California; and the [2002 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Winter_Olympics) in Salt Lake City, Utah.

Los Angeles will host the Summer Olympics for a third time in [2028](12695048.md). Salt Lake City will host the Winter Olympics for a second time in [2034](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2034_Winter_Olympics), marking the tenth Olympics hosted in the United States.

The United States has won the [most gold and overall medals in the Summer Olympic Games](3729318.md#Summer_Olympics_\(including_precursors\)), even if the medal totals of the Soviet Union/CIS and Russia are combined, and has topped the medal table 19 times.[^cite_note-Bolavip-31] The country has won the second most gold and overall medals in the Winter Olympic Games, behind Norway, but has topped the medal table only one time, in 1932. If all of Germany's and Russia's incarnations are combined, the United States slips to fourth in the [all-time Winter Olympic Games table](3729318.md#Winter_Olympics_\(including_precursors\)).

### Golf

[Jack Nicklaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Nicklaus) is widely regarded as the greatest golfer of all time, winning a total of 18 career major championships.



[Mickey Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Wright), often regarded as one of the most dominant players in the history of women's golf

Golf is one of the most popular participation sports in the United States, with approximately 24 million people playing golf on a regular basis as of 2023.[^cite_note-32] Golf's origins can be traced back to 15th century Scotland, where players would hit a pebble around sandy dunes using a stick or primitive club. The game has evolved over centuries into the version played today, which involves hitting a small, dimpled ball into each hole on a course in as few strokes as possible.

Introduced to America in the late 19th century, it quickly gained favor among the upper class. The United States Golf Association was founded in 1894 to establish rules. Iconic courses like Augusta National and Pebble Beach have hosted legendary tournaments.

Since the 1970s, the landscape of golf in the United States has seen significant advancements, particularly in the recognition of female players. This has been highlighted by increased support for women's golf programs, the expansion of women's tournaments, and greater representation of female golfers at all levels of the sport.[^cite_note-33]

In 2020, nearly 25 million people or around 8% of the total population of the U.S., played golf on a golf course in the United States, according to the National Golf Foundation.[^cite_note-34]

### Boxing

The United States became the center of [professional boxing](4283645.md) in the early 20th century.[^cite_note-35] The [National Boxing Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Boxing_Association) was founded in 1921 and began to sanction title fights. [Joe Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Louis) was an American professional boxer who competed from 1934 to 1951. He reigned as the world heavyweight champion from 1937 to 1949, and is widely considered to be one of the greatest heavyweight boxers of all-time.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-PBS1-39] Louis is widely regarded as the first person of African-American descent to achieve the status of a nationwide hero within the United States, and was also a focal point of anti-Nazi sentiment leading up to and during World War II.[^cite_note-matters-40] Since the late 1990s, boxing has declined in popularity.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

As of 2021, boxing participation in the United States reached approximately 6.7 million people, indicating a robust growth in the sport's popularity.[^cite_note-44]

### American football

Main article: [American football in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football_in_the_United_States)

Football has the most participants of any sport at both high school and college levels, the vast majority of its participants being male.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-census.gov-54] Millions watch [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football) throughout the fall months, and some communities, particularly in rural areas, place great emphasis on their local [high school football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_football) teams. The popularity of college and high school football in areas such as the [Southern United States](179553.md) ([Southeastern Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_Conference)) and the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains) ([Big 12 Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_12_Conference) and [Big Ten Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference)) stems largely from the fact that these areas historically generally did not possess markets large enough for a professional team.[^cite_note-55] Nonetheless, college football has a rich history in the United States, predating the NFL by decades, and fans and alumni are generally very passionate about their teams.



The [Dallas Cowboys](8121.md) playing against the [Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Broncos) in [Super Bowl XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XII).

The [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL) has the highest average [attendance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sports_attendance_figures) (67,591) of any professional sports league in the world and has the highest [revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_professional_sports_leagues_by_revenue)[^cite_note-urlMajor_sports_leagues_all_make_a_lot_of_money,_heres_how_they_do_it:,_Major_sports_leagues_all_make_a_lot_of_money,_heres_how_they_do_it:-56] out of any single professional sports league.[^cite_note-NFL_is_world's_best_attended_pro_sports_league-57] The NFL has two conferences, the [AFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Football_Conference) and the [NFC.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_Conference) The AFC has 4 divisions (AFC East, AFC North, AFC South, and AFC West). The NFC also has 4 divisions (NFC East, NFC North, NFC South, and NFC West.) Each division has 4 teams, with a total of 32 teams in the NFL.[^cite_note-58]

Although historically lower-paying than the NBA, the WNBA has seen improvements in player salaries and sponsorships in recent years; particularly with the arrival of [Caitlin Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Clark) in the WNBA in 2024; Clark's arrival brought with her a "[Caitlin Clark effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caitlin_Clark_effect)" of a large fanbase that substantially increased ticket sales and television viewership compared to the league's baseline.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

[Baseball](3850.md) and a variant, [softball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softball), are popular participatory sports in the U.S. Baseball was the first professional sport in the United States.[^cite_note-urlGovernment,_Community,_and_Sports_Teams:_Tax_Credits_-_The_Policy_Circle-68][^cite_note-urlMajor_Professional_Sports_Leagues:_The_US_&_Canada_|_The_Daily_Gazette,_Major_Professional_Sports_Leagues:_The_US_&_Canada_|_The_Daily_Gazette-69][^cite_note-urlWhat_Is_the_Oldest_Major_Sports_League_in_the_U.S.?-70] The highest level of baseball in the U.S. and the world is [Major League Baseball](38776.md) (MLB). There are a total of 30 MLB teams. The [World Series](23489161.md) of Major League Baseball is the culmination of the sport's postseason each October. It is played between the winner of each of the two leagues, the [American League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_League) and the [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_\(baseball\)), and the winner is determined through a best-of-seven playoff.

With 27 World Series, the [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees) have won more titles than any other U.S. major professional sports franchise. The Yankees' chief rivals, the [Boston Red Sox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Red_Sox), also enjoy a following in [Boston](24437894.md) and throughout [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England). The [Philadelphia Phillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies) of the National League are the oldest continuous, one-name, one-city franchise in all of [professional American sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_teams_of_the_United_States_and_Canada),[^cite_note-phils1800-71] and have a fanbase throughout [Philadelphia](50585.md) and the [Delaware Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Valley), dubbed as the "Meanest Fans in America".[^cite_note-72]

Every four years in March, the [World Baseball Classic](1877416.md) is held, which is the most prestigious international baseball competition.[^cite_note-73]

With an average attendance of over 21,000 per game (prior to [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_North_America)), [Major League Soccer](71802.md) has the third-highest average attendance of any sports league in the U.S. after the [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL) and [Major League Baseball](38776.md) (MLB),[^cite_note-ReferenceC-74] and is the ninth-highest attended professional soccer league worldwide.[^cite_note-mlssoccer.com-75]

In 2024, the NWSL received competition as a top-level league with the launch of the [USL Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Super_League) (USLS), which started play with 8 teams. Shortly before its first season, USLS received Division I sanctioning from the U.S. Soccer Federation, placing it at the same level as the NWSL. Unlike most U.S. soccer leagues, USLS is playing a fall-to-spring season, in line with most European leagues and the sport's international calendar.[^cite_note-77]

The [Major Arena Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_Arena_Soccer_League) (MASL) is a North American professional [indoor soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_soccer) league. ### Volleyball

Main article: [Volleyball in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volleyball_in_the_United_States)



The U.S. Women's Volleyball team in 2008.

[Volleyball](32558.md) is played in the United States, especially at the college and university levels. Unlike most Olympic sports which are sponsored widely at the collegiate level for both sexes, the women's college volleyball teams are more common than men's college volleyball teams. In the 2011–12 school year, over 300 schools in NCAA Division I alone (the highest of three NCAA tiers) sponsored women's volleyball at the varsity level,[^cite_note-93] while fewer than 100 schools in all three NCAA divisions combined sponsored varsity men's volleyball, with only 23 of them in Division I.[^cite_note-94] Men's volleyball has grown at the non-scholarship [NCAA Division III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_III) level in the 21st century, with a [national championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_III_men's_volleyball_tournament) established in 2012. As of the most recent 2022 season (2021–22 school year), 113 schools sponsor the sport at that level. At the same time, 26 D-I and 31 D-II members sponsored men's volleyball at the National Collegiate level, defined for the purposes of that sport as the combination of Divisions I and II.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] The [National Volleyball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Volleyball_Association) is the professional men's volleyball league, while the [Pro Volleyball Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Volleyball_Federation) is the professional women's volleyball league.

## Sports media in the United States

See also: [List of TV markets and major sports teams in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_TV_markets_and_major_sports_teams_in_the_United_States)

Half time show at the game between Frisco High vs. Centennial football game.

Sports have been a major part of American broadcasting since the early days of radio.[^cite_note-108] Today, [television networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_network) and [radio networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_network) pay millions (sometimes billions) of dollars for the rights to [broadcast sporting events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcasting_of_sports_events). Contracts between leagues and broadcasters stipulate how often games must be interrupted for [commercials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_commercial). Because of all of the advertisements, broadcasting contracts are very lucrative and account for the biggest chunk of major professional teams' revenues. Broadcasters also covet the television contracts for the major sports leagues (especially in the case of the [NFL](21211.md)) in order to amplify their ability to promote their programming to the audience, especially young and middle-aged adult males.[^cite_note-109]



A group of high school [marching bands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marching_band) performs at the halftime show of the [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_football_bowl_games,_2006-07) [Chick-fil-A Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chick-fil-A_Bowl).

The advent of [cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television) and [satellite television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_dish) has greatly expanded sports offerings on American TV.[^cite_note-Catsis,_1996-110] [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN), the first all-sports cable network in the U.S., went on the air in 1979. It has been followed by several sister networks and competitors. Some sports television networks are national, such as [CBS Sports Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports_Network) and [Fox Sports 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Sports_1), whereas others are regional, such as [NBC Sports Regional Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Sports_Regional_Networks), [Bally Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bally_Sports) and [Spectrum Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum_Sports).[^cite_note-111] General entertainment channels like [TBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TBS_\(U.S._TV_channel\)), [TNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_\(U.S._TV_network\)), and [USA Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Network) also air sports events. Some sports leagues have their own sports networks, such as [NFL Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Network), [MLB Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_Network), [NBA TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_TV), [NHL Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_Network_\(United_States\)), [Big Ten Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Network), [ACC Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACC_Network) and [SEC Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEC_Network). Some sports teams run their own television networks as well.[^cite_note-112]

Sports are also widely broadcast at the local level, ranging from college and professional sports down to (on some smaller stations) recreational and youth leagues.[^cite_note-Catsis,_1996-110] [Internet radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_radio) and [webcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webcast) has allowed these broadcasts to reach a worldwide audience.

## Most popular sports in the United States

In the broadest definition of sports—physical recreation of all sorts—the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), in 2007, found the four most popular sports among the general population of the United States are exercise walking (90 million), exercising with equipment (53 million), swimming (52 million) and camping (47 million). The most popular competitive sport (and fifth most popular recreational sport) is bowling (43 million). Other popular sports included fishing (35 million), bicycling (37 million), weightlifting (33 million), aerobics (30 million), and hiking (28 million).[^cite_note-ReferenceA-117]

According to a January 2018 poll by [Gallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup,_Inc.), 37% of Americans consider football their favorite spectator sport, while 11% prefer basketball, 9% baseball, and 7% soccer. There is some variation by viewer demographics. Men show a stronger preference for football than women, conservatives a stronger preference than liberals, and those over 35 a stronger preference than those under 35. In all groups, however, football is still the most popular. Basketball and soccer are more popular among liberals than conservatives.[^cite_note-118]

For three years in a row, 2021, 2022 and 2023 [pickleball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickleball) was named the fastest growing sport in the United States by the Sports and Fitness Industry Association (SFIA).[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120][^cite_note-SFIA2023-121] Between 2019 and 2023, the SFIA estimated the number of US players increased almost 223% to 13.6 million. In March 2023 the Association of Pickleball Players estimated the number of US pickleball players in the previous 12 months had surpassed 48 million.[^cite_note-122]
