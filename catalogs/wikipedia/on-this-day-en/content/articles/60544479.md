**Władysław Marcin Kosiniak-Kamysz**[^cite_note-1] (born 10 August 1981) is a Polish physician and politician, who has served as the [Deputy Prime Minister of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Poland), and [Minister of National Defence](7425373.md) since 2023. He has also served as the chairman of the [Polish People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Party) (PSL) since 2015. From 2011 to 2015, he was [Minister of Labour and Social Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Family,_Labour_and_Social_Policy_\(Poland\)) in the governments of [Donald Tusk](648724.md) and [Ewa Kopacz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewa_Kopacz). He was a candidate for president in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Polish_presidential_election).

## Early life

### Family

Kosiniak-Kamysz was born in [Kraków](16815.md) in 1981, with his family originating from [Siedliszowice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siedliszowice), a village in [Lesser Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland_Voivodeship). He was brought up in the tradition of the folk movement. His father, [Andrzej Kosiniak-Kamysz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Kosiniak-Kamysz?action=edit\&redlink=1), a doctor and politician, served as [Minister of Health and Social Welfare](12329239.md) under the first non-communist government of [Tadeusz Mazowiecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadeusz_Mazowiecki) where he waged a constant struggle to ensure that in a situation of a constant lack of resources, health care at a technological level began to catch up with the West.[^cite_note-wprost-2] His uncle is [Zenon Kosiniak-Kamysz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenon_Kosiniak-Kamysz), politician and diplomat.[^cite_note-3] His paternal grandfather and namesake, Władysław, born at the beginning of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), served as a soldier in the [13th Wilno Uhlan Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_Wilno_Uhlan_Regiment) and [Farmers' Battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataliony_Ch%C5%82opskie) during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War). After the war, he returned to his hometown of [Bieniaszowice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bieniaszowice), where he ran his farm next to the mouth of the [Dunajec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunajec) to the [Vistula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula), with his grandmother.

### Education

He studied in Kraków, where his parents moved. Kosiniak-Kamysz attended the [Jan III Sobieski High School, Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_III_Sobieski_High_School,_Krak%C3%B3w). He subsequently studied [medical studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_education) at the [Jagiellonian University Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian_University_Medical_College) until 2006. He became an assistant at the Department of Internal Medicine and Rural Medicine of the Jagiellonian University.[^cite_note-gazeta-17-11-2010-4] In 2010, Kosiniak-Kamysz obtained his doctorate in medical science along with [Tomasz Guzik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomasz_Guzik). He received his doctorate based on the thesis labelled "The relationship of gene variation encoding GTP cyclohydrolase with the function of vascular endothelium in patients with type 2 diabetes", in which he studied the genetic determinants of diabetes. Kosiniak-Kamysz conducted scientific research, amongst others at the [Emory University School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University_School_of_Medicine) in [Atlanta](3138.md).

He started his first job at the Department of Internal Diseases and Rural Medicine of the Collegium Medicum of the Jagiellonian University, which he held a medical internship there.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-gazeta-17-11-2010-4] Throughout his studies, Kosiniak-Kamysz was a volunteer of the Volunteer Association of Saint Elijah operated at the [Church of the Visitation of the Blessed Virgin Mary, Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Visitation_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary,_Krak%C3%B3w).

## Political career

Kosiniak-Kamysz has always been associated with the [Polish People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Party). He co-founded the PSL [youth wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_wing) - Young People's Forum. Together they represented the people in [TVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telewizja_Polska) *"Młodzież Kontra"*, in which they interviewed politicians. In this way, they gained their first skills in politics. Kosiniak-Kamysz took part in 12 electoral campaigns of the Polish People's Party. This was the first time during the [2000 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Polish_presidential_election) when he supported [Jarosław Kalinowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaros%C5%82aw_Kalinowski)'s organisation by issuing leaflets, hanging posters and collecting signatures for electoral lists.

In the [2010 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Polish_local_elections), Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz received 763 votes.[^cite_note-wybory2010-6] Since [Jacek Majchrowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacek_Majchrowski) was the mayor of Kraków, he was able to take his seat in the city council of Krakow.[^cite_note-Rada-Krakowa-7]

On 18 August 2011 he was appointed as [Minister of Labor and Social Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Family,_Labour_and_Social_Policy_\(Poland\)) in [Donald Tusk's second cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Cabinet_of_Donald_Tusk).[^cite_note-prezydent-8] He served in this post until 16 November 2015.

In the [2015 parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Polish_parliamentary_election), the PSL and [Civic Platform](23998.md) lost their parliamentary majority, and since then have been in the opposition. Due to the poor election result and because he did not get a mandate, [Janusz Piechociński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janusz_Piechoci%C5%84ski) resigned as party chairman, and Kosiniak-Kamysz succeeded him on 7 November 2015.[^cite_note-9] In 2019, the PSL jointed to the [Polish Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Coalition) with political movement [Kukiz'15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kukiz'15) and political party [Union of European Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_European_Democrats). In [2019 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Polish_parliamentary_election), he received 33 784 votes.[^cite_note-10]

He was one of the main candidates in the [2020 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Polish_presidential_election); in some polls, he obtained the second place, granting him a place in the second round;[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] though the current president [Andrzej Duda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Duda) had a large advantage above him (about 30% or more). Finally, Kosiniak-Kamysz received 2.36%.[^cite_note-13]

Following the [2023 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Polish_parliamentary_election) and the subsequent formation of [Donald Tusk's third government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Cabinet_of_Donald_Tusk), Kosiniak-Kamysz was appointed minister of national defence and deputy prime minister on 13 December.[^cite_note-14]

In May 2024, Kosiniak-Kamysz attracted some criticism after revealing that he has had an emergency backpack at the ready since [Russia's invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in February 2022, with some saying this did not send a reassuring message about Poland's security. Kosiniak-Kamysz claimed his comments were taken out of context, posting on [X](9988187.md) that "Preparedness for crisis situations is not only an obligation, but also our responsibility."[^cite_note-AP_9_May_24-15]

In August 2024, he stated in an interview that Ukraine would not enter the [European Union](9317.md) until the issue of the exhumation of the Polish victims of the [Volhynian Genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynian_Genocide) and their proper remembrance is resolved. His words came as a reaction to Ukrainian Foreign Minister [Dmytro Kuleba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmytro_Kuleba) who suggested that this issue should be left for historians.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

## Personal life

He divorced his first wife in 2016.[^cite_note-18] He married Paulina Kosiniak-Kamysz in 2019, with whom he has two daughters and a son.[^cite_note-19]

He is [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic).[^cite_note-20]
