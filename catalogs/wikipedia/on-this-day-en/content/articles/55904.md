**Belgrade** ([/bɛlˈɡreɪd/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*bel-GRAYD*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key) or [/ˈbɛlɡreɪd/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*BEL-grayd*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-10] [Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): Београд / *Beograd*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'White City', pronounced [\[beǒɡrad\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Serbo-Croatian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/fd/Sr-beograd-native.ogg/Sr-beograd-native.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/fd/Sr-beograd-native.ogg/Sr-beograd-native.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is the [capital](181337.md) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Serbia) of [Serbia](29265.md). It is located at the [confluence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence) of the [Sava](587845.md) and [Danube](21209639.md) rivers and at the crossroads of the [Pannonian Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Basin) and the [Balkan Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkan_Peninsula).[^cite_note-city_of_belgrade-2-11] According to the 2022 census, the population of Belgrade city proper stands at 1,197,114, its contiguous urban area has 1,298,661 inhabitants, while the population of the city's administrative area (which roughly corresponds to its metro area) totals 1,682,720 people.[^cite_note-12] It is one of the [major cities of Southeast Europe](4829.md#Urbanization) and the [third-most populous city on the river Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_on_the_river_Danube).

Belgrade is one of the [oldest continuously inhabited cities in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_continuously_inhabited_cities). One of the most important prehistoric cultures of [Europe](9239.md), the [Vinča culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin%C4%8Da_culture), evolved within the Belgrade area in the 6th millennium BC. In antiquity, [Thraco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracians)-[Dacians](100550.md) inhabited the region and, after 279 BC, [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) settled the city, naming it *[Singidūn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singidunum)*.[^cite_note-city_of_belgrade-3-13] It was [conquered by the Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Serbia) under the reign of [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus) and awarded Roman [city rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_privileges) in the mid-2nd century.[^cite_note-rich-1992-14] It was settled by the [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) in the 520s, and changed hands several times between the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), the [Frankish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankish_Empire), the [Bulgarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire), and the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) before it became the seat of the [Serbian king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Serbian_monarchs) [Stefan Dragutin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Dragutin_of_Serbia) in 1284. Belgrade served as capital of the [Serbian Despotate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Despotate) during the reign of [Stefan Lazarević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Lazarevi%C4%87), and then his successor [Đurađ Branković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90ura%C4%91_Brankovi%C4%87) returned it to the Hungarian king in 1427. Noon bells in support of the Hungarian army against the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) during the [siege in 1456](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Belgrade_\(1456\)) have remained a widespread church tradition to this day. In 1521, Belgrade was conquered by the Ottomans and became the seat of the [Sanjak of Smederevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak_of_Smederevo).[^cite_note-15] It frequently passed from Ottoman to [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) rule, which saw the destruction of most of the city during the [Ottoman–Habsburg wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Habsburg_wars).

Following the [Serbian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Revolution), Belgrade was once again named the capital of Serbia in 1841. [Northern Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemun) remained the southernmost [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) post until 1918, when it was attached to the city, due to former Austro-Hungarian territories becoming part of the new [Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbs,_Croats_and_Slovenes) after [World War I](4764461.md). Belgrade was the capital of [Yugoslavia](34244.md) from its [creation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_of_Yugoslavia) to its dissolution.[^cite_note-16] In a fatally strategic position, the city has been battled over in 115 wars and razed 44 times, being bombed five times and besieged many times.[^cite_note-nurden-2009-17]

Being Serbia's [primate city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate_city), Belgrade has special [administrative status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Serbia) within Serbia.[^cite_note-city_of_belgrade-18] It is the seat of the central government, administrative bodies, and government ministries, as well as home to almost all of the largest Serbian companies, media, and scientific institutions. Belgrade is classified as a Beta-[Global City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city).[^cite_note-19] The city is home to the [University Clinical Centre of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_Centre_of_Serbia), a hospital complex with one of the [largest capacities in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_by_capacity); the [Church of Saint Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Sava), one of the [largest Orthodox church buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_Eastern_Orthodox_church_buildings); and the [Belgrade Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Arena), one of the [largest capacity indoor arenas in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_indoor_arenas_in_Europe).

Belgrade hosted major international events such as the [Danube River Conference of 1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_River_Conference_of_1948), the first [Non-Aligned Movement Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement_Summit) (1961), the first major gathering of the [OSCE](22796.md) (1977–1978), and the [Eurovision Song Contest](9954.md) ([2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2008)), as well as sports events such as the first [FINA World Aquatics Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FINA_World_Aquatics_Championships) ([1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_World_Aquatics_Championships)), [UEFA Euro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) ([1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1976)), [Summer Universiade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universiade) ([2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Summer_Universiade)) and three [EuroBasket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket) championships ([1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_1961), [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_1975), [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_2005)). Belgrade will also host the BIE- Specialized Exhibition [Expo 2027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2027).[^cite_note-20]

### Prehistory

See also: [Prehistoric sites in Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_sites_in_Serbia) and [Prehistory of Southeastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_Southeastern_Europe)



[Vinča culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin%C4%8Da_culture) figurine, 4000–4500 BC.

[Chipped stone tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithic_reduction) found in [Zemun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemun) show that the area around Belgrade was inhabited by [nomadic foragers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer) in the [Palaeolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeolithic) and [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic) eras. Some of these tools are of [Mousterian industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mousterian)—belonging to [Neanderthals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthals) rather than modern humans. [Aurignacian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurignacian) and [Gravettian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravettian) tools have also been discovered near the area, indicating some settlement between 50,000 and 20,000 years ago.[^cite_note-21] The first farming people to settle in the region are associated with the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) [Starčevo culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star%C4%8Devo_culture), which flourished between 6200 and 5200 BC.[^cite_note-22] There are several Starčevo sites in and around Belgrade, including the eponymous site of [Starčevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star%C4%8Devo). The Starčevo culture was succeeded by the [Vinča culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin%C4%8Da_culture) (5500–4500 BC), a more sophisticated farming culture that grew out of the earlier Starčevo settlements and also named for a site in the Belgrade region ([Vinča-Belo Brdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin%C4%8Da-Belo_Brdo)). The Vinča culture is known for its very large settlements, one of the earliest [settlements by continuous habitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_time_of_continuous_habitation) and some of the largest in prehistoric Europe.[^cite_note-23] Also associated with the Vinča culture are anthropomorphic figurines such as the [Lady of Vinča](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_of_Vin%C4%8Da), the earliest known [copper metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_Age) in Europe,[^cite_note-24] and a [proto-writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-writing) form developed prior to the [Sumerians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumer) and [Minoans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoans) known as the [Old European script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_European_script), which dates back to around 5300 BC.[^cite_note-25] Within the city proper, on Cetinjska Street, a skull of a Paleolithic human dated to before 5000 BC was discovered in 1890.[^cite_note-26]

### Antiquity

See also: [Serbia in the Roman era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_in_the_Roman_era)



[Belgrade Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Fortress), built during a long period of time from the 2nd to the 18th century, located on the confluence of the two rivers Sava and [Danube](21209639.md)

Evidence of early knowledge about Belgrade's geographical location comes from a variety of ancient myths and legends. The ridge overlooking the confluence of the [Sava](587845.md) and [Danube](21209639.md) rivers, for example, has been identified as one of the places in the story of [Jason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason) and the [Argonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonauts).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] In the time of antiquity, too, the area was populated by [Paleo-Balkan tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_Southeastern_Europe), including the [Thracians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracians) and the [Dacians](100550.md), who ruled much of Belgrade's surroundings.[^cite_note-29] Specifically, Belgrade was at one point inhabited by the Thraco-Dacian tribe Singi;[^cite_note-city_of_belgrade-3-13] following [Celtic invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallic_invasion_of_Balkans) in 279 BC, the [Scordisci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scordisci) wrested the city from their hands, naming it *Singidūn* (*dūn*, fortress).[^cite_note-city_of_belgrade-3-13] In 34–33 BC, the Roman army reached Belgrade. It became the [romanised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) *[Singidunum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singidunum)* in the 1st century AD and, by the mid-2nd century, the city was proclaimed a *[municipium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipium)* by the Roman authorities, evolving into a full-fledged *[colonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonia_\(Roman\))* (the highest city class) by the end of the century.[^cite_note-rich-1992-14] While the first Christian [Emperor of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Emperor)—[Constantine I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_I), also known as [Constantine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great)[^cite_note-30]—was born in the territory of [Naissus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naissus) to the city's south, Roman Christianity's champion, Flavius Iovianus (Jovian/Jovan), was born in Singidunum.[^cite_note-31] Jovian reestablished Christianity as the official religion of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), ending the brief revival of [traditional Roman religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_ancient_Rome) under his predecessor [Julian the Apostate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_the_Apostate). In 395 AD, the site passed to the Eastern Roman or [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire).[^cite_note-32] Across the Sava from Singidunum was the Celtic city of [Taurunum (Zemun)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemun); the two were connected with a bridge throughout Roman and Byzantine times.[^cite_note-33]

### Middle Ages

See also: [Serbia in the Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_in_the_Middle_Ages)



Belgrade 1400, before Ottoman occupation.



The *themata* of the Byzantine Empire, at the death of Basil II in 1025. Belgrade was part of the theme of Bulgaria, after the conquest of Bulgaria in 1018.

In 442, the area was ravaged by [Attila the Hun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attila_the_Hun).[^cite_note-34] In 471, it was taken by [Theodoric the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great), king of the Ostrogoths, who continued into Italy.[^cite_note-35] As the [Ostrogoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogoths) left, another Germanic tribe, the [Gepids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gepids), invaded the city. In 539, it was retaken by the Byzantines.[^cite_note-36] In 577, some 100,000 Slavs poured into [Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrace) and [Illyricum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyricum_\(Roman_province\)), pillaging cities and more permanently settling the region. [^cite_note-37]

The [Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avars_\(Carpathians\)), under [Bayan I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayan_I), conquered the whole region and its new Slavic population by 582.[^cite_note-38] Following Byzantine reconquest, the Byzantine chronicle *[De Administrando Imperio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Administrando_Imperio)* mentions the [White Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Serbs), who had stopped in Belgrade on their way back home, asking the *[strategos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategos)* for lands; they received provinces in the west, towards the Adriatic, which they would rule as subjects to [Heraclius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraclius) (610–641).[^cite_note-39] In 829, [Khan Omurtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Omurtag) was able to add Singidunum and its environs to the First Bulgarian Empire.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] The first record of the name *Belograd* appeared on 16 April 878, in a Papal missive[^cite_note-42] to [Bulgarian ruler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire) [Boris I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_I). This name would appear in several variants: *Alba Bulgarica* in Latin, *Griechisch Weissenburg* in High German, *Nándorfehérvár* in Hungarian, and *Castelbianco* in Venetian, among other names, all variations of 'white fortress' or '[Bulgar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Bulgar_Khanate) white fortress'. For about four centuries, the city would become a battleground between the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), the medieval [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), and the [Bulgarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bulgarian_Empire).[^cite_note-43] [Basil II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_II) (976–1025) installed a garrison in Belgrade.[^cite_note-44] The city hosted the armies of the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Crusade) and the [Second Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Crusade),[^cite_note-beligradcom-1934-45] but, while passing through during the [Third Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Crusade), [Frederick Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa) and his 190,000 [crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Crusade) saw Belgrade in ruins.[^cite_note-46]

King [Stefan Dragutin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Dragutin_of_Serbia) (r. 1276–1282) received Belgrade from his father-in-law, [Stephen V of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_V_of_Hungary), in 1284, and it served as the capital of the [Kingdom of Syrmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Syrmia), a vassal state to the Kingdom of Hungary. Dragutin (Hungarian: *Dragutin István*) is regarded as the first [Serbian king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Serbian_monarchs) to rule over Belgrade.[^cite_note-beogradrs-4-47]

Following the battles of [Maritsa (1371)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Maritsa) and [Kosovo field (1389)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kosovo), Moravian Serbia, to Belgrade's south, began to fall to the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

The northern regions of what is now Serbia persisted as the [Serbian Despotate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Despotate), with Belgrade as its capital. The city flourished under [Stefan Lazarević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Lazarevi%C4%87), the son of Serbian prince [Lazar Hrebeljanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazar_Hrebeljanovi%C4%87). Lazarević built a castle with a citadel and towers, of which only the [Despot's tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Despot_Stefan_Tower) and the west wall remain. He also refortified the city's ancient walls, allowing the Despotate to resist Ottoman conquest for almost 70 years. During this time, Belgrade was a haven for many Balkan peoples fleeing Ottoman rule, and is thought to have had a population ranging between 40,000 and 50,000 people.[^cite_note-beogradrs-4-47]



[Conquest of Belgrade, 1521](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Belgrade_\(1521\)), Sultan [Suleiman the Magnificent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suleiman_the_Magnificent) laid siege to the [Belgrade Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Fortress)

In 1427, Stefan's successor [Đurađ Branković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90ura%C4%91_Brankovi%C4%87), returning Belgrade to the [Hungarian king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), made [Smederevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smederevo_fortress) his new capital. Even though the Ottomans had captured most of the [Serbian Despotate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Despotate), Belgrade, known as Nándorfehérvár in Hungarian, was [unsuccessfully besieged in 1440](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Belgrade_\(1440\))[^cite_note-beligradcom-1934-45] and 1456.[^cite_note-50] As the city presented an obstacle to the Ottoman advance into Hungary and further, over 100,000 Ottoman soldiers[^cite_note-belgradenetcom-51] [besieged it in 1456](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Belgrade_\(1456\)), in which the Christian army led by the Hungarian General [John Hunyadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hunyadi) successfully defended it.[^cite_note-52] The *[noon bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noon_bell)* ordered by [Pope Callixtus III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Callixtus_III) commemorates the victory throughout the Christian world to this day, which is now a cultural symbol of [Hungary](13275.md).[^cite_note-beligradcom-1934-45][^cite_note-53]

### Ottoman rule and Austrian invasions

See also: [History of Ottoman Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ottoman_Serbia) and [Ottoman–Habsburg wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Habsburg_wars)



Belgrade in 1684

Seven decades after the initial siege, on 28 August 1521, the fort was finally captured by [Suleiman the Magnificent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suleiman_the_Magnificent) with 250,000 Turkish soldiers and over 100 ships. Subsequently, most of the city was razed to the ground and its entire Orthodox Christian population was deported to [Istanbul](3391396.md)[^cite_note-beligradcom-1934-45][^cite_note-54] to an area that has since become known as the [Belgrade forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_forest).[^cite_note-55]

Belgrade was made the seat of the [Pashalik of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashalik_of_Belgrade) (also known as the Sanjak of Smederevo), and quickly became the second largest Ottoman town in Europe at over 100,000 people, surpassed only by [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople).[^cite_note-belgradenetcom-51] Ottoman rule introduced [Ottoman architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_architecture), including numerous mosques, and the city was resurrected—now by [Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental) influences.[^cite_note-beogradrs-3-56]



[Siege of Belgrade (1717)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Belgrade_\(1717\)).

In 1594, a major [Serb rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banat_Uprising) was crushed by the Ottomans. In retribution, [Grand Vizier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_vizier) [Sinan Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinan_Pasha) ordered the [relics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relic) of [Saint Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sava) to be publicly torched on the [Vračar plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vra%C4%8Dar_plateau); in the 20th century, the [church of Saint Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Sava) was built to commemorate this event.[^cite_note-57]

Occupied by the [Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) three times ([1688–1690](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Belgrade_\(1688\)), [1717–1739](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia_\(1718%E2%80%9339\)), [1789–1791](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Belgrade_\(1789\))), headed by the [Holy Roman Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) [Maximilian of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_II_Emanuel,_Elector_of_Bavaria) and [Eugene of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Eugene_of_Savoy),[^cite_note-58] and [field marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_marshal) [Baron Ernst Gideon von Laudon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baron_Ernst_Gideon_von_Laudon), respectively, Belgrade was recaptured by the Ottomans and substantially razed each time.[^cite_note-beogradrs-3-56] During this period, the city was affected by the two [Great Serbian Migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Serbian_Migrations), in which hundreds of thousands of Serbs, led by two [Serbian Patriarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Patriarchs), retreated together with the Austrian soldiers into the Habsburg Empire, settling in today's [Vojvodina](176801.md) and [Slavonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavonia).[^cite_note-59]

### Principality and Kingdom of Serbia

See also: [Principality of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Serbia) and [Kingdom of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia)



View of Belgrade in 1890

At the beginning of the 19th century, Belgrade was predominantly inhabited by a Muslim population. Traces of Ottoman rule and architecture—such as mosques and [bazaars](556904.md), were to remain a prominent part of Belgrade's townscape into the 19th century; several decades, even, after Serbia was granted autonomy from the Ottoman Empire.[^cite_note-60]

During the [First Serbian Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Serbian_Uprising), Serbian revolutionaries held the city from 8 January 1807 until 1813, when it was retaken by the Ottomans.[^cite_note-beogradrs-2-61] In 1807, Turks in Belgrade were massacred and forcefully converted to Christianity. The massacre was encouraged by Russia in order to cement divisions between the Serb rebels and the [Porte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Porte). Around 6,000 Muslims and Jews were forcibly converted to Christianity. Most mosques were converted into churches. Muslims, Jews, Aromanians and Greeks were subjected to forced labour, and Muslim women were widely made available to young Serb men, and some were taken into slavery. [Milenko Stojković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milenko_Stojkovi%C4%87) bought many of them, and established his harem for which he gained fame. In this circumstances Belgrade demographically transformed from Ottoman to Serb.[^cite_note-AMH-62] After the [Second Serbian Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Serbian_Uprising) in 1815, Serbia achieved some sort of sovereignty, which was formally recognised by the Porte in 1830.[^cite_note-63]

The development of Belgrade architecture after 1815 can be divided into four periods. In the first phase, which lasted from 1815 to 1835, the dominant architectural style was still of a Balkan character, with substantial Ottoman influence. At the same time, an interest in joining the European mainstream allowed Central and Western European architecture to flourish. Between 1835 and 1850, the amount of [neoclassicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassicism) and [baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) buildings south of the Austrian border rose considerably, exemplified by [Cathedral of Saint Archangel Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Archangel_Michael,_Belgrade) (Serbian: *Saborna crkva)*, completed in 1840. Between 1850 and 1875, new architecture was characterised by a turn towards the newly popular [Romanticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism), along with older European architectural styles. Typical of Central European cities in the last quarter of the 19th century, the fourth phase was characterised by an [eclecticist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eclecticism) style based on the Renaissance and Baroque periods.[^cite_note-64]



[Republic Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Square_\(Belgrade\)) today, Left: [National Museum of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Serbia) – Centre: [Courtyard by Marriott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtyard_by_Marriott) Hotel Belgrade – Right: [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_in_Belgrade).

In 1841, Prince [Mihailo Obrenović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihailo_Obrenovi%C4%87) moved the capital of the [Principality of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Serbia) from [Kragujevac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kragujevac) to Belgrade.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] During his first reign (1815–1839), Prince [Miloš Obrenović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo%C5%A1_Obrenovi%C4%87) pursued expansion of the city's population through the addition of new settlements, aiming and succeeding to make Belgrade the centre of the Principality's administrative, military and cultural institutions. His project of creating a new market space (the Abadžijska čaršija), however, was less successful; trade continued to be conducted in the centuries-old Donja čaršija and Gornja čaršija. Still, new construction projects were typical for the Christian quarters as the older Muslim quarters declined; from Serbia's autonomy until 1863, the number of Belgrade quarters even decreased, mainly as a consequence of the gradual [disappearance of the city's Muslim population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exodus_of_Muslims_from_Serbia_\(1862\)). An Ottoman city map from 1863 counts only 9 Muslim quarters (*mahalas*). The names of only five such neighbourhoods are known today: Ali-pašina, Reis-efendijina, Jahja-pašina, Bajram-begova, and Laz Hadži-Mahmudova.[^cite_note-67] Following the [Čukur Fountain incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Cukur_Fountain_incident), Belgrade was bombed by the Ottomans.[^cite_note-68]



Belgrade City Courts Complex and [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_Yugoslavia) of the [Kingdom of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia) under construction, before [World War II](32927.md).

On 18 April 1867, the Ottoman government ordered the Ottoman garrison, which had been since 1826 the last representation of Ottoman suzerainty in Serbia, withdrawn from [Kalemegdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalemegdan). The forlorn Porte's only stipulation was that the Ottoman flag continue to fly over the fortress alongside the Serbian one. Serbia's *[de facto](43594.md)* independence dates from this event.[^cite_note-69] In the following years, urban planner [Emilijan Josimović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilijan_Josimovi%C4%87) had a significant influence on Belgrade. He conceptualised a regulation plan for the city in 1867, in which he proposed the replacement of the town's crooked streets with a [grid plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_plan). Of great importance also was the construction of independent Serbian political and cultural institutions, as well as the city's now-plentiful parks. Pointing to Josimović's work, Serbian scholars have noted an important break with Ottoman traditions. At the time of independence, Belgrade had a mainly Ottoman look, and Josimović stated he wanted to rebuild Belgrade so that "the capital does not retain the form that barbarism gave it".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic201918-70] Josimović designed Belgrade to resemble Vienna, right down to building grand boulevards inspired by the *Ringstrasse*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic201918-70] All that remains of Ottoman Belgrade today are two mosques, the citadel, and a fountain with Arabic inscriptions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic201918-70] However, Istanbul—the capital city of the state to which Belgrade and Serbia *de jure* still belonged—underwent similar changes.[^cite_note-71]

In May 1868, *knez* Mihailo was assassinated with his cousin [Anka Konstantinović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anka_Konstantinovi%C4%87) while riding in a carriage in his country residence.[^cite_note-72]

With the [Principality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Serbia)'s full independence in 1878 and its transformation into the [Kingdom of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia) in 1882, Belgrade once again became a key city in the Balkans, and developed rapidly.[^cite_note-beogradrs-2-61][^cite_note-beogradrs-73] Nevertheless, conditions in Serbia remained those of an overwhelmingly agrarian country, even with the opening of a railway to [Niš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni%C5%A1), Serbia's second city. In 1900, the capital had only 70,000 inhabitants[^cite_note-74] (at the time Serbia numbered 2.5 million). Still, by 1905, the population had grown to more than 80,000 and, by the outbreak of World War I in 1914, it had surpassed the 100,000 citizens, disregarding [Zemun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemun), which still belonged to [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary).[^cite_note-75]

The first-ever projection of motion pictures in the Balkans and Central Europe was held in Belgrade in June 1896 by André Carr, a representative of the [Lumière brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_and_Louis_Lumi%C3%A8re). He shot the first motion pictures of Belgrade in the next year; however, they have not been preserved.[^cite_note-76] The first permanent cinema was opened in 1909 in Belgrade.[^cite_note-77]

### World War I: Austro–German invasion



Belgrade seen from [Topčider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top%C4%8Dider) hill in 1920.

&#x20;The First World War began on 28 July 1914 when Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia. Most of the subsequent Balkan offensives occurred near Belgrade. [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Navy) [monitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monitor_\(warship\)) shelled Belgrade on 29 July 1914, and it was taken by the [Austro-Hungarian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Army) under General [Oskar Potiorek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Potiorek) on 1 December. On 16 December, it was re-taken by [Serbian troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Campaign_\(World_War_I\)) under Marshal [Radomir Putnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radomir_Putnik). After a prolonged battle which destroyed much of the city, starting on 6 October 1915, Belgrade fell to [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army_\(German_Empire\)) and Austro-Hungarian troops commanded by Field Marshal [August von Mackensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_von_Mackensen) on 9 October of the same year.&#x20;



Old postcard from Belgrade.

&#x20;The city was liberated by Serbian and [French troops](611740.md) on 1 November 1918, under the command of Marshal [Louis Franchet d'Espèrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Franchet_d'Esp%C3%A8rey) of France and [Crown Prince Alexander of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Yugoslavia). Belgrade, devastated as a front-line city, lost the title of largest city in the [Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia) to [Subotica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subotica) for some time.[^cite_note-78]

### Kingdom of Yugoslavia

After the war, Belgrade became the capital of the new Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes, renamed the [Kingdom of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia) in 1929. The Kingdom was split into [banovinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_the_Kingdom_of_Yugoslavia) and Belgrade, together with [Zemun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemun) and [Pančevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan%C4%8Devo), formed a separate administrative unit.[^cite_note-79] During this period, the city experienced fast growth and significant modernisation. Belgrade's population grew to 239,000 by 1931 (with the inclusion of Zemun), and to 320,000 by 1940. The population growth rate between 1921 and 1948 averaged 4.08% a year.[^cite_note-80]

In 1927, Belgrade's first airport opened, and in 1929, its first radio station began broadcasting. The [Pančevo Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan%C4%8Devo_Bridge), which crosses the Danube, was opened in 1935,[^cite_note-81] while [King Alexander Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Alexander_Bridge) over the Sava was opened in 1934. On 3 September 1939 the first [Belgrade Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Grand_Prix), the last [Grand Prix motor racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_motor_racing) race before the outbreak of World War II, was held around the [Belgrade Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Fortress) and was followed by 80,000 spectators.[^cite_note-82] The winner was [Tazio Nuvolari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tazio_Nuvolari).[^cite_note-83]

### World War II



People of Belgrade demonstrate their support for break-up with the [Tripartite Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartite_Pact) on 27 March 1941

&#x20;On 25 March 1941, the government of [regent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent) [Crown Prince Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Paul_of_Yugoslavia) signed the [Tripartite Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripartite_Pact), joining the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers_of_World_War_II) in an effort to stay out of the Second World War and keep Yugoslavia neutral during the conflict. This was immediately followed by mass protests in Belgrade and a military [coup d'état](25914154.md) led by Air Force commander General [Dušan Simović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du%C5%A1an_Simovi%C4%87), who proclaimed [King Peter II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_II_of_Yugoslavia) to be of age to rule the realm. As a result, the city was [heavily bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Retribution_\(1941\)) by the [Luftwaffe](17885.md) on 6 April 1941, killing up to 2,274 people.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] Yugoslavia was then [invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Yugoslavia) by [German](21212.md), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy), [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_between_the_two_world_wars), and [Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Bulgaria_during_World_War_II) forces. Belgrade was captured by subterfuge, with six German soldiers led by their officer [Fritz Klingenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Klingenberg) feigning threatening size, forcing the city to capitulate.



Ruins in Belgrade after the [German bombing of 1941](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Retribution_\(1941\))

[^cite_note-87] Belgrade was more directly occupied by the [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army_\(Wehrmacht\)) in the same month and became the seat of the puppet [Nedić regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nedi%C4%87_regime), headed by its namesake general.[^cite_note-88] Some of today's parts of Belgrade were incorporated in the [Independent State of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_State_of_Croatia) in occupied Yugoslavia, another puppet state, where [Ustashe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ustashe) regime carried out the [Genocide of Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_of_Serbs_in_the_Independent_State_of_Croatia).[^cite_note-89]

During the summer and autumn of 1941, in reprisal for guerrilla attacks, the Germans carried out several massacres of Belgrade citizens; in particular, members of the [Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Serbia) were subject to mass shootings at the order of General [Franz Böhme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_B%C3%B6hme), the German [Military Governor of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_the_Military_Commander_in_Serbia). Böhme rigorously enforced the rule that for every German killed, 100 Serbs or Jews would be shot.[^cite_note-90] Belgrade became the first city in Europe to be declared by the Nazi occupation forces to be *[judenfrei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judenfrei)*.[^cite_note-91] The resistance movement in Belgrade was led by Major [Žarko Todorović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDarko_Todorovi%C4%87) from 1941 until his arrest in 1943.[^cite_note-92]

Just like [Rotterdam](26049.md), which was devastated twice by both German and Allied bombing, [Belgrade was bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_bombing_of_Yugoslavia_in_World_War_II#1944_Easter_bombing) once more during [World War II](32927.md), this time by the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) on 16 April 1944, killing at least 1,100 people. This bombing fell on the [Orthodox Christian](10186.md) [Easter](9325.md).[^cite_note-93] Most of the city remained under German occupation until 20 October 1944, when it was liberated by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) and the Communist [Yugoslav Partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partisans_\(Yugoslavia\)).

On 29 November 1945, Marshal [Josip Broz Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito) proclaimed the [Federal People's Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_People's_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) in Belgrade (later renamed to [Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia) on 7 April 1963).[^cite_note-94]

### Socialist Yugoslavia

When the war ended, the city was left with 11,500 demolished housing units.[^cite_note-95] During the post-war period, Belgrade grew rapidly as the capital of the [renewed Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia), developing as a major industrial centre.[^cite_note-beogradrs-73]



The First [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement) Summit Conference took place in Belgrade, [Yugoslavia](34244.md) in September 1961

In 1948, construction of [New Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Belgrade) started. In the late 1940s and 1950s, the new regime made a point of rebuilding Belgrade in a modernistic style inspired by Le Corbusier to show that Yugoslavia was in the forefront of progress.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019196-96] In 1958, Belgrade's first television station began broadcasting. In 1961, Belgrade hosted the first and founding conference of the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement) under Tito's chairmanship.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENorris2008134-97] In 1962, [Belgrade Nikola Tesla Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Nikola_Tesla_Airport) was built. In the 1960s, a number of prestige buildings were put up such as the parliament building, the [Ušće Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U%C5%A1%C4%87e_Towers) which served as the headquarters for the Central Committee of the League of Communists, and the [Hotel Jugoslavija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Jugoslavija).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019196-96] An American journalist from the *Washington Post* newspaper wrote in 1967: "Belgrade is a lively, frivolous, noisy, jam-packed city compared with the one I remember from twenty years ago".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019196-96] In 1964, Tito embarked upon the policy of "market socialism" that allowed capitalism to co-exist with communism. As a result, Western visitors to Belgrade in the late 1960s were astonished to see that the main streets were dominated by gigantic and garish billboards promoting Western brands such as Coca-Cola, Volkswagen, Siemens and Pan Am, giving Belgrade a very Western feel.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019196-96] Likewise, the Belgradians in the 1960s were described as dressed very much in fashionable Western styles with visitors noting that many women had an obsession with having their hair bleached blonde.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019196-96] The German historian Marie-Jannine Calic wrote that in the 1960s Belgrade "a vibrant hustle and bustle prevailed in the streets, squares, and numerous cafes."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019196-96] She also noted that the core of Belgrade had a very modern look, but that "along the mighty boulevards huddled pathetic little stores selling cloth, metal goods and dishware next to the dingy workshops of shoemakers, silversmiths and candle makers".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019197-98] On the periphery of Belgrade, there was a semi-rural feel as chickens and cows wandered about the streets while the people lived in dire poverty, making their living as peddlers, wandering musicians, rag pickers, panhandlers, shoe shiners, scissor grinders, casual laborers and other marginal occupations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019197-98] In 1968, [major student protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_student_demonstrations_in_Belgrade) led to several street clashes between students and the police.[^cite_note-99]

By 1969, the population of Belgrade passed the one million mark for the first time.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019195-100] The population increase was largely due to people moving from rural areas to Belgrade as it was estimated in 1969 that two of three Belgradians had been born in the countryside.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019195-100] The population increase in the 1960s imposed serious social strains as more people were moving to Belgrade faster than the pace of housing construction, leading a housing crisis.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019197-98] The 1961 Yugoslav census showed that an average of 2.5 people lived per one room in Belgrade compared to the average of 1.6 people per room in the rest of Yugoslavia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019197-98] In 1965, it was estimated that there was a shortage of 50,000 housing units in Belgrade, which led to many people to make illegal makeshift homes in shops, basements, laundry rooms and even elevator shafts while on the periphery of Belgrade people built wooden huts and cottages.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019197-98] The mayor of Belgrade, [Branko Pešić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branko_Pe%C5%A1i%C4%87), told a journalist in 1965: "In the last seven or eight years, 20,000 to 30,000 people have come to Belgrade each year. That equals an entire small town...And all of these people find shelter somewhere, hole up someplace. Some get an apartment, but that is the smallest percentage of them. A great number are forced...to house in basement, in unhygienic apartments and barracks. And whoever has not yet seen this should definitely see what this looks like...Something like this doesn't even exist in Africa".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECalic2019197-98]

In 1972, Belgrade faced a [smallpox outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Yugoslav_smallpox_outbreak), the last major outbreak of [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox) in [Europe](9239.md) since World War II.[^cite_note-101] Between October 1977 and March 1978, the city hosted the first major gathering of the [Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe](22796.md) with the aim of implementing the [Helsinki Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helsinki_Accords) from, while in 1980 Belgrade hosted the [UNESCO General Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_General_Conference).[^cite_note-102] Josip Broz Tito died in May 1980 and his [funeral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Josip_Broz_Tito) in Belgrade was attended by high officials and state delegations from 128 of the 154 [members of the United Nations](31969.md) from all over the world, based on which it became one of the [largest funerals in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_funerals).[^cite_note-103]



[General Staff Building](5722707.md) damaged in the [1999 NATO bombing](322473.md). Belgrade is one of the most destroyed cities due to its strategic location, which has led to it being battled over in 115 wars and razed to the ground 44 times throughout its 7,000-year history. This cycle of destruction and rebuilding earned it the nickname the "White Phoenix" and has significantly shaped its history and urban landscape.

### Breakup of Yugoslavia

On 9 March 1991, [massive demonstrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_9,_1991_protest) led by [Vuk Drašković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuk_Dra%C5%A1kovi%C4%87) were held in the city against [Slobodan Milošević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobodan_Milo%C5%A1evi%C4%87).[^cite_note-104] According to various media outlets, there were between 100,000 and 150,000 people on the streets.[^cite_note-105] Two people were killed, 203 were injured and 108 were arrested during the protests, and later that day tanks were deployed onto the streets to restore order.[^cite_note-106] Many [anti-war protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991%E2%80%931992_anti-war_protests_in_Belgrade) were held in Belgrade, with the largest protests being dedicated to solidarity with the victims from the [besieged Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sarajevo).[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] [Further anti-government protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996%E2%80%931997_protests_in_Serbia) were held in Belgrade from November 1996 to February 1997 against the same government after alleged electoral fraud in local elections.[^cite_note-109] These protests brought [Zoran Đinđić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoran_%C4%90in%C4%91i%C4%87) to power, the first [mayor of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Belgrade) since World War II who did not belong to the [League of Communists of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Communists_of_Yugoslavia) or its later offshoot, the [Socialist Party of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Serbia).[^cite_note-110]

In 1999, during the [Kosovo War](16760.md), the [NATO bombing](322473.md) campaign targeted a number of buildings in Belgrade. Among the sites bombed were some ministry buildings, the [RTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Television_of_Serbia) building, hospitals, [Hotel Jugoslavija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Jugoslavija), the [Central Committee building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U%C5%A1%C4%87e_Tower), [Avala Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avala_Tower), and the [Chinese embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Bombing_of_the_Chinese_embassy_in_Belgrade).[^cite_note-111] Between 500[^cite_note-112] and 2,000 civilians[^cite_note-113] were killed in Serbia and Montenegro as a result of the NATO bombings, of which 27 were killed in Belgrade.[^cite_note-114] After the [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars), Serbia became home to the highest number of [refugees](45547.md) and [internally displaced persons](427408.md) in Europe, with more than a third of these refugees having settled in Belgrade.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

After the [2000 presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Yugoslavian_general_election), Belgrade was the site of major public protests, with over half a million people taking part. These demonstrations resulted in the [ousting of president Milošević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overthrow_of_Slobodan_Milo%C5%A1evi%C4%87) as a part of the [Otpor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otpor) movement.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]

### Recent period



[Belgrade Waterfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Waterfront)

In 2014, [Belgrade Waterfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Waterfront), an [urban renewal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_renewal) project, was initiated by the [Government of Serbia](608371.md) and its [Emirati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirati) partner, [Eagle Hills Properties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Hills_Properties). Over 4 billion Euros was invested so far jointly by the Serbian government and their Emirati partners by May 2025.[^cite_note-121] By May 2025 it has opened 10,000 working places, opened 900 companies within Belgrade Waterfront and sold over 10,000 apartments.[^cite_note-122] So far it is build 6,000 apartments, 3,000 is currently under construction and over 6,000 is more planned. The project includes office and luxury apartment buildings, five-star hotels, a shopping mall and the envisioned '[Belgrade Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Tower)'. Plan is to triple current project and investment in total of over 12 billion Euros. [^cite_note-123]

In addition to [Belgrade Waterfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Waterfront), the city is under rapid development and reconstruction, especially in the area of [Novi Beograd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Belgrade), where (as of 2020) apartment and office buildings were under construction to support the burgeoning [Belgrade IT sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_IT_sector), now one of Serbia's largest economic players. In September 2020, there were around 2000 active construction sites in Belgrade.[^cite_note-124] The city budget for 2023 stood at 205,5 billion dinars (1.750 billion Euros).[^cite_note-125]

## Geography

### Topography



The confluence of the [Sava](587845.md) (river on the right side) into the [Danube](21209639.md) at Belgrade with view on [Kalemegdan Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Fortress) (up-right)

Belgrade lies 116.75 m (383.0 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level) and is located at the [confluence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence) of the [Danube](21209639.md) and [Sava](587845.md) rivers.[^cite_note-nurden-2009-17] The historical core of Belgrade, [Kalemegdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Fortress), lies on the right banks of both rivers. Since the 19th century, the city has been expanding to the south and east; after World War II, [New Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Belgrade) was built on the left bank of the Sava river, connecting Belgrade with [Zemun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemun). Smaller, chiefly residential communities across the Danube, like [Krnjača](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krnja%C4%8Da), [Kotež](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kote%C5%BE) and [Borča](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bor%C4%8Da), also merged with the city, while [Pančevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan%C4%8Devo), a heavily industrialised satellite city, remains separate. The city has an urban area of 360 km<sup>2</sup> (140 sq mi), while together with its metropolitan area it covers 3,223 km<sup>2</sup> (1,244 sq mi).[^cite_note-city_of_belgrade-3-13]



[Dorćol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dor%C4%87ol) and [Stari Grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stari_Grad,_Belgrade), Belgrade downtown at night.

On the right bank of the Sava, central Belgrade has a hilly terrain, while the highest point of Belgrade proper is [Torlak hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torlak_\(Belgrade\)) at 303 m (994 ft). The mountains of [Avala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avala) (511 m (1,677 ft)) and [Kosmaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosmaj) (628 m (2,060 ft)) lie south of the city. Across the Sava and Danube, the land is mostly flat, consisting of [alluvial plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvial_plain) and [loessial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loam) [plateaus](538005.md).[^cite_note-126] One of the characteristics of the city terrain is [mass wasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_wasting). On the territory covered by the General Urban Plan there are 1,155 recorded mass wasting points, out of which 602 are active and 248 are labeled as 'high risk'. They cover almost 30% of the city territory and include several types of mass wasting. [Downhill creeps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downhill_creep) are located on the slopes above the rivers, mostly on the [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay) or loam soils, inclined between 7 and 20%. The most critical ones are in [Karaburma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaburma), [Zvezdara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvezdara), [Višnjica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vi%C5%A1njica,_Serbia), [Vinča](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin%C4%8Da) and [Ritopek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritopek), in the Danube valley, and [Umka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umka), and especially its neighbourhood of Duboko, in the Sava valley. They have moving and dormant phases, and some of them have been recorded for centuries. Less active downhill creep areas include the entire [Terazije slope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terazijska_Terasa) above the Sava (Kalemegdan, [Savamala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savamala)), which can be seen by the inclination of the [Pobednik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pobednik) monument and the tower of the [Cathedral Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Michael's_Cathedral,_Belgrade), and the Voždovac section, between [Banjica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjica) and [Autokomanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autokomanda).

Landslides encompass smaller areas, develop on the steep cliffs, sometimes being inclined up to 90%. They are mostly located in the artificial [loess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loess) hills of Zemun: [Gardoš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardo%C5%A1), [Ćukovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%86ukovac) and [Kalvarija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalvarija_\(Zemun\)).



[Monument to the Unknown Hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_to_the_Unknown_Hero) on the mountain [Avala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avala), [Avala Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avala_Tower) and city of Belgrade in distance.

However, the majority of the land movement in Belgrade, some 90%, is triggered by the construction works and faulty water supply system (burst pipes, etc.). The neighbourhood of [Mirijevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirijevo) is considered to be the most successful project of fixing the problem. During the construction of the neighbourhood from the 1970s, the terrain was systematically improved and the movement of the land is today completely halted.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

### Climate

&#x20;Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), Belgrade has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cfa*) bordering on a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (*Dfa*) with four seasons and uniformly spread precipitation. Monthly averages range from 1.9 °C (35.4 °F) in January to 23.8 °C (74.8 °F) in July, with an annual mean of 13.2 °C (55.8 °F).&#x20;



Aerial view of Belgrade and [Ada Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Bridge) from [Košutnjak park forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%A1utnjak), known as the city's "oxygen factory" and a popular recreational spot.

There are, on average, 44.6 days a year when the maximum temperature is at or above 30 °C (86 °F),[^cite_note-nceinoaagov-129] and 95 days when the temperature is above 25 °C (77 °F), On the other hand, Belgrade experiences 52.1 days per year in which the minimum temperature falls below 0 °C (32 °F), with 13.8 days having a maximum temperature below freezing as well.[^cite_note-nceinoaagov-129] Belgrade receives about 698 mm (27 in) of precipitation a year, with late spring being wettest. The average annual number of sunny hours is 2,020.

Belgrade may experience thunderstorms at any time of the year, experiencing 31 days annually, but it's much more common in spring and summer months. [Hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail) is rare and occurs exclusively in spring or summer.[^cite_note-nceinoaagov-129]

The highest officially recorded temperature in Belgrade was 43.6 °C (110.5 °F) on 24 July 2007,[^cite_note-130] while on the other end, the lowest temperature was −26.2 °C (−15 °F) on 10 January 1893.[^cite_note-131] The highest recorded value of daily precipitation was 109.8 millimetres (4.32 inches) on 15 May 2014.[^cite_note-nceinoaagov-129]

Belgrade is a separate territorial unit in Serbia, with its own autonomous city authority.[^cite_note-city_of_belgrade-18] The Assembly of the City of Belgrade has 110 members, elected on four-year terms.[^cite_note-135] A 13-member City Council, elected by the Assembly and presided over by the mayor and his deputy, has the control and supervision of the city administration,[^cite_note-136] which manages day-to-day administrative affairs. It is divided into 14 Secretariats, each having a specific portfolio such as traffic or health care, and several professional services, agencies and institutes.[^cite_note-137]

The [2024 Belgrade City Assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Belgrade_City_Assembly_election) was won by the [Serbian Progressive Party](19454384.md), which formed a ruling coalition with the [Socialist Party of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_of_Serbia). Between 2004 and 2013, the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Serbia\)) was in power.[^cite_note-138] Due to the importance of Belgrade in political and economic life of Serbia, the office of city's mayor is often described as the third most important office in the state, after the [President of the Government](606320.md) and the [President of the Republic](774382.md).[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]



[Supreme Court of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_\(Serbia\)).

As the capital city, Belgrade is seat of all Serbian state authorities – [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)), [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative), [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary), and the headquarters of almost all national political parties as well as 75 [diplomatic missions](8970.md).[^cite_note-142] This includes the [National Assembly](619753.md), the Presidency, the [Government of Serbia](608371.md) and all the ministries, [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_\(Serbia\)) and the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Serbia).

According to the 2022 census, the population of the city proper stands at 1,197,714, its contiguous urban area (including the adjacent settlements of [Borča](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bor%C4%8Da), [Kaluđerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalu%C4%91erica), and [Surčin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sur%C4%8Din)) has 1,298,661 inhabitants, while the population of the city's administrative area (often equated with its metropolitan area) totals 1,681,405 people.[^cite_note-auto1-147] However, Belgrade's metropolitan area has not been defined, either statistically or administratively, and it [sprawls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl) into the neighboring municipalities of [Pančevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan%C4%8Devo), [Pećinci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C4%87inci), and [Stara Pazova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stara_Pazova).

Belgrade is home to many ethnicities from across the former Yugoslavia and the wider Balkans region. The main ethnic group comprising over 86% of the metropolitan population of Belgrade are [Serbs](59512.md) (1,449,241). Some significant minorities include [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Serbia) (23,160), [Yugoslavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavs_in_Serbia) (10,499), [Gorani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorani_people) (5,249), [Montenegrins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegrins_of_Serbia) (5,134), [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Serbia) (4,659), [Croats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croats_of_Serbia) (4,554), [Macedonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonians_in_Serbia) (4,293), and [ethnic Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims_\(South-Slavic_ethnic_group\)) (2,718).[^cite_note-148] Many people came to the city as economic migrants from smaller towns and the countryside, while tens of thousands arrived as refugees from [Croatia](5573.md), [Bosnia-Herzegovina](3463.md) and [Kosovo](17391.md), as a result of the [Yugoslav wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_wars) of the 1990s.[^cite_note-149] The most recent wave of immigration following the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) saw tens of thousands of Russians and Ukrainians register their residence in Serbia, majority of them in Belgrade.[^cite_note-150]



Administrative area of the City of Belgrade on a map of [districts of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Serbia).

Between 10,000 and 20,000[^cite_note-151] [Chinese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) are estimated to live in Belgrade and, since their arrival in the mid-1990s, [Block 70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blocks_\(New_Belgrade\)) in [New Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Belgrade) has been known colloquially as the Chinese quarter.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] Many [Middle Easterners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Easterners), mainly from [Syria](7515849.md), [Iran](14653.md), [Jordan](7515964.md) and [Iraq](7515928.md), arrived in order to pursue their studies during the 1970s and 1980s, and have remained in the city.[^cite_note-154] Throughout the 19th and early 20th century, small communities of [Aromanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromanians), [Czechs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs), [Greeks](42056.md), [Germans](152735.md), [Hungarians](17106358.md), [Jews](25955086.md), [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people), [Armenians](387816.md) and [Russian](59510.md) [White émigrés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_%C3%A9migr%C3%A9s) also existed in Belgrade. There are two suburban settlements with significant minority population today: [Ovča](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ov%C4%8Da) and the village of [Boljevci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boljevci), both with about one quarter of their population being [Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians) and [Slovaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovaks), respectively. Immigration to Belgrade from other countries accelerates. In 2023, more than 30,000 foreign workers got working and residence permits only in Belgrade.[^cite_note-155]

Although there are several historic religious communities in Belgrade, the religious makeup of the city is relatively homogeneous. The [Serbian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church) community is by far the largest, with 1,475,168 adherents. There are also 31,914 Muslims, 13,720 Roman Catholics, and 3,128 [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism).

There once was a significant [Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Belgrade) in Belgrade but, following the World War II [Nazi occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Serbia#Serbia_in_World_War_I) of the city and subsequent Jewish emigration, their numbers have fallen from over 10,000 to just 295.[^cite_note-156] Belgrade also used to have one of the largest [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) colonies in Europe outside Russia when some 400 mostly Buddhist [Kalmyks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalmyks) settled on the outskirts of Belgrade following the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War). The first Buddhist temple in Europe was built in Belgrade in 1929. Most of them moved away after the World War II and their temple, [Belgrade pagoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_pagoda), was abandoned, claimed by the new Communist regime and eventually demolished.[^cite_note-157]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Belgrade)

[Stari Grad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stari_Grad,_Belgrade) with [Dorćol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dor%C4%87ol) aerial view.

Belgrade is the [financial centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_centre) of Serbia and [Southeast Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Europe), with a total of 17×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> m<sup>2</sup> (180×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> sq ft) of office space.[^cite_note-158] It is also home to the country's [Central Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Serbia). 750,550 people are employed (July 2020)[^cite_note-biznistelegrafrs-2020-159] in 120,286 companies,[^cite_note-večernje_novosti-2013-160] 76,307 enterprises and 50,000 shops.[^cite_note-biznistelegrafrs-2020-159][^cite_note-161] The City of Belgrade itself owns 267,147 m<sup>2</sup> (2,875,550 sq ft) of rentable office space.[^cite_note-162]

As of 2024, Belgrade contained 29.8% of Serbia's employed population and generated over 43.2% of its GDP.[^cite_note-BelgradeGDP24-6][^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] For 2026, the [IMF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMF) estimated Serbia's GDP PPP to be \$226,4 billion,[^cite_note-166] while Belgrade's GDP stood at \$96,45 billion or \$58,452 per capita.[^cite_note-BelgradeGDP24-6]

[New Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Belgrade) is the country's [Central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district) and one of [Southeastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_Europe)'s financial centres. It offers a range of facilities, such as hotels, congress halls (e.g. [Sava Centar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sava_Centar)), Class A and B office buildings, and business parks (e.g. [Airport City Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_City_Belgrade)). Over 1.2×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> m<sup>2</sup> (13×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> sq ft) of land is under construction in New Belgrade, with the value of planned construction over the next three years estimated at over 1.5 billion euros. The [Belgrade Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Stock_Exchange) is also located in New Belgrade.

With 6,924 companies in the IT sector (according to 2013 data), Belgrade is one of the foremost [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology) hubs in [Southeast Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Europe).[^cite_note-večernje_novosti-2013-160] [Microsoft](19001.md)'s [Development Center Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Development_Center_Serbia), located in Belgrade, was, at the time of its establishment, the fifth such programme on the globe.[^cite_note-167] Many global IT companies choose Belgrade as their European or regional centre of operations, such as [Asus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asus),[^cite_note-168] [Intel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel),[^cite_note-169] [Dell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dell),[^cite_note-170] [Huawei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huawei), [Ubisoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubisoft), [Yandex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yandex), [Nutanix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutanix),[^cite_note-171] [NCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCR_Corporation) etc.[^cite_note-172] The most famous Belgrade IT startups, among others, are [Nordeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordeus), [ComTrade Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ComTrade_Group), [MicroE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikroelektronika), [FishingBooker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FishingBooker), and [Endava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endava_d.o.o.). IT facilities in the city include the [Mihajlo Pupin Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihajlo_Pupin_Institute) and the [ILR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivo_Lola_Ribar_Institute),[^cite_note-173] as well as the brand-new IT Park Zvezdara.[^cite_note-174] Many prominent IT innovators began their careers in Belgrade, including [Voja Antonić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voja_Antoni%C4%87) and [Veselin Jevrosimović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veselin_Jevrosimovi%C4%87).

In July 2026, the average [New Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Belgrade) CBD monthly net salary stood at 188.455 RSD (\$1900) in net terms, with the gross equivalent at 255.254 RSD (\$2508),central Belgrade 213.000 RSD, while in Belgrade with suburbs is Euros 1.258.[^cite_note-175]

According to [Cushman & Wakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cushman_&_Wakefield), [Knez Mihajlova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knez_Mihajlova) street is 36th most expensive retail street in the world in terms of renting commercial space.[^cite_note-176]

As an example of the attractiveness of the city and its importance in this part of the continent is the fact that numerous multinational companies choose precisely Belgrade to place its local headquarters. An early example of this was the multinational food-giant [McDonald's](2480627.md) opening its first ever restaurant in a communist country in Europe in Belgrade.[^cite_note-177] City budget in 2026 is 202.16 billion dinars or 1.9 billion Euros.[^cite_note-178]

Other major libraries include the Belgrade City Library and the [Belgrade University Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_University_Library). Belgrade's two opera houses are: National Theatre and [Madlenianum Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madlenianum_Opera_and_Theatre).[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192] Following the victory of Serbia's representative [Marija Šerifović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marija_%C5%A0erifovi%C4%87) at the [Eurovision Song Contest 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2007), Belgrade hosted the Contest in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2008).[^cite_note-193]

The [Ethnographic Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnographic_Museum_\(Belgrade\)), established in 1901, contains more than 150,000 items showcasing the rural and urban culture of the Balkans, particularly the countries of [former Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Yugoslavia).[^cite_note-210] Belgrade also houses the [Museum of African Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_African_Art,_Serbia), founded in 1977, which has a large collection of art from West Africa.[^cite_note-211] Another major Belgrade museum is the [Museum of Vuk and Dositej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Vuk_and_Dositej), which showcases the lives, work and legacy of [Vuk Stefanović Karadžić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuk_Stefanovi%C4%87_Karad%C5%BEi%C4%87) and [Dositej Obradović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dositej_Obradovi%C4%87), the 19th century reformer of the Serbian literary language and the first Serbian Minister of Education and a man of letters, respectively.[^cite_note-212] The [Military Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Museum_\(Belgrade\)), established in 1878 in Kalemegdan, houses a wide range of more than 25,000 military objects dating from the prehistoric to the medieval to the modern eras. Notable items include Turkish and oriental arms, national banners, and [Yugoslav Partisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Partisans) regalia.[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214] The [Aviation Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_Museum,_Belgrade), located near [Belgrade Nikola Tesla Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Nikola_Tesla_Airport), has more than 200 aircraft, of which about 50 are on display, and a few of which are the only surviving examples of their type, such as the [Fiat G.50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_G.50). This museum also displays parts of shot down US and [NATO](21133.md) aircraft, such as the [F-117](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-117_Nighthawk) and [F-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-16_Fighting_Falcon).[^cite_note-215]

The [Nikola Tesla Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_Tesla_Museum), founded in 1952, preserves the personal items of [Nikola Tesla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_Tesla), the inventor after whom the [Tesla unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_\(unit\)) was named. It holds around 160,000 original documents and around 5,700 personal other items including his urn.[^cite_note-216] The [Museum of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Science_and_Technology_\(Belgrade\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), founded in 1989, moved to the building of the first city's power plant in Dorćol in 2005.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218]

With around 95,000 copies of national and international films, the [Yugoslav Cinematheque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Audiovisual_Archives_of_Serbia_%E2%80%93_Yugoslav_Cinematheque) is the largest in the region and among the 10 largest archives in the world.[^cite_note-219] The institution also operates the Museum of Yugoslav Cinematheque, with movie theatre and exhibition hall. The archive's long-standing storage problems were finally solved in 2007, when a new modern depository was opened.[^cite_note-220] The [Yugoslav Film Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Film_Archive) also exhibits original [Charlie Chaplin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Chaplin)'s stick and one of the first movies by [Auguste and Louis Lumière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_and_Louis_Lumi%C3%A8re).[^cite_note-221]

The [Museum of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Yugoslavia) has collections from the Yugoslav era. Beside paintings, the most valuable are [Moon](19331.md) rocks donated by [Apollo 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11) crew [Neil Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Armstrong), [Buzz Aldrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzz_Aldrin) and [Michael Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Collins_\(astronaut\)) while visiting Belgrade in 1969 and from mission [Apollo 17](1971.md) donated by [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) in 1971.[^cite_note-222] The museum also houses [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)'s sabre with 260 brilliants and diamonds, donated by Stalin himself.[^cite_note-223]

### Architecture

See also: [List of buildings in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_buildings_in_Belgrade), [List of streets and squares in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_streets_and_squares_in_Belgrade), [Bridges of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridges_of_Belgrade), [Architectural projects in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architectural_projects_in_Belgrade), [Religious architecture in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_architecture_in_Belgrade), and [Gates of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gates_of_Belgrade)













**Clockwise, from upper left**: Interior of the [Church of Saint Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Sava); [Church of Saint Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Mark,_Belgrade); [Hotel Moskva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Moskva,_Belgrade); [Belgrade Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Fortress); [Knez Mihailova Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knez_Mihailova_Street) - the main pedestrian and shopping zone in Belgrade; Sava Square - [Former Belgrade Main railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Belgrade_Main_railway_station) and monument to [Stefan Nemanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Nemanja).

Belgrade has widely varying styles of architecture, ranging from those typical of a Central European town such as the centre of [Zemun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemun)[^cite_note-224] to the more modern architecture and spacious layout of [New Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novi_Beograd).

The oldest architecture is found in Kalemegdan Park. Outside of Kalemegdan, the oldest buildings date only from the 18th century, due to its geographic position and frequent wars and destructions.[^cite_note-manević-225] The oldest public structure in Belgrade is a nondescript Turkish [türbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%BCrbe), while the oldest house is a modest clay house on [Dorćol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dor%C4%87ol), from late 18th century.[^cite_note-226] Western influence began in the 19th century, when the city completely transformed from an oriental town to the contemporary architecture of the time, with influences from [neoclassicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassicism), [romanticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism), and [academic art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_art). Serbian architects took over the development from foreign builders in the late 19th century, producing the [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_in_Belgrade), [Stari Dvor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stari_dvor), [Cathedral of Saint Archangel Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Saint_Archangel_Michael,_Belgrade) and later, in the early 20th century, the [House of the National Assembly](39875666.md) and [National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Serbia), influenced by [art nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_nouveau).[^cite_note-manević-225] Elements of [Serbo-Byzantine Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbo-Byzantine_Revival) are present in buildings such as [Vuk Foundation House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuk_Foundation_House), old Post Office in Kosovska street, and sacral architecture, such as [Church of Saint Mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Mark,_Belgrade) and the [Church of Saint Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Sava).[^cite_note-manević-225] In the socialist period, housing was built quickly and cheaply for the huge influx of people fleeing the countryside following World War II, sometimes resulting in the [brutalist architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brutalist_architecture) of the [*blokovi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blocks_\(New_Belgrade\)) ('blocks') of [New Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Belgrade); a [socrealism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socrealism) trend briefly ruled, resulting in buildings like the [Trade Union Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_Sindikata).[^cite_note-manević-225] However, in the mid-1950s, [modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism) trends took over, and still dominate the Belgrade architecture.[^cite_note-manević-225] Belgrade has the second oldest sewer system in Europe.[^cite_note-227] The [Clinical Centre of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_Centre_of_Serbia) spreads over 34 hectares and consists of about 50 buildings, while also has 3,150 beds considered to be the highest number in Europe,[^cite_note-228] and among [highest in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_by_capacity).[^cite_note-229]

### Tourism

See also: [Tourism in Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Serbia)



Belgrade during the [New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year) holiday

Lying on the main artery connecting Europe and Asia, as well as, eventually, the [Orient Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient_Express), Belgrade has been a popular place for travellers through the centuries. In 1843, on Dubrovačka Street (today Kralj Petar Street ), Serbia's *[knez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knez_\(title\))* [Mihailo Obrenović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihailo_Obrenovi%C4%87) built a large edifice which became the first hotel in Belgrade: *Kod jelena* ('at the deer's'), in the neighbourhood of [Kosančićev Venac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosan%C4%8Di%C4%87ev_Venac). Many criticised the move at the time due to the cost and the size of the building, but it soon became the gathering point of the Principality's wealthiest citizens. Colloquially, the building was also referred to as the *staro zdanje*, or the 'old edifice'. It remained a hotel until 1903 before being demolished in 1938.[^cite_note-230][^cite_note-231] After the *staro zdanje*, numerous hotels were built in the second half of the 19th century: *Nacional* and *Grand*, also in Kosančićev Venac, *Srpski Kralj*, *Srpska Kruna*, *[Grčka Kraljica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C4%8Dka_Kraljica)* near Kalemegdan, *Balkan* and *Pariz* in [Terazije](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terazije), *[London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London,_Belgrade)*, etc.[^cite_note-perić-2018-232]



[Kalemegdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalemegdan)



[Jevremovac Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jevremovac)

As Belgrade became connected via steamboats and railway (after 1884), the number of visitors grew and new hotels were opened with luxurious commodities. In Savamala, the hotels *Bosna* and *Bristol* were opened. Other hotels included *Solun* and *Orient*, which was built near the [Financial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Park). Tourists who arrived by the [Orient Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient_Express) mostly stayed at the Petrograd Hotel in [Wilson Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savamala#Sava_Square). Hotel *Srpski Kralj*, at the corner of Uzun Mirkova and Pariska Streets was considered the best hotel in Belgrade during the [Interbellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interbellum). It was destroyed during World War II.[^cite_note-perić-2018-232]

The historic areas and buildings of Belgrade are among the city's premier attractions. They include [Skadarlija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skadarlija), the [National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Serbia) and adjacent [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_in_Belgrade), [Zemun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemun), [Nikola Pašić Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_Pa%C5%A1i%C4%87_Square), Terazije, [Students' Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studentski_Trg), the [Kalemegdan Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Fortress), [Knez Mihailova Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knez_Mihailova), the [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Serbia), the [Church of Saint Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Saint_Sava), and the [Old Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Old_Palace). On top of this, there are many parks, monuments, museums, cafés, restaurants and shops on both sides of the river. The hilltop [Avala Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_to_the_Unknown_Hero) and [Avala Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avala_Tower) offer views over the city. According to *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)*, [Dorćol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dor%C4%87ol) is the one of top ten coolest suburbs in Europe.[^cite_note-233] The elite neighbourhood of [Dedinje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedinje) is situated near the [Topčider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top%C4%8Dider) and [Košutnjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%A1utnjak) parks. The [Dedinje Royal Compound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedinje_Royal_Compound) which houses the former royal residences of ([Kraljevski Dvor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kraljevski_Dvor) and [Beli Dvor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beli_dvor)) is open for visitors. The palace has many valuable artworks.[^cite_note-234] Nearby, [Josip Broz Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito)'s mausoleum, called *[The House of Flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Flowers_\(mausoleum\))*, documents the life of the former Yugoslav president.[^cite_note-235]

[Ada Ciganlija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Ciganlija) is a former island on the [Sava River](587845.md), and Belgrade's biggest sports and recreational complex. Today it is connected with the right bank of the Sava via two causeways, creating an artificial lake. It is the most popular destination for Belgraders during the city's hot summers. There are 7 km (4 mi) of long beaches and sports facilities for various sports including golf, [football](10568.md), basketball, volleyball, [rugby union](25405.md), baseball, and tennis.[^cite_note-tourist_organization_of_belgrade-236] During summer there are between 200,000 and 300,000 bathers daily.[^cite_note-237]

Belgrade is also known for tourist activities involving extreme sports such as [bungee jumping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungee_jumping), [water skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_skiing), and [paintballing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paintball).[^cite_note-tourist_organization_of_belgrade-236][^cite_note-sport_activities_in_belgrade-238] There are numerous trails on the island, where it is possible to ride a bike, go for a walk, or go jogging.[^cite_note-tourist_organization_of_belgrade-236][^cite_note-sport_activities_in_belgrade-238] Apart from Ada, Belgrade has total of 16 islands[^cite_note-239] on the rivers, many still unused. Among them, the [Great War Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_War_Island), at the confluence of Sava, stands out as an oasis of unshattered wildlife (especially birds).[^cite_note-240] These areas, along with nearby [Small War Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_War_Island), are protected by the city's government as a [nature preserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_preserve).[^cite_note-241] There are [37 protected natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_natural_monuments_in_Belgrade) in the Belgrade urban area, among which eight are geo-heritage sites, i.e. [Straževica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stra%C5%BEevica) profile, Mašin Majdan-[Topčider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top%C4%8Dider), Profile at the [Kalemegdan Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalemegdan_Fortress), Abandoned quarry in [Barajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barajevo), Karagača valley, Artesian well in [Ovča](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ov%C4%8Da), Kapela [loess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loess) profile, and Lake in [Sremčica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srem%C4%8Dica). Other 29 places are biodiversity sites.[^cite_note-242]

Tourist income in 2016 amounted to nearly half a billion [euros](9472.md);[^cite_note-243] with a visit of almost a million registered tourists.[^cite_note-vestionlinecom-244] Of those, in 2019 more than 100,000 tourists arrived by 742 river cruisers.[^cite_note-vestionlinecom-244][^cite_note-245] Average annual growth is between 13% and 14%.[^cite_note-vestionlinecom-244]

As of 2018, there are three officially designated [camp grounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_grounds) in Belgrade. The oldest one is located in [Batajnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batajnica), along the Batajnica Road. Named "Dunav", it is one of the most visited campsites in the country. The second one is situated within the complex of the ethno-household "Zornić's House" in the village of [Baćevac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C4%87evac), while the third is located in [Ripanj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripanj), on the slopes of Avala mountain. In 2017 some 15,000 overnights were recorded in camps.[^cite_note-246]

Belgrade is a common stop on the [Rivers Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EV6_The_Rivers_Route), European [cycling route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-distance_cycling_route) known as "Danube Bike Trail" in Serbia as well as on the [Sultans Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultans_Trail), a long-distance hiking footpath between [Vienna](55866.md) and [Istanbul](3391396.md).

### Nightlife

Main article: [Nightlife in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightlife_in_Belgrade)



Galerija Belgrade, the largest shopping, entertainment, and gastronomic destination in the city, covers 300,000 square meters and is the biggest shopping mall in the region.



Belgrade nightlife

Belgrade has a reputation for vibrant nightlife; many [clubs](19321330.md) that are open until dawn can be found throughout the city.[^cite_note-247] The most recognisable nightlife features of Belgrade are the barges (*splav*) spread along the banks of the Sava and Danube.[^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250]

Many weekend visitors—particularly from [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), [Croatia](5573.md) and [Slovenia](27338.md)—prefer Belgrade nightlife to that of their own capitals due to its perceived friendly atmosphere, plentiful clubs and bars, cheap drinks, lack of significant language barriers, and a lack of night life regulation.[^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252] One of the most famous sites for alternative cultural happenings in the city is the SKC (Student Cultural Centre), located right across from Belgrade's highrise landmark, the [Belgrade Palace tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beogra%C4%91anka). Concerts featuring famous local and foreign bands are often held at the centre. SKC is also the site of various art exhibitions, as well as public debates and discussions.[^cite_note-253]

A more traditional Serbian nightlife experience, accompanied by traditional music known as *[Starogradska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starogradska_muzika)* (roughly translated as *Old Town Music*), typical of northern Serbia's urban environments, is most prominent in [Skadarlija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skadarlija), the city's old [bohemian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemianism) neighbourhood where the poets and artists of Belgrade gathered in the 19th and early 20th centuries. Skadar Street (the centre of Skadarlija) and the surrounding neighbourhood are lined with some of Belgrade's best and oldest traditional restaurants (called [kafanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafana) in Serbian), which date back to that period.[^cite_note-254] At one end of the neighbourhood stands Belgrade's oldest beer brewery, founded in the first half of the 19th century.[^cite_note-255] One of the city's oldest kafanas is the [Znak pitanja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Znak_pitanja) ('?').[^cite_note-256]

*[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* reported that Europe's best nightlife can be found in Belgrade.[^cite_note-257] In the [Lonely Planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonely_Planet) *1000 Ultimate Experiences* Guide of 2009, Belgrade was placed at the 1st spot among the top 10 party cities in the world.[^cite_note-258]

### Fashion and design

Since 1996,[^cite_note-259] semiannual (autumn/winter and spring/summer seasons) [fashion weeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_week) are held citywide. Numerous Serbian and foreign designers and fashion brands have their shows during [Belgrade Fashion Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Fashion_Week). The festival, which collaborates with [London Fashion Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Fashion_Week), has helped launch the international careers of local talents such as [George Styler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Styler) and [Ana Ljubinković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Ljubinkovi%C4%87). British fashion designer [Roksanda Ilinčić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roksanda_Ilin%C4%8Di%C4%87), who was born in the city, also frequently presents her runway shows in Belgrade.

In addition to fashion, there are two major design shows held in Belgrade every year which attract international architects and industrial designers such as [Karim Rashid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karim_Rashid), [Daniel Libeskind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Libeskind), [Patricia Urquiola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricia_Urquiola), and [Konstantin Grcic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Grcic). Both the [Mikser Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikser_Festival) and [Belgrade Design Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Design_Week) feature lectures, exhibits and competitions. Furthermore, international designers like [Sacha Lakic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacha_Lakic), [Ana Kraš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Kra%C5%A1), [Bojana Sentaler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bojana_Sentaler), and [Marek Djordjevic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marek_Djordjevic) are originally from Belgrade.

## Media

See also: [List of media organisations in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_media_organisations_in_Belgrade)



[Tanjug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjug), the former Serbian state-owned news agency building.&#x20;

Belgrade is the most important media hub in Serbia. The city is home to the main headquarters of the national broadcaster [Radio Television Serbia (RTS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Television_of_Serbia), which is a public service broadcaster.[^cite_note-260] The most popular commercial broadcaster is [RTV Pink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTV_Pink), a Serbian media multinational, known for its popular entertainment programmes. One of the most popular commercial broadcasters is [B92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B92), another media company, which has its own TV station, radio station, and music and book publishing arms, as well as the most popular website on the Serbian internet.[^cite_note-261][^cite_note-262] Other TV stations broadcasting from Belgrade include [1Prva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prva_Srpska_Televizija) (formerly *Fox televizija*), [Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova.rs), [N1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N1_\(television\)) and others which only cover the greater Belgrade municipal area, such as [Studio B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTV_Studio_B).

High-circulation daily newspapers published in Belgrade include *[Politika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politika)*, *[Blic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blic)*, *[Alo!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alo!)*, *[Kurir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurir)* and *[Danas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danas_\(newspaper\))*. There are two sporting dailies, *[Sportski žurnal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportski_%C5%BEurnal)* and *[Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSL_Sport)*, and one economic daily, *Privredni pregled*. A new free distribution daily, *[24 sata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24_sata_\(Serbia\))*, was founded in the autumn of 2006. Also, Serbian editions of licensed magazines such as *[Harper's Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harper's_Bazaar)*, *[Elle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elle_\(magazine\))*, *[Cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitan_Serbia)*, *[National Geographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_\(magazine\))*, *[Men's Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men's_Health)*, *[Grazia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazia)* and others have their headquarters in the city.

## Sport and recreation

See also: [List of sporting events in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sporting_events_in_Belgrade)



[Sava Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Ciganlija)



[Belgrade Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Arena)

There are approximately **one-thousand sports facilities** in Belgrade, many of which are capable of serving all levels of sporting events.[^cite_note-263]

[Ada Ciganlija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Ciganlija) island, with its lake and beaches, is one of the most important recreational areas in the city. With a total of 8 km beaches, and a variety of bars, cafés, restaurants and sport facilities, Ada Ciganlija attracts many visitors, especially in summertime.

[Košutnjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko%C5%A1utnjak) Park Forest has numerous running and bike trails, sports facilities for a variety of sports, and indoor and outdoor pools. It is a popular destination that is located only 2 km from Ada Ciganlija.



[Red Star Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Star_Stadium)

During the 1960s and 1970s Belgrade held a number of major international events such as the first ever World Aquatics Championships in [1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_World_Aquatics_Championships), [1976 European Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1976) and [1973 European Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972%E2%80%9373_European_Cup), European Athletics Championships in [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_European_Athletics_Championships) and [European Indoor Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Athletics_Indoor_Championships) in 1969, European Basketball Championships in [1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_1961) and [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_1975), European Volleyball Championship for [men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Men's_European_Volleyball_Championship) and [women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Women's_European_Volleyball_Championship) in 1975 and World Amateur Boxing Championships in [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_World_Amateur_Boxing_Championships).

Since the early 2000s Belgrade again hosts major sporting events nearly every year. Some of these include [EuroBasket 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroBasket_2005), European Handball Championship ([men's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_European_Men's_Handball_Championship) and [women's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_European_Women's_Handball_Championship)) in 2012, [World Handball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Women's_Handball_Championship) for women in 2013, European Volleyball Championships for men in [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_European_Volleyball_Championship) and [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Women's_European_Volleyball_Championship) for women, the 2006 and 2016 [European Water Polo Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Water_Polo_Championship), the [European Youth Olympic Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Youth_Olympic_Festival) 2007 and the [2009 Summer Universiade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universiade).[^cite_note-264] More recently, Belgrade hosted [European Athletics Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Athletics_Indoor_Championships) in 2017 and the basketball [EuroLeague Final Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroLeague_Final_Four) tournaments in 2018 and 2022. Global and continental championships in other sports such as [tennis](29773.md), [futsal](188197.md), [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo), [karate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karate), [wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling), [rowing](25736.md), [kickboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickboxing), [table tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_tennis), and [chess](5134.md) have also been held in recent years.

The city is home to Serbia's two biggest and most successful [football](10568.md) clubs, [Red Star Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Star_Belgrade) and [Partizan Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Partizan). Red Star won the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) (*European Cup*) in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990%E2%80%9391_European_Cup), and Partizan was runner-up in [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965%E2%80%9366_European_Cup). The two major stadiums in Belgrade are *Marakana* ([Red Star Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Star_Stadium)) and [Partizan Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partizan_Stadium).[^cite_note-265] The [rivalry between Red Star and Partizan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternal_derby_\(Serbia\)) is one of the fiercest in world football.[^cite_note-266]

With a capacity of 19,384 spectators, [Belgrade Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Arena) is one of the largest indoor arenas in Europe.[^cite_note-267] It is used for major sporting events and large concerts. In May 2008, it was the venue for the [53rd Eurovision Song Contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2008).[^cite_note-268][Aleksandar Nikolić Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_Nikoli%C4%87_Hall) is the main venue of [basketball](3921.md) clubs [KK Partizan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KK_Partizan), the European champion of 1992, and [KK Crvena Zvezda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KK_Crvena_Zvezda).[^cite_note-269][^cite_note-270] In recent years, Belgrade has also given rise to several world-class tennis players such as [Ana Ivanovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Ivanovic), [Jelena Janković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelena_Jankovi%C4%87) and [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md). Ivanovic and Djokovic are the first female and male Belgraders, respectively, to win [Grand Slam](197638.md) singles titles and been [ATP number 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ATP_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players) with Jelena Janković. The Serbian national team won the [2010 Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Davis_Cup_World_Group), beating the French team in the finals played in the Belgrade Arena.[^cite_note-271]

The [Belgrade Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Marathon) is held annually since 1988. Belgrade was a candidate to host the 1992 and the 1996 [Summer Olympic Games](27566.md).[^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273]

## Education

See also: [List of educational institutions in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_educational_institutions_in_Belgrade)



[Captain Miša Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Mi%C5%A1a_Mansion) which serves as the [Rectorate of the University of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Mi%C5%A1a's_Mansion).



Palace of Science.

Belgrade has two state universities and several private institutions of higher education. The [University of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Belgrade), founded in 1808 as the *[Velika škola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velika_%C5%A1kola)*, is the oldest institution of higher learning in Serbia.[^cite_note-274] Having developed with much of the rest of the city in the 19th century, several university buildings are recognised as forming a constituent part of Belgrade's architecture and [cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_heritage). With enrolment numbers of nearly 90,000 students, the university is one of Europe's largest.[^cite_note-275]

The city is also home to 195 primary (elementary) schools and 85 secondary schools. The primary school system has 162 regular schools, 14 [special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_education) schools, 15 art schools, and 4 adult schools, while the secondary school system has 51 vocational schools, 21 [gymnasiums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnasium_\(school\)), 8 art schools and 5 special schools. The 230,000 pupils are managed by 22,000 employees in over 500 buildings, covering around 1.1×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> m<sup>2</sup> (12×1&#x30;~~^~~<sup>6</sup> sq ft).[^cite_note-276]

## Transportation

Main article: [Transport in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Belgrade)

See also: [Trams in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Belgrade) and [Bridges in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridges_in_Belgrade)

[Belgrade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Centre_Railway_Station) and underground [Vukov Spomenik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vukov_Spomenik_railway_station) railway stations





Belgrade has an extensive public transport system consisting of buses (118 urban lines and more than 300 suburban lines), trams (12 lines), [trolleybuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) (8 lines) and [BG Voz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BG_Voz) (6 lines).[^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278] Buses, trolleybuses and trams are run by [GSP Beograd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSP_Beograd) and [SP Lasta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasta_Beograd) in cooperation with private companies on some bus routes. The suburban train network, BG Voz, run by city government in cooperation with [Serbian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Railways), is a part of the integrated transport system, and has three lines (Batajnica-Ovča and Ovča-Resnik and Belgrade centre-Mladenovac), with more announced.[^cite_note-279][^cite_note-280] As of 27 February 2024 tickets may be purchased either via SMS or in physical paper form via the *Beograd plus* / Београд плус system.[^cite_note-281] Daily connections link the capital to other towns in Serbia and many other European destinations through the city's [central bus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Bus_Station). Since January 2025 all public transport in Belgrade is free.[^cite_note-282]

[Beovoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beovoz) was the [suburban/commuter railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_rail) network that provided [mass-transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass-transit) services in the city, similar to Paris's [RER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9seau_Express_R%C3%A9gional) and Toronto's [GO Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GO_Transit). The main usage of system was to connect the suburbs with the city centre. Beovoz was operated by [Serbian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Railways).[^cite_note-283] However, this system was abolished back in 2013, mostly due to introduction of more efficient BG Voz. Belgrade is one of the last big European capitals and cities with over a million people to have no metro or subway or other [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system. As of 2025, the [Belgrade Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Metro) is currently under construction, which will have 2 lines. The first line is expected to be operational by August 2028.[^cite_note-284][^cite_note-285]

The new [Belgrade Centre railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Centre_railway_station) is the hub for almost all national and international trains. The [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) that connects Belgrade with [Novi Sad](113288.md) started its service on 19 March 2022.[^cite_note-286] The extension towards [Subotica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subotica) and [Budapest](36787.md) is under construction,[^cite_note-287] and there are plans for a southwards extension towards [Niš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni%C5%A1) and [North Macedonia](23564616.md).[^cite_note-288]

Transport in Belgrade - [Trams in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Belgrade) and [New Belgrade railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Belgrade_railway_station)





The city is placed along the [Pan-European corridors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_corridors) [X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_Corridor_X) and VII.[^cite_note-city_of_belgrade-2-11] The motorway system provides for easy access to Novi Sad and Budapest to the north, Niš to the south, and [Zagreb](55906.md) to the west. Expressway is also toward Pančevo and new Expressway construction toward Obrenovac (Montenegro) is scheduled for March 2017. [Belgrade bypass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_bypass) is connecting the [E70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E70_in_Serbia) and [E75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E75_in_Serbia) motorways and is under construction.[^cite_note-289]

Situated at the confluence of two major rivers, the Danube and the Sava, Belgrade has 11 bridges, the most important of which are [Branko's Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branko's_Bridge), [Gazela Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazela_Bridge), [Ada Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Bridge) and [Pupin Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pupin_Bridge), the first two of which connect the core of the city to [New Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Belgrade). In addition, an 'inner magistral semi-ring' is almost done and includes a new Ada bridge across the Sava river (opened in 2012)[^cite_note-290] and a new Pupin bridge (opened in 2014)[^cite_note-291] across the Danube, which ease [commuting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuting) within the city and unload traffic from the Gazela Bridge and Branko's Bridge.[^cite_note-292]

Transport in Belgrade - [Trolleybuses in Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Belgrade) and [Belgrade Nikola Tesla Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Nikola_Tesla_Airport)





The [Port of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Belgrade) is on the Danube, and allows the city to receive goods by river.[^cite_note-293] The city is also served by [Belgrade Nikola Tesla Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Nikola_Tesla_Airport), 12 km (7.5 mi) west of the city centre, near [Surčin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sur%C4%8Din). At its peak in 1986, almost 3 million passengers travelled through the airport, though that number dwindled to a trickle in the 1990s.[^cite_note-294] Following renewed growth in 2000, the number of passengers reached approximately 2 million in 2004 and 2005,[^cite_note-295] over 2.6 million passengers in 2008,[^cite_note-296] reaching over 3 million passengers.[^cite_note-297] A record with over 4 million passengers was broken in 2014, when Belgrade Nikola Tesla Airport became the second fastest growing major airport in Europe.[^cite_note-298] The numbers continued to grow steadily and the all-time peak of over 6 million passengers was reached in 2019.[^cite_note-299]
