# Man-portable air-defense system



Chinese made [QW-2 MANPADS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QW-2_MANPADS) of the [Bangladesh Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Army)

A U.S. Marine fires a [FIM-92 Stinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIM-92_Stinger) missile during a July 2009 training exercise in California.

**Man-portable air-defense systems** (**MANPADS** or **MPADS**) are portable shoulder-launched [surface-to-air missiles](182664.md). They are [guided weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile) and are a threat to low-flying [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft), especially [helicopters](8286923.md) and also used against low-flying [cruise missiles](6590.md). These short-range missiles can also be fired from vehicles, tripods, weapon platforms, and warships.

## Overview



[Japanese airmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Air_Self-Defense_Force) aiming a [Type 91](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_91_surface-to-air_missile) Kai MANPADS at a mock airborne target in the Pacific Alaskan Range Complex as part of Red Flag – Alaska in 2008.

MANPADS were developed in the 1950s to provide military ground forces with protection from jet aircraft. They have received a great deal of attention, partly because armed [terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) groups have used them against commercial airliners. These missiles, affordable and widely available through a variety of sources, have been used successfully over the past three decades, both in military conflicts, by militant groups, and by terrorist organizations.[^cite_note-1]

Twenty-five countries, including China, Iran, Poland, Russia, Sweden, the United Kingdom and the United States produce man-portable air defense systems.[^cite_note-CRS1-2][^cite_note-3] Possession, export, and trafficking of such weapons is tightly controlled due to the threat they pose to [civil aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_aviation) although such efforts have not always been successful.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

The missiles are about 1.5 to 1.8 m (5 to 6 ft) in length and weigh about 17 to 18 kg (37 to 40 lb), depending on the model. MANPADS generally have a target detection range of about 10 km (6 mi) and an engagement range of about 6 km (4 mi); aircraft flying at 6,100 metres (20,000 ft) or higher are relatively safe.[^cite_note-schaffer-6]

### Infrared

Main article: [Infrared homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing)

Infrared homing missiles are designed to home-in on a heat source on an aircraft, typically the [engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_engine) exhaust plume, and detonate a [warhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhead) near the heat source to destroy or otherwise damage the aircraft. These missiles use [passive guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_guidance), meaning that they do not emit [heat signatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_signature), making them difficult to detect by aircraft employing countermeasure systems.[^cite_note-7]

#### First generation



Soviet [9K32 Strela-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K32_Strela-2) in use

The first missiles deployed in the 1960s were infrared missiles. First generation MANPADS, such as the early prototypes of the American [Redeye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIM-43_Redeye),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZaloga20236-8] early versions of the Soviet [9K32 Strela-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K32_Strela-2),[^cite_note-10] and the Chinese [HN-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HN-5) (A copy of the Soviet Strela-2), are considered "tail-chase weapons" as their uncooled spin-scan [seekers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrared_homing) can only discern the superheated interior of the target's [jet engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_engine) from background noise. This means they are only capable of accurately tracking the aircraft from the rear when the engines are fully exposed to the missile's seeker and provide a sufficient thermal signature for engagement. First generation IR missiles are also highly susceptible to interfering thermal signatures from background sources, including the sun, which many experts feel makes them somewhat unreliable, and they are prone to erratic behaviour in the terminal phase of engagement.[^cite_note-CRS2-11] While less effective than more modern weapons, they remain common in irregular forces as they are not limited by the short shelf-life of gas coolant cartridges used by later systems, requiring only a battery, but the missiles themselves have degraded over the years;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZaloga202370−71-12] For instance, during the [Syrian Civil War](30741795.md), the [Ahfad al-Rasul Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahfad_al-Rasul_Brigades) captured 50 Strela-2M missiles (decades past their warranty) from a Syrian Army base in late 2012, but none of them worked.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZaloga202375-13]

#### Second generation

In 1967, the American FIM-43C Redeye was the first mass-produced design to make use of cooled-detector technology,[^cite_note-14] followed by the Soviet [Strela-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K34_Strela-3) in 1975 and Chinese [HN-5A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HN-5), [QW-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QW-1) in the late 1980s.[^cite_note-guancha_hn-15][^cite_note-weg_qw-16] They use gas-cooled seeker heads and a conical scanning technique, which enables the seeker to filter out most interfering background IR sources as well as locking on the cooler portions of the jet exhaust plume and other IR-emitting portions of the aircraft, giving them a limited capability of head-on and side engagements.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZaloga202314,_27-17]

#### Third generation

Third generation infrared MANPADS, such as the Chinese [QW-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QW-2),[^cite_note-guancha_hn-15] and the Soviet [9K38 Igla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K38_Igla), uses dual waveband infra-red tracking; while Chinese [HN-6B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HN-6), French [Mistral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral_missile), and the US [FIM-92B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIM-92_Stinger#Variants), use [rosette scanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosette_seeker) detectors to produce a quasi-image of the target. Their seeker compares input from multiple detection bands, either two widely separated IR bands or IR and [UV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet), giving them much greater ability to discern and reject countermeasures deployed by the target aircraft.[^cite_note-schaffer-6][^cite_note-CRS2-11]

#### Fourth generation

Fourth generation missiles, such as the canceled American [FIM-92 Stinger Block 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIM-92_Stinger#Variants), Russian [Verba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SA-25), Chinese [QW-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QW-4), and Japanese [Type 91 surface-to-air missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_91_surface-to-air_missile) use imaging infrared [focal plane array](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staring_array) guidance systems and other advanced sensor systems, which permit engagement at greater ranges.[^cite_note-raytheon1-18]

### Command line-of-sight

Main article: [Command line-of-sight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_line-of-sight)

[Command guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_guidance) (CLOS) missiles do not home in on a particular aspect (heat source or radio or radar transmissions) of the targeted aircraft. Instead, the missile operator or gunner visually acquires the target using a magnified optical sight and then uses radio controls to "fly" the missile into the aircraft. One of the benefits of such a missile is that it is virtually immune to flares and other basic countermeasure systems that are designed primarily to defeat IR missiles. The major drawback of CLOS missiles is that they require highly trained and skilled operators. Numerous reports from the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War) in the 1980s cite Afghan [mujahedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahedin) as being disappointed with the British-supplied [Blowpipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blowpipe_missile) CLOS missile because it was too difficult to learn to use and highly inaccurate, particularly when employed against fast-moving jet aircraft.[^cite_note-diplomat1-19] Given these considerations, many experts believe that CLOS missiles are not as ideally suited for untrained personnel use as IR missiles, which sometimes are referred to as "fire and forget" missiles.[^cite_note-20]

Later versions of CLOS missiles, such as the British [Javelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javelin_surface-to-air_missile), use a solid-state television camera in lieu of the optical tracker to make the gunner's task easier. The Javelin's manufacturer, [Thales Air Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Air_Defence), claims that their missile is virtually impervious to countermeasures.[^cite_note-21]

### Laser guided

Main articles: [Laser guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_guidance) and [beam riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beam_riding)



RBS 70 and operators in Australian service, 2021

[Laser guided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_guidance) MANPADS can use laser beam-riding guidance where a sensor in the missile's tail detects the emissions from a laser on the launcher and attempts to steer the missile to fly at the exact middle of the beam, or between two beams. Missiles such as Sweden's [RBS-70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RBS-70) and Britain's [Starstreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starstreak) can engage aircraft from all angles and only require the operator to continuously track the target using a joystick to keep the laser aim point on the target: the latest version of RBS 70 features a tracking engagement mode where fine aim adjustments of the laser emitter are handled by the launcher itself, with the user only having to make coarse aim corrections. Because there are no radio data links from the ground to the missile, the missile cannot be effectively jammed after it is launched. Even though beam-riding missiles require relatively extensive training and skill to operate, many experts consider these missiles particularly menacing due to the missiles' resistance to most conventional countermeasures in use today.[^cite_note-CRS3-22][^cite_note-23]

Another laser guidance method is the semi-active laser (SAL) homing, which the missile seeker head tracks the target that is being continuously painted by the shooter. The [QW-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QW-3) utilizes a SAL seeker, which is highly resistant to infrared countermeasures.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The QW-3 is the only MANPADS in the world using SAL guidance mode.[^cite_note-26]

### Against civilian aircraft

- [Air Rhodesia Flight 825](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Rhodesia_Flight_825) is the first example of a civilian airliner shot down by a man-portable surface-to-air missile. The pilot of the aircraft managed to make a controlled crash landing.

- [Air Rhodesia Flight 827](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Rhodesia_Flight_827) was also shot down in February 1979 by the [Zimbabwe People's Revolutionary Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimbabwe_People's_Revolutionary_Army) with a [Strela 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strela_2) missile. All 59 passengers and crew were killed.

- The [1993 Sukhumi airliner attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Sukhumi_airliner_attacks) involved 5 civilian aircraft shot down within a total of 4 days in [Sukhumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhumi), [Abkhazia](18933375.md), [Georgia](48768.md), killing 108 people.

- [2007 Mogadishu TransAVIAexport Airlines Il-76 crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Mogadishu_TransAVIAexport_Airlines_Il-76_crash): On 23 March 2007, a [TransAVIAexport Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransAVIAexport_Airlines) [Ilyushin Il-76](229943.md) airplane crashed in the outskirts of [Mogadishu](60154.md), [Somalia](27358.md), during the [2007 Battle of Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_\(March%E2%80%93April_2007\)). ## Countermeasures



A 9K38 Igla (NATO reporting name: SA-18) dual missile launch platform mounted on a Mercedes-Benz [Unimog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unimog) of the Mexican Navy in a Mexican military parade.

Man-portable air defense systems are a popular [black market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_market) item for insurgent forces.[^cite_note-41] Their proliferation became the subject of the [Wassenaar Arrangement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassenaar_Arrangement)'s *(WA)22 Elements for Export Controls of MANPADS*, the [G8](59755.md) *Action Plan* of 2 June 2003,[^cite_note-42] the October 2003 [Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Economic_Cooperation) (APEC) Summit, *Bangkok Declaration on Partnership for the Future* and in July 2003 the [Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe](22796.md) (OSCE), Forum for Security Co-operation, *Decision No. 7/03: Man-portable Air Defense Systems*.[^cite_note-43]

Understanding the problem in 2003, [Colin Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Powell) remarked that there was "no threat more serious to aviation" than the missiles,[^cite_note-44] which can be used to shoot down helicopters and commercial airliners, and are sold illegally for as little as a few hundred dollars. The U.S. has led a global effort to dismantle these weapons, with over 30,000 voluntarily destroyed since 2003, but probably thousands are still in the hands of insurgents, especially in [Iraq](7515928.md), where they were looted from the military arsenals of the former dictator [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein),[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] and in [Afghanistan](737.md) as well. In August 2010, a report by the [Federation of American Scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_American_Scientists) (FAS) confirmed that "only a handful" of illicit MANPADS were recovered from national resistance caches in Iraq in 2009, according to media reports and interviews with military sources.[^cite_note-47]

### Military

With the growing number of MANPADS attacks on civilian airliners, a number of different [countermeasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countermeasure) systems have been developed specifically to protect aircraft against the missiles.

- **AN/[ALQ-144](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALQ-144)**, **AN/ALQ-147** and **AN/ALQ-157** are U.S.-produced systems, developed by Sanders Associates in the 1970s.

- **[AN/ALQ-212 ATIRCM, AN/AAQ-24 Nemesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directional_Infrared_Counter_Measures)** are NATO systems developed by [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems) and [Northrop Grumman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman) respectively.

### Black market

Although most MANPADS are owned and accounted for by governments, political upheavals and corruption have allowed thousands of them to enter the black market. In the years 1998–2018, at least 72 non-state groups have fielded MANPADS.[^cite_note-55] Civilians in the United States cannot legally own MANPADS.[^cite_note-56]
