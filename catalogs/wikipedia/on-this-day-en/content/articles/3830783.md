**Al Udeid Air Base** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): قاعدة العديد الجوية; also known as **Abu Nakhlah Airport** (مطار أبو نخلة)) is one of two military bases to the southwest of [Doha](26214389.md), Qatar, in the [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Qatar) of [Al Rayyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Rayyan). It is the largest US military base in the Middle East, hosting approximately 10,000 military personnel as of 2026.[^cite_note-5]

It houses the [Qatar Emiri Air Force](4014890.md), [United States Air Force](32090.md) (USAF), United Kingdom [Royal Air Force](25679.md) (RAF), and other foreign forces. It is host to a forward headquarters of [U.S. Central Command](338482.md), headquarters of the [USAF Central Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Forces_Central_Command), [No. 83 Expeditionary Air Group RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._83_Expeditionary_Air_Group), and the [379th Air Expeditionary Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/379th_Air_Expeditionary_Wing) of the USAF.

In June 2017, the base hosted over 11,000 U.S. and Coalition [anti-ISIL coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_intervention_against_ISIL) forces and over 100 operational aircraft.[^cite_note-6]

On 23 June 2025,[^cite_note-timezone-7] [Iran](14653.md) launched missiles at the base, in retaliation for the 21 June [strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Midnight_Hammer) by the United States on Iranian nuclear facilities as part of the [Iran–Israel war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Iran struck al-Udeid again during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).

## History

### United States Air Force

An aerial view of "Ops Town" at Al Udeid Air Base in 2004

An aerial view of "Log Town" at Al Udeid Air Base in 2004

Following joint military operations during [Operation Desert Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Storm) in 1991, [Qatar](8238258.md) and the United States concluded a Defense Cooperation Agreement that has been subsequently expanded. In 1996, Qatar built Al Udeid Air Base at a cost of more than \$1 billion. The U.S. first used the then-secret base in late September 2001, when the [U.S. Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Air_Force) (USAF) needed to get aircraft in position for its operations in Afghanistan. The U.S. has nearly 40,000 military personnel in the [Middle East](19323.md). The U.S. [Fifth Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Fleet) is in [Bahrain](18933277.md) and has 28,000 military personnel in [Kuwait](7515890.md), Bahrain, and Qatar. Kingdoms, including Qatar, cover 60 percent of the costs, around \$650 million.[^cite_note-10]

Official acknowledgement of the base came in March 2002, when U.S. Vice President [Dick Cheney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Cheney) stopped there during a trip to the region with a group of reporters. In April 2003, shortly after the start of the [U.S.-led invasion of Iraq](201936.md), the U.S. Combat [Air Operations Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Operations_Center) for the Middle East moved from [Prince Sultan Air Base](5553272.md) in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) to what was then a backup headquarters, built a year prior in Qatar that was viewed as a more congenial location for basing U.S. troops.[^cite_note-11]

Al Udeid and other facilities in Qatar serve as [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics), [command](7092305.md), and basing hubs for the [U.S. Central Command](338482.md) (CENTCOM) area of operations, and oversees U.S. air operations in countries, including [Iraq](7515928.md), [Afghanistan](737.md), and [Syria](7515849.md).[^cite_note-12]

### Royal Air Force

Between 2004 and 2009 the airbase was used by the British [Royal Air Force](25679.md) with transport and fast-jet aircraft to support [Operation Telic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Telic) ([Iraq War](5043324.md)) and [Operation Herrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Herrick) ([War in Afghanistan](19666611.md)).[^cite_note-ahb-13]

These included 6 to 8 [Panavia Tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panavia_Tornado) GR4 aircraft drawn from different parts of the Royal Air Force as well as multiple [Vickers VC10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_VC10) from the [No. 101 Squadron RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._101_Squadron_RAF). British Tornados were equipped with a range of stores, including the Vicon Recce Pod, [LITENING targeting pod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LITENING_targeting_pod), [1000 lb HE bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_explosive), [Paveway II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paveway_II) and [Paveway III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paveway_III) laser-guided bombs, and the [RAPTOR Recce Pod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAPTOR). They were chosen for their currency and up-to-date modification state.[^cite_note-RAF-2005-2009-14]

The RAF aircraft were accompanied by aircraft engineers and personnel active in support and operation management roles. Between 20 March to 15 April 2003, the Al Udeid Wing of the RAF flew 268 sorties in operations against the Government of [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) in Iraq at the start of the Iraq War.[^cite_note-ahb-13]

### Royal Australian Air Force

As part of [Australia's contribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Falconer) to coalition forces in the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md), 14 [F/A-18 Hornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F/A-18_Hornet) [fighters](10929.md) from [No. 75 Squadron RAAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._75_Squadron_RAAF) were based at Al Udeid, along with two [Lockheed P-3 Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_P-3_Orion) [maritime patrol aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_patrol_aircraft) and three [Lockheed C-130 Hercules](7697.md) [military transport aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_transport_aircraft). During the early phases of the war, the Hornets flew long missions escorting and protecting coalition [AWACS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_early_warning_and_control) [early warning aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_warning_aircraft) and [tanker aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanker_aircraft) used for [air-to-air refueling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air-to-air_refueling).[^cite_note-15]

Later, when the threat to coalition aircraft was reduced, the Hornets switched to ground attack and combat support roles and were used to [attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_aircraft) Iraqi [ground forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_forces) with [laser-guided bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser-guided_bomb). The Orions flew long endurance missions over the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) tracking vessels, curbing smuggling, and guarding against the threat posed by suicide boats. The deployed Hercules flew supplies and equipment into Iraq, and later flew some of the first [humanitarian aid](434679.md) into Baghdad. The 14 [Royal Australian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Australian_Air_Force) Hornets flew over 670 [sorties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sortie) during the war, including 350 combat sorties over Iraq.[^cite_note-16]

### Iranian missile strikes



Donald Trump visiting Al Udeid Air Base Qatar in May 2025, 1 month before the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War)

Main article: [2025 Iranian strikes on Al Udeid Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Iranian_strikes_on_Al_Udeid_Air_Base)

On 23 June 2025, as a response to *[Operation Midnight Hammer](80262844.md)* by the [USA](3434750.md), where nuclear facilities were targeted, [Iran](14653.md) started [missile](19594.md) strikes on the military base, shortly before 8 p.m. [AST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+03\:00) and after Qatar had closed its airspace.[^cite_note-17] This was followed by explosions in [Doha](26214389.md).[^cite_note-18]

Qatar said that there were no casualties and that all but one missile was intercepted.[^cite_note-\:2-19] Iran said six missiles hit successfully.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Satellite images by Planet Labs revealed the destruction of the white [radome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radome) that contained American secure communication equipment.[^cite_note-22]

On 28 February 2026, during the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), Iran attacked the base again. [CBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_News), citing the [Canadian Department of National Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_National_Defence_\(Canada\))'s website, reported that Canadian soldiers were present at the time of the attack.[^cite_note-CBC-23]

### Royal Air Force



Combined [Air and Space Operations Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_and_Space_Operations_Center) (CAOC) provided command and control of air power throughout [Iraq](7515928.md), [Syria](7515849.md), [Afghanistan](737.md), and 17 other nations.

After the withdrawal of British Tornados and VC10s in Summer 2009 to other locations, Royal Air Force activities at Al Udeid were reduced.[^cite_note-RAF-2005-2009-14]

Since 2014 it has been used as HQ for British involvement in airstrikes against [ISIS](9087364.md) in Iraq ([Operation Shader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Shader)).[^cite_note-RAF-Al-Udeid-ISIS-26]

The Royal Air Force formally stationed an [RC-135 Rivet Joint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC-135_Rivet_Joint) signals intelligence aircraft at the base to operate over Iraq and Syria, although this aircraft has been pictured operating from [Chania International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chania_International_Airport) in [Crete](6591.md) on occasion.[^cite_note-27]

#### Military cooperation and foreign assistance

With its small territory and narrow population base, Qatar relies to a large degree on external cooperation and support for its security. Qatar invested over [US\$](18717338.md)1 billion to construct the Al Udeid airfield during the 1990s; it did not have a large [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force) of its own at the time. The [United States Army Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Corps_of_Engineers) also awarded over \$100 million in Military Construction Air Force (MCAF) contracts for the construction of U.S. storage, housing, service, command, and communication facilities.[^cite_note-28]



[U.S. Air Force](32090.md) [KC-135 Stratotankers](17441.md) at Al Udeid Air Base in 2017

In early June 2017, the Pentagon said that the diplomatic [rupture and tensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Qatar_diplomatic_crisis) between Qatar and some of its Arab neighbors would not affect U.S. operations at the Air Base.[^cite_note-29]

In June 2019, the base stationed [Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-22_Raptor) for the first time.[^cite_note-30] A month later, an expansion of the base was announced to be funded by Qatar,[^cite_note-31] which would cost \$1.8 billion.[^cite_note-32]

#### Congress appropriations and authorizations

The [National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2008) (P.L. 110-181) authorized \$81.7 million in FY2008 spending to build new Air Force and [Special Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_special_operations_forces) facilities in Qatar.[^cite_note-33]

The [National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2009) (P.L. 110-417) authorized \$69.6 million in FY2009 spending to build new Air Force and Special Operations facilities.[^cite_note-34]

The [National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2010) (P.L. 111-84) authorized \$117 million in FY2010 spending to build new Air Force recreational, dormitory, and other facilities at Al Udeid.[^cite_note-35]
