The **Department of the Treasury** (**USDT**)[^cite_note-isbn0313245789-pp275-2] is the [national treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury) and finance department of the [federal government of the United States](195149.md). It is one of 15 current [U.S. government departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_executive_departments).[^cite_note-3] The treasury executes [currency circulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_in_circulation) in the domestic fiscal system, [collects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_collector) all [federal taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_the_United_States) through the [Internal Revenue Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Revenue_Service), manages [U.S. government debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Treasury_security), [licenses and supervises banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_regulation#Licensing_and_supervision) and [thrift institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savings_and_loan_association), and advises the [legislative](195149.md#Legislative_branch) and [executive branches](195149.md#Executive_branch) on [fiscal policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_policy_of_the_United_States).&#x20;

The department is administered by the [secretary of the treasury](43338.md), who is a member of the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States). The department oversees the [Bureau of Engraving and Printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Engraving_and_Printing) and the [U.S. Mint](358818.md), two federal agencies responsible for printing all [paper currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_currency) and minting [coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_coinage) respectively. The [treasurer of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer_of_the_United_States) has limited statutory duties, but advises the Secretary on various matters such as coinage and currency production.[^cite_note-ustreasurydepttreasurer2018-4] Both the treasurer and treasury secretary's signatures appear on all [U.S. dollar bank notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Note).[^cite_note-crutsinger2017-5]&#x20;

The department was established by an [act of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Congress) in 1789 to manage government [revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue).[^cite_note-Act-to-establish-6] The first secretary of the treasury was [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton) from [New York](8210131.md), a [Founding Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_Fathers_of_the_United_States). Appointed by [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington), the nation's first [president](24113.md), Hamilton was sworn into office on September 11, 1789.[^cite_note-7] Hamilton established the nation's early financial system and for several years was a major presence in [Washington's administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_Washington).[^cite_note-wolffscanlan2006-8] The department is customarily referred to as "Treasury", solely, without any [preceding article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_\(grammar\)) – a transitional remnant from [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English) to [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English).&#x20;

## History

### American Revolution

The history of the Department of the Treasury began during the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution) when the [Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Congress), convening in [Philadelphia](50585.md), deliberated the crucial question of how it would finance the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) in which the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) ultimately sought [independence](31874.md) from the [Kingdom of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain), then led by [King George III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_III).[^cite_note-\:0-9]&#x20;

The Congress had no power to [levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_levies) and [collect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_collector) taxes, nor was there a tangible basis for securing funds from foreign investors or governments. The delegates resolved to issue paper money in the form of [bills of credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bills_of_credit), promising [redemption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat_money) in [coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coin) on faith in the revolutionary cause.[^cite_note-\:0-9] On June 22, 1775, only a few days after the [Battle of Bunker Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bunker_Hill), the Continental Congress issued \$2 million in bills; on July 25, 28 citizens of Philadelphia were employed by Congress to sign and number the currency.[^cite_note-10]&#x20;

On July 29, 1775, the [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress) assigned responsibility for the administration of the revolutionary government's finances to joint Continental treasurers [George Clymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Clymer) and [Michael Hillegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Hillegas).[^cite_note-\:0-9] Congress stipulated that each of the colonies contribute to the Continental government's funds. To ensure proper and efficient handling of the growing [national debt](695460.md) in the face of weak economic and political ties between the colonies, the Congress, on February 17, 1776, designated a committee of five to superintend the treasury, [settle accounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlement_\(finance\)), and report periodically to the Congress.[^cite_note-\:0-9] On April 1, a Treasury Office of Accounts, consisting of an auditor general and [clerks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clerk), was established to facilitate the settlement of claims and to keep the public accounts for the government of the United Colonies. With the signing of the [Declaration of Independence](31874.md) on July 4, 1776, the newborn republic as a [sovereign nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_nation) was able to secure loans from abroad.[^cite_note-11]&#x20;

Despite the infusion of foreign and domestic loans, the [united colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) were unable to establish a well-organized agency for financial administration.[^cite_note-\:0-9] Michael Hillegas was first called Treasurer of the United States on May 14, 1777. The Treasury Office was reorganized three times between 1778 and 1781. The \$241.5 million in paper Continental bills [devalued](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devaluation) rapidly. By May 1781, the dollar [collapsed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperinflation) at a rate of from 500 to 1000 to 1 against [hard currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_currency).[^cite_note-\:03-12] Protests against the worthless money swept the colonies, giving rise to the expression "[not worth a Continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_American_currency#Continental_currency)".[^cite_note-\:02-13]&#x20;

### Late 18th century

Since the late 18th century, the office has been [customarily referred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English) to as the singular "Treasury", without any [preceding article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_\(grammar\)), as a remnant of the country's transition from [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English) to [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] For example, the department notes its guiding purpose as "Treasury's mission" instead of "the Treasury's mission."[^cite_note-16]&#x20;

[Robert Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Morris_\(financier\)) was designated Superintendent of Finance in 1781 and restored stability to the nation's finances. Morris, a wealthy colonial [merchant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant), was nicknamed "the financier" because of his reputation for procuring funds or goods on a moment's notice. His staff included a [comptroller](965040.md), a [treasurer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasurer), a [register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Register_of_the_Treasury), and [auditors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auditor), who managed the country's finances through 1784, when Morris resigned because of ill health. The treasury board, consisting of three commissioners, continued to oversee the finances of the confederation of former colonies until September 1789.&#x20;

### Founding



Original seal, dating from before 1968

&#x20;The [First United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_United_States_Congress) convened in [New York City](645042.md) on March 4, 1789, marking the beginning of government under the [U.S. Constitution](31644.md). On September 2, 1789, Congress created a permanent institution for the management of government finances:

> Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled, That there shall be a Department of Treasury, in which shall be the following officers, namely: a Secretary of the Treasury, to be deemed head of the department; a Comptroller, an Auditor, a Treasurer, a Register, and an [Assistant to the Secretary of the Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Assistant_Secretary_of_the_Treasury), which assistant shall be appointed by the said Secretary.[^cite_note-Act-to-establish-6][^cite_note-17]

[Robert Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Morris_\(financier\)) from [Pennsylvania](23332.md), who was also a Second Continental Congress delegate, was Washington's first choice for the position, but declined the appointment.[^cite_note-adams2018-18] [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton) was recommended instead to Washington by Morris. Hamilton took the oath of office as the first [secretary of the treasury](43338.md) on September 11, 1789. Hamilton had served as [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington)'s *[aide-de-camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aide-de-camp)* during the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) and was influential in the [ratification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution#1788_ratification) of the Constitution. Hamilton's [financial and managerial acumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_acumen) made him a logical choice for addressing the problem of the new nation's heavy [war debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_debt). His first official act as secretary was to submit a report to Congress in which he laid the foundation for the nation's financial health. Hamilton's portrait appears on the [obverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obverse_and_reverse) of the [ten-dollar bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_ten-dollar_bill), while the [Treasury Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_Building_\(Washington,_D.C.\)) in Washington, D.C. is depicted on the [reverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obverse_and_reverse).[^cite_note-uscep2018\$10-19]&#x20;

To the surprise of many legislators, he insisted upon [federal assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt_Assumption) and dollar-for-dollar repayment of the country's \$75 million debt in order to revitalize the [public credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_credit): "\[T]he debt of the United States was the price of liberty. The faith of America has been repeatedly pledged for it, and with solemnities that give peculiar force to the obligation."[^cite_note-syrett1962vol6-20] Hamilton foresaw the development of industry and trade in the United States, suggesting that government revenues be based upon [customs duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_duties).[^cite_note-syrett1962vol6-20] His sound [financial policies](132645.md) also inspired investment in the [Bank of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Bank_of_the_United_States), which acted as the government's [fiscal agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_agent).&#x20;

The Department of Treasury believes their seal was created by [Francis Hopkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Hopkinson#Great_Seal_of_the_United_States), the treasurer of loans. He submitted bills to Congress in 1780 that authorized the design of department seals, including a seal for the Board of Treasury. While it is not certain that Hopkinson designed the seal, it closely resembles others he created.[^cite_note-21]&#x20;

### 19th and 20th centuries

In 1861, Sophia Holmes became the first Black woman to be employed by the Treasury Department and by the [Federal government of the United States](195149.md) when [Senator Henry Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Wilson), [James G. Blaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_G._Blaine) and others advocated for her hiring as a janitor under Treasurer [Francis Spinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_E._Spinner). She was paid fifteen dollars per month. In 1862, she prevented a major theft from the department of more than \$200,000 when she came across a box filled with U.S. currency, including a number of thousand-dollar bills, and reported it to Spinner.&#x20;

U.S. president [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) subsequently honored her with a commendation for her actions, and the [federal government](195149.md) rewarded her with an appointment for life as a messenger with its department of Issues.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] Another early woman hired by the department was [Jennie Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennie_Douglas) in 1862.[^cite_note-25] Douglas, who was also recruited by Spinner, is sometimes attributed to having been the first woman to have held an appointed position in the federal government.[^cite_note-26]&#x20;

In response to widespread counterfeiting during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), the [United States Secret Service](58221.md) was founded within the Treasury Department.[^cite_note-27] In 1870 Secret Service headquarters was moved to [New York City](645042.md) but was returned to Washington, D.C., four years later in 1874, where it remains. After the [assassination of William McKinley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_William_McKinley), the Secret Service was tasked with protecting the president, but remained in the Treasury Department.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]&#x20;

### 21st century

#### Departmental reorganization

Further information: [Homeland Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Security_Act)



The [Treasury Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_Building_\(Washington,_D.C.\)) at 1500 [Pennsylvania Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Avenue), [NW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest,_Washington,_D.C.) in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md)

The [U.S. Congress](31756.md) transferred several agencies that had previously been under the aegis of the Treasury Department to other departments as a consequence of the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md). Effective January 24, 2003, the [Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Alcohol,_Tobacco_and_Firearms) (ATF), which had been a bureau of the department since 1972, was extensively reorganized under the provisions of the [Homeland Security Act of 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Security_Act). The [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_agency) functions of ATF, including the regulation of legitimate traffic in [firearms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firearms) and [explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosives), were transferred to the [Department of Justice](52563.md) as the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms, and Explosives (BATFE).[^cite_note-Washington_Post_2003-30] The regulatory and tax collection functions of ATF related to legitimate traffic in alcohol and tobacco remained with the treasury at its new [Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_and_Tobacco_Tax_and_Trade_Bureau) (TTB).[^cite_note-TTBGov_2015-31]&#x20;

Effective March 1, 2003, the [Federal Law Enforcement Training Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Law_Enforcement_Training_Center), the [U.S. Customs Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Customs_Service), and the [U.S. Secret Service](58221.md) were transferred to the newly created [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md).[^cite_note-32] In the 2015 [Center for Effective Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Effective_Government) analysis of the 15 federal agencies that receive the most [Freedom of Information Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Information_Act_\(United_States\)) (FOIA) requests (using 2012 and 2013 data, the most recent years available), Treasury failed to earn a satisfactory overall grade.[^cite_note-33]&#x20;

### Basic functions

The basic functions of the Department of the Treasury include:[^cite_note-37]&#x20;

- Producing all currency and coinage of the U.S.;

- Collecting [taxes, duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax,_tariff_and_trade) and money paid to and due to the U.S.;

- Paying all bills of the U.S.;

- Managing the federal finances;

- Managing government accounts (including the [Treasury General Account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_General_Account)) and the [United States public debt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_public_debt);

- Supervising national banks and [thrift institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_banking);

- Advising on domestic and international financial, [monetary](297032.md), [economic](210522.md), [trade and tax policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_policy) ([fiscal policy](132645.md) being the sum of these);

- Enforcing federal finance and tax laws;

- Investigating and prosecuting [tax evaders](2256795.md);

- Publishing statistical reports

With respect to the estimation of revenues for the [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)), Treasury serves a purpose parallel to that of the [Office of Management and Budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Management_and_Budget) for the estimation of spending for the executive branch, the [Joint Committee on Taxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Congress_Joint_Committee_on_Taxation) for the estimation of revenues for Congress, and the [Congressional Budget Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Budget_Office) for the estimation of spending for Congress.&#x20;

From 1830 until 1901, responsibility for overseeing [weights and measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weights_and_measures) was carried out by the Office of Standard Weights and Measures under the auspices of the Treasury Department.[^cite_note-archives-38] After 1901, that responsibility was assigned to the agency that subsequently became known as the [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology).&#x20;

## Organization

The Department of the Treasury is organized into two major components: the departmental offices and the operating bureaus. The departmental offices are primarily responsible for the formulation of policy and management of the department as a whole, while the operating bureaus carry out the specific operations assigned to the department.&#x20;

| [Internal Revenue Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Revenue_Service) | The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) is the largest of Treasury's bureaus. It is responsible for determining, assessing, and collecting internal revenue in the United States. |
