**Rakhine State** ([/rəˈkaɪn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ad/Rakhine_pronunciation.mp3?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org\&utm_campaign=index\&utm_content=original](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ad/Rakhine_pronunciation.mp3?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org\&utm_campaign=index\&utm_content=original)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*rə-KYN*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_language): ရခိုင်ပြည်နယ်, [MLCTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLC_Transcription_System): *ra.hkuing pranynai*, pronounced [\[jəkʰàiɰ̃ pjìnɛ̀\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Burmese); Rakhine pronunciation: [\[ɹəkʰàiɰ̃ pɹènè\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA)), formerly known as **Arakan State**, is a [first-level administrative division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Myanmar) in [Myanmar](19457.md) (Burma). Situated on the western coast, it is bordered by [Chin State](493366.md) to the north, [Magway Region](493360.md), [Bago Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bago_Region) and [Ayeyarwady Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayeyarwady_Region) to the east, the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) to the west and the [Chattogram Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Division) of [Bangladesh](3454.md) to the northwest. The north–south [Arakan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_Mountains) or [Rakhine Yoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_Yoma) separate Rakhine State from central Myanmar. Off the coast of Rakhine State there are some fairly large islands such as [Ramree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramree_Island), [Cheduba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheduba) and [Myingun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myingun_Island). Rakhine State has an area of 36,762 square kilometres (14,194 sq mi) and its capital is [Sittwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sittwe) (formerly known as Akyab).[^cite_note-6]

As of November 2025, the [Arakan Army](38106209.md) which is the armed wing of the ethnic [Rakhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people), maintains de facto control over approximately 90 percent of the state, including 14 of its 17 townships.[^cite_note-7]

## Etymology

See also: [Rakhine people § Ethnonyms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people#Ethnonyms)

The region is named after the [Rakhine people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people), who form the majority of its population. The state was historically known as **Arakan** in English until the Burmese government adopted the English name **Rakhine** in 1989.[^cite_note-8]

The province was officially designated as a state under Myanmar’s administrative system during the 1974 Constitution referendum. Rakhine State Day is celebrated every year on 15 December.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

## History

Main articles: [Arakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan) and [History of Rakhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Rakhine)



Silver coin of king [Nitichandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitichandra?action=edit\&redlink=1), Arakan. [Brahmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi) legend "NITI" in front, [Shrivatasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrivatasa) symbol on the reverse. 8th century CE.

The history of the region of [Arakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan) (now renamed Rakhine State) can be roughly divided into seven parts. The first four divisions and the periods are based on the location of the centre of power of the main polities in the northern Rakhine region, especially along the Kaladan River. Thus, the history is divided into the [Dhanyawadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhanyawadi), [Waithali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waithali_Kingdom), [Laymro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Rakhine#Le-Mro) and [Mrauk U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mrauk_U). Mrauk U was conquered by the [Konbaung dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konbaung_dynasty) of Burma in 1784–85, after which Rakhine became part of the Konbaung kingdom of Burma. In 1824, the first [Anglo-Burmese war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Burmese_war) erupted and in 1826, Rakhine (alongside [Tanintharyi](15162240.md)) was ceded to the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) as [war reparations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_reparations) by the Burmese. Rakhine thus became part of the [province of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule_in_Burma) in [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_India). In 1948, Burma was given independence and Rakhine became part of the newly independent state.

### Independent kingdom

Beginning in the 400s, the [Pyus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyu_city-states) and Kanyans of Tibeto-Burman people began migrating westward, crossing the [Arakan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_Mountains) and settling in what is now Rakhine State.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-\:20-12] By the 1100s, they had consolidated control of the region, becoming a tributary state of the [Pagan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagan_Kingdom) until the 13th century.[^cite_note-\:21-13][^cite_note-\:20-12] Over time, these Tibeto-Burmans mixed with [Indo-Aryans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_peoples) and formed a distinct cultural identity, eventually becoming the [Rakhine people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_people) (also known as the Arakanese).[^cite_note-\:20-12][^cite_note-\:21-13]

According to Arakanese legend, the first recorded kingdom was founded by the [Sakya clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakya) of Buddha who are ancient Hindus Indo-aryans, centred around the northern town of [Dhanyawadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhanyawadi), arose in the 34th century BCE and lasted until 327 CE. Rakhine documents and inscriptions state that the famed [Mahamuni Buddha image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahamuni_Buddha_Temple) was cast in Dhanyawady in around 554 BCE when the Buddha visited the kingdom. After the fall of Dhanyawadi in the 4th century CE, the centre of power shifted to a new dynasty based in the town of [Waithali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waithali). The [Waithali kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waithali_Kingdom) ruled the regions of Arakan from the middle of the 4th century to 818 CE. The period is seen as the classical period of Arakan culture, architecture and Buddhism, as the Waithali period left behind more archaeological remains than its predecessor. A new dynasty emerged in four towns along the [Lemyo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemyo_River) as Waithali waned in influence, and ushered in the Lemro period, where four principal towns served as successive capitals.



World Map during 1555 CE, Rakhine (Arakan) is seen neighbouring the Toungoo Empire and Bengal

The [Kingdom of Mrauk U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mrauk_U) which was founded in 1429 by [Min Saw Mon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Saw_Mon) was the final independent Arakanese kingdom. It is seen by the Rakhine people as the golden age of their history, as Mrauk U served as a commercially important port and base of power in the Bay of Bengal region and involved in extensive maritime trade. The Kingdom of Mrauk-U went on to conquer [Chittagong](380451.md) with the help of the Portuguese. The country steadily declined from the 18th century onwards after its loss of Chittagong to the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire). Internal instability, rebellion and dethroning of kings were very common. The Portuguese, during the era of their greatness in Asia, gained a temporary establishment in Arakan.[^cite_note-EB1911-14][^cite_note-Phayre1841-15][^cite_note-Subrahmanyam2012-16][^cite_note-LachKley1993-17]

### Burmese annexation

In 1784, a group of Rakhine representatives sought assistance from [Bodawpaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodawpaya), king of the [Konbaung dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konbaung_dynasty), to mediate internal disputes within the [Rakhine royal court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mrauk_U). Responding to their request—and motivated by expansionist ambitions—Bodawpaya dispatched his son, [Thado Minsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thado_Minsaw), the [Prince of the Front Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_the_Front_Palace?action=edit\&redlink=1), to lead a full-scale military expedition into [Arakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan). The invasion force was divided into three land columns and a naval flotilla.

The campaign began on 2 December 1784, and on 2 January 1785, the [Kingdom of Mrauk U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mrauk_U)—weakened by internal strife—fell to the invading forces of the [Konbaung dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konbaung_dynasty). [Maha Thammada Raza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maha_Thammada), the last king of Mrauk U, was captured along with his queen, royal family members, ministers, monks, and artisans, and deported to [Amarapura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarapura).

One of the most significant war trophies was the sacred [Mahamuni Buddha Image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahamuni_Buddha_Temple), which was dismantled and transported to Amarapura, and later enshrined in [Mandalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandalay). The Burmese also seized numerous cultural and religious artifacts, including [gold](12240.md) and [silver](27119.md) regalia, [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze) statues (some originally taken from the [Ayutthaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayutthaya_Kingdom)), weapons, and sacred manuscripts.

Although the Rakhine people initially believed the Burmese would restore peace, they soon recognized the move as an annexation. Uprisings ensued, and many rebels fled to [British-controlled Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency). When Burmese forces crossed the border in pursuit, tensions escalated between Burma and the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_India_Company), setting the stage for future conflict.

With the annexation of Arakan, [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) came into direct territorial contact with [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India). These tensions eventually culminated in the [First Anglo-Burmese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Burmese_War) (1824–1826). During the conflict, British forces captured Arakan and occupied parts of Lower Burma.

In a symbolic reversal of earlier events, the [Presidency armies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_armies) took the great bell from the Mahamuni shrine as war loot. It was awarded to Bhim Singh, a Risaldar in the 2nd Battalion of the [Bengal Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Army), for his bravery. The bell is still housed in a temple in [Nadrai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadrai?action=edit\&redlink=1), near [Kasganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasganj), in present-day [Kasganj district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasganj_district) of [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md), [India](14533.md).

The war concluded with the signing of the [Treaty of Yandabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Yandabo) in 1826, under which Burma ceded [Arakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan) and [Tenasserim](15162240.md) to the British. [Akyab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akyab) (now [Sittwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sittwe)) became the administrative capital of Arakan. Later, Arakan was integrated into the province of [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) under the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), and then into [British Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Burma) after it became a separate [Crown colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_colony). Administratively, Arakan was divided into three districts, following the traditional divisions of the [Mrauk U period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrauk_U_kingdom).

### British rule

Main article: [Arakan Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_Division)

Rakhine was the centre of multiple insurgencies which fought against British rule, notably led by the monks [U Ottama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Ottama) and [U Seinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Seinda?action=edit\&redlink=1). During the Second World War, Rakhine was given autonomy under the [Japanese occupation of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_Burma) and was even granted its own army known as the Arakan Defense Force. The Arakan Defense Force went over to the allies and turned against the Japanese in early 1945.[^cite_note-BaylyHarper2005-18] Rakhine (Arakan) was the site of many battles during the Second World War, most notably the [Arakan Campaign 1942–43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_Campaign_1942%E2%80%9343) and the [Battle of Ramree Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ramree_Island).

### Burmese independence

In 1948, Rakhine became a division within the [Union of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Burma_\(1948%E2%80%931962\)), and the three districts became Arakan Division. From the 1950s, there was a growing movement for [secession](144732.md) and restoration of Arakan independence.

### 2010 onwards (after 2008 constitution)

Since 2010, Rakhine state has had two chief ministers: Hla Maung Tin and Major General Maung Maung Ohn. Hla Maung Tin (January 2011 – 20 June 2014) was an elected Rakhine [State Hluttaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_and_Region_Hluttaws) member representing USDP from [Ann Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Township) in 2010 general election. He resigned from the post after recurrent intense inter-communal conflicts between Muslims and Rakhine ethnic groups in 2012–14.[^cite_note-19] In 2014, he was replaced by Major General Maung Maung Ohn (30 June 2014 – present). Ohn was Deputy Minister for Border Affairs and head of the Rakhine State's Emergency Coordination Center before he was named to become a military-appointed Rakhine State Hluttaw member by Election Commission on 21 June 2014. His appointment as Chief Minister was formalized on 30 June 2014 although Arakan National Party opposed it.[^cite_note-20]

In June 2012, Rakhine State in Myanmar experienced [severe communal violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Rakhine_State_riots) between ethnic Rakhine Buddhists and [Rohingya](1918632.md) Muslims, resulting in at least 88 fatalities, 4,600 homes being burnt and displacement of approximately 100,000 people.[^cite_note-21]

The NLD government refused to share executive power at the state level after the [Arakan National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_National_Party) (ANP) won a majority of votes in Rakhine State during the [2015 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Myanmar_general_election). Arakanese lawmakers frequently complained that their proposals in the state parliament were rejected or ignored.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

In 2017, State Counsellor [Aung San Suu Kyi](2847.md) and the [Tatmadaw](20394.md) reportedly rejected national-level political dialogue in Rakhine State, a mandatory step under the [Nationwide Ceasefire Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationwide_Ceasefire_Agreement) (NCA). This dialogue would have allowed regional stakeholders to present proposals at large-scale public consultations, with the outcomes discussed at the Union Peace Conference, also known as the 21st Century Panglong. In February 2017, the [Arakan Liberation Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_Liberation_Party) (ALP)—one of eight NCA signatories—requested to hold ethnic-based national-level political dialogue in Rakhine State. Aung San Suu Kyi declined, stating that the ALP was not yet ready. Despite submitting three formal requests, the government did not respond. At a Joint Implementation Coordination Meeting (JICM), Suu Kyi again denied the request, citing the sensitive situation involving Rohingya Muslims in Rakhine State.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

On 16 January 2018, thousands of residents in [Mrauk U](2636746.md) staged a protest after officials banned a memorial event commemorating the 233rd anniversary of the fall of the [Mrauk U Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Mrauk_U). Local police opened fire on the crowd, killing seven people and injuring 12. Two speakers at the event—[Aye Maung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aye_Maung), a prominent Rakhine politician, and [Wai Hun Aung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wai_Hun_Aung), a Sittwe-based activist—were subsequently charged. Aye Maung was charged under Section 17(1) of the Unlawful Associations Act and Sections 121 and 505 of the Penal Code, relating to high treason and incitement, while Wai Hun Aung was charged with public mischief. Eight Rakhine youths injured in the protest were also detained and charged under Article 6(1) for allegedly damaging government and public property.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

On 16 October 2020, the [Union Election Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Election_Commission) (UEC) announced that the [2020 Myanmar general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Myanmar_general_election) would not be held in several areas of Rakhine State, including the townships of [Pauktaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauktaw), [Ponnagyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ponnagyun), [Rathedaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rathedaung), [Buthidaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buthidaung), [Maung Daw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maung_Daw), [Kyauktaw](15160309.md), [Minbya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minbya), [Myebon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myebon), and [Mrauk U](2636746.md); along with multiple quarters and village tracts in [Kyaukphyu Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyaukphyu_Township), [Ann Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Township), [Sittwe Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sittwe_Township), and [Toungup Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toungup_Township). The UEC stated that free and fair elections could not be held due to ongoing conflict and instability.[^cite_note-29]

With the exception of Toungup Township, ethnic Rakhine parties have historically been dominant in these areas. The [Rakhine Nationalities Development Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_Nationalities_Development_Party) (RNDP) and [Arakan National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_National_Party) (ANP) won the majority of seats in these townships during the 2010 and 2015 general elections.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

### 2020–present

Main articles: [Rakhine offensive (2023–present)](76441089.md) and [Operation 1027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_1027)

In November 2020, the Myanmar military and the Arakan Army (AA) reached an informal ceasefire, leading to a temporary reduction in hostilities in Rakhine State. However, after the military coup in February 2021, clashes resumed in the region.[^cite_note-\:3-32]

Following the military coup in February 2021, Rakhine State experienced intensified armed conflicts, particularly between the [Myanmar Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_Armed_Forces) (MAF) and the Arakan Army (AA). By September 2021, the AA and its political wing, the [United League of Arakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_League_of_Arakan) (ULA), had gained control over roughly 75% of Rakhine’s townships.[^cite_note-\:3-32]

In early 2024, the battle for the border town of [Maungdaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maungdaw) resulted in heavy fighting, leading to numerous civilian casualties and displacements. Reports indicate that during this period, civilians, including the Rohingya population, were subjected to coordinated killings, large-scale arson attacks, and shelling. Notably, on August 5, 2024, an attack in Maungdaw targeted Rohingya civilians attempting to flee by boat, resulting in numerous deaths.[^cite_note-33]

On 27 October 2023, [Operation 1027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_1027) was launched by the [Three Brotherhood Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Brotherhood_Alliance), consisting of the Arakan Army (AA), the [Ta'ang National Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta'ang_National_Liberation_Army) (TNLA), and the [Myanmar National Democratic Alliance Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_National_Democratic_Alliance_Army) (MNDAA). The offensive led to the capture of over 130 military outposts and strategic bases in northern [Shan State](493376.md), including the key border town of [Laukkai](24155033.md). Reports indicated significant losses for the Myanmar military, with hundreds of soldiers killed and over 4,000 troops, including senior officers, surrendering.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

By February 2025, a [CSIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Strategic_and_International_Studies) report indicated that the Arakan Army had begun establishing local governments in the Rakhine townships under its control. However, little is known about their governance structure or the inclusion of non-Arakan ethnic groups. It remains unclear whether the AA will halt military operations after securing its claimed territory, as its offensives in [Paletwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paletwa), [Magway](493360.md), and other areas suggest broader territorial ambitions. The group's stance on Myanmar’s political future is also uncertain, while it previously sought autonomy under the 2008 constitution like the [United Wa State Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Wa_State_Army), it may now pursue full statehood or independence.[^cite_note-36]

On 24 February 2026, Myanmar military aircraft dropped two bombs on a market in Yoe Ngu village, killing 17 and injuring 15. The village is home to approximately 850 [internally displaced people](427408.md) (IDPs) with a total population of about 2,500. In July, a second aerial attack occurred about one mile from the village at a camp of IDPs, resulting in one injury.[^cite_note-\:4-37] A defected Myanmar military Major told The Diplomat the day after the February airstrikes that attacks on the civilian population would increase in Rakhine’s liberated region, citing the military's mission to defend the three townships of Sittwe, Kyaukphyu, and Manaung that are still under its control. The other 14 townships in the state are effectively governed by the Arakan Army.[^cite_note-\:4-37] A United Nations report stated that airstrikes were the leading cause of civilian casualties in Myanmar in 2025, killing at least 982 people, including 232 children.[^cite_note-38]

### Religion



The [Bya La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawarupa) statue in Sittwe

According to the [State Sangha Maha Nayaka Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Sangha_Maha_Nayaka_Committee)'s 2016 statistics, 12,943 Buddhist monks were registered in Rakhine State, comprising 2.4% of Myanmar's total [Sangha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangha) membership, which includes both novice [samanera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samanera) and fully-ordained bhikkhu.[^cite_note-mahana-45] The majority of monks belong to the [Thudhamma Nikaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thudhamma_Nikaya) (88.9%), followed by [Shwegyin Nikaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shwegyin_Nikaya) (3.9%), with the remainder of monks belonging to other small [monastic orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nik%C4%81ya).[^cite_note-mahana-45] 534 [thilashin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thilashin) were registered in Rakhine State, comprising 0.9% of Myanmar's total thilashin community.[^cite_note-mahana-45]

The Rakhine are traditionally [Theravada Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theravada). As per the 1983 Census, 98.63% of the Rakhine in Rakhine State were Buddhist and another 1.19% were Muslim who are recognized separately as [Kamein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamein). The Chin were the 3rd largest ethnic group, contributing 4% of the population in the 1983 Census. At that time, out of the 64,404 Chin people in Rakhine, 55.76% were Buddhist and 33.79% were Animist.[^cite_note-dop.gov.mm-46] Muslims constituted more than 80–96% of the population near the border with [Bangladesh](3454.md) and the coastal areas. As per the 1983 Census, 99.82% of the Rohingya, 99.24% of the "Other foreigners", 89.20% of the "Mixed races", 85.50% of the Indians and 67.51% of the "Pakistanis" in the state were Muslims.[^cite_note-dop.gov.mm-46]

## Government

### Executive

The [Rakhine State Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_State_Government) is the government of Rakhine State and is responsible for the state's administration and governance. Until 2026, the state was governed and administered by the [State Administration Council](66589972.md) (SAC), following the [2021 Myanmar coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Myanmar_coup).

### Legislative

The [Rakhine State Hluttaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhine_State_Hluttaw) is the legislature of Rakhine State, responsible for local governance, passing state laws, approving localized budgets, and overseeing the state cabinet. The latest election for the Hluttaw was the [2025-2026 general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025-26_Myanmar_general_election), which has been deemed a "sham" by many countries. The Hluttaw has 47 seats and is [unicameral](204304.md).

## Transport

Few roads cross the [Arakan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arakan_Mountains) from central Burma to Rakhine State. The three highways that do are the [Ann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann,_Burma) to [Munbra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munbra) ([Minbya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minbya) in Burmese pronunciation) road in central Rakhine,[^cite_note-RS-Map-48] the [Toungup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toungup) to [Pamtaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamtaung?action=edit\&redlink=1) road in south central Rakhine,[^cite_note-RS-Map-48] and the [Gwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwa,_Burma) to [Ngathaingchaung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngathaingchaung) road in far southern Rakhine.[^cite_note-RS-Map-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] Air travel still is the usual mode of travel from [Yangon](57404.md) and [Mandalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandalay) to Sittwe and [Ngapali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngapali), the popular beach resort. Only in 1996 was a highway from Sittwe to the mainland constructed. The state still does not have a rail line (although [Myanmar Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_Railways) has announced a 480-km rail extension to Sittwe from Pathein via Ponnagyun-Kyauttaw-Mrauk U-Minbya-Ann).[^cite_note-rail-51]

## Economy

Rakhine is one of the poorest states in Myanmar.[^cite_note-veconomist-53] Over 69% of the population live in poverty.[^cite_note-54]

Rice is the main crop in the region, occupying around 85% of the total agricultural land. [Coconut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut) and [nipa palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipa_palm) plantations are also important. Fishing is a major industry, with most of the catch transported to [Yangon](57404.md), but some is also exported. Wood products such as timber, [bamboo](39029.md) and fuel wood are extracted from the mountains. Small amounts of inferior-grade [crude oil](23195.md) are produced from primitive, shallow, hand-dug wells, but there is yet unexplored potential for petroleum and natural gas production.

Tourism is slowly being developed. The ruins of the ancient royal town [Mrauk U](2636746.md) and the beach resorts of [Ngapali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngapali_Beach) are the major attractions for foreign visitors, but facilities are still primitive, and the transportation infrastructure is still rudimentary.



[Mrauk U](2636746.md)&#x20;

The region suffers from chronic power shortages. In 2009, the electricity consumption of a state of 3 million people was 30 [MW](21347693.md), or 1.8% of the country's total generation capacity.[^cite_note-xn-55] In December 2009, the military government added three more hydropower plants, Saidin, Thahtay Chaung and [Laymromyit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laymro), at a cost of over US\$800 million. The three plants together can produce 687 megawatts, with surplus electricity distributed to other states and divisions.[^cite_note-xn-55]



[Temple of 90,000 Buddhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koe-thaung_Temple) in Mrauk U of Rakhine State

Additionally, as of November 2024, reports indicate that over 2 million people in Rakhine State are at risk of famine due to reduced food production and ongoing conflicts. Without urgent action, it is predicted that 95% of the population will regress into survival mode.[^cite_note-56]

## Global interests

### China

Rakhine State hosts several major [Belt and Road Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_and_Road_Initiative) (BRI) projects, including the [Kyaukphyu deep-sea port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyaukphyu_Deep-Sea_Port) and oil and gas pipelines. The region serves as an important land and maritime route which allows China access to the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) and reducing its dependence on the [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca).[^cite_note-bng-57][^cite_note-nla-58][^cite_note-baffa-59]

Myanmar also acts as a [buffer state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffer_state) for China in managing Western and Indian influence in [Southeast Asia](28741.md). Despite international criticism, it is known that China has maintained close relations with Myanmar's military while also engaging with major ethnic armed organizations (EAOs).[^cite_note-\:2-60][^cite_note-61]

### India

India sees Myanmar as an important partner under its ["Act East" policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_East_policy), which aims to improve connectivity and strengthen India's influence in Southeast Asia. The nation has invested in projects such as the [Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Transport Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaladan_Multi-Modal_Transit_Transport_Project) and the [India-Myanmar-Thailand Trilateral Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India-Myanmar-Thailand_Trilateral_Highway).[^cite_note-\:0-62][^cite_note-update3-63]



Route map of the Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Transport Project

Like China, India also maintains flexible diplomacy by engaging with both Myanmar’s military government and [EAOs](48437766.md) operating near its borders, including the AA.[^cite_note-\:1-64]

### Others

The [United States](3434750.md) and [European countries](9317.md) has stated to support Rakhine state's political aspirations if it aligns with the broader goals of [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) and human rights.[^cite_note-\:1-64]

[Bangladesh](3454.md), which shares a border with Rakhine State, has primarily shown concerns about the Rohingya refugee movements. The country hosts over more than one million [Rohingya refugees](53544313.md) who fled the state.[^cite_note-\:2-60]

## Health care

The general state of [health care in Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care_in_Myanmar) is poor. The military government spends anywhere from 0.5% to 3% of the country's GDP on health care, consistently ranking among the lowest in the world.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] Although health care is nominally free, in reality, patients have to pay for medicine and treatment, even in public clinics and hospitals. Public hospitals lack many of the basic facilities and equipment. The entire Rakhine State has fewer hospital beds than the [Yangon General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangon_General_Hospital).
