A [constitutional referendum](51582.md) concerning the reform of the judicial system was held in Italy on 22 and 23 March 2026. Its purpose was to revise Title II and Title IV of Part II of the [Italian constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_constitution). It was the fifth constitutional referendum in the [history of the Italian Republic](1161302.md).

Voters were asked whether they approved a [constitutional law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_law), often called the "Nordio Reform" (*Riforma Nordio*) after Minister of Justice [Carlo Nordio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Nordio), that would amend the Italian Constitution in various aspects, most notably by proposing the constitutional separation of career paths between [judges](44719.md) and [public prosecutors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_prosecutor), the splitting of the [High Council of the Judiciary](52834029.md) (CSM) into two distinct bodies, and the selection of CSM members by [sortition](19288053.md) rather than traditional election, as well as the establishment of a High Disciplinary Court to oversee disciplinary proceedings.

The government-initiated constitutional bill was approved by the [Senate of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Republic_\(Italy\)) on 30 October 2025. Since it did not reach the qualified two-thirds majority in each chamber of the [Italian Parliament](1417859.md) during the parliamentary approval process, pursuant to Article 138 of the Italian Constitution, the necessary signatures were soon collected to request a confirmatory constitutional referendum.

In a higher-than-expected turnout and in contrast to late polls showing a close election, after earlier polls had initially shown a significant advantage for those in favour, voters rejected the [constitutional reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_reform) 53.2% to 46.8%. This was seen as a significant blow to the [Meloni government](72067724.md). A [quorum](237014.md) was not required for the referendum to be valid, and the Nordio law submitted to referendum would have been promulgated if confirmed by a majority of valid votes.

## Background

### Details of the reform



[Carlo Nordio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Nordio), the [Minister of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Justice_\(Italy\)) after whom the reform was named

The constitutional law, published on 30 October 2025 in the General Series No. 253 of the *Official Gazette* (*[Gazzetta Ufficiale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazzetta_Ufficiale)*), pursuant to Article 138 of the Constitution, was subject to a constitutional referendum as it was approved without the required two-thirds majority;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOpenParlamento,_16_September_2025Il_portale_delle_Prefetture,_19_January_2026-1] on 16 July 2025, the Senate of the Republic had approved the eight articles of the reform.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECamere_Penali,_16_July_2025-2] The constitutional reform, promoted by the government of [Giorgia Meloni](29228976.md), would have introduced structural amendments to Articles 87, 102, 104, 105, 106, 107, and 110 of the Constitution of the Italian Republic, with the complete replacement of Articles 104 and 105, and would have required the constitutional separation of the careers of judicial magistrates (judges) and prosecutorial magistrates (public prosecutors), as well as the division of the CSM (*Consiglio Superiore della Magistratura*), the self-governing body of the Italian judiciary, into a Judicial CSM and a Prosecutorial CSM, each responsible for the career management of judicial and prosecutorial magistrates respectively.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Gazzetta_Ufficiale'',_30_October_2025Dipartimento_per_il_programma_di_Governo,_30_October_2025Logroscino2026-3]

The reform would have replaced the elective system for selecting CSM members with a random selection mechanism for the members of the two councils according to two different procedures: the "lay members" (one third of the total) would have been drawn by lot from a list of full university professors in legal disciplines and lawyers with at least fifteen years of professional practice, compiled by the Italian Parliament in joint session through election; the judicial members (two thirds of the total) would have been drawn by lot from among judicial magistrates and prosecutorial magistrates. A subsequent ordinary law would have been entrusted with determining the number of members of the two Councils and the detailed procedures for the drawing of lots.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPaolini2025Logroscino2026-4]

The reform would have established the High Disciplinary Court (*Alta Corte Disciplinare*), which would have assumed disciplinary jurisdiction over ordinary magistrates, removing this function from the disciplinary section of the CSM that exercises it. The High Disciplinary Court was to be composed of fifteen members: three appointed by the [president of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Italy) from among full university professors in legal disciplines and lawyers with at least twenty years of professional practice; three drawn by lot from a list of individuals with the same qualifications compiled by Parliament in joint session through election; six judicial magistrates and three prosecutorial magistrates drawn by lot from among members of the respective categories with at least twenty years of judicial service who perform or have performed functions at the [Supreme Court of Cassation](3588041.md) level. The High Disciplinary Court would have elected its president from among the judges appointed by the president of the Republic or drawn from the list compiled by Parliament. Appeals against decisions of the High Disciplinary Court, including on the merits, would have been admissible solely before the same High Disciplinary Court. A subsequent ordinary law would have been tasked with defining disciplinary offenses and corresponding sanctions, specifying the composition of the panels, regulating the disciplinary procedure, and establishing the rules necessary for the functioning of the High Disciplinary Court.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECampigli2025-5]

Ballot paper of the referendum

## Debate

### Criticism

> "This reorganisation of the office and role of the public prosecutor, partly planned for some time, would impact the independence of the entire judicial system, as the judge would be called upon to rule only on cases selected by the government. Those who rely solely on the text of the constitutional reform as a guarantee against the risks denounced by the 'Right to Say No' committee, without considering the aforementioned context, are reminiscent of a character from Manzoni's novel, Don Ferrante, who died of the plague, a disease he didn't believe in because it was incompatible with Aristotelian principles."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENappi2026-13]

— Aniello Nappi

The reform and the referendum gave rise to a debate.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Altalex'',_14_January_2026''Altalex'',_28_January_2026Occhipinti2026-14] Critics of the reform generally argued that it did not tackle the real issues of Italian justice,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Il_Sole_24_Ore'',_24_January_2026''Il_Fatto_Quotidiano'',_30_January_2026-15] and would not make justice more efficient or just as claimed by some supporters,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDemocratic_Party,_24_January_2026-16] and that it was not democratic as it would eliminate the right of [magistrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magistrate) to freely elect their representatives in the CSM.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPower_to_the_People,_17_November_2025-17] They further argued that the public prosecutor would become a "super policeman" rather than an impartial one,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFive_Star_Movement,_20_January_2026Travaglio_2026_(second_part)''Il_Fatto_Quotidiano'',_7_February_2026-18] who must seek the judicial truth and [exculpatory evidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exculpatory_evidence) (Article 358 of the [Italian Code of Criminal Procedure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Code_of_Criminal_Procedure)),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEValentinotti2025-19] that the separation of careers was irrelevant as they would account for less than one percent of all magistrates and that the [Cartabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Cartabia) Reform already applied a separation of career, and that it would represent the end of the principle that "the law is the same for everyone" (*la legge è uguale per tutti*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Il_Fatto_Quotidiano'',_11_January_2026-20]

Critics, including among others [Marco Travaglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Travaglio), negatively compared the proposed reform to the United States justice system, arguing that the prosecutor, who is not "the prosecution's lawyer" but "the citizen's first defender" (a magistrate "with the obligation to seek the truth, just like a judge"), and that by transforming him into a separate figure, the new type of prosecutor under the reform "will inevitably be trained to be a policeman, that is, to accuse without considering whether someone is innocent or guilty", and that "our trial system risks becoming like that in the US, where the jury decides not who is guilty and who is innocent, but who has the best lawyer."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPower_to_the_People,_17_November_2025''Il_Fatto_Quotidiano'',_7_February_2026-21] Prominent judge and jurist [Gustavo Zagrebelsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavo_Zagrebelsky) described the reform as "a retaliation by certain politicians against certain judiciaries to shift the constitutional balance in favour of the former's impunity and to the detriment of the latter's autonomy and independence".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Il_Fatto_Quotidiano'',_30_January_2026-22]

Another argument, represented by the anti-mafia magistrate [Raffaele Cantone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffaele_Cantone), was that judges were at the same time accused of lacking independence from prosecutors and thus supporting the prosecution's arguments, and of excessive [garantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garantism_\(legal_theory\)) when rejecting the prosecution, for example when releasing suspects.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOttoni2026-23] The latter criticism was shared by Gian Domenico Caiazza (chairman of a pro-reform committee) in reference to [Matteo Salvini](1767165.md)'s public messaging in the aftermath of a clash between a minority of violent protesters and the police in Turin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarlino2026-24] Cantone also observed that criminal justice is on the one hand accused of lacking effectiveness, blaming magistrates for direct consequences for public safety, and on the other hand is accused of being too "invasive" with respect to politics.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOttoni2026-23] 2021 data showed that first-instance acquittals were equal to 50%, a percentage that rises to 69.7% for trials held following opposition to a criminal conviction by the prosecutor, with critics suggesting that sharing the same order does not impact or condition judicial decisions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEValentinotti2025-19]

Critics, even among those in favour of the career separation,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoato2026-25] cited the lack of discussion between the government and the opposition in writing the reform, and those who voted against the reform in Parliament, such as [Pier Ferdinando Casini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_Ferdinando_Casini), described it as "useless".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''L'Eco_del_Sannio'',_22_July_2025-26] Others also argued that the career separation was bad because the prosecutors should be trained as judges as in the Italian system the goal of both prosecutors and judges is to reach the judicial truth, with the prosecutors free to request the acquittal of the defendant; data showed that around 50% of the time judges acquit defendants in cases where the prosecutor had requested a trial.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOttoni2026-23] Additionally, the magistrate would no longer be able to appeal to the Supreme Court of Cassation against a decision by the High Disciplinary Court and would not be granted the three-degrees of judgement; instead the magistrate could only appeal the decision to the same High Disciplinary Court, albeit it would not be composed of the same judicial panel.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETravaglio_2026_(first_part)''Il_Fatto_Quotidiano'',_31_January_2026-27] Finally, critics cited the high cost of the reform when the same amount of spending could be used to hire more magistrates and digitalise the system,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFive_Star_Movement,_20_January_2026-28] that no country use sortition and that it would be "a genuine humiliation of the democratic rules and constitutional principles on active and passive suffrage", citing the board of the Union of the Italian Criminal Chambers (UCPI) in 2019 but that was in favour of the 2025 reform,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Altalex'',_14_January_2026-29] and also observed that there were unclear aspects due to the fact that part of the reform would require ordinary laws for its functioning, in particular the organisation of the new bodies.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOttoni2026-23]

Critics did not see the Nordio Reform as a justice reform but as a magistrature reform that the government was seeking to control politically,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeft_Greens_Alliance,_16_January_2026-30] and it would lead towards [illiberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illiberalism) and [authoritarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authoritarianism), citing the abolition of the crime of [abuse of office](4308572.md), the weakening of the [Court of Audit](52876273.md) and the crime of [influence peddling](630570.md), a *de facto* [penal populism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penal_populism) (*giustizialismo*) through various repressive decrees and laws establishing new crimes under the guise of "[law and order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_and_order_\(politics\))" and security decrees, and the direct election of the prime minister (*premierato*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEItalian_Left,_28_January_2026-31] Critics also argued that the separation of career was part of a reform project that marked the [history of the Italian Republic](1161302.md) between the mid-1960s and the early 1980s, having already been envisaged in the "Democratic Rebirth Plan" drawn up by [Licio Gelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licio_Gelli) and [Propaganda Due](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_Due) (P2), a criminal Masonic lodge with [far-right](221220.md) and [neo-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-fascist) links involved through Gelli as the instigator of the [Bologna station massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_station_massacre), and that in turn was consubstantial with the various attempts at subversion, such as the [Borghese coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borghese_coup), the [Piano Solo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Solo), and the "[strategy of tension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategy_of_tension)".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETanisi2024''Il_Fatto_Quotidiano'',_20_November_2025-32] Gelli's son, Maurizio Gelli, said that Meloni's government was implementing many of his father's ideas, including the separation of careers, [psycho-aptitude tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psycho-aptitude_tests) for magistrates, and [presidentialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidentialism).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

### Response

> "It's shameful to forget that the idea of separating careers originated within the history of the Italian left ... Giuliano Vassalli, in presenting the 1988 accusatory reform of the code to the foreign press, explained that it was a wonderful reform, but that without revising the Constitution and the judicial system by dividing careers, it would be nullified. Unfortunately, he was a good prophet ... the Bicameral Commission chaired by Massimo D'Alema placed great emphasis on separating careers, and the Democratic Party, during Maurizio Martina's leadership, also shared the same opinion."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECarlino2026-24]

— Gian Domenico Caiazza

Supporters generally responded that the career separation is typical of liberal democracy, while the unitary system is typical of authoritarian regimes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlicino2025Camere_Penali,_5_February_2026-36] They also argued that if *de facto* there already is a career separation with the Cartabia Reform, there should be no issue in making it *de jure*, and observed that the text of the reform would maintain the same wording about the autonomy and independence of the magistrature, that the public prosecutors would not be put under the executive and would still be bound by Article 358 of the Code of Criminal Procedure requiring the prosecutor to also seek evidence in favour of the defendant.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECamere_Penali,_5_February_2026-37] Another line of argument was that the reform would make the judiciary more efficient and just, by providing a truly impartial judge and that the prosecutor would also be more impartial, and that by reducing political factionalism within the CSM, the reform would make the magistrature more independent.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlicino2025-38] The argument was that judges are not truly independent because they are judged by a CSM with a strong presence of investigating magistrates (prosecutors), also citing 5,933 [wrongful detentions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrongful_detention) from 2017 to 2024 that costed the state €254 million in compensation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlicino2025-38]

Supporters observed that the reform was different by the ones proposed by Gelli, former Prime Minister and [Italian Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Socialist_Party) (PSI) leader [Bettino Craxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bettino_Craxi), and [Silvio Berlusconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Berlusconi),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECamere_Penali,_5_February_2026-37] and that the career separation was proposed by the centre-left coalition in the 1990s under [Massimo D'Alema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimo_D'Alema) and in the late 2010s during the secretary of [Maurizio Martina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurizio_Martina), and that it would be in line with the 1988 reform of the Code of Criminal Procedure, which moved the Italian system from a traditionally [inquisitorial system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inquisitorial_system) to a more adversial or [accusatorial system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accusatorial_system) and bore the name of the PSI jurist [Giuliano Vassalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuliano_Vassalli).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESacchi2026Carlino2026-39] Supporters of the reform criticised their opponents for engaging in what they saw as [fearmongering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fearmongering) by postulating that the reform would lead towards illiberalism and authoritarianism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPuente2026-40] They cited as evidence left-wing individuals who came out in support of the reform and publicly expressed their favourable vote in the referendum.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESacchi2026-41] Supporters of the reforms lamented that "blacklists" circulated in [WhatsApp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhatsApp) chats among magistrates opposed to the reform, criticising [constitutionalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionalists) who came in favour of the reform.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoezi2026Alicino2026-42]

Supporters, particularly those of the [centre-right coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_coalition_\(Italy\)) and within the Meloni government, often claim a political bias or argue that the magistrature is politicised,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERomanelli2025-43] an allegation dating back to the [Berlusconi era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlusconi_era), where he often adopted [red-baiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-baiting) rhetoric by attacking magistrates as "communists" or with the epithet "red robes" (*toghe rosse*), the latter an expression still used by the Italian right.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlicino2025-38] In an official statement on 21 December 2024, in response to the sentences handed down by judges against senators [Matteo Renzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Renzi) and Salvini, the UCPI stated that there had been a "political use of the judicial system by the judiciary", that such use had "subversive overtones", and therefore hoped to "put an end to this trend through a comprehensive constitutional reform of the judiciary".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERomanelli2025-43] In January 2026, the "Pannella Sciascia Tortora Committee", promoted by the [Transnational Radical Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnational_Radical_Party) (PRT) to support the referendum on the separation of judicial careers, was reported to have filed a complaint against the leaders of the "Right to Say No" committee, accusing them of the misdemeanor offense under Article 656 of the Criminal Code ("publication or dissemination of false, exaggerated, or biased news, capable of disturbing public order") for promoting the dissemination of a propaganda poster reading: "Would you like judges who depend on politicians? No. With the Nordio law, politicians want to control the decisions of magistrates. VOTE NO IN THE REFERENDUM."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENappi2026-13]

### Concerns about the reform's effects

The view that the government sought to politically control the magistrature was notably held by historian [Alessandro Barbero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Barbero), who was criticised by [fact-checkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fact-checkers) for a number of misleading claims; while they found that he presented a fairly accurate summary of the Italian justice system, they found that the text of the reform did not reflect the issues presented by Barbero.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Pagella_Politica'',_19_January_2026Puente2026Bechis2026-44] In particular, they found that Barbero's criticism was essentially valid for past proposed judicial reforms such as that of Berlusconi but did not fit the Nordio reform.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Pagella_Politica'',_19_January_2026Bechis2026-45] In turn, there was a controversy about censorship as his video was removed by [Facebook](7529378.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBechis2026-46] Fact-checkers denied that it was a censorship, stated that they verify the facts, not the opinions, and clarified that while Barbero's criticism was not factual, his view that the reform risks leading towards illiberalism or authoritarianism (through the political control of the magistrature) represented a legitimate political opinion that cannot be presently verified as it was a prediction about the reform's effects, and that the video should not have been removed by Facebook.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Pagella_Politica'',_19_January_2026Bechis2026Fontana2026-47]

Others rejected the textualist argument adopted by fact-checkers and argued that context was important, and the perceived fearmongering was in fact legitimate or even justified, citing the statements by the government ministers, who appeared to suggest that the reason of the reform was to better control the magistrature, which it accused of an alleged political bias and interference, after courts rejected several of the government's key proposals.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENappi2026-13] On 30 October 2025, Meloni, after denouncing "yet another act of invasion of jurisdiction over the decisions of the government and Parliament", stated that "the constitutional reform of the justice system and the reform of the Court of Auditors, soon to be approved by the Senate, are the most appropriate response to this intrusiveness, which will not halt the government's action."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENappi2026-13] In a speech to the National Council of Lawyers, [Alfredo Mantovano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Mantovano) denounced "the courts' tendency to deny regulatory space to the legislature", which he said eroded the scope for direct expression of popular sovereignty, adding: "Today, there is a block on expulsions thanks to judicial decisions, there is a block on security, on industrial policy that seeks to achieve certain objectives—think of the Ilva steelworks—thanks to judicial decisions. This is an invasion of the field that must be addressed."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENappi2026-13] On 22 November 2025, Salvini described the decision by the Juvenile Court of L'Aquila to remove six- and eight-year-old children from their parents in the "family in the woods" case as a "kidnapping".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENappi2026-13] Nordio himself stated that the phenomenon of the alleged invasion of judicial power was not unique to Italy but also concerned other countries.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENappi2026-13] In January 2026, Meloni made a statement on the [April 2026 Hungarian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2026_Hungarian_parliamentary_election), calling on Hungarian voters to confirm the government of [Viktor Orbán](750582.md); according to a [European Union](9317.md) report, Orbán's government severely reduced the independence of judges, so much so that the [European Commission](9974.md) decided to suspend funds destined for Hungary due to violations of the [rule of law](25166191.md), including those related to the [independence of the judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_the_judiciary).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENappi2026-13]

### Carlo Nordio's statements

In support of their arguments, critics of the reform cited statements by Nordio himself that they say are a "confession" of the real intention behind the reform and that justifies their concerns. Cited statements by Nordio included that the reform would be useful for the opposition if they were to return to government ("It amazes me that an intelligent person like Elly Schlein doesn't understand that this reform would also benefit them, if they were to take office") and that just because Gelli supported the career separation, this did not entail that the reform was automatically bad ("I don't know P2's plan. I can say that if Mr. Licio Gelli's interpretation, or rather his opinion, was correct, there's no reason why it shouldn't be followed because he said so. Truths don't depend on who proclaims them, but on the objectivity they represent ... If Gelli said that Jesus died on the cross, that doesn't mean we should say he died of pneumonia, or that a broken clock tells the right time twice a day. Even if Gelli stumbled over the truth, that doesn't mean the truth is no longer the truth"), which was described as a gaffe.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Il_Fatto_Quotidiano'',_3_November_2025''Il_Fatto_Quotidiano'',_18_November_2025Di_ChioRomano2025-48] In February 2026, Nordio stated that the reform "will not and must not have political effects" and that if those in favour of the reform were to win the referendum, there would be a dialogue with the magistrature.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Open'',_6_February_2026-49]

On 9 March 2026, the Ministry of Justice chief of staff Giusi Bartolozzi controversially called on voters to vote in favour of the reform "so we can get rid of the judiciary, which is \[like] firing squads", and added that "I'm running away... I have an ongoing investigation. I'm running away from this country", if the reform was rejected,[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] receiving criticism from the opposition,[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMilano2026b-54] as well as the government; however, unlike the opposition, it refused to call for her resignation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESannino2026b-55][^cite_note-56] Nordio apologised and later stated that Bartolozzi had already clarified that she was referring to "a small portion of politicised judges" and that she would "certainly have no difficulty apologising for words that I am sure don't reflect her thoughts and the esteem she has for the judiciary, of which, among other things, she herself is a part."[^cite_note-57] Bartolazzi also explained that the comparison with firing squads was because it "alluded to the state of absolute prostration one finds oneself in when involved in a trial, especially if innocent. Effects that no acquittal can erase."[^cite_note-58]

Simonetta Matone, a former magistrate and member of the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_\(Italy\)), criticised Nordio for causing the reform to lose support with "crazy statements", and said that he confused "what can be said in a living room with what can be said publicly". She added: "We all think the things he said, but they're things we can't say publicly because we've given the green light to a resurgence of the No front."[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] In particular, she criticised the comparison made by Nordio between the CSM and "a para-mafia system",[^cite_note-62] a statement he made a few days earlier.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

### Sensationalism and misleading claims

Both sides of the debate engaged in some form of sensationalism or made misleading claims. Notably, Naples public prosecutor [Nicola Gratteri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicola_Gratteri) made misleading claims about anti-mafia magistrates [Paolo Borsellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Borsellino) and [Giovanni Falcone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Falcone), citing quotes by Borsellino and Falcone in interviews that never took place and again citing a real quote by Falcone expressing opposition to the career separation but decontexualising it.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPuente2025Cigna2025Milano2025-66] Among the first to express doubts about the veridicity of the quotes was the journalist Damiano Aliprandi, who wrote for *[Il Dubbio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Dubbio)* an article about it, and in turn *La Notizia*, which had published an article in July 2025 reporting the quotes, issued a retraction.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPuente2025-67] In response, *[Il Foglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Foglio)* journalist Luciano Capone said that Gratteri, whom he described as a "frontman of the 'No' vote in the referendum", had read "a fake interview with Giovanni Falcone against career separation: one of those memes circulating on Facebook and WhatsApp, sometimes on *Il Fatto*", and joked: "If he verifies the evidence like this as a prosecutor, we're screwed."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPuente2025-67] As observed by Capone and as found by fact-checkers, the fake quotes dated back to the summer of 2025 and appeared in [Facebook groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook_groups) and were shared as meme.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPuente2025-67]

On 13 November 2025, *[Il Fatto Quotidiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Fatto_Quotidiano)* editor-in-chief Marco Travaglio issued a retraction, stating: "When we're wrong, unlike the hoaxers who deliberately tell twenty lies a day, we apologise to our readers. And we do so today for having accepted at face value two incorrect quotes from Falcone and Borsellino, taken from written and online publications."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPuente2025-67] Former Turin prosecutor Armando Spataro shared Gratteri's views, stating that Falcone himself underwent a change of career by switching from judge to prosecutor.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPipitone2025-68] Fact-checkers observed that it was misleading to claim that Borsellino and Falcone were either for or against the career separation as the judicial system they worked in was significantly different, that Falcone did not discuss the career separation as a magistrate, and that he was a member of a [political faction](40362512.md) within the CSM, narrowly losing the presidency by 13 votes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBechis2025-69] Among the other misleading claims was that the reform would avert cases of [miscarriage of justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage_of_justice) or so that as stated by Meloni from the stage of [Atreju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atreju), calling for voting in favour of the justice reform, "there never will be another shameful case" like the [Garlasco case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlasco_case), described by fact-checkers as "one of the most controversial legal cases of the last twenty years".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDardana2026-70]

## Campaign

Unlike other types that are abrogative, the justice reform referendum was confirmatory, meaning that the law would come into force only if the [popular vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_vote) was in favour, and thus there was no campaign in favour of abstention as parties ran a campaign to [get out the vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_out_the_vote).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUnion_of_the_Centre,_18_January_2026''Il_Fatto_Quotidiano'',_31_January_2026-71]

### Support

Although the majority centre-right coalition government in Parliament generally supported the reform, the governing parties did not organise a unified effort in favour of the reform. Meloni's [Brothers of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brothers_of_Italy) (FdI) notably avoided using its party symbol in its promotional materials, unlike the [League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lega_\(political_party\)) (Lega) and [Forza Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forza_Italia_\(2013\)) (FI), which maintained their traditional branding.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Il_Foglio'',_21_January_2026-72] Lega launched the campaign with the slogan "I Vote Yes – The Reform that Brings Justice".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELega_Lombarda_Salvini,_27_January_2026-73] [Us Moderates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Us_Moderates) (NM) and the [Associative Movement of Italians Abroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associative_Movement_of_Italians_Abroad) (MAIE) established the Committee for the Yes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAgenzia_Nova,_29_November_2025-74] FI leaders, such as Joseph William Catalano in Forlì, campaigned in honour of Berlusconi, stating that justice reform "precisely because it affects the citizen's identity and freedom, was for Silvio Berlusconi, and it is for us today, the reform of reforms" and that it would "finally realise Silvio Berlusconi's great dream", and called for an open debate "without ideological barriers".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''ForlìToday'',_26_January_2026-75]

The [Liberal Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democratic_Party_\(Italy\)) (PLD) of [Luigi Marattin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Marattin), a former member of the PD and [Italia Viva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italia_Viva) (IV), became the first political party to formally establish a pro-reform committee named "Justice Yes".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiriami2025-76] [Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_\(Italian_political_party\)) party leader [Carlo Calenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Calenda) also came out in support, describing it as a good reform.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdnkronos,_15_January_2026-77] Other parties expressed their favourable vote in the referendum despite flaws in the reform,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEItalian_Radicals,_20_September_2025Marattin2025Now!,_24_January_2026-78] and criticism of Meloni's rhetoric,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMore_Europe,_10_January_2026-79] reflecting their liberal, libertarian, and garantist views,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEItalian_Socialist_Party,_7_January_2026-80] with some speculating about what prominent socialist figure [Bettino Craxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bettino_Craxi) would think of it.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarani2026-81] Similarly, on the opposite side, there were those who expressed criticism of the magistrature and Italian justice but nonetheless expressed their vote against the reform in the referendum,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Il_Fatto_Quotidiano'',_18_December_2025-82] and those who generally agreed with the reform but would vote against it in the referendum as a political act against the Meloni government, as was also argued by Nordio.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMilano2026-83]

Several independent committees emerged in support of the constitutional changes. The "Yes to the Reform" committee was chaired by constitutional judge [Nicolò Zanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%B2_Zanon), with journalist Alessandro Sallusti serving as spokesperson.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''QuiComo'',_26_January_2026-84] The group included individuals affiliated with or formerly involved in FdI, Lega, and FI. The [Luigi Einaudi Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Einaudi_Foundation) launched the committee "Yes to Separation", presided over by lawyer Gian Domenico Caiazza and supported by various figures, such as [Matteo Hallissey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Hallissey), former magistrate [Antonio Di Pietro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Di_Pietro),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAntonucci2025-85] former senator [Andrea Cangini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Cangini), and historian Ernesto Galli della Loggia. In July 2025, the UCPI had already established its own committee to back the reform. Notably, some figures traditionally associated with the [centre-left coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left_coalition_\(Italy\)) endorsed the "Yes" campaign, including former [Italian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Communist_Party) deputy and [Constitutional Court of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Italy) former president [Augusto Barbera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_Barbera),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''ForlìToday'',_26_January_2026-75] as did Francesca Scopelliti, the partner of [Enzo Tortora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Tortora), who was a well-known victim of a miscarriage of justice, and other prominent constitutionalists, such as [Giulio Prosperetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giulio_Prosperetti) and [Nicolò Zanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%B2_Zanon).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlicino2026''ForlìToday'',_26_January_2026-86] The association Libertà Eguale formed the committee "The Left That Votes Yes" led by Barbera, the former PD deputy [Stefano Ceccanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefano_Ceccanti), and the former minister [Cesare Salvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Salvi), who opposed the broader left-wing decision to campaign against the reform. Additionally, [Fabrizio Cicchitto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrizio_Cicchitto), together with former ministers [Claudio Signorile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Signorile) and [Salvo Andò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvo_And%C3%B2), launched the "Giuliano Vassalli Committee for Yes". On 21 January 2026, the "Populars for Yes" committee was established, chaired by constitutional scholar [Giulio Prosperetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giulio_Prosperetti), further expanding the network of organisations advocating in favour of the constitutional reform.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''La_Repubbilca'',_21_January_2026-87][^cite_note-88]

### Opposition



A "No" meeting in [Bologna](21069333.md) with senator [Pier Ferdinando Casini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_Ferdinando_Casini)

The [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Italy\)) (PD) officially launched its campaign in favour of a "No" vote in the constitutional referendum on 24 January 2026, adopting the slogan "Vote No to defend the Constitution".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESannino2026-89] The [Five Star Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Star_Movement) (M5S) similarly structured its opposition campaign around the slogan "Vote No to the Save-the-Political-Elite Referendum", portraying the reform as a measure designed to protect the Meloni government's powers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Il_Fatto_Quotidiano'',_11_January_2026-20] The [Green and Left Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_and_Left_Alliance) (AVS) also criticised the constitutional reform, accusing it of aiming to concentrate excessive powers in the hands of the government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Il_Sole_24_Ore'',_24_January_2026-90] Extra-parliamentary parties soon started collecting signatures to reach the goal of 500,000 in order to have a longer electoral campaign.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPossible,_26_December_2025Communist_Refoundation_Party,_15_January_2026-91] Alongside political parties, several civil society groups and professional organisations formed committees to oppose the reform. On 18 November 2025, the "Right to Say No" committee began its campaign under the leadership of the [National Association of Magistrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Magistrates?action=edit\&redlink=1) (ANM), with constitutional law professor Enrico Grosso as its head. This was followed on 10 December 2025 by the creation of "Lawyers for No", a committee launched by a group of attorneys and chaired by Franco Moretti.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''La_Magistratura'',_18_November_2025-92]

On 10 January 2026, a broader coalition of organisations inaugurated the committee "Civil Society for No in the Constitutional Referendum" (bringing together "associations, [jurists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurist), and citizens to defend the Constitution, the autonomy of the judiciary, and oppose the Nordio Law, differentiated autonomy, and the premierate"), and Scientific Council of the "Civil Society Committee for the No" was established. In addition to joining the anti-reform committee, around 117 constitutional scholars joined its Scientific Committee, including three presidents emeritus of the Constitutional Court of Italy (Ugo De Siervo, Gaetano Silvestri, and Zagrebelsky) and a former vice president (Enzo Cheli).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELisi2026-93] This group included among others the trade union [CGIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CGIL), the anti-mafia association [Libera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libera_\(association\)), the [National Association of Italian Partisans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Italian_Partisans) (ANPI), and the environmental organisation [Legambiente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legambiente). The committee was presided over by former deputy Giovanni Bachelet and supported by physicist and Nobel laureate [Giorgio Parisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Parisi). In parallel with its campaigning activities, the committee also promoted a signature collection in support of the referendum process.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPicariello2026-94]

Several public intellectuals took positions in the debate. On 18 January 2026, Alessandro Barbero publicly announced his opposition to the reform in a video message explaining his reasons.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarbero2026Fontana2026''Pagella_Politica'',_19_January_2026-95] On 23 January, the video was removed by [Meta Platforms](62420226.md), prompting the PD to submit a parliamentary inquiry addressed to Prime Minister Meloni regarding the episode.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERaiNews,_24_January_2026''Pagella_Politica'',_19_January_2026-96] Opposition to the reform was further expressed by local government representatives. More than 150 mayors signed an appeal promoted by Italian Local Autonomies and the Civil Society for No committee. Among the signatories were prominent city leaders such as [Roberto Gualtieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Gualtieri) of Rome, [Gaetano Manfredi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaetano_Manfredi) of Naples, [Stefano Lo Russo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefano_Lo_Russo) of Turin, [Matteo Lepore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Lepore) of Bologna, [Sara Funaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Funaro) of Florence, [Silvia Salis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvia_Salis) of Genoa, [Massimo Zedda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimo_Zedda) of Cagliari, and [Vittoria Ferdinandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittoria_Ferdinandi) of Perugia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE''Il_Fatto_Quotidiano'',_24_January_2026-97] In February 2026, after the neo-fascist [CasaPound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CasaPound) came out in support of the reform, the PD took advantage of this to further their defence of the [anti-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-fascist) Constitution, sparking criticism among its own ranks from those who supported the reform and are anti-fascists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBerizzi2026Adnkronos,_4_February_2025-98]

### Analysis

The victory of the "No" side was seen as a blow to the Meloni government and a boost to opposition parties ahead of the [2027 Italian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Italian_general_election),[^cite_note-146] and ended a three-year period of relative calmness that was unusual in Italian politics.[^cite_note-147] In an effort to restore credibility after the defeat, in addition to the resignations of Bartolozzi, the Ministry of Justice undersecretary [Andrea Delmastro Delle Vedove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Delmastro_Delle_Vedove), and the FI group leader [Maurizio Gasparri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurizio_Gasparri), Meloni asked for [Daniela Santanchè](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniela_Santanch%C3%A8), the [Minister of Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Tourism_\(Italy\)) who is involved in legal proceedings related to fraud and false accounting, to step down.[^cite_note-148]

### Controversies

During a political event in [Genzano di Lucania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genzano_di_Lucania) less than a week prior to the referendum, in a context where the "No" side was surging in polls, the FdI deputy Aldo Mattia called for the use of the "usual clientelist system" to get out the vote in favour of approving the government's constitutional reform, sparking an outcry from the opposition, which called on Meloni to condemn it.[^cite_note-149] Mattia appeared to suggest the illegal practice of [vote buying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_buying) (*voto di scambio*), which was to be used to convince acquaintances to vote "Yes" once rational arguments had failed, using kinship ("Well, do me this favour because you're my cousin") or mutual favours ("because I've already done you so many") as leverage.[^cite_note-150] The day before the referendum, Salvini ([Deputy Prime Minister of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_Italy) and [Minister of Infrastructure and Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Infrastructure_and_Transport_\(Italy\))) broke [election silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_silence) by posting an image on [X (formerly Twitter)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_\(formerly_Twitter\)) with the word "SÌ" ("YES").[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153]

During the first day of the referendum, many parties, in particular the M5S and AVS, reported irregularities during the voting, such as a billboard with the word "YES!" in Genoa,[^cite_note-154] police officers contesting the list representatives at the seats of the "No" committee for the legitimate use of the sign with the committee's logo and also preventing them from being admitted and therefore voting,[^cite_note-155] and the presence of representatives of the FdI and Lega lists who gave voting instructions (for "Yes") at the polling stations (even with pins).[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157] FI members, such as [Fulvio Martusciello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulvio_Martusciello), also reported the use of public transport in Naples to indicate to vote "No".[^cite_note-158] A polling station in [Sassari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassari) did not conclude the count of votes after the polls closed because there was a discrepancy between the number of ballots and votes. Ballots were sent for counting by the Provincial Office for the Referendum at the Court of Sassari;[^cite_note-159] the ballots were tabled on official tally on 25 March 2026.[^cite_note-160]
