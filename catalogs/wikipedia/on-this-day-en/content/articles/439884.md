**Kulob**[^cite_note-3] or **Khatlon**[^cite_note-4] is a city in [Khatlon Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khatlon_Region) in southern Tajikistan. Located 203 km (126 mi) southeast of the capital [Dushanbe](56658.md) on the river Yakhsu (a right tributary of [Panj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panj_\(river\))), it is one of the largest cities in the country. Its population is estimated at 106,300 for the city proper and 214,700 for the city with the outlying communities (2020).[^cite_note-pop2020-1] The city is served by [Kulob Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulab_Airport).

## History

### Greek inscription

During the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period) following the [conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquests_of_Alexander_the_Great) of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), the region of modern Kulob was part of the [Greco-Bactrian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Bactrian_Kingdom). A [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language) inscription dating to the period 200–195 BC[^cite_note-SW-5] has been discovered in which a person named Heliodotos dedicates a [fire altar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_worship) to [Hestia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hestia) for the sake of the king [Euthydemus I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euthydemus_I) and his son [Demetrius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demetrius_I_of_Bactria).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-SW-5]



Kulob inscription, 200–195 BC: "Heliodotos dedicated this fragrant altar for [Hestia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hestia), venerable goddess, illustrious amongst all, in the grove of [Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeus), with beautiful trees; he made libations and sacrifices so that the greatest of all kings Euthydemos, as well as his son, the glorious, victorious and remarkable Demetrios, be preserved of all pains, with the help of [Tyche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyche) with divine thoughts."

### Later history



Tomb of [Mir Sayyid Ali Hamadani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_Sayyid_Ali_Hamadani)

The historian [Muhammad ibn Jarir al-Tabari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_ibn_Jarir_al-Tabari) refers to Khatlon as early as AD 737, although its founding is said to have been much earlier.[^cite_note-io-9] The Sufi mystic [Mir Sayyid Ali Hamadani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_Sayyid_Ali_Hamadani) died while travelling through Central Asia in 1384 and was buried in Khatlon in a tomb which still stands.[^cite_note-rafiabadi-10]



Kulob Marketplace

The city was conquered by the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) under [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan) and subsequently formed part of the [Timurid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire).[^cite_note-hd-11] It was incorporated into the [Khanate of Bukhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Bukhara) in 1559[^cite_note-hd-11] and subsequently usually aligned with the [Emirate of Bukhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Bukhara) against [Kokand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanate_of_Kokand) and [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Afghanistan) after the 18th century. Its name was changed to Kulob in 1750.[^cite_note-io-9] The local lord Surrah Khan imprisoned the British and Kokand agent [Abdul Mejid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Mejid_\(explorer\)) on behalf of Bukhara in 1861 before the disorder in the emirate prompted him to release the party.[^cite_note-12]

Following agreements between the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) and [Russian Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) over the disposition of Afghanistan, the city and its hinterland were incorporated into the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) as part of [its conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_conquest_of_Central_Asia) of most of [Central Asia](6742.md). After the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution), Kulob was only incorporated into the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in March 1921[^cite_note-hd-11] and was organized as part of the [Tajik Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) in 1929. Kulob was one of the largest cities in the republic.

During the [Tajikistani Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajikistani_Civil_War) in the early 1990s, the city served as the main base of the [Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_of_Tajikistan) militias. [Danghara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danghara), a village in the Kulob area, is the birthplace of Tajikistan's president [Emomali Rahmon](393634.md). In September 2006, Kulob celebrated its 2700th anniversary.[^cite_note-13]

After Tajikistan's independence in 1991, Kulob was one of the three cities—alongside [Dushanbe](56658.md) and [Qurghonteppa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurghonteppa)—where the Russian [201st Motor Rifle Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/201st_Motor_Rifle_Division) was deployed. Following a number of scandals with local residents, Russia unexpectedly pulled its troops from Kulob in November 2015, effectively abandoning the base there.[^cite_note-14]

## Geography

### Climate

Kulob has a [hot-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*). The average annual temperature is 15.8 °C (60.4 °F). The warmest month is July with an average temperature of 28.3 °C (82.9 °F) and the coolest month is January with an average temperature of 2.2 °C (36.0 °F). The average annual precipitation is 590.0 mm (23.23 in) and has an average of 103 days with precipitation. The wettest month is March with an average of 133 mm (5.2 in) of precipitation and the driest month is August with an average of 1.1 mm of precipitation.
