**Mistissini** ([Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree_language): ᒥᔅᑎᓯᓃ, romanized: *Mistisinî*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) '**Big Rock**') is a [Cree village municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree_village_municipality_\(Quebec\)) in the territory of [Eeyou Istchee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eeyou_Istchee_\(territory\)) in northern [Quebec](7954867.md), Canada; it has a distinct legal status and classification from other kinds of village municipalities in Quebec: Naskapi village municipalities, northern villages ([Inuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inuit) communities), and ordinary villages.

As with all other [Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree) village municipalities in Quebec, there is a counterpart [Cree reserved land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree_reserved_land_\(Quebec\)) of the same name located nearby: [Mistissini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistissini,_Quebec).

Despite the title of "village municipality" and the formalities that go along with it (for instance, having a mayor), Statistics Canada lists it (and all other Cree village municipalities in Quebec) as having few resident population or residential infrastructure (dwellings); it is the Cree reserved lands that are listed as having most of the population and residential dwellings in the [2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_2021_Census), the [2016 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_2016_census), and earlier censuses.

## Geography

The village is located on the Watso peninsula in the form of a large C (open to the northeast) attached to the east bank, forming the north-eastern shore of the Du Poste Bay, barring the exit of this bay and including the village of Mistissini in the North-West. A road connected to Route 167 serves this peninsula. The village is located at the mouth of the Du Poste Bay and in the southern part of Abatagouche Bay.

## History



Nine-passenger Nordic [seaplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplane) operated by Waasheshkun Airways Ltd, [Mistassini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Mistassini) lake, Watson peninsula, 2005

The Cree have lived in the area of the watershed of the [Rupert River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_River) and around Lake Mistassini for centuries. French explorers and traders entered it in the early 17th century. During the late 1600s, the French established a trading post on Lake Mistassini. The post was moved from time to time until the [Hudson's Bay Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_Company) established it in the 19th century at what has developed as the current location of the village. The trading post was supplied by a canoe brigade based at Fort Rupert (today [Waskaganish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waskaganish_\(Cree_village_municipality\))) up the Rupert River or through Neoskweskau (a former Cree site) on the [Eastmain River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastmain_River). The itinerary of the trip moved south - first by Oskelaneo when the railway was built in 1910, and later by the region of [Lac Saint-Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lac_Saint-Jean). The road reached Mistissini in 1970. Over time, Mistissini and the various posts in the area have been designated: "Maison Dorval", "Patagoosh", "Abatagoushe", "Mistassini" and "Baie-du-Poste".

## Languages

In Mistissini, according to the [Institut de la statistique du Québec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institut_de_la_statistique_du_Qu%C3%A9bec), the language most often spoken at home in 2011, by the population of 3,405 inhabitants, was [Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree_\(language\)) - 85.76%, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) - 2.35% of inhabitants, and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) - 10.87%.[^cite_note-5]
