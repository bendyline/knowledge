**Burgas** ([Bulgarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_language): Бургас, pronounced [\[burˈɡas\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Bulgarian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/94/LL-Q7918\_%28bul%29-Kiril_kovachev-%D0%91%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B3%D0%B0%D1%81.wav/LL-Q7918\_%28bul%29-Kiril_kovachev-%D0%91%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B3%D0%B0%D1%81.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/94/LL-Q7918_%28bul%29-Kiril_kovachev-%D0%91%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B3%D0%B0%D1%81.wav/LL-Q7918_%28bul%29-Kiril_kovachev-%D0%91%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B3%D0%B0%D1%81.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), sometimes transliterated as **Bourgas**, is the second largest city on the [Bulgarian Black Sea Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Black_Sea_Coast) in the region of [Northern Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Thrace) and the [fourth-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Bulgaria) in [Bulgaria](3415.md) after [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia), [Plovdiv](182553.md), and [Varna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varna,_Bulgaria), with a population of 210,382 inhabitants, while 225,945 live in its urban area. It is the capital of [Burgas Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgas_Province) and an important industrial, transport, cultural and tourist centre.

The city is surrounded by the [Burgas Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgas_Lakes) and located at the westernmost point of the [Black Sea](3386.md), at the large [Burgas Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgas_Bay). [LUKOIL Neftochim Burgas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUKOIL_Neftochim_Burgas) is the largest [oil refinery](195137.md) in southeastern Europe and the largest industrial enterprise. The [Port of Burgas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Burgas) is the second largest port in Bulgaria, and [Burgas Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgas_Airport) is the second most important in the country. Burgas is the centre of the Bulgarian fishing and fish processing industry.[^cite_note-USNI-2]

## Names



Burgas as seen from space

The city's name is considered to go back to the Greek word for "tower", "*Pyrgos*" ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Πύργος). It is mentioned by the Byzantine poet [Manuel Philes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Philes) and rendered in Turkish as *Burgaz*. There are several alternative explanations for the name's origin. One of them states the city's name comes from [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_language) name "baurgs", meaning "signified consolidated [walled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_wall) villages".[^cite_note-3] According to Bulgarian prof. Kiril Vlahov, the name of the city comes from the [Thracian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracian_language) word "*pyurg*", meaning "fortification of wooden beams".

## Geography

### Topography

Burgas is situated at the westernmost point of the bay by the same name and in the eastern part of the [Burgas Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgas_Plain?action=edit\&redlink=1), in the east of the [Upper Thracian Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Thracian_Plain). Burgas is located some 360 kilometres (224 mi) from Sofia, 255 km (158 mi) from Plovdiv, 130 km (81 mi) from Varna, and 332 km (206 mi) from [Istanbul](3391396.md). To the west, south and north, the city is surrounded by the [Burgas Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgas_Lakes): [Burgas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Burgas), [Atanasovsko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Atanasovsko), and [Mandrensko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Mandrensko), which are home to several hundred bird species. [Pan-European corridor 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_corridors) passes through the city,[^cite_note-4] the [European routes E87](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E87) and [E773](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E773), and the longest national rout I/6. The [St. Anastasia Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Anastasia_Island) is a part of the city.[^cite_note-5]

### Climate

Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), Burgas has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (Köppen: *Cfa*). The summertime in Burgas lasts about five months from mid-May until late September. Average temperatures during high season is 24 °C (75 °F). Summertime sea temperatures stay around 23–24 °C (73–75 °F) at sunrise and go up to 29–30 °C (84–86 °F) by dusk, averaging 26 °C (79 °F). Winters are milder compared with the inland part of the country, with average temperatures of 4–5 °C (39–41 °F) and below 0 °C (32 °F) during the night. Snow is possible in December, January, February and rarely in March; however, it can quickly melt. The highest temperature was recorded in June 2007, at 42.8 °C (109 °F) and the lowest at −21.6 °C (−7 °F) in January 1942.

### Flora and fauna



[Usungeren Protected Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usungeren_Protected_Area?action=edit\&redlink=1)



&#x20;[Lake Mandrensko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Mandrensko)

The [Burgas Wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgas_Wetlands) are highly recognized for their significance to biodiversity and as a resource pool for products used by people.

Lake Burgas is Bulgaria's largest lake and is in the middle of the city. It is important for migrating birds. Over 250 species of birds inhabit the lake area, 61 of which are endangered in Bulgaria and 9 globally, attracting keen birdwatchers from all over the world. The lakes are also home to important fish and [invertebrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invertebrate). In the site have been recorded several [IUCN Red-Listed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUCN_Red_List) species of animals – 5 invertebrates, 4 fish, 4 [amphibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibian), 3 [reptiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptiles), 5 birds and 3 [mammals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal). Situated along the second largest migration path of birds in Europe, the [Via Pontica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Pontica), the site is an important stopover and staging site for a large number of water-birds, raptors and passerines. Yearly during migration and wintering more than 20,000 (up to 100,000) waterbirds congregate there.[^cite_note-Ramsar_Convention-9]

The Atanasovo Lake is one of two salt-water lakes in the Black Sea region and contains rare and representative examples of wetland habitats. It is a hot spot for biodiversity, with many Red-Listed species of plants and animals. It is a well-known bottleneck site for migratory birds, with around 60,000 raptors and 240,000 storks, pelicans and cranes passing over the site and often landing in large numbers for staging. The highest numbers in Europe of migrating [Great white pelican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_white_pelican) (*Pelecanus onocrotalus*), [Dalmatian pelican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatian_pelican) (*Pelecanus crispus*), [Western marsh harrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_marsh_harrier) (*Circus aeruginosus*) and [Red-footed Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-footed_Falcon) (*Falco vespertinus*) have been recorded here.[^cite_note-Ramsar_Convention-9]

## History



[Alexander Severus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Severus) coin from the *Colonia Flavia Pacis Deultensium*

The earliest signs of life in the region date back 3,000 years, to the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) and the early [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age). The favorable conditions on the fertile plain, around the sea, have brought people here from early antiquity. The biggest mark was left by the Thracians who made the region rich in archaeological finds (from around 4th c. BC). This includes their sanctuary at [Beglik Tash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beglik_Tash) along the south coast and a burial mound near [Sunny Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunny_Beach). They built the mineral baths of [Aquae Calidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquae_Calidae,_Bulgaria) and the fortress *Tyrsis*.[^cite_note-municipality-10][^cite_note-pocket-11] Under [Darius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_I), it became part of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), before the [Odrysian kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odrysian_kingdom) was established. Greeks from [Apollonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sozopol) built a marketplace to trade with the Thracians, in what is now the neighborhood of [Pobeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pobeda,_Burgas).

During the rule of the [Ancient Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), near Burgas, [*Colonia Flavia Pacis Deultensium*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Develtos) (*Deultum*, *Dibaltum*, or *Develtum*) was established as a military colony for veterans by [Vespasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vespasian) in AD 70. The Romans built this *colonia* on the main road [Via Pontica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Pontica). It was the second most important city in the province [Haemimontus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haemimontus).

In 376 the Goths destroyed an elite Roman [company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_\(military_unit\)) near Develtum at the [Battle of Dibaltum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dibaltum).[^cite_note-12]

### Bulgarian and Byzantine Middle Ages

In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), there were important settlements in the area: the fortress *Skafida*, *Poros*, *Rusokastron* ([Battle of Rusokastro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Rusokastro)), the Baths called Aquae Calidae and used by Byzantine, Bulgarian and Ottoman Emperors; a small fortress called *Pyrgos* was erected where Burgas is today and was most probably used as a watchtower. Under the [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) it became an important city on the [Black Sea](3386.md) coast. The Bulgarian ruler [Krum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krum) built the [Erkesiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erkesiya?action=edit\&redlink=1), a 140 km (87 mi)-long border wall from the Black Sea (near Gorno Ezerovo) to the [Maritsa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritsa_River).

In 1206, the [Latin Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Empire) [Henry of Flanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_of_Flanders) (see [Fourth Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Crusade)) destroyed Aquae Calidae, which was known as Thermopolis at this time, The baths were later rebuilt by the Byzantines and Bulgarians. Poros was mentioned in a 1270 document of the Patriarchate of Constantinople.[^cite_note-IW6065-13] Close to Poros took place the [Battle of Skafida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Skafida) in 1304, when the Bulgarian Tsar [Todor Svetoslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Svetoslav_of_Bulgaria) defeated the Byzantines and conquered the southern Black Sea coast.

At the beginning of the 14th century, the region was sacked by the [Catalan Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_Company). In the 13th century Burgas is mentioned by the Byzantine poet [Manuel Philes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Philes) in his works as *Burgas*.[^cite_note-IW6065-13]

### Ottoman rule



Burgas in [Ottoman Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Bulgaria), painted by [Luigi Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Mayer)

Like many of the towns surrounding it, Burgas was conquered by the Ottomans with the rest of Bulgaria in the late 14th century, only to be [returned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Gallipoli) to the Byzantine Empire during the [Ottoman Interregnum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Interregnum) and retained by the Byzantines until the fall of the Empire to the Ottomans in 1453. It was only in the 17th century that a settlement renamed to *Ahelo-Pirgas* grew in the modern area of the city. It was later renamed to *Burgas* again and had only about 3,000 inhabitants. In the early 19th century Burgas was depopulated after raids by *kurzdhali* bandits. By the mid-19th century it had recovered its economic prominence through the growth of craftsmanship and the export of grain.[^cite_note-14] The city was a small town in [İslimye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sliven) (Sliven) sanjak in at first [Rumelia Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumelia_Eyalet), after that in the [Silistra Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silistra_Eyalet) and [Edirne Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edirne_Eyalet) before the liberation in 1878.

In the 17th and 18th centuries Burgas became an important port for [cereal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal) and possesses its own grain measure, the *Burgas-Kile*. The town was the regional centre of trade and administrative centre of the Burgas Kaaza.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

In 1865 the port of Burgas was after [Trapezunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trabzon) the second most important Ottoman port in the Black Sea. Burgas was at this time the major centre on the southern [Bulgarian Black Sea Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgarian_Black_Sea_Coast).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

### From liberation to 1945



Alexandrovska Street in 1906

It was a department centre in [Eastern Rumelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Rumelia) before incorporated in the [Principality of Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Bulgaria) in 1885. From the late 19th century Burgas became an important economic and industry center. The first development plan of the city was adopted in 1891 and the city's layout and appearance changed, especially through the newly constructed public buildings.[^cite_note-RMB-20] In 1888, the city library was founded, in 1891 the sea garden was created and in 1897 the Cathedral of the Holy brothers Cyril and Methodius was built. In 1895 Georgi Ivanov opened the first Printing house in Burgas, followed by the house of *Christo Velchev* in 1897, which changed in 1900 his name in *Velchevi Brothers Printing house*.[^cite_note-IK220-21]



A 1913 plan of the city

The opening of the railway line to Plovdiv on 27 May 1890 and the deep water port in 1903 were important stages of this boom and led to the rapid industrialization of the city.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] In the period after 151 factories were founded. Among them were the *Sugar refinery* founded by [Avram Chaliovski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avram_Chaliovski?action=edit\&redlink=1), the *Great Bulgarian Mills* of [Ivan Chadzipetrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Chadzipetrov?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the oil and soap factory *Kambana*.[^cite_note-RMB-20] In 1900 the mineral springs by the ancient Aquae Calidae were included in the urban area. In 1903, the new building of the [Burgas Central railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgas_Central_railway_station) opened.[^cite_note-IW210-24][^cite_note-25]

Founded in 1924 in Burgas *Deweko* (now HemusMark AD) was the first pencil factory in Southeastern Europe and became in 1937 official supplier to the Bulgarian Monarchy.[^cite_note-26] 1925 opened in Burgas a specialized high school for mechanics and technologies. The following year, a large covered market was opened.[^cite_note-RMB-20] Because of the cold wave in winter 1928/29 the Black Sea iced in late January and early February, so that the island of Sveta Anastasia could be reached on foot.[^cite_note-27] 1934, Burgas already had 34,260 inhabitants.

### Communism

During World War II on 9 September 1944 Red Army troops occupied the city and soon the whole country.[^cite_note-28] In the following [People's Courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Court_\(Bulgaria\)), especially members of the wealthy families of the intelligentsia and members of the [Bar Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Association) were convicted. The two Chambers of the People's Courts met in Burgas in the former building of the Chamber of Commerce and Industry of Burgas (now the seat of the Governor of the Province Burgas).[^cite_note-IW250-29]

After the Communists took power in 1945, the German and Italian School and the People's University were closed[^cite_note-Burneva/Murdsheva-30] and over 160 factories and businesses (including the large companies *Great Bulgarian Mills*, *Veriga*, *Plug*, *Dab*, etc.), shops, baths and other private property were nationalized. The [nationalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization) and inability to lead by the new rulers led the companies to the collapse of the food supply and the shortage of goods of daily life in the city.[^cite_note-IW250-29] The political repression against the population of Burgas continued for the next few years. Access to universities and other higher education in the Bulgarian capital was refused for the young people of Burgas and some of them were interned in [prison](19008450.md) and [labor camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_camps_in_Communist_Bulgaria).[^cite_note-IW250-29]



The [Neftohim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUKOIL_Neftochim_Burgas) refinery, one of the major Bulgarian industrial capacities, built during the Socialist era

After the end of the Second World War, the [Haganah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haganah) organised several convoys for the European survivors of the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust), which departed on ships from Burgas for Palestine. These convoys allowed 12,000 people, including the Jewish population of the city, to emigrate.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] In the following years the city center of Burgas, unlike many other Bulgarian cities, was not much affected by Communist-type urbanization and has kept much of its 19th- and early-20th-century architecture. A number of oil and chemical companies were gradually built.

The terrorists of the [2 June Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_June_Movement), Till Meyer, [Gabriele Rollnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriele_Rollnik), Gudrun Stürmer and Angelika Goder were arrested on 21 June 1978 in Burgas by West German officials and then brought into the [Federal Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Germany).[^cite_note-33]

### Post 1990

Today the local port is the largest in Bulgaria adding significantly to the regional economy. Burgas also hosts annual national exhibitions and international festivals and has a vibrant student population of over 6,000 that add to the city's appeal. The historical society also maintains open-air museums at [Beglik Tash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beglik_Tash) and [Develtum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Develtum).

Several countries have General Consulates in Burgas, among them [Belarus](3457.md), [Czech Republic](5321.md), [Estonia](28222445.md), [Georgia](48768.md),[^cite_note-34] [Greece](12108.md), [Romania](25445.md), [Russia](25391.md), [Sierra Leone](27308.md),[^cite_note-35] [Turkey](11125639.md) and [Ukraine](31750.md).[^cite_note-36]

In 2023 Bulgaria took back the concession it had granted to Lukoil in 2011 to run the Rosenets oil terminal.[^cite_note-37]

#### 2012 bus bombing

Main article: [2012 Burgas bus bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Burgas_bus_bombing)

On 18 July 2012 a terrorist attack was carried out by a [suicide bomber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicide_bomber)[^cite_note-38] on a passenger bus transporting Israeli tourists at the Burgas Airport. The bus was carrying forty-two Israelis, mainly youths, from the airport to their hotels, after arriving on a flight from [Tel Aviv](31453.md). The explosion killed the Bulgarian bus driver and five Israelis.[^cite_note-haaretznames19j-39]

## Economy

&#x20;Burgas is an important industrial centre. The most notable industrial enterprise was [LUKOIL Neftochim Burgas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LUKOIL_Neftochim_Burgas) - the largest [oil refinery](195137.md) now owned and run by Bulgaria,[^cite_note-47] and the largest manufacturing plant in the [Balkans](4829.md). The city, along with Sofia, is one of the key elements in supporting Bulgaria's future European transport network (TEN-T) EU and Pan-European Transport Corridor 4, which includes construction of the railway and road infrastructure and the development of the Port of Burgas and [Burgas Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgas_Airport).

## Education

### University of Bourgas "Prof. doc. Asen Zlatarov"

The university of Bourgas "Prof doc. Asen Zlatarov"[^cite_note-48] is the first university and still the only public higher education facility in the Bourgas region.[^cite_note-49] It was founded on 6 October 1963 by Decree No 162 of the Council of Ministers as Higher Chemico-technological institute "Prof. d-r Asen Zlatarov" The university itself is named after Professor Doctor Asen Zlatarov - a Bulgarian scientist, the founder of the Bulgarian biochemistry school, and a major public figure in Bulgarian history.

In the past, the university included only specialties related to Chemistry, but now the university is the major educational institution in the Bourgas region, that incorporates the several faculties and colleges:[^cite_note-50]

- Faculty of technical sciences

- Faculty of social sciences

- Faculty of natural sciences

- Technical college

- College of tourism

- Medical college

  [^cite_note-51]

### Burgas Free University

[Burgas Free University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgas_Free_University) (BFU) was established with an Act of The Great National Assembly on 18 September 1991 and is one of the first non-state universities in the country. The university is accredited by the National Evaluation and Accreditation Agency and is certified under the international quality standard ISO 9001\:2008. BFU is a modern and innovative university, caring for its students and their professional realization, and which has significant academic achievements and an internationally recognized status. BFU is a member of the [European Universities Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Universities_Association) (EUA). It has signed Agreements of Cooperation with 36 universities and organizations in Europe, America, Asia and Africa. It implements students and staff exchange programmes with 24 universities in Europe. It participates in the [European Credit Transfer and Accumulation System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Credit_Transfer_and_Accumulation_System) (ECTS).

BFU works under joint international projects with more than 100 universities and organizations and is a partner of [UNESCO](21786641.md) under the UNITWIN/UNESCO Chairs Programme and is a host institution of UNESCO Chair on Culture of Peace and Human Rights. Burgas Free University is certified according to the quality standards of the [International Organization for Standardization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Organization_for_Standardization) (ISO). In 2005 BFU was certified under ISO 9001\:2000 and in 2010 under ISO 9001\:2008. All the university's main activities have been certified, as well as its degree programmes: education of students in Bachelor, Master and PhD programmes, qualification, research and international relations activities.

### School of Commerce

The [School of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Commerce,_Bourgas) is a vocational college specialized in the fields of economics, finance, management and accounting education. Established on 1 October 1905, it is the second oldest business school in Bulgaria.
