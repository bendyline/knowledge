**Pengshui Miao and Tujia Autonomous County** ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 彭水苗族土家族自治县; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 彭水苗族土家族自治縣; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Péngshuǐ Miáozú Tǔjiāzú Zìzhìxiàn*) is an [autonomous county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_county) for the [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people) and [Tujia people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tujia_people) located in southeastern [Chongqing](171752.md), China, bordering [Guizhou](173815.md) province to the south and southwest and [Hubei](83319.md) province to the northeast.[^cite_note-\:1-2] It is 270 kilometres (170 mi) away from downtown Chongqing.

The county spans an area of 3,903 square kilometres (1,507 mi<sup>2</sup>),[^cite_note-\:2-3] and has a population of 545,094 as of 2010.[^cite_note-\:3-4] The county's government is located in [Hanjia Subdistrict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanjia_Subdistrict?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-\:1-2]

## History

During the [Shang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty) and [Zhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_dynasty) dynasties, the region of contemporary Pengshui belonged to the [State of Ba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_\(state\)).[^cite_note-\:3-4] During the [Warring States period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period), the area belonged to the [Qianzhong Commandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianzhong_Commandery), which would later be incorporated into [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty) China.[^cite_note-\:3-4]

During the Han dynasty, the area would be reorganized as Fuling County ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 涪陵縣).[^cite_note-\:3-4]

Under the [Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty),[^cite_note-\:3-4] in 593 CE, the area was organized as Pengshui County ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 彭水縣).[^cite_note-\:3-4] *Pengshui* means "Peng River",[^cite_note-\:3-4] which was the old name for the [Yu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yu_River_\(Hubei\)). During the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty), Pengshui County was put under the administration of [Shannan Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannan_Circuit?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Qianzhong Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianzhong_Circuit?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-\:3-4] Under the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty), Pengshui was administered by [Qian Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianzhou_\(in_modern_Chongqing_and_Guizhou\)?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-\:3-4] Under the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty), it was administered by [Shaoqing Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaoqing_Fu?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-\:3-4] By 1645 during the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty), it was part of Chongqing Prefecture ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 重慶府). In 1913 ([Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\))), it was part of Dongchuan [Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_\(political_division\)) ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 東川道). In 1927, it was reassigned to the Changgong Bureau ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 長公署) of [Sichuan Province](65185.md). And in 1935, of the Eighth Administrative Inspection Area ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 第八行政督察區) of Sichuan.

On November 16, 1949, Pengshui was captured by the [communists](7175.md). In January 1950, it was put under the jurisdiction of [Fuling Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuling_Prefecture?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-\:3-4] under the Chuandong administrative territory ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 川東行署涪陵專區). In September 1952, Fuling Special District was transferred to Sichuan, and was changed to a [prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_of_China) in June 1968.

On November 14, 1983 Pengshui was approved as an [autonomous county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_county).[^cite_note-\:3-4] The official status began November 10 of the following year. In 1987, it was assigned to [Qianjiang Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianjiang_Prefecture?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-\:3-4] In June 1997, it fell under the administration of [Chongqing](171752.md), which it remains today.[^cite_note-\:3-4]

## Geography



Map including Pengshui (labeled as P'ENG-SHUI 彭水) ([AMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Map_Service), 1953)

Pengshui Miao and Tujia Autonomous County is located in the [Sichuan Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_Basin) along the [Wu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_River_\(Yangtze_tributary\)),[^cite_note-\:1-2] in the mountainous region of [Wuling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuling_Mountains). A total of 54.2% of Pengshui is covered by forest.[^cite_note-\:2-3] Geographic coordinates: 28°57′ – 29° 51′ North, 107°48′ – 108°36′ East. Altitude of the seat: 245 m (804 ft).

The county is home to three peaks over 1,000 metres (3,300 ft) above sea level: Qiliangzi ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 七梁子) at 1,434 metres (4,705 ft), Baila Mountain ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 白蜡山) at 1,393 metres (4,570 ft), and Fenghuang Mountain ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 凤凰山) at 1,048 metres (3,438 ft).[^cite_note-\:1-2]

Neighbours:

- North: [Shizhu Tujia Autonomous County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shizhu)

- Northeast: [Lichuan City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichuan_City), [Enshi Tujia and Miao Autonomous Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enshi_Tujia_and_Miao_Autonomous_Prefecture), Hubei

- East: [Qianjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianjiang,_Chongqing) District

- Southeast: [Youyang Tujia and Miao Autonomous County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youyang_Tujia_and_Miao_Autonomous_County)

- South: [Yanhe Tujia Autonomous County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanhe_Tujia_Autonomous_County), Guizhou; [Wuchuan Gelao and Miao Autonomous County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuchuan_Gelao_and_Miao_Autonomous_County)

- Southwest: [Daozhen Gelao and Miao Autonomous County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daozhen_Gelao_and_Miao_Autonomous_County), Guizhou

- West: [Wulong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wulong_District,_Chongqing) County

- Northwest: [Fengdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fengdu) County

## Demographics

As of the [2010 Chinese Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_National_Population_Census_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), the county had a population 545,094, down from the 590,228 reported in the [2000 Chinese Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_National_Population_Census_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China).[^cite_note-\:3-4] In 1996, the county had an estimated population of 587,000.[^cite_note-\:3-4]

59.5% of Pengshui residents are members of the following 11 [ethnic minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalities_of_China): [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people), [Tujia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tujia_people), [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_Mongols_in_China), [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people), [Gelao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelao_people), [Dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_people), [Tibetans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people), [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu), [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people), [Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people), and [Hani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hani_people). There are 270,000 Miao and 90,000 Tujia in Pengshui.

## Economy

Mineral deposits in the county include [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [barite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baryte), [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble), [fluorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorite), and [calcite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcite).[^cite_note-\:1-2]

## Transportation

The [Chongqing–Huaihua railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongqing%E2%80%93Huaihua_railway) runs through the county.[^cite_note-\:1-2] Major expressways which pass through the county include the [G65 Baotou–Maoming Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G65_Baotou%E2%80%93Maoming_Expressway) and [National Highway 319](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Highway_319).[^cite_note-\:1-2]
