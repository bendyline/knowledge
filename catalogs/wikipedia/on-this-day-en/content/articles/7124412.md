**Illegal immigration** is the migration of people into a country in violation of that country's [immigration laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_law), including unauthorized entry or continued residence after the expiration of a valid visa. Illegal immigration tends to be financially upward, with migrants moving from poorer to richer countries.[^cite_note-1] Unauthorized residence can carry risks of [detention](1700791.md), [deportation](412990.md), or other administrative sanctions, depending on national laws.[^cite_note-2]

Some migrants, including [asylum seekers](276005.md), are legally protected from penalties under international law. Under Article 31 of the [1951 Refugee Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Refugee_Convention) and its [1967 Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_Relating_to_the_Status_of_Refugees), people who enter a country without authorization to seek asylum cannot be penalized solely for irregular entry if they present themselves promptly, and are lawfully allowed to remain until their cases are fully processed. Asylum seekers denied asylum may face [impediment to expulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impediment_to_expulsion) if the home country refuses to receive the person or if new asylum evidence emerges after the decision. In some cases, these people are considered illegal aliens. In others, they may receive a [temporary residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_residence) permit, for example regarding the principle of [non-refoulement](2702190.md) in the International [Refugee Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee_Convention). The [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights), referring to the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights), has shown in a number of indicative judgments that there are enforcement barriers to expulsion to certain countries, for example, due to the risk of torture.[^cite_note-3]

## Terminology

In Europe, the [Platform for International Cooperation on Undocumented Migrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PICUM) (PICUM) launched its international "Words Matter"[^cite_note-4] campaign in 2014 to promote the use of the terms *undocumented* or *irregular migrants* instead of *illegal*.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Depending on jurisdiction, culture, or context, alternatives to *illegal aliens* or *illegal immigrants* can include ***irregular migrants***, ***undocumented immigrants***, ***undocumented persons***, and ***unauthorized immigrants***, and ***sans-papiers*** (paritcularly when lacking a [permit authorizing residency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residence_permit)).[^cite_note-k779-8][^cite_note-\:3-9][^cite_note-\:4-10][^cite_note-\:6-11][^cite_note-\:5-12] In some contexts, the term *illegal immigrants* is shortened, often [pejoratively](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pejorative),[^cite_note-13] to *illegals*.[^cite_note-14] *Irregular migration* is a related term that is sometimes used, e.g. by the [International Organization for Migration](36893.md); however, because of the word *migration*, this term describes a somewhat wider concept, including [illegal emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_emigration).[^cite_note-\:4-10]

### News media

Some news associations have in their [style guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_guide) discontinued or discouraged the term *illegal* *immigrant,* except in quotations. These organizations presently include the [Associated Press](18935732.md) (US),[^cite_note-\:7-15] [Press Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_Association) (UK), [European Journalism Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Journalism_Observatory),[^cite_note-16] [European Journalism Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Journalism_Centre),[^cite_note-17] [Association of European Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_European_Journalists), [Australian Press Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Press_Council),[^cite_note-18] and [Media, Entertainment and Arts Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media,_Entertainment_and_Arts_Alliance) (AU).[^cite_note-19] Most commonly they use the alternative term *undocumented immigrant*. Related terms that describe actions are not similarly discouraged. For example, the Associated Press continues to use the term *illegal immigration*, whereby *illegal* describes the action rather than the person.[^cite_note-\:7-15]

On the other hand, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* described *undocumented immigrant* as a "term preferred by many immigrants and their advocates, but it has a flavor of [euphemism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphemism) and should be used with caution outside the quotation".[^cite_note-\:3-9] *[Newsweek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsweek)* questions the use of the phrase *undocumented immigrants* as a method of [euphemistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphemism) [framing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framing_\(social_sciences\)), namely, "a psychological technique that can influence the perception of social phenomena".[^cite_note-\:8-20] *Newsweek* also suggests that persons who enter a country unlawfully cannot be entirely "undocumented", as they "just lack the certain specific documents for [legal residency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_residency) and employment", while "\[m]any have driver's licences, debit cards, library cards, and school identifications which are useful documents in specific contexts but not nearly so much for immigration".[^cite_note-\:8-20] For example, in the US, youths brought into the country illegally are granted access to public K-12 education and benefits regardless of citizenship status;[^cite_note-Gonzales-21] therefore the youths are not entirely undocumented, since they are documented for educational purposes.

### US government

See also: [Illegal aliens (Library of Congress Subject Heading)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_aliens_\(Library_of_Congress_Subject_Heading\))

[Title 8 of the US Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_8_of_the_United_States_Code) is the portion of United States law that contains legislation on [citizenship](25151566.md), [nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_nationality_law), and [immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_immigration_laws). Defining the legal term *[alien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_\(law\))* as "any person, not a citizen or national of the United States",[^cite_note-\:5-12] The terminology used in Title 8 includes illegal alien (33 times), unauthorized alien (21 times), undocumented alien (18 times), *illegal immigrant* (6 times), *undocumented person* (2 times), and others.[^cite_note-\:6-11] An analysis by [PolitiFact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PolitiFact) concluded that the term *illegal alien* "occurs scarcely, often undefined or part of an introductory title or limited to apply to certain individuals convicted of felonies".[^cite_note-\:02-22]

In the United States, while overstaying a visa is a [civil violation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_violation) handled by the [immigration court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_court), entering (including re-entering) the US without approval from an immigration officer is a crime; specifically a [misdemeanor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misdemeanor) on the first offense. [Illegal reentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal-reentry) after deportation is a [felony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felony) offense. This is the distinction between the larger group referred to as *unauthorized immigrants* and the smaller subgroup referred to as criminal immigrants.[^cite_note-23]

Democratic Senator and Senate Majority Leader [Chuck Schumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Schumer) has spoken out against the term *undocumented*, stating that "Illegal immigration is wrong – plain and simple" and that proponents of the term were "not serious" about combatting illegal immigration.[^cite_note-24] During President [Joe Biden](145422.md)'s term, government websites used the term "non-citizen" instead of "alien". This change was reversed in January 2025 after President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) returned to office.[^cite_note-25]

### Canadian government



An [asylum](1621432.md) seeker that has illegally crossed from the United States to Canada is being detained.

In Canada, as in the US, *illegal immigrant* is a commonly used term; however, there is confusion and deep dissent among many about what the term means under the law and what circumstances, and what it implies socially.[^cite_note-26] *Irregular* is a term used by government authorities to refer to migrants who enter Canada outside of official border crossings ("points of entry"). Entrance into Canada outside of a POE is considered unlawful but not a criminal offence, or a civil offence under the *Immigration and Refugee Protection Act*, SC 2001, c. 27.[^cite_note-27]

The government of Canada,[^cite_note-28] as well as the Immigration and Refugee Board, use the term *irregular* to refer to these crossings.[^cite_note-29] The [Liberal Party of Canada](149536.md) and the [New Democratic Party](19283982.md) typically use the term *irregular*, while the [Conservative Party of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_of_Canada) typically uses the term *illegal*.[^cite_note-30] The use of the term *undocumented* is increasingly prevalent among individual MPs and MLAs in Canada, and was also used in a NDP policy document,[^cite_note-31] as well as by Ontario NDP leader Andrea Horwath in a 2018 platform document.[^cite_note-32] Conservative MP Dave Epp referred to "undocumented workers" in a 2020 interview with the CBC wherein he called for an end to the use of contract migrant labour by Canadian agriculture businesses, in part because many such workers are undocumented and therefore vulnerable to exploitation and unsafe working conditions.[^cite_note-33]

### Poverty

Some examples do show that increases in poverty, especially when associated with immediate crises, can increase the likelihood of illegal migration. The [1994 economic crisis in Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_economic_crisis_in_Mexico), after the start of the [North American Free Trade Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Free_Trade_Agreement) (NAFTA), was associated with widespread poverty and a lower valuation for the peso relative to the dollar.[^cite_note-pietro-34] It also marked the start of a massive swell in Mexican immigration, in which net illegal migration to the US increased every year from the mid-1990s until the mid-2000s. There are also examples where [natural disasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_disasters) and [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth) can amplify [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty)-driven migration flows.[^cite_note-35]

### Gender violence

Many leave their country fleeing [gender-based violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender-based_violence), such as [honor crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honor_crime) or [forced marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_marriage), especially from conflicts area.[^cite_note-36] Women in illegal situations are especially at risk of [sexual exploitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_exploitation) or [rape](7980471.md).

### Family reunification

Some illegal immigrants seek to live with relatives who already live in a country that they are not allowed to enter, such as a spouse or other family members.[^cite_note-washpost1-37][^cite_note-lamigra-38][^cite_note-haaretz1-39] Having a family who has immigrated or being from a community with many immigrants is a much better predictor of one's choice to immigrate than poverty.[^cite_note-pew_survey-40] Family reunification visas may be applied for by legal residents or naturalized citizens to bring their family members into a destination state legally, but these visas may be limited in number and subject to yearly quotas. This may result in family members entering illegally to reunify. From studying Mexican migration patterns, Douglas Massey finds that the likelihood that a Mexican national will emigrate illegally to the US increases dramatically if they have one or more family members already residing in the United States, legally or illegally.[^cite_note-massey-41]

### Asylum



Arrested refugees/immigrants in [Fylakio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fylakio) detention centre, [Evros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evros_\(regional_unit\)), northern Greece

[Unauthorised arrival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unauthorised_arrival) into another country may be prompted by the need to escape civil war or [repression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_repression) in the country of origin. The [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Declaration_of_Human_Rights) upheld by the [United Nations](31769.md) guarantees the right of asylum,[^cite_note-Nations_1948-42] and as such, [asylum seekers](276005.md) are typically treated differently from illegal immigrants. In practice, however, many asylum seekers are subjected to exceedingly long waiting periods, isolated and unsafe detention facilities, and a high probability of being denied. This has led some authors to suggest that the ideal of asylum has eroded in recent years in the [Global North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_North).[^cite_note-University_of_Minnesota_Press_2019-43][^cite_note-Jacobsen_Karlsen_Khosravi_2020_p._159-44] According to the [1951 Refugee Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Refugee_Convention) [refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees) should be exempted from immigration laws and should expect protection from the country they entered.[^cite_note-45]

### Deprivation of citizenship

See also: [Denaturalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denaturalization)

In a 2012 news story, the [*CSM*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Sense_Media) reported, "The estimated 750,000 [Rohingya](1918632.md), one of the most miserable and oppressed minorities in the world, are deeply resentful of their almost complete absence of civil rights in [Myanmar](19457.md). In 1982, the military junta stripped the Rohingya of their Myanmar citizenship, classing them as illegal immigrants and rendering them [stateless](789786.md)."[^cite_note-46] In some countries, people born on national territory (henceforth not "immigrants") do not automatically obtain the nationality of their birthplace, and may have no legal title of residency.[^cite_note-47]

### Persecution

With a pattern of [persecution of Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians) in Iran, Iranian [converts to Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_to_Christianity) from Islam face the death penalty.[^cite_note-Jervis-48][^cite_note-Eisner-49] Peyman Malaz, chief operating officer of the PARS Equality Center, noted that "Those who arrive at the \[Mexico–United States] border are often the most persecuted and desperate, such as [Iranian Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Iran)".[^cite_note-Jervis-48] Matthew Soerens, US director of Church Mobilization for [World Relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Relief), noted that in 2024, "30,000 of the 100,000 refugees resettled in the US were Christians fleeing persecution."[^cite_note-Eisner-49] If deported back to Iran, [converts to Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_to_Christianity) from Islam face the death penalty given the pattern of [persecution of Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians) there.[^cite_note-Jervis-48][^cite_note-Eisner-49]

### Education

Families want to have better lives for their children and to succeed. The article "Learning to be Illegal" discusses the safety the children have in K-12 schooling. The children are guaranteed education in a safe environment.[^cite_note-Gonzales-21]

Aside from illegal immigration status, illegal immigration is related to other effects.[^cite_note-50]

### Illegal employment

Main articles: [Unreported employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreported_employment) and [Informal economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Informal_economy)

The employment of aliens, which is illegal under US law,[^cite_note-k790-51] has been found to be enabled by low employer [sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_\(law\)) and rare [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement) in particular for [subcontractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcontractor) and [gig economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gig_economy).[^cite_note-u727-52][^cite_note-53] The search for employment is central to illegal international migration.[^cite_note-54]

### Exploitation of labour

Main article: [Exploitation of labour](40067623.md)

Illegal employment makes it easier for corporations to take advantage of [wage labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wage_labour). If an employer does not maintain proper safety standards, refuses to pay, or creates overall precarious working conditions, looking for remedies or redress would also mean the risk of having to disclose illegal immigration status.[^cite_note-55] Employers sometimes pay less than the legal minimum wage or have unsafe working conditions, relying on the reluctance of illegal workers to report the violations to the authorities.[^cite_note-56] Another consequence on labor is the lack of regulations and fairness programs leading to an increased barrier to employment for women[^cite_note-57] or handicapped persons. Unfair and unjust, the exploitation of illegal immigrants' labor can go unpunished.[^cite_note-58]

According to data from the US Census Bureau, illegal immigrants in the United States often work in dangerous industries such as agriculture and construction.[^cite_note-59] A recent study suggests that the complex web of consequences resulting from illegal immigrant status limits illegal workers' ability to stay safe at work.[^cite_note-60] In addition to physical danger at work, the choice to immigrate for work often entails work-induced lifestyle factors which impact the physical, mental and social health of immigrants and their families.[^cite_note-61]

#### Slavery

Main article: [Human trafficking](19333662.md)

Research at [San Diego State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_State_University) estimates that there are 2.4 million victims of human trafficking among illegal Mexican immigrants in the United States.[^cite_note-62] Some workers are smuggled into the United States and Canada by human traffickers.[^cite_note-63] People have been kidnapped or tricked into slavery to work as laborers, after entering the country, for example in factories. Those trafficked in this manner often face additional barriers to escaping slavery, since their status as illegal immigrants makes it difficult for them to gain access to help or services. For example, [Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) women trafficked into Thailand and forced to work in factories or as prostitutes may not speak the language and may be vulnerable to abuse by police due to their illegal immigrant status.[^cite_note-64]

#### Sexual exploitation

Main article: [Sex trafficking](2101268.md)

Since the [fall of the Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1989), [Western Europe](33800.md) is being confronted with a serious problem related to the sexual exploitation of illegal immigrants (especially from [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe)), for prostitution.[^cite_note-65] In the United States, human trafficking victims often pass through the porous border with Mexico. To curb the spread of [sex slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_slavery) and other predation on unauthorized immigrants, then California Attorney General [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris) and Mexico Attorney General [Marisela Morales Ibáñez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marisela_Morales_Ib%C3%A1%C3%B1ez) signed an accord in 2012 to expand prosecutions of criminals typically members of [transnational gangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnational_gangs) who engage in the trafficking of human beings between the two countries.[^cite_note-66]

### Economy and labor market

Further information: [Economic results of migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_results_of_migration) and [Economic migrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_migrant)

Research on the economic effects of illegal immigration is scant, but existing studies suggest that the effects can be positive for the native population,[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] and for public coffers.[^cite_note-\:18-69][^cite_note-\:0-70] One 2015 study states that "increasing deportation rates and tightening border control weakens low-skilled labor markets, increasing unemployment of native low-skilled workers. Legalization, instead, decreases the unemployment rate of low-skilled natives and increases income per native." This is because the presence of illegal immigrants reduces the labor costs of employers, providing them more opportunities to create more jobs.[^cite_note-\:1-71] A 2013 study by the liberal think tank [Center for American Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_American_Progress) found that granting citizenship to people who immigrated illegally would boost the US economy: doing so would raise the incomes of illegal immigrants by a quarter (increasing [US GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_GDP) by approximately \$1.4 trillion over a 10-years);[^cite_note-72] a 2016 study found that "legalization would increase the economic contribution of the unauthorized population by about 20%, to 3.6% of private-sector GDP";[^cite_note-73] and a 2019 working paper by the [University of Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cyprus) found that "all types of immigrants generate a larger surplus to US firms than natives do".[^cite_note-74]

According to economist [George Borjas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Borjas), immigrants may have caused the decline of real wages of US workers without a high school degree by 9% between 1980 and 2000 due to increased competition.[^cite_note-75] Other economists, such as [Gordon Hanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Hanson), criticized these findings.[^cite_note-logic-76] [Douglas Massey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Massey) argues that [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country) need [unskilled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unskilled_labour) immigrant labor to fill undesirable jobs, which citizens do not seek regardless of wages.[^cite_note-massey-41] Massey argues that this may refute claims that illegal immigrants are "lowering wages" or stealing jobs from native-born workers and that it instead shows that illegal immigrants "take jobs that no one else wants".[^cite_note-massey-41] A paper by Spanish economists found that, upon granting work permits to the illegal [immigrant population in Spain](10499603.md), the fiscal revenues increased by around €4,189 per newly legalized immigrant.[^cite_note-\:0-70] The paper found that the wages of the immigrants increased after receiving work permits. At the same time, some low-skilled natives had worse labor market outcomes and high-skilled natives had improved labor market outcomes.[^cite_note-\:0-70]

Since the decline of working-class [blue-collar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-collar) jobs in manufacturing and industry, younger native-born generations have acquired higher education. In the US, only 12% of the labor force has less than a high school education, but 70% of illegal workers from Mexico lack a high school degree.[^cite_note-logic-76] Support for this claim can be seen in a [Pew Hispanic Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Hispanic_Center) poll of over 3,000 illegal immigrants from Mexico in the US, which found that 79% would voluntarily join a [temporary worker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_worker) program that allowed them to work legally for several years but then required them to leave.[^cite_note-pew_survey-40] From this, it is assumed that the willingness to take undesirable jobs is what gives undocumented immigrants their employment.[^cite_note-massey-41] Evidence for this may be seen in the average wages of illegal day laborers in California, which was between \$10 and \$12 per hour according to a 2005 study, higher than many entry-level white-collar or service jobs.[^cite_note-77]

Research indicates that the advantage to firms employing illegal immigrants increases as more firms in the industry do so, further increases with the breadth of a firm's market, and also with the labor intensity of the firm's production process; however, the advantage decreases with the skill level of the firm's workers, meaning that illegal immigrants do not provide as much competitive advantage when a high-skilled workforce is required.[^cite_note-78]

### Lack of access to services

Illegal immigrants usually have no or very limited access to [public health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_health) systems, proper housing, [education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education) and [banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank). For instance, the current international human rights framework stipulates in various documents that the right to health and access to healthcare is fundamental and independent of a person's legal status. However, on a domestic level, many States in Europe have established the right to health as a welfare right, making it subject to citizenship or other administrative requirements.[^cite_note-79] Whether it's due to the danger behind disclosing their status or because of the inherently unfair social infrastructures, these barriers are present in all types of services, from social security to health.

## Methods

### Illegal border crossing

Main article: [Illegal entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_entry)



Border patrol at sea by the [US Customs and Border Protection](757040.md)



[HMC *Vigilant*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMC_Vigilant), one of several customs cutters of the UK [Border Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_Force), and capable of speeds up to 26 knots, departing [Portsmouth Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Naval_Base)

Immigrants from countries that do not have automatic visa agreements, or who would not otherwise qualify for a visa, often cross the borders illegally in some areas such as the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border), the Mona Channel between the [Dominican Republic](8060.md) and [Puerto Rico](23041.md), the [Strait of Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Gibraltar), [Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura), and the [Strait of Otranto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Otranto). Because these methods are illegal, they are often dangerous. Would-be immigrants have been known to suffocate in [shipping containers](22849124.md),[^cite_note-85] [boxcars](336679.md),[^cite_note-86] and trucks,[^cite_note-87] sink in [shipwrecks](272778.md) caused by unseaworthy vessels, die of [dehydration](155056.md),[^cite_note-88] or [exposure](146879.md) during long walks without water. An official estimate puts the number of people who died in illegal crossings across the US–Mexican border between 1998 and 2004 at 1,954. [Human smuggling](68714.md) is the practice of intermediaries aiding illegal immigrants in crossing over international borders for financial gain, often in large groups. Human smuggling differs from but is sometimes associated with [human trafficking](19333662.md). A human smuggler will facilitate [illegal entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_entry) into a country for a fee, but on arrival at their destination, the smuggled person is usually free. Trafficking involves a process of using physical force, [fraud](58790.md), or [deception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deception) to obtain and transport people. Types of notorious human smugglers include [Snakehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snakehead_\(gang\)) [gangs](80408.md) present in [mainland China](55079.md) (especially in [Fujian](88585.md)) that smuggle laborers into [Pacific Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Rim) states (making [Chinatowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown) frequent centers of illegal immigration) and "coyotes", who smuggle illegal immigrants to the [Southwestern United States](179571.md) and have been known to abuse or even kill their passengers.[^cite_note-89]

### Overstaying visa

Further information: [Visa fraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_fraud)

Many illegal immigrants are migrants who originally arrive in a country lawfully but overstay their authorized residence (overstaying a [visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_\(document\))).[^cite_note-iom5-90][^cite_note-91] For example, most of the estimated 200,000 illegal immigrants in Canada (perhaps as high as 500,000) are refugee claimants whose refugee applications were rejected but who have not yet been expelled from the country.[^cite_note-92] Another example is formed by children of foreigners born in countries observing *[jus soli](54035.md)* ('right of territory'), such as was the case in France until 1994[^cite_note-93] and in Ireland until 2005.[^cite_note-citizensinformation.ie-94] In these countries, it was possible to obtain French or Irish nationality (respectively) solely by being born in France before 1994 or in Ireland before 2005[^cite_note-citizensinformation.ie-94] (respectively). At present, a French born child of foreign parents does not automatically obtain French nationality until residency duration conditions are met.[^cite_note-iom5-90] Since 1 January 2005, a child born in Ireland does not automatically acquire Irish nationality unless certain conditions are met.[^cite_note-citizensinformation.ie-94]

### Sham marriages

Some people enter into [sham marriages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sham_marriage), whereby marriage is contracted into for purely immigration advantage by a couple who are not in a genuine relationship. Common reasons for sham marriages are to gain [immigration](4599312.md) (i.e., immigration fraud),[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] residency, work, or [citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship) rights for one or both of the spouses or other benefits. In the United Kingdom, those who arrange, participate in, or officiate over a sham marriage may be charged with several offenses, including assisting unlawful immigration and conspiracy to facilitate a breach of immigration law.[^cite_note-97] The United States has a penalty of a \$250,000 fine and five-year prison sentence for such arrangements.[^cite_note-98] The [US Immigration and Customs Enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement) (ICE) and the Justice Department say that they do not have accurate numbers on the rate of attempted marriage fraud.[^cite_note-99] In the 2009 fiscal year, 506 (0.2%) of the 241,154 petitions filed were denied for suspected fraud; 7% were denied on other grounds.[^cite_note-100]

## Legal protections and exceptions

Not all unauthorized immigration are subject to penalties, and many countries provide legal protections or give temporary status for certain categories of irregular residents.

### Asylum seekers

Individuals entering a country without authorization with the purpose to seek asylum are protected by international law, notably Article 31 of the [1951 Refugee Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_Relating_to_the_Status_of_Refugees) and its [1967 Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocol_Relating_to_the_Status_of_Refugees). These laws stipulate that asylum seekers cannot be penalized for unauthorized entry if they present themselves promptly and are permitted to reside lawfully until at least their cases are fully processed. Per [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights) under the [European Convention on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_on_Human_Rights), they cannot be illegally returned to countries where they face persecution, torture, or inhuman treatment, under the principle of [non-refoulement](2702190.md). However, some individuals may attempt to misuse this pathway through fraudulent claims.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

### Family-based protections

Many countries, such as the US, limit or defer the removal of unauthorized residents who are close family members of citizens or long-term residents to protect family unity or prevent undue hardship. This may include parents, spouses, or children of citizens, and can allow exception from penalties and temporary legal residence or deferred removal.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]

### Temporary or humanitarian status

Legal exceptions from deportation and penalties can be made for Temporary Protected Status, deferred action, or similar forms of humanitarian relief for unauthorized immigrants that arrived from countries affected by conflict, natural disaster, or other emergencies. Programs often permit such irregular residents to remain lawfully for a defined period and may include work authorization. In the US, Temporary Protected Status allows individuals who were previously unauthorized to obtain legal, temporary status but the pathway in itself does not lead to permanent residence ([Green Card](300237.md)). However TPS holders who qualify for asylum, may still apply for asylum to attain permanent residence status.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108]

### Pathways to regularization

In many jurisdictions, temporary or protected residence that began with unauthorized entry can, under specific, lawful criteria, provide a pathway to permanent residency and, eventually, citizenship. This pathway is generally not automatic and illustrates that certain unauthorized residence does not necessarily equate to criminality or deportation, at least until the individual's status is fully resolved under the applicable legal process.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]

Brazil last legalized all immigrants in 1998; bilateral deals, one of which promoted the legalization of all reciprocal immigrants with Bolivia to date, signed in 2005, are also common.[^cite_note-Globo-157] Illegal immigrants in Brazil enjoy the same legal privileges as native Brazilians regarding access to social services such as [public education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_education) and the [Brazilian public healthcare system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sistema_%C3%9Anico_de_Sa%C3%BAde).[^cite_note-Globo-157] A [Federal Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Police_Department) operation investigated Chinese immigrants who traveled through six countries before arriving in [São Paulo](390875.md) to work under substandard conditions in the [textile industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry).[^cite_note-158] An October 2009 piece from [O Globo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Globo), quoting a [UNDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNDP) study, estimates the number of illegal immigrants at 0.7 million,[^cite_note-159] and points out to a recent wave of xenophobia among the general populace.[^cite_note-160]

#### Russia

Main article: [Illegal immigration in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_immigration_in_Russia)

Russia experiences a constant flow of immigration. On average, 200,000 legal immigrants enter the country every year; about half are ethnic [Russians](59510.md) from other republics of the former Soviet Union. There are an estimated 10–12 million foreigners working in the country without legal permission to be there.[^cite_note-206] There has been a significant influx of ethnic [Georgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgians), [Armenians](387816.md), [Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijanis), [Tajiks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C4%81jik_people), and [Uzbeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbeks) into large Russian cities in recent years, which has been viewed very unfavourably by many citizens and contributed to [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_nationalism) sentiments.[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208]

Many immigrant ethnic groups have much higher birth rates than native Russians, further shifting the balance. Some [Chinese](372983.md) flee the overpopulation and birth control regulations of their home country and settle in the [Far East](173135.md) and southern [Siberia](29639.md). Russia's main Pacific port and naval base of [Vladivostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok), once closed to foreigners, today is bristling with Chinese markets, restaurants and trade houses.[^cite_note-209] Illegal border crossing is considered a crime, and captured illegal border crossers have been sentenced to prison terms. For example, *[Rossiyskaya Gazeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rossiyskaya_Gazeta)* reported in October 2008 the case of a [North Korean](21255.md) who was detained after illegally crossing the [Amur River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amur_River) from China. Considered by Russian authorities an "[economic migrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_migrant)", he was sentenced to 6 months in prison and was to be deported to the country of his nationality after serving his sentence, although he may now risk an even heavier penalty there. That was just one of the 26 cases year-to-date of illegal entrants, of various nationalities, receiving criminal punishment in [Amur Oblast](87627.md).[^cite_note-210]

### Europe

See also: [European migrant crisis](46415102.md), [Open borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_borders), and [Migration and asylum policy of the European Union](62251245.md)



Eurostat: Non-EU citizens found to be illegally present in the EU-28 and EFTA, 2015

The [Schengen Area](16565139.md) is a multilateral agreement between 27 states in which they in most cases abolish the border control among themselves. These states include most of the EU countries, as well as the EEC countries Norway, Switzerland and Iceland. Any person who is physically inside any of the Schengen states will usually be able to travel to any other Schengen state without hindrance from the law enforcement, even if they have no legal right to enter another Schengen Area member state. A person who wishes to immigrate illegally to a Schengen Area member state may therefore find it more practical to enter it through another member state. According to a BBC report from 2012, over 80% of illegal immigrants entering the European Union pass through Greece.[^cite_note-213]



Migrants along the Balkan route crossing from Serbia into Hungary, 24 August 2015

EU countries that are not members of the Schengen Agreement are still committed to allow lawful entry by citizens of EU countries;[^cite_note-214] however, they may exercise border control at their discretion. Citizens within the EU is an economic and political partnership between 28 European countries that together cover much of the European continent.[^cite_note-215] A citizen of an EU member state has the right to seek employment within any other member state.[^cite_note-216] The Schengen Agreement does not regulate treatment of persons who enter the Schengen Area illegally. This is therefore left to the individual states, and other applicable international treaties and European [case law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_law). Illegal immigration to Schengen and to Europe in general was increasing sharply since approximately early 2014.[^cite_note-217]

See also: [Illegal immigration from Africa to Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_immigration_from_Africa_to_Israel)



Demonstration against the expulsion of undocumented immigrants and their families from Israel, Tel Aviv, 2009

Tens of thousands of migrants, mostly from Sudan and Eritrea, had crossed the Israeli border between 2009 and 2012.[^cite_note-afreuters-255] Prime Minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) said that, "This phenomenon is very grave and threatens the social fabric of society, our national security and our national identity."[^cite_note-256] In May 2012, Israel introduced a law which would allow illegal immigrants to be detained for up to three years, a measure that the Interior Ministry intended to stem the flow of Africans entering Israel across the desert border with Egypt.[^cite_note-afreuters-255] As a result, completing a [barrier along the border with Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Egypt_barrier), illegal immigration from Africa decreased by over 99%.[^cite_note-257]

Israel faces substantial (estimated at 40,000 in 2009)[^cite_note-forty_thousand-258] illegal immigration of Arab workers from the Palestinian Authority territories, a migration that includes both workers seeking employment, and homosexuals escaping the social opprobrium of Arab society.[^cite_note-259][^cite_note-260][^cite_note-261][^cite_note-forty_thousand-258][^cite_note-262] Thousands of foreign workers who entered the country on temporary visas have overstayed and live illegally in Israel.[^cite_note-263] There is a debate within Israel as to whether the Israel-born children of foreign workers should be allowed to remain in the country.[^cite_note-264]

#### Libya

Main article: [Illegal immigration in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_immigration_in_Libya)

Before the [Libyan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_civil_war), [Libya](17633.md) was home to a large population of illegal immigrants from [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md), numbering as much as 2,000,000.[^cite_note-265] The mass expulsion plan to summarily deport all illegally residing foreigners was announced by then-current Libyan leader Colonel [Muammar al-Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muammar_al-Gaddafi) in January 2008, "No resident without a legal visa will be excluded."[^cite_note-266][^cite_note-267]

#### Saudi Arabia

Main article: [Illegal immigration to Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_immigration_to_Saudi_Arabia)

In 2004, [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) began construction of a [Saudi–Yemen barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi%E2%80%93Yemen_barrier) between its territory and [Yemen](350939.md) to prevent the unauthorized movement of people and goods into and out of the Kingdom. [Anthony H. Cordesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Cordesman) labeled it a "separation barrier".[^cite_note-268] In February 2004, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* reported that Yemeni opposition newspapers likened the barrier to the Israeli West Bank barrier,[^cite_note-Gdn1-269] while [The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent) wrote, "Saudi Arabia, one of the most vocal critics in the Arab world of Israel's 'security fence' in the West Bank, is quietly emulating the Israeli example by erecting a barrier along its porous border with Yemen."[^cite_note-270] Saudi officials rejected the comparison saying it was built to prevent infiltration and smuggling.[^cite_note-Gdn1-269]

#### Syria

Since the US-led [invasion of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Iraq) in March 2003, there are more [refugees from Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_of_Iraq). The [United Nations](31769.md) estimates that nearly 2,200,000 Iraqis have fled the country since 2003,[^cite_note-271] with nearly 100,000 fleeing to [Syria](7515849.md) and [Jordan](7515964.md) each month.[^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273] Most ventured to Jordan and Syria, creating demographic shifts that have worried both governments. Refugees are mired in poverty as they are generally barred from working in their host countries.[^cite_note-274][^cite_note-275] Syrian authorities worried that the new influx of refugees would limit the country's resources. Sources such as oil, heat, water and electricity were said to be becoming scarcer as demand were rising.[^cite_note-276] On 1 October 2007, news agencies reported that Syria reimposed restrictions on Iraqi refugees, as stated by a spokesperson for the [United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees](31964.md). Under Syria's new rules, only Iraqi merchants, businessmen and university professors with visas acquired from Syrian [embassies](8970.md) may enter Syria.[^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278][^cite_note-279]
