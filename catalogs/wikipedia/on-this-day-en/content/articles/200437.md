**Hatra** ([/ˈhætrə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الحَضْر (Arabic pronunciation: [\[al.ˈħadˤr\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic)); [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language): ܚܛܪܐ) was an ancient city in [Upper Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Mesopotamia) located in present-day eastern [Nineveh Governorate](200568.md) in northern [Iraq](7515928.md). The ruins of the city lie 290 km (180 mi) northwest of [Baghdad](4492.md) and 110 km (68 mi) southwest of [Mosul](200475.md). It was a strongly fortified [caravan city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_city) and capital of the [Arab](2185.md) [Kingdom of Hatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hatra),[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] located between the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and [Parthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire)/[Sasanian Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire).

Hatra flourished in the 2nd century AD, and was [destroyed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Hatra) by the [Sassanid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid_Empire) and deserted in the 3rd century AD alongside other Neo-Assyrian populated states such as [Assur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assur), [Adiabene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adiabene), [Osroene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osroene) and [Beth Nuhadra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Nuhadra). Its ruins were discovered in the 19th century.[^cite_note-Schmitt-4] It is considered the richest archaeological site from the [Parthian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire) known to date.[^cite_note-Dirven_2013-5]

## Name

Hatra is known as *_al-Ḥaḍr_* (الحضر) in [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic). It is recorded as 𐣧𐣨𐣣𐣠 (*ḥṭrʾ*, vocalized as: *Ḥaṭrāʾ*) in [Hatran Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatran_Aramaic) inscriptions, probably meaning "enclosure, hedge, fence". In [Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_language), it is usually recorded in the plural form *Ḥaṭrē*. In Roman works, it is recorded as Greek *Átra* and Latin *_Hatra_* and *_Hatris_*.[^cite_note-Schmitt-4]

The temple of the local [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people) god [Shamash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamash),[^cite_note-Kubiak-Schneider-6] was officially called *Beit ʾElāhāʾ* 𐣡𐣩𐣵 𐣠𐣫𐣤𐣠‎ "House of God", in Mesopotamian Aramaic inscriptions[^cite_note-7] The city was recorded as "Enclosure of [Shamash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamash)" (*ḥtrʾ d-šmš* 𐣧𐣨𐣣𐣠 𐣣𐣴𐣬𐣴‎) on a coin.[^cite_note-Schmitt-4]

## History

There is no archeological information on the city before the Parthian period but settlement in the area likely dates back to at least the [Seleucid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire) period.[^cite_note-Schmitt-4] Hatra flourished under the [Parthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire), during the 1st and 2nd centuries AD, as a religious and trading center.[^cite_note-britannica-8] Later on, the city became the capital of possibly the first [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab)-ruled kingdom in the chain of largely [Arab-controlled cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Islamic_Arabia#North_Arabia) running from Hatra, in the northeast, via [Palmyra](46709309.md), [Baalbek](58207.md) and [Petra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra), in the southwest. The region controlled from Hatra was the [Kingdom of Hatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hatra), a semi-autonomous buffer kingdom on the western limits of the Parthian Empire, governed by Arabian princes.

Plan of Hatra

Hatra became an important fortified frontier city and played an important role in the [Second Parthian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman%E2%80%93Parthian_Wars), withstanding repeated attacks by the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire). During the 2nd century AD, the city repulsed sieges by both [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan) (116/117) and [Septimius Severus (198/199).](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Hatra_\(197%E2%80%93198\))[^cite_note-AdvBodyEval-9] Hatra's forces defeated the ascendant [Sassanid Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) in 238 at the battle of [Shahrazoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahrazoor?action=edit\&redlink=1), but fell shortly after in 241 to the army of Sassanid king [Shapur I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapur_I) and was destroyed.[^cite_note-AdvBodyEval-9] The traditional stories of the [fall of Hatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Hatra) tell of [al-Nadirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nadirah), daughter of the king of Araba, who betrayed the city into the hands of Shapur as she fell in love with him. The story tells of how Shapur killed the king and married al-Nadirah, but later had her killed also after realizing her ingratitude towards her father.[^cite_note-britannica-8][^cite_note-10]

Hatra was the best preserved and most informative example of ancient Arabian architecture. Its plan was circular,[^cite_note-11] and was encircled by inner and outer walls nearly 2 kilometres (1.2 mi) in diameter[^cite_note-12] and supported by more than 160 towers.

### Religion

A [temenos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temenos) (τέμενος) surrounded the principal sacred buildings in the city's centre. The temples covered some 1.2 hectares and were dominated by the Great Temple, an enormous structure with [vaults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vault_\(architecture\)) and [columns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column) that once rose to 30 metres. The city was famed for its fusion of [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Ancient_Greece), [Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_religion), [Canaanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaanite_religion), [Aramean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramean) and Arabian [pantheons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantheon_\(gods\)), known in [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic_language) as *_Beiṯ Ĕlāhā_* ("House of God"). The city had temples to [Nergal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nergal) ([Assyrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria)-[Babylonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_mythology) and [Akkadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_Empire)), [Hermes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermes) ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_religion)), [Atargatis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atargatis) ([Syro-Aramaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elohim_\(gods\))), [Allat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allat), [Shamiyyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamiyyah?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_mythology)), and [Shamash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamash) (the [Mesopotamian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_mythology) sun god).[^cite_note-britannica-8] Other deities mentioned in the Hatran Aramaic inscriptions were the Aramaean [Ba'al Shamayn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%60al_Sham%C3%AEm), and the female deity known as [Ashurbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashurbel?action=edit\&redlink=1), which was perhaps the assimilation of the two deities the Assyrian god [Ashur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashur_\(god\)) and the Assyrian-Babylonian [Bel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bel_\(mythology\))—despite their being individually masculine.

## Art of Hatra

According to [John M. Rosenfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_M._Rosenfield), the statuary of Hatra belong to the [Parthian cultural sphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire), with numerous similarities in terms of clothing, decorative elements or posture, which tend to be massive and frontal, with feet often splayed.[^cite_note-JMR-13] The architecture of Hatra itself is generally seen as an example of Parthian architecture.[^cite_note-JMR-13] Similarities can be seen with the [Art of the Kushans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_the_Kushans) as well, due either to direct cultural exchanges between the area of [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia) and the [Kushan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire) at that time, or from a common Parthian artistic background leading to similar types of representation.[^cite_note-JMR-13]

## Modern Hatra



Hatra in the late 1960s. Towering columns and one of the site's preserved ancient temples are seen in the background.

Archaeological site of Hatra before destruction, 0\:59, UNESCO video

Hatra was used as the setting for the opening scene in the 1973 film *[The Exorcist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Exorcist)*,[^cite_note-tele-uk-xrcst-14] and since 1985 has been a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-unesco-hatra-15]

The site was first surveyed by [Walter Andrae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Andrae) of the German excavation team working in Assur from 1906 to 1911. But systematic excavations have been undertaken only from 1951 by Iraqi archeologists. From the 1980s, the Italian Archaeological Expedition,[^cite_note-16] directed by R. Ricciardi Venco ([University of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Turin)), made major discoveries at Hatra. The excavations were focused on an important house ("Building A"[^cite_note-17]), located close to the Temenos, and on deep soundings in the Temenos central area.[^cite_note-18] Now the Expedition is active in different projects regarding the preservation and development of the archaeological site.[^cite_note-19] In 1990, a Polish expedition of the [Polish Centre of Mediterranean Archaeology University of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Centre_of_Mediterranean_Archaeology) recorded and studied the city's defense walls.[^cite_note-20]

In 2004, *[The Daily Telegraph](23797577.md)* stated "Hatra's finely preserved columns and statues make it one of the most impressive of Iraq's archaeological sites"[^cite_note-21]

### Restoration by Saddam Hussein

[Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) saw the site's Mesopotamian history as reflecting glory on himself, and sought to restore the site, and others in [Ninevah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninevah), [Nimrud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimrud), [Ashur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assur) and [Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon), as a symbol of Arab achievement,[^cite_note-22] spending more than US\$80 million in the first phase of restoration of Babylon. Saddam Hussein demanded that new bricks in the restoration use his name (in imitation of [Nebuchadnezzar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebuchadnezzar)) and parts of one restored Hatra temple have Saddam's name.[^cite_note-23]

### Partial destruction and looting by ISIL

Actions by the forces of the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md), which occupied the area in mid-2014, were a major threat to Hatra. In early 2015 they announced their [intention to destroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_cultural_heritage_by_ISIL) many artifacts, claiming that such "graven images" were un-Islamic, encouraged [shirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirk_\(Islam\)) (or polytheism), and could not be permitted to exist, despite the preservation of the site for 1,400 years by various Islamic regimes. [ISIL](9087364.md) militants pledged to destroy the remaining artifacts. Shortly thereafter, they released a video showing the destruction of some artifacts from Hatra.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] After the bulldozing of Nimrud on March 5, 2015, "Hatra of course will be next" said [Abdulamir Hamdani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulamir_Hamdani?action=edit\&redlink=1), an Iraqi archaeologist from [Stony Brook University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Brook_University).[^cite_note-merhi-26] On March 7, Iraqi official sources reported ISIS had begun the demolishing the ruins of Hatra.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-bbc-hatra-28] A video released by ISIL during the next month showed the destruction of the monuments.[^cite_note-29]

UNESCO and [ISESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISESCO) issued a joint statement saying "With this latest act of barbarism against Hatra, (the IS group) shows the contempt in which it holds the history and heritage of Arab people."[^cite_note-30]

The pro-Iraqi government [Popular Mobilization Forces](45671374.md) captured the city on 26 April 2017.[^cite_note-31] A spokeswoman for the militias stated that ISIL had destroyed the sculptures and engraved images of the site, but its walls and towers were still standing though they contained holes and scratches received from ISIL bullets. PMF units also stated that the group had mined the site's eastern gates, thus temporarily preventing any assessment of damage by archaeologists.[^cite_note-32] It was reported on 1 May that the site had suffered less damage than feared earlier. A journalist of [EFE](387067.md) had earlier reported finding many destroyed statues, burnt buildings as well as signs of looting. [Layla Salih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layla_Salih), head of antiquities for Nineveh Governorate, stated that most of the buildings were intact and the destruction didn't compare with that of other archaeological sites of Iraq. A PMF commander also stated that the damage was relatively minor.[^cite_note-33]

## Climate

Hatra has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BSh*). Most rain falls in the winter. The average annual temperature in Hatra is 20.7 °C (69.3 °F). About 257 mm (10.12 in) of precipitation falls annually.
