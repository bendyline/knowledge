# Firearms in Thailand

&#x20;**Firearms in Thailand** refers to the number and types of firearms in the country in civilian (unofficial) hands and the laws and societal norms that govern their possession and use.&#x20;

About one in ten people in [Thailand](30128.md) legally own a gun. There are more than six million registered guns in a country with a population of 66.7 million.[^cite_note-DW-20160219-1] [Small Arms Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Arms_Survey) estimates that the total number of guns, both licit and illicit, held by Thai civilians in 2017 is 10,300,000,[^cite_note-SAS-2018-2] equating to 15.1 firearms per 100 inhabitants. Figures for the other [ASEAN](106539.md) nations are Cambodia, 4.5 per 100 inhabitants; Philippines, 3.6; Laos, 3.0; Myanmar, 1.6; Vietnam, 1.6; Brunei, 1.4; Malaysia, 0.7; Singapore, 0.3; and Indonesia, zero.[^cite_note-3]

Ownership of firearms is particularly high in the [southern provinces](1453076.md) of [Pattani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattani_province), [Yala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yala_province), and [Narathiwat](241795.md) that have witnessed significant [insurgency and rebellion since 2004](2326869.md).[^cite_note-DW-20160219-1]

## Gun laws

The Thai laws applicable to firearms regulation are the [Firearms, Ammunition, Explosives, Fireworks, and Imitation Firearms Act, B.E. 2490](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firearms_Act_\(Thailand\)) (1947)[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-TLF-2011-5] and *Amendments to the Act Controlling Firearms, Ammunition, Explosives, Fireworks and Imitation of Firearms, B.E. 2490* (2017).[^cite_note-6]

Gun ownership in Thailand is restricted to Thai citizens.[^cite_note-7]

## Violent gun deaths

In 2016 Thailand's rate of violent gun-related deaths stood at 4.45 deaths per 100,000 inhabitants. In comparison, that of the Philippines was 7.42; the US, 3.85; Cambodia, 0.96; Myanmar, 0.56; Malaysia, 0.46; Indonesia, 0.10; and Singapore, 0.03.[^cite_note-8]
