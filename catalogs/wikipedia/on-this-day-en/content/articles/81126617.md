"**The Fate of Ophelia**" is a song by the American singer-songwriter [Taylor Swift](5422144.md) and the lead single from her twelfth studio album, *[The Life of a Showgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Life_of_a_Showgirl)*. The song was released on October 3, 2025, through [Republic Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Records). Swift wrote and produced "The Fate of Ophelia" with [Max Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Martin) and [Shellback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellback_\(music_producer\)). It is a [dance-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance-pop), [synth-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synth-pop), and [funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk) song with a [new wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_wave_music) [groove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groove_\(music\)). The track opens with a [drum roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_roll) and [minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_chord) piano chords, progressing into an upbeat arrangement of [steel guitars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_guitar), [synthesizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesizer), and [Omnichords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnichord), over a driving [bassline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassline). Lyrically, Swift was inspired by [Ophelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophelia), a character who drowns due to madness from grief and romantic rejection in [William Shakespeare](32897.md)'s tragedy *[Hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet)*. The song addresses a soulmate who saves Swift from a destiny of death like that of Ophelia.

Music critics generally praised "The Fate of Ophelia" for its literary influences and opined that the production choices resulted in a [catchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catchy) melody, while some took issue with the lyrics' conventional tropes and interpretation of Shakespeare's Ophelia. Commercially, the single became [the most-streamed song in a day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Spotify_streaming_records#Most-streamed_songs_in_a_single_day) and [a week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Spotify_streaming_records#Most-streamed_songs_in_a_single_week) on the streaming platform [Spotify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify). It peaked atop the [*Billboard* Global 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Global_200) and became Swift's first number-one single in Austria, Denmark, Germany, Norway, Spain, and the Netherlands. In the United States, "The Fate of Ophelia" spent 10 weeks atop the [*Billboard* Hot 100](423161.md), marking Swift's longest-running number-one song. It has been [certified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_recording_certification) multi-platinum in Australia, Belgium, Canada, New Zealand, Portugal, and the United Kingdom, diamond in France, and triple diamond in Brazil.

Swift wrote and directed the song's music video, which premiered as part of the album's promotional film, *[The Official Release Party of a Showgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Official_Release_Party_of_a_Showgirl)*. The video draws on various historical, cultural, and artistic inspirations of female performers, portraying Swift as a [showgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showgirl) across different periods of time and incorporating hints at other songs from *The Life of a Showgirl*. She worked with the cinematographer [Rodrigo Prieto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_Prieto), the choreographer [Mandy Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandy_Moore_\(choreographer\)), and the production designer [Ethan Tobman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethan_Tobman) on the video, and cast her band and dance crew from [the Eras Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Eras_Tour)—the tour that inspired the album. It won [Video of the Year](476903.md) at the [2026 MTV Video Music Awards](82993690.md).

## Background and release

The American singer-songwriter [Taylor Swift](5422144.md) created her twelfth studio album, *[The Life of a Showgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Life_of_a_Showgirl)*, to reflect her triumphant state of mind amidst the success of [the Eras Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Eras_Tour) and her romantic relationship with the [American football](18951490.md) player [Travis Kelce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Kelce) throughout 2024.[^cite_note-1] She announced the album during the August 13, 2025, episode of Travis and [Jason Kelce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Kelce)'s podcast *[New Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Heights)*; "The Fate of Ophelia" was revealed as the opening track.[^cite_note-2] During the episode, Swift discussed [William Shakespeare](32897.md)'s tragedy *[Hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet)*, specifically the descent into madness and death by drowning of the character [Ophelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophelia), which influenced the album's cover artwork.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-Trowbridge-2025-5]

"The Fate of Ophelia" was released as the lead single from *The Life of a Showgirl*. On October 3, 2025, concurrently with the album's release, [Universal Music Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Music_Group) released the song to [radio airplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_airplay) in Italy,[^cite_note-Mompellio-2025-6] and its music video premiered as part of the film *[Taylor Swift: The Official Release Party of a Showgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift:_The_Official_Release_Party_of_a_Showgirl)*, before being released onto [YouTube](3524766.md) two days later.[^cite_note-7] In the United States, Republic Records released the song to [hot adult contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_adult_contemporary) radio on October 6 and to [contemporary hit radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_hit_radio) on October 7.[^cite_note-us-8] Throughout October, "The Fate of Ophelia" was released on limited-time [CD singles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD_single) and download (including the original, acoustic, and instrumental versions).[^cite_note-cd1-9][^cite_note-cd2-10][^cite_note-instrumental1-11][^cite_note-instrumental2-12] In December, the single was released on [7-inch vinyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-inch_vinyl).[^cite_note-vinyl-13] Four alternate versions of the song were released: an acoustic "Alone in My Tower" version,[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] and three remixes by [Loud Luxury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loud_Luxury),[^cite_note-16] [the Chainsmokers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chainsmokers),[^cite_note-Dailey-2025-17] and Telykast and XanTz.[^cite_note-telykastmix-18] All three remixes were accompanied by extended versions.[^cite_note-loudextended-19][^cite_note-chainremix2-20][^cite_note-txer-21]

## Production and music

Swift wrote and produced "The Fate of Ophelia" with [Max Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Martin) and [Shellback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellback_\(music_producer\)). The track was recorded at Shellback Studios and produced at [MXM Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MXM_Studios), both in [Stockholm](26741.md), Sweden.[^cite_note-LinerNotes-22] At 3 minutes and 46 seconds,[^cite_note-origdig-23] "The Fate of Ophelia" is a [dance-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance-pop),[^cite_note-Hunter-Tilney-2025-24][^cite_note-25] [synth-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synth-pop),[^cite_note-Sherman-2025-26][^cite_note-Rolling_Stone-2025-27] and [funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk) track,[^cite_note-28] with a [new wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_wave_music) [groove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groove_\(music\)).[^cite_note-Wood-2025-29] The production begins with a [drum roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_roll) and minor-key piano chords[^cite_note-Georgi-2025-30][^cite_note-The_New_York_Times-2025-31] and progresses into an upbeat production driven by cascading [synthesizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesizer), [steel guitars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_guitar), [Omnichords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnichord), and a driving [bassline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassline).[^cite_note-Georgi-2025-30][^cite_note-Powers-2025-32][^cite_note-33] Swift's singing, mostly in her lower [register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Register_\(music\)) in the verses,[^cite_note-Hague-2025-34] is assisted by [reverb effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverb_effect).[^cite_note-Powers-2025-32]

Critics compared the song to the music of other artists and Swift's past works. Some reviews likened "The Fate of Ophelia" to the music of [Fleetwood Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleetwood_Mac);[^cite_note-Georgi-2025-30][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] *[Slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_\(magazine\))*'s Chris Molanphy wrote that the opening drum roll evoked their song "[Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreams_\(Fleetwood_Mac_song\))" (1977).[^cite_note-Molanphy-2025-37] According to *[Clash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clash_\(magazine\))*'s Lauren Hague, the song is a 2020s take on 2000s [funk-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk-pop), reminiscent of [Duffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duffy_\(singer\))'s "[Mercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy_\(Duffy_song\))" (2008) and [Adele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adele)'s "[Rumour Has It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rumour_Has_It_\(Adele_song\))" (2011).[^cite_note-Hague-2025-34] Mikael Wood of the *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* wrote that the song evoked the British new wave duo [Eurythmics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurythmics),[^cite_note-Wood-2025-29] and [Ann Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Powers) of [NPR](29697232.md) compared some production elements to those in [Stevie Nicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevie_Nicks)'s "[Stand Back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_Back)" (1983), [Gorillaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorillaz)'s "[Clint Eastwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Eastwood_\(song\))" (2001), and Swift's "[Wildest Dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildest_Dreams)" (2015).[^cite_note-Powers-2025-32] Giselle Au-Nhien Nguyen of *[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald)* opined that the track has a "darker tone" that recalls Swift's 2017 album *[Reputation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reputation_\(album\))*.[^cite_note-38] Meanwhile, [Jon Pareles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Pareles) from *[The New York Times](30680.md)* likened the minor-key piano sounds to the production of her 2020 album *[Folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore_\(Taylor_Swift_album\))*.[^cite_note-The_New_York_Times-2025-31]

## Lyrics

Painting of Laertes and Ophelia from Hamlet

The [bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_\(music\)) references Act 1, scene iii of [William Shakespeare](32897.md)'s *[Hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet)*, where Ophelia confides in her brother [Laertes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laertes_\(Hamlet\)) (here portrayed in [W. G. Wills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._G._Wills)'s c. 1880 painting).

The lyrics of "The Fate of Ophelia" were influenced by Swift and Kelce's relationship.[^cite_note-Savage-2025-39] According to Swift, the idea for the track came while she was in the studio with Martin and Shellback, scrolling through her list of song ideas on her smartphone. As Shellback was playing a "really cool [chord progression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chord_progression)", Swift came across the word "Ophelia" in her list and imagined a scenario where Ophelia did not go insane and die, but instead met someone who treated her well. From there, she conceived the [hook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hook_\(music\)), "You saved my heart from the fate of Ophelia."[^cite_note-Yahr-2025-40]

In the song, Swift's narrator pledges loyalty to a man who saved her from a fate of "insanity", "drowning", "deception", "purgatory", and ultimately death, similar to that of Ophelia.[^cite_note-Savage-2025-39][^cite_note-Zoladz-2025-41][^cite_note-Puckett-Pope-2025-42] Ophelia's tragedy is referenced in the second verse: "The eldest daughter of a nobleman/ Ophelia lived in fantasy/ But love was a cold bed full of scorpions/ The venom stole her sanity."[^cite_note-Trowbridge-2025-5] The [refrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refrain) depicts Swift's narrator as "alone in the tower", waiting for her suitor to come.[^cite_note-Trowbridge-2025-5] He then "dug her out of \[her] grave", which rescued her from death.[^cite_note-Yahr-2025-40] The [bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_\(music\)) ("'Tis locked inside my memory/ And only you possess the key") references Act 1, scene iii of *Hamlet*, where Ophelia tells her brother [Laertes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laertes_\(Hamlet\)): "Tis in my memory locked, and you yourself shall keep the key of it."[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

According to various publications, the song contains real-life references to Kelce, including his career as a footballer, his public declaration of affection for Swift, and his frequent use of the phrase "keep it one hundred".[^cite_note-Puckett-Pope-2025-42][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] Some interpretations of "The Fate of Ophelia" likened its narrative to that of Swift's "[Love Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Story_\(Taylor_Swift_song\))" (2008), which was inspired by another Shakespearean tragedy, *[Romeo and Juliet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romeo_and_Juliet).* Both songs depict happy romantic endings, contrary to Shakespeare's conclusions;[^cite_note-Breihan-2025-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] Swift herself acknowledged that this was the second time, after "Love Story", that she attempted to rewrite a Shakespearean ending.[^cite_note-Zoladz-2025-41] According to Serena Trowbridge, a scholar of [Victorian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_literature), "The Fate of Ophelia" was possibly also inspired by the [Pre-Raphaelite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Raphaelite) model and poet [Elizabeth Siddal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Siddal), who was the muse for [John Everett Millais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Everett_Millais)'s early 1850s painting *[Ophelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophelia_\(Millais\))*, which influenced *The Life of a Showgirl*'s cover artwork.[^cite_note-Trowbridge-2025-5][^cite_note-50]

## Critical reception

"The Fate of Ophelia" received generally positive reviews for its literary inspiration and production.[^cite_note-51] Multiple critics selected the song as a highlight on *The Life of a Showgirl*,[^cite_note-Sherman-2025-26][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] most of whom described the composition and production as clever that resulted in a [catchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catchy) melody.[^cite_note-Wood-2025-29][^cite_note-Savage-2025-39][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* ranked "The Fate of Ophelia" eighth on their list of the 100 best songs of 2025,[^cite_note-Rolling_Stone-2025-27] and *[Billboard](18309966.md)* placed it at number 14 on their year-end list.[^cite_note-56] Powers, Ludovic Hunter-Tilney of the *[Financial Times](136566.md)*, and Jason Lipshutz of *Billboard* complimented the transition and build-up from the piano to the bassline.[^cite_note-Hunter-Tilney-2025-24][^cite_note-Powers-2025-32][^cite_note-Lipshutz-2025-57] Lipshutz ranked it the second-best song of the album behind "[Ruin the Friendship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruin_the_Friendship)".[^cite_note-Lipshutz-2025-57]

Maya Georgi of *Rolling Stone* commended the "tantalizing" melody and "wondrous" combination of instruments,[^cite_note-Georgi-2025-30] while Matthew Dwyer of *[PopMatters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PopMatters)* highlighted the dynamic composition and "glamorous tension".[^cite_note-58] Georgi and Hague opined that the track embraced a new musical direction for Swift.[^cite_note-Georgi-2025-30][^cite_note-Hague-2025-34] Swift's vocals were also a subject of praise: Hague opined that her lower register enhanced the track,[^cite_note-Hague-2025-34] Hunter-Tilney described her singing as having "characteristic commitment and charisma",[^cite_note-Hunter-Tilney-2025-24] and Powers opined that her voice "has never sounded stronger", assisted by reverb that made her sound "massive and intimate at the same time".[^cite_note-Powers-2025-32] On a less enthusiastic side, Spencer Kornhaber of *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic)* commented that the song was "relatively strong, if unexpectedly downcast".[^cite_note-59] Molanphy said that although "The Fate of Ophelia" was Swift's "most banger-adjacent" single in the 2020s, it still contained a "broodiness" that more resembled *Folklore* than her 2010s "frothy, peppy bops".[^cite_note-Molanphy-2025-37]

The reception of the lyrics was not as uniformly positive. *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)*'s Roisin O'Connor dubbed "The Fate of Ophelia" a sublime song for its "literary flair",[^cite_note-60] and Hague commented that the track exhibited Swift's signature confessional songwriting with poetic verses and a [tongue-in-cheek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongue-in-cheek) refrain.[^cite_note-Hague-2025-34] Comparing it to "Love Story", Tom Breihan of *[Stereogum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereogum)* wrote that both songs "\[miss] the entire point" of the Shakespearean tragedies, but "that kind of writerly excess" still turned out engaging and "fucking rules".[^cite_note-Breihan-2025-47] In a mixed review, Carlos Marcos of *[El País](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Pa%C3%ADs)* praised the song's "original" production but contended that it was burdened by the "plaintive" lyrics.[^cite_note-61] [Will Hodgkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Hodgkinson) of *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* opined that although the lyrics were poetic, the analogy comparing Swift to Ophelia "doesn't really work".[^cite_note-62]

Some reviewers debated the progressive nature of the lyrics. Hunter-Tilney wrote that the lyrics conflating "doomed Ophelia with a *[Rapunzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapunzel)*-style figure waiting in a tower for a heroic lover" felt misplaced.[^cite_note-Hunter-Tilney-2025-24] In congruence, *The New York Times'* Lindsay Zoladz commented that it was dispiriting that Swift reduced Ophelia to "just another princess waiting for her [Prince Charming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Charming)".[^cite_note-Zoladz-2025-41] In a more moderate take, Trowbridge opined that the narrative of a woman waiting for a rescuer was not progressive, but it helped explore "the public face of women" and their painful experiences in the context of the album as a whole.[^cite_note-Trowbridge-2025-5] In *[Psychology Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychology_Today)*, the psychotherapist Whitney Coulson wrote that "The Fate of Ophelia" captures the "emotional alchemy" of female pain in the context of [patriarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchy) and popular culture, providing psychological insight into "how easily a woman's pain is rewritten as instability" through the use of literary archetypes.[^cite_note-63]

## Commercial performance

On the streaming platform [Spotify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotify), "The Fate of Ophelia" broke the records for [the highest single-day streams and the highest single-week streams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Spotify_streaming_records#Most-streamed_songs_in_a_single_day), becoming the first song to accumulate 30 million streams in a single day.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] The single topped the [*Billboard* Global 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Global_200) chart for seven weeks.[^cite_note-66] It peaked atop the record charts of over 26 territories,[^cite_note-Thomas-2025-67] including Belgium (Flanders),[^cite_note-Flanders-68] Canada,[^cite_note-69] Iceland,[^cite_note-Iceland-70] Ireland,[^cite_note-Ireland-71] New Zealand,[^cite_note-New_Zealand-72] Portugal,[^cite_note-Portugal-73] Singapore,[^cite_note-Singapore-74] the Philippines,[^cite_note-75] and the United Arab Emirates.[^cite_note-MENAchart-76]

"The Fate of Ophelia" marked Swift's first number-one single in Austria,[^cite_note-Austria-77] Denmark,[^cite_note-Denmark-78] Germany,[^cite_note-DE-79] Spain,[^cite_note-Spain-80] and the Netherlands.[^cite_note-Netherlands-81] In France, it debuted at number four and became her highest-charting solo single, surpassing "[Shake It Off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shake_It_Off)" (2014).[^cite_note-82] "The Fate of Ophelia" spent 15 weeks at number one in Canada;[^cite_note-CAN-83] 14 weeks in Switzerland;[^cite_note-Switzerland-84] 13 weeks in Germany;[^cite_note-DE-79] 12 weeks in Austria;[^cite_note-Austria-77] 10 weeks in Norway and the Netherlands;[^cite_note-Norway-85][^cite_note-Netherlands-81] nine weeks in Ireland;[^cite_note-86] and eight weeks in Belgium (Flanders).[^cite_note-Flanders-68] In the United Kingdom, "The Fate of Ophelia" debuted atop the [UK singles chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_singles_chart) with 132,000 first-week units, registering the largest first-week sales of the year and of Swift's career.[^cite_note-87] It spent seven non-consecutive weeks atop the chart, becoming her longest-running number-one song in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-88] The single has been certified triple diamond in Brazil;[^cite_note-PMB-89] diamond in France;[^cite_note-SNEP-90] quadruple platinum in Portugal;[^cite_note-AFP-91] triple platinum in Canada[^cite_note-MC-92] and New Zealand;[^cite_note-RMNZ-93] double platinum in Australia,[^cite_note-ARIA-94] Belgium,[^cite_note-BRMA-95] Spain,[^cite_note-PROMUSICAE-96] and the United Kingdom;[^cite_note-BPI-97] and platinum in Austria,[^cite_note-IFPIAT-98] Denmark,[^cite_note-IFPIDEN-99] Germany,[^cite_note-BVMI-100] Italy,[^cite_note-FIMI-101] and Poland.[^cite_note-ZPAV-102]

In the United States, "The Fate of Ophelia" became Swift's 13th number-one single on the [*Billboard* Hot 100](423161.md), debuting atop the chart with 92.5 million on-demand streams.[^cite_note-103] It spent 10 non-consecutive weeks at number one on the Hot 100, becoming her longest-running number-one song.[^cite_note-104] After being made available for digital downloads, the single became Swift's record-extending 30th number-one song on the [Digital Song Sales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Song_Sales).[^cite_note-105] On the [US airplay charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_charts), "The Fate of Ophelia" debuted in the top 10 of [Pop Airplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_Airplay), [Adult Pop Airplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_Pop_Airplay), and [Adult Contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_Contemporary_\(chart\)). On Pop Airplay, it became the first song in history to debut in the top-10 region and subsequently became Swift's record-extending 14th number-one single, spending five weeks at number one.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] The track spent four weeks at number one on Adult Pop Airplay.[^cite_note-Adult_Pop-108]

## Music video

### Production and synopsis

Screenshot of a music video showing Taylor Swift and dancers in red bodysuits performing in a dressing room

Swift as a [showgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showgirl) in the music video for "The Fate of Ophelia", featuring the same dancers from [the Eras Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Eras_Tour)

Swift wrote and directed the music video for "The Fate of Ophelia", which features dancers from the Eras Tour. On the video, she worked with the choreographer [Mandy Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandy_Moore_\(choreographer\)) and the production designer [Ethan Tobman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethan_Tobman), both of whom had collaborated on the tour, as well as the cinematographer [Rodrigo Prieto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_Prieto).[^cite_note-Willman-2025-109][^cite_note-Zuckerman-2025-110] The music video is played twice during *The Official Release Party of a Showgirl*, which also features behind-the-scenes footage from the production process.[^cite_note-Gleiberman-2025-111] Rehearsals took place within over three weeks,[^cite_note-Pascuale-2025-112] and filming for the opening scene and the dressing room scenes took place at the [Los Angeles Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Theatre).[^cite_note-113]

The music video opens inside the foyer of the Los Angeles Theatre before panning to a painting on the wall, the first of many depictions of various [showgirls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showgirl) from different historical times.[^cite_note-West-2025-114] Swift starts as a model for a Pre-Raphaelite portrait of Ophelia, in a vein similar to the *[Ophelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophelia_\(Heyser\))*, an [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau) painting by [Friedrich Heyser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Heyser).[^cite_note-115] Swift is then portrayed as a blonde [burlesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlesque) performer in the style of [Marilyn Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marilyn_Monroe), a 1960s [Ronnie Spector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronnie_Spector)-like [go-go dancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go-go_dancer), a stage actress similar to [Sarah Bernhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Bernhardt), and an [Esther Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esther_Williams)-type actress in a musical number in the style of [Busby Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busby_Berkeley).[^cite_note-Willman-2025-109][^cite_note-Zuckerman-2025-110][^cite_note-Gleiberman-2025-111][^cite_note-Pascuale-2025-112]

Later, Swift wears a dress made of rope and resembles [Elizabeth Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Taylor) before the scene transitions to a [Bob Mackie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Mackie)-style showgirl routine, and finally to a pop star at an afterparty in a hotel room.[^cite_note-Pascuale-2025-112][^cite_note-West-2025-114] The video ends with Swift half-submerged in a bathtub, an image featured on the album cover for *The Life of a Showgirl*.[^cite_note-Zuckerman-2025-110] The video features [Easter eggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_egg_\(media\)) referencing other tracks from *The Life of a Showgirl*[^cite_note-West-2025-114][^cite_note-Elle-MV"-116] and moments from the Eras Tour, as well as Kelce.[^cite_note-Pascuale-2025-112] [Vancouver](32706.md)'s [Science World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_World_\(Vancouver\)) makes a brief appearance near the end of the video.[^cite_note-117]

### Reception

The music video trended at the number one spot on YouTube, receiving 25 million views within three days.[^cite_note-118] Journalists praised the spectacle and production value of the music video. *[Vogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_\(magazine\))* called the video a "true visual feast", highlighting the costumes and detailed production design.[^cite_note-119] *Elle* and *[The Times of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_India)* praised the nods to classical art and pop-cultural references.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-Elle-MV"-116] *Rolling Stone* highlighted how the video leans into theatrical imagery and costume transformations, emphasizing visual flair that matches *The Life of a Showgirl* theme.[^cite_note-121] *[Deadline Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadline_Hollywood)* commended Swift's direction and visual storytelling.[^cite_note-122]

Painting of Ophelia from Hamlet by Friedrich Heyser

Fans visited the [Museum Wiesbaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Wiesbaden) in Germany to view the c. 1900 painting *[Ophelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ophelia_\(Heyser\))* by [Friedrich Heyser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Heyser) that inspired the music video.

The video caused Swift's fans, the [Swifties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swifties), to visit the [Museum Wiesbaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Wiesbaden) in [Wiesbaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiesbaden), Germany, to view Heyser's *Ophelia*.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125] [Timon Gremmels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timon_Gremmels), the [minister of science and research, arts and culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hessian_Ministry_of_Higher_Education,_Research_and_the_Arts) of the German state [Hesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesse), expressed his delight over the attention the painting has received: "When a music video gets people to visit a museum, it shows that culture works – on all levels", and extended an official invitation to Swift to visit Wiesbaden.[^cite_note-126] On November 2, 2025, the museum hosted an event featuring a guided tour of *Ophelia,* for which tickets sold out in a few hours.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] Attendees wearing outfits inspired by Swift or Ophelia received free admission.[^cite_note-129] *[Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parade_\(magazine\))* reported a December 2025 study showing that the internet search for "Ophelia" peaked 1,231 percent above normal, while "Hamlet" experienced a 66 percent spike in interest.[^cite_note-130]

The choreography created a [viral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_video) trend on social media, where fans and creators imitated Swift's dance moves.[^cite_note-131] Among those who attempted the choreography were the [Prime Minister of Australia](24117.md), [Anthony Albanese](556745.md), on the [Melbourne](17306237.md) radio station [Nova 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_100),[^cite_note-132] and the Indian parliamentarian and author [Shashi Tharoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashi_Tharoor).[^cite_note-133]

## In popular culture

Journalists described the release and success of "The Fate of Ophelia" as a cultural phenomenon. Sunil Thomas of *[The Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Week_\(Indian_magazine\))* opined that the "unprecedented" global phenomenon was spurred by its "high art" references, inspiration from 1960s stage productions, and its adult contemporary musical composition. He also highlighted its viral dance on Instagram, YouTube, and [TikTok](56822861.md).[^cite_note-Thomas-2025-67]

The October 18, 2025, episode of the American late-night show *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* included a [sketch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sketch_comedy) featuring a [parody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parody) of "The Fate of Ophelia", performed by the American singer [Sabrina Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabrina_Carpenter), actress [Chloe Fineman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloe_Fineman), and comedians [Marcello Hernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcello_Hern%C3%A1ndez) and [Andrew Dismukes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Dismukes).[^cite_note-149] The November 8 episode of the British dance contest show [*Strictly Come Dancing* series 23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strictly_Come_Dancing_series_23) featured a [tango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tango) performance by the British television personality [Vicky Pattison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicky_Pattison) and choreographer [Kai Widdrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Widdrington) to "The Fate of Ophelia".[^cite_note-150]

The song featured on social media posts by [NATO](21133.md)[^cite_note-151] and the [British royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_royal_family).[^cite_note-152] On November 4, 2025, the official [White House](33057.md) TikTok account posted a patriotic video of the U.S. president [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s [administration](78298108.md) with "The Fate of Ophelia", featuring images of the [US flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_flag) and [military](32212.md), despite Trump's anti-Swift stance. Responding to *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))*'s request for comment, a White House official said: "We made this video because we knew fake news media brands like *Variety* would breathlessly amplify them. Congrats, you got played." The video received backlash from social media users and Swifties.[^cite_note-153][^cite_note-154]

In December 2025, [AI-generated songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI-generated_songs) featuring a translated version of "The Fate of Ophelia" in the Portuguese language, named "A Sina de Ofélia", with voices resembling a duet by [Luísa Sonza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%ADsa_Sonza) and [Dilsinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilsinho), went viral.[^cite_note-155] It entered the top 50 of Spotify's Brazilian chart before being removed from streaming platforms.[^cite_note-156] This brought some legal debates about music generated by artificial intelligence and ownership rights.[^cite_note-157] Under the title "Todo Tempo Sozinha Nessa Torre", it peaked at number 85 on the [Brasil Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brasil_Hot_100).[^cite_note-158]
