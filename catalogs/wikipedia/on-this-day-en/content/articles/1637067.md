**Gregory Jack Biffle** (December 23, 1969 – December 18, 2025), nicknamed "**the Biff**", was an American professional [stock car racing](28349.md) driver. He most notably raced from [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_NASCAR_Winston_Cup_Series) to [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NASCAR_Cup_Series) in the [NASCAR Cup Series](56571945.md), most notably driving the No. 16 [Ford](30433662.md) for [Roush Fenway Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roush_Fenway_Racing) from [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_NASCAR_Winston_Cup_Series) to [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NASCAR_Sprint_Cup_Series) and last competed part-time in the [ARCA Menards Series West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARCA_Menards_Series_West), driving the No. 23 and No. 24 [Chevrolet SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_SS) for [Sigma Performance Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Performance_Services) in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_ARCA_Menards_Series_West).

After racing in the NASCAR [Winter Heat Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Heat_Series) in the mid-1990s, he was recommended to [Jack Roush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Roush) by former racer and announcer [Benny Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Parsons). With [Roush Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roush_Racing), he was the [1998 NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series) [Rookie of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series#Rookie_of_the_Year). He won the [2000 Craftsman Truck championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series). He reprised this progression in the [NASCAR Busch Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Busch_Series), winning the [2001 Rookie of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_NASCAR_Busch_Series), immediately followed by winning the [2002 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_NASCAR_Busch_Series). Biffle drove in the [NASCAR Sprint Cup Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Sprint_Cup_Series) for Roush from [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_NASCAR_Winston_Cup_Series) until [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NASCAR_Sprint_Cup_Series), winning 19 races in the No. 16 Ford.

Biffle, who began his NASCAR career in 1995, was the first of only three drivers that have won a championship in both the [Busch Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_O'Reilly_Auto_Parts_Series) and the [Craftsman Truck Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series), and the sixth of only thirty-six drivers to win a race in each of NASCAR's three national series.&#x20;

After retirement, Biffle returned to NASCAR with a Truck Series start for [Kyle Busch Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Busch_Motorsports) in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_NASCAR_Gander_Outdoors_Truck_Series), and for [GMS Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMS_Racing) in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_NASCAR_Gander_RV_&_Outdoors_Truck_Series). Biffle was [killed in an airplane crash](81871726.md) in [Statesville, North Carolina](127986.md), in December 2025, along with all other passengers and crew of the plane, which included his family, which consisted of himself, his wife, Christina, and their two children, Emma, age 14, and Ryder, age 5.

## Racing career

Biffle was born on December 23, 1969 in [Vancouver, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver,_Washington),[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] and grew up in [Camas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camas,_Washington).[^cite_note-4] He began his racing career driving on [short tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_track_racing) around the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest). He first gained attention as a driver when he raced in the nationally televised [Winter Heat Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Heat_Series) in the winter of 1995–1996. Biffle dominated the series championship that winter, leading former ESPN announcer and NASCAR champion, [Benny Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Parsons), to recommend the driver to [Jack Roush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Roush).[^cite_note-5]

Biffle entered the first two races of the [1996 NASCAR Winston West Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_NASCAR_Winston_West_Series), finishing thirtieth at [Tucson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucson_Speedway) and fourth at [Altamont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altamont_Raceway_Park). His debut in one of NASCAR's national divisions came later that year when he ran the final two [Busch Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationwide_Series) races of the season. Driving a [Chevrolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet) for [Dick Bown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Bown), he finished twenty-third at [Rockingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockingham_Speedway) but lost an engine the following race at [Homestead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead%E2%80%93Miami_Speedway) and finished thirty-sixth. In 1997, Biffle competed in the now-defunct [NASCAR Northwest Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_AutoZone_Elite_Division,_Northwest_Series) and won the Most Popular Driver Award.[^cite_note-6]

### Truck Series

Roush Racing promoted Biffle to a full-time driver in the [Craftsman Truck Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series) in 1998. Despite not winning a race that season, his four pole positions are the most by a Truck Series rookie to date, and they helped him earn an 8th-place finish in the final standings and the [Rookie of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Rookie_of_the_Year) Award. He followed it up with a stellar [1999 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series) in which he recorded nine wins, the single-season Truck Series record that stood (until [Corey Heim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Heim) broke his record with 12 wins in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series)). He finished second in the final standings, just eight points behind champion [Jack Sprague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sprague). In [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_NASCAR_Craftsman_Truck_Series), Biffle won the [Truck Series title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NASCAR_Truck_Series_champions) with another five-win season, beating his Roush teammate [Kurt Busch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Busch) by 230 points. It was Biffle's first championship in one of NASCAR's three major series. It was announced that Biffle would move up to the Busch Series for 2001; however, he ran four more Truck races for Roush that season and won at Phoenix. Biffle made a Truck Series start in 2004 for another long-time Ford team, [Circle Bar Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_Bar_Racing), at Homestead.



Biffle ahead of [Matt Crafton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Crafton) at [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Motor_Speedway) in 2019

On March 28, 2019, Biffle announced he would be testing with [Kyle Busch Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Busch_Motorsports) the next day at [Texas Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Motor_Speedway) in the No. 51 truck. He was eventually tabbed to drive the truck for the [SpeedyCash.com 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_SpeedyCash.com_400) at Texas.[^cite_note-7] Biffle started sixth and won in his series return, leading 18 laps and holding off [Matt Crafton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Crafton) while winning \$50,000 in a promotion with [Gander Outdoors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gander_Outdoors); it was his first Truck victory since 2001.[^cite_note-8]

On August 27, 2020, it was announced that Biffle would make another one-off start in the Truck Series, this time in the No. 24 for [GMS Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMS_Racing) at [Darlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_South_Carolina_Education_Lottery_200). He went on to finish nineteenth.[^cite_note-9]

### Nationwide Series

Biffle joined the Busch Series full-time in 2001 and won the Rookie of the Year Award with five wins and a fourth-place finish in the final standings. The following season, he won another four races and earned 20 top-five finishes out of 34 races en route to his first Busch Series title and the second NASCAR national championship of his career.

Biffle ran only part-time in Busch competition in 2003 as Roush moved him up to a full-time ride in the [Winston Cup Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprint_Cup_Series) for that season, but he returned to contend for the Busch Series championship again in 2004. He placed third in the standings behind [Martin Truex Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Truex_Jr.) and [Kyle Busch](1467805.md). From 2005 to 2009, Biffle raced part-time for Roush Fenway Racing in the Busch (now O'Reilly Auto Parts Series) every year. He won twice in 2009, at [Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Motor_Speedway) and [Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_International_Raceway), after going winless the previous two seasons. Biffle returned to the Nationwide Series in 2010, driving the No. 27 Ford for [Baker Curb Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_Curb_Racing).[^cite_note-10]

### Cup Series



Biffle racing at [Texas Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Motor_Speedway) in 2005

Biffle began his Cup Series career in the 2002 season. He attempted to qualify in a Roush Ford for the [2002 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Daytona_500) but failed to make the race. He would make his first Cup debut nine races later at [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auto_Club_Speedway), a race in which he finished thirteenth. That was his best finish in seven races that year as he also drove four in a Chevrolet for [Andy Petree Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Petree_Racing) and two in a Dodge for [Petty Enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petty_Enterprises).

Biffle began competing full-time in NASCAR's top division in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_NASCAR_Winston_Cup_Series), with sponsorship from [W. W. Grainger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._W._Grainger), who had previously sponsored him in the Busch and Truck Series. He earned his first win in the [Pepsi 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coke_Zero_400) at [Daytona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytona_International_Speedway) that season and finished second to [Jamie McMurray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_McMurray) (who would later join him as a teammate at Roush) for Raybestos Rookie of the Year. His Daytona win made him the first Winston Cup winner to succeed in all three of NASCAR's national series in succession (as opposed to others who won in the Busch or Truck Series after graduating to Winston Cup); a feat which has since become quite common for young stock car drivers climbing the ladder. Biffle placed twentieth in the final points standings. Biffle made an immediate impact in his sophomore season in 2004, earning the pole in the Daytona 500. However, Biffle was forced to start at the rear due to an engine change. Despite missing NASCAR's first-ever [Chase for the NEXTEL Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_for_the_Sprint_Cup), Biffle won twice that season, at [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_International_Speedway) and Homestead en route to a 17th-place finish in the final points standings.

2005 was Biffle's breakout season. He won six races (at California Speedway, Texas, Darlington, Dover, and Michigan along with the season finale at Homestead), the most of any driver that year, and qualified for the Chase for the first time in his career, bringing home a second place finish in the standings, 35 points behind champion [Tony Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Stewart); Biffle tied with his teammate [Carl Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Edwards) in points but won the tie-breaker based on race wins.

Biffle regressed in 2006, missing the Chase for the Cup despite winning twice at [Darlington Raceway](685467.md) and [Homestead-Miami Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead%E2%80%93Miami_Speedway) (both of which were tracks at which he had also won in the previous season). He finished thirteenth in the standings, third-best of the drivers not to qualify for the Chase. He also missed the Chase the following year, in a season marred by the No. 16 team's new primary sponsor [Ameriquest Mortgage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ameriquest_Mortgage) suffering financial difficulty and having to sell off a number of its race sponsorships. Biffle won only one race in 2007, at [Kansas Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_Speedway). As Biffle was doing burnouts on the track, third place [Jimmie Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Johnson) and second place [Clint Bowyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Bowyer) accused Biffle of not maintaining speed under a final lap caution, but this was denied by NASCAR, which said Biffle had pace car speed.

In 2007, Biffle finished 14th in the standings, second-best of the non-Chase drivers as the Chase expanded to a twelve-driver format that year.



Biffle racing at [Daytona International Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daytona_International_Speedway) in 2008

In June 2008, Biffle signed a three-year-long contract extension with Roush Fenway Racing.[^cite_note-11] Despite going winless during the 26-race regular season, Biffle made for the Chase for the Sprint Cup that year and won the first two Chase races, at [New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hampshire_Motor_Speedway) and [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover_International_Speedway). In doing so, he became the first driver to win the first two Chase races in a season.

Biffle qualified for the Chase for the second year in a row in 2009 but, for the first time since 2002 (when he ran a limited schedule), failed to record a win. During a test in January 2009 at [Texas World Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_World_Speedway), Biffle managed to reach a top speed of 218 miles per hour (351 km/h) and lap average of 195 mph (314 km/h) in a test for [Roush Fenway Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roush_Fenway_Racing) as part of evading NASCAR's testing ban.[^cite_note-12] This became the fastest time ever achieved on this track by any competitor (amateur or professional).

In 2010, Biffle qualified for the third year in a row for the Chase despite spotty performance in the regular season. He won twice at Pocono and Kansas. In 2011, Biffle's season improved, thanks in part to the implementation of Ford's new FR9 engine. However, crew chief [Greg Erwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Erwin) was replaced after Kentucky by Matt Puccia. The addition of Puccia helped Biffle's performance late in the season, despite the team missing the Chase and finishing sixteenth in points. Biffle missed the Chase in 2011 for the first time since 2007.[^cite_note-13]



Biffle competes in the [2013 STP Gas Booster 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_STP_Gas_Booster_500)

In 2012, Biffle and Puccia remained at RFR and gained the points lead after Las Vegas after three consecutive third-place finishes. At the [2012 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Daytona_500), Biffle found himself second coming to the white flag for the third time in two years and again finished third. Eerily, the third place at Vegas came in Biffle's 333rd Cup start. Biffle's first win of the 2012 season came at [Texas Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Motor_Speedway) in the [Samsung Mobile 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Samsung_Mobile_500) after passing [Jimmie Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmie_Johnson) with thirty laps left in the race.[^cite_note-winstexas20121-14] Biffle won at Michigan holding off Brad Keselowski after Jimmie Johnson blew an engine.[^cite_note-15]

Biffle started off 2013 by being in the same position for the third time in four years; in second place coming to the white flag in the [2013 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Daytona_500), but this time ended up sixth. In the [2013 Quicken Loans 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Quicken_Loans_400) at Michigan, Biffle won his fourth race at the track and the 1,000th victory for Ford.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-usatoday2013michiganjune-17]

At the [2014 Coca-Cola 600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Coca-Cola_600), Biffle surpassed [Clint Bowyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Bowyer)'s record for most consecutive races without failing to finish with 84, tying [Herman Beam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Beam)'s record, dating back to the [2011 Ford 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Ford_400). The following week at the [FedEx 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FedEx_400), he broke Beam's record by finishing the race 108 laps down in 38th.[^cite_note-18] Later in the season, he finished with a DNF for the first time in 89 races after a wreck in the [Coke Zero 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Coke_Zero_400), finishing 29th.



Biffle racing at Richmond International Raceway in 2016

Biffle began the pre-season with an announcement that he would stay at [Roush Fenway Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roush_Fenway_Racing) to help the team with a new sponsor [Ortho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotts_Miracle-Gro_Company). Biffle began 2015 with a tenth-place finish in the [Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Daytona_500). Unfortunately, that was as good as it could get for Biffle as he faded outside the top twenty in points. He struggled mightily before picking up a second-place finish in the [2015 Coca-Cola 600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Coca-Cola_600), after starting fourth. He went on to pick up a fifth-place finish at Pocono in the [2015 Windows 10 400](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Windows_10_400) and a fourth-place at New Hampshire in the [2015 Sylvania 300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Sylvania_300), finishing twentieth in points.

Ortho announced it would depart Roush following the 2015 season, leaving Biffle without a primary sponsor for the [2016 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NASCAR_Sprint_Cup_Series). KFC then announced it would sponsor Biffle throughout Speedweeks and in the Daytona 500. He earned his first pole position in four seasons during qualifying for the [Coke Zero 400 at Daytona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Coke_Zero_400) and went on to finish eighth in the race. After finishing 23rd in points in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NASCAR_Sprint_Cup_Series), Biffle and Roush Fenway mutually parted ways, making Biffle a free agent for the upcoming 2017 season. He did not sign on with a ride for the 2017 season.[^cite_note-19]



Biffle racing at Atlanta Motor Speedway in 2022

Biffle returned to the series in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NASCAR_Cup_Series), driving the No. 44 for [NY Racing Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NY_Racing_Team) at the [Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Daytona_500) and the remainder of the team's races that year as well.[^cite_note-20] He was originally rumored to drive the No. 44 for [NY Racing Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NY_Racing_Team) in the [Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Daytona_500) in 2024, although he announced that he would not be in the car due to "unfulfilled contract obligations" from 2022, and would not drive any races for the team until said obligations are fulfilled, and was replaced by [J. J. Yeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Yeley), despite his name still displayed on the car and firesuit.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

### Stadium Super Trucks



Biffle racing in the No. 57 Stadium Super Truck at [Road America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_America) in 2018

On August 21, 2018, Biffle tested a [Stadium Super Truck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadium_Super_Trucks) that was driven by fellow ex-NASCAR driver [Casey Mears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casey_Mears). A day later, he announced he would make his series debut at [Road America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_America).[^cite_note-23] He finished seventh and second in the weekend's two races,[^cite_note-24] though he did not receive points in the standings as he drove the No. 57 truck in place of [Bill Hynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Hynes), who received the points earned by Biffle under series rules.[^cite_note-25]

Biffle returned to SST for the 2019 season, sharing a [Continental AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_AG)-sponsored truck with [Sheldon Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheldon_Creed) and [Ryan Beat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Beat).[^cite_note-26] He ran his first races of the year in late July at [Mid-Ohio Sports Car Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Ohio_Sports_Car_Course).[^cite_note-27] In the weekend's first round, he battled with [Gavin Harlien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavin_Harlien) for the lead until Harlien began suffering mechanical trouble; on the final restart, [Cole Potts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cole_Potts?action=edit\&redlink=1) passed Biffle to take the race win.[^cite_note-28] The second round saw Biffle pit under caution for damage, but was able to finish fourth.[^cite_note-29] In October, he ran his first SST race in Australia at [Surfers Paradise Street Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfers_Paradise_Street_Circuit).[^cite_note-30] In practice, he was the fastest in the ten-driver field but did not set a qualifying time after flipping his truck when he hit a tire barrier.[^cite_note-31] He ran in second for much of the first race before falling to fourth after an erratic landing; the second race saw a late spin drop him to eighth.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] After not racing in 2020, Biffle rejoined the series at the 2021 Mid-Ohio NASCAR weekend.[^cite_note-34] He finished second in Race 2.[^cite_note-35]

### Superstar Racing Experience

In 2021, it was announced that Biffle would pilot the No. 69 car in select [2021 Camping World SRX Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Camping_World_SRX_Series) races. He started his season off at the inaugural SRX race at [Stafford Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stafford_Motor_Speedway), winning the first heat but getting caught up in a wreck in the second heat. For the main event, he switched to a backup car and drove to second by the end of the race, behind only [Doug Coby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Coby).

### Other racing

In 2003, Biffle competed in the [International Race of Champions](659845.md). He recorded a best finish of third at Talladega and finished seventh in points.[^cite_note-36] Biffle, along with Roush teammates [Kurt Busch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Busch) and [Matt Kenseth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Kenseth), raced at the [2005 24 Hours of Daytona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_24_Hours_of_Daytona) for [Multimatic Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimatic_Motorsports). The team lost a halfshaft during the race and finished 27th.[^cite_note-37]

In 2018, Biffle started the Sand Outlaw Series for racing [UTVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side-by-side_\(vehicle\)).[^cite_note-38] Since then the series has grown into a popular multi-class drag racing championship featuring hill climbs and flat track events. He competed out of his own UTV shop, Pace Offroad.[^cite_note-39]

## Personal life



Biffle signs autographs along pit lane at Pocono Raceway.

Greg Biffle was the son of Garland Jack Biffle II (1941–2016) and Sally Frye. He had a brother, Jeffrey. He was of German and English descent, as his ancestor Johannes Büffel (1728–1804) came from [Contwig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contwig) in western [Germany](11867.md) and settled in [North Carolina](21650.md).

Biffle married Nicole (née Lunders) on October 17, 2007.[^cite_note-40] Their daughter was born in 2011.[^cite_note-41] Greg and Nicole Biffle separated in early 2015, and were divorced in early 2016.

Biffle's second child, a son, was born in 2020. The child's mother was Cristina Grossu. Biffle and Grossu were married in [the Bahamas](3451.md) on December 31, 2022.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

Biffle was a fan of fishing and enjoyed deep-sea fishing in [Mexico](3966054.md), where he kept a factory-sponsored Luhrs fishing boat. He once owned a pub in his hometown of Vancouver called "Biffle's Pub and Grill" located at 11500 NE 76th St. In 2010 he announced that he had become part-owner of Willamette Speedway in [Lebanon, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon,_Oregon), along with Sunset Speedway in [Banks, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banks,_Oregon).[^cite_note-44] He was also a [private pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_pilot) and owned a [Cessna 210](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_210_Centurion) as well as a [Dassault Falcon 50](3098320.md) with the tail number N116GB, a [Dassault Falcon 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault_Falcon_10) with the tail number N316GB, and a [Bell 206](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_206) with tail number N216GB.[^cite_note-45] Biffle was a private [helicopter](8286923.md) pilot and used his own helicopter to help people in [Western North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_North_Carolina) which were affected by [Hurricane Helene](77945659.md).[^cite_note-46]

Biffle owned a 2005 [Ford GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_GT#First_generation_\(2005%E2%80%932006\))[^cite_note-47] and a 2007 [Shelby Mustang GT500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelby_Mustang_GT500).[^cite_note-48] The Shelby Mustang engine was rebuilt in 2013, having 983 horsepower and 799 lb-ft of torque.[^cite_note-49]

A civil suit for invasion of privacy against Biffle was filed by his ex-wife and mother-in-law, alleging that Biffle had placed secret cameras in his home. The jury awarded Biffle's ex-wife \$1 in compensatory damages and \$500,000 in punitive damages, reduced to a total of \$250,001 due to a legal limit on punitive damages.[^cite_note-50] On February 17, 2017, Biffle announced that instead of racing full-time in 2017, he would join [NBC Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Sports)' *[NASCAR America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_America)* as an analyst beginning March 1, 2017.[^cite_note-51]

### Death

Main article: [2025 North Carolina Cessna Citation II crash](81871726.md)

On December 18, 2025, a [Cessna Citation 550](3878365.md) private jet registered by Biffle crashed while attempting to land at [Statesville Regional Airport](76607890.md).[^cite_note-52] All seven people aboard the plane died as a result of the crash. Biffle was identified as a passenger by the [North Carolina State Highway Patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_Highway_Patrol). The families of those affected released a joint statement saying that in addition to Greg Biffle, his wife Cristina and both of his children were aboard. Pilots Dennis and Jack Dutton, and former [Kenny Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Wallace) hauler driver Craig Wadsworth were also onboard the aircraft.[^cite_note-wcnc_death1-53]
