**Seychelles**[^cite_note-11] ([/seɪˈʃɛl(z)/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/4f/En-us-Seychelles.ogg/En-us-Seychelles.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/4f/En-us-Seychelles.ogg/En-us-Seychelles.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, [/ˈseɪʃɛl(z)/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] French: [\[sɛʃɛl\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] or [\[seʃɛl\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)[^cite_note-17]), officially the **Republic of Seychelles** (French: *République des Seychelles*; [Seychellois Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychellois_Creole): *Repiblik Sesel*),[^cite_note-18] is an [archipelagic country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelagic_country) consisting of 115 islands[^cite_note-19] in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md). Its capital and largest city, [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_Seychelles), is 1,500 kilometres (800 nautical miles) east of mainland [Africa](5334607.md). Nearby island countries and territories include the [Maldives](19117.md), [Comoros](5403.md), [Madagascar](18964.md), [Mauritius](19201.md), and the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Fifth_Republic) [overseas departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_departments_and_regions_of_France) of [Mayotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayotte) and [Réunion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9union) to the south; and the [Chagos Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagos_Archipelago) to the east. Seychelles is the [smallest country in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_area) as well as the [least populated sovereign African country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_population), with an estimated population of 100,600 in 2022.[^cite_note-World_Bank_Open_Data_v619-20]

The Seychelles archipelago was uninhabited prior to sustained external contact. Although Arab and Swahili sailors likely knew of the islands earlier through [Indian Ocean trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_trade) routes, there is no evidence of permanent settlement before European involvement. The islands were first recorded by Europeans in the 16th century, but were not settled until the 18th century, when France formally claimed them. During the period of French colonization, enslaved Africans were brought to the islands for plantation labor; many had already been captured and enslaved through existing African-[Arab slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_slave_trade) or [Indian Ocean slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_slave_trade).

It faced competing French and British interests until it came under full British control in the early 19th century. After Britain assumed control in the early 19th century, slavery was abolished and later replaced in part by indentured laborers from India. Since proclaiming independence from the United Kingdom in 1976, it has developed from a largely [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) society to a market-based diversified economy, characterized by [service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_sector), [public sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_sector), and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism) activities. From 1976 to 2015, nominal [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) grew nearly 700%, and [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) nearly 1600%. Since the late 2010s, the government has taken steps to encourage [foreign investment](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/foreign%20investment).

As of the early 21st century, Seychelles has the highest [nominal per capita GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)_per_capita) and the highest [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) ranking of any African country.[^cite_note-21] According to the 2024 [V-Dem Democracy indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-Dem_Democracy_indices), Seychelles is the 43rd-ranked electoral democracy worldwide, the 1st-ranked [liberal democracy in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_in_Africa), and the 2nd-ranked electoral democracy on the continent.[^cite_note-vdem_dataset-22] Seychellois culture and society is an eclectic mix of French, British, Indian and African influences, with infusions of Chinese elements. The country is a member of the [United Nations](31769.md), the [African Union](10634933.md), the [Southern African Development Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Development_Community), and the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations).

## History

Main article: [History of Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Seychelles)



The first Europeans to discover the Seychelles were the [4th Portuguese India Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Portuguese_India_Armada_\(Gama,_1502\)), led by [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama).

### Early history

Seychelles was uninhabited throughout most of recorded history, although simulations of [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) migration patterns indicate a good probability that they visited the islands.[^cite_note-23] Tombs visible until 1910 at Anse Lascars on Silhouette Island have also been conjectured to belong to later [Maldivian](23582208.md) and [Arab](2185.md) traders visiting the archipelago.[^cite_note-24] [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama) and his [4th Portuguese India Armada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Portuguese_India_Armada_\(Gama,_1502\)) discovered the Seychelles on 15 March 1503; the first sighting was made by [Thomé Lopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thom%C3%A9_Lopes) aboard *Rui Mendes de Brito*. Da Gama's ships passed close to an elevated island, probably [Silhouette Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silhouette_Island), and the following day [Desroches Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desroches_Island). Later, the Portuguese mapped a group of seven islands and named them *The Seven Sisters.*[^cite_note-25] The earliest recorded landing was in January 1609, by the crew of the *Ascension* under Captain [Alexander Sharpeigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Sharpeigh) during the fourth voyage of the British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company).

A transit point for trade between Africa and Asia, the islands were said to be occasionally used by pirates until the French began to take control in 1756 when a Stone of Possession was laid on [Mahé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mah%C3%A9) by Captain [Corneille Nicholas Morphey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corneille_Nicholas_Morphey). The islands were named after French politician [Jean Moreau de Séchelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Moreau_de_S%C3%A9chelles), and were formally part of the colony of [Isle de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_de_France_\(Mauritius\)).[^cite_note-26] In August 1770, the French ship *Thélémaque* under Captain Leblanc Lécore landed 15 white settlers and 13 African and Indian slaves on [Ste. Anne Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ste._Anne_Island).[^cite_note-27]

During the [French Revolutionary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars), the [Royal Navy](26061.md) frigate [HMS *Orpheus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Orpheus_\(1780\)) under Captain Henry Newcombe arrived at Mahé on 16 May 1794. [Jean-Baptiste Quéau de Quinssy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Qu%C3%A9au_de_Quinssy?action=edit\&redlink=1), the senior administrator in the Seychelles, refused to resist *Orpheus* and instead successfully negotiated with the British, resulting the islands remaining under French control as "neutral" territory. After British forces completed their [invasion of Isle de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Isle_de_France) in December 1810, they assumed control over the Seychelles, which was formalised in the 1814 [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1814\)) that ended the [War of the Sixth Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Sixth_Coalition). Seychelles became a separate [crown colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_colony) from Mauritius in 1903. [Elections in Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Seychelles) were held in 1966 and 1970.

### Independence

In 1976, Seychelles gained independence from the [United Kingdom](31717.md) as a republic. It has since become a member of the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations).[^cite_note-History_of_Seychelles-28] In the 1970s Seychelles was "the place to be seen, a playground for film stars and the international jet set".[^cite_note-telegraph.co.uk-29] In 1977, a [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Seychelles_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) by [France-Albert René](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France-Albert_Ren%C3%A9) ousted the first president of the republic, [James Mancham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mancham).[^cite_note-africanhistory.about.com-30] René discouraged over-dependence on tourism and declared that he wanted "to keep Seychelles for the Seychellois".[^cite_note-telegraph.co.uk-29]

The 1979 constitution declared a [socialist one-party state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_party_rule_in_Seychelles), which lasted until 1991.[^cite_note-31]

In the 1980s there were a series of coup attempts against President René, some of which were supported by South Africa. In 1981, [Mike Hoare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Hoare) led a team of 43 South African mercenaries masquerading as holidaying rugby players in the [1981 Seychelles coup d'état attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_Seychelles_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt).[^cite_note-telegraph.co.uk-29] There was a gun battle at the airport, and most of the mercenaries later escaped in a hijacked [Air India](26457880.md) plane.[^cite_note-telegraph.co.uk-29] The leader of this hijacking was German mercenary D. Clodo, a former member of the [Rhodesian SAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesian_Special_Air_Service).[^cite_note-32] Clodo later stood trial in South Africa (where he was acquitted) as well as in his home country Germany for air piracy.[^cite_note-33]

In 1986, an attempted coup led by the Seychelles Minister of Defence, Ogilvy Berlouis, caused President René to request assistance from India. In [Operation Flowers are Blooming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Flowers_are_Blooming), the Indian Navy's [*Nilgiri*-class frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilgiri-class_frigate_\(1972\)) [*Vindhyagiri*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INS_Vindhyagiri_\(F42\)) arrived in Port Victoria to help avert the coup.[^cite_note-34]

The first draft of a new constitution failed to receive the requisite 60% of voters in 1992, but an amended version was approved in 1993.[^cite_note-35]

In January 2013, Seychelles declared a state of emergency when the tropical [cyclone Felleng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Felleng) caused torrential rain, and flooding and landslides destroyed hundreds of houses.[^cite_note-UN-36][^cite_note-AL-37]

Following the coup in 1977, the president always represented the same political party until the October [2020 Seychellois general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Seychellois_general_election), which was historic in that the opposition party won. [Wavel Ramkalawan](2501053.md) was the first president who did not represent United Seychelles (the current name of the former Seychelles People's Progressive Front).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

In January 2023, Seychelles announced its final stages of completing its marine spatial plan. It would become the second largest ocean area at 1.35 million km<sup>2</sup> (520,000 sq mi) behind [Norway](21241.md), in support of its [blue economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_economy).[^cite_note-40]

In October 2025, [presidential runoff](76588149.md) was won by former parliamentary speaker and main opposition leader, [Patrick Herminie](20114965.md), meaning Herminie's party, [United Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Seychelles) (US) returned to power.[^cite_note-41] On 26 October 2025, Patrick Herminie was sworn in as Seychelles' sixth president.[^cite_note-reuters.com-42]

## Politics

Main article: [Politics of Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Seychelles)

The Seychelles president, who is [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and [head of government](37570.md), is elected by popular vote for a five-year term of office. The cabinet is presided over and appointed by the president, subject to the approval of a majority of the legislature. As of 2026, the president is [Patrick Herminie](20114965.md).

The [unicameral](204304.md) Seychellois parliament, the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Seychelles\)) or *Assemblée Nationale*, consists of 35 members, 26 of whom are elected directly by popular vote, while the remaining nine seats are appointed proportionally according to the percentage of votes received by each party. All members serve five-year terms.

The [Supreme Court of Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Seychelles), created in 1903, is the highest trial court in Seychelles and the first court of appeal from all the lower courts and tribunals. The highest court of law in Seychelles is the Seychelles Court of Appeal, which is the court of final appeal in the country.[^cite_note-43]

### Political culture



President [James Michel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Michel) in his office in [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_Seychelles), 2009

Seychelles' long-term president [France-Albert René](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France-Albert_Ren%C3%A9) came to power after his supporters overthrew the first president [James Mancham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mancham) on 5 June 1977 in a [coup d'état](25914154.md) and installed him as president. René was at that time the prime minister. René ruled as a [strongman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strongman_\(politics\)) under a [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist) [one-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-party_system) until 1993, when he was forced to introduce a multi-party system. He stepped down in 2004 in favour of his vice-president, [James Michel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Michel), who was re-elected in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Seychellois_presidential_election), [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Seychellois_presidential_election) and again in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Seychellois_presidential_election).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-Seychelles_Nation-45][^cite_note-Reuters-46][^cite_note-Agence_France-Presse-47] On 28 September 2016, the Office of the President announced that Michel would step down effective 16 October, and that Vice President [Danny Faure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Faure) would complete the rest of Michel's term.[^cite_note-48]

On 26 October 2020, [Wavel Ramkalawan](2501053.md), a 59-year-old Anglican priest, was elected the fifth President of the Republic of Seychelles. Ramkalawan was an opposition MP from 1993 to 2011, and from 2016 to 2020. He served as the [Leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(Seychelles\)) from 1998 to 2011 and from 2016 to 2020. Ramkalawan defeated incumbent Danny Faure by 54.9% to 43.5%. This marked the first time the opposition had won a presidential election in Seychelles.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

The primary political parties are the former long-time ruling socialist [People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Party_\(Seychelles\)) (PP), known until 2009 as the Seychelles People's Progressive Front (SPPF) now called [United Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Seychelles) (US), and the [socially liberal](765430.md) [Seychelles National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_National_Party) (SNP).[^cite_note-51]

The [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Seychellois_general_election) of the National Assembly was held on 22–24 October 2020. The Seychelles National Party, the Seychelles Party for Social Justice and Democracy and the Seychelles United Party formed a coalition, [Linyon Demokratik Seselwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linyon_Demokratik_Seselwa) (LDS). LDS won 25 seats and US got 10 seats of the 35 seats of the National Assembly.[^cite_note-52] However, in the 2025 Seychellois general [election](76588149.md) United Seychelles won 15 out of 26 seats in the parliament.[^cite_note-53]

### Foreign relations

Further information: [Foreign relations of Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Seychelles)

Seychelles is a member of the [United Nations](31769.md), the [African Union](10634933.md), the [Indian Ocean Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_Commission), [La Francophonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Francophonie), the [Southern African Development Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_African_Development_Community) and the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations).

Between 1979 and 1983, various plots to overthrow the non-aligned government of France-Albert René were, according to leading participants, supported by the United States, France, and South Africa. Commonly cited reasons for such attempts include René's socialist politics, his non-aligned stance toward the Western and Eastern Blocs, and the United States' military lease in the country, which was set to expire in 1990. All coup efforts in this period failed.[^cite_note-54] Under the Obama administration, the US began running drone operations out of Seychelles.[^cite_note-\:0-55] In the Spring of 2013, members of the [Special-Purpose Marine Air-Ground Task Force Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Purpose_Marine_Air-Ground_Task_Force_%E2%80%93_Crisis_Response_%E2%80%93_Africa) mentored troops in Seychelles, along with a variety of other African nations.[^cite_note-\:0-55]

### Military

Main article: [Seychelles People's Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_People's_Defence_Force)

The [military of Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Seychelles) is the [Seychelles People's Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_People's_Defence_Force) which consists of a number of distinct branches: an infantry unit and coast guard, air force and a presidential protection unit. India has played and continues to play a key role in developing the military of Seychelles. After handing over two [SDB Mk5 patrol vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinkat-class_patrol_vessel) built by [GRSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GRSE), the INS *Tarasa* and INS *Tarmugli*, to the [Seychelles Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_Coast_Guard), which were subsequently renamed [PS *Constant*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PS_Constant) and [PS *Topaz*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PS_Topaz), India also gifted a [Dornier 228](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dornier_228) aircraft built by [Hindustan Aeronautics Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Aeronautics_Limited).[^cite_note-56] India also signed a pact to develop [Assumption Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_Island). Spread over 11 km<sup>2</sup> (4 sq mi), it is strategically located in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md), north of [Madagascar](18964.md). The island is being leased for the development of strategic assets by India.[^cite_note-57] In 2018, Seychelles signed the UN [Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons).[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

### Incarceration

Further information: [List of countries by incarceration rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_incarceration_rate)

In 2014, Seychelles had the highest incarceration rate in the world of 799 prisoners per 100,000 population, exceeding the United States' rate by 15%.[^cite_note-60] However, the country's actual population was less than 100,000; as of September 2014, Seychelles had 735 actual prisoners, 6% of whom were female and were incarcerated in three prisons.[^cite_note-61] By 2022, the incarceration rate was 287 per 100,000 population, having dropped to the 31st highest in the world.[^cite_note-62]

### Modern piracy

Seychelles is a key participant in the fight against Indian Ocean [piracy](50715.md) primarily committed by [Somali pirates](17015903.md#Effects_and_perceptions).[^cite_note-wc-63] Former president [James Michel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Michel) said that piracy costs between \$7 million – \$12 million a year to the international community: "The pirates cost 4% of the Seychelles [GDP](12594.md), including direct and indirect costs for the loss of boats, fishing, and tourism, and the indirect investment for the maritime security." These are factors affecting local fishing – one of the country's main national resources – which had a 46% loss in 2008–2009.[^cite_note-wc-63] International contributions of patrol boats, planes or drones have been provided to help Seychelles combat sea piracy.[^cite_note-wc-63]

[Early Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Jurassic) breakup of [Gondwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwana) (left) and A- [Early Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Cretaceous), B- [Late Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Cretaceous), C-[Paleocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleocene), D- Present Day (right)

An island nation, Seychelles is located in the [Somali Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Sea) segment of the Indian Ocean, northeast of [Madagascar](18964.md) and about 1,600 km (860 nmi) east of [Kenya](188171.md). The Constitution of Seychelles lists 155 named islands,[^cite_note-Constitution-64] and a further 7 reclaimed islands have been created subsequent to the publication of the Constitution. The majority of the islands are uninhabited, with many dedicated as nature reserves. Seychelles' largest island, [Mahé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mah%C3%A9), is located 1,550 km (835 nmi) from [Mogadishu](60154.md) ([Somalia](27358.md)'s capital).[^cite_note-65]

Seychelles sits atop a large submerged [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) block known as the [Seychelles microcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_microcontinent), which was sandwiched between [Madagascar](18964.md) and [India](14533.md) before the breakup of [Gondwanaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondwanaland).[^cite_note-Torsvik2013-66] [Alfred Wegener](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Wegener) cited the Seychelles' granitic composition, akin to that of continents, as evidence of [continental drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_drift).[^cite_note-67] After Madagascar separated from India during the [Late Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Cretaceous), interaction with the [Réunion hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A9union_hotspot) caused the diverging oceanic rift to jump several times, breaking off a piece that is currently on the [Somali plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_plate) and includes Seychelles, [Mauritius](19201.md), and Réunion, as well as another fragment on the present [Indian plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_plate) that includes [Lakshadweep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakshadweep), the [Chagos Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagos_Archipelago), and the [Maldives](19117.md).[^cite_note-Torsvik2013-66][^cite_note-68]

A group of 44 islands (42 granitic and 2 coralline) occupy the shallow waters of the Seychelles Bank and are collectively referred to as the inner islands. They have a total area of 244 km<sup>2</sup> (94 sq mi), accounting for 54% of the total land area of the Seychelles and 98% of the entire population.

### Climate

The climate is very humid, as the islands are small,[^cite_note-69] and is classified by the [Köppen-Geiger system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen-Geiger_climate_classification_system) as a [tropical rain forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate) (*Af*). The temperature varies little throughout the year. Temperatures on Mahé vary from 24 to 30 °C (75 to 86 °F), and rainfall ranges from 2,900 mm (114 in) annually at [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_Seychelles) to 3,600 mm (142 in) on the mountain slopes. [Precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) levels are somewhat less on the other islands.[^cite_note-Climate-70]

During the coolest months, July and August, the average low is about 24 °C (75 °F). The southeast trade winds blow regularly from May to November, and this is the most pleasant time of the year. The hot months are from December to April, with higher humidity (80%). March and April are the hottest months, but the temperature seldom exceeds 31 °C (88 °F). Most of the islands lie outside the cyclone belt, so high winds are rare.[^cite_note-Climate-70]

### Wildlife

Main article: [Wildlife of Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Seychelles)





Left: [Seychelles paradise flycatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_paradise_flycatcher); right: bird flocks on [Bird Island, Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_Island,_Seychelles)



An [Aldabra giant tortoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldabra_giant_tortoise)

Seychelles is among the world's leading countries to protect lands for [threatened species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threatened_species), allocating 42% of its territory for [conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_\(ethic\)).[^cite_note-73] Like many fragile island ecosystems, Seychelles saw the [loss of biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_loss) when humans first settled in the area, including the disappearance of most of the [giant tortoises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldabrachelys) from the granitic islands, the felling of coastal and mid-level forests, and the extinction of species such as the [chestnut flanked white eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_chestnut-flanked_white-eye), the [Seychelles parakeet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_parakeet), and the [saltwater crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltwater_crocodile). However, extinctions were far fewer than on islands such as [Mauritius](19201.md) or [Hawaii](13270.md), partly due to a shorter period of human occupation. Seychelles today is known for success stories in protecting its flora and fauna. The rare [Seychelles black parrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_black_parrot), the national bird of the country, is now protected.

The [freshwater crab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_crab) genus *[Seychellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychellum)* is endemic to the granitic Seychelles, and a further 26 species of crabs and five species of [hermit crabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermit_crab) live on the islands.[^cite_note-Haig-74] From the year 1500 until the mid-1800s (approximately), the then-previously unknown [Aldabra giant tortoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldabra_giant_tortoise) was killed for food by pirates and sailors, driving their numbers to near-extinction levels. Today, a healthy yet fragile population of 150,000 tortoises live solely on the atoll of Aldabra, declared a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] Additionally, these ancient reptiles can further be found in numerous zoos, botanical gardens, and private collections internationally. Their protection from poaching and smuggling is overseen by [CITES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CITES), whilst [captive breeding](2965243.md) has greatly reduced the negative impact on the remaining wild populations. The granitic islands of Seychelles supports three extant species of [Seychelles giant tortoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_giant_tortoise).

Seychelles hosts some of the largest [seabird colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_colony) in the world, notably on the outer islands of Aldabra and Cosmoledo. In [granitic Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granitic_Seychelles) the largest colonies are on [Aride Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aride_Island) including the world's largest numbers of two species. The [sooty tern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sooty_tern) also breeds on the islands. Other common birds include [cattle egret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle_egret) (*Bubulcus ibis*) and the [fairy tern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairy_tern) (*Gygis alba*).[^cite_note-77] More than 1,000 species of fish have been recorded.

The granitic islands of Seychelles are home to roughly 268 [flowering plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiosperms) species, of which 70 (28%) are endemic.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] Particularly well known is the [coco de mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_de_mer), a species of palm that grows only on the islands of [Praslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praslin) and neighbouring [Curieuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curieuse). Sometimes nicknamed the "love nut" (the shape of its "double" coconut resembles buttocks), the coco-de-mer produces the world's heaviest seed. The [jellyfish tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jellyfish_tree) is to be found in only a few locations on Mahé. This strange and ancient plant, in a genus of its own, *[Medusagyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medusagyne)* seems to reproduce only in cultivation and not in the wild. Other unique plant species include Wright's gardenia (*[Rothmannia annae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothmannia_annae)*), found only on [Aride Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aride_Island)'s Special Reserve. There are 6 potentially endemic species of orchid living on the islands.[^cite_note-80]

Seychelles is home to two terrestrial ecoregions: [Granitic Seychelles forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granitic_Seychelles_forests) and [Aldabra Island xeric scrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldabra_Island_xeric_scrub).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-81] The country had a 2019 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index) mean score of 10/10, ranking it first globally out of 172 countries.[^cite_note-FLII-Supplementary-82]

### Environmental issues

Since the use of [spearguns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speargun) and dynamite for fishing was banned through efforts of local conservationists in the 1960s, the wildlife is unafraid of [snorkelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snorkeling) and [divers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuba_diving). [Coral bleaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_bleaching) in 1998 has damaged most reefs, but some reefs show healthy recovery (such as [Silhouette Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silhouette_Island)).

Seychelles claims to have achieved nearly all of its [Millennium Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Development_Goals), with 7 out of 8 goals having been achieved.[^cite_note-83] Environmental protection has been emphasized by the government.[^cite_note-84]

The government's *Seychelles Climate Guide* describes the nation's climate as rainy, with a dry season with an ocean economy in the ocean regions. The Southeast Trades is on the decline but still fairly strong.[^cite_note-85] Reportedly, weather patterns there are becoming less predictable.[^cite_note-86]

[Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_Seychelles), capital and largest city of Seychelles

When the British gained control of the islands during the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), they allowed the French upper class to retain their land. Both the French and British settlers used enslaved Africans, and although the British prohibited slavery in 1835, African workers continued to come. The *Gran blan* ("big whites") of French origin dominated economic and political life. The British administration employed Indians on [indentured servitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indentured_servant) to the same degree as in Mauritius resulting in a small Indian population. The Indians, like a similar minority of Chinese, were generally in the merchant class.[^cite_note-87]

Today, Seychelles is described as a fusion of peoples and cultures. Numerous Seychellois are considered multiracial: blending from African, Asian and European descent to create a modern creole culture. Evidence of this blend is also revealed in Seychellois food, incorporating various aspects of French, Chinese, Indian and African cuisine.[^cite_note-88]

As the islands of Seychelles had no indigenous population, the current Seychellois descend from people who immigrated, of which the largest ethnic groups were those of African, French, [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-resident_Indian_and_person_of_Indian_origin) and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese) origin. The [median](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median) age of the Seychellois is 34 years.[^cite_note-CIA-89]

### Languages

[French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) are official languages along with [Seychellois Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychellois_Creole), which is a [French-based](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French-based_creole) [creole language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_language) related to those spoken in Mauritius and Réunion. Seychellois [Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_language) is the most widely spoken native language and de facto the [national language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_language) of the country. Seychellois Creole is often spoken with English words and phrases mixed in.[^cite_note-ethno-90] About 91% of the population are native speakers of Seychellois Creole, 5.1% of English and 0.7% of French.[^cite_note-ethno-90] Most business and official meetings are conducted in English and nearly all official websites are in English. National Assembly business is conducted in Creole, but laws are passed and published in English.

[Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language) is also a prominent language in Seychelles, spoken primarily by the [Indo-Seychellois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Seychellois) community, who form a significant part of the country's multilingual society.[^cite_note-91]

### Religion

Main articles: [Religion in Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Seychelles), [Catholic Church in Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Seychelles), [Hinduism in Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Seychelles), and [Islam in Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Seychelles)



[St. Francis of Assisi Church, Baie Lazare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Francis_of_Assisi_Church,_Baie_Lazare), Mahé island



[Arulmigu Navashakti Vinayakar Temple in Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Seychelles)

According to the 2022 census, most Seychellois are Christians: 61.3% were [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Seychelles), pastorally served by the exempt [Diocese of Port Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Port_Victoria); 5.0% were [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism) and 8.6% follows other sects of Christianity.[^cite_note-2022Census-5][^cite_note-92]

[Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) is the second largest religion, adhered to by more than 5.4% of the population.[^cite_note-2022Census-5][^cite_note-CIA-89] Hinduism is followed mainly by the [Indo-Seychellois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Seychellois) community.[^cite_note-93]

[Islam](6037917.md) is followed by another 1.6% of the population. Other faiths accounted for 1.1% of the population, while a further 5.9% were non-religious or did not specify a religion.[^cite_note-CIA-89]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Seychelles)



The sailfish at Mahé Beach



Colourful skirts at a Seychelles market

During the plantation era, [cinnamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinnamon), [vanilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanilla) and [copra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copra) were the chief exports. In 1965, during a three-month visit to the islands, futurist [Donald Prell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Prell) prepared for the [crown colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_colony)'s Governor General an economic report containing a scenario for the future of the economy. Quoting from his report, in the 1960s, about 33% of the working population worked at plantations, and 20% worked in the public or government sector.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] The Indian Ocean Tracking Station on Mahé used by the United States' [Air Force Satellite Control Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Satellite_Control_Network) was closed in August 1996 after the Seychelles government attempted to raise the rent to more than \$10,000,000 per year.

Since independence in 1976, per capita output has expanded to roughly seven times the old near-subsistence level[^cite_note-96]. Growth has been led by the tourist sector, which employs about 30% of the labour force[^cite_note-97], compared to agriculture which today employs about 3% of the labour force. Despite the growth of tourism, farming and fishing continue to employ some people, as do industries that process coconuts and vanilla.[^cite_note-98]

As of 2013, the main export products are processed fish (60%) and non-fillet frozen fish (22%).[^cite_note-99]

The prime agricultural products currently produced in Seychelles include [sweet potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potatoes), vanilla, coconuts and cinnamon. These products provide much of the economic support of the locals. Frozen and canned fish, copra, cinnamon and vanilla are the main export commodities.

The Seychelles government has prioritised a curbing of the [budget deficit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_deficit), including the containment of [social welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_welfare) costs and further [privatisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatisation) of public enterprises. The government has a pervasive presence in economic activity, with public enterprises active in petroleum product distribution, banking, imports of basic products, telecommunications and a wide range of other businesses. According to the 2013 [Index of Economic Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_of_Economic_Freedom), which measures the degree of limited government, market openness, regulatory efficiency, rule of law, and other factors, economic freedom has been increasing each year since 2010.[^cite_note-100]&#x20;

The national currency of Seychelles is the [Seychellois rupee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychellois_rupee). Initially tied to a basket of international currencies, it was unpegged and allowed to be devalued and float freely in 2008 on the presumed hopes of attracting further foreign investment in the Seychelles economy.[^cite_note-101]

Seychelles has emerged as the least corrupt country in Africa in the latest Corruption Perception Index report released by [Transparency International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International) in January 2020.[^cite_note-102]

### Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Seychelles)



Beach resort at Seychelles



Aircraft at [Seychelles International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_International_Airport)

In 1971, with the opening of [Seychelles International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_International_Airport), tourism became a significant industry, essentially dividing the economy into plantations and tourism. The tourism sector paid better, and the plantation economy could expand only so far. The plantation sector of the economy declined in prominence, and tourism became the primary industry of Seychelles. Consequently, there was a sustained spate of hotel construction throughout almost the entire 1970s which included the opening of Coral Strand Smart Choice, Vista Do Mar and Bougainville Hotel in 1972.

In recent years the government has encouraged foreign direct investment to upgrade its hospitality infrastructure and service sectors. These policy frameworks, championed by the Seychelles Investment Board,[^cite_note-103] have catalyzed significant capital inflows[^cite_note-104] into luxury real estate and integrated resort developments. Central to this growth was the World Bank's Sustainability and Climate Resilience Development Policy Loan[^cite_note-105]—which succeeded earlier structural adjustment programs to strengthen the business environment and fiscal management.

Since then the government has moved to reduce the dependence on tourism by promoting the development of farming, fishing, small-scale manufacturing and most recently the offshore financial sector, through the establishment of the Financial Services Authority and the enactment of several pieces of legislation (such as the International Corporate Service Providers Act, the International Business Companies Act, the Securities Act, the Mutual Funds and Hedge Fund Act, amongst others). In March 2015, Seychelles allocated [Assumption Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_Island) to be developed by India.[^cite_note-106]

Owing to the effects of [COVID-19](63030231.md), Seychelles shut down its borders to international tourism in the year 2020. As the national vaccination programme progressed well, the nation's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Tourism decided to reopen the borders to international tourists on 25 March 2021.

### Energy



Electricity generation in Seychelles in terawatt-hours

Although multinational oil companies have explored the waters around the islands, no oil or gas has been found.[^cite_note-107] In 2005, a deal was signed with US firm Petroquest, giving it exploration rights to about 30,000 km<sup>2</sup> (12,000 sq mi) around Constant, Topaz, Farquhar and Coëtivy islands until 2014.[^cite_note-\:1-108] Seychelles imports oil from the Persian Gulf in the form of refined petroleum derivatives at the rate of about 5,700 barrels per day (910 m<sup>3</sup>/d).[^cite_note-\:1-108]

In recent years oil has been imported from the United Arab Emirates and Oman[^cite_note-\:1-108]. In 2023, the UAE accounted for \$298M (roughly 83%) of all refined petroleum imports to the Seychelles[^cite_note-\:1-108]. Seychelles imports three times more[^cite_note-109] oil than is needed for internal uses: this surplus is strategically re-exported as bunker fuel for international shipping and aviation at Port Victoria[^cite_note-110]. There are no refining capacities on the islands. Oil and gas imports, distribution and re-export are the responsibility of Seychelles Petroleum (SEYPEC[^cite_note-111]), while oil exploration is the responsibility of PetroSeychelles[^cite_note-112], formerly the Seychelles National Oil Company (SNOC).

## Culture

### Art

The National Art Gallery was inaugurated in 1994 on the occasion of the official opening of the National Cultural Centre on 18 June 1994[^cite_note-113], which houses the National Library[^cite_note-114] and National Archives along with other offices of the Ministry of Culture. At its inauguration, the Minister of Culture decreed that the exhibition of works of Seychellois artists, painters and sculptors was "a testimony to the development of art in Seychelles as a creative form of expression", providing a view of the state of the country's contemporary art.&#x20;

Painters have traditionally been inspired by Seychelles' natural features to produce a wide range of works in media ranging from watercolours to oils, acrylics, collages, metals, aluminium, wood, fabrics, [gouache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouache), varnishes, recycled materials, pastels, charcoal, embossing, etching, and [giclee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gicl%C3%A9e) prints. Local sculptors (such as Egbert Marday, who was awarded the Seychelles Arts Award for Lifetime Achievement in 2019[^cite_note-115]) produce fine works in wood, stone, bronze and [cartonnage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartonnage). One Seychelles painter, Michael Adams, was awarded the [Member of the Order of the British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire) for his services to art[^cite_note-116].

There are several art galleries around the island, such as the National Gallery in Victoria, the traditional wooden house galleries, including the Kenwyn House Gallery[^cite_note-117] and the Kaz Zanana Art Gallery; the Pagoda Art and Design Gallery[^cite_note-118] in the Seychelles Chinese Culture Centre near the Selwyn Clarke market, and the Eden gallery on [Eden Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Island,_Seychelles).

### Music

Main article: [Music of Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Seychelles)

Music and dance have always played prominent roles in Seychelles culture and local festivities. Rooted in African, Malagasy and European cultures, music characteristically features drums such as the [tambour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambourine) and [tam-tam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gong), and simple string instruments. The violin and guitar are relatively recent foreign imports which play a prominent role in contemporary music.

Among popular dances are the [Sega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_\(genre\)), with hip-swaying and shuffling of the feet, and the [Moutya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moutya), a dance dating back to the days of slavery, when it was often used to express strong emotions and discontent.

The [music of Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Seychelles) is diverse, a reflection of the fusion of cultures through its history. The [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music) of the islands incorporates multiple influences in a syncretic fashion. It includes African rhythms, aesthetic and instrumentation, such as the *zez* and the *bom* (known in Brazil as [berimbau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berimbau)); European [contredanse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contredanse_\(organisation\)), [polka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polka) and [mazurka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazurka); French folk and pop; [*sega*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega_music) from [Mauritius](19201.md) and Réunion; [taarab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taarab), [soukous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soukous) and other pan-African genres; and [Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesia), [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_India) and [Arcadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcadia_\(regional_unit\)) music.

Contombley is a popular form of percussion music, as is Moutya, a fusion of native folk rhythms with Kenyan [benga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benga_music). Kontredans, based on European [contra dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contra_dance), is also popular, especially in district and school competitions during the annual Festival Kreol (International Creole Festival). Moutya playing and dancing often occur at beach bazaars. Music is sung in the [Seychellois Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychellois_Creole) of the French language, and in French and English.

In 2021,[^cite_note-119] the Moutya, a slave trade-era dance, was added to the [UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Intangible_Cultural_Heritage_List) as a symbol of psychological comfort in its role of resistance against hardship, [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty), [servitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servitude_in_civil_law) and [social injustice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_injustice).[^cite_note-120]

### Media

Main article: [Media and telecommunications in Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_and_telecommunications_in_Seychelles)

The main daily newspaper is the *[Seychelles Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_Nation) and [Seychelles News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_News_Agency)* dedicated to local government views and current topics. Other newspapers include *Le Nouveau Seychelles Weekly*, *The People*, *Regar*, and *Today in Seychelles*.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-bbcmedia-128] Foreign newspapers and magazines are readily available at most bookshops and newsagents. The papers are published mostly in [Seychellois Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychellois_Creole), French and English.

Seychelles' prominent digital newspaper [Seychelles News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_News_Agency) was set to cease its operations completely on 1 January 2025, following the decision of the Seychelles government and *National Information Service Agency* (NISA) after ten years of news reporting in Seychelles.[^cite_note-129]

The main television and radio network, operated by the [Seychelles Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seychelles_Broadcasting_Corporation), offers locally produced news and discussion programmes in the Seychellois Creole language, between 3 pm and 11\:30 pm on weekdays and longer hours on weekends. There are also imported English- and French-language television programmes on Seychellois terrestrial television, and international satellite television has grown rapidly in recent years.

### Sports

Seychelles' most popular sport is [football](10568.md), which has significantly grown in popularity in the last decade.[^cite_note-130] In 2015, Seychelles hosted the [African Beach Soccer Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_CAF_Beach_Soccer_Championship). Ten years later, in May 2025, Seychelles hosted the [2025 FIFA Beach Soccer World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Beach_Soccer_World_Cup) making it the first ever FIFA Beach Soccer World Cup to be held in Africa.

Mothers tend to be dominant in the household, controlling most expenditure and looking after children's interests.[^cite_note-cs-131] [Unwed mothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unwed_mother) are the societal norm, and the law requires fathers to [support their children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_support).[^cite_note-hr-132] Men are important for their earning ability, but their domestic role is relatively peripheral.[^cite_note-cs-131][^cite_note-hr-132]

### LGBT rights

Main article: [LGBT rights in Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Seychelles)

Same-sex sexual activity has been legal since 2016.[^cite_note-133] The bill decriminalising homosexuality was approved in a 14–0 vote.[^cite_note-134] Employment discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation is banned in the Seychelles, making it one of the few [African](5334607.md) countries to have such protections for LGBT people.[^cite_note-ILGA_2012-135][^cite_note-136]

### Education

Main article: [Education in Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Seychelles)

Seychelles has the highest literacy rate of any country in sub-Saharan Africa.[^cite_note-cia.gov-137] According to The World Factbook of the Central Intelligence Agency, as of 2018, 95.9% of the population aged 15 and over can read and write in the Seychelles.[^cite_note-cia.gov-137]

Until the mid-19th century, little formal education was available in Seychelles. The Catholic and Anglican churches opened mission schools in 1851. The Catholic mission later operated boys' and girls' secondary schools with religious brothers and nuns from abroad even after the government became responsible for them in 1944.[^cite_note-138]

A teacher training college opened in 1959, when the supply of locally trained teachers began to grow, and in short time many new schools were established. Since 1981 a system of free education has been in effect, requiring attendance by all children in grades one to nine, beginning at age six. Ninety-four percent of all children attend primary school.[^cite_note-139]

The literacy rate for school-age children rose to more than 90% by the late 1980s. Many older Seychellois had not been taught to read or write in their childhood; adult education classes helped raise adult literacy from 60% to a claimed 96% in 2020.[^cite_note-140]

There are a total of 68 schools in Seychelles. The public school system consists of 23 [crèches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindergarten), 25 primary schools and 13 secondary schools. They are located on [Mahé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mah%C3%A9), [Praslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praslin), [La Digue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Digue) and [Silhouette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silhouette_Island). Additionally, there are three private schools: École Française, International School and the independent school. All the private schools are on Mahé, and the International School has a branch on [Praslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praslin). There are seven post-secondary (non-tertiary) schools: the Seychelles Polytechnic, School of Advanced Level Studies, Seychelles Tourism Academy, University of Seychelles Education, Seychelles [Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Technology), Maritime Training Centre, Seychelles Agricultural and Horticultural Training Centre and the National Institute for Health and Social Studies.

The administration launched plans to open a university in an attempt to slow down the [brain drain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_drain) that had occurred. The [University of Seychelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Seychelles), initiated in conjunction with the [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London), opened on 17 September 2009 across three locations and offers qualifications awarded by the University of London.[^cite_note-141] The establishment of the university was intended to expand domestic access to higher education and reduce reliance on overseas study.
