**Sudan Airways** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الخطوط الجوية السودانية) is the [flag carrier](465816.md) airline of [Sudan](27421.md),[^cite_note-Sudan_Airways_problems_“unsolvable”,_says_former_official-1] headquartered in [Khartoum](17320.md). Since 2012, the company has been fully owned by the [Government of Sudan](5698926.md).[^cite_note-Sudan_Dreams_Big_With_New_Airports-2]

One of the oldest African [carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_carrier),[^cite_note-Sudan_Airways_problems_“unsolvable”,_says_former_official-1] it was formed in February 1946 (1946-02) and started scheduled operations in July the following year. As of December 2011, Sudan Airways had 1,700 employees.[^cite_note-Sanctions_are_hell-3] The airline has been included in the [list of air carriers banned in the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_air_carriers_banned_in_the_European_Union) since March 2010 (2010-03).

## History

An Air Advisory Board was formed in 1945 to assess on the feasibility of starting air services in the country, recommending to set up an air company with the aid of foreign [carriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_carrier) that would provide their technical and management expertise. Initially, the new airline would restrict its operations to on-demand services.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998202-4] Sudan Airways was formed in February 1946 with the technical assistance of [Airwork Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airwork_Services), and the commercial support of Sudan Railways.[^cite_note-Profile-5]<sup>: 89 </sup>



[De Havilland Dove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Dove) of Sudan Airways in 1967

The initial fleet was composed of four [de Havilland Doves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Dove), with test flights commencing in April 1947 (1947-04).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998202-4] The first scheduled operations were launched in July the same year,[^cite_note-Profile-5]<sup>: 90 </sup> with the first timetable being published in September.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998202-4] Khartoum became Sudan Airways' [hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airline_hub) from the very beginning. From there, the carrier started flying four different services all across the [Sudanese](27421.md) territory, as well as to the not yet independent [Eritrea](17238590.md). The first routes the company flew linked Khartoum with [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara), [Atbara](1515227.md), [El Fashir](3956787.md), [El Obeid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ubayyid), [Geneina](1501534.md), [Juba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juba,_South_Sudan), [Kassala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassala), [Malakal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malakal), and [Port Sudan](213572.md), all of them served by de Havilland Dove aircraft.[^cite_note-Profile-5]<sup>: 90 </sup> An Airwork [Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_VC.1_Viking) flew the [Blackbushe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackbushe)–Khartoum long-haul route. A fifth Dove was ordered in January 1948 (1948-01). That year, a route to [Wadi Halfa](1126657.md) was launched. Sudan Railways withdrew from the airline's management in 1949; the government and Airwork continued running the company thereafter.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998202-4]



Sudan Airways [Douglas C-47B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-3) in 1971

Kassala and Asmara were removed from the airline's list of destinations in 1952. In February that year, a fifth Dove was phased in. There was such a demand for flying that the toilets on the Doves were removed to make room for more seats, with these aircraft even carrying passengers in the cockpit. This prompted the airline to look for newer and bigger airliners, with the [Douglas DC-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-3) and the [de Havilland Heron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Heron) being under consideration.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998202-4] Flown with [Austers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auster_Aircraft) and [Doves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Dove), by March 1953 (1953-03) the carrier was operating a domestic network that was 4,800 kilometres (3,000 mi) long.[^cite_note-Flight1953-312-6] That year, the carrier incorporated the first four [DC-3s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC-3) into the fleet.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998202-4] The boost in capacity allowed the company to carry both passengers and mail, to introduce new regular routes to [Cairo](6293.md) and [Wad Medani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wad_Medani),[^cite_note-Profile-5]<sup>: 91 </sup> and to carry out aerial survey tasks for the government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998202-4] Also in 1953, the [Chadian](5488.md) city of [Abeche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeche) was made part of the route network, whereas regular flights to [Jeddah](277718.md) were launched in June 1954 (1954-06).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998203-7] Services to [Athens](1216.md) commenced in the mid-1950s. Two more DC-3s were bought in 1956.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998203-7] In 1958, after taking office, the Supreme Council of the Armed Forces decided to expand the carrier's international operations.[^cite_note-Profile-5]<sup>: 91 </sup> A seventh DC-3 was incorporated into the fleet that year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998203-7] [Long-haul services](8758086.md#Long-haul_flights) started in June 1959 between Khartoum and [London](17867.md) via [Rome](25458.md) –the so-called "Blue Nile" service[^cite_note-FI1962-747-8]– using a [Viscount 831](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_Viscount_variants) that was acquired new earlier that year in a joint venture with [British United Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_United_Airways).[^cite_note-Profile-5]<sup>: 91 </sup> [Beirut](37428.md) was added to the destination network in November the same year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998203-7] Also in 1959, the airline joined IATA.[^cite_note-Flight1959-805-9]



A Sudan Airways Comet 4C at [London Heathrow Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Heathrow_Airport) in 1972. The airline took delivery of the first aircraft of the type in November 1962 (1962-11).

[^cite_note-FI1962-813-10]

By April 1960 (1960-04), the fleet included seven DC-3s, four Doves, and a Viscount 831.[^cite_note-Flight_1960-511-11] The latter aircraft was used to resume operations to Asmara in December 1960 (1960-12).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998203-7] Aimed at replacing the DC-3s and the Doves in domestic and regional routes,[^cite_note-Profile-5]<sup>: 91 </sup>[^cite_note-FI1962-159-12] the airline acquired three [Fokker F27s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_F27) in October that year;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998203-7] these were delivered in early 1962,[^cite_note-FI1962-121-13] with the first of them being deployed on domestic routes, making Sudan Airways the first African airline in operating the type.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998203-7] Also in 1962, two [Comet 4Cs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Comet) were bought in May,[^cite_note-FI1962-747-8] intended as a replacement of the Viscount service;[^cite_note-FI1962-227-14] Sudan Airways had considered the acquisition of two jets for deployment on the ″Blue Nile″ route since the frequency on the service was increased to twice weekly in 1961.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998203-7] The airline took delivery of the first Comet in November 1962 (1962-11),[^cite_note-FI1962-813-10] and the second aircraft of the type was delivered a month later.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998203-7] Comets commenced flying the ″Blue Nile″ service in January 1963 (1963-01); that year, the frequency was again increased to operate three times a week. The ″Blue Nile″ service first served [Frankfurt](10992.md) in May 1963 (1963-05).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998203-7] Also in 1963, a fourth [Friendship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_F27_Friendship) was ordered.[^cite_note-FI1963-462-15] In 1967, the company became a corporation run on a commercial basis;[^cite_note-FI1973-769/770-16]<sup>: 770 </sup> also, three [Twin Otters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Otter) were ordered as a replacement for the DC-3s.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998203-7] The first of these aircraft joined the fleet in 1968;[^cite_note-FI1973-769/770-16]<sup>: 770 </sup> the second aircraft of the type delivered to the company was the 100th produced by [de Havilland Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Canada).[^cite_note-FI1968-327-17]

[^cite_note-FI1973-914-18]

&#x20;the pictured aircraft, [registered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_registration) ST-AFA, entered the fleet in 1974.

By March 1970 (1970-03), the route network totaled 20,715 kilometres (12,872 mi), with international destinations including [Aden](38050.md), [Addis Ababa](52593.md), [Asmara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asmara), [Athens](1216.md), Beirut, Cairo, [Entebbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entebbe), [Fort Lamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lamy), Jeddah, London, [Nairobi](21482.md) and Rome. At this time, the fleet was composed of two Comet 4Cs, three DC-3s, four [F-27s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_F27_Friendship) and three Twin Otters.[^cite_note-FI1970-502-19] The last passenger DC-3 left the fleet in 1971.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGradidge2006213-20] In 1972, the Comets were put on sale and were replaced by two [Boeing 707s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707) [leased](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_lease) from [British Midland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Midland).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuttery1998203-7][^cite_note-FI1973-914-18][^cite_note-FI1972-768-21] Sudan Airways ordered two [Boeing 707-320Cs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707-320C) in 1973, for delivery in June and July 1974 (1974-07).[^cite_note-FI1973-914-18] Pending delivery of two [Boeing 737-200Cs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-200C) ordered a year earlier,[^cite_note-FI1974-516-22] the two Boeing 707-320Cs were part of the fleet by March 1975 (1975-03), along with five F-27s, three Twin Otters, and a single DC-3.[^cite_note-FI1975-503-23]



A Jordan-registered [Lockheed L-1011 TriStar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_L-1011_TriStar) in Sudan Airways livery in 1987

The company had 2,362 employees at April 2000 (2000-04), with an aircraft park that included one [Airbus A300-600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A300-600), one [Airbus A300-600R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A300-600R), three [Boeing 707-320Cs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_707-320C), one [Boeing 727-200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727-200), one [Boeing 737-200C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-200C) and one [Fokker F27-600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_F27-600). By this time, the airline provided scheduled services to [Abu Dhabi](18950756.md), [Addis Ababa](52593.md), [Al Ain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Ain), [Amman](45007.md), [Bangui](56617.md), [Cairo](6293.md), [Damascus](8914.md), [Doha](26214389.md), [Dongola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongola), [Dubai](211583.md), [El Fasher](3956787.md), [El Obeid](1481380.md), [Al Dabbah](26485047.md), [Geneina](1501534.md), [Istanbul](3391396.md), [Jeddah](277718.md), [Juba](1514879.md), [Kano](508782.md), [Lagos](85232.md), [London](17867.md), [Malakal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malakal), [Merowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merowe,_Sudan), [Muscat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscat,_Oman), [Ndjamena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndjamena), [Niamey](57061.md), [Nyala](508734.md), [Paris](22989.md), [Port Sudan](213572.md), [Riyadh](51554.md), [Sanaa](18691665.md), [Sharjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharjah), [Tripoli](31232.md), [Wadi Halfa](1126657.md) and [Wau](3888859.md).[^cite_note-FI2000-105-24] In 2007, the [Sudanese government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_government) privatised the airline, maintaining only a 30% stake of the national carrier.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-Sudanese_government_sells_70%_of_flag_carrier-26] The [Kuwaiti](7515890.md) private group that owned 49% of the shares since then sold its stake back to the state in 2011.[^cite_note-Sanctions_are_hell-3]

In the wake of the crash of [Flight 109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_Airways_Flight_109), in June 2008 (2008-06) the airline was grounded following an indefinite suspension of its operating certificate by the Sudanese government,[^cite_note-Other_News_-_06/23/2008-27][^cite_note-Sudan_grounds_its_national_airline-28][^cite_note-Sudan_crash_airline_is_grounded-29] despite the fact that it was stated as not being in connection with the accident.[^cite_note-Sudan_grounds_national_airline-30] This decision was later rolled back, and the company was allowed to resume operations.[^cite_note-Reprieve_for_Sudan_Air-31][^cite_note-Sudan_lifts_suspension_on_national_carrier_for_two_weeks-32]



Sudan Airways [Boeing 737-200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-200) at [London Heathrow Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Heathrow_Airport) in 1989. The type pictured here would be later involved in a [fatal accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_Airways_Flight_139) in 2003.

In 2017, it was announced that the Sudanese President Omar al Bashir signed several cooperation agreements with King Salman of Saudi Arabia during a visit to Riyadh. Among the agreements was a pledge from the Saudi General Authority of Civil Aviation to restructure SAR22.5 million riyals (US\$6 million) worth of debt. In addition, provisions for fleet renewal at Sudan Airways were also made. It was reported Saudi Arabia may equip the Sudan Airways with fourteen aircraft including three B777s, three A320-200s, six Embraer Regional Jets, and two A330-200s.[^cite_note-33]

Following the lifting of American sanctions in 2017, Sudan Airways announced plans to revive its fleet.[^cite_note-veconomist-34]

### European Union ban

In late March 2010 (2010-03), all Sudan-based airlines were banned by the [European Union](9317.md) (EU) from flying into or within the member states due to safety concerns.[^cite_note-New_EU_blacklist_features_Iran_Air,_Philippine_carriers-35][^cite_note-EU_bans_all_airlines_from_Sudan_over_safety_concerns-36] All the subsequent released ban lists included all airlines with an [operator's certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_operator's_certificate) issued in Sudan as banned to operate into the member countries of the EU.[^cite_note-37]

## Accidents and incidents
