**L 98-59** (TOI-175, TIC 307210830) is a [red dwarf](56099.md) star located in the [constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation) of [Volans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volans), at a distance of 10.608 [parsecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsec) (34.60 [light-years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-year)), as measured by the [*Gaia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaia_\(spacecraft\)) spacecraft.[^cite_note-Cloutier2019-8]

Broadband photometry shows that it is an M [dwarf star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_star), redder, smaller and fainter than the [Sun](26751.md). It has three confirmed terrestrial-size planets in transit, which were announced in 2019 by the [Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite](20024409.md) (TESS),[^cite_note-Kostov2019-9] as well as two additional non-transiting planets, for a total of five known planets. The outermost planet is in the [habitable zone](1072751.md).[^cite_note-Schwarz2026-10]

## Planetary system

The planets L 98-59 b, c, and d were discovered in 2019 by [TESS](20024409.md).[^cite_note-Kostov2019-9] The non-transiting potential super-Venus planet L 98-59 e was discovered in 2021, along with indications of the presence of L 98-59 f, another non-transiting [super-Earth](10883868.md) located in the system's habitable zone.[^cite_note-Demangeon2021-4][^cite_note-eso2112-11] In 2025, the planet f was confirmed, and an additional planet candidate orbiting interior to planet b was detected.[^cite_note-Schwarz2026-10]

The two innermost confirmed planets, [L 98-59 b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_98-59_b) and c, as well as L 98-59 e are possibly hot rocky planets. [L 98-59 d](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_98-59_d) has a low [density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density), around 2.2 g/cm<sup>3</sup> versus 5.5 g/cm<sup>3</sup> for Earth, indicating large amounts of volatiles.[^cite_note-ES-20210806-12]

### Atmosphere characterization

In September 2021, suggested tests of the abilities of the [Hubble Space Telescope](40203.md) and the [James Webb Space Telescope](434221.md) (JWST) to detect and describe the atmospheric features of the three inner planets were reported.[^cite_note-AJ-20210929-13] In 2022, Hubble observations of three transiting planets found with no clear evidence of [atmospheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere), with high mean [molecular weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_weight) atmospheres, cloudy or hazy atmospheres, or no atmospheres all being consistent with the observed flat spectra.[^cite_note-Damiano2022-14][^cite_note-Zhou2022-15][^cite_note-Zhou2023-16][^cite_note-Barclay2025-17] However, the JWST did find evidence of [sulfur dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide), [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen), and [hydrogen sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_sulfide) for planets b and d, suggesting that both are sulfur-rich volcanic worlds with strong [tidal heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_heating),[^cite_note-2024ApJ...975L..10G-18][^cite_note-2024ApJ...975L..11B-19] and may contain a global internal [magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma) ocean.[^cite_note-Nicholls2026-6][^cite_note-BelloArufe2025-20][^cite_note-Oxford_2026-03-16_L98-59d-21] A 2026 study was able to confirm the presence of hydrogen sulfide for planet d using ground-based telescope.[^cite_note-22]
