***Alta Velocidad Española*** (**AVE**)[^cite_note-7] is a [high-speed rail service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_service) operated by [Renfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfe), the Spanish State railway company. The first AVE service was inaugurated in 1992, with the introduction of the first Spanish high-speed railway connecting the cities of [Madrid](41188263.md), [Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B3rdoba,_Andalusia) and [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville). In addition to Renfe's use of the [Administrador de Infraestructuras Ferroviarias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrador_de_Infraestructuras_Ferroviarias)-managed rail infrastructure in Spain, Renfe offers two AVE services partially in France, connecting respectively Barcelona-Lyon and Madrid-Marseille.[^cite_note-8] *Alta Velocidad Española* translates to "Spanish High Speed", but the initials are also a play on the word *ave*, meaning "bird". AVE trains operate at speeds of up to 300 km/h (186 mph).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-geotren-max-speeds-10]

## Services

As of 2024 Renfe offers the following AVE services:[^cite_note-11]

### Eastern corridor

- Madrid–Alicante via Cuenca, Albacete, and Villena (non-stop trains and trains with limited stops also scheduled).

- Madrid–Castellón via Cuenca, Requena-Utiel and Valencia.

- Madrid–Murcia via Elche and Orihuela (some trains arrive in Alicante and reverse to continue to Murcia).

- Madrid–Valencia via Cuenca and Requena-Utiel (non-stop trains also scheduled).

### Northeast corridor

- Madrid–Barcelona via Guadalajara, Calatayud, Zaragoza, Lleida, and Tarragona (non stop trains and trains with selective stops are also scheduled).

- Madrid–Figueres via Guadalajara, Calatayud, Zaragoza, Lleida, Tarragona, Barcelona and Girona (trains are scheduled with selective stops).

- Madrid–Huesca via Guadalajara, Calatayud, Zaragoza, and Tardienta.

### Northern corridor

- Madrid–Gijón via Valladolid, Palencia, León and Oviedo.

- Madrid–León via Segovia, Valladolid and Palencia.

### Northwest corridor

- Madrid–A Coruña via Zamora, Ourense and Santiago De Compostela.

- Madrid–Ourense via Zamora.

- Madrid–Vigo via Zamora, Sanabria, A Gudiña, Ourense, Santiago de Compostela, Vilagarcía de Arousa and Pontevedra (trains with selective stops are also scheduled).

### Southern corridor

- Madrid–Granada via Ciudad Real, Puertollano, Córdoba, Puente Genil-Herrera, Antequera and Loja (trains with selective stops are also scheduled).

- Madrid–Málaga via Ciudad Real, Puertollano, Córdoba, Puente Genil-Herrera, and Antequera (non stop trains and trains with selective stops are also scheduled).

- Madrid–Seville via Ciudad Real, Puertollano, and Córdoba (non stop trains and trains with selective stops are also scheduled).

### Cross-country

- Alicante–León via Albacete, Cuenca, Madrid Chamartín, Valladolid and Palencia.

- Alicante–Ourense via Albacete, Cuenca, Madrid Chamartín and Zamora.

- Barcelona–Granada via Tarragona, Lleida, Zaragoza, Ciudad Real, Puertollano, Córdoba and Antequera.

- Barcelona–Málaga via Tarragona, Lleida, Zaragoza, Ciudad Real, Córdoba, Puente Genil-Herrera, and Antequera.

- Barcelona–Seville via Tarragona, Lleida, Zaragoza, Ciudad Real, Puertollano and Córdoba (trains with selective stops are also scheduled).

- Burgos–Murcia via Valladolid, Segovia, Madrid-Chamartín, Elche and Orihuela.

- Gijón–Castellón via Oviedo, Mieres Del Camín, La Pola, León, Palencia, Valladolid, Segovia, Madrid-Chamartín, Cuenca, Valencia and Sagunto.

- Gijón–Vinaros, via Oviedo, Mieres Del Camín, La Pola, León, Palencia, Valladolid, Segovia, Madrid-Chamartín, Cuenca, Valencia, Sagunto, Castellón, Benicàssim, Oropesa del Mar and Benicarló (only in summertime).

- Huesca–Seville via Tardienta, Zaragoza, Calatayud, Guadalajara, Madrid-Puerta de Atocha and Córdoba

- Málaga–Murcia via Madrid-Puerta de Atocha, Cuenca, Albacete, Villena, Alicante, Elche and Orihuela.

- Valencia–Burgos via Requena-Utiel, Cuenca, Madrid Chamartín and Valladolid (trains with selective stops are also scheduled).

- Valencia–León via Requena-Utiel, Cuenca, Madrid-Chamartín, Segovia, Valladolid and Palencia (trains with selective stops are also scheduled).

- Valencia–Seville via Cuenca, Ciudad Real, Puertollano, and Córdoba.

### International services

Source:[^cite_note-12]

- Barcelona–Lyon via Girona, Figueres, Perpignan, Narbonne, Montpellier, Nîmes, and Valence.

- Madrid–Marseille via Guadalajara, Zaragoza, Tarragona, Barcelona, Girona, Figueres, Perpignan, Narbonne, Béziers, Montpellier, Nîmes, Avignon and Aix-en-Provence.

The central hub of the AVE system is Madrid's [Puerta de Atocha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Atocha_railway_station), except for the [Madrid–Asturias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid%E2%80%93Asturias_high-speed_rail_line), Madrid–Burgos, [Madrid–Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid%E2%80%93Galicia_high-speed_rail_line) and Madrid–Alicante lines as well as the majority of the services on the Madrid-Murcia and Madrid-Valencia lines, that terminate at [Chamartín station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Chamart%C3%ADn_railway_station).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Madrid Atocha and Chamartín station are linked by [Madrid Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Metro) ([Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_1_\(Madrid_Metro\))) and [Cercanías Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercan%C3%ADas_Madrid) services but As of 2025 not by any standard gauge lines, making it difficult for high speed trains to serve both.

## Trains

There are several series of high-speed trains that run the AVE service:

- [S-100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfe_Class_100), manufactured by [Alstom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alstom), based on the [TGV](57212.md) family trains.

- [S-102](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfe_Class_102), manufactured by [Talgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talgo) and [Bombardier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Transportation), marketed globally as Talgo 350.

- [S-103](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfe_Class_103), manufactured by [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens), marketed globally under the brand [Siemens Velaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens_Velaro).

- [S-106](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talgo_AVRIL), manufactured by Talgo, marketed globally as Talgo AVRIL.

- [S-112](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfe_Class_112), manufactured by Talgo and Bombardier, an improved version of the S-102 with a different seat layout.

## Passenger usage

The still-growing network transported a record 43.8 million passengers in 2025.[^cite_note-AveINE-15] Though the network length is extensive, it lags in ridership behind comparable high-speed rail systems in Japan, France, Germany, China, Taiwan, and Korea.
