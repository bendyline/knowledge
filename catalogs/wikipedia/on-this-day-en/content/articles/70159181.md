Protests against the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) occurred simultaneously in many places worldwide, including [in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_anti-war_protests_in_Russia) and [in Russian-occupied Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_protests_in_Russian-occupied_Ukraine).

## In Russia

Main article: [Anti-war protests in Russia (2022–present)](70159281.md)

See also: [2022 North Caucasian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_North_Caucasian_protests) and [2022 Russian Far East protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Russian_Far_East_protests)

In the seven days from 24 February to 2 March, over 6,500 people in 53 cities across Russia have been detained by police for protesting against the invasion.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-CNN-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] Since the start of the war until 6 March nearly 13,000 have been detained.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

On 24 February, over 60 Russian activists and journalists have been arrested.[^cite_note-10]

In violation of the [Russian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_assembly_in_Russia) and [international human rights law](219877.md),[^cite_note-11] Putin has made [peaceful public protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_assembly) without permission of the authorities illegal.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

This background information is necessary to understand the extreme intimidation caused by the large number of arrests and by the seemingly reasonable warning of the authorities to not take part in "unsanctioned" protests.[^cite_note-14] Russian authorities warned Russians of legal repercussions for joining anti-war protests.[^cite_note-aljazeera-protests-15]

On 27 February, another 2,063 people were detained at street protests against the war.[^cite_note-16]

On 27 February, a van with markings that read "People, wake up!", "This is war", "Putin is scum!" crashed and caught fire in [Pushkinskaya Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushkinskaya_Square).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

More than 30,000 technology workers,[^cite_note-19] 6,000 medical workers, 3,400 architects,[^cite_note-20] more than 4,300 teachers,[^cite_note-21] more than 17,000 artists,[^cite_note-artists-invasion-22] 5,000 scientists,[^cite_note-23] and 2,000 actors, directors, and other creative figures signed open letters calling for Putin's government to stop the war.[^cite_note-rferl-petition-24][^cite_note-france24-protests-25] Some Russians who signed petitions against Russia's war in Ukraine have already lost their jobs.[^cite_note-26]

Over 281,000 Russians signed a petition to impeach Putin.[^cite_note-27]

On 9 March, an unnamed person burned down a [military comissariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_comissariat) in [Lukhovitsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukhovitsy) ([Moscow Oblast](372315.md)) with the intent of destroying the personal files of conscripts to prevent them from being sent to war.[^cite_note-28]

### Academics

Troitsky variant (also known as TrV-Nauka), an independent Russian popular science newspaper, published an open letter against war signed by more than 7400 Russian scientists including many famous academics and members of the [Russian Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Academy_of_Sciences).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] Oleg Anisimov, a scientist delegate from Russia, apologized to his colleague from Ukraine at the UN climate conference.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] [Mikhail Gelfand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gelfand), bioinformatician and a member of the [Academia Europaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Europaea), also made a statement against war.[^cite_note-35]

1,200 students, faculty and staff of the [Moscow State Institute of International Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_Institute_of_International_Relations), affiliated with the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](4432213.md), signed an open letter stating that they "consider it morally unacceptable to stay on the sidelines and keep silent when people are dying in a neighboring state. They are dying through the fault of those who preferred weapons instead of peaceful diplomacy. .... Many generations of future diplomats will have to rebuild the trust in Russia and the good relations with our neighbors that have been lost."[^cite_note-36] Students, graduate students, teachers, staff and graduates of the oldest university in Russia, [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) named after M.V. Lomonosov – 7500 people in total – also signed an open letter against the "special military operation".[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

### Activists

On 24 February, human rights activist [Lev Ponomaryov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lev_Ponomaryov) started a petition to protest the invasion, which gathered more than 1,5 million signatures by 3 March.[^cite_note-39]

### Businesspeople and oligarchs

A number of Russian entrepreneurs, businesspeople and billionaires, including [Oleg Deripaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Deripaska),[^cite_note-Guardian-40][^cite_note-RFERL-41] [Mikhail Fridman](354597.md),[^cite_note-Guardian-40] [Oleg Tinkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Tinkov),[^cite_note-WSJ-42] [Mikhail Khodorkovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Khodorkovsky),[^cite_note-43] Nikolay Storonsky of [Revolut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolut),[^cite_note-44] [Vladimir Lisin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lisin),[^cite_note-RFERL-41] [Alexei Mordashov](1324566.md)[^cite_note-RFERL-41] and [Andrei Melnichenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Melnichenko_\(industrialist\)),[^cite_note-45] have spoken out against the invasion, and called on for the Russian military forces to be withdrawn from Ukraine.

On 3 March, [Lukoil](1027881.md), the second-largest [Russian energy company](14049009.md) after Gazprom, called for a ceasefire and diplomatic means to solve the conflict.[^cite_note-46]

### Celebrities



Russian rapper [Oxxxymiron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxxxymiron) announced a series of [benefit concerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benefit_concert) outside Russia, titled *"Russians Against War"*, the proceeds from which would be donated to NGOs helping [Ukrainian refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ukrainian_refugee_crisis).

The invasion was condemned by television presenter [Ksenia Sobchak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ksenia_Sobchak), pop star [Valery Meladze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valery_Meladze), writer [Dmitry Glukhovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Glukhovsky), journalist and YouTuber [Yury Dud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yury_Dud), film director [Roman Volobuev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Volobuev), rapper [Noize MC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noize_MC), Dynamo Moscow striker [Fyodor Smolov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Smolov), actress [Chulpan Khamatova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chulpan_Khamatova), actor [Danila Kozlovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danila_Kozlovsky)[^cite_note-screendaily.com-47][^cite_note-48] and the television host [Ivan Urgant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Urgant).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-aljazeera-protest-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] Urgant's late-night show subsequently disappeared from the scheduled programs on the state-owned TV station [Channel One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_One_Russia).[^cite_note-aljazeera-protest-50] Dozens of other Russian artists, TV presenters and other celebrities spoke out on social networks against Russia's military actions in Ukraine.[^cite_note-53]

Russian rapper [Oxxxymiron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxxxymiron) cancelled six sold-out concerts in Moscow and St. Petersburg, stating, "I cannot entertain you when Russian missiles are falling on Ukraine. When residents of Kyiv are forced to hide in basements and in the metro, while people are dying."[^cite_note-54] He went on to announce a series of [benefit concerts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benefit_concert) in other countries, entitled "Russians Against War", the proceeds from which would be donated to NGOs helping Ukrainian refugees. The first of these concerts was held in [Istanbul](3391396.md), which has a large Russian diaspora consisting of people who left the country in protest of the invasion.[^cite_note-55]

In September 2022, Russian pop legend [Alla Pugacheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alla_Pugacheva) spoke out against the invasion, writing that Russians were dying in Ukraine for "illusory goals", and that the invasion was "turning our country into a pariah and worsening the lives of our citizens."[^cite_note-56] Russian authorities began investigating Pugacheva for "discrediting" the military.[^cite_note-57]

Russian comedian and singer [Maxim Galkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_Galkin) has been openly critical about the Russian invasion of Ukraine. He accused the Russian authorities of hypocrisy and lies with respect to war crimes that Russia has committed in firing rockets at the Ukrainian city of [Odesa](55545.md), its destruction of [Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariupol) and its [atrocities in Bucha](70455343.md).[^cite_note-58]



[Maxim Galkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_Galkin) publicly condemned the invasion

In October 2022, Russian actor [Artur Smolyaninov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artur_Smolyaninov) was charged for "discrediting" the Russian army – that was after his anti-war-statements and after he had left Russia. In an interview in early January 2023, he stated that he would fight for Ukraine, not Russia, if he had to take part in the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md).[^cite_note-59]

### Sports

NHL hockey player [Alexander Ovechkin](755749.md) criticized Russia's invasion of Ukraine.[^cite_note-60] Tennis players [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev), [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev) and [Yevgeny Kafelnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevgeny_Kafelnikov) and other Russian sportsmen also made statements against the war.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

Figure skater [Evgenia Medvedeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgenia_Medvedeva) spoke out on her Instagram account, writing "I hope this all ends as soon as possible, like a bad dream."[^cite_note-63]

Dutch motorsports racer [Max Verstappen](41758713.md) refused to race in Russia and said “When a country is at war, it is not correct to race there.”[^cite_note-64]

### Military

A commander of Russia's [74th Guards Motor Rifle Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/74th_Guards_Motor_Rifle_Brigade) was reportedly quoted as saying; "We were not going to fight – we were collecting information" after the entire platoon was reportedly captured in [Chernihiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv).[^cite_note-65] These comments were frequently shown in videos published by the Ukrainian military, of captured Russian soldiers that were potentially made under duress. The soldiers in the video's made comments such as; "I didn't know we were going to Ukraine, I was tricked." and "We were deceived and used like a meat shield."[^cite_note-66] Others were reportedly refusing to carry out orders or sabotaging their own equipment in order to avoid combat, while suffering from lack of resources and low morale.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

### Organizations

The founders of the "[Immortal Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immortal_Regiment)" commemoration movement, in which ordinary Russians carry photographs of veteran family members in marches around Russia held annually to mark WWII [Victory Day](337670.md) on 9 May, called on the Putin "to cease fire", describing the use of force as "inhuman".[^cite_note-france24-protests-25]

Olga Larkina, the director of Russia's [Committee of Soldiers' Mothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Soldiers'_Mothers_of_Russia), raised concerns to Russian investigative news outlet *[Meduza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meduza_\(website\))* that many of the soldiers in Ukraine had been forcibly sent to Ukraine. Larkina alleged that Russian conscripts had been pressured or forced into signing contracts to become soldiers, sent to Ukraine and family members have lost contact with them.[^cite_note-69]

### Politicians and government officials



[Novosibirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk) [city councilor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_council) [Helga Pirogova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helga_Pirogova?action=edit\&redlink=1) wearing the Ukrainian [vyshyvanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyshyvanka) and a wreath of sunflowers to protest the invasion

Jailed Russian opposition leader [Alexei Navalny](29174356.md) condemned Russia's attack on Ukraine and called those who launched the war "bandits and thieves."[^cite_note-70] He also called for more anti-war protests as well as protests against Putin.[^cite_note-71] Russian opposition activist and politician [Marina Litvinovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Litvinovich) called for [anti-war protests](39766716.md) in Russian cities. She was detained by Russian police as she left her house.[^cite_note-72]

[State Duma](1010187.md) deputy [Mikhail Matveev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Matveyev_\(politician\)) voted in favor of the [recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_the_Donetsk_People's_Republic_and_the_Luhansk_People's_Republic) of the Donetsk and Luhansk People's Republics but later denounced the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, stating "I voted for peace, not for war. I wanted Russia to become a shield so that Donbas would not be bombed, not for Kyiv to be bombed."[^cite_note-73] State Duma deputy [Oleg Smolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Smolin) said he was "shocked" by the invasion.[^cite_note-pbs-protests-74] Liza Peskova, daughter of Putin's spokesman [Dmitry Peskov](9214683.md), shared an image with the "No to war" hashtag on Instagram.[^cite_note-75]

More than 100 Russian municipal deputies signed a letter against the war with Ukraine.[^cite_note-76] One of the deputies, Alexander Budberg, sent a request to State Duma for the impeachment of Vladimir Putin.[^cite_note-77]

On 27 February, Russian politician [Lyudmila Narusova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyudmila_Narusova), a member of the [Federation Council](1268578.md), stated in a television interview: "I do not identify myself with those representatives of the state that speak out in favor of the war. I think they themselves do not know what they are doing. They are following orders without thinking."[^cite_note-78]

[Arkady Dvorkovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkady_Dvorkovich), who served as Russia's Deputy Prime Minister from 2012 to 2018, also condemned the invasion.[^cite_note-79]

On 16 March, the member of [Novosibirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk) [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_council) Helga Pirogova called on to immediately withdraw the Russian troops from Ukrainian territory, and attended the city council meeting wearing a blue [vyshyvanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyshyvanka) and a wreath of sunflowers (both of which are traditional symbols of Ukraine) to protest the invasion.[^cite_note-80]

On 23 March, Putin's longtime advisor and Russian climate envoy [Anatoly Chubais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Chubais) resigned from his position and left Russia due to his opposition to the war.[^cite_note-81]



[Ilya Yashin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Yashin) was sentenced to eight-and-a-half years in prison for his anti-war statements

On 23 May 2022, [Boris Bondarev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Bondarev), a senior career diplomat, announced that he had resigned from his position in protest over the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), referring to the invasion as an "aggressive war".[^cite_note-82]

In July 2022, [Alexei Gorinov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Gorinov), a member of the [Krasnoselsky district council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnoselsky_District,_Moscow) in Moscow, was sentenced to seven years in prison after making anti-war comments at a council meeting in March,[^cite_note-83] including stating that "our country has aggressively attacked a neighbouring country" and "kids in Ukraine are dying each day".[^cite_note-84] Lawyer [Pavel Chikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Chikov) said that this was the first jail term under the new [Russian 2022 war censorship laws](70646883.md).[^cite_note-85]

In August 2022, opposition politician [Yevgeny Roizman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevgeny_Roizman) was detained for his use of the word "invasion" for Russia's invasion of Ukraine.[^cite_note-86]

In December 2022, Russian opposition politician [Ilya Yashin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Yashin) was sentenced to 8+1⁄2 years in prison.[^cite_note-87] Yashin condemned the killings in Bucha and said that Russian forces in Ukraine were responsible for the massacre.[^cite_note-88]

### Press

Russian Nobel Peace Prize winner [Dmitry Muratov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Muratov) announced that the *[Novaya Gazeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novaya_Gazeta)* newspaper would publish its next edition in both Ukrainian and Russian. Muratov, journalist [Mikhail Zygar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Zygar), director [Vladimir Mirzoyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Mirzoyev), and others signed a document stating that Ukraine was not a threat to Russia and calling for Russian citizens "to say no to this war."[^cite_note-89] Elena Chernenko, a journalist at *[Kommersant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kommersant)*, circulated a critical open letter that was signed by 170 journalists and academics.[^cite_note-90]

### State television pundits



A digital recreation of [Marina Ovsyannikova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Ovsyannikova)'s anti-war poster as seen on a live broadcast of [Channel One Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_One_Russia).

On 10 March, during a broadcast on [Russia-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia-1), the filmmaker and pundit [Karen Shakhnazarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Shakhnazarov), who initially supported the invasion, called for an end to it, saying that the situation was at risk of becoming "an absolute humanitarian disaster", and that there is no realistic possibility for the Russian forces to seize Kyiv and other major Ukrainian cities.[^cite_note-Telegraph-91]

During the broadcast of [Sunday Evening with Vladimir Solovyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_Evening_with_Vladimir_Solovyov), the [State Duma](1010187.md) member [Semyon Bagdasarov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semyon_Bagdasarov) described the situation with Ukraine as "another [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), but even worse". He added that on the Ukrainian side, "there are more people and they're more advanced in their weapon handling \[than the Russians]", and concluded: "We don't need that. Enough already".[^cite_note-Telegraph-91]

On 14 March, a live broadcast of [Channel One Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_One_Russia) was interrupted by news editor [Marina Ovsyannikova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Ovsyannikova) holding a poster stating "No War. Stop the war, don't believe the propaganda, here you are being lied to. Russians against war." and shouting "Stop the war. No to war." before she was arrested.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] Following Ovsyannikova's protest, an anonymous source working at the [All-Russia State Television and Radio Broadcasting Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Russia_State_Television_and_Radio_Broadcasting_Company) stated that the majority of Russian state TV employees oppose the invasion, and Ovsyannikova merely voiced out loud the general sentiment of her colleagues.[^cite_note-94] This information was independently confirmed by Elena Afanasyeva, the former head of creative planning at Channel One, who said that many state TV employees disagree with the channel's coverage of the war, and by another anonymous source close to Channel One who asserted that all of the channel's employees acknowledge that they're spreading lies about the war, and are dissatisfied with this state of affairs.[^cite_note-95] Ever since 24 February, a number of employees, including [Lilia Gildeeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilia_Gildeeva), [Zhanna Agalakova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhanna_Agalakova), [Maria Baronova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Baronova) and several other [RT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT_\(TV_network\)) reporters, resigned from state TV in protest of the invasion; some of them subsequently left the country.[^cite_note-96]

### Universities

At the beginning of March, the community of [Moscow State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_State_University) issued a joint statement condemning the invasion and calling on withdraw the Russian troops from Ukraine.[^cite_note-97] On April 17, a similar statement was issued by the community of [Novosibirsk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk_State_University).[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]

### Other professional communities

Open letters against the war were also published by professional associations of doctors and healthcare workers, NGO workers, lawyers,[^cite_note-100] psychologists and psychiatrists,[^cite_note-101] teachers, students,[^cite_note-102] economists,[^cite_note-103] employees of IT companies, workers of culture and art,[^cite_note-104] comedians,[^cite_note-105] workers in the beauty and fashion industry, film makers,[^cite_note-screendaily.com-47][^cite_note-106] advertising and gaming industries, designers, animators, and architects.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109]

### Arrests

By March 6, reports regarding citizens arrested for protesting reached over 4000; an estimated 4888 protestors became the victim of the arrest in 69 cities.[^cite_note-\:2-110] About 2319 people were detained in Moscow as of March 7, and more reports suggested that police might retain more people than the issued list.[^cite_note-\:2-110][^cite_note-111]

St. Petersburg continually witnessed the proliferation of mass detentions, with 750 detained among 1500 participants. In other locations, 1061 people were arrested among 1200 people who participated in the demonstration.[^cite_note-\:2-110]

Putin introduced [prison sentences of up to 15 years](70646883.md) for publishing "fake news" about Russian military operations.[^cite_note-112] More than 2,000 people were charged by May 2022 under the laws prohibiting "fake" information about the military.[^cite_note-113] As of December 2022, more than 4,000 people were prosecuted for criticizing the war in Ukraine.[^cite_note-114]

### Russian property

On 27 February, Taras Ostapchuk, a Ukrainian mechanic, appeared in [Mallorcan](59310.md) court on charges of partially sinking the \$7 million [superyacht](3652673.md) that he worked on. He stated that his boss ran a Russian state-owned supplies of military products and that he had gotten angry at the thought that his boss's company has supplied the missile that had struck a Kyiv apartment building that he had watched on his cellphone. Ostapchuk stated, "What do I need a job for if I don't have a country?", telling the court, "I don't regret anything I've done, and I would do it again."[^cite_note-236]

On 14 March, a group called the London Maknovists, named after Ukrainian [anarchist](12.md) [Nestor Makhno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestor_Makhno), announced they had reclaimed a [Belgrave Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrave_Square) property in central London for Ukrainian refugees. The property is believed to be owned by Putin ally [Oleg Deripaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Deripaska), who was sanctioned by the UK government following the invasion. Multiple protestors hung banners off of the home's balcony and told reporters; "[Michael Gove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Gove) said oligarchs' homes should be expropriated to house Ukrainian refugees....We're doing the government's work for them..."[^cite_note-237]
