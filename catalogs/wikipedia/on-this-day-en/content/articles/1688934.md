The **Finnish women's national ice hockey team** represents [Finland](10577.md) at the [International Ice Hockey Federation](16760449.md) (IIHF) [Women's World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_World_Women's_Championship), the [Olympic Games](22576.md), the [Four Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_Nations_Cup), and other international-level women's [ice hockey](14790.md) competitions. The women's national team is overseen by the [Finnish Ice Hockey Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Ice_Hockey_Association) and its general manager is [Tuula Puputti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuula_Puputti). Finland's national women's program is ranked third in the world by the IIHF and had 5,858 active players as of 2019.[^cite_note-2]

## History

Finland has finished third or fourth in almost every World Championships and Olympics, with one exception being a fifth place finish at the [2014 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2014_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament) and second place at the [2019 World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_IIHF_Women's_World_Championship). They are ranked behind [Canada](1329942.md) (#2) and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women's_national_ice_hockey_team) (#1). Historically, Finland's primary rival was [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_women's_national_ice_hockey_team), which finished second to Canada at the [2006 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2006_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament). Finland finished fourth, losing the game for the bronze medal to the United States. Finland defeated the United States for the first time, at the [2008 World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_IIHF_Women's_World_Championship) in China, 1–0 in overtime. Finland defeated Canada 4–3 for the first time at the [2017 World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_IIHF_Women's_World_Championship) in the United States. However, Finland lost the semi-final game against Canada in the same tournament, proceeding to win the bronze medal game.

At the 2019 World Championship, Finland reached the championship final for the first time in tournament history after beating Canada 4–2 in the semi-final. During the [gold medal game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_IIHF_Women's_World_Championship_final), [Petra Nieminen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra_Nieminen) scored in overtime but her goal was overturned after a video review for [goalie interference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_\(ice_hockey\)). The IIHF released a press statement the next day citing rules 186 and 183ii as the reasons for overturning the goal. Finland finished as runners-up and won a silver medal after losing to the United States in a shootout.[^cite_note-3]
