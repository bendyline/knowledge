**Hiroshima Prefecture** (広島県, *Hiroshima-ken*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[çi.ɾo.ɕi.ma, -maꜜ.keɴ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-2]) is a [prefecture](74517.md) of [Japan](15573.md) located in the [Chūgoku region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%ABgoku_region) of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu).[^cite_note-3] Hiroshima Prefecture has a population of 2,811,410 (1 June 2019) and has a geographic area of 8,479 km<sup>2</sup> (3,274 sq mi). Hiroshima Prefecture borders [Okayama Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okayama_Prefecture) to the east, [Tottori Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottori_Prefecture) to the northeast, [Shimane Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimane_Prefecture) to the north, and [Yamaguchi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi_Prefecture) to the southwest. Hiroshima Prefecture also borders [Ehime Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehime_Prefecture) for 74 metres (243 ft) on [Hyōtanjima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hy%C5%8Dtanjima).[^cite_note-4]

[Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima) is the capital and largest city of Hiroshima Prefecture, and the largest city in the Chūgoku region, with other major cities including [Fukuyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuyama,_Hiroshima), [Kure](329326.md), and [Higashihiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higashihiroshima).[^cite_note-5] Hiroshima Prefecture is located on the [Seto Inland Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seto_Inland_Sea) across from the island of [Shikoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikoku), and is bounded to the north by the [Chūgoku Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%ABgoku_Mountains). Hiroshima Prefecture is one of the three prefectures of Japan with more than one [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md). Known globally for the atomic bombing of 1945, Hiroshima has since rebuilt into a major regional center of industry, education, and culture. Its demographic profile is particularly significant from a population-studies perspective: the city has undergone dramatic population reconstruction after WWII, experienced the demographic transitions associated with Japan’s rapid economic growth, and now faces the contemporary challenges of low fertility, population aging, and gradual population decline.

## History

See also: [Historic Sites of Hiroshima Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Sites_of_Hiroshima_Prefecture)

The area around Hiroshima was formerly divided into [Bingo Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingo_Province) and [Aki Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aki_Province).[^cite_note-6] This location has been a center of trade and culture since the beginning of Japan's recorded history. Hiroshima is a traditional center of the Chūgoku region and was the seat of the [Mōri clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C5%8Dri_clan) until the [Battle of Sekigahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sekigahara).

Together with [Nara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_Prefecture) and [Tokyo](30057.md), Hiroshima is one of the three prefectures with more than one [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md). The two such sites in Hiroshima Prefecture are:

- The [Atomic Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima_Peace_Memorial) in Hiroshima, one of the few remnants of prewar Hiroshima following the [atomic bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki) in 1945;

- The [Itsukushima Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itsukushima_Shrine) in [Miyajima](1067163.md), famed for filling with water and appearing to "float" during high tide.

Hiroshima prefecture lies in the middle of Japan. Most of the prefecture consists of mountains leading towards [Shimane Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimane_Prefecture); and rivers produce rich plains near the coast.

The province faces [Shikoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikoku) across the [Seto Inland Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seto_Inland_Sea). [Hiroshima Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima_Bay) opens on the [Inland Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seto_Inland_Sea).[^cite_note-7] The prefecture also includes many small islands.

The sheltered nature of the Inland Sea makes Hiroshima's climate very mild.

As of 1 April 2014, 4% of the total land area of the prefecture was designated as [Natural Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan#History) (the lowest percentage of any prefecture), namely [Setonaikai National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Setonaikai_National_Park); [Hiba-Dōgo-Taishaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiba-Dogo-Taishaku_Quasi-National_Park) and [Nishi-Chūgoku Sanchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishi-Chugoku_Sanchi_Quasi-National_Park) Quasi-National Parks; and six Prefectural Natural Parks.[^cite_note-8]

Hiroshima's main industries include automobiles ([Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazda) is headquartered there) and tourism in two [World Heritage Sites](44940.md): the [A-Bomb dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima_Peace_Memorial) and [Itsukushima Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itsukushima_Shrine).

Components of the economy are primary industry, secondary industry, and tertiary industry, which compose 0.6%, 32.6%, and 66.2% in 2015. There is 0.6% of unclassified production.[^cite_note-10]

Value of production of manufacturing is 10,343 billion yen in 2016, which is the 10th largest in Japan. After 2012, production of manufacturing is continuously increasing in 2015.[^cite_note-11]

## Religion

Religious denominations in the Hiroshima Prefecture (1996)

[^cite_note-1996statistics-12]

1. Pure Land Buddhism (35.3%)

2. Zen Buddhism (3.60%)

3. Tendai or Shingon Buddhism (4.40%)

4. Soka Gakkai (4.90%)

5. Nichiren Buddhism (3.00%)

6. Other Buddhist schools (1.00%)

7. Christianity (2.00%)

8. Shinto sects (2.00%)

9. Folk Shinto or no religion (44.8%)

Similar to the rest of Japan, most people in the Hiroshima Prefecture are [Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto) and or [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist). In 1996, 51.2% of the population was Buddhist, 2% were affiliated with Shinto Sects, 44.8% practiced Folk Shinto, and 2% were Christian.[^cite_note-1996statistics-12]
