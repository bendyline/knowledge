**Mohammed Odeh**[^cite_note-12] ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مُحَمَّد عَوْدَة‎, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Muḥammad ʿŌde*; 1974 – 26 May 2026) was a Palestinian [Hamas](13913.md) militant who briefly served as the [leader of Hamas](38626632.md#Hamas_leader_in_the_Gaza_Strip) in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) and as the [commander](77528143.md#List_of_military_leaders_in_the_Gaza_Strip) of the [Al-Qassam Brigades](1703333.md) in May 2026, following the assassination of his predecessor, [Izz al-Din al-Haddad](79997859.md), before being assassinated himself later that month.

He was a member of the Brigades' Military Council and had long served as its head of [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_\(information\)). Following the assassination of [Ra'ad Sa'ad](80190564.md) in December 2025, he reportedly took over responsibility for the Brigades' operations and weapons manufacturing.[^cite_note-\:1-3][^cite_note-aawsat-1]

He was reportedly among the last members of the Brigades' senior leadership council.[^cite_note-auto-13]

## Early life

Odeh was born in 1974 in al-Khulafa al-Rashidin in the [Jabalia refugee camp](18169472.md).[^cite_note-janoubia-14][^cite_note-auto1-4]

## Militant career

Odeh joined [Hamas](13913.md) during the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada) in 1987. He was a member of the Majd force, which was responsible for pursuing Palestinian collaborators with Israel. He joined [al-Qassam Brigades](1703333.md) in 2000, being one of the first people to participate in its activities during the [Second Intifada](71717.md). He held relations with Hamas leaders such as [Yahya Sinwar](53195047.md), [Nizar Rayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizar_Rayan), and [Mohammed Deif](92534.md).[^cite_note-aawsat-1][^cite_note-janoubia-14]

While primarily working in the intelligence sector, Odeh also commanded a battalion in central [Jabalia](453723.md) for several years before briefly joining the al-Qassam Brigades' arms manufacturing department. Between 2017 and 2019, he served as the commander of the militia's northern Gaza brigade. During this time, he hosted [Mohammed Sinwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Sinwar) at a tunnel in [Beit Hanoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Hanoun).[^cite_note-aawsat-1][^cite_note-janoubia-14]

He played a key role in the development of the al-Qassam Brigades' intelligence division, particularly after the discovery of an Israeli espionage ring in Gaza in November 2018, which Hamas described as an "intelligence treasure trove". As commander of the intelligence division, he oversaw the gathering of information on Israeli military bases in the [Gaza envelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_envelope). He emphasized analyzing weaknesses in the [IDF](39237.md)'s [Gaza Division](6405376.md), and constantly handed intelligence over to Hamas's military council.[^cite_note-aawsat-1][^cite_note-janoubia-14]

During the Gaza war, he was a close associate of [Izz al-Din al-Haddad](79997859.md), being in continuous contact with him. The two developed plans to restructure the military command after the [assassinations of Mohammed Deif](77347017.md) and [Mohammed Sinwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Gaza_European_Hospital_strikes).[^cite_note-janoubia-14] After the assassination of Mohammed Sinwar in May 2025, Odeh was offered the leadership of the al-Qassam Brigades, but declined at that time.[^cite_note-aawsat-1] In October 2025, it was reported that he had replaced [Ahmed Ghandour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Ghandour_\(militant\)), who died in 2023, as Commander of the al-Qassam Brigades in North Gaza.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] In December, he took command of the group's operations and arms production wings after the killing of [Ra'ad Sa'ad](80190564.md).[^cite_note-\:1-3] According to sources close to Hamas, he was possibly the last member of the Brigades' higher leadership council.[^cite_note-17] On 15 May 2026, after al-Haddad was killed in an Israeli airstrike, Odeh was chosen to succeed him as the commander of the al-Qassam Brigades.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-aawsat-1]

On 18 May 2026, three days after he became Commander of the Izz al-Din al-Qassam Brigades, he also became the Hamas leader in the Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-aawsat-1] With him in command the al-Qassam Brigades are considered to have held the *[de facto](43594.md)* leadership over [Hamas](13913.md).[^cite_note-21]

## Assassination

Odeh survived several Israeli assassination attempts during and before the [Gaza war](74998519.md), including one after the [October 2025 ceasefire](81215079.md) that targeted his father's home in the Jabalia refugee camp, which killed his oldest son, Amr.[^cite_note-aawsat-1]

On 26 May 2026, 11 days after Odeh's predecessor al-Haddad's assassination, Israeli prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) and defense minister [Israel Katz](19041378.md) announced that the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) had targeted Odeh in an airstrike. The strikes reportedly hit a building in [Rimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimal), [Gaza City](55387.md), killing at least three and injuring over 20 others.[^cite_note-auto-13][^cite_note-22] On 27 May 2026, Odeh's death was confirmed by Katz.[^cite_note-23] Gaza media outlets confirmed that he was killed alongside his wife and three of his children.[^cite_note-24] Hamas and its military wing later confirmed his death.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] After his death, it was speculated that [Hussein Fayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_Fayyad), [Haitham Khuwajari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitham_Khuwajari), [Nafez Sabih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nafez_Sabih) or [Imad Aqel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imad_Aqel_\(Palestinian_militant\)) would succeed him.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]
