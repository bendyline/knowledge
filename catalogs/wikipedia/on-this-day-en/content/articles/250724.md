**Odisha** ([Oriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriya_language): *oṛiśā*, pronounced [\[oˈɽisa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Odia) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b4/Or-%E0%AC%93%E0%AC%A1%E0%AC%BC%E0%AC%BF%E0%AC%B6%E0%AC%BE.oga/Or-%E0%AC%93%E0%AC%A1%E0%AC%BC%E0%AC%BF%E0%AC%B6%E0%AC%BE.oga.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b4/Or-%E0%AC%93%E0%AC%A1%E0%AC%BC%E0%AC%BF%E0%AC%B6%E0%AC%BE.oga/Or-%E0%AC%93%E0%AC%A1%E0%AC%BC%E0%AC%BF%E0%AC%B6%E0%AC%BE.oga.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), spelled **Orissa** until 2011,[^cite_note-17] is a [state](375986.md) located in [eastern India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India). It is the [eighth-largest state by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_area), and the [eleventh-largest by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_union_territories_of_India_by_population), with over 41 million inhabitants. The state also has the third-largest population of [Scheduled Tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_and_Scheduled_Tribes) in [India](14533.md).[^cite_note-18] It neighbours the states of [Jharkhand](197225.md) and [West Bengal](34040.md) to the north, [Chhattisgarh](47734.md) to the west, and [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md) to the south. Odisha has a [coastline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastline) of 485 kilometres (301 mi) along the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md).[^cite_note-CoastalSecurity-19] The region is also known as **Utkaḷa** and is mentioned by this name in India's national anthem, [Jana Gana Mana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jana_Gana_Mana).

The ancient kingdom of [Kalinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_\(historical_region\)), which was invaded by the [Mauryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire) Emperor [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka) in 261 BCE resulting in the [Kalinga War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_War), coincides with the borders of modern-day Odisha.[^cite_note-GovHistory-20] The modern boundaries of Odisha were demarcated by the [British Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India) government, the [Orissa Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orissa_Province) was established on 1 April 1936, consisting of the Odia-speaking districts of [Bihar and Orissa Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihar_and_Orissa_Province), [Madras Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras_Presidency) and [Central Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Provinces).[^cite_note-GovHistory-20] [Utkala Divas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odisha_Day) (lit. 'Odisha Day') is celebrated on 1 April.[^cite_note-FlavoursOfOdisha-21] [Cuttack](1776398.md) was made the capital of the region by [Anantavarman Chodaganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anantavarman_Chodaganga) in c. 1135,[^cite_note-Chakraborty1985-22] after which the city was used as the capital by many rulers, through the British era until 1948. Thereafter, [Bhubaneswar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhubaneswar) became the capital of Odisha.[^cite_note-Kalia1994-23]

## Etymology

See also: [History of Odisha § Historical names of Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Odisha#Historical_names_of_Odisha)

The terms *Odisha* and *Orissa* ([Odia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language): ଓଡ଼ିଶା, *Oṛissa*) derive from the ancient [Prakrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prakrit) word *Odda Visaya* (also *Udra Bibhasha* or *Odra Bibhasha*) as in the [Tirumalai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirumalai_\(Jain_complex\)) inscription of [Rajendra Chola I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_Chola_I), which is dated to 1025.[^cite_note-orissareview-24] [Sarala Das](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarala_Das), who translated the *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata)* into the [Odia language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language) in the 15th century, refers to the Odra Rashtra as "Odisha". The inscriptions of [Kapilendra Deva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapilendra_Deva) of the [Gajapati Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gajapati_Kingdom) (1435–67) on the walls of temples in [Puri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puri_\(city\)) call the region Odisha or Odisha Rajya.[^cite_note-Acharya2008-25]

In 2011, the English rendering of ଓଡ଼ିଶା was changed from *Orissa* to *Odisha*, and the name of its language from *Oriya* to *Odia*, by the passage of the *Orissa (Alteration of Name) Bill, 2010* and the *Constitution (113th Amendment) Bill, 2010* in the [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Parliament). The Hindi rendering उड़ीसा (*uṛīsā*) was also modified to ओड़िशा (*or̥iśā*). After a brief debate, the lower house, [Lok Sabha](275456.md), passed the bill and amendment on 9 November 2010.[^cite_note-AmidClashBillPass-26] On 24 March 2011, [Rajya Sabha](232210.md), the upper house of [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament), also passed the bill and the amendment.[^cite_note-ChangeOrissaName-27] The changes in spelling were made with the intention of having the English and Hindi renditions conform to the Odia transliteration.[^cite_note-28] However, the underlying Odia texts were nevertheless transliterated incorrectly as per the [Hunterian system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunterian_transliteration), the official national transliteration standard, in which the transliterations would be *Orisha* and *Oria* instead.

## History

Main articles: [History of Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Odisha) and [Historic sites in Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_sites_in_Odisha)



[Lingaraja Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingaraja_Temple) built by the Somavanshi king Jajati Keshari

Prehistoric [Acheulian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acheulian) tools dating to [Lower Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Paleolithic) era have been discovered in various places in the region, implying an early settlement by humans.[^cite_note-Ghosh2012-29] Kalinga has been mentioned in ancient texts like *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata)*, *[Vayu Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vayu_Purana)* and *Mahagovinda Suttanta*.[^cite_note-sonofvaruna-30][^cite_note-vayu-31]

According to political scientist Sudama Misra, the Kalinga [janapada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janapada) originally comprised the area covered by the [Puri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puri_district) and [Ganjam](1857310.md) districts.[^cite_note-32] The [Sabar people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabar_people) of Odisha have also been mentioned in the Mahabharata.[^cite_note-DanceBow-33][^cite_note-Pati2008-34] [Baudhayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baudhayana_sutras) mentions Kalinga as not yet being influenced by [Vedic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic) traditions, implying it followed mostly tribal traditions.[^cite_note-Chatterjee1998-35]



[Hathigumpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hathigumpha_inscription) on the [Udayagiri Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udayagiri_and_Khandagiri_Caves) built in c. 150 BCE



[Shanti Stupa at Dhauli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhauli) is the location where [Kalinga War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_War) was fought in c. 260 BCE

[Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka) of the [Mauryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryan) dynasty conquered Kalinga in the bloody [Kalinga War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_War) in 261 BCE,[^cite_note-ashoka-36] which was the eighth year of his reign.[^cite_note-Radhakumud1995-37] According to his own [edicts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edicts), in that war about 100,000 people were killed, 150,000 were captured and more were affected.[^cite_note-ashoka-36] The resulting bloodshed and suffering of the war is said to have deeply affected Ashoka. He turned into a pacifist and converted to Buddhism.[^cite_note-Radhakumud1995-37][^cite_note-Sen1999-38]

By c. 150 BCE, Emperor [Kharavela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharavela), who was possibly a contemporary of [Demetrius I of Bactria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demetrius_I_of_Bactria),[^cite_note-Texas2006-39] conquered a major part of the Indian sub-continent. Kharavela was a [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain) ruler. He also built the monastery atop the [Udayagiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udayagiri_and_Khandagiri_Caves) hill.[^cite_note-Reddy2006-40] Subsequently, the region was ruled by monarchs, such as [Samudragupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudragupta)[^cite_note-41] and [Shashanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shashanka).[^cite_note-Davidson2013-42] It was also a part of [Harsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harsha)'s empire.[^cite_note-Majumdar1996-43]

The city of [Brahmapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmapur,_Odisha) in Odisha is also known to have been the capital of the [Pauravas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauravas) during the closing years of 4th century CE. Nothing was heard from the Pauravas from about the 3rd century CE, because they were annexed by the [Yaudheya Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaudheya), who in turn submitted to the [Mauryans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauryans). It was only at the end of 4th century CE, that they established royalty at Brahmapur, after about 700 years.

Later, the kings of the [Somavamsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somava%E1%B9%83%C5%9B%C4%AB_dynasty) dynasty began to unite the region. By the reign of Yayati II, c. 1025 CE, they had integrated the region into a single kingdom. Yayati II is supposed to have built the [Lingaraj temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingaraj_temple) at [Bhubaneswar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhubaneswar).[^cite_note-GovHistory-20] They were replaced by the [Eastern Ganga dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ganga_dynasty). Notable rulers of the dynasty were [Anantavarman Chodaganga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anantavarman_Chodaganga), who began reconstruction on the present-day Shri [Jagannath Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagannath_Temple,_Puri) in [Puri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puri_\(city\)) (c. 1135), and [Narasimhadeva I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narasimhadeva_I), who constructed the [Konark temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konark_temple) (c. 1250).[^cite_note-Dalal2014-44][^cite_note-IndianHistory-45]

The Eastern Ganga Dynasty was followed by the [Gajapati Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gajapati_Kingdom). The region resisted integration into the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) until 1568, when it was conquered by [Sultanate of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Bengal).[^cite_note-sen2-46] Mukunda Deva, who is considered the last independent king of Kalinga, was defeated and was killed in battle by a rebel Ramachandra Bhanja. Ramachandra Bhanja himself was killed by [Bayazid Khan Karrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayazid_Khan_Karrani).[^cite_note-47] In 1591, [Man Singh I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_Singh_I), then governor of [Bihar](442482.md), led an army to take Odisha from the [Karranis of Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_Bengal#Karrani_dynasty_\(Mughal_vassals\)). They agreed to treaty because their leader Qutlu Khan Lohani had recently died. But they then broke the treaty by attacking the temple town of [Puri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puri_\(city\)). Man Singh returned in 1592 and pacified the region.[^cite_note-O'malley2007-48]

In 1751, the Nawab of Bengal [Alivardi Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alivardi_Khan) ceded the region to the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire).[^cite_note-GovHistory-20]

The [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India) had occupied the [Northern Circars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Circars), comprising the southern coast of Odisha, as a result of the [Second Carnatic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnatic_Wars) by 1760, and incorporated them into the [Madras Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras_Presidency) gradually.[^cite_note-Sen2010-49] In 1803, the British ousted the Marathas from the Puri-Cuttack region of Odisha during the [Second Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Maratha_War). The northern and western districts of Odisha were incorporated into the [Bengal Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_Presidency).[^cite_note-Bandita1992-50]

The [Orissa famine of 1866](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orissa_famine_of_1866) caused an estimated 1 million deaths.[^cite_note-Dando2012-51] Following this, large-scale [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) projects were undertaken.[^cite_note-SamalNayak1996-52] In 1903, the [Utkal Sammilani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utkal_Sammilani) organisation was founded to demand the unification of [Odia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language)-speaking regions into one state.[^cite_note-PADHY2011-53] On 1 April 1912, the [Bihar and Orissa Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bihar_and_Orissa_Province) was formed.[^cite_note-Jha2003-54] On 1 April 1936, [Bihar](442482.md) and Orissa were split into separate provinces.[^cite_note-Devi1992-55] The new [province of Orissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orissa_Province) came into existence on a linguistic basis during the British rule in India, with Sir John Austen Hubback as the first governor.[^cite_note-Devi1992-55][^cite_note-Hubback-56] Following [India's independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India's_independence), on 15 August 1947, 27 [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) signed the document to join Orissa.[^cite_note-Krishna2007-57] Most of the [Orissa Tributary States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orissa_Tributary_States), a group of princely states, acceded to Orissa in 1948, after the collapse of the [Eastern States Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_States_Union).[^cite_note-58]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Odisha)



[Mahanadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanadi) river near Cuttack

Odisha lies between the latitudes 17.780N and 22.730N, and between longitudes 81.37E and 87.53E. The state has an area of 155,707 km<sup>2</sup>, which is 4.87% of total area of India, and a coastline of 450 km.[^cite_note-GeographyKnowIndia-59] In the eastern part of the state lies the coastal plain. It extends from the [Subarnarekha River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarnarekha_River) in the north to the [Rushikulya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushikulya) River in the south. The lake [Chilika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilika) is part of the coastal plains. The plains are rich in fertile [silt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silt) deposited by the six major rivers flowing into the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md): [Subarnarekha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarnarekha_river), [Budhabalanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budhabalanga), [Baitarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baitarani), [Brahmani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmani_River), [Mahanadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanadi), and [Rushikulya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushikulya).[^cite_note-GeographyKnowIndia-59] The [National Rice Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rice_Research_Institute) (NRRI), a [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_and_Agriculture_Organization)-recognised rice [gene bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_bank) and research institute, is situated on the banks of Mahanadi in [Cuttack](1776398.md).[^cite_note-60] The stretch between Puri and Bhadrak in Odisha juts out a little into the sea, making it vulnerable to any cyclonic activity.[^cite_note-61]



Satellite view of the [Mahanadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahanadi) [river delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta)

Three-quarters of the state is covered in mountain ranges. Deep and broad valleys have been made in them by rivers. These valleys have fertile soil and are densely populated. Odisha also has [plateaus](538005.md) and [rolling uplands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland), which have lower elevation than the plateaus.[^cite_note-GeographyKnowIndia-59] The [highest point in the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_highest_point) is [Deomali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deomali_\(mountain\)) at 1,672 metres in [Koraput district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koraput_district). Some other high peaks are: Sinkaram (1,620 m), Golikoda (1,617 m), and Yendrika (1,582 metres).[^cite_note-Socio-economic_Profile-62]

### Climate

The state experiences four [meteorological seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Season#Modern_mid-latitude_meteorological): [winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_India#winter) (January to February), [pre-monsoon season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_India#Summer) (March to May), [south-west monsoon season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_India#Monsoon) (June to September) and [north east monsoon season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Heat) (October–December). However, locally the year is divided into six traditional seasons (or *[rutus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritu_\(Indian_season\))*): *Grishma* (summer), *Barsha* (rainy season), *Sharata* (autumn), *Hemanta* (dewy),*Sheeta*(winter season) and *Basanta* (spring).[^cite_note-GeographyKnowIndia-59]

### Biodiversity

Main articles: [Flora and fauna of Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_and_fauna_of_Odisha) and [Forests in Odisha](23924363.md)

According to a [Forest Survey of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Survey_of_India) report released in 2012, Odisha has 48,903 km<sup>2</sup> of wild forest, covering 31.41% of the state's total area. The forests are classified into areas of dense forest (7,060 km<sup>2</sup>), medium dense forest (21,366 km<sup>2</sup>), open forest (forest without closed [canopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canopy_\(biology\)); 20,477 km<sup>2</sup>) and [scrub forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrubland) or scrubland (4,734 km<sup>2</sup>). The state also has [bamboo forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_forest) (10,518 km<sup>2</sup>) and tidal areas of [mangrove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove) swamp (221 km<sup>2</sup>). The state is gradually losing its wilderness areas to [timber smuggling](465780.md), [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation), destructive mining, and general urban industrialisation, as well as livestock [grazing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazing). There have been attempts at conservation and [reforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reforestation).[^cite_note-ForrestCover-64]

Due to the climate and good rainfall, Odisha's evergreen and moist forests are uniquely suitable habitats for wild [orchids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchid). Around 130 species have been reported from the state.[^cite_note-Orchids-65] Around 97 of them are found in [Mayurbhanj district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayurbhanj_district) alone. The Orchid House of the [Nandankanan Zoological Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandankanan_Zoological_Park) maintains some of these species.[^cite_note-OrchidHouse-66]

[Simlipal National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simlipal_National_Park) is a protected wildlife area and Bengal tiger reserve spread over 2,750 km<sup>2</sup> of the northern part of [Mayurbhanj district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayurbhanj_district). The park has around 1,078 species of plants, including 94 of the aforementioned orchids. The [*sal*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sal_tree) is the primary tree species. For fauna, the park is home to around 55 species of [mammal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal), including the [Bengal tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal_tiger), [chital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_deer), [chousingha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chousingha), [common langur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_langur), [gaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaur), [Indian elephant](5323507.md), [Indian giant squirrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_giant_squirrel), [jungle cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_cat), [leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_leopard), [muntjac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_muntjac), [sambar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambar_deer), [small Indian civet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_Indian_civet) and [wild boar](52255.md). There are over 300 species of birds in the park, such as the [common hill myna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_hill_myna), as well as [grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_grey_hornbill), [Indian pied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_pied_hornbill) and [Malabar pied hornbills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabar_pied_hornbill). There are also some 60 species of reptiles and amphibians, including the famed [king cobra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_cobra), plus [banded krait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banded_krait) and [tricarinate hill turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tricarinate_hill_turtle). There is also a [mugger crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mugger_crocodile) breeding programme in nearby Ramtirtha.[^cite_note-WWFSimilipal-67]

The [Chandaka Elephant Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandaka_Elephant_Sanctuary) is a 190 km<sup>2</sup> protected area near the capital city, Bhubaneswar. However, urban expansion and over-grazing have reduced the forests, driving the herds of elephants to migrate away, as well as increasing human-elephant conflicts, which sometimes results in injury and death (on both sides). Some elephants have died in conflicts with villagers, while some have died during migration after being accidentally electrocuted by power lines or even hit by trains. Outside the protected area, they are killed by ivory [poachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaching). In 2002, there were about 80 elephants, but by 2012, their numbers had been reduced to 20. Many of the animals have migrated toward the Barbara Reserve forest, Chilika, [Nayagarh district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayagarh_district), and [Athagad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athagad).[^cite_note-ElephantBanished-68][^cite_note-ElephantWipeout-69] Besides elephants, the sanctuary also has leopards, jungle cats and herds of chital.[^cite_note-Negi1993-70]

The [Bhitarkanika National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhitarkanika_National_Park) in [Kendrapara district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendrapara_district) covers 650 km<sup>2</sup>, of which 150 km<sup>2</sup> are mangroves. [Gahirmatha Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gahirmatha_Beach), in Bhitarkanika, is the world's largest nesting site for [olive ridley sea turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_ridley_sea_turtle).[^cite_note-Salagrama2006-71] In 2013, the Indian Coast Guard initiated [Operation Oliver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Oliver) to protect the endangered sea turtle population of the region.[^cite_note-72] Other major nesting grounds for the turtle in the state are [Rushikulya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushikulya), in [Ganjam district](1857310.md),[^cite_note-Olive2-73] and the mouth of the [Devi river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_river).[^cite_note-Olive3-74] The Bhitarkanika sanctuary is also noted for its large population of [saltwater crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltwater_crocodile) and [Asian water monitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_water_monitor),[^cite_note-inaturalist.org-75] the second-largest lizard species on earth,[^cite_note-CrocCensus-76] in addition to [axis deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_deer) and [rhesus macaques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhesus_macaque).[^cite_note-inaturalist.org-75] The coastal mangrove environments are home to several types of [mudskippers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudskipper), including the [barred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barred_mudskipper), [Boddart's blue-spotted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boleophthalmus_boddarti) and [great blue-spotted mudskippers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boleophthalmus_pectinirostris).[^cite_note-inaturalist.org-75]

In winter, Bhitarkanika is also visited by [migratory birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migratory_birds). Among the many species, both resident and migratory, are [kingfishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingfishers) (including [black-capped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-capped_kingfisher), [collared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collared_kingfisher) and [common kingfishers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_kingfisher)), [herons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heron) (such as [black-crowned night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-crowned_night_heron), [grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_heron), [purple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_heron) and [striated herons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striated_heron)), [Indian cormorants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cormorant), [openbill storks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_openbill), [Oriental white ibis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_white_ibis), [pheasant-tailed jacana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheasant-tailed_jacana), [sarus cranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarus_crane), [spotted owlets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_owlet) and [white-bellied sea-eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-bellied_sea-eagle).[^cite_note-BirdCount-77][^cite_note-inaturalist.org-75] The possibly endangered [horseshoe crab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseshoe_crab) is also found in this region.[^cite_note-horseshoe-78]

[Chilika Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilika_Lake) is a brackish water lagoon on the east coast of Odisha with an area of 1,105 km<sup>2</sup>. It is connected to the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) by a 35-km-long narrow channel and is a part of the Mahanadi delta. In the dry season, the tides bring in salt water. In the rainy season, the rivers falling into the lagoon decrease its salinity.[^cite_note-AgarwalSingh2007-79] Birds from places as far as the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md), [Lake Baikal](56865.md) (and other parts of Russia), [Central Asia](6742.md), [Southeast Asia](28741.md), [Ladakh](303611.md) and the [Himalayas](37966.md) migrate to the lagoon in winter.[^cite_note-ChilikaFalls-80] Among the [waterfowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfowl) and [wading birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wading_birds) spotted there are [Eurasian wigeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_wigeon), [pintail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_pintail), [bar-headed goose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar-headed_goose), [greylag goose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greylag_goose), [greater flamingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_flamingo), common [mallard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallard) and [Goliath heron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goliath_heron).[^cite_note-SharpDropChilika-81][^cite_note-TwoNewChilika-82] The lagoon also has a small population of the endangered [Irrawaddy dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrawaddy_dolphin).[^cite_note-DolphinRise-83] The state's coastal region has also had sightings of the rare [finless porpoise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finless_porpoise), as well as the more common [bottlenose dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottlenose_dolphin), [humpback dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humpback_dolphin) and [spinner dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinner_dolphin) in its waters.[^cite_note-DolphinCensus-84]

[Satapada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satpada) is situated close to the northeast cape of Chilika Lake and the Bay of Bengal. It is famous for dolphin watching in their natural habitat. There is a tiny island en route to watching dolphins, where tourists often take a short stop. Apart from that, this island is also home to tiny red crabs.[^cite_note-85]

According to a census conducted in 2016, there are around 2000 elephants in the state. [^cite_note-86]

## Government and politics



Odisha State Secretariat at [Bhubaneswar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhubaneswar)

Main article: [Government of Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Odisha)

All states in India are governed by a [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) of government based on [universal adult franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_adult_franchise).[^cite_note-SenguptaCorbridge2013-88][^cite_note-OurParliament-89]

The main parties active in the politics of Odisha are the [Biju Janata Dal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biju_Janata_Dal), the [Indian National Congress](149333.md) and [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md).[^cite_note-BJDLandslide-90] Currently, BJP, who won for the first time, formed the government after winning the majority in [2024 Odisha Legislative Assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Odisha_Legislative_Assembly_election). [Mohan Charan Majhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohan_Charan_Majhi) is the 17th Chief Minister of Odisha.[^cite_note-91]

### Legislative assembly

Main articles: [Odisha Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odisha_Legislative_Assembly) and [Vidhan Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidhan_Sabha)

The Odisha state has a [unicameral](204304.md) legislature.[^cite_note-Finifter-92] The [Odisha Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odisha_Legislative_Assembly) consists of 147 elected members,[^cite_note-BJDLandslide-90] and special office bearers such as the Speaker and Deputy Speaker, who are elected by the members. Assembly meetings are presided over by the Speaker, or by the Deputy Speaker in the Speaker's absence.[^cite_note-Kumar-93] [Executive authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) is vested in the Council of Ministers headed by the [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Odisha), although the titular head of government is the [Governor of Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Odisha). The governor is appointed by the [President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India). The leader of the party or coalition with a majority in the Legislative Assembly is appointed as the Chief Minister by the governor, and the Council of Ministers are appointed by the governor on the advice of the Chief Minister. The Council of Ministers reports to the Legislative Assembly.[^cite_note-AroraGoyal1995-94] The 147 elected representatives are called [Members of the Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Legislative_Assembly), or MLAs. One MLA may be nominated from the [Anglo-Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Indian) community by the governor.[^cite_note-Shukla2008-95] The term of the office is for five years, unless the Assembly is dissolved prior to the completion of the term.[^cite_note-Kumar-93]

The [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) is composed of the [Odisha High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odisha_High_Court), located at Cuttack, and a system of lower courts.

### Subdivisions

Main article: [List of districts of Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Odisha)

Odisha has been divided into 30 districts. These 30 districts have been placed under three different revenue divisions to streamline their governance. The divisions are North, Central and South, with their headquarters at [Sambalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambalpur), [Cuttack](1776398.md) and [Berhampur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berhampur) respectively. Each division consists of ten districts and has as its administrative head a Revenue [Divisional Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisional_Commissioner_\(India\)) (RDC).[^cite_note-RevenueDept-96] The position of the RDC in the administrative hierarchy is that between that of the district administration and the state secretariat.[^cite_note-Laxmikanth-97] The RDCs report to the Board of Revenue, which is headed by a senior officer of the [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service).[^cite_note-RevenueDept-96]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Odisha)

### Macro-economic trend

Odisha is experiencing a rapid economic growth post-Covid. The impressive growth in gross domestic product of the state has been reported by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation. Odisha's growth rate is above the national average.[^cite_note-101] The central Government's Urban Development Ministry has recently announced the names of 20 cities selected to be developed as [smart cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_cities). The state capital [Bhubaneswar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhubaneswar) is the first city in the list of [smart Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Cities_Mission) released in January 2016, a pet project of the Indian Government. The announcement also marked with sanction of Rs 508.02 billion over the five years for development.[^cite_note-102]

### Industrial development



One of the iron ore mines in [Keonjhar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendujhar_district)



[Rourkela Steel Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rourkela_Steel_Plant)

Odisha has abundant natural resources and a large coastline. Odisha has emerged as the most preferred destination for overseas investors with investment proposals.[^cite_note-103] It contains a fifth of India's coal, a quarter of its iron ore, a third of its [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite) reserves and most of the [chromite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromite).

[Rourkela Steel Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rourkela_Steel_Plant)[^cite_note-104] was the first integrated steel plant in the public sector in India, built with collaboration of [Germany](11867.md).

Arcelor-Mittal has also announced plans to invest in another mega steel project amounting to \$10 billion. Russian major [Magnitogorsk Iron and Steel Company (MMK)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitogorsk_Iron_and_Steel_Works) plans to set up a 10 MT steel plant in Odisha, too. [Nippon Steel Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Steel_Corporation) has recently announced to set up their own plants, one of which will be the world's largest and most advanced steel plant in Odisha, with a production capacity of 30 MT annually.[^cite_note-105] [Bandhabahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandhabahal) is a major area of open cast coal mines in Odisha. The state is attracting an unprecedented amount of investment in aluminium, coal-based power plants, petrochemicals, and information technology as well. In power generation, [Reliance Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliance_Power) (Anil Ambani Group) is putting up the world's largest power plant with an investment of US\$13 billion at Hirma in [Jharsuguda district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jharsuguda_district).[^cite_note-106]

In 2009 Odisha was the second top domestic investment destination with [Gujarat](53707.md) first and [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md) in third place according to an analysis of [ASSOCHAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASSOCHAM) Investment Meter (AIM) study on corporate investments. Odisha's share was 12.6 per cent in total investment in the country. It received an investment proposal worth ₹2.01 trillion (equivalent to ₹4.9 trillion or US\$51 billion in 2026) in 2010. Steel and power were among the sectors which attracted maximum investments in the state.[^cite_note-107]

The recently concluded Make in Odisha Conclave 2022 saw the state generate investment proposals worth ₹10.5 trillion with an employment potential for 10,37,701 people. Out of the total investment proposals received, the metals, ancillary and downstream sectors fetched ₹5.50 lakhs crore (trillion), power, green energy, and renewable energy sector fetched ₹2.38 trillion, and chemicals-petrochemicals and logistics-infrastructure sector attracted ₹76,000 crores and ₹1.20 trillion, respectively. Odisha has the potential to become a trillion-dollar economy by 2030.

Odisha has a network of roads, railways, airports and seaports. [Bhubaneswar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhubaneswar) is well connected by air, rail and road with the rest of India. ### Population



[Tribal people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Tribes_in_India) of [Koraput](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koraput), Odisha

According to the [2011 census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India), Odisha accounted for approximately 3% of India's total population. The state had a population of 41,974,218, with 21,212,136 males (50.54%) and 20,762,082 females (49.46%), resulting in a [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio) of 978 females per 1,000 males. This marked a [growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_rate_\(group_theory\)) of 13.97% during the 2001-2011 period, a decline from 16.25% in the previous decade (1991-2001). The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) stood at 269 people per square kilometre, with [Ganjam district](1857310.md) having the highest population among all districts in Odisha. In contrast, [Debagarh district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debagarh_district) has the lowest population. The population in the age group of 0–6 years comprised 12% of the total population, with a child sex ratio of 934 females for every 1,000 males in this age group. Additionally, [Scheduled Castes (SC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_and_Scheduled_Tribes) constituted a population of 7.2 million, making up 16.5% of the total population, while [Scheduled Tribes (ST)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Tribes) accounted for 9.6 million, representing 22.1% of the population.[^cite_note-CensusIndia2011PCA12018-4]

### Literacy and Socioeconomic Indicators

According to the 2011 Census, Odisha's overall [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy) is 72.87%. Male literacy stands at 81.59%, while female literacy is recorded at 64.01%. Odisha's literacy rate is slightly below the national average of 74.04%. Literacy rates vary within the state, with [Khordha district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khordha_district) having the highest literacy rate at 86.88%, while [Nabarangpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabarangpur_district) has the lowest at 46.43%. In rural areas, the average literacy rate is 70.22%, compared to 85.57% in urban areas. Among the [Scheduled Tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_and_Scheduled_Tribes) population, the literacy rate is 52.24%.

In terms of poverty, Odisha had a poverty rate of 57.15% in 2004–2005, nearly double the national average of 26.10% at the time. However, since 2005, the state has made significant progress, reducing the poverty rate by 24.6 percentage points, with the current estimate at 32.6%.[^cite_note-World_Bank_2016-120][^cite_note-Pragativadi\:NITI_Aayog_report_2017-121]

### Health and Vital Statistics

Data from 1996–2001 indicated that the state’s life expectancy was 61.64 years, slightly above the national average. Odisha also records a [birth rate](515898.md) of 23.2 per 1,000 people annually, a [death rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortality_rate) of 9.1 per 1,000, an [infant mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality) of 65 per 1,000 live births.[^cite_note-\:0-122] In 2011-2013, Odisha recorded a maternal mortality ratio (MMR) of 222 per 100,000 live births, according to a report by NITI Aayog. As of 2018, Odisha’s Human Development Index (HDI) stands at 0.606.[^cite_note-\:0-122] The [Total Fertility Rate (TFR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) in Odisha declined from 2.1 in 2015-16 to 1.8 in 2020-21, paralleling the national trend, which saw a decrease from 2.2 to 2.0 during the same period.[^cite_note-123]

With a cumulative score of 67.8, Odisha tops the ranking in fiscal health index 2025.[^cite_note-124]

Linguistic maps of Odisha

[Odia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language) is the official language of Odisha[^cite_note-134] and is spoken by 82.70% of the population according to the [2011 Census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Census_of_India).[^cite_note-census2011-langreport-133] It is also one of the [classical languages of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_India#Classical_languages_of_India). English is the official language of correspondence between the state government and the union of India. Spoken Odia is not homogeneous as one can find different dialects spoken across the state. Some of the major dialects found inside the state are [Sambalpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambalpuri_language), Cuttacki, Sundargarhi, [Baleswari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baleswari_Odia), [Ganjami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganjami_Odia), [Desiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desia_language), [Kalahandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalahandia_Odia) and [Phulbani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phulbani_Odia). The standard language is based on the Cuttacki dialect. In addition to Odia, significant populations of people speaking other major Indian languages like [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi), [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language), [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) and [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language) are also found in the state, mainly in cities.[^cite_note-LSI-Orissa-135]

The different *[tribal (Adivasi) communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adivasi)* who mostly reside in western and southern Odisha have their own languages belonging to [Munda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munda_languages) and [Dravidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravidian_languages) family of languages. Some of these major tribal languages are [Santali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santali_language), [Kui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kui_language_\(India\)), [Mundari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mundari_language) and [Ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_language). Due to increasing contact with outsiders, migration and socioeconomic reasons, many of these indigenous languages are slowly becoming extinct or are on the verge of extinction.[^cite_note-\:1-136]

The Odisha Sahitya Academy Award was established in 1957 to actively develop Odia language and literature. The Odisha government launched a portal in 2018 to promote Odia language and literature.[^cite_note-137]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Odisha)

Odisha is home to several Hindu figures. [Sant Bhima Bhoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhima_Bhoi) was a leader of the [Mahima sect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahima_Dharma). [Sarala Das](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarala_Das), a Hindu [Khandayat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khandayat), was the translator of the epic [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata) into Odia. Chaitanya Das was a [Buddhistic-Vaishnava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishnavism) and writer of the *Nirguna Mahatmya*. [Jayadeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayadeva) was the author of the *[Gita Govinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gita_Govinda)*.

The *Odisha Temple Authorisation Act* of 1948 empowered the government of Odisha to open temples for all Hindus, including Dalits.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-139]

Perhaps the oldest scripture of Odisha is the *[Madala Panji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madala_Panji)* from the Puri Temple believed from 1042 AD. Famous Hindu Odia scripture includes the 16th-century *Bhagabata* of [Jagannatha Dasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagannatha_Dasa_\(Odia_poet\)).[^cite_note-140] In the modern times [Madhusudan Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhusudan_Rao) was a major Odia writer, who was a [Brahmo Samajist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmo_Samaj) and shaped modern Odia literature at the start of the 20th century.[^cite_note-141]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Cuisine of Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Odisha)

Odisha has a culinary tradition spanning centuries. The kitchen of the [Shri Jagannath Temple, Puri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagannath_Temple,_Puri) is reputed to be the largest in the world, with 1,000 chefs, working around 752 wood-burning clay hearths called *chulas*, to feed over 10,000 people each day.[^cite_note-AffairsAffairs1949-142][^cite_note-SharmaGupta2006-143]

The syrupy dessert [Pahala rasagola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasagola) made in Odisha is known throughout the world.[^cite_note-ToI_Bishwa_2015-144] [Chhenapoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhenapoda) is another major Odisha sweet cuisine, which originated in [Nayagarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nayagarh).[^cite_note-chenapodaorigin-145] Dalma (a mix of dal and selected vegetables) is widely known cuisine, better served with ghee.



Rasagola, Odisha's special culinary delight made from cottage cheese, which has received GI tag in Geographical Indication Registry.

The "Odisha [Rasagola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasgulla)" was awarded a geographical indication (GI) tag in 2019 after a long battle about the origin of the famous sweet with West Bengal.[^cite_note-146]

This decision involved a broader interpretation of the law established under the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999. Section 2(e) of the Act stipulates that a geographical indication can be provided to a product if its quality, characteristics, or reputation are attributable to its geographical origin and are unique. The uniqueness of Odisha’s Rasagola lies in its color, texture, and taste, distinguishing it from the West Bengal variant. Historical references in Odisha’s texts support the geographical origin of the dish.[^cite_note-147]

### Dance

Main article: [Odissi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odissi)

[Odissi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odissi) dance and music are classical art forms. Odissi is the oldest surviving dance form in India on the basis of archaeological evidence.[^cite_note-148] Odissi has a long, unbroken tradition of 2,000 years, and finds mention in the *[Natyashastra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natya_Shastra)* of [Bharatamuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharata_Muni), possibly written c. 200 BC. However, the dance form nearly became extinct during the British period, only to be revived after India's independence by a few gurus.

The variety of dances includes [Ghumura dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghumura_dance), [Chhau dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhau_dance), [Jhumair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhumair), [Mahari dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahari_dance), [Dalkhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalkhai), [Dhemsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhemsa) and [Gotipua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotipua).

Odissi is not only a dance form but also a spiritual expression deeply rooted in Odisha’s temple traditions. Characterized by graceful movements, intricate footwork, and sculpturesque poses, it draws inspiration from the carvings of the Konark Sun Temple and other ancient shrines of the region. The dance is traditionally performed as an act of devotion to Lord Jagannath and often depicts stories from Hindu epics such as the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and Geeta Govinda. The unique tribhangi posture, which divides the body into three bends (head, torso, and hips), and the fluid chauka stance, give Odissi its distinctive identity among classical dances of India.

Over centuries, Odissi has evolved from being a temple ritual to a respected performing art practiced worldwide. It blends lyrical music with expressive gestures (mudras) that communicate emotions and narratives with precision. Gurus like Kelucharan Mohapatra, Pankaj Charan Das, and others played a pivotal role in reviving and popularizing the form in the 20th century, ensuring its survival and global recognition. Today, Odissi is celebrated on prestigious stages across the world, symbolizing Odisha’s cultural heritage and artistic excellence.

The traditional performance of Odissi usually begins with Mangalacharan, an invocation to the deities, followed by Batu Nritya that highlights pure dance techniques. Pallavi showcases the dancer’s mastery through elaborate rhythmic patterns, while Abhinaya brings mythological stories to life through expressive storytelling. The recital often concludes with Moksha, symbolizing spiritual liberation, where the dancer blends technical skill with a deep sense of devotion. This structured presentation makes Odissi both aesthetically rich and spiritually profound.

Costumes and music also play an important role in Odissi. Dancers typically wear brightly colored saris made of Sambalpuri or Bomkai silk, adorned with silver jewelry crafted in Odisha’s traditional style. The music is based on Odissi classical ragas and talas, blending elements of both Hindustani and Carnatic traditions, and is performed with instruments like the mardala (a traditional percussion instrument), flute, sitar, and violin. Together, the visual elegance and the melodic accompaniment create an atmosphere that connects performers and audiences to the deep cultural and devotional roots of Odisha.

## Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Odisha)

The Lingaraja Temple at [Bhubaneswar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhubaneswar) has a 150-foot (46 m) high deula while the [Jagannath Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagannath_Temple), [Puri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puri_\(city\)) is about 200 feet (61 m) high and dominates the skyline. Only a portion of the [Konark Sun Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konark_Sun_Temple) at [Konark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konark) in [Puri district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puri_district), the largest of the temples of the "Holy Golden Triangle" exists today, and it is still staggering in size. It stands out as a masterpiece in Odisha architecture. [Sarala Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarala_Temple), regarded as one of the most spiritually elevated expressions of Shaktism is in [Jagatsinghpur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagatsinghpur_district). It is also one of the holiest places in Odisha and a major tourist attraction. [Maa Tarini Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maa_Tarini_Temple) situated in [Kendujhar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendujhar_district) is also a famous pilgrimage destination. Every day thousands of coconuts are given to Maa Tarini by devotees for fulfilling their wishes.[^cite_note-154]

Odisha's varying topography – from the wooded Eastern Ghats to the fertile river basin – has proven ideal for evolution of compact and unique ecosystems. This creates treasure troves of flora and fauna that are inviting to many migratory species of birds and reptiles. [Bhitarkanika National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhitarkanika_National_Park) in [Kendrapada district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendrapada_district) is famous for its second largest mangrove ecosystem. The bird sanctuary in [Chilika Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilika_Lake) (Asia's largest brackish water lake). The tiger reserve and waterfalls in [Simlipal National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simlipal_National_Park), [Mayurbhanj district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayurbhanj_district) are integral parts of eco-[tourism in Odisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Odisha), arranged by Odisha Tourism.[^cite_note-155]

[Daringbadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daringbadi) is a hill station in the [Kandhamal district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandhamal_district). It is known as "Kashmir of Odisha", for its climatic similarity. Chandipur, in [Baleswar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baleswar_district) is a calm and serene site, is mostly unexplored by tourists. The unique speciality of this beach is the ebb tides that recede up to 4 km and tend to disappear rhythmically.

In the western part of Odisha, [Hirakud Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirakud_Dam) in [Sambalpur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambalpur_district) is the longest earthen dam in the World. It also forms the biggest artificial lake in Asia. The [Debrigarh Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debrigarh_Wildlife_Sanctuary) is situated near Hirakud Dam. [Samaleswari Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaleswari_Temple) is a Hindu temple in [Sambalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambalpur) city, dedicated to the goddess known as 'Samaleswari', the presiding deity of Sambalpur, is a strong religious force in western part of Odisha and [Chhattisgarh](47734.md) state. The [Leaning Temple of Huma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leaning_Temple_of_Huma) is located near Sambalpur. The temple is dedicated to the Hindu god Lord Bimaleshwar. Sri Sri [Harishankar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harishankar) Devasthana, is a temple on the slopes of [Gandhamardhan hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhamardhan_hills), [Balangir district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balangir_district). It is popular for its scenes of nature and connection to two Hindu lords, [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu) and [Shiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva). On the opposite side of the Gandhamardhan hills is the temple of Sri [Nrusinghanath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nrusinghanath), is situated at the foothills of Gandhamardhan Hill near [Paikmal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paikmal), [Bargarh district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bargarh_district).

In the southern part of Odisha, The [Taratarini Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taratarini_Temple) on the Kumari hills at the bank of the Rushikulya River near [Berhampur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berhampur) city in [Ganjam district](1857310.md). Here worshiped as the Breast Shrine (Sthana pitha) and manifestations of Adi Shakti. The Tara Tarini Shakta pitha is one of the oldest pilgrimage centers of the Mother Goddess and is one of four major ancient Tantra pitha and [Shakta pithas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakta_pithas) in India. [Deomali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deomali) is a mountain peak of the [Eastern Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ghats). It is located in [Koraput district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koraput_district). This peak with an elevation of about 1,672 m, is the highest peak in Odisha.

The share of foreign tourists' arrival in the state is below one per cent of total foreign tourist arrivals at all India level.[^cite_note-156]
