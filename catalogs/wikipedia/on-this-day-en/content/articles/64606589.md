# Russo-Ukrainian cyberwarfare



Pro-Russian bot farm in Ukraine.

[Cyberwarfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare) has been a component of the confrontation between [Russia](25391.md) and [Ukraine](31750.md) since the [Revolution of Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity) in 2013–2014.  While the first attacks on information systems of private enterprises and state institutions of Ukraine were recorded during mass protests in 2013, Russian cyberweapon [Uroburos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uroburos) had been around since 2005. An attempt to hack the Ukrainian presidential election in May 2014 marked the beginning of Russian cyber aggression. [Russian cyberwarfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_cyberwarfare) continued with the [2015 Ukraine power grid hack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Ukraine_power_grid_hack) at Christmas 2015 and [again in 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Kyiv_cyberattack), paralysis of the State Treasury of Ukraine in December 2016, a [mass hacker supply-chain attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_cyberattacks_on_Ukraine) in June 2017, and attacks on Ukrainian government websites in January 2022.

## History

Russian–Ukrainian [cyberwarfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare) is a component of the confrontation between Russia and Ukraine since the [Revolution of Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity) in 2013–2014. Russian cyberweapon [Uroburos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uroburos) had been around since 2005.[^cite_note-cso-1] In 2013, Operation Armageddon, a Russian campaign of systematic cyber espionage on the information systems of government agencies, law enforcement, and defense agencies, began, thought to help Russia on the battlefield.[^cite_note-jw-2] Between 2013 and 2014, some information systems of Ukrainian government agencies were affected by a computer virus known as [Snake / Uroborus / Turla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turla_\(malware\)).[^cite_note-jw-2] In February–March 2014, as Russian troops [entered Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), communication centers were raided, and Ukraine's fiber optic cables were tampered with, cutting the connection between the peninsula and mainland Ukraine. Additionally, Ukrainian government websites, news, and social media were shut down or targeted in DDoS attacks, while cell phones of many Ukrainian parliamentarians were hacked or jammed.[^cite_note-jw-2][^cite_note-3]

A series of cyberattacks on the Central Election Commission of Ukraine during the presidential elections in May 2014 is considered to be "the first strike in the cyberwar," according to [Victor Zhora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Zhora), then the leader of the election's cyber defenders.[^cite_note-4] Cybersecurity companies began to register an increase in the number of cyberattacks on information systems in Ukraine. The victims of Russian cyberattacks were government agencies of Ukraine, the EU, the United States, defense agencies, international and regional defense and political organizations, think tanks, the media, and dissidents.[^cite_note-jw-2] As of 2015, researchers had identified two groups of Russian hackers who have been active in the Russian-Ukrainian cyber war: the so-called [APT29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APT29) (also known as Cozy Bear, Cozy Duke) and [APT28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fancy_Bear) (also known as Sofacy Group, Tsar Team, Pawn Storm, Fancy Bear).[^cite_note-jw-2]

Russia has conducted cyberattacks against [Ukraine's wartime satellite internet service Starlink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starlink_in_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War).[^cite_note-\:11-5]

## Russian-Ukrainian cyberwarfare amidst Russian invasion of Ukraine in 2022

In June 2022, [Microsoft](19001.md) published the report on Russian cyberattacks, and concluded that state-backed Russian hackers "have engaged in 'strategic espionage' against governments, think tanks, businesses, and aid groups" in 42 countries supporting Kyiv.[^cite_note-36]

In April 2022, a Microsoft report shared new details on Russian cyberwarfare against Ukraine; for instance, Microsoft has reported that in some cases, hacking and military operations worked in tandem against targets connected to Ukraine.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]
