The **Cambodia–Thailand border** is the international border between [Cambodia](334751.md) and [Thailand](30128.md). The border is 817 km (508 mi) in length and runs from the tripoint with [Laos](17752.md) in the north-east to the [Gulf of Thailand](198107.md) in the south.[^cite_note-1]

## Description

The border starts in the north-east at the tripoint with Laos at Preah Chambot peak in the [Dângrêk Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A2ngr%C3%AAk_Mountains) and follows the crest of the mountains westwards.[^cite_note-IBS-2] Upon leaving the mountains the border turns south-westwards in a broad arc, occasionally utilizing rivers such as the [Svay Chek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svay_Chek_River), the [Sisophon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisophon_River), the Phrom Hot and [Mongkol Borei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongkol_Borei_River). It then proceeds south, partly along the [Cardamom Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardamom_Mountains), terminating at the Gulf of Thailand coast. This latter section runs very close to the Gulf, producing a long, thin [strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khlong_Yai_District) of Thai territory.[^cite_note-IBS-2]

## History

The border crossing at Poipet

### Colonial period

The boundary area has historically switched back and forth between various Khmer and Thai empires.[^cite_note-IBS-2] From the 1860s [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Empire) began establishing a presence in the region, initially in modern [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Protectorate_of_Cambodia) and [Vietnam](202354.md), and later Laos, with the colony of [French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina) being created in 1887.[^cite_note-IBS-2][^cite_note-RBSJ-3] In 1867 a Franco-Thai treaty confirmed Thai ownership of the [Battambang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battambang) and [Angkor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angkor) (Nakhou Siemrap) regions.[^cite_note-IBS-2][^cite_note-RBSJ-3] In 1896, Britain (based in [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma)) and France agreed to leave Siam (the then name for Thailand) as a [buffer state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffer_state) between their respective colonies.[^cite_note-IBS-2] However France continued to expand at the expense of Siam, annexing northern Cambodia in 1904 and then [Battambang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battambang), [Sisophon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisophon) and [Siem Reap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siem_Reap) in 1907, whilst ceding [Trat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trat) to Siam. The modern Cambodian-Thai border was as such delimited through several treaties between France and Siam between 1867 [and 1907](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Siamese_treaty_of_1907).[^cite_note-IBS-2][^cite_note-RBSJ-3] Following the [Japanese invasion of French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_invasion_of_French_Indochina) in 1940 areas ceded to France in 1904 and 1907 were returned to Thailand, however this was reversed following Japan's defeat and the pre-war border restored in 1946.[^cite_note-IBS-2][^cite_note-RBSJ-3]



Map of the Cambodia-Thailand border

### Since Cambodian independence

Cambodia gained independence in 1953, and since then the two states have had a fractious relationship.[^cite_note-Kamm-4] A [Cambodian–Thai border dispute](80516236.md) arose in the late 1950s over the ownership of [Preah Vihear](2367753.md), a temple in the Dângrêk Mountains, adjacent to the border.[^cite_note-IBS-2] In 1962 the case was referred to the [International Court of Justice](14918.md), which ruled in favour of Cambodia, however Thailand expressed reservations as to the outcome.[^cite_note-IBS-2][^cite_note-5] The mid-1960s also saw a dispute over ownership of [Ko Kut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_Kut) island.[^cite_note-RBSJ-3] These disputes faded as Cambodia became engulfed in a series of conflicts in the following decades and the disastrous rule of the [Khmer Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Rouge), with thousands of refugees crossing the border.[^cite_note-RBSJ-3] By 1981, over 250,000 Cambodian and Vietnamese refugees lived in twenty camps along the border, supported by international aid agencies and the [United Nations Border Relief Operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNBRO).[^cite_note-6]

Sporadic [fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_border_raids_in_Thailand) broke out along the border following Vietnam's [invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian%E2%80%93Vietnamese_War) of Cambodia in 1979 which forced the Khmer Rouge to flee to camps on the border with Thailand, using them as a base throughout the 1980s until Vietnam withdrew from the country in 1989. On 18 February 1994, the Khmer Rouge led by [Saloth Sâr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pol_Pot) (*Pol Pot*) rebuilt their headquarters at [Dângrêk Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A2ngr%C3%AAk_Mountains) located in [Anlong Veng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anlong_Veng) near the Thai border, which became the final hideout inside the dense jungle.[^cite_note-7] In an effort to stop Khmer Rouge infiltration from Thailand, Cambodia built a large [fortified fence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K5_Plan) along the border in the second half of the 1980s.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-8][^cite_note-9]

Since the advent of peace in Cambodia in the early 1990s relations with Thailand have once again soured over the Preah Vihear issue, as well as, to a lesser extent, Ko Kut island.[^cite_note-RBSJ-3] A border commission was set up in 1995 in an attempt to settle the issues peacefully, however by 2013 it had made little progress and a full border demarcation satisfactory to both sides remained outstanding.[^cite_note-RBSJ-3][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

Armed fighting broke out at various times [from 2008 to 2011](80516236.md). As of September 2022, government officials said border demarcation had been completed in 13 areas, and that the remaining 11 were under negotiation.[^cite_note-e851-12]

On 28 May 2025, one [Royal Cambodian Army](13916116.md) soldier was killed after a Thai patrol encountered Cambodian soldiers digging trenches. Both Cambodia and Thailand accused the other of trespassing on their respective territories and firing first. [Phumtham Wechayachai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phumtham_Wechayachai), Thailand's Defense Minister, claimed that opening fire was not intended on both sides.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Cambodia stated that it will litigate the dispute with the [International Court of Justice](14918.md).[^cite_note-17]

In July 2025, [the conflict was reignited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Cambodia%E2%80%93Thailand_border_conflict) with both sides' military attacking each other. Thailand also sealed all land border crossings with Cambodia. Multiple civilians were reported to be killed.[^cite_note-18] On 24 July 2025, Cambodian Prime Minister [Hun Manet](42929019.md) submitted a request to the United Nations Security Council to call for an urgent meeting to stop Thai aggression.[^cite_note-19] The UNSC held the meeting on 25 July 2025.[^cite_note-20] With mediation effort from 2025 ASEAN Chair country Malaysia, as well as call for a ceasefire from U.S. President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) to both countries, Hun Manet and Thailand Acting Prime Minister [Phumtham Wechayachai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phumtham_Wechayachai) met in Kuala Lumpur for peace talks on 28 July 2025. Both countries agreed on an immediate and unconditional ceasefire taking effect on 12\:00 on 29 July 2025.[^cite_note-21] On 8 December 2025, after both countries reported violations of the ceasefire, fighting across the border resumed[^cite_note-22] until another ceasefire agreement was signed on 27 December.[^cite_note-23]

### Checkpoints for border trade

There are 9 checkpoints officially recognized by the Ministry of Interior which are open for cross-border local trade only, located in [Ubon Ratchathani](242147.md), [Buriram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buriram_province), [Sa Kaeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa_Kaeo_province), [Chanthaburi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanthaburi_province) and [Trat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trat_province) provinces. Entering the opposite country beyond these checkpoints and their associated markets is illegal.

### Checkpoint for tourism

There is one checkpoint for tourism, which is currently closed since June 2008 due to the [Preah Vihear dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preah_Vihear_dispute) and eventual cession of [Preah Vihear](2367753.md) to Cambodia.
