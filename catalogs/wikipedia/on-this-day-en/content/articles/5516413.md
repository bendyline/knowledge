**Pokrovsk** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Покровськ, IPA: [\[poˈkrɔu̯sʲk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f2/Uk-%D0%9F%D0%BE%D0%BA%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%81%D1%8C%D0%BA.ogg/Uk-%D0%9F%D0%BE%D0%BA%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%81%D1%8C%D0%BA.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f2/Uk-%D0%9F%D0%BE%D0%BA%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%81%D1%8C%D0%BA.ogg/Uk-%D0%9F%D0%BE%D0%BA%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%81%D1%8C%D0%BA.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Покровск), formerly known as **Krasnoarmiisk**[^cite_note-1] (until 2016) and **Grishino**[^cite_note-2] (until 1934), is a city and the administrative center of [Pokrovsk Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokrovsk_Raion) in [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md), [Ukraine](31750.md). It is located 56 kilometres (35 mi) northwest of [Donetsk](562951.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECohen19981615-3] Before 2020, it was incorporated as a [city of oblast significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_regional_significance_\(Ukraine\)). Its population was about 60,127 (2022 estimate).[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-4][^cite_note-5] As residents fled or were killed during Russia's [Pokrovsk offensive](77627696.md), the population declined to around 7,000 as of January 2025, then under 1,500 by late July 2025. Russia captured the city in early 2026.[^cite_note-ISW20260226-6]

## History



Railway station at the turn of the 19th and 20th centuries

Pokrovsk was founded as Grishino in 1875 by a decision of the Ministry of Railways of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) authorizing a railway station. The [railway settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_town) had two thousand inhabitants.[^cite_note-mainhistory1-11]

In 1881, a locomotive depot which became one of the main locomotive repair companies, Ekaterinoslavskaya railway, was built in the town. Two years later, in 1883, there was an enlargement to the station building; the central portion survives to this day. In May 1884, trains began transiting the rail station in Grishino.[^cite_note-mainhistory1-11]

With the development of the railway station, Grishino grew and there were new businesses, in particular for exploitation of underground minerals, starting with [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_in_Ukraine). By 1913, the population around Grishino station had more than doubled to about 4.5 thousand people.[^cite_note-mainhistory1-11]

During the [Ukrainian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence), from 1917 to 1920, it passed between various factions. Afterwards, Hryshyne was administratively part of the [Donets Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets_Governorate) of Ukraine.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

After the war, Hryshyne continued its growth and by 1925 had a locomotive depot, a brick factory, and six mines. The name of the station was changed to Postyshevo in 1934 to honor [Pavel Postyshev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Postyshev), and in 1938, the name of the city became Krasnoarmeyskoe, commemorating the [Soviet Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Red_Army), after Postyshev was repressed during the [Great Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Purge).[^cite_note-mainhistory2-15]

### World War II

[World War II](32927.md) heavily impacted the population of the city. The first Axis forces to arrive were [Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_participation_on_the_Eastern_Front), followed by the [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) who [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissariat_Ukraine) it on 19 October 1941. German forces proceeded to [forcibly transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) many civilians by train to labor camps in Austria. Many residents defended their hometown. 8,295 Soviet soldiers [perished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_casualties_of_the_Soviet_Union) on the battlefield and 4,788 residents of the town were killed in World War II. The Germans operated a Nazi prison, a penal forced labour camp and a subcamp of the Stalag 378 [prisoner-of-war camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) in the city.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

The city witnessed an atrocity when its remaining [Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Ukraine) was massacred in [The Holocaust in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_Ukraine) by the German Nazi army in midwinter 1942.[^cite_note-19] Furthermore, in February 1943, the Red Army perpetrated the [massacre of Grishino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_of_Grishino), in which 508 POWs and 88 civilians were massacred, mainly Germans and Italians, but also Romanians, Ukrainians, Hungarians and Danes.[^cite_note-\:3-20]<sup>: 187–191 </sup> On 8 September 1943, the town was re-taken by Red Army troops.[^cite_note-mainhistory2-15]

### Post World War II period

In the 1950s, in the post-war period, the city renewed its industrial and residential construction.[^cite_note-mainhistory2-15] In 1952, coal mines, railway maintenance plants, and the large F.E. Dzerzhinsky Dinas Plant were operating here. In 1959, the Krasnoarmeysk Large-Panel Block Plant began operations, and industrial and residential construction began in the city. On December 30, 1962, the city became a [city of oblast significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_regional_significance_\(Ukraine\)), Krasnoarmeysk.[^cite_note-21]

In 1972, large coal mines operated here (No. 1 "Central", "Krasnolimanskaya", "Rodinskaya", named after G. Dimitrov, named after T. G. Shevchenko, and others).[^cite_note-22] In the 1970s, a dairy plant, a meat processing plant, and the Krasnoarmeyskaya-Zapadnaya No. 1 mine were built.[^cite_note-23] In [January 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Soviet_Census), the city's population was 72,900 people. The economy was based on coal mining, electric motor production and refractory materials.[^cite_note-24]

### Ukrainian independence period

In May 1995, the [Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Ministers_of_Ukraine) approved a decision to privatize the Elektrodvigatel plant, Motor Transport Enterprise ATP-11411, ATP-11464, and oil depot located in the city,[^cite_note-25] repair and transport enterprise and district agricultural chemicals.[^cite_note-26] In July 1995, the decision to privatize the Stroydetal plant was approved, bakery and state farm.[^cite_note-27]

### Russo-Ukrainian War

#### War in Donbas

During the 2014–2022 [War in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas), the city was near the frontline with the [separatist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_separatist_forces_in_Ukraine) [Donetsk People's Republic](42425747.md).[^cite_note-28]

In May 2015, a decision was made to liquidate the Rodinskaya mine and mine of [Georgi Dimitrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgi_Dimitrov).[^cite_note-29] In May 2016, as part of the [decommunization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decommunization_in_Ukraine) policy, the [Verkhovna Rada](844993.md) of Ukraine renamed the city to *Pokrovsk*, and the Krasnoarmeyskaya-Zapadnaya No. 1 mine to Pokrovskaya. The new names for the city and the mine were given by the Holy Nicholas-Pokrovsky Church, built between 2003 and 2006.[^cite_note-post-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The new name was honoring the [Intercession of the Theotokos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercession_of_the_Theotokos) known as Pokrova in Ukrainian.[^cite_note-33]

##### 2023

On 7 August 2023 during the [Russo-Ukrainian war](70149799.md), Russian missiles [struck the city twice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pokrovsk_missile_strike),[^cite_note-34] killing nine people.[^cite_note-35]

##### 2024

In July 2024, Russia renewed efforts to reach and capture Pokrovsk in [a new offensive](77627696.md).[^cite_note-WAPO72724-36] On 15 August, Serhii Dobriak, the head of the Pokrovsk City Military Administration, reported that Russian forces were only 10 km from the city,[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] and urged all citizens, especially the elderly and families with young children, to evacuate.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] On 19 August, Ukrainian officials announced that families with children living in Pokrovsk and surrounding villages would be forced to leave.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] The population reportedly had decreased to 36,000 by 1 September.[^cite_note-44] On 5 September, the train station closed for civilian evacuation due to a deteriorating security situation, using buses and the train station in [Pavlohrad](3889613.md) instead. According to Donetsk Oblast Governor Vadym Filashkin, 26,000 people, including 1,076 children, were still remaining in the city.[^cite_note-45] By October, the population declined to 13,000.[^cite_note-rferfl-46]



School №3 of Pokrovsk after a Russian missile attack on 28 February 2024

##### 2025 and 2026

By January 2025, after the gradual advances of the Russian military toward the city, its civilian population had declined to around 7,000, the lowest level in two centuries.[^cite_note-pop7000-47] Continued warfare and evacuations reduced the population to less than 1,500 civilian residents by late July.[^cite_note-48] By early November, military battles were reported inside the city.[^cite_note-49] On 1 December 2025, the [Russian Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Ministry_of_Defence) stated that the city was fully captured by its forces, a claim denied by Ukraine.[^cite_note-Reutn1-50] The [Institute for the Study of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_the_Study_of_War) determined that Russia captured the city in early 2026.[^cite_note-ISW20260226-6]

## Economy



Terricon and mine in the city.

Pokrovsk has central importance for the [Ukrainian steel industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_production_in_Ukraine). The city is home to 20 enterprises in six main industries. The main [coal industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_in_Ukraine) enterprises are the [Krasnolimanskaya Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krasnolimanskaya_Mine?action=edit\&redlink=1) and PJSC Pokrovskoe Mine Administration (formerly OJSC Krasnoarmeyskaya-Zapadnaya Mine No. 1 Coal Company) — coal production in 2005 was 6,241 thousand tons.

The Pokrovsk Mine Management also operates one of the largest [coal mines in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_in_Ukraine) in the village [Udachne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udachne).[^cite_note-blast-62] In July 2021, an explosion occurred in the Udachne coal mine, injuring 10 miners in the blast. The affected workers were hospitalized in the Pokrovsk-based Central District Hospital.[^cite_note-blast-62] Because of the war, the site is under constant danger of shelling.[^cite_note-63]

Pischane, besides Pokrovsk Mine Management, is the largest plant for coking coal production in Ukraine. The site provided half of Metinvest's coal volume, used to produce steel at the company's plants. Loss of the Pokrovsk coal mine would thus be a major blow to Ukraine's steel production industry. According to the head of Ukraine's steelmakers' association, Oleksandr Kalenkov, a full closure of the plant could diminish Ukrainian steel production to only 2–3 million metric tons, down from a projected 7.5 million by the end of 2024.[^cite_note-shutdown2-64]

Pokrovsk is known for its industrial enterprises, including the Pokrovsk Machine-Building Plant,[^cite_note-65] Krasnoarmeysk Electrodvigatel Plant, Krasnoarmeysk Silica Plant, Krasnoarmeysk Large-Panel Housing Construction Plant, Krasnoarmeysk Repair and Mechanical Plant, Krasnoarmeysk Foundry and Mechanical Plant,[^cite_note-66] and Donetskstal.[^cite_note-67]

In addition, the city had light industry:

- Krasnoarmeyskaya Sewing Factory

- Carriage Depot (Shmidta Street);

- PJSC Metinvest PokrovskUgol (Metinvest PU; Zashchitnikov Street) Ukraine);

- Business center of Krasnoarmeysk Electromechanical Plant LLC (Yuzhny microdistrict);

- Krasnoarmeysk locomotive depot.

Following the loss of Ukrainian government control over [Donetsk](562951.md) in 2014 during the [War in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)), the [Donetsk National Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donetsk_National_Technical_University) was evacuated to Pokrovsk.[^cite_note-68] On 28 February 2024, the university was partially destroyed by a Russian missile attack.[^cite_note-69]
