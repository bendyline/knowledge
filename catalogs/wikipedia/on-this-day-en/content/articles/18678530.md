**William Michael Albert Broad** (born 30 November 1955), known professionally as **Billy Idol**, is an English rock singer and songwriter. He achieved fame in the 1970s on the London [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock) scene as the lead singer of [Generation X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_X_\(band\)). He later embarked on a solo career which led to international recognition and made him a lead artist during the [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV)-driven "[Second British Invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_British_Invasion)" in America.

Idol began his music career in 1976 as a guitarist in the punk rock band [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_\(band\)), but left the group after a few weeks and formed Generation X with his former Chelsea bandmate [Tony James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_James_\(musician\)). With Idol as lead singer, the band achieved success in the UK and released three studio albums on [Chrysalis Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysalis_Records) before disbanding. In 1981, Idol moved to [New York City](645042.md) to pursue his solo career in collaboration with guitarist [Steve Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Stevens). His debut studio album *[Billy Idol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Idol_\(album\))* (1982) was a commercial success, and Idol became a staple of the then-new television channel MTV with the music videos for his singles "[Dancing with Myself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_Myself)" and "[White Wedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Wedding_\(song\))".

Idol's second studio album *[Rebel Yell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Yell_\(album\))* (1983) was a major commercial success, featuring hit singles "[Rebel Yell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Yell_\(song\))" and "[Eyes Without a Face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyes_Without_a_Face_\(song\))". The album was certified [double platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIAA_certification) by the [Recording Industry Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America) (RIAA) for selling 2 million copies in America, and he then released *[Whiplash Smile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiplash_Smile)* (1986). He released the greatest hits album *[Idol Songs: 11 of the Best](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idol_Songs:_11_of_the_Best)* (1988), which went platinum in the UK, and then released *[Charmed Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charmed_Life_\(Billy_Idol_album\))* (1990) and the [concept album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept_album) *[Cyberpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberpunk_\(album\))* (1993).

Idol spent the second half of the 1990s focusing on his personal life out of the public eye. In 1990, he broke his leg in a motorcycle accident, and later said in his biography *Dancing With Myself* (2014) that "by the time the motorcycle accident happened, \[he] had been living by the credo 'live every day as if it's your last, and one day you're sure to be right'".[^cite_note-1] He made a musical comeback with the release of *[Devil's Playground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil's_Playground_\(album\))* (2005) and later released *[Kings & Queens of the Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_&_Queens_of_the_Underground)* (2014) and *[Dream Into It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_Into_It)* (2025). Idol is a three time [Grammy Award](44635.md) nominee in the now defunct [Best Male Rock Vocal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Male_Rock_Vocal_Performance) category. In 2023, he received a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame).[^cite_note-2] Idol and Stevens were inducted into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](58902.md) in 2026.[^cite_note-3]

## Early life

William Michael Albert Broad was born in [Stanmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanmore) on 30 November 1955,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuinness19921222-4] to Irish housewife Joan (née O'Sullivan)[^cite_note-5] and English salesman William Alfred Broad.[^cite_note-6] His mother was from [Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cork_\(city\)), his father was from [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] His parents attended [Church of England](5955.md) services regularly. In 1958, at the age of two, he moved with his parents to the U.S. and settled in [Patchogue, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patchogue,_New_York); they also lived in [Rockville Centre, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockville_Centre,_New_York). His younger sister, Jane, was born during this time. The family returned to England four years later and settled in [Dorking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorking).[^cite_note-filmr-9] In 1971, when Idol was 15, the family moved to [Bromley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromley), where he attended [Ravensbourne School for Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravensbourne_School,_Bromley). His family later moved to [Goring-by-Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goring-by-Sea), where he attended [Worthing High School for Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worthing_High_School,_West_Sussex).[^cite_note-10] In October 1975, he began attending the [University of Sussex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Sussex) to pursue a Philosophy with Literature degree, but left after year one in 1976. He later joined the [Bromley Contingent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromley_Contingent), a loosely organised gang of [Sex Pistols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_Pistols) fans who travelled to see the band wherever they played.[^cite_note-Telegraph-11][^cite_note-12]

## Career

### 1976–1981: Generation X

Main article: [Generation X (band)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_X_\(band\))

The [stage name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_name) "Billy Idol" was coined after a chemistry teacher, Bill Price, described Idol as "idle" on his school report card.[^cite_note-13] Idol has stated that he had hated chemistry and underachieved in it.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-Radio_with_Pictures-15] In a 1983 interview, he said of the name, "It was a bit of a goof, but part of the old English school of rock. It was a 'double thing', not just a poke at the superstar-like people... It was fun, you know?"[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-Radio_with_Pictures-15] In another interview for *[BBC Breakfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Breakfast)* in October 2014, he said that he wanted to use the name "Billy Idle", but thought the name would be unavailable due to its similarity to the name of [Monty Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monty_Python) star [Eric Idle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Idle) and chose Billy Idol instead.[^cite_note-17]

In late 1976, Idol joined the newly formed West London 1960s [retro-rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retro-rock) band [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_\(band\)) as a guitarist.[^cite_note-18] The act's singer/frontman [Gene October](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_October) styled Idol's image, advising him to use [contact lenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_lens) instead of [eyeglasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyeglasses) for his short sight, and dye his hair blonde with a [crew cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew_cut) for a retro-1950s rocker look. After a few weeks performing with Chelsea, Idol and [Tony James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_James_\(musician\)), the band's bass guitarist, quit and co-founded [Generation X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_X_\(band\)), with Idol switching from guitarist to the role of singer/frontman. Generation X was one of the first punk bands to appear on the BBC Television music programme *[Top of the Pops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_of_the_Pops)*.[^cite_note-19] Although a punk rock band, they were inspired by mid-1960s British pop, in sharp contrast to their more militant peers, with Idol stating, "We were saying the opposite to [the Clash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clash) and the Pistols. They were singing 'No [Elvis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis), [Beatles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatles) or [the Rolling Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rolling_Stones)', but we were honest about what we liked. The truth was we were all building our music on the Beatles and the Stones."[^cite_note-Telegraph-11]

In 1977, Idol sang "Your Generation" on the TV series *[Marc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_\(TV_series\))*. Generation X signed a recording contract with [Chrysalis Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysalis_Records), released three studio albums, performed in the 1980 film *[D.O.A.: A Rite of Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.O.A.:_A_Rite_of_Passage)*, and then disbanded.[^cite_note-20]

### 1981–1985: Solo career and breakthrough

> MTV has paved the way for a host of invaders from abroad: [Def Leppard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Def_Leppard), [Adam Ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Ant), [Madness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madness_\(band\)), [Eurythmics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurythmics), [the Fixx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fixx) and Billy Idol, to name a few. In return, grateful Brits, even superstars like [Pete Townshend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Townshend) and [the Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Police), have mugged for MTV promo spots and made the phrase 'I want my MTV' a household commonplace.

— Anglomania: The [Second British Invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_British_Invasion), by Parke Puterbaugh for *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone)*, November 1983.

[^cite_note-21]



Billy Idol in 1984

Idol moved to New York City in 1981 and became a solo artist, working with former [Kiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_\(band\)) manager Bill Aucoin. Idol's punk-like image worked well with the glam rock style of his new partner on guitar, [Steve Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Stevens).[^cite_note-22] Together they worked with bassist Phil Feit and drummer Gregg Gerson. Idol's solo career began with the Chrysalis Records EP titled *[Don't Stop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Stop_\(Billy_Idol_EP\))* in 1981, which included the Generation X song "[Dancing with Myself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_Myself)", originally recorded for their last album *[Kiss Me Deadly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_Me_Deadly_\(album\))*, and a cover of [Tommy James and the Shondells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_James_and_the_Shondells)' song "[Mony Mony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mony_Mony)". Idol's debut solo album *[Billy Idol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Idol_\(album\))* was released in July 1982.[^cite_note-23] The album debuted at number 45 on the [Billboard 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200) and number 5 in [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Aotearoa_Music_Charts).[^cite_note-24]

Part of the [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV)-driven "[Second British Invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_British_Invasion)" of the US in 1982, Idol became an MTV staple with "[White Wedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Wedding_\(song\))" and "Dancing with Myself". The music video for "White Wedding" was filmed by the British director [David Mallet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Mallet_\(director\)) and played frequently on MTV. The motorcycle smashing through the church window stunt was carried out by John Wilson, a London motorcycle courier. In 1983, Idol's label released "Dancing with Myself" in the US in conjunction with a music video directed by [Tobe Hooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobe_Hooper), which played on MTV for six months.

*[Rebel Yell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Yell_\(album\))* (1983), Idol's second LP, was a major success[^cite_note-billboard1-25] peaking at number 2 in both [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GfK_Entertainment_charts) and New Zealand, while also establishing Idol in the United States with hits such as "[Rebel Yell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Yell_\(song\))", "[Eyes Without a Face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyes_Without_a_Face_\(song\))", and "[Flesh for Fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flesh_for_Fantasy)". "Eyes Without a Face" peaked at number four on the US [*Billboard* Hot 100](423161.md), and "Rebel Yell" reached number six in the [UK Singles Chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Singles_Chart).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-Roberts-27] “Rebel Yell” also garnered Idol his first Grammy nomination for [Best Male Rock Vocal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Male_Rock_Vocal_Performance) at the [27th Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/27th_Annual_Grammy_Awards).[^cite_note-\:1-28] The record has since been [certified platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certified_platinum) by the [RIAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America), [RMNZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorded_Music_NZ), [Music Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_Canada) and [Pro-Música Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-M%C3%BAsica_Brasil).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-\:2-30][^cite_note-\:3-31]

### 1986–1992: *Whiplash Smile* and *Charmed Life*



Idol performing during the Cradle of Love Tour, 1990

Idol released *[Whiplash Smile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiplash_Smile)* in 1986, which sold well, charting in the top ten in 9 countries with the highest spot being number 3 in Finland.[^cite_note-billboard1-25][^cite_note-32] The album included the hits "[To Be a Lover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Be_a_Lover)", "[Don't Need a Gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Need_a_Gun)" and "[Sweet Sixteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Sixteen_\(song\))". Idol filmed a video for the song "Sweet Sixteen" in Florida's [Coral Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_Castle).[^cite_note-33] “To Be a Lover” peaked at number 6 on the Billboard Hot 100, number 3 in both [Australia](3002226.md) and [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_Finnish_Charts) and number 2 in New Zealand.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] The song also garnered him another nomination for Best Male Rock Vocal Performance at the [29th Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/29th_Annual_Grammy_Awards).[^cite_note-\:1-28] The album has since been certified platinum in the US, Canada and New Zealand.[^cite_note-\:2-30][^cite_note-\:3-31]

A remix album was released in 1987, titled *[Vital Idol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vital_Idol)*. The album featured a live rendition of his cover of [Tommy James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_James)' "[Mony Mony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mony_Mony)". In 1987, the single topped the United States chart and reached number 7 in the UK.[^cite_note-billboard1-25][^cite_note-Roberts-27] The following year in 1988 a compilation album titled *[Idol Songs: 11 of the Best](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idol_Songs:_11_of_the_Best)* was release&#x64;*,* it charted at number 2 in both the UK and [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B3nlist) along with reaching number 1 in [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suomen_virallinen_lista). It has since gone platinum in multiple countries.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

On 6 February 1990 in Hollywood, Idol was involved in a serious motorcycle accident that nearly cost him a leg.[^cite_note-38] He was hit by a car when he ran a stop sign while riding home from the studio one night, requiring a steel rod to be placed in his leg.[^cite_note-39] While he was hospitalised, he vowed to stop wearing clothing featuring the [Confederate flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_flag), after a black employee tending to him explained his feelings on it.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

Prior to the accident, film director [Oliver Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Stone) had chosen Idol for the role of [Jim Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Morrison)'s drinking pal Cat in his film *[The Doors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Doors_\(film\))* (1991), but it prevented him from participating in a major way and Idol's role was reduced to a small part.[^cite_note-42] He was [James Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Cameron)'s first choice for the role of the villainous [T-1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-1000) in *[Terminator 2: Judgment Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminator_2:_Judgment_Day)* (1991); the role was recast as a result of the accident.[^cite_note-43]

*[Charmed Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charmed_Life_\(Billy_Idol_album\))* was released in 1990, and continued the commercial success seen in his previous albums, charting in the Top 10 in nine countries along with topping the charts in Finland. The album's first single "[Cradle of Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_Love_\(Billy_Idol_song\))" reached number 2 on the [Billboard Hot 100](423161.md) and was featured in the [Andrew Dice Clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Dice_Clay) film *[The Adventures of Ford Fairlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Ford_Fairlane)*. A video for the single was commissioned, but because Idol was unable to walk due to injuries he sustained in a motorcycle accident,[^cite_note-44] director [David Fincher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Fincher) shot him from the waist up. The video featured footage of Idol singing in large frames throughout an apartment while a young woman (played by Betsy Lynn George) was trying to seduce a businessman. The video was placed in rotation on MTV and won an award for Best Video from a Film at the [1990 MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_MTV_Video_Music_Awards). "Cradle of Love" also earned Idol a third Grammy nomination for [Best Male Rock Vocal Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Male_Rock_Vocal_Performance).[^cite_note-45] The album has since been certified platinum in the US and Canada.[^cite_note-\:2-30][^cite_note-\:3-31] Idol then embarked on a tour in support of the album with [Faith No More](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_No_More).[^cite_note-46]

### 1993–2004: *Cyberpunk*, decline, and resurgence



Idol performing at the [Milton Keynes Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bowl) in Buckinghamshire, 1993

In 1993, Idol released *[Cyberpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberpunk_\(album\))*.[^cite_note-47] Regarded as experimental, it was recorded in a home studio using a [Macintosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macintosh) computer. Idol used Studiovision and [Pro Tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Tools) to record the album. The album took ten months to make. The album did not perform well in the United States and the lead single "[Shock to the System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_to_the_System_\(Billy_Idol_song\))" did not chart in the *Billboard* Hot 100. In comparison, the lead single from Idol's previous album, "[Cradle of Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_Love_\(Billy_Idol_song\))", peaked at No. 2. In Europe, the album fared slightly better, achieving moderate chart success and peaking within the top 20 in 7 countries, along with getting into the top 10 in Austria and Finland. Idol toured in Europe and played a Generation X reunion show in 1993.[^cite_note-48]

He recorded and released the single "[Speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_\(Billy_Idol_song\))" in 1994; the song was featured as the first track in the [film soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_\(1994_film\)_Soundtrack) album. Idol appeared in a 1996 live version of [the Who](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Who)'s *[Quadrophenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrophenia)*.[^cite_note-49] Idol played Lee Turner in the 1996 film *[Mad Dog Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mad_Dog_Time)*, he also made a cameo appearance as himself in the 1998 film *[The Wedding Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wedding_Singer)* with [Adam Sandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Sandler), in which Idol played a pivotal role in the plot. The film featured "White Wedding" on its soundtrack.[^cite_note-50] In 2000, he was invited to be a guest vocalist on [Tony Iommi's debut solo album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iommi_\(album\)). His contribution was on the song "Into the Night", which he co-wrote. That year, he voice acted the role of Odin, a mysterious alien character, in the adult animated science fiction film *[Heavy Metal 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_Metal_2000)*, also providing a song for the soundtrack.

[VH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VH1) aired *Billy Idol – [Behind the Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behind_the_Music)* on 16 April 2001. Idol and Stevens took part in a *VH1 Storytellers* show three days later. The reunited duo set out to play a series of acoustic/storytellers shows before recording the VH1 special. Another *[Greatest Hits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greatest_Hits_\(Billy_Idol_album\))* CD was issued in 2001, with [Keith Forsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Forsey) and [Simple Minds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_Minds)' "[Don't You (Forget About Me)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_You_\(Forget_About_Me\))" appearing on the compilation. The LP includes a live acoustic version of "Rebel Yell", taken from a performance at Los Angeles station [KROQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KROQ-FM)'s 1993 Acoustic Christmas concert. The *Greatest Hits* album sold 1 million copies in the United States alone.[^cite_note-51]

In the [2002 NRL Grand Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_NRL_Grand_Final) in Sydney, Idol entered the playing field for the half-time entertainment on a [hovercraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hovercraft) to the intro of "White Wedding", of which he managed to sing only two words before a power failure ended the performance.[^cite_note-52]

### 2005–2009: *Devil's Playground*



Idol performing on stage at the [Brixton Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brixton_Academy) in London, 2005

*[Devil's Playground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil's_Playground_\(album\))*, which came out in March 2005, was Idol's first new studio album in nearly 12 years. The album reached No. 46 on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200) and charted the highest in Germany at number 15. The album included a cover of "[Plastic Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_Jesus_\(song\))". Idol played a handful of dates on the 2005 [Warped Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warped_Tour) and appeared at the [Download Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Download_Festival) at [Donington Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donington_Park), the [Voodoo Music Experience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voodoo_Experience) in [New Orleans](53842.md), and [Rock am Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_am_Ring).[^cite_note-53] Idol was awarded the comeback of the year at the inaugural [Classic Rock Roll of Honour Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Rock_Roll_of_Honour_Awards).

In 2006 Idol released a Christmas album titled *[Happy Holidays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Holidays_\(Billy_Idol_album\))* it included renditions of many traditional [Christmas songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_music) along with 2 original songs.[^cite_note-54]

In 2008, "Rebel Yell" appeared as a playable track on the video game *[Guitar Hero World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar_Hero_World_Tour)* and "White Wedding" on *[Rock Band 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Band_2)*. The *Rock Band 2* platform later gained "Mony Mony" and "Rebel Yell" as downloadable tracks. On 24 June 2008, Idol released the greatest hits album *[The Very Best of Billy Idol: Idolize Yourself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Very_Best_of_Billy_Idol:_Idolize_Yourself)*. He embarked on a worldwide tour, co-headlining with [Def Leppard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Def_Leppard).[^cite_note-55]

In June 2006, Idol performed at the [Congress Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_Theater), Chicago, for the United States television series *[Soundstage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soundstage_\(TV_series\))*. This performance was recorded and then released on DVD/Blu-ray as *In Super Overdrive Live*, on 17 November 2009.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

### 2010–2021: *Kings & Queens of the Underground*



Idol performing live at the [Bonnaroo Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnaroo_Music_Festival) in [Manchester, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester,_Tennessee), 2013

On 16 February 2010, Idol was announced as one of the acts to play at the [Download Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Download_Festival) in Donington Park, England. He stated, "With all of these great heavyweight and cool bands playing Download this year, I'm going to have to come armed with my punk rock attitude, Steve Stevens, and all of my classic songs plus a couple of way out covers. Should be fun!"[^cite_note-58] In March 2010, Idol added [Camp Freddy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Freddy) guitarist Billy Morrison[^cite_note-59] and drummer Jeremy Colson to his touring line-up.



Idol performing live at the [2015 NHL Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_NHL_Winter_Classic) in Washington, D.C.

In 2013, Idol appeared on the third episode of the [BBC Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Four) series *How the Brits Rocked America*.[^cite_note-60] Idol also lent his voice as Spikey Hair Bot to [Disney XD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_XD)'s *[Randy Cunningham: 9th Grade Ninja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Cunningham:_9th_Grade_Ninja)* episode "McSatchle"[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62]

In October 2014, Idol released his eighth studio album *[Kings & Queens of the Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_&_Queens_of_the_Underground)*. It debuted at number 34 on the Billboard 200, and charted in multiple countries in Europe including number 8 in Germany. While recording the album between 2010 and 2014, he worked with producer [Trevor Horn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Horn), Horn's former [Buggles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buggles) and [Yes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes_\(band\)) bandmate [Geoff Downes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Downes)[^cite_note-63] and [Greg Kurstin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Kurstin). Idol's autobiography, *Dancing with Myself*, was published on 7 October 2014 and became a *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times)* bestseller.[^cite_note-64]

Idol embarked on a worldwide tour in support of his new album. The tour started on 5 November 2014 and ended 4 April 2015. Over the 40-date tour, Idol with his band performed in Europe, North America and Australia.[^cite_note-auto1-65] He continued touring North America in 2016 and 2017.[^cite_note-66]

On 30 October 2018, former Generation X members Idol and [Tony James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_James_\(musician\)) joined with [Steve Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Jones_\(musician\)) and [Paul Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Cook), former members of another first wave English punk rock band, the [Sex Pistols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_Pistols), to perform a free gig at [the Roxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roxy_Theatre_\(West_Hollywood\)) in Hollywood, Los Angeles, under the name Generation Sex, playing a combined set of the two former bands' material.[^cite_note-67]

In 2019, Idol went on a co headlining tour alongside [Bryan Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Adams).[^cite_note-68]

In late February 2020, Idol starred in a public service campaign with the [New York City Department of Environmental Protection Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Department_of_Environmental_Protection_Police) titled "Billy Never Idles", intended to fight the unnecessary idling of automobile engines in New York City, to reduce air pollution. Idol teamed with New York Mayor [Bill de Blasio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_de_Blasio) to open the campaign, which features Idol saying, "If you're not driving, shut your damn engine off!" and other strong advice.[^cite_note-69] He was a guest vocalist on the song "Night Crawling" from [Miley Cyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miley_Cyrus)' album *[Plastic Hearts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_Hearts)*, released in November 2020.[^cite_note-70] In 2016, Idol and Cyrus performed "Rebel Yell" at the iHeartRadio Festival in Las Vegas.[^cite_note-71]

&#x20;On 12 August 2021, Idol's music video "Bitter Taste", directed by Stephen Sebring, was uploaded to YouTube. Idol announced his new EP *[The Roadside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Roadside)*, which was released on 17 September.[^cite_note-72]

### 2022–present: *Dream Into It*

In March 2022, Idol was diagnosed with [MRSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methicillin-resistant_Staphylococcus_aureus), which forced him to cancel a co-headlining tour with [Journey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_\(band\)).[^cite_note-73] In September that year, he embarked on the postponed Roadside Tour with [Killing Joke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_Joke) and [Toyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyah_Willcox) as his UK [opening acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opening_act) in October. American rock band The Foxies opened for him during the EU leg of the tour.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] Another EP, *The Cage*, was released on 23 September 2022. A video for the title track, also directed by Sebring, premiered on YouTube 17 August. On 6 January 2023, Idol was honoured with a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame).[^cite_note-\:4-76] On 24 January, he announced a North American tour from late March through mid-May: the tour was to begin in [Scottsdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottsdale,_Arizona) on 30 March and conclude with a concert at the [Cruel World Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruel_World_Festival) on 20 May in [Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena).[^cite_note-77] In April that year, Idol played the first concert in history at the [Hoover Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoover_Dam).[^cite_note-78] Idol is a member of Generation Sex who are on tour and performed at Glastonbury in 2023.[^cite_note-79]

In 2025, Idol was nominated for induction into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](58902.md) for the first time.[^cite_note-80] Idol said [Steve Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Stevens) is nominated with him, and they will both be inducted into the Hall should Idol be selected for induction.[^cite_note-81] On 26 February, Idol announced his first studio album in nearly 11 years, *[Dream Into It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_Into_It)*, along with its first single, "[Still Dancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Dancing_\(song\))".[^cite_note-82] The album charted at number 5 in Austria, 3 in Switzerland and number 2 in Germany. The album featured guest appearances from [Avril Lavigne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avril_Lavigne), [Alison Mosshart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Mosshart) and [Joan Jett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Jett), it received generally positive reception.[^cite_note-83] In April 2025 Idol started his "It's a Nice Day to Tour again" North American tour alongside [Joan Jett and the Blackhearts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Jett_and_the_Blackhearts). The European leg of the tour saw him play venues such as [Königsplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6nigsplatz,_Munich)[^cite_note-auto1-65] with [New Model Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Model_Army_\(band\)) as support.

In June 2025, a full-length documentary about Idol premiered at the [Tribeca Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribeca_Festival) in New York.[^cite_note-84] The film, titled "Billy Idol Should Be Dead," chronicles Idol's rise to fame from his early days with Chelsea and Generation X, his struggles with drug addiction, and his family life from childhood through the present.[^cite_note-85]

In November 2025, ending his Latin-American tour, Idol celebrated his 70th birthday in [Mexico City](18987.md). [Dany Villarreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniela_Villarreal) (from [The Warning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Warning_\(band\))) was invited to the stage for a joint performance of "[Dancing With Myself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_Myself)", and later also a [mariachi band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariachi) joined to sing ["Las Mañanitas"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Ma%C3%B1anitas) and "Happy Birthday" dedicated to Idol.[^cite_note-86] On March 1, 2026 it was announced Idol had received his second nomination for the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.[^cite_note-87]

## Artistry

Billy Idol established himself in the late-1970s London [punk scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_scene) as the frontman of Generation X, a band criticized as too "poppy."[^cite_note-AllMusic-88] [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic) critic Greg Adams labelled their [self-titled debut album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_X_\(album\)) as "bubblegum punk",[^cite_note-89] while their second album, *[Valley of the Dolls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_the_Dolls_\(album\))* has a [glam punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glam_punk) sound,[^cite_note-90] influenced by [progressive rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_rock), and [Bruce Springsteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen)'s mid-1970s work.[^cite_note-91] After renaming to Gen X and going in a more [new wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_wave_music) direction on their third album, *[Kiss Me Deadly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiss_Me_Deadly_\(album\))*, the band released the single "[Dancing with Myself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_with_Myself)", an early example of Idol incorporating [dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_music) and [pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music) into his music. The song was a commercial disappointment, and the band disbanded soon after.[^cite_note-Breihan_2021-92]

The musical style of Idol's solo career is a blend of pop hooks, punk attitude, and dance beats, described as [dance-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance-punk),[^cite_note-93] [dance-rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance-rock),[^cite_note-94] glam punk,[^cite_note-95] [glam rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glam_rock),[^cite_note-96] [hard rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_rock),[^cite_note-EP-97] new wave,[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] [pop-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop-punk),[^cite_note-\:0-100] [pop rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_rock),[^cite_note-AllMusic-88] [post-punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-punk),[^cite_note-EP-97] and [power pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_pop).[^cite_note-101] *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* critic Johnathan Taylor labelled his debut EP and solo release, *Don't Stop*, as dance-rock, but on his self-titled debut album, he transitioned to "mainstream power rock" with vocals reminiscent of Bruce Springsteen, [Lou Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Reed) and [Dave Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Clark_\(musician\)).[^cite_note-102] His second album, *Rebel Yell*, is a mix of hard rock, new wave and pop,[^cite_note-103] cementing Idol as a "punk-pop star", according to [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic) critic Matt Collar.[^cite_note-\:0-100] He retained this sound into the early 1990s, but experimented with other genres, such as [synth-pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synth-pop) in "[Eyes Without a Face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyes_Without_a_Face_\(song\))" (1984)[^cite_note-104] and [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz) in "Endless Sleep" (1990).[^cite_note-105]

Idol's biggest change in sound was on *Cyberpunk*, a [techno-rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techno-rock) album based on the [cyberdelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberdelic) subculture of the late 1980s and early 1990s,[^cite_note-106] with [synthesised vocals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_synthesis), [electronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music) and [industrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_music) elements, looped beats, as well as keyboard riffs.[^cite_note-107] *Devil's Playground*, his first album in 12 years, has a more hard rock-oriented style, while keeping up with pop-punk bands, such as [Green Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Day) and [Sum 41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sum_41).[^cite_note-108] Since then, he's returned to a sound more reminiscent of his 1980s work, combining new wave and hard rock on his two most recent albums, *Kings & Queens of the Underground* and *Dream Into It*.[^cite_note-109] The latter is his second concept album, and has elements of [soft rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_rock)[^cite_note-Arts_Desk-110] and [grunge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grunge).[^cite_note-NR-111]

## Personal life

Idol lives in [Los Angeles](18110.md).[^cite_note-naturalised-112] He has never married, but was previously in a long-term relationship with English singer and dancer [Perri Lister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perri_Lister). They have a son, Willem (born 1988),[^cite_note-hochman512-113] who has been a member of the rock band FIM.[^cite_note-amica-114] They separated in 1989.[^cite_note-Billy_Idol_biography-115] Idol also has a daughter, Bonnie, from another relationship.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] As of 2026, he has four granddaughters.[^cite_note-people1-118] Idol released his memoir *Dancing with Myself* in 2014.[^cite_note-119] Idol has a love for motorcycles, specifically [Harley Davidsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley-Davidson_Sportster); he even performed at the company's 100th anniversary concert celebration in 2003.[^cite_note-120]

In 2023, Idol revealed he had another son, Brant Broad, after Bonnie took a [23andMe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23andMe) DNA test and learned she had a half-brother. Idol introduced Broad publicly as his son at his Hollywood Walk of Fame induction ceremony that year.[^cite_note-121]

Idol has struggled with alcoholism and drug addiction, including addiction to heroin and cocaine.[^cite_note-auto-122] In his 2014 memoir, he said there was more than one time he woke up in a hospital after passing out in a nightclub.[^cite_note-123] In 1994, he collapsed outside a Los Angeles nightclub due to an overdose[^cite_note-124] of [gamma-Hydroxybutyric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma-Hydroxybutyric_acid) (GHB).[^cite_note-125] He stopped his drug use after this incident, as he decided his children would never forgive him for dying of a drug overdose.[^cite_note-126] Idol then started working out and going to the gym regularly, and has credited it as helping him with his recovery. In a 2025 interview he stated "Working out became a big part of my life. And it helped me get over drug addiction".[^cite_note-127] In 2014, he said he had not taken hard drugs since 2003 but added that he smoked cannabis regularly and was an occasional drinker.[^cite_note-auto-122] In addition to his British citizenship, Idol gained American citizenship in 2018.[^cite_note-naturalised-112][^cite_note-128]

Idol's interest in [Westerns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_\(genre\)), [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) history and other [Americana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americana_\(culture\)) was shared by Ozzy Osbourne, whom Idol helped induct into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame.[^cite_note-f897-129]
