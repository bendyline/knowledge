# East Asian monsoon

The **East Asian monsoon** is a [monsoonal](57630.md) flow that carries moist air from the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) and [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) to [East Asia](19605700.md). It affects approximately one-third of the global population, influencing the climate of [China](5405.md), [Japan](15573.md), [Vietnam](202354.md), the [Korean Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Peninsula), [Taiwan](25734.md), the [Philippines](23440.md), and mainland [Southeast Asia](28741.md). It is driven by temperature differences between the East Asian continent and the Pacific Ocean. The East Asian monsoon is divided into a warm and wet summer monsoon and a cold and dry winter monsoon. This cold and dry winter monsoon is responsible for the [aeolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolian_processes) dust deposition and [pedogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedogenesis) that resulted in the creation of the [Loess Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loess_Plateau). The monsoon influences weather patterns as far north as [Siberia](29639.md), causing wet summers that contrast with the cold and dry winters caused by the [Siberian High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_High), which counterbalances the monsoon's effect on latitudes in the north.

In most years, the monsoonal flow shifts in a very predictable pattern, with winds being southeastern in late June, bringing significant rainfall to the region, resulting in the [East Asian rainy season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_rainy_season) as the monsoon boundary advances northward during the spring and summer. This leads to a reliable precipitation spike in July and August. However, this pattern occasionally fails, leading to drought and crop failure. In the winter, the winds are northeasterly and the monsoonal precipitation bands move back to the south, and intense precipitation occurs over South China and Taiwan.

Over Japan and Korea, the monsoon boundary typically takes the form of a [quasi-stationary front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary_front) separating the cooler air mass associated with the [Okhotsk High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okhotsk_High) to the north from the hot, humid air mass associated with the [subtropical ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_ridge) to the south. After the monsoon boundary passes north of a given location, it is not uncommon for daytime temperatures to exceed 32 °C (90 °F) with dewpoints of 24 °C (75 °F) or higher. The spring-summer rainy season is referred to as "plum rain" in various languages of East Asia. In Japan the monsoon boundary is referred to as the *tsuyu* (梅雨) as it advances northward during the spring, while it is referred to as the *shurin* when the boundary retreats back southward during the autumn months.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETakaoFujioSeita2001-1] The East Asian monsoon is known as *meiyu* (梅雨) in China and Taiwan, and *jangma* ([장마](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%EC%9E%A5%EB%A7%88)) in Korea.

The location[^cite_note-2] and strength of the East Asian monsoon has varied during the [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene) which scientists track using pollen[^cite_note-3] and dust.[^cite_note-4]
