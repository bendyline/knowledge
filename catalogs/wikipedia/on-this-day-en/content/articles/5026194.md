# Alcoholic drinks in Canada



Map of drinking age per province

&#x20; Minimum age is 19

&#x20; Minimum age is 18

This article covers various topics involving **alcoholic drinks in Canada**. The Government of Canada defines an [alcoholic drink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_drink) as "a beverage containing 1.1% or more alcohol by volume."[^cite_note-1]

## History

Further information: [Alcohol in New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_in_New_France)

### Beer

This paragraph is an excerpt from [Beer in Canada § History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_in_Canada#History).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Beer_in_Canada\&action=edit)]

Beer was first introduced to Canada by European [settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settler) in the [seventeenth century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Oldest_Breweries_of_Canada), as Canada had an ideal climate for making beer before [refrigeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refrigeration) was introduced. However, the preferred drink of the citizens of New France was imported wine or brandy. Although the first commercial brewery was built by [Louis Prud'homme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Prud'homme) in [Montreal](7954681.md) (then [Fort Ville-Marie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Ville-Marie)) in 1650, it failed. [Jean Talon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Talon), the first appointed Intendant of New France put limits on the amount of wine and spirits that could be imported and established the La brasserie de Roy in [Quebec City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_City), in the year 1668.[^cite_note-2] This brewery also failed after Talon returned to France in 1672 and import limits were increased.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECoutts20109–10-3] What instead sprung up was the development of [spruce beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spruce_beer), both alcoholic and non-alcoholic.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECoutts201010-4]

### Whisky

This paragraph is an excerpt from [Canadian whisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_whisky).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Canadian_whisky\&action=edit)]

[Canadian whisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_whisky) is a type of [whisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whisky) produced in Canada. Most Canadian whiskies are [blended whiskies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blended_whiskies) consisting of a mix of different whisky styles, and are typically lighter and smoother than other whisky styles.[^cite_note-Canadian_whisky_Whisky.com-5] While most Canadian whiskies are based predominantly on [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize)-based whiskies, Canadian distillers have long included [rye-based whisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye_whiskey) for flavour; in Canadian law the terms *Canadian whisky* and *rye whisky* are interchangeable to refer to the same product, even when it is made with only a small amount of rye spirits.[^cite_note-6]

### Wine

This paragraph is an excerpt from [Canadian wine § History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_wine#History).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Canadian_wine\&action=edit)]

Canadian wine has been produced for over 400 years. In 1611, [Louis Hérbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_H%C3%A9bert) planted a hillside vineyard near what is today, [Bear River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear_River,_Nova_Scotia), Nova Scotia.[^cite_note-7] He and other settlers tried to cultivate *[Vitis vinifera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitis_vinifera)* grapes from Europe with limited success. They found it necessary to focus on the native species of *[Vitis labrusca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitis_labrusca)* and *[Vitis riparia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitis_riparia)* along with various hybrids. However, the market was limited for such wines because of their peculiar taste which was often called "[foxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxiness)". However, this became less apparent when the juice was made into [Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_wine)- and [Sherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherry)-styled wines. In 1866, the first commercial winery opened in Canada, situated on [Pelee Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelee_Island) in Ontario.[^cite_note-Canadian_wine_BCW-8]

### Prohibition

This paragraph is an excerpt from [Prohibition in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_Canada).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Prohibition_in_Canada\&action=edit)]

[Prohibition in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_Canada) was a ban on [alcoholic beverages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage) that arose in various stages, from local municipal bans in the late 19th century (extending to the present in some cases), to provincial bans in the early 20th century, and national prohibition (a temporary wartime measure) from 1918 to 1920. The relatively large and powerful beer and alcohol manufacturing sector, and the huge working class that purchased their products, failed to convince any of the governments to reverse their stance on prohibition.[^cite_note-Prohibition_in_Canada_Bellamy-magazine2009-9] Most provinces repealed their bans in the 1920s, though alcohol was illegal in [Prince Edward Island](23071.md) from 1901 to 1948. By comparison, the *[Ontario Temperance Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Temperance_Act)* was in effect from 1916 to 1927.[^cite_note-Prohibition_in_Canada_Bellamy-journal2009-10]

## Distribution

Under the [Constitution of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Canada), responsibility for enacting laws and regulations regarding the sale and distribution of [alcoholic drinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_drink) in [Canada](5042916.md) is the sole responsibility of the ten [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Canada). Canada's three [territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territories_of_Canada) have also been granted similar autonomy over these matters under the provisions of [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_federalism) [legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislation).

This means that there is a separate agency (or agencies) in each province responsible for regulating the consumption of and, in all but one case, the sale of alcoholic drinks. [Alberta](717.md) is currently the only jurisdiction to have completely [privatized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatisation) its retail liquor industry (the [AGLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Gaming,_Liquor_and_Cannabis_Commission) maintains a monopoly over the wholesale distribution of wine, distilled spirits and imported beer — the distribution operation itself being contracted out to a private operator). Most of the other jurisdictions have maintained a total or near-total control over the sale of [hard liquor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_liquor) while allowing limited privatisation of country-originated [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer) and [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine) sales.

Most provinces of Canada enacted prohibition of alcohol sales, consumption and distribution between the years of 1910 and 1920, during [Prohibition in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_Canada). After prohibition ended, provinces enacted minimum drinking ages of 20 or 21 years. In the early 70s, the age limits were lowered to either 18 or 19 years of age to align with the [age of majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_majority). Later, a few provinces and territories raised their age limit from 18 to 19 in the late 1970s and early 1980s.[^cite_note-28]

### Sales



Alcohol and cannabis sales in Canada, April 2022 to March 2023

The selling hours of alcohol, both on and off-premises, are also appointed by provincial and territorial jurisdiction, as long as off-premises sale hours do not coincide with curfew hours. Many provinces and territories define the off-premises sale of hard liquor, either by alcohol volume or by quantities thereof, to be sold only within specific hours, which usually correspond to the opening hours of a given vendor. However, in some of them, it is also possible to derogate to the current norm upon applying for a distributor's licence, under certain circumstances. The on-premises sale is allowed at the discretion of the premise, with the hours being regulated by every province.

In general, most provinces have banned "[tied houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tied_house)" (bars that are affiliated with only one alcohol supplier), in favour of [free houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_house_\(pub\)) which sell products from a variety of suppliers. A partial exception is made for [brewpubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewpubs) where a bar and brewery are on the same site.

### Consumption

The consumption of alcohol in public places is generally forbidden, regardless of the time ([in a few provinces and territories this is still not enforced](https://www.partnershipagainstcancer.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/provincial-territorial-alcohol-policy-pack-en.pdf)), unless a permit to do so is delivered by the responsible municipal authorities. In Quebec the consumption of drinks with low alcohol contents is permitted in public if accompanied by food. In all of the provinces and territories, the consumption of alcohol is forbidden while driving, with Ontario and Quebec also forbidding the possession of open non-empty containers within a motionless vehicle. Police in Canada are known to show considerable discretion to public consumption based on the amount of public disruption.
