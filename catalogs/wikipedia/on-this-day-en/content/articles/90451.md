**Amazon.com, Inc.**[^cite_note-10K-1] ([doing business as](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doing_business_as) **Amazon**)[^cite_note-5] is an American multinational conglomerate engaged in [e-commerce](9611.md), [retail](183515.md), [cloud computing](19541494.md), [online advertising](1555671.md), [digital streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_streaming), [entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment), and [artificial intelligence](1164.md).[^cite_note-6] Founded in 1994 by [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos) in [Bellevue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue,_Washington), Washington,[^cite_note-7] the company started as an online book marketplace that gradually expanded to include virtually all consumer products as well as many other categories, positioned as "The Everything Store".[^cite_note-8]

As of 2026 it was the world's biggest online [retailer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retailer). Amazon sells [books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Books), [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics), [pharmaceuticals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacy), [car parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_parts), [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_games), [dining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dining) products, [jewelry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewelry), [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food), [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music), and [sports equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_equipment).[^cite_note-9]

The company has multiple subsidiaries, including [Amazon Web Services](1691376.md) (AWS), which provides cloud computing; [Zoox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoox_\(company\)), a [self-driving car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-driving_car) division; satellite Internet provider [Kuiper Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuiper_Systems); and [Amazon Lab126](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Lab126), a computer hardware [R\&D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R\&D) provider. Other subsidiaries include [Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_\(company\)), [Twitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)), [IMDb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMDb), and grocer [Whole Foods Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_Foods_Market). Amazon distributes downloadable and streaming content through its [Amazon Prime Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video), [MGM+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM+), [Amazon Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Music), Twitch, [Audible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audible_\(service\)), and [Wondery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wondery)[^cite_note-10] units. It publishes books through its publishing arm, [Amazon Publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Publishing); produces and distributes film and television content through [Amazon MGM Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_MGM_Studios), including the [Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer) studio; and owns Brilliance Audio and [Audible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audible_\(service\)), which produce and distribute [audiobooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audiobook), respectively. Amazon produces consumer electronics, most notably [Kindle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Kindle) e-readers, [Echo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Echo) devices, [Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Fire) tablets, and [Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Fire_TV) TVs.

Amazon has a reputation as an industry disruptor through innovation and aggressive reinvestment of profits.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] As of 2023, it was the largest [online retailer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_retailer) and [marketplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_marketplace), [smart speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_speaker) provider, cloud computing service provider through AWS,[^cite_note-15] [live-streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live-streaming) service provider through Twitch, and [Internet company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_Internet_companies) as measured by revenue and market share.[^cite_note-16] In 2021, it surpassed [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart) as the world's largest retailer outside of China, supported by its subscription plan, [Amazon Prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime), which had 200 million subscribers worldwide.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] It is the [second-largest private employer in the US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_United_States%E2%80%93based_employers_globally),[^cite_note-19] [second-largest company in the US by revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_companies_in_the_United_States_by_revenue) as of 2024 (after Walmart),[^cite_note-20] and the [largest company in the world by revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_companies_by_revenue) as of 2026.[^cite_note-21] As of October 2024, Amazon was the world's [12th-most visited website](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-visited_websites). 84% of its traffic comes from the US.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-auto5-23]

[Amazon has been criticized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criticism_of_Amazon) for its alleged business practices, including [surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance) partnerships, poor working conditions, [anti-union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_busting) efforts, [environmental harm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_degradation), [anticompetitive behavior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-competitive_practices), [censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship) controversies, and exploitative treatment of small businesses and suppliers.

## History

Main article: [History of Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Amazon)

### 1994–2010



Jeff Bezos's home in [Bellevue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue,_Washington), Washington, where the company was founded in 1994

On July 5, 1994, Amazon was founded as "Cadabra" by [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos), after he relocated from [New York City](645042.md) to [Bellevue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue,_Washington), Washington, to operate an online bookstore. Bezos chose Seattle for its abundance of technical talent from [Microsoft](19001.md) and the [University of Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Washington), as well as its smaller population and the proximity to a major book distribution warehouse in [Roseburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roseburg,_Oregon), Oregon. Seattle beat out [Portland](23503.md), Oregon and [Boulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulder,_Colorado), Colorado.[^cite_note-SeattleTimes-25-24]

In November 1994, Bezos renamed the company Amazon to reflect his ambitions for his company. (Amazon is the world's largest river.)[^cite_note-25]

On July 16, 1995, the Amazon website launched, initially sourcing its books directly from wholesalers and publishers.[^cite_note-SeattleTimes-25-24][^cite_note-26]

In May 1997, Amazon went [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_company).[^cite_note-anders1999a-27][^cite_note-anders1999b-28]

In 1998, Amazon began selling music and videos. It began international operations by acquiring online booksellers in the UK and Germany. In 1999, it expanded to sell more products, including music, video games, consumer electronics, home improvement items, software, games, and toys.[^cite_note-anders1999a-27][^cite_note-anders1999b-28]

In 2000, US toy retailer [Toys "R" Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toys_%22R%22_Us) entered into a 10-year agreement with Amazon, valued at \$50 million per year plus a cut of sales, under which Toys "R" Us would be the exclusive supplier of toys and baby products on the service, and the chain's website would redirect to Amazon's Toys & Games category. In 2004, Toys "R" Us sued Amazon, claiming that because of a perceived lack of variety in Toys "R" Us stock, Amazon had knowingly allowed third-party sellers to offer items on the service in categories that Toys "R" Us had been granted exclusivity. In 2006, a court ruled in favor of Toys "R" Us, giving it the right to unwind its agreement with Amazon and establish its independent e-commerce website. The company was later awarded \$51 million in damages.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

In 2001, Amazon entered into a similar agreement with [Borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_\(retailer\)), under which Amazon would comanage Borders.com as a co-branded service.[^cite_note-32] Borders pulled out of the arrangement in 2007.[^cite_note-33]

In 2002, the company launched [Amazon Web Services](1691376.md) (AWS), which initially focused on providing APIs for web developers to build [web applications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_application) on top of Amazon's [e-commerce](9611.md) platform.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] In 2004, AWS expanded to provide website popularity statistics and web crawler data from its Alexa Web Information Service.[^cite_note-36] AWS later shifted toward providing enterprise services with [Simple Storage Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_S3) (S3) in 2006,[^cite_note-37] and [Elastic Compute Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Elastic_Compute_Cloud) (EC2) in 2008, allowing companies to rent data storage and computing power.[^cite_note-38]

In 2006, Amazon launched th&#x65;*&#x20;Fulfillment by Amazon&#x20;*&#x70;rogram, which allowed individuals and small companies (called "third-party sellers") to sell products through Amazon's warehouses and fulfillment infrastructure.[^cite_note-amazon-fulfill-press-39]

In March 2008, Amazon bought Audible for about \$300 million.[^cite_note-40]

The AmazonBasics brand was introduced in 2009, offering a range of Amazon-branded products.[^cite_note-\:4-41]

In June 2010, Amazon Seller Product Suggestions was launched.[^cite_note-\:5-42]

### 2011–2020

On October 18, 2011, Amazon.com announced a partnership with [DC Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Comics) for the exclusive digital rights to many popular comics, including *[Superman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman)*, *[Batman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman)*, *[Green Lantern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Lantern)*, *[Sandman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandman_\(comics\))*, and *[Watchmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watchmen)*. The partnership caused booksellers such as [Barnes & Noble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnes_&_Noble) to remove these titles from its shelves.[^cite_note-43]

Amazon acquired [Kiva Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiva_Systems), a warehouse automation company, in 2012.

Amazon bought [Goodreads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodreads) in March 2013.[^cite_note-\:0-44]

In November 2013, Amazon announced a partnership with the [US Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service) to begin delivering orders on Sundays. The service, included in Amazon's standard shipping rates, initiated in metropolitan areas of [Greater Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles) and [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area), with plans to expand into [Dallas](53838.md), [Houston](13774.md), [New Orleans](53842.md) and [Phoenix](49121.md) by 2014.[^cite_note-45]

Amazon acquired [Twitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)) in August 2014 for \$970 million.[^cite_note-46]

In November 2015, Amazon opened a physical [Amazon Books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Books) store in [University Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Village,_Seattle) in [Seattle](11388236.md). The store was 5,500 square feet and prices for all products matched those on its website.[^cite_note-\:1-47] Amazon opened its tenth physical bookstore in 2017.[^cite_note-48] All locations were closed in 2022 along with other retail locations under the "Amazon 4-Star" brand.[^cite_note-49]

In July 2016, the company announced that it was opening a 1,100,000 ft (335,280.0 m) square foot facility in [Palmer Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmer_Township,_Pennsylvania) in the [Lehigh Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley) region of eastern [Pennsylvania](23332.md). As of 2024, Amazon is the Lehigh Valley region's third-largest employer.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

In June 2017, [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) agreed to sell products through Amazon in exchange for better policing of counterfeit goods.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-CNBC-53] This proved unsuccessful and Nike withdrew from the partnership in November 2019.[^cite_note-CNBC-53][^cite_note-54] Companies including [IKEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IKEA) and [Birkenstock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birkenstock) also stopped selling through Amazon around the same time, citing similar frustrations over business practices and counterfeit goods.[^cite_note-55]

Amazon purchased [Whole Foods Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_Foods_Market) supermarket chain for \$13.7 billion in 2017.[^cite_note-56] It had over 300 million active customer accounts as of 2020.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-wf-60]

In September 2017, Amazon ventured with one of its sellers JV Appario Retail owned by Patni Group which recorded income of US\$104.44 million ([₹](490783.md)759 [crore](121420.md)) in financial year 2017–2018.[^cite_note-61]

As of October 11, 2017, [Amazon Fresh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Fresh) sold a range of [Booths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booths) branded products for home delivery in selected areas.[^cite_note-62]

Amazon bought [Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_\(company\)) for US\$1 billion in 2018.[^cite_note-63] On November 13, 2018, Amazon announced that it would divide its planned [HQ2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_HQ2) between [New York City](645042.md) and [Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia).[^cite_note-64] On February 14, 2019, Amazon canceled its plan for the New York City HQ2 site.

In November 2018, Amazon reached an agreement with [Apple Inc.](856.md) to sell selected products through the service, via the company and selected Apple Authorized Resellers. As a result of this partnership, only Apple Authorized Resellers were allowed to sell Apple products on Amazon effective January 4, 2019.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

The Amazon Live e-commerce streaming service launched in 2019.[^cite_note-AmazonLive-67]

Amazon experienced rapid growth during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), hiring more than 100,000 staff in the US and [Canada](5042916.md).[^cite_note-68]

### 2021–present

On July 5, 2021, Bezos moved from CEO to executive board chair. Former AWS CEO [Andy Jassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Jassy) replaced him as CEO.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] In January 2023, Amazon cut over 18,000 jobs, primarily in consumer retail and its human resources division in an attempt to cut costs.[^cite_note-71]

In September 2021, Amazon announced the launch of [Astro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Astro), its first household robot.[^cite_note-\:8-72]

On November 8, 2023, a plan was adopted for [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos) to sell approximately 50 million [shares](867877.md) of the company over the next year with a deadline of January 31, 2025. The first step was the sale of 12 million shares for about \$2 billion.[^cite_note-73]

In 2023, Amazon invested \$1.25B in [Anthropic](6206236.md). In return Anthropic designated AWS as a primary cloud partner and began using Amazon's Trainium and Inferentia chips. Anthropic Claude models became available on Amazon Bedrock. In March 2024, Amazon invested a further \$2.75B. In November, Amazon invested \$4B more. In April 2026, Amazon invested \$5B.[^cite_note-74]

In January 2023, Amazon announced the launch of RXPass, a prescription drug delivery service.[^cite_note-\:9-75]

In February 2024, Amazon announced its first chatbot, "Rufus", for use in the US; in July it was widely available to all customers in the US.[^cite_note-76] "Rufus" is available in the US, India and the UK to help shoppers get product recommendations, shopping list advice, compare products, and see what other customers have responded to regarding their specific questions.[^cite_note-77]

On February 26, 2024, Amazon became a component of the [Dow Jones Industrial Average](47361.md).[^cite_note-78]

On December 19, 2024, Amazon workers, led by the [International Brotherhood of Teamsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Brotherhood_of_Teamsters) labor union, went on [strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Teamsters_Amazon_Strike) against Amazon in at least four states.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

On October 8, 2025, Amazon launched prescription vending machines at One Medical clinics in Los Angeles. The machines are operated by Amazon Pharmacy and are able to prescribe a range of common medications, such as antibiotics, inhalers and blood pressure treatments.[^cite_note-81]

On October 28, 2025, the company announced plans to reduce its corporate workforce by 14,000 positions, reported as Amazon's largest round of job cuts since 2022.[^cite_note-82] In January 2026, Amazon announced a second round of layoffs, affecting approximately 16,000 corporate employees. Company statements described the layoffs as part of a broader effort to streamline operations, reduce [bureaucracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureaucracy), and adapt to changing business conditions, including [artificial intelligence](1164.md) technologies. Affected employees were offered severance packages and support for internal job placement.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

In January 2025, Amazon filed an objection to [Saks Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exemplar_Luxury_Group)'s bankruptcy plan. Amazon invested \$475 million into Saks' acquisition of [Neiman Marcus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neiman_Marcus), and worried that Saks' bankruptcy would harm creditors and push the tech company further down the repayment pecking order.[^cite_note-86]

In January 2026, Amazon entered talks with [OpenAI](48795986.md) to invest up to \$50 billion.[^cite_note-87] Amazon had a deep strategic partnership with [Anthropic](6206236.md), OpenAI's competitor.[^cite_note-88] In January 2026, Amazon laid off 16,000 employees in its second round of large-scale job reductions in three months due to [AI](1164.md).[^cite_note-89]

In March 2026, Amazon pledged to bear the entire cost of new electricity generation to power its data centers.[^cite_note-90]

In 2020, [Amazon Fresh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Fresh) opened several physical stores in the US and the [UK](31717.md).[^cite_note-91] In January 2026, Amazon announced it would close all of its Amazon Go and Amazon Fresh stores to shift focus to its online same-day delivery service and expand its Whole Foods Market business. The closure affects 57 Fresh stores and 15 Amazon Go locations, some of which will be converted into Whole Foods stores.[^cite_note-92]

Amazon launched Amazon Supply Chain Services in May 2026.[^cite_note-93]

Launched in 2005, Mechanical Turk provided a crowdsourcing platform through which businesses and researchers could outsource human-completed tasks. The service was also used for human data annotation and review in machine-learning applications, including through Amazon SageMaker. In August 2026, Amazon announced that AWS Mechanical Turk would be discontinued on September 30, 2026, ending the service after more than 21 years.[^cite_note-94]

Amazon sells many products under its [own brand names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_label), including phone chargers, batteries, and diaper wipes. and features hundreds of product lines. Amazon owned 34 private-label brands as of 2019. These brands account for 0.15% of Amazon's global sales, whereas the average for other large retailers is 18%.[^cite_note-\:4-41] Other Amazon retail brands include AmazonBasics, Presto!, Mama Bear, and Amazon Essentials.[^cite_note-110]

#### Third-party sellers

Amazon derives many of its sales (around 40% in 2008) from third-party sellers who sell products on Amazon.[^cite_note-111] Some other large e-commerce sellers use Amazon to sell their products in addition to selling direct. Orders are processed through Amazon.com and end up at individual sellers for processing and order. Some sellers lease space from Amazon. Small sellers of used and new goods go to Amazon Marketplace to offer goods.[^cite_note-112]

#### Affiliate program

Publishers can sign up as affiliates and receive a commission for referring customers to Amazon by placing links to Amazon on their websites should the referral result in a sale. Worldwide, Amazon had over 900,000 affiliates (2010).[^cite_note-113] As of mid-2014, the Amazon Affiliate Program was used by 1.2% of all websites and was the second most popular advertising network after [Google Ads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Ads).[^cite_note-114] It is frequently used by sellers and non-profits to provide a way for supporters to earn them a commission.[^cite_note-115]

Associates can access the Amazon catalog directly on their websites by using the [Amazon Web Services](1691376.md) (AWS) [XML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XML) service. An affiliate service, aStore, allows Associates to embed a subset of Amazon products within another website, or linked to another website. Amazon Seller Product Suggestions provides transparency to sellers by recommending specific products to third-party sellers to sell on Amazon.[^cite_note-\:5-42]

#### Product reviews

See also: [Criticism of Amazon § Amazon reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criticism_of_Amazon#Amazon_reviews)

Amazon allows users to submit reviews of each product. Reviewers rate the product from one to five stars and offer commentary. Amazon provides a badging option for reviewers that indicates the real name of the reviewer (based on confirmation of a credit card account) or that indicates that the reviewer is one of the top reviewers by popularity. Readers can indicate whether the review is helpful or report that it abuses Amazon policies. If a review is given enough "helpful" hits, it appears on the product's page.[^cite_note-116]

When publishers asked Bezos why Amazon would publish negative reviews, he defended the practice by claiming that Amazon.com was "taking a different approach...we want to make every book available—the good, the bad and the ugly...to let truth loose".[^cite_note-117]

Some positive reviews were written and posted by [public relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_relations) companies on behalf of their clients[^cite_note-118] and instances of pseudonyms users leaving negative reviews of their rivals' works.

#### Amazon sales rank

The Amazon sales rank (ASR) indicates the popularity of a product sold on any Amazon locale. It is a relative indicator of popularity that is updated hourly. Effectively, it is a "best sellers list" for the millions of products.[^cite_note-amazon-119] While the ASR has no direct effect on product sales, it determines which products appear in its bestseller lists.[^cite_note-amazon-119] Products that appear in these lists enjoy additional exposure and may increase sales. Products that experience large jumps (up or down) in the rankings may appear on Amazon's lists of "movers and shakers".[^cite_note-120] For competitive reasons, Amazon does not release sales figures. However, Amazon releases [point of sale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_of_sale) data via the [BookScan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BookScan) service to verified authors.[^cite_note-121] While the ASR was the source of much speculation by publishers, manufacturers, and marketers, Amazon does not release the details of its sales rank algorithm. Some companies analyze Amazon sales data to generate sales estimates based on the ASR,[^cite_note-122] though Amazon states:

> Please keep in mind that our sales rank figures are simply meant to be a guide of general interest for the customer and not definitive sales information for publishers—we assume you have this information regularly from your distribution sources

— Amazon.com Help[^cite_note-123]

#### Amazon Web Services



[Amazon Web Services](1691376.md), [HOU-14](13774.md)

This section is an excerpt from [Amazon Web Services](1691376.md).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Amazon_Web_Services\&action=edit)]

[Amazon Web Services, Inc.](1691376.md) (AWS) is a subsidiary of Amazon that provides [on-demand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_as_a_service) [cloud computing](19541494.md) [platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computing_platform) and [APIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/API) to individuals, companies, and governments, on a metered, pay-as-you-go basis.

Clients often use this in combination with [autoscaling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elasticity_\(system_resource\)) (a process that allows a client to use more computing in times of high application usage, and then scale down to reduce costs when there is less traffic). These cloud computing [web services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_service) provide various services related to networking, compute, storage, [middleware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middleware), [IoT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_of_things) and other processing capacity, as well as [software tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_tools) via AWS [server farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Server_farm). This frees clients from managing, scaling, and patching hardware and operating systems.

One of the foundational services is [Amazon Elastic Compute Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Elastic_Compute_Cloud) (EC2), which allows users to have at their disposal a [virtual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtualization) [cluster of computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_cluster), with extremely high availability, which can be interacted with over the Internet via [REST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/REST) APIs, a [CLI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command-line_interface) or the AWS console. AWS's virtual computers emulate most of the attributes of a real computer, including hardware [central processing units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_processing_unit) (CPUs) and [graphics processing units](390214.md) (GPUs) for processing; local/[RAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random-access_memory) memory; [hard-disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_disk_drive) (HDD)/[SSD storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-state_drive); a choice of [operating systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system); networking; and pre-loaded application software such as [web servers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_server), [databases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Database), and [customer relationship management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customer_relationship_management) (CRM).

AWS services are delivered to customers via a network of AWS server farms located throughout the world. Fees are based on a combination of usage (known as a "Pay-as-you-go" model), hardware, operating system, software, and networking features chosen by the subscriber requiring various degrees of [availability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Availability_\(system\)), [redundancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redundancy_\(engineering\)), [security](7398.md), and service options. Subscribers can pay for a single virtual AWS computer, a dedicated physical computer, or clusters of either.[^cite_note-Amazon_Web_Services_AWSagreement-126] Amazon provides select portions of security for subscribers (e.g. physical security of the data centers) while other aspects of security are the responsibility of the subscriber (e.g. account management, vulnerability scanning, patching). AWS operates from many global geographical regions, including nine in North America.[^cite_note-Amazon_Web_Services_GlobalInfrastructure-127]

Amazon markets AWS to subscribers as a way of obtaining large-scale computing capacity more quickly and cheaply than building an actual physical server farm.[^cite_note-128] All services are billed based on usage, but each service measures usage in varying ways. As of 2023 Q1, AWS has 31% market share for cloud infrastructure while the next two competitors [Microsoft Azure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Azure) and [Google Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Cloud_Platform) have 25%, and 11% respectively, according to Synergy Research Group.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130] AWS was the first cloud provider to build infrastructure specifically for U.S. government with respect to security and compliance requirements (2011; launched AWS GovCloud), accredited to support classified workloads (2014; launched AWS Top Secret), and achieve accreditation across all U.S. government data classifications (2017; launched AWS Secret).[^cite_note-Amazon_Web_Services_auto3-131][^cite_note-132]

#### Ring

[Ring LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_\(company\)) is a manufacturer of [home security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_security) and [smart home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_automation) devices owned by Amazon. Ring's product lines have faced scrutiny over privacy issues. The Neighbors service has been criticized by [civil rights](37071.md) advocacy groups as building a private surveillance network backed by law enforcement agencies until the 'Request for Assistance (RFA)' option was discontinued in 2024. [Twitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\)) is an American video [live-streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live-streaming) service popular in [video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_live_streaming), including broadcasts of [esports](564204.md) competitions. At the same time, Justin.tv's parent company was re-branded as Twitch Interactive to represent the shift in focus when Justin.tv was getting shut down in August 2014.[^cite_note-Twitch_(service)_Truong-2014-153] The same month, the service was acquired by Amazon for US\$970 million,[^cite_note-Twitch_(service)_Twitch_Blog-2014-154][^cite_note-Twitch_(service)_Kim-2014-155] which later led to the introduction of synergies with the company's subscription service [Amazon Prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime).

### Supply chain

Amazon launched its distribution network in 1997 with fulfillment centers in Seattle and [New Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Castle,_Delaware), Delaware. Amazon has several types of distribution facilities including cross-dock centers, fulfillment centers, sorting centers, delivery stations, Prime now hubs, and Prime air hubs. As of 2018 the US had 75 fulfillment centers and 25 sorting centers with over 125,000 employees.[^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234] Employees are responsible for:

- unpacking and inspecting incoming goods

- placing goods in storage and recording their location

- picking goods from their computer recorded locations to make up an individual shipment

- sorting and packing orders

- shipping

A picker may walk 10 or more miles a day. Employees carry hand-held computers that monitor their progress. Some warehouses are partially automated with systems built by [Amazon Robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Robotics).[^cite_note-235] Amazon [fulfillment centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulfillment_house) host hundreds or thousands of employees.

The Fulfillment By Amazon program handles storage, packing and distribution of products and services for small sellers.[^cite_note-amazon-fulfill-press-39][^cite_note-236] Third-party sellers can use FBA to ship for other platforms, such as [eBay](130495.md).[^cite_note-237]

In some fulfillment centers, items are stored on pods and are brought to pickers by four-wheeled robots. The robot slides under and lifts a pod slightly off the ground, before carrying it to a picker station.

In the US, many workers are hired as Amazon employees and granted shares of stock or sign-on bonuses, while others are offered seasonal positions.[^cite_note-FT02813-238]

### Corporate culture

Jeff Bezos became renowned for his annual shareholder letters.[^cite_note-\:7-263] These letters expressed Bezos's perspectives and strategic focus.[^cite_note-\:7-263][^cite_note-264] A common theme was his desire to instill customer-centricity (in his words, "customer obsession") at all levels of Amazon, notably by making all senior executives field customer support queries for a time at Amazon call centers. He read many emails addressed by customers to his public email address.[^cite_note-265] One of Bezos's most well-known internal memos was his mandate for all teams to "expose their data and functionality" through service interfaces "designed from the ground up to be externalizable". This process, commonly known as a [service-oriented architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service-oriented_architecture) (SOA), resulted in mandatory [dogfooding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eating_your_own_dog_food) of services that later became commercialized via AWS.[^cite_note-266]

Amazon used internal recognition programs such as the Just Do It Award to encourage employee initiative.[^cite_note-267][^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269]

#### Meeting culture

Amazon's meeting culture is document-driven. It is designed to encourage deep thinking, shared context, and efficient decision-making, avoiding polished presentations and large gatherings. Practices:[^cite_note-270]

- Narrative memos: Amazon bans slide decks in favor of detailed memos—typically 4–6 pages of complete sentences and paragraphs (not bullet points). These are intended to reveal weak or incomplete ideas. Memos may take significant time (days or longer) to prepare, sometimes collaboratively.

- Reading: Meetings commonly begin with reading the memo in silence. The information is fresh in attendees' minds, and discussion begins using a shared context.

- Two-pizzas: Teams and meetings are small enough that two pizzas could feed the attendees (6–8 or fewer people).

- "Messy," exploratory discussions: meeting conversations are unscripted, encouraged to wander, explore controversy, expose dissenting views, and explore ideas deeply. Bezos preferred a "crisp document and a messy meeting." Senior voices often delay speaking so juniors can contribute freely. The approach elevates written clarity over persuasion or hierarchy. Meetings may include an empty chair to represent the customer.

### Lobbying

Amazon lobbies governments on issues such as the enforcement of sales taxes on online sales, transportation safety, privacy and [data protection](237536.md), and intellectual property. According to regulatory filings, Amazon.com focuses its US lobbying on the [US Congress](31756.md), the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) and the [Federal Reserve](10819.md). In 2019, it spent \$16.8 million and had a team of 104 lobbyists.[^cite_note-271]

Amazon.com was a corporate member of the [American Legislative Exchange Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Legislative_Exchange_Council) (ALEC) until it dropped membership following protests at its shareholders' meeting on May 24, 2012.[^cite_note-272]

In 2014, Amazon expanded its lobbying practices as it prepared to lobby the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) to approve its drone delivery program, hiring the [Akin Gump Strauss Hauer & Feld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akin_Gump_Strauss_Hauer_&_Feld) lobbying firm in June.[^cite_note-273] Amazon and its lobbyists have visited with Federal Aviation Administration officials and aviation committees in Washington, D.C. to explain its plans to deliver packages.[^cite_note-274] In September 2020 this moved one step closer with the granting of a critical certificate by the FAA.[^cite_note-275]

During the second Trump Administration, Amazon donated several times to various events and aspects of his presidency. Along with several other major companies, Amazon donated \$1 million to Trump's inaugural fund.[^cite_note-276] In April 2025, Amazon was a corporate sponsor of the [White House Easter Egg Roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_rolling#United_States), after Donald Trump solicited corporate sponsors for the event for the first time.[^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278] In October 2025, Amazon was listed among the donors funding the construction of the [White House State Ballroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_State_Ballroom).[^cite_note-279]

Amazon lobbies on climate policy internationally. In the US, following Trump's 2025 inauguration, Amazon began frequent engagements with the executive branch to advocate for permitting reform and energy development to support data center growth. In July 2025, Amazon joined policymakers at the Pennsylvania Energy and Innovation Summit, where it supported the development of nuclear energy to meet growing electricity demand, and lobbied for permitting reform to support grid development nationwide.[^cite_note-280][^cite_note-281] Amazon sponsored the Conservative Coalition for Climate Solutions' (C3) Energy Summit in Washington D.C. in December 2025, which brought together administration officials and industry representatives to discuss meeting the country's growing electricity demand through modernization of the permitting process, investment in energy infrastructure, and scaling new technologies.[^cite_note-282] A December 2025 DeSmog article reported that the company's sponsorship came alongside several organizations that had supported climate change denial, and a continued role for fossil fuels to meet growing energy demand.[^cite_note-283][^cite_note-284] In 2026, Amazon spent \$5 million lobbying against the Delivery Protection Act, a New York City bill introduced by [Tiffany Cabán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_Cab%C3%A1n) that would require companies like Amazon to directly employ [last](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_mile_\(transportation\)) [mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_mile_\(transportation\)) delivery drivers and warehouse workers, as well as set licensing, safety, and training requirements for last mile facilities.[^cite_note-285]

Amazon has been criticized on many issues, including [anti-competitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-competitive_practices) business practices, its treatment of workers, offering counterfeit or plagiarized products, objectionable content of its books, tax and subsidy deals with governments[^cite_note-286] supplying law enforcement with facial recognition surveillance tools;[^cite_note-Criticism_of_Amazon_washingtonpost.com-287] forming cloud computing partnerships with the [CIA](5183633.md);[^cite_note-Criticism_of_Amazon_vanity-fair-288] leading customers away from bookshops;[^cite_note-289] adversely impacting the environment;[^cite_note-Criticism_of_Amazon_Cavaillez-290] placing a low priority on warehouse conditions for workers;[^cite_note-291][^cite_note-292] actively opposing unionization efforts;[^cite_note-293] remotely deleting content purchased by Amazon Kindle users; taking public subsidies; seeking to patent its [1-Click](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-Click) technology; engaging in anti-competitive actions and [price discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_discrimination);[^cite_note-Criticism_of_Amazon_\:6-294][^cite_note-Criticism_of_Amazon_baum-295] and reclassifying [LGBTQ](78174325.md) books as adult content.[^cite_note-296][^cite_note-297] Criticism has also concerned various decisions over whether to censor or publish content such as the [WikiLeaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WikiLeaks) website, works containing [libel](28661.md), anti-LGBT merchandise, and material facilitating [dog fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog_fighting), [cockfighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockfighting), or [pedophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedophilia) activities.&#x20;

An article published by *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* in the wake of social media website [Parler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parler)'s termination of service by Amazon Web Service highlights the power companies like Amazon now have over the internet.[^cite_note-298] In December 2011, Amazon faced a backlash from small businesses for running a one-day deal to promote its new Price Check app. Shoppers who used the app to check prices in a brick-and-mortar store were offered a 5% discount to purchase the same item from Amazon.[^cite_note-299] Companies like [Groupon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groupon), [eBay](130495.md) and Taap have countered Amazon's promotion by offering \$10 off from its products.[^cite_note-300][^cite_note-301]

The company has also faced accusations of putting undue pressure on suppliers to maintain and extend its profitability. One effort to squeeze the most vulnerable book publishers was known within the company as the Gazelle Project, after Bezos suggested, according to [Brad Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Stone_\(journalist\)), "that Amazon should approach these small publishers the way a cheetah would pursue a sickly gazelle." In July 2014, the [Federal Trade Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Trade_Commission) launched a lawsuit against the company alleging it was promoting in-app purchases to children, which were being transacted without parental consent.[^cite_note-302] In 2019, Amazon banned selling [skin-lightening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_whitening) products after pushback from [Minnesota](19590.md) health and [environmental activists.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_movement)[^cite_note-303] In 2022, a lawsuit filed by state attorney-general [Letitia James](1598829.md) was dismissed by the New York state court of appeals.[^cite_note-304] After the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), Amazon faced criticism for complying, under pressure from the [Biden Administration](65732174.md), to "reduce the visibility" of books critical of the [COVID-19 vaccine](63319438.md),[^cite_note-305][^cite_note-306] which was revealed after Rep. [Jim Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Jordan) (acting on behalf of the [House Judiciary Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_the_Judiciary)) subpoenaed emails between the company and the Biden Administration.[^cite_note-307]



An Amazon Prime truck blocking a bike lane and double parking in Washington, D.C.

Amazon Prime has been criticized for its vehicles systemically [double parking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_parking), blocking [bike lanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike_lane), and otherwise violating traffic laws while dropping off packages, contributing to [traffic congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion) and endangering other road users.[^cite_note-308][^cite_note-309][^cite_note-310][^cite_note-311]

Jane Friedman[^cite_note-janefriedman/books-312] discovered six listings of books fraudulently using her name, on Amazon and [Goodreads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodreads). Amazon and Goodreads resisted removing the fraudulent titles until the author's complaints went viral on social media, in a blog post titled "I Would Rather See My Books Get Pirated Than This (Or: Why Goodreads and Amazon Are Becoming Dumpster Fires)."[^cite_note-janefriedman/rather-my-books-pirated-313][^cite_note-arstechnica/2023/08/ai-generated-counterfeit-books-amazon-314][^cite_note-vice/v7b774-315][^cite_note-washingtonpost/amazon-goodreads-316]

In 2024, following years of criticism for providing law enforcement footage in the custody of [Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_\(company\)) (a home security company owned by Amazon) without a warrant, Ring has halted this practice.[^cite_note-Criticism_of_Amazon_auto4-317] It received cautious praise from privacy-focused organizations such as the [Electronic Frontier Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Frontier_Foundation) for this change.[^cite_note-Criticism_of_Amazon_auto4-317]

In February 2025, [Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_UK) accused Amazon of not doing enough to prevent the piracy of its sports rights via "jailbroken" Fire Sticks.[^cite_note-318]

[Project Nimbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Nimbus) is a contract by which Amazon and Google provide [Israel](9282173.md) and [its military](39237.md) with artificial intelligence, machine learning, and other cloud-computing services.[^cite_note-319][^cite_note-320] The contract has been criticized by shareholders and employees concerned that the project may lead to abuses of [Palestinian](23267.md) human rights in the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](46216.md).[^cite_note-321] In October 2025, Amazon suspended an employee who posted messages to the company's Slack and wrote a letter to Andy Jassy criticizing Nimbus.[^cite_note-322][^cite_note-323]

On July 22, 2025, Amazon announced its plan to acquire [San Francisco](49728.md)-based startup Bee AI. Bee produces a wristband, similar to most modern [smartwatches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartwatch), equipped with AI and microphones. The wristband is capable of providing summaries of conversations and reminders of tasks, effectively serving as a 24/7 note taker. News outlets criticize the fact that the devices saves transcripts, potentially utilizing it to train generative AI models which require vast quantities of data.[^cite_note-324]

In January 2026, Amazon sent a notice out to cloud staffers in an apparent error acknowledging “organizational changes” at the company. The note also mentioned a "Project Dawn" which was cancelled, although it is unclear what the project is about.[^cite_note-325]
