# Inland port

This article is about a shipment facility on a river, lake, or canal. For a facility without water access, see [dry port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_port).

"River port" redirects here. For other uses, see [Riverport (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverport_\(disambiguation\)).



The [Port of Lappeenranta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Lappeenranta) on the [Lake Saimaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Saimaa) in [Lappeenranta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lappeenranta), [Finland](10577.md)

An **inland port** is a [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) on an inland [waterway](174851.md), such as a [river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River), [lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake), or [canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal), which may or may not be connected to the sea. The term "inland port" is also used to refer to a [dry port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_port).

## Examples

The [United States Army Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Corps_of_Engineers), which has three primary mission areas: [Engineer Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Corps_of_Engineers#The_Engineer_Regiment), [military construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_construction), and [civil works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_works), publishes biannually a list of such locations and for this purpose states that "inland ports" are ports that are located on rivers and do not handle deep [draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_\(hull\)) [ship traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_transport). The list includes ports such as [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri), [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati,_Ohio), [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh,_Pennsylvania), [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri), and [Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee). A dense network of inland waterways including ports exists also in Europe (France, Germany, Poland, Russia, the United Kingdom and the [Benelux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benelux) countries), as well as in China and Brazil.



The [Port of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Vancouver), [Canada](5042916.md)'s busiest port
