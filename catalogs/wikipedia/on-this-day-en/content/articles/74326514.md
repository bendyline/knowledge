**Anicet Francois Dybantsa Jr.** ([/ˌɑːniˈseɪ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*ah-nee-SAY*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), [/diːˈbɑːntsɑː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*dee-BAHNT-sah*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born January 29, 2007) is an American professional [basketball](3921.md) player for the [Washington Wizards](72858.md) of the [National Basketball Association](22093.md) (NBA). Dybantsa played one year of [college basketball](768856.md) for the [BYU Cougars](9969579.md), earning the [Julius Erving Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Erving_Award) after [leading the nation in scoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_season_scoring_leaders) for the [2025–26 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_NCAA_Division_I_men's_basketball_season), prior to being selected [first overall](737534.md) by the Wizards in the [2026 NBA draft](82721211.md). He is also a member of the [U.S. national under-19 team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_men's_national_under-19_basketball_team), earning a gold medal at the [2025 FIBA Under-19 Basketball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIBA_Under-19_Basketball_World_Cup) and [tournament MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Under-19_World_Cup_MVP) honors.

## Early life

Anicet Francois Dybantsa Jr. was born on January 29, 2007, in [Boston](24437894.md), Massachusetts, and grew up in [Brockton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brockton,_Massachusetts) where he attended [Saint Sebastian's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sebastian's_School).[^cite_note-CBS_Boston-1][^cite_note-The_Boston_Globe-2] His father Ace was born in the [Republic of the Congo](19599929.md) and later moved to [Grigny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigny,_Essonne), France, while his mother Chelsea is from [Jamaica](15660.md).

He was named the 2023 Massachusetts Boys' Basketball Gatorade Player of the Year following his freshman season after averaging 19.1 points, 9.6 rebounds, 2.9 assists and 2.5 blocks per game.[^cite_note-ProlificPrepTransfer-3] Dybantsa helped lead Saint Sebastian's to the [NEPSAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEPSAC) Class A state championship, but lost to [Milton Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Academy) 77–76.[^cite_note-2023U16USA-4]

Dybantsa transferred to [Prolific Prep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prolific_Prep) in [Napa, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napa,_California), at the end of his freshman year, where he teamed up with the second-ranked player of the 2026 class, [Tyran Stokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyran_Stokes).[^cite_note-ProlificPrepTransfer-3][^cite_note-ProlificPrep-5] In July 2023, Dybantsa led the [Nike Peach Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_Elite_Youth_Basketball_League#EYBL_circuit) in scoring with 25.8 points per game[^cite_note-On3#1Player-6] as a member of Expressions Elite, an [Amateur Athletic Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_Athletic_Union) team from Boston.[^cite_note-2023PeachJam-7]

Dybantsa concluded his high school basketball career playing for Utah Prep Academy in [Hurricane, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane,_Utah), during the 2024–25 season.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

### Recruiting

Dybantsa was named the number one NBA prospect in the 2026 class by [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) following his high school freshman season,[^cite_note-Dybantsa#1ESPN-10] with his first [NCAA Division I](669931.md) college offer coming from [Boston College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_College_Eagles_men's_basketball).[^cite_note-DybantsaBCOffer-11] Additional schools that offered him a scholarship and/or were under consideration include [Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alabama_Crimson_Tide_men's_basketball), [Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas_Razorbacks_men's_basketball), [Auburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auburn_Tigers_men's_basketball), [Baylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baylor_Bears_men's_basketball), [BYU](9969579.md), [UConn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UConn_Huskies_men's_basketball), [Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Blue_Devils_men's_basketball), [Georgia Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Tech_Yellow_Jackets_men's_basketball), [Georgetown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_Hoyas_men's_basketball), [Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_Wildcats_men's_basketball), [North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Tar_Heels_men's_basketball), [Providence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Friars_men's_basketball), [USC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USC_Trojans_men's_basketball), [Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Utes_men's_basketball) and [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Huskies_men's_basketball).[^cite_note-Dybantsa#Suitors-12][^cite_note-EarlyOffers-13] In July 2023, [On3.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On3.com) named Dybantsa the top high school player in the country regardless of class.[^cite_note-On3#1Player-6] In October 2023, Dybantsa announced that he had [reclassified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reclassification_\(education\)) to the 2025 class.[^cite_note-Reclassified-14]

## College career

On December 10, 2024, Dybantsa announced on *[First Take](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Take_\(talk_show\))* that he would be committing to [BYU](9969579.md) as he wanted to play basketball without off-court distractions.[^cite_note-15] He became the highest-ranked committed recruit to join BYU in program history.[^cite_note-16]

On January 24, 2026, Dybantsa passed [Danny Ainge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Ainge) for the most points posted by a BYU freshman in a game, recording 43 points during a 91–78 victory over [Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Utah_Utes_men's_basketball_team).[^cite_note-17] On April 22, Dybantsa declared for the [2026 NBA draft](82721211.md), in which he was projected as a potential first overall pick.[^cite_note-18]

## Professional career

On June 23, 2026, Dybantsa was selected [first overall](737534.md) by the [Washington Wizards](72858.md) in the [2026 NBA draft](82721211.md).[^cite_note-19] He signed a four-year rookie contract worth \$66.91 million on July 2, 2026.[^cite_note-20]

## National team

Dybantsa won a gold medal while representing the United States at the [2023 FIBA Under-16 Americas Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIBA_Under-16_Americas_Championship) in [Mérida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9rida,_Yucat%C3%A1n), Mexico. He scored 12 points in the gold medal game and averaged 13.9 points for the tournament.[^cite_note-On3#1Player-6][^cite_note-U16FIBAGold-21][^cite_note-22] The following year, he helped the United States win the title at the [2024 FIBA U17 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_FIBA_Under-17_Basketball_World_Cup) in Turkey, where he averaged 14.1 points, 3.9 rebounds and 4.1 assists during the competition.[^cite_note-23] In 2025, Dybantsa was named [MVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Under-19_Basketball_World_Cup_Most_Valuable_Player) of the [2025 FIBA U19 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIBA_Under-19_Basketball_World_Cup), after helping the United States win their ninth title at the event.[^cite_note-24] He averaged 14.3 points, 4.1 rebounds and 2.3 assists during the tournament.[^cite_note-25]
