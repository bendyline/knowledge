The **9K720 Iskander** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): «Искандер»; [NATO reporting name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_reporting_name) **SS-26 Stone**) is a family of Russian mobile [short-range ballistic missile](1320069.md) systems. The basic M model has a range of 500 kilometres (270 nmi; 310 mi). It was intended to replace the [OTR-21 Tochka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OTR-21_Tochka) in the Russian military by 2020.

The Iskander has several different conventional warheads, including a cluster munitions warhead, a [fuel–air explosive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermobaric_weapon) enhanced-blast warhead, a high-explosive fragmentation warhead, an earth penetrator for [bunker busting](167751.md) and an [electromagnetic pulse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_pulse) device for anti-radar missions. The missile can also carry nuclear warheads.[^cite_note-IskHist-9][^cite_note-Isk-10][^cite_note-11] In September 2017, the [KB Mashinostroyeniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KB_Mashinostroyeniya) (KBM) general designer Valery M. Kashin said that there were at least seven types of missiles (and "perhaps more") for Iskander, including one [cruise missile](6590.md).

## History

The road-mobile Iskander[^cite_note-12] was the second attempt by Russia to replace the [Scud missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scud_missile). The first attempt, the [OTR-23 Oka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OTR-23_Oka), was eliminated under the [INF Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INF_Treaty). The design work on Iskander was begun in December 1988, initially directed by the KBM [rocket weaponry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_weapon) designer [Sergey Nepobedimy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Nepobedimy), and was not significantly affected by the [dissolution of the USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1991.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-rs190514-14]

The first successful launch occurred in 1996.[^cite_note-expert.ru-15]

In September 2004, at a meeting with senior defense officials reporting to [President](239646.md) [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) on the drafting of a defense budget for 2005, the [Russian Defence Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Ministry_of_Defence) [Sergei Ivanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Ivanov) spoke about the completion of static tests of a new tactical missile system called the Iskander. He said that the system would go into quantity production in 2005 and toward the end of that year, Russia would have a brigade armed with it.[^cite_note-IskHist-9] In March 2005, a source in the Russian defence industry told Interfax-AVN the development of new missiles with a range of 500–600 kilometres (270–320 nmi; 310–370 mi), based on existing Iskander-E tactical missile systems, was a possibility. He said, however, that it "may take up to five or six years".[^cite_note-IskHist-9]

In 2006, serial production of the Iskander-M tactical ballistic missile system was launched, and the system was adopted by the Russian army.[^cite_note-IskHist-9] The production cost of the missile system was reported in 2014 to have been slashed by a third by cutting the 20% markup applied by the missile manufacturer at each stage of the components supply chain from a cumulative 810% to markup of 21% applied only to the finished product.[^cite_note-16]

There was a report by GosNIIP, the design bureau that builds guidance for cruise missiles, that Russia completed state acceptance trials of the "ground-based 9M728/9M729 missiles and their modernized version."

In November 2016, the Russian military announced that the modernization of the Iskander-M system was underway.[^cite_note-полностью-17] A number of countries were reported to have shown interest in purchasing the export version of Iskander, but such possibility was only announced in early February 2017.[^cite_note-expert.ru-15]

The United States has argued that the [9M728/9M729](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9M729) (SSC-X-7/SSC-X-8) cruise missiles used by Iskander-K violates the [INF Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate-Range_Nuclear_Forces_Treaty) because their estimated range is beyond 500 kilometres (270 nmi; 310 mi).[^cite_note-18]

The general director of the company-developer of the system said in April 2024 that the precision of the missile has been improved since the start of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-19]

## Design



Iskander-M launched in 2018.

The Iskander-M system is equipped with two [solid-propellant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-propellant) single-stage [guided missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_missile), model **9M723K1**. The missiles are controlled throughout the entire flight path and fitted with an inseparable [warhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhead).&#x20;

The missiles can reportedly be re-targeted during flight in the case of engaging mobile targets.[^cite_note-Isk-10] Another unique feature of Iskander-M [^cite_note-20] is the optically guided warhead, which can also be controlled by encrypted radio transmission, including such as those from [AWACS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_early_warning_and_control) or [UAV](58900.md).&#x20;

During boost phase, thrust vector control (TVC) is accomplished by graphite vanes similar in layout to the [V-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-2_rocket) and Scud series tactical ballistic missiles. The missile is reportedly controlled during the whole flight with [gas-dynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust_vectoring) and aerodynamic control surfaces, using small fins to reduce its radar signature.[^cite_note-rbase.new-factoria.ru-21] At least some versions are equipped with [decoys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decoy) in order to aid in [penetrating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penetration_aid) missile defense systems.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-Ismay-23]

Iskander is a tactical missile system designed to be used in [theater level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_ballistic_missile) conflicts.[^cite_note-24]

In 2007, a new missile for the system (and launcher) was test fired, the [R-500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-500_\(missile\)) cruise missile,[^cite_note-25] with a range of applications up to 2000 km or more.[^cite_note-rnns.ru-26] In 2018, the Iskander missile reportedly gained the capability to strike static sea targets.[^cite_note-27]

In 2020 it was said that the [MAZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk_Automobile_Plant) was the primary supplier for the chassis of the launchers for the [Iskander-M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskander-M) because the domestic Russian products are of comparatively low quality.[^cite_note-efrusi-28]

The system can be transported by various vehicles, including [airplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kh-47M2_Kinzhal).[^cite_note-dokwar.ru-29]

When nuclear armed, the warhead is estimated to have a yield of 5 to 50 kilotonnes of TNT (21 to 209 TJ) (Iskander-M).[^cite_note-vpk.name-30]

#### Iskander-E



&#x20;Iskander-E of the [Armenian army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_army) during the military parade in [Yerevan](34352.md)

"E" for *Eksport*. The director of the state corporation Rostec [Sergey Chemezov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Chemezov) commented that the Iskander missile complex is a serious offensive weapon capable of carrying a nuclear warhead.

In 2016, Armenia, a Russian ally and a member of the [Collective Security Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Security_Treaty_Organization) (CSTO) became the first foreign country to operate the system. Iskander-E has a maximum range up to 280 km, to comply with [Missile Technology Control Regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_Technology_Control_Regime) restrictions for export,[^cite_note-38north-20190508-35] and is fitted with a simplified inertial guidance system. It flies on a flattened trajectory under 50 km altitude, allowing aerodynamic steering using tail fins, permitting a less predictable flight path and accurate delivery.[^cite_note-38north-20190508-35] The system can also use missiles carrying warheads with [cluster munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluster_munitions).[^cite_note-isrbm-36]

## Operational history

### Russia



A camouflaged Iskander-M launcher during an exercise in Transbaikalia. June 2021



A Russian missile rocket booster stage lies in the bedroom of a home in [Gori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gori,_Georgia) (2008)

The first documented use of the Iskander was in the [Russo-Georgian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Georgian_War) in which Dutch journalist [Stan Storimans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Storimans) was killed on 12 August 2008 in [Gori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gori,_Georgia). An investigation by the [Dutch government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_government) revealed that a single, 5 mm fragment from an anti-personnel sub-munition, carried by an Iskander missile, killed the Dutch journalist.[^cite_note-37]

In September 2009, the Russian military announced plans to deploy Iskander missiles in all the [military districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_district#Russian_Federation) of Russia "in a short time".[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

According to the [Stratfor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratfor) report in 2010 there were five Iskander brigades stationed and operational in Russia, namely the [26th Rocket Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26th_Rocket_Brigade) in the town of [Luga, Leningrad Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luga,_Leningrad_Oblast), south of [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg); [92nd Rocket Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/92nd_Rocket_Brigade?action=edit\&redlink=1) at Kamenka, near [Penza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penza) in the [Volga region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_region); [103rd Rocket Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/103rd_Rocket_Brigade) at [Ulan-Ude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulan-Ude), north of Mongolia; [107th Rocket Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/107th_Rocket_Brigade_\(Russia\)) at [Semistochni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semistochni?action=edit\&redlink=1), in the Far East;[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

In June 2013, it was revealed that Russia had deployed several Iskander-M ballistic missile systems in [Armenia](10918072.md) at undisclosed locations.[^cite_note-Harutyunyan-42] In 2016, it was reported by media that Armenia had received a *[divizion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divizion)* of Iskander missiles.[^cite_note-43]

In November 2014, US General Breedlove stated that Russian forces "capable of being nuclear" had been moved into Crimea,[^cite_note-44] the Ukrainian peninsula which the Russian Federation [had annexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) in March, and the following month Ukrainian Armed Forces announced that Russia had deployed a nuclear-capable Iskander division in the territory.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] Russian Foreign Ministry officials declared the right to deploy nuclear weapons in the peninsula, which is generally [recognized as part of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_68/262), in December 2014[^cite_note-47] and June 2015.[^cite_note-48]

In March 2016, at least one Iskander system was reportedly deployed at Russia's [Hmeimim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmeimim_\(air_base\)) airbase in [Syria](7515849.md).[^cite_note-49] In January 2017 an Israeli company claimed satellite photography confirmed the Syrian deployment.[^cite_note-50]

According to a Fox News report in early February 2017, four Iskander missiles had been fired at opposition targets in the [Idlib province](7445043.md) in Syria.[^cite_note-51]

#### Russo-Ukrainian war

During the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), Russia launched several Iskander missiles over their border into Ukraine as part of their assault.[^cite_note-52] These missiles demonstrated a previously unknown capability that employed decoys to confuse air defense systems. It is believed this technology was kept a closely guarded secret, and not included on Iskander missiles exported outside of Russia.[^cite_note-Ismay-23] From 23 April 2022, Russia deployed more units equipped with Iskander-M to the [Belgorod Oblast](478857.md), as close as 60 km from the border of Ukraine.[^cite_note-53]

In March 2023, [Ukrainian Air Force](1135769.md) spokesman Yuri Ihnat reported that Ukraine was unable to shoot down Iskander ballistic missiles. However, on 29 May, Ukraine claimed to have destroyed 11 Iskander missiles of both the ballistic and cruise missile variants.[^cite_note-54]

On 5 March 2024, it was confirmed that a [M142 HIMARS](147011.md) was destroyed by an Iskander ballistic missile near Nykanorivka in eastern Ukraine's Donetsk Oblast. It was the first time a HIMARS system was destroyed in Ukraine.[^cite_note-55]

On 9 March 2024, it was confirmed that an Iskander ballistic missile was used to destroy two German-supplied M901 launchers for the [MIM-104](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIM-104) Patriot air defence system near [Pokrovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokrovsk,_Ukraine). It was the first time components of a Patriot were destroyed in Ukraine.[^cite_note-56]

On 14 March 2024, an Iskander strike destroyed two Ukrainian Mi-8/17 helicopters from the 12th Army Aviation Brigade that were rearming in an open field in Novopavlivka, next to Avdiivka.[^cite_note-57]

In early July 2024, Russian forces launched a wave of attacks using Iskander missiles at Ukrainian airbases, after first using drones for aerial reconnaissance:&#x20;

- on 1 July, an attack on [Myrhorod Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrhorod_Air_Base) resulted in the destruction of at least two [Sukhoi Su-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-27) fighter jets

  [^cite_note-58]

- on 2 July, an attack on [Poltava Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Air_Base) resulted in a [Mi-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi-24) gunship helicopter being severely damaged

  [^cite_note-59]

- on 3 July, an attack on [Krivoi Rog Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krivoi_Rog_Air_Base) resulted in the destruction of a [Mikoyan MiG-29](185653.md) fighter jet and a [decoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_dummy) [Sukhoi Su-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-25) attack jet

  [^cite_note-60]

On 22 November 2024, another MiG-29 was destroyed in Aviatorskoe-Dnipro air base by an Iskander missiles attack guided by a Russian drone.[^cite_note-61]

On 23 February 2025, the Russian Ministry of Defense claimed that it has hit over 1,400 targets with the Iskander system during the war.[^cite_note-62]

On 1 March 2025, Russian forces attacked a Ukrainian military [training site in Cherkaske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherkaske_missile_attack), leaving scores of Ukrainian soldiers killed and wounded.[^cite_note-63]

On 5 June 2025, Ukrainian military claimed that their missiles struck a base in the city of [Klintsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klintsy) Bryansk Oblast, destroying one Iskander missile launcher and damaged two more.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

According to a report by the US [Defense Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Intelligence_Agency), Russia upgraded 9K720 Iskander and [Kh-47M2 Kinzhal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kh-47M2_Kinzhal) missiles with a terminal phase maneuvering capability in spring 2025 in order to bypass Ukraine's Patriot systems.[^cite_note-66] A *[Financial Times](136566.md)* article from October 2025 said, citing current and former Ukrainian and Western officials, that interception rates dropped from 37% in August to 6% in September, allowing Russia to seriously damage key military sites, four drone plants, and critical infrastructure ahead of the winter.[^cite_note-67]

According to the French Army, Ukrainian [SAMP/T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAMP/T) air defence systems have outperformed [Patriot missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriot_missiles) in intercepting Iskander missile, after Russia modified the flight profiles of Iskanders.[^cite_note-68]

On 14 March 2026, Ukrainian drones destroyed an [Iskander ballistic missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskander_ballistic_missile) launcher close to the village of Vyshneve in Crimea. [^cite_note-69]

#### Kaliningrad region

In November 2008, the Russian president [Dmitry Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev) in his first [annual address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Address_to_the_Federal_Assembly_\(Russia\)) to the [Federal Assembly of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Assembly_of_Russia) announced plans to deploy Iskander missiles to the [Kaliningrad Oblast](17311.md), Russia's westernmost territory on the south-eastern coast of the [Baltic Sea](3335.md), if the U.S. went ahead with its [European Ballistic Missile Defense System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_missile_defence_system).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] On 17 September 2009, [US president](24113.md) [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) announced the cancellation of the U.S. missile defense project in Poland and the Czech Republic.[^cite_note-72] The following day, Moscow indicated it might in turn cancel the plans to deploy Iskander missiles to Kaliningrad;[^cite_note-73] a few days later, the decision not to deploy was confirmed by Medvedev.[^cite_note-74]

According to Russian unofficial media reports, Russia deployed Iskander missiles to the Kaliningrad Oblast as part of military exercises in March 2015.[^cite_note-75]

On 8 October 2016, the Russian military confirmed that they had moved Iskander-M missiles into the Kaliningrad Oblast, adding the move was part of routine drills and had happened previously multiple times and would happen in future.[^cite_note-76]

In early February 2018, Shamanov confirmed that Russia had deployed an unidentified number of Iskander missiles to the Kaliningrad region.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] Days prior, the local military commanders said that the "park zones" for Iskander missiles deployment had been completed in the Kaliningrad region, as well as in [North Ossetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Ossetia-Alania).[^cite_note-79]

### Armenia

Armenia reportedly used its Iskander missiles against Azerbaijani forces during the [2020 Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_War). According to unconfirmed claims by ex-president of Armenia [Serzh Sargsyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serzh_Sargsyan), the missiles were fired on the town of [Shushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shusha) after its [capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Shusha_\(2020\)) by Azerbaijani forces in the last days of the war.[^cite_note-80] Responding to these claims, Prime Minister [Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md) neither confirmed nor denied the claim that an Iskander was fired on Shushi, but implied that the missiles that were launched did not explode or only exploded "by 10 percent." The Armenian prime minister's claims were rejected by a number of Russian lawmakers and military experts as well as by ex-defense minister of Armenia [Seyran Ohanyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seyran_Ohanyan) (under whom the missiles were acquired by Armenia).[^cite_note-\:0-81][^cite_note-82] The Russian [Ministry of Defence](7875884.md) released a statement claiming that the Iskander missiles were not used at all during the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh War. Another claim was made by an anonymous [Azerbaijani](746.md) official that Armenian forces fired an Iskander missile at Azerbaijan's capital [Baku](4566.md) in the last days of the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh War, but it was shot down by an Israeli-made [Barak 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barak_8).[^cite_note-83] On 15 March, employees of the Azerbaijan National Agency for Mine Action, who were demining and clearing the territories from mines and shells in Shushi, discovered the wreckage of an Iskander-M missile with identification number 9M723.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

### Intended targets

The system is intended to use [conventional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conventional_weapon) [warheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhead) for the engagement of point and area targets, including:[^cite_note-118]

- hostile fire weapons (missile systems, multiple launch rocket systems, long-range artillery pieces)

- air and missile defense weapons, aerodrome

- fixed- and rotary-wing aircraft at airfields

- command posts and communications nodes

- troops in concentration areas

- critical civilian infrastructure facilities

It is also capable of striking strongly protected targets, such as bunkers or hardened aircraft shelters[^cite_note-rbase.new-factoria.ru-21]
