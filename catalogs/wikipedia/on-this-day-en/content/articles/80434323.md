The **2026 Wimbledon Championships** was the 139th edition of the [Wimbledon Championships](91195.md), a [major tennis tournament](197638.md), which took place at the [All England Lawn Tennis and Croquet Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_England_Lawn_Tennis_and_Croquet_Club) in [Wimbledon, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wimbledon,_London), England, from 29 June to 12 July 2026, with the qualifying rounds taking place between 22 and 25 June 2026.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-\:0-2]

For the first time in Wimbledon championships history, matches had video reviews. Initially, players would only be allowed to challenge certain calls made by the [chair umpires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_\(tennis\)) on matches at the [Centre Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_Court) and [No. 1 Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._1_Court) and on the other four show courts when they were hosting singles matches ([No. 2 Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._2_Court), [No. 3 Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._3_Court_\(Wimbledon\)), No. 12 Court and No. 18 Court).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

The Gentlemen's singles event began on 29 June with the first of seven total rounds. Thirty-two players were seeded.[^cite_note-GSingles-5] Of those seeded players, ten were defeated in the first round, notably No. 4 [Ben Shelton](71310148.md),[^cite_note-7] No. 11 [Casper Ruud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casper_Ruud),[^cite_note-8] and No. 12 [Andrey Rublev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Rublev).[^cite_note-9] With his first-round victory, [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) continued a 21-year-long streak of having never lost in the opening round of Wimbledon, and became the first player in the Open Era to win more than 80 opening-round matches at the majors.[^cite_note-10] [Jakub Menšík](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakub_Men%C5%A1%C3%ADk), [Learner Tien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learner_Tien), and [Arthur Fils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Fils) were the highest of the five seeded players to exit in the second round,[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] and a further seven seeded players were defeated in the third round, notably No. 8 [Daniil Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Medvedev),[^cite_note-14] No. 17 [Frances Tiafoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Tiafoe),[^cite_note-15] and No. 19 [Karen Khachanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Khachanov).[^cite_note-16] [Alex de Minaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_de_Minaur), [Alexander Bublik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Bublik), [Jiří Lehečka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Lehe%C4%8Dka) were the highest of the four seeded players who were defeated in the Round of 16.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Aged 36 years, 71 days, [Jan-Lennard Struff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Lennard_Struff) became the oldest first-time quarterfinalist at a major in the Open Era.[^cite_note-20] After his win in the fourth round, Djokovic surpassed [Roger Federer](262376.md)'s record of Wimbledon men's singles match wins, reaching 106.[^cite_note-21]

In the quarterfinals, defending champion [Jannik Sinner](60061043.md) defeated Struff in straight sets.[^cite_note-22] No. 7 Djokovic won his match against No. 3 [Félix Auger-Aliassime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix_Auger-Aliassime) in five sets, the longest quarterfinal in tournament history, at 5 hours and 15 minutes long.[^cite_note-23] At 39 years, 49 days old, Djokovic was the second-oldest Wimbledon semi-finalist in the Open Era, only behind [Ken Rosewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Rosewall) in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles) (aged 39 years, 243 days).[^cite_note-24] [Arthur Fery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Fery), a [wildcard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_card_\(tennis\)), defeated No. 9 [Flavio Cobolli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavio_Cobolli) in straight sets, becoming the second wildcard to reach the Wimbledon semi-finals in the Open Era, after [Goran Ivanišević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goran_Ivani%C5%A1evi%C4%87) in [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles),[^cite_note-25] and the first British male wildcard to do so at any major in the Open Era.[^cite_note-26] No. 2 [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md) advanced to the semi-finals after winning against No. 6 [Taylor Fritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Fritz).[^cite_note-27] The first semi-final was played between second seed Zverev and wildcard Fery. Zverev won the match in four sets and reached his first Wimbledon final. Zverev was the first man born in the 1990s to reach the final of all four majors, and the first German man to contest the final since [Boris Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Becker) in [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men's_singles).[^cite_note-28] In the second semi-final, Sinner defeated Djokovic in straight sets.[^cite_note-29]

In the final match, Zverev won the first set in a tie-breaker, but Sinner would win the following three sets to win his second consecutive Wimbledon title and fifth major title overall.[^cite_note-30]

### Ladies' singles

Main article: [2026 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles](83354904.md)

An extreme wide shot of the match between Elena Rybakina and Elise Mertens at Wimbledon's No. 1 Court

[Elena Rybakina](55541443.md) and [Elise Mertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elise_Mertens) at the 2026 Wimbledon Championships

- [Czech Republic](5321.md) **[Linda Nosková](67929086.md)** defeated [Czech Republic](5321.md) [Karolína Muchová](55266410.md) 6–2, 5–7, 6–3

The Ladies' singles event began on 29 June with the first of seven total rounds. Thirty-two players were seeded.[^cite_note-LSingles-6] This tournament marked the singles return of former world No. 1 and 23-time major champion [Serena Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams), who last played at the [2022 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-31] She lost in the first round to [Maya Joint](75466424.md).[^cite_note-32] [Maja Chwalińska](53209114.md) was the third player in the Open Era to be seeded at a major while also being entered as a wildcard, following [Martina Hingis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Hingis) at the [2002 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) and [Patty Schnyder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patty_Schnyder) at the [2004 Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-33] [Lina Gjorcheska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lina_Gjorcheska) became the first player from [North Macedonia](23564616.md) to compete in a major main draw.[^cite_note-34] Seven seeded players lost in the first round, most notably the No. 8 seed [Elina Svitolina](27630639.md).[^cite_note-35] Three more seeded players fell in the second round, including No. 5 [Mirra Andreeva](71915009.md) and No. 15 [Diana Shnaider](68209798.md).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] Seven seeded players lost in the third round, most notable of them being defending champion [Iga Świątek](51838558.md), No. 2 [Elena Rybakina](55541443.md), and No. 6 [Amanda Anisimova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova).[^cite_note-EalaVsSwiatek-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] Świątek lost to [Alexandra Eala](62712906.md), who became the first Filipina player in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era) to reach the third and fourth rounds of a major.[^cite_note-EalaVsSwiatek-38] Six other seeded players lost their fourth round matches, including the singles' [world No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players), [Aryna Sabalenka](50222577.md).[^cite_note-41] Sabalenka's fourth-round defeat to [Naomi Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Osaka) marked the first time she failed to reach the quarterfinals of a major since the [2022 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), and her first straight-sets defeat at a major since the [2020 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-42]

In the quarterfinals, No. 10 [Karolína Muchová](55266410.md) defeated No. 14 Osaka in straight sets.[^cite_note-43] No. 12 [Marta Kostyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Kostyuk) won in straight sets against No. 13 [Jasmine Paolini](46663470.md).[^cite_note-44] No. 9 [Linda Nosková](67929086.md) was victorious in straight sets against No. 25 [Elise Mertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elise_Mertens).[^cite_note-45] No. 7 [Coco Gauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_Gauff) pulled an upset against No. 4 [Jessica Pegula](32944701.md) after fighting back and winning the match in three sets.[^cite_note-46] In the first semi-final, Muchová met Gauff. Muchová won the tie-breaker 12–10 in the third set to reach her first Wimbledon final.[^cite_note-47] In the second semi-final match, Nosková defeated Kostyuk in the straight sets to reach her first Wimbledon final.[^cite_note-48]

## The Wimbledon Court in Central Park

In June 2026, the All England Lawn Tennis and Croquet Club organised "**The Wimbledon Court in Central Park**", a four-day promotional event which was held at the [Wollman Rink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollman_Rink) in [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park), New York City, from 26 to 29 June. The event formed part of Wimbledon's annual outreach activities in the United States, following the "**The Hill in New York**" event, which has been staged annually since 2022.[^cite_note-wimbledon_central_park_2026-130]

For the first time, the venue was transformed into a temporary grass court constructed and maintained by Wimbledon's groundstaff. The centrepiece of the event was "The Wimbledon Court Invitational", an exhibition doubles match featuring former professional players and tennis personalities, which was held on 26 June.[^cite_note-wimbledon_central_park_2026-130]

On 27 and 28 June, members of the public were given the opportunity to play on the grass court through a ballot system. The event also featured tennis clinics, interactive activities, and Wimbledon-themed hospitality, including traditional strawberries and cream, Pimm's, and Champagne.[^cite_note-wimbledon_central_park_2026-130]

As part of Wimbledon's community engagement initiatives, a tennis session was held on 29 June for participants of the Barclays Net Work programme, a youth employment and skills initiative operating in the Bronx in partnership with Good Shepherd Services. The programme supports young people aged 15–24 who are unemployed, disconnected from education, or have experience with the justice system.[^cite_note-wimbledon_central_park_2026-130]

The event was concluded with a public screening of the opening day's play from the 2026 Wimbledon Championships, broadcast live on giant screens in [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park). According to the All England Club, the initiative is intended to provide American tennis fans with an opportunity to experience elements of Wimbledon's traditions and atmosphere outside the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-wimbledon_central_park_2026-130]
