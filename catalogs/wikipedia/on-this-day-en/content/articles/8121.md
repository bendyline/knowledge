The **Dallas Cowboys** are a professional [American football](18951490.md) team based in the [Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas%E2%80%93Fort_Worth_metroplex). The Cowboys compete in the [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL) as a member of the [National Football Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_Conference) (NFC) [East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFC_East) division. The team is headquartered in [Frisco, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frisco,_Texas), and has played its home games at [AT\&T Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Stadium) in [Arlington, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Texas), since its opening in [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_NFL_season). The stadium took its current name prior to the [2013 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_NFL_season), following the team's decision to sell the stadium's naming rights to telecommunications company [AT\&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-namechangenfl-8]

The Cowboys joined the NFL as an [expansion team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_team) in [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_NFL_season).[^cite_note-9] The team's national following might best be represented by its NFL record of consecutive sellouts. The Cowboys' streak of 190 consecutive sold out regular and postseason games (home and away) began in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Dallas_Cowboys_season).[^cite_note-10] The franchise has made it to the [Super Bowl](27718.md) eight times, tying it with the [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers), [Denver Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Broncos), and [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers) for second-most [Super Bowl appearances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Super_Bowl_champions) in history behind the [New England Patriots](21719.md)' record 12 appearances. Their eight [NFC championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFC_Championship_Game) are tied for most in the conference's history. The Cowboys are the only NFL team to record 20 straight winning seasons (from 1966 to 1985) during which they missed the playoffs only twice (1974 and 1984).[^cite_note-11]

In [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Dallas_Cowboys_season), the Dallas Cowboys became the first sports team to be valued at \$4 billion, making it the [most valuable sports team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes'_list_of_the_most_valuable_sports_teams) in the world, according to *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)*.[^cite_note-Forbes-12] In 2016, the Cowboys also became the most valuable sports team at \$4 billion, a record they have held since by also being the first sports team to reach a worth of \$10 billion dollars. As of late 2025, the Cowboys are valued at about \$13 billion. The Cowboys also generated \$620 million in revenue in 2014, a record for a U.S. sports team.[^cite_note-Forbes-12] In 2018, they also became the first NFL franchise to be valued at \$5 billion[^cite_note-13] and making Forbes' list as the most valued NFL team for the 12th straight year.[^cite_note-14]

## History

Main article: [History of the Dallas Cowboys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Dallas_Cowboys)

### Origins

Prior to the formation of the Dallas Cowboys, there had not been an NFL team south of Washington, D.C. since the [Dallas Texans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Texans_\(NFL\)) folded in 1952 after only one season. Two businessmen had tried and failed to get Dallas a team in the NFL: [Lamar Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamar_Hunt) responded by forming the [American Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Football_League) with a group of owners, which would spur the NFL to expand beyond twelve teams. Oilman [Clint Murchison Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Murchison,_Jr.) persisted with his intent to bring a team to Dallas, but [George Preston Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Preston_Marshall), owner of the [Washington Redskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Commanders), had a monopoly in the [South](179553.md) (after the addition of Dallas, the South would see three further teams - NFL teams in Atlanta and New Orleans, and an AFL team in Miami - added in the next six years).[^cite_note-l020-15]

Murchison had tried to purchase the Washington Redskins (now Commanders) from Marshall in 1958 with the intent of moving them to Dallas. An agreement was struck, but as the deal was about to be finalized, Marshall called for a change in terms, which infuriated Murchison, and he called off the deal.[^cite_note-16] Marshall then opposed any franchise for Murchison in Dallas. Since NFL expansion needed unanimous approval from team owners at that time, Marshall's position would prevent Murchison from joining the league.

Marshall had a falling out with the Redskins band leader Barnee Breeskin, who had written the music to the Redskins fight song "[Hail to the Redskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail_to_the_Redskins)", and Marshall's wife had penned the lyrics. Breeskin owned the rights to the song and was aware of Murchison's plight to get an NFL franchise. Angry with Marshall, Breeskin approached Murchison's attorney to sell him the rights to the song before the expansion vote in 1959: Murchison subsequently purchased "Hail to the Redskins" for \$2,500.[^cite_note-Snider-17]

Before the vote to award franchises in 1959, Murchison revealed to Marshall that he now owned the song, and barred Marshall from playing it during games. After Marshall launched an expletive-laced tirade, Murchison sold the rights to "Hail to the Redskins" back to Marshall in exchange for his vote, the lone one against Murchison getting a franchise at that time, and a [rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboys%E2%80%93Redskins_rivalry) was born.[^cite_note-Snider-17] Murchison hired [CBS Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Sports) executive and former [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams) general manager [Tex Schramm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tex_Schramm) as team president and general manager,[^cite_note-18] [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers) scout [Gil Brandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gil_Brandt) as head of player personnel,[^cite_note-a557-19] and [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants) defensive coordinator [Tom Landry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Landry) as head coach,[^cite_note-20] thus forming a triumvirate that would lead the Cowboys' football operations for three decades.

### Tom Landry years (1960–1988)

See also: [Tom Landry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Landry)



[Tom Landry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Landry), pictured here in 1997, coached the team from 1960 to 1988 and led the Cowboys to five [Super Bowl](27718.md) appearances and two Super Bowl victories in [1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Dallas_Cowboys_season) and [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Dallas_Cowboys_season)



[Don Meredith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Meredith) was the first [franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franchise_player) quarterback of the Cowboys. [NFL Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Films) cited Meredith as the first "star" of the franchise,

[^cite_note-21]

&#x20;leading them to back-to-back [NFL Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFC_Championship_Game) appearances during the [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_NFL_season) and [1967 seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_NFL_season), both times falling one game shy of the [Super Bowl](27718.md)

Like most expansion teams, the Cowboys struggled at first. They failed to win a game in their inaugural season.[^cite_note-22] However, Landry slowly brought the team to respectability. In 1965, they finally got to .500. They broke all the way through a year later, winning consecutive Eastern Conference titles in 1966 and 1967. However, they lost the [NFL Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Championship_Game) each time to the [Green Bay Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers) with the second loss coming in the [1967 Ice Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_NFL_Championship_Game).[^cite_note-23] They would win consecutive division titles in 1968 and 1969 when the NFL adopted a divisional format, but were defeated in the playoffs both years by the [Cleveland Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Browns).

From [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_NFL_season) through [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_NFL_season), the Cowboys won 105 regular season games, more than any other NFL franchise during that time span.[^cite_note-24] In addition, they appeared in five [Super Bowls](27718.md), winning two ([1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_NFL_season) and [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_NFL_season)).

Led by quarterback [Craig Morton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Morton), the [Cowboys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Dallas_Cowboys_season) had a 10–4 season in [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_NFL_season). They defeated Detroit 5–0 in the lowest-scoring playoff game in NFL history[^cite_note-25] and then defeated San Francisco 17–10 in the first-ever [NFC Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFC_Championship_Game) to qualify for their first Super Bowl appearance in franchise history, a mistake-filled [Super Bowl V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_V), where they lost 16–13 to the [Baltimore Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Baltimore_Colts_season) courtesy of a [field goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_goal_\(football\)) by Colts' kicker Jim O'Brien with five seconds remaining in the contest. Despite the loss, linebacker [Chuck Howley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Howley) was named the [Super Bowl MVP](291253.md), the first and only time in Super Bowl history that the game's MVP did not come from the winning team.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

#### Super Bowl VI champions (1971)

See also: [Super Bowl VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_VI)



The Cowboys playing the [Miami Dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Dolphins) in [Super Bowl VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_VI) in [1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_NFL_playoffs)

The Cowboys moved from the [Cotton Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_Bowl_\(stadium\)) to [Texas Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Stadium) in week six of the [1971 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_NFL_season). Landry named [Roger Staubach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Staubach) as the permanent starting quarterback to start the second half of the season, and Dallas was off and running. The Cowboys won their last seven regular season games (finishing 11–3) before dispatching the [Minnesota Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Minnesota_Vikings_season) and [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_San_Francisco_49ers_season) in the playoffs to return to the Super Bowl. In [Super Bowl VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_VI), behind an MVP performance from Staubach and a then Super Bowl record 252 yards rushing, the Cowboys crushed the upstart [Miami Dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Miami_Dolphins_season), 24–3, to finally bury the "Next Year's Champions" stigma.[^cite_note-28]

After missing the playoffs in 1974, the [team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Dallas_Cowboys_season) drafted well the [following year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_NFL_season), adding defensive lineman [Randy White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_White_\(American_football\)) (a future Hall of Fame member) and linebacker [Thomas "Hollywood" Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_%22Hollywood%22_Henderson). The fresh influx of talent helped the Cowboys back to the playoffs in [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975%E2%80%9376_NFL_playoffs) as a wild card, losing to the [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Pittsburgh_Steelers_season), 21–17, in [Super Bowl X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_X).[^cite_note-29]

#### Super Bowl XII champions (1977)

See also: [Super Bowl XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XII)



The Cowboys playing against the [Denver Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Broncos) in [Super Bowl XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XII) in [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977-78_NFL_playoffs)

Dallas began the [1977 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_NFL_season) 8–0, finishing 12–2. In the [postseason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977%E2%80%9378_NFL_playoffs), the Cowboys routed the [Chicago Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Chicago_Bears_season) 37–7 and [Minnesota Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Minnesota_Vikings_season) 23–6 before defeating the [Denver Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Denver_Broncos_season) 27–10 in [Super Bowl XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XII) in New Orleans.[^cite_note-30] As a testament to [Doomsday's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doomsday_Defense) dominance in the hard-hitting game, defensive linemen Randy White and [Harvey Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Martin) were named co-Super Bowl MVPs, the first and only time multiple players have received the award. Dallas returned to the [Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XIII) following the [1978 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_NFL_season), losing to Pittsburgh 35–31. Bob Ryan, an NFL Films editor, dubbed the Cowboys "[America's Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Team)" following the Super Bowl loss, a nickname that has earned derision from non-Cowboys fans but has stuck through both good times and bad.[^cite_note-31] [Danny White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_White) became the Cowboys' starting quarterback in 1980 after quarterback [Roger Staubach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Staubach) retired. Despite going 12–4 in [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Dallas_Cowboys_season), the Cowboys came into the playoffs as a wild card team. In the opening round of the [1980–81 NFL playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980%E2%80%9381_NFL_playoffs), they avenged their elimination from the prior year's playoffs by defeating the Rams. In the Divisional Round, they squeaked by the [Atlanta Falcons](2103.md) 30–27. For the NFC Championship, they were pitted against division rival [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles), the team that won the division during the regular season. The Eagles captured their first conference championship and Super Bowl berth by winning 20–7.

[1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_Dallas_Cowboys_season) brought another division championship for the Cowboys. They entered the [1981–82 NFL playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_NFL_playoffs) as the number 2 seed. Their first postseason saw them blow out Tampa Bay in a 38–0 shutout. The Cowboys then advanced to the [NFC Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFC_Championship_Game) against the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_San_Francisco_49ers_season), the number 1 seed. Despite having a late 4th quarter 27–21 lead, they would lose to the 49ers 28–27. 49ers quarterback [Joe Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Montana) led his team on an 89-yard game-winning touchdown drive, connecting with [Dwight Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_Clark) in a play known as [The Catch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Catch_\(American_football\)).[^cite_note-32]

The [1982 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Dallas_Cowboys_season) was shortened after a player strike. With a 6–3 record, Dallas made it to the playoffs for the 8th consecutive season. As the number 2 seed for the [1982–83 NFL playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_NFL_playoffs), they eliminated the Buccaneers 30–17 in the Wild Card round and dispatched the Packers 37–26 in the Divisional round to advance to their 3rd consecutive Conference championship game. However, the third time was not the charm for the Cowboys as they fell 31–17 to their division rival and eventual [Super Bowl XVII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XVII) champions, the [Washington Redskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Washington_Redskins_season).

Although it was not apparent at the time, the loss in the 1982 NFC title game marked the end of an era. For the [1983 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Dallas_Cowboys_season), the Cowboys went 12–4 and made it once again to the playoffs, but were upset at home in the Wild Card by the Rams 24–17. However, 1983 was a missed opportunity as prior to their playoff defeat, the Cowboys had a chance to clinch the NFC East and home-field advantage throughout the playoffs against Washington in the penultimate week of the regular season, but were defeated soundly 31–10 at home, and conceded control of the division to the Redskins in which they would not relinquish a week later. Prior to the [1984 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Dallas_Cowboys_season), Murchison sold the Cowboys to another Texas oil magnate, [H.R. "Bum" Bright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bum_Bright) and his ten partners.[^cite_note-33] Dallas posted a 9–7 record that season but missed the playoffs for the first time in 10 seasons and only the second time in 18 years. After going 10–6 in [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Dallas_Cowboys_season) and winning a division title, the Cowboys were shut out 20–0 by the Rams in the Divisional round in Los Angeles.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

Hard times came for the organization as they went 7–9 in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Dallas_Cowboys_season), 7–8 in [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Dallas_Cowboys_season), and 3–13 in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Dallas_Cowboys_season).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] During this time period, Bright became disenchanted with the team. During an embarrassing home loss to Atlanta in 1987, Bright told the media that he was "horrified" at Landry's play calling. During the [savings and loan crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savings_and_Loan_crisis), Bright's savings and loan was taken over by the [FSLIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Savings_and_Loan_Insurance_Corporation). With most of the rest of his money tied up in the Cowboys, Bright was forced to sell the team to [Jerry Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Jones) on February 25, 1989, for \$150 million.[^cite_note-39]

### Jerry Jones era (1989–present)

#### Jimmy Johnson years (1989–1993)

See also: [Jimmy Johnson (American football coach)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Johnson_\(American_football_coach\))



The five-time world champions mural

Jones immediately fired Tom Landry, the only head coach in franchise history, replacing him with [University of Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Miami) head coach [Jimmy Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Johnson_\(American_football_coach\)), who was also Jones' teammate at the [University of Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Arkansas) as a fellow defensive lineman.[^cite_note-40] The hiring of Johnson also reunited Johnson with second-year wide receiver [Michael Irvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Irvin), who had played collegiately at Miami. With the first pick in the draft, the Cowboys selected [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA) quarterback [Troy Aikman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_Aikman). Later that same year, they would [trade veteran running back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herschel_Walker_trade) [Herschel Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herschel_Walker) to the [Minnesota Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Vikings) for five veteran players and eight draft choices. Although the Cowboys finished the [1989 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Dallas_Cowboys_season) with a 1–15 record, their worst in almost 30 years, "The Trade" later allowed Dallas to draft a number of impact players to rebuild the team.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

Johnson quickly returned the Cowboys to the NFL's elite. Skillful drafts added fullback [Daryl Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daryl_Johnston) and center [Mark Stepnoski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Stepnoski) in 1989, running back [Emmitt Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmitt_Smith) in 1990, defensive tackle [Russell Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Maryland) and offensive tackle [Erik Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Williams) in 1991, and safety [Darren Woodson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Woodson) in 1992. The young talent joined holdovers from the Landry era such as wide receiver Michael Irvin, guard [Nate Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nate_Newton_\(American_football\)), middle linebacker [Ken Norton Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Norton_Jr.), and offensive tackle [Mark Tuinei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Tuinei), defensive tackle [Jim Jeffcoat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Jeffcoat), and veteran pickups such as tight end [Jay Novacek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Novacek) and defensive end [Charles Haley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Haley).

Things started to look up for the franchise in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Dallas_Cowboys_season). On Week 1 Dallas won their first home game since September 1988 when they defeated the [San Diego Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Chargers) 17–14. They went 2–7 in their next 9 games but won 4 of their last 6 games to finish the season with a 4th place 7–9 record.[^cite_note-43]

Coming into [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Dallas_Cowboys_season) the Cowboys replaced offensive coordinator [Dave Shula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Shula) with [Norv Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norv_Turner); the Cowboys raced to a 6–5 start, then defeated the previously unbeaten [Redskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Washington_Redskins_season) despite injury to Troy Aikman. Backup [Steve Beuerlein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Beuerlein) took over and the Cowboys finished 11–5. In the Wild Card round they defeated the [Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Chicago_Bears_season) 17–13 for the Cowboys' first playoff win since 1982. In the Divisional round their season ended in a 38–6 playoff rout by the [Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Detroit_Lions_season).[^cite_note-44]

#### Super Bowl XXVII champions (1992)

See also: [Super Bowl XXVII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVII)

In [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Dallas_Cowboys_season) Dallas set a team record for regular-season wins with a 13–3 mark.[^cite_note-45] They started off the season by defeating the defending Super Bowl champion Redskins 23–10.[^cite_note-46] Going into the playoffs as the number 2 seed they had a first round bye before facing division rival the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles).[^cite_note-47] The Cowboys won that game 34–10 to advance to the NFC Conference Championship game for the first time in 10 years.[^cite_note-48] They were pitted against the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers), the top seed. On January 17, 1993, the Cowboys went to [Candlestick Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candlestick_Park) and defeated the 49ers 30–20 to clinch their first Super Bowl berth since 1978.[^cite_note-49] Dallas defeated the [Buffalo Bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bills) 52–17 in [Super Bowl XXVII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVII), during which they forced a record nine turnovers.[^cite_note-50] Johnson became the first coach to claim a national championship in college football and a Super Bowl victory in professional football.[^cite_note-51]

#### Super Bowl XXVIII champions (1993)

See also: [Super Bowl XXVIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVIII)



Dallas Cowboys championship banners inside [AT\&T Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Stadium)

Despite starting the [1993 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Dallas_Cowboys_season) 0–2, Dallas finished the regular season 12–4 as the number 1 seed of the NFC.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] Dallas sent a then-NFL record 11 players to the Pro Bowl in 1993: Aikman, safety [Thomas Everett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Everett), Irvin, Johnston, Maryland, Newton, Norton, Novacek, Smith, Stepnoski, and Williams.[^cite_note-54] They defeated the Green Bay Packers 27–17 in the divisional round.[^cite_note-55] In the NFC Conference Championship, Dallas beat the 49ers in Dallas, 38–21.[^cite_note-56] The Cowboys again defeated the Buffalo Bills in [Super Bowl XXVIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXVIII), 30–13 (becoming the first team in NFL history to win a Super Bowl after starting 0–2).[^cite_note-57]

#### Barry Switzer years (1994–1997)

Only weeks after Super Bowl XXVIII, however, friction between Johnson and Jones culminated in Johnson stunning the football world by announcing his resignation.[^cite_note-58] Jones then hired former [University of Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oklahoma) head coach [Barry Switzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Switzer) to replace Johnson.[^cite_note-u140-59] The Cowboys finished 12–4 in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Dallas_Cowboys_season).[^cite_note-i887-60] They once again clinched a first-round bye and defeated Green Bay 35–9 in the Divisional Round.[^cite_note-61] They missed the Super Bowl, however, after losing to the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers) in the NFC Championship Game, 38–28.[^cite_note-62]

#### Super Bowl XXX champions (1995)

See also: [Super Bowl XXX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXX)

Prior to the start of [1995 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Dallas_Cowboys_season) Jerry Jones lured All-Pro cornerback [Deion Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deion_Sanders) away from San Francisco.[^cite_note-63] Dallas started the season 4–0 including shutting out their division rival New York Giants 35–0 at [Giants Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giants_Stadium) to open their season. Emmitt Smith set an NFL record with 25 rushing touchdowns that season.[^cite_note-64] They ended the season 12–4 and went into the playoffs as the number 1 seed.[^cite_note-65] In the Divisional round, they dispatched their division rival Eagles 30–11 to advance to their fourth consecutive NFC Conference Championship Game, in which they defeated Green Bay, 38–27.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] In [Super Bowl XXX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXX) the Cowboys defeated the [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers) 27–17 at [Sun Devil Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Devil_Stadium) for their fifth Super Bowl championship, tied with the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers) for the most by any NFC team.[^cite_note-68] Switzer joined Johnson as the only coaches to win a college football national championship and a Super Bowl.[^cite_note-69]



The trio of [Troy Aikman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_Aikman), [Emmitt Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmitt_Smith), and [Michael Irvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Irvin) (known as "The Triplets") led the Cowboys to three Super Bowl wins

The glory days of the Cowboys were again beginning to dim as free agency, age, and injuries began taking their toll. Star receiver Michael Irvin was suspended by the league for the first five games of [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Dallas_Cowboys_season) following a drug-related arrest; he came back after the Cowboys started the season 2–3.[^cite_note-70] They finished the regular season with a 10–6 record, won the [NFC East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFC_East) title, and entered the playoffs as the number 3 seed in the NFC.[^cite_note-71] They defeated Minnesota 40–15 in the Wild Card round but were eliminated in the Divisional Round of the playoffs 26–17 by the [Carolina Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Carolina_Panthers_season).[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

The Cowboys went 6–10 in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Dallas_Cowboys_season), losing the last six consecutive games of the season, with discipline and off-field problems becoming major distractions.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] As a result, Switzer resigned as head coach in January 1998 and former Steelers offensive coordinator [Chan Gailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Gailey) was hired to take his place.[^cite_note-76]

#### Gailey and Campo years (1998–2002)

See also: [Chan Gailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Gailey) and [Dave Campo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Campo)

Gailey led the team to two playoff appearances with a 10–6 record in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Dallas_Cowboys_season) and an [NFC East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFC_East) championship, the Cowboys' sixth in seven years, but the Cowboys were upset at home in the Wild Card Round of the playoffs by the [Arizona Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Arizona_Cardinals_season) 20–7.

In [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Dallas_Cowboys_season) Dallas went 8–8 in a season that featured Irvin suffering a career-ending [cervical spine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervical_vertebrae) injury in a loss to the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Philadelphia_Eagles_season) at [Veterans Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterans_Stadium). The season ended in a 27–10 Wild Card playoff loss to the [Minnesota Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Minnesota_Vikings_season). Gailey was fired and became the first Cowboys coach who did not take the team to a [Super Bowl](27718.md).[^cite_note-77]

Defensive coordinator [Dave Campo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Campo) was promoted to head coach for the [2000 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Dallas_Cowboys_season).[^cite_note-78] Prior to the season starting cornerback [Deion Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deion_Sanders) was released after 5 seasons with the team.[^cite_note-79] He later signed with the division rival [Washington Redskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Washington_Redskins_season). In Week 1, they were blown out 41–14 by the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Philadelphia_Eagles_season). That game was very costly when veteran quarterback Troy Aikman suffered a serious concussion which ultimately ended his career. Longtime NFL quarterback [Randall Cunningham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randall_Cunningham) filled in for Aikman for the rest of the season at quarterback. The Cowboys finished the season in 4th place with a 5–11 record. The only highlights of 2000 were [Emmitt Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmitt_Smith) having his 10th consecutive 1,000-yard rushing season and a season sweep over the Redskins.[^cite_note-80]

[2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Dallas_Cowboys_season) was another hard year in Dallas. Prior to the season starting Aikman was released from the team and he retired due to the concussions he had received.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] Jerry Jones signed [Tony Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Banks_\(American_football\)) as a quarterback. Banks had been a starter for half of the season the previous year for the [Super Bowl XXXV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXV) champion [Baltimore Ravens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Baltimore_Ravens_season) before being benched. Jones also drafted quarterback [Quincy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quincy_Carter) in the second round of that year's draft, and Banks was released during the preseason. [Ryan Leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Leaf), [Anthony Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Wright_\(American_football\)), and [Clint Stoerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Stoerner) all competed for the quarterback position that season. Dallas again finished at 5–11, last place in the NFC East, but they swept the [Washington Redskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Washington_Redskins_season) for the 4th consecutive season.

Prior to the [2002 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Dallas_Cowboys_season) Dallas drafted safety [Roy Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Williams_\(safety\)) with the 8th overall pick. The season started out low as the Cowboys lost to the [expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_team) [Houston Texans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Houston_Texans_season) 19–10 in Week 1. By far the highlight of 2002 was on October 28, when during a home game against the [Seattle Seahawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Seattle_Seahawks_season), Emmitt Smith broke the all-time NFL rushing record previously held by [Walter Payton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Payton). Their [Thanksgiving Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_on_Thanksgiving_Day) win over the [Washington Redskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Washington_Redskins_season) was their 10th consecutive win against the Redskins. However, that was their final 2002 win as the team lost their next four games to finish with another last-place 5–11 record. The losing streak was punctuated with a Week 17 20–14 loss against the Redskins. That game was Smith's last game as a Cowboys player; he was released during the offseason.[^cite_note-83] Campo was immediately fired as head coach at the conclusion of the season.[^cite_note-84]

#### Bill Parcells years (2003–2006)

See also: [Bill Parcells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Parcells)

Jones then lured [Bill Parcells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Parcells) out of retirement to coach the Cowboys.[^cite_note-85] The Cowboys became the surprise team of the [2003 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Dallas_Cowboys_season) getting off to a hot 7–2 start, but went 3–4 for the rest of the season.[^cite_note-86] They were able to grab the second NFC wild-card spot with a 10–6 record but lost in the Wild Card round to eventual conference champion [Carolina Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Carolina_Panthers_season), 29–10.[^cite_note-87]

In [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Dallas_Cowboys_season) Dallas was unable to replicate their 2003 success and ended 6–10.[^cite_note-88] Quincy Carter was released during the preseason and was replaced at quarterback by [Vinny Testaverde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinny_Testaverde).[^cite_note-r966-89][^cite_note-90]

Dallas got off to a great 7–3 start for the [2005 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Dallas_Cowboys_season) but ended up only in 3rd place with a 9–7 record.[^cite_note-91] Prior to the beginning of that season, they signed veteran [Drew Bledsoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Bledsoe) as starting quarterback.[^cite_note-92]

[2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Dallas_Cowboys_season) was an interesting year for the Cowboys. Prior to the season, they signed free agent wide receiver [Terrell Owens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrell_Owens) who was talented yet controversial.[^cite_note-93] The Cowboys started the season 3–2. During a week 7 matchup against the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_New_York_Giants_season), Bledsoe, who had been struggling since the start of the season, was pulled from the game and was replaced by backup [Tony Romo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Romo).[^cite_note-94] Romo was unable to salvage that game and Dallas lost 36–22. However, Romo was named the starter for the team and went 5–1 in his first 6 games. Dallas ended the season with a 9–7 2nd-place finish.[^cite_note-95] They were able to clinch the number 5 playoff seed. They traveled to play the [Seattle Seahawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Seattle_Seahawks_season) where the Seahawks won 21–20.[^cite_note-96] After the season Parcells retired and was replaced by [Wade Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade_Phillips).[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

#### Wade Phillips years (2007–2010)

See also: [Wade Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade_Phillips)

Dallas started the [2007 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Dallas_Cowboys_season) with a bang, winning their first five games. They won 12 of their first 13 games, with their only loss during that span being to the [New England Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_New_England_Patriots_season), who went undefeated that season. Despite dropping two of their last three regular-season games, the Cowboys clinched their first number 1 NFC seed in 12 years, which also granted them a first-round bye and home-field advantage throughout the playoffs.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] They lost in the divisional round 21–17 to the eventual [Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLII) champion [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_New_York_Giants_season).[^cite_note-b752-101]

In the tumultuous [2008 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Dallas_Cowboys_season), the Cowboys started off strong, going 3–0 for the second straight year, en route to a 4–1 start. However, things soon went downhill from there, after quarterback [Tony Romo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Romo) suffered a broken pinkie in an overtime loss to the [Arizona Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Arizona_Cardinals_season). With [Brad Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Johnson_\(American_football\)) and [Brooks Bollinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooks_Bollinger) playing as backups, Dallas went 1–2 during a three-game stretch. Romo's return showed promise, as Dallas went 3–0. However, injuries mounted during the season, with the team losing several starters for the year, such as [Kyle Kosier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Kosier), [Felix Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Jones), safety [Roy Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Williams_\(safety\)), punter [Mat McBriar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mat_McBriar), and several other starters playing with injuries.[^cite_note-102] Entering December, the 8–4 Cowboys underperformed, finishing 1–3. They failed to make the playoffs after losing to the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Philadelphia_Eagles_season) in the final regular-season game which saw the Eagles reach the playoffs instead.[^cite_note-103]

On May 2, 2009, the Dallas Cowboys' practice facility collapsed during a wind storm.[^cite_note-104] The collapse left twelve Cowboys players and coaches injured. The most serious injuries were special teams coach [Joe DeCamillis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_DeCamillis), who suffered fractured cervical vertebrae and had surgery to stabilize fractured vertebrae in his neck, and Rich Behm, the team's 33-year-old scouting assistant, who was permanently paralyzed from the waist down after his spine was severed.[^cite_note-105]

The [2009 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Dallas_Cowboys_season) started positively with a road win against the [Tampa Bay Buccaneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Tampa_Bay_Buccaneers_season), but fortunes quickly changed as Dallas fell to a 2–2 start. In week five, with starting wide receiver Roy Williams sidelined by injury, receiver [Miles Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Austin) got his first start of the season and had a record-setting day (250 yards receiving and 2 touchdowns) to help lead Dallas to an overtime win over the [Kansas City Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Kansas_City_Chiefs_season).[^cite_note-106] Following their [bye week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bye_week), they went on a three-game winning streak including wins over the [Atlanta Falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Atlanta_Falcons_season) and NFC East division rival [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Philadelphia_Eagles_season). Despite entering December with a record of 8–3, they lost their slim grip on 1st place in the division with losses to the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_New_York_Giants_season) and [San Diego Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_San_Diego_Chargers_season). Talks of past December collapses resurfaced, and another collapse in 2009 seemed validated. However, the team surged in the final three weeks of the season with a 24–17 victory against the eventual [Super Bowl XLIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLIV) champion [New Orleans Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_New_Orleans_Saints_season) at the Louisiana Superdome, ending the Saints' previously unbeaten season in week 15. For the first time in franchise history, they posted back-to-back shutouts when they beat their division rivals, the [Washington Redskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Washington_Redskins_season) (17–0) and Philadelphia Eagles (24–0) to end the season. In the process, the Cowboys clinched their second NFC East title in three years as well as the third seed in the NFC Playoffs.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] Six days later, in the wild-card round of the playoffs, Dallas played the Eagles in a rematch of week 17. The Cowboys defeated the Eagles for the first Cowboys post-season win since the 1996 season, ending a streak of six consecutive NFL post-season losses.[^cite_note-109] However, their playoff run ended after being routed 34–3 in the Divisional Round against the [Minnesota Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Minnesota_Vikings_season).[^cite_note-110]

After beginning the [2010 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Dallas_Cowboys_season) at 1–7, Phillips was fired as head coach and was replaced by offensive coordinator [Jason Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Garrett) as the interim head coach.[^cite_note-111]

#### Jason Garrett years (2010–2019)

See also: [Jason Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Garrett)

With Garrett as interim head coach, the Cowboys finished the 2010 season 6–10 after beginning at 1–7.[^cite_note-112] With this improvement, the Cowboys signed Garrett as the head coach for the 2011 season.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]



[Tony Romo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Romo), pictured here in 2008, was starting quarterback for the Cowboys from 2006 to 2015

To start the [2011 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Dallas_Cowboys_season) the Cowboys played the [New York Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_New_York_Jets_season) on a Sunday night primetime game in New York, on September 11. The Cowboys held the lead through most of the game, until a fumble, blocked punt, and interception led to the Jets coming back to win the game. In week 2 the Cowboys traveled to San Francisco to play the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_San_Francisco_49ers_season). In the middle of the 2nd quarter, while the Cowboys trailed 10–7, Tony Romo suffered a rib injury and was replaced by [Jon Kitna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Kitna). Kitna threw 1 touchdown and 2 interceptions until Romo returned in the 3rd quarter as Dallas trailed 17–7. Romo then threw 3 touchdown passes to [Miles Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Austin) as the Cowboys rallied to send the game into overtime. On their opening possession after a 49ers punt, Romo found wide receiver [Jesse Holley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Holley) on a 78-yard pass, which set up the game-winning field goal by rookie kicker [Dan Bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Bailey_\(American_football\)).[^cite_note-m401-115] The Cowboys ended the season 8–8. They were in a position to win the NFC East but lost to the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_New_York_Giants_season) in a Week 17 primetime Sunday Night game on [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) which allowed the Giants to win the division.[^cite_note-116] The Giants would go on to win [Super Bowl XLVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLVI).[^cite_note-117]

The Cowboys started off the [2012 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Dallas_Cowboys_season) on a high note by defeating the defending Super Bowl champion [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_New_York_Giants_season) 24–17 on the opening night of the season.[^cite_note-118] They would hover around the .500 mark for the majority of the season. They lost a close Week 6 game to eventual [Super Bowl XLVII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLVII) champion [Baltimore Ravens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Baltimore_Ravens_season) 31–29 at [M\&T Bank Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M\&T_Bank_Stadium) in Baltimore. Going into Week 17 they found themselves once again one win away from winning the division. Standing in their way were the [Washington Redskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Washington_Redskins_season), who had beaten them on Thanksgiving at [AT\&T Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Stadium) and who were one win away from their first division title since [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Washington_Redskins_season). Led by [Robert Griffin III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Griffin_III) the Redskins defeated the Cowboys at home 28–18.[^cite_note-119] Dallas once again finished the season 8–8.[^cite_note-120]

In the [2013 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Dallas_Cowboys_season) the Cowboys started off by defeating the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_New_York_Giants_season) for the second straight year; this time 36–31. It was the first time since 2008 that the Cowboys were able to defeat the Giants at home. The win was punctuated by [Brandon Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Carr) intercepting an [Eli Manning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Manning) pass for a touchdown late in the 4th quarter. For the third straight year, Dallas once again found itself stuck in the .500 area. In Week 5, they lost a shootout to the eventual AFC champion [Denver Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Denver_Broncos_season) 51–48.[^cite_note-q532-121] They battled it out with the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Philadelphia_Eagles_season) for control of the division throughout the season. In December however they lost 2 crucial back-to-back games to the [Chicago Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Chicago_Bears_season) and [Green Bay Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Green_Bay_Packers_season). They were very successful in division games having a 5–0 division record heading into another Week 17 showdown for the NFC East crown against the Eagles. That included beating the [Washington Redskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Washington_Redskins_season) 24–23 on Week 16 thanks to the late-game heroics of [Tony Romo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Romo). However, Romo received a severe back injury in that game which prematurely ended his season. The Cowboys called upon backup quarterback [Kyle Orton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Orton) to lead them into battle on the final week of the season. Orton was unsuccessful who threw a game-ending interception to the Eagles which allowed the Eagles to win 24–22. Dallas ended the year at 8–8 for the third year in a row. The two differences from this 8–8 ending compared to the others was that Dallas ended the season in second place compared to the 2 previous 3rd-place finishes, along with their season-ending defeat taking place at home instead of on the road.[^cite_note-122]

To start off the [2014 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Dallas_Cowboys_season) Dallas began by losing to the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_San_Francisco_49ers_season) 28–17. After that, they went on a 6-game winning streak. The highlight of this streak was defeating the [Super Bowl XLVIII](4057023.md) champion [Seattle Seahawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Seattle_Seahawks_season) at [CenturyLink Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CenturyLink_Field) 30–23. In Week 8, the [Washington Redskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Washington_Redskins_season) ended the Cowboys' winning streak by winning in overtime 20–17, and Romo injured his back again. He missed next week, a home loss to the [Arizona Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Arizona_Cardinals_season) 28–17 with backup quarterback [Brandon Weeden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Weeden). Romo returned in Week 9 to lead a 31–17 victory over the [Jacksonville Jaguars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Jacksonville_Jaguars_season), which was played at [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md) in [London, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London,_England) as part of the [NFL International Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_International_Series). Dallas played their traditional Thanksgiving home game against their division rival [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Philadelphia_Eagles_season). Both teams were vying for first place in the division with identical 8–3 records. The Eagles got off to a fast start and the Cowboys were unable to catch up, losing 33–10. They would rebound the next week when they defeated the [Chicago Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Chicago_Bears_season) 41–28. Week 15 was a rematch against 1st place Philadelphia. This time it was the Cowboys who got off to a fast start going up 21–0. Then the Eagles put up 24 points but Dallas came back to win 38–27 to go into first place for the first time in the season and improve to 10–4. Going into their Week 16 matchup at home against the [Indianapolis Colts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Indianapolis_Colts_season), Dallas was in a position to clinch their first division title since 2009 by defeating the Colts 42-7 and the Eagles losing that week to the Redskins. They became the 2014 NFC East Champions, eliminating the Eagles from the playoffs. Dallas ended the regular season with a 12–4 record and an 8–0 away record when they won on the road against Washington 44–17.[^cite_note-123]

On January 4, 2015, the Cowboys, as the number 3 seed, hosted the number 6 seed [Detroit Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Detroit_Lions_season) in the wild-card round of the [NFL playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_NFL_playoffs). In the game, the Lions got off to a hot start, going up 14–0 in the first quarter. Dallas initially struggled on both sides of the ball. However, towards the end of the second quarter, Romo threw a 76-yard touchdown pass to Terrance Williams. [Matt Prater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Prater) of the Lions would kick a field goal before halftime to go up 17–7. Dallas came out swinging to start the second half by picking off Detroit quarterback [Matthew Stafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Stafford) on the first play of the third quarter. However, the Cowboys failed to capitalize on the turnover, as Dan Bailey missed a field goal during Dallas's ensuing drive. Detroit then kicked another field goal to make the score 20–7. A DeMarco Murray touchdown later in that quarter closed the gap to 20–14. A 51-yard Bailey field goal almost 3 minutes into the fourth quarter trimmed the Cowboys' deficit to 3. The Lions got the ball back and started driving down the field. On 3rd down-and-1 of that Lions drive, Stafford threw a 17-yard pass intended for Lions tight end [Brandon Pettigrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Pettigrew), but the ball hit Cowboys linebacker [Anthony Hitchens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Hitchens) in the back a fraction of a second before he ran into Pettigrew. The play was initially flagged as defensive pass interference against Hitchens. However, the penalty was then nullified by the officiating crew. The Cowboys got the ball back on their 41-yard line and had a successful 59-yard drive which was capped off by an 8-yard touchdown pass from Romo to Williams to give the Cowboys their first lead of the game at 24–20. The Lions got the ball back with less than 2\:30 to play in regulation. Stafford fumbled the ball at the 2-minute mark. The fumble was recovered by Cowboys defensive end [DeMarcus Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeMarcus_Lawrence), who then fumbled the ball which was recovered by the Lions. Lawrence would redeem himself by sacking Stafford on a 4th down-and-3 play. The sack led to Stafford fumbling the ball again, which Lawrence recovered to seal the game for the Cowboys, who won 24–20.[^cite_note-m743-124] This was the first time in franchise playoff history that Dallas had been down by 10 or more points at halftime and rallied to win the game.

The following week, the Cowboys traveled to [Lambeau Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambeau_Field) in [Green Bay, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay,_Wisconsin) to play the [Green Bay Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Green_Bay_Packers_season) in the [Divisional Round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dez_Caught_It). Despite having a 14–7 halftime lead, the Cowboys fell to the Packers 26–21, thus ending their season. The season ended on a [controversial overturned call](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dez_Caught_It) of a completed catch at the 1-yard line by [Dez Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dez_Bryant). The catch was challenged by the Packers, and the referees overturned the call because of the "Calvin Johnson rule".[^cite_note-125]

During the 2015 offseason the Cowboys allowed running back [DeMarco Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeMarco_Murray) to become a free agent. Murray signed with the [division rival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboys%E2%80%93Eagles_rivalry) [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles).[^cite_note-126] On July 15 wide receiver [Dez Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dez_Bryant) signed a 5-year, \$70 million contract.[^cite_note-127]

The [Cowboys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Dallas_Cowboys_season) started the 2015 season at home against the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_New_York_Giants_season), in which Dallas won 27–26. Dez Bryant left the game early with a fractured bone in his foot. On the road against the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Philadelphia_Eagles_season), Romo suffered a broken left collarbone, the same one he injured in 2010, and Brandon Weeden replaced him. Dallas won 20–10 to begin the season 2–0, but then went on a seven-game losing streak. They finished the season 4–12 and last in their division.[^cite_note-128]

In [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Dallas_Cowboys_season), after a preseason injury to Tony Romo, rookie quarterback [Dak Prescott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dak_Prescott) was slated as the starting quarterback, as Romo was expected to be out 6–8 weeks.[^cite_note-129] In week 1 against the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_New_York_Giants_season), Dallas lost 20–19. After this loss, Dallas would go on an eleven-game winning streak. After much speculation leading to a potential quarterback controversy, Romo made an announcement that Prescott had earned the right to take over as the Cowboys starting quarterback.[^cite_note-130]

In game 10, Romo suited up for the first time in the season and was the backup quarterback. Dallas defeated the [Baltimore Ravens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Baltimore_Ravens_season) to win their 9th straight game, breaking a franchise record of 8 straight games set in 1977. It also marked rookie running back [Ezekiel Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezekiel_Elliott) breaking [Tony Dorsett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Dorsett)'s single-season rushing record for a Cowboys rookie. Prescott also tied an NFL rookie record held by [Russell Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Wilson) and [Dan Marino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Marino) by throwing multiple touchdowns in 5 straight games. Dallas finished 13–3, tying their best 16-game regular-season record. While Dallas defeated the [Green Bay Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Green_Bay_Packers_season) at Lambeau Field in week 6, the Packers would win at AT\&T Stadium in the divisional round of the [NFL playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_NFL_playoffs) on a last-second field goal, ending the Cowboys' season.[^cite_note-i327-131]



[Dak Prescott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dak_Prescott) and [Ezekiel Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezekiel_Elliott) were both [Rookie of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Rookie_of_the_Year_Award) finalists in 2016 after leading the team to a 13–3 record, with the former winning the award

Dak Prescott was named [NFL Rookie of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Rookie_of_the_Year_Award) in the NFL honors on February 4, 2017, and Ezekiel Elliott led the league in rushing yards. Jason Garrett was named [Coach of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Coach_of_the_Year_Award). Both Prescott and Elliott made the [2017 Pro Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Pro_Bowl). This is the first time the Cowboys sent two rookies to the Pro Bowl.[^cite_note-132]

[2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Dallas_Cowboys_season) was the first season since 2002 without quarterback Tony Romo, who retired on April 4 after 14 seasons with the Cowboys.[^cite_note-j437-133] The season also featured second-year running back Ezekiel Elliott being suspended for 6 games after violating the league's conduct policy.[^cite_note-l3531-134] The suspension was to begin at the start of the year but was pushed back to November.[^cite_note-j890-135] The Cowboys finished the year at 9-7 without making the playoffs. Following the season, Dez Bryant was released after eight seasons in Dallas and tight end [Jason Witten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Witten), who holds several franchise receiving records, retired after 15 seasons, ending an era.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137]

The Dallas Cowboys' 2017 season was the subject of the third season of [Amazon's](90451.md) sports documentary series *[All or Nothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_or_Nothing_\(franchise\))*. The series is produced by NFL Films.[^cite_note-138]

In the 2018 season, the Cowboys finished with a 10–6 record and won the NFC East.[^cite_note-139] In the Wild Card Round, the Cowboys defeated the Seattle Seahawks 24–22 before losing 30–22 to the Los Angeles Rams in the Divisional Round.[^cite_note-p414-140][^cite_note-v313-141]

#### Mike McCarthy years (2020–2024)

See also: [Mike McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_McCarthy)

Following the end of the 2019 season, where the Cowboys missed the playoffs for the 7th time in the last 10 seasons, it was announced that the team had parted ways with longtime head coach [Jason Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Garrett). Both [Marvin Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Lewis) (former [Bengals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati_Bengals) coach) and [Mike McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_McCarthy) (former [Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers) coach who led Green Bay to a Super Bowl win) were interviewed for the head coaching position.[^cite_note-q406-142]

McCarthy and the Cowboys picked up the first win against the [Atlanta Falcons](2103.md) in Week 2.[^cite_note-143] On October 11, the Cowboys' [2020 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Dallas_Cowboys_season) was all but lost when quarterback [Dak Prescott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dak_Prescott) suffered a grievous ankle injury that ended his season.[^cite_note-c794-144] Despite the loss of Prescott, McCarthy's first year Cowboys still remained in the running for a playoff appearance throughout most of the regular season. They would go on to finish the season with a 6–10 record, which ranked the team third in the NFC East Division.[^cite_note-145] Throughout the 2020 season, the Cowboys' defense struggled massively. Following the season, defensive coordinator [Mike Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Nolan) and defensive line coach [Jim Tomsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Tomsula) were dismissed.[^cite_note-q521-146]

The Cowboys' [2021 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Dallas_Cowboys_season) resulted in the first winning season since [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Dallas_Cowboys_season), and with the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_San_Francisco_49ers_season)' Week 16 loss to the [Tennessee Titans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Tennessee_Titans_season), the Cowboys clinched their first playoff berth also since 2018. Following a [Denver Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Denver_Broncos_season)' loss to the [Las Vegas Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Las_Vegas_Raiders_season), the Cowboys clinched the NFC East, based on [strength-of-victory tiebreakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_playoffs#Breaking_ties); this was their first division title since 2018.[^cite_note-g640-147] They swept the NFC East for the first time since [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Dallas_Cowboys_season). Rookie [Micah Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micah_Parsons) was awarded as [Defensive Rookie of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Rookie_of_the_Year_Award#Defensive_ROTY_winners_\(1969%E2%80%93present\)), and contributed to a league-leading defense.[^cite_note-o153-148] The Cowboys' strong offense finished the year with 530 points, the most in the league, and a team record.[^cite_note-w574-149] They finished the season with a 12–5 record, their best since 2016. But despite high expectations, the Cowboys lost in the [wild card round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_NFL_playoffs#NFC:_San_Francisco_49ers_23,_Dallas_Cowboys_17) of the playoffs to the San Francisco 49ers 23–17.[^cite_note-s179-150]

The [2022 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Dallas_Cowboys_season) saw a repeat of the 12–5 record. Despite losing to the [Jacksonville Jaguars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Jacksonville_Jaguars_season) in Week 15, the Cowboys clinched a playoff berth after a loss by the [Washington Commanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Washington_Commanders_season) later that day. This marked the first time since [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Dallas_Cowboys_season)–[2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Dallas_Cowboys_season) the Cowboys qualified for the postseason in consecutive seasons.[^cite_note-151] Quarterback Dak Prescott was awarded as [Walter Payton NFL Man of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Payton_NFL_Man_of_the_Year_Award), for his contributions to the community and charity.[^cite_note-i169-152] In the [wild-card round of the playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_NFL_playoffs#NFC:_Dallas_Cowboys_31,_Tampa_Bay_Buccaneers_14), the Cowboys defeated the [Tampa Bay Buccaneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Tampa_Bay_Buccaneers_season) to win their first road playoff game since their [Super Bowl-winning 1992 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Dallas_Cowboys_season), and ended their winless streak against [Tom Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Brady) in what proved to be Brady's last game in his career.[^cite_note-153] However, they were defeated by the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_San_Francisco_49ers_season) for the second consecutive season, this time in the [divisional round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_NFL_playoffs#NFC:_San_Francisco_49ers_19,_Dallas_Cowboys_12), their seventh consecutive divisional round defeat.[^cite_note-d417-154]

On February 16, 2022, a settlement of \$2.4 million was paid after four cheerleaders accused Rich Dalrymple, the now-retired senior vice president of public relations and communications, of voyeurism in their locker room as they undressed during a 2015 event at AT\&T Stadium.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156]

After the NFL allowed teams to seek blockchain sponsorships,[^cite_note-157] the Cowboys became the first team to do so, signing a multi-year contract with the platform [Blockchain.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain.com) on April 13, 2022.[^cite_note-158]



Dak Prescott and CeeDee Lamb led the league in multiple statistical categories in 2023, including passing touchdowns, passing completions, and receptions.

In [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Dallas_Cowboys_season), the Cowboys again achieved a 12–5 record, for the third year in a row.[^cite_note-159] The team won the [NFC East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFC_East) division for the first time since the 2021 season and the second time in three seasons.[^cite_note-u019-160] They ended up in a three-way tie with the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_San_Francisco_49ers_season) and the [Detroit Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Detroit_Lions) for first place in the NFC at 12–5. However, they lost the conference record tiebreaker to the 49ers but won the head to head tiebreaker over the Lions, giving them the second seed in the playoffs.[^cite_note-161] Although the Cowboys lost to the [Buffalo Bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Buffalo_Bills_season) in Week 15, they clinched their third straight playoff berth before taking the field when the [Green Bay Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Green_Bay_Packers_season) and [Atlanta Falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Atlanta_Falcons_season) lost to the [Tampa Bay Buccaneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Tampa_Bay_Buccaneers_season) and [Carolina Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Carolina_Panthers_season), respectively.[^cite_note-162] This marked the Cowboys' first run of three consecutive postseason appearances since appearing in six straight from [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Dallas_Cowboys_season) to [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Dallas_Cowboys_season). However, the Cowboys collapsed in the [playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_NFL_playoffs), and, despite having one of the best-ranked offenses and defenses of the league, were defeated 48–32 by their [rival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboys%E2%80%93Packers_rivalry), seventh-seeded Green Bay Packers in the Wild Card round, at one point trailing 48–16 during the fourth quarter.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] With the loss, the Cowboys became the first team to lose to a #7 seed since the playoff bracket expanded for the [2020–21 NFL playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_NFL_playoffs).[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168] This also marked the first time a team failed to reach a Conference Championship Game despite winning at least 12 games in three consecutive seasons.[^cite_note-169] Dak Prescott led the league in passing touchdowns, with 36, and passing completions, with 410, and [CeeDee Lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CeeDee_Lamb) led the league in receptions, with 135.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] The latter also set single-season franchise records for receptions and receiving yards.[^cite_note-172]

The [2024 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Dallas_Cowboys_season) was their first season since [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Dallas_Cowboys_season) without [Dan Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Quinn_\(American_football\)) as defensive coordinator, as the Washington Commanders hired him to be their head coach. He was replaced in that role by former Minnesota Vikings head coach [Mike Zimmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Zimmer), who returned to the Cowboys after eighteen years. The Cowboys failed to improve upon their 12–5 record from the past three seasons, and finished the season with a 7–10 record and failed to make the playoffs for the first time since [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Dallas_Cowboys_season).[^cite_note-s162-173]

On January 13, 2025, it was announced that McCarthy would not be returning as head coach of the Dallas Cowboys due to a contract dispute.[^cite_note-174]

#### Brian Schottenheimer years (2025–present)

On January 24, 2025, it was announced that [Brian Schottenheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Schottenheimer) had been promoted from offensive coordinator to head coach.[^cite_note-175]

The [2025 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Dallas_Cowboys_season) was the Cowboys' first season since 2013 without perennial All-Pro guard [Zack Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_Martin) or defensive end [DeMarcus Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeMarcus_Lawrence). Martin announced his retirement on February 20,[^cite_note-176] and Lawrence signed with the [Seattle Seahawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Seattle_Seahawks_season) during free agency.[^cite_note-177] This season also marked the departure of All-Pro defensive end [Micah Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micah_Parsons), as the Cowboys traded him to the [Green Bay Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers) in exchange for defensive tackle [Kenny Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Clark_\(defensive_tackle\)) and two first round draft picks amid a contract [holdout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holdout_\(sports\)).[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179]

The Cowboys improved upon their 7–10 record from the previous season, with a 7–9–1 record, but missed the playoffs for the second straight season. Despite having the second-best offense in the league[^cite_note-180], due in part to the arrival of wide receiver [George Pickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Pickens) and running back [Javonte Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javonte_Williams), as well as one of the best special teams units led by [Brandon Aubrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Aubrey), the defense was ranked as the league's worst in many statistical categories, most notably points allowed per game, at 551, and touchdowns allowed, at 60, as well as ranking in the bottom three in multiple defensive categories, such as yards allowed.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182] The season also marked franchise record-worsts for points and touchdowns allowed.[^cite_note-183] Defensive coordinator [Matt Eberflus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Eberflus) was dismissed after a single season.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185]

## Thanksgiving Day games

In their seventh season in [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Dallas_Cowboys_season), the Cowboys agreed to host a second [NFL Thanksgiving game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_on_Thanksgiving_Day); the tradition of a team hosting on [Thanksgiving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanksgiving_\(United_States\)) had been popularized by the [Detroit Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Lions) (who had hosted a game on the day mostly un-interrupted since moving to Detroit in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_Detroit_Lions_season)). General manager [Tex Schramm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tex_Schramm) wanted to find a way to boost publicity on a national level for his team, which had struggled for most of the 1960s. In fact, the NFL guaranteed a cut of the gate revenue in the belief that the game would not be a hit because of said struggle. With a kickoff just after 5 p.m. [CST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Time_Zone),[^cite_note-clldbg-186] over eighty thousand fans (and millions viewing on [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_on_CBS)) saw the Cowboys beat the [Cleveland Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Cleveland_Browns_season) 26–14 at the [Cotton Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_Bowl_\(stadium\)).[^cite_note-bcpbr-187][^cite_note-mpdiel-188][^cite_note-tgexpl-189]

In [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Dallas_Cowboys_season) and [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Dallas_Cowboys_season), at the behest of [Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_NFL_Commissioner) [Pete Rozelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Rozelle), the [St. Louis Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_St._Louis_Cardinals_\(NFL\)_season) replaced Dallas as a host team. Dallas then hosted St. Louis in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Dallas_Cowboys_season) in an effort by the NFL to give St. Louis national exposure. Although the Cardinals, at the time known as the "Cardiac Cards" due to their propensity for winning very close games, were a modest success at the time, the games did not prove as successful. Owing to factors that ranged from ugly contests to opposition from the [Kirkwood–Webster Groves Turkey Day Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkwood%E2%80%93Webster_Groves_Turkey_Day_Game) (a local [high school football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_football) contest) led to Dallas resuming regular hosting duties in [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Dallas_Cowboys_season). It was then, after Rozelle asked Dallas to resume hosting Thanksgiving games, that the Cowboys requested (and received) an agreement guaranteeing the Cowboys a spot on Thanksgiving Day for good; as such, the Cowboys play in the late afternoon.[^cite_note-190]

## Logos and uniforms

### Logo



The throwback double-star uniforms introduced in 1994 for the NFL's 75th Anniversary

The Dallas Cowboys' blue star logo, which represents Texas as "The Lone Star State", is one of the most well-known team logos in professional sports. The blue star originally was a solid shape until a white line and blue border were added in 1964. The logo has remained the same since. Today, the blue star has been extended to not only the Dallas Cowboys, but owner Jerry Jones' defunct [AFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Football_League) team, the [Dallas Desperados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Desperados) that used a similar logo based on that of the Cowboys. The blue star also is used on other entries like an imaging facility and storage facility.

### Uniforms





Front and back of Cowboys helmet

The Dallas Cowboys' white home jersey has royal blue (PMS 287 C) solid socks, numbers, lettering, and two stripes on the sleeves outlined in black. The home pants are a common metallic silver-green color (PMS 8280 C) that helps bring out the blue in the uniform. The navy (PMS 289 C) road jerseys (nicknamed the "Stars and Stripes" jersey) have white lettering and numbers with navy pinstripes. A white/gray/white stripe is on each sleeve as well as the collared V-neck, and a Cowboys star logo is placed upon the stripes. A "Cowboys" chest crest is directly under the NFL shield. The away pants are a pearlish metallic-silver color (PMS 8180 C) and like the home pants, enhance the navy in the uniforms. The team uses a serifed font for the lettered player surnames on the jersey nameplates.[^cite_note-CowboysMediaGuide-5][^cite_note-CowboysTeamCapsule-6]

The team's helmets are also a unique silver with a tint of blue known as "Metallic Silver Blue" (PMS 8240 C) and have a blue/white/blue vertical stripe placed upon the center of the crown. The Cowboys also include a unique, if subtle, feature on the back of the helmet: a blue strip of [Dymo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dymo) tape with the player's name embossed, placed on the white portion of the stripe at the back of the helmet.

#### Home and away uniform history

When the Dallas Cowboys franchise debuted in 1960, the team's uniform included a white helmet adorned with a simple blue star and a blue-white-blue stripe down the center crown. The team donned blue jerseys with white sleeves and a small blue star on each shoulder for home games and the negative opposite for away games. Their socks also had two horizontal white stripes overlapping the blue.

In 1964, the Cowboys opted for a simpler look (adopting essentially the team's current uniform) by changing their jersey/socks to one solid color with three horizontal stripes on the sleeves; the white jersey featured royal blue stripes with a narrow black border, the royal blue jersey white stripes with the same black outline. The star-shouldered jerseys were eliminated; "TV" numbers appeared just above the jersey stripes. The new helmet was silver-blue, with a blue-white-blue tri-stripe down the center (the middle white stripe was thicker). The blue "lone star" logo was retained, but with a white border setting it off from the silver/blue. The new pants were silver/blue, with a blue-white-blue tri-stripe. In 1964, the NFL allowed teams to wear white jerseys at home; several teams did so, and the Cowboys have worn white at home ever since, except on certain and special "throwback" days.[^cite_note-Morgan-191][^cite_note-192][^cite_note-u873-193]

In 1966, the team modified the jerseys, which now featured only two sleeve stripes, slightly wider; the socks followed the same pattern. In 1967 the "lone star" helmet decal added a blue outline to the white-bordered star, giving the logo a bigger, bolder look. The logo and this version of the uniform have seen little change to the present day.

The only notable changes from 1970 to the present were:

- 1970 to 1973: The "TV" numbers were moved from the shoulders to the sleeves above the stripes (the TV numbers returned to the shoulders on the white jerseys in 1974, but remained on the sleeves of the blue jerseys through 1978).

- 1982 to 1988: The pants featured a white uniform number in an elliptical blue circle worn near the hip.

- the removal of the indented [serifs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serif) on the front and back jersey numbers in the early 1980s (seen currently on the throwback jersey)

- 1980: The blue jersey was rendered in a slightly darker shade than the 1964–79 version; from 1981 to 1994 the dark jerseys sported numbers that were gray with white borders and a blue pinstripe. The stripes on the sleeves and socks also used the same gray with white border scheme (sans navy pinstripe).

- 1982 to present: Player names on jersey backs, which were originally in block-letter style, were slightly smaller and in a footed "serif" style.

- 1996 to present: The blue jersey features white/gray/white stripes on each sleeve and the collared V-neck, the Cowboys star logo placed upon the sleeve stripes, white lettering and numbers with navy pinstripes, and the "Cowboys" wordmark in the center of the neckline. The "Cowboys" wordmark was also placed at that same spot on the white jersey from 1996 to 1998.

During the 1976 season, the blue-white-blue stripe on the crown of the helmets was temporarily changed to red-white-blue to commemorate the United States' bicentennial anniversary. This stripe configuration returned in 2021 and is now worn for one regular season game annually when the team pays tribute to [Medal of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_Honor) recipients.[^cite_note-194]

In 1994, the NFL celebrated their 75th Anniversary, and the Dallas Cowboys celebrated their back-to-back Super Bowl titles by unveiling a white "Double-Star" jersey on Thanksgiving Day. This jersey was used for special occasions and was worn throughout the 1994–95 playoffs. During the same season, the Cowboys also wore their 1960–63 road jersey with a silver helmet for one game as part of a league-wide "throwback" policy.

During the 1995 season, the team wore the navy "Double-Star" jersey for games at Washington and Philadelphia and permanently switched to solid color socks (royal blue for the white uniform, and navy blue for the dark uniform). The navy "Double-Star" jersey was not seen again until the NFL's *Classic Throwback Weekend* on Thanksgiving Day 2001–2003.

In 2004, the Cowboys resurrected their original 1960–1963 uniform on Thanksgiving Day. This uniform became the team's alternate or "third jersey" and was usually worn at least once a year, primarily Thanksgiving Day. Two exceptions were when the Cowboys wore their normal white uniforms on Thanksgiving in 2007 and 2008. While the team didn't wear the throwback uniform exactly on Thanksgiving Day in those two years, Dallas wore them on a date around Thanksgiving for those two years. In 2007 Dallas wore the throwback uniform on November 29, 2007, against the Green Bay Packers. In 2008 Dallas wore the throwback uniform on November 23, 2008, against the San Francisco 49ers. The team went back to wearing this uniform at home on Thanksgiving Day in 2009 while their opponent was the Oakland Raiders who wore their AFL Legacy Weekend throwbacks. Dallas wore this alternate uniform on October 11, 2009, as part of one of the NFL's AFL Legacy Weekends when they traveled to Kansas City to play the Chiefs who were sporting their AFL Dallas Texans' uniforms. This created a rare game in which neither team wore a white jersey and the first time the Cowboys wore the alternative uniform as a visiting team. The 1960–1963 uniform may also be used on other special occasions. Other instances include the 2005 Monday Night game against the Washington Redskins when the team inducted Troy Aikman, Emmitt Smith, and Michael Irving into the Cowboys Ring of Honor, and the 2006 Christmas Day game against the Philadelphia Eagles.

In 2013, the NFL issued a new helmet rule stating that players would no longer be allowed to use alternate helmets due to the league's enhanced concussion awareness. This caused the Cowboys' white 1960s throwback helmets to become non-compliant. However, this rule became moot in 2022 when the NFL once again allowed teams to use an alternate helmet again, and the Cowboys reintroduced the 1960s white helmet.

During the "one-shell era", in 2013,[^cite_note-195] 2014,[^cite_note-196] 2016,[^cite_note-197] and 2017,[^cite_note-198] the team wore their normal blue jerseys at home for Thanksgiving; the only exceptions were in 2015[^cite_note-199] and 2020[^cite_note-200] when the Cowboys wore the "Color Rush" uniforms (*see below*), and in 2018,[^cite_note-201] 2019[^cite_note-202] and 2021[^cite_note-203] when they wore their regular white uniforms.[^cite_note-2013BlueJerseys-204] In 2017, the team initially announced that they will wear blue jerseys at home on a more regular basis, only to rescind soon after.[^cite_note-HanzusCowboys-4][^cite_note-DaSilvaCowboys-205]

In 2015, the Cowboys released their [Color Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Color_Rush) uniform, featuring a variation of the 1990s "Double Star" alternates with white pants and socks. The uniform was first used in a Thanksgiving game against the Carolina Panthers and in subsequent *[Thursday Night Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thursday_Night_Football)* games since 2016. In 2022, the "Color Rush" uniforms would be worn with a white helmet; this design would emulate their current silver helmets but without any silver elements.[^cite_note-206]

The Cowboys also unveiled a navy uniform-white pants combination which was first used on December 10, 2017, against the Giants. On [Christmas Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_on_Christmas_Day) in 2025 at the Commanders, the Cowboys wore this combination with their "Arctic White" alternate helmets; this was after the NFL allowed teams to wear their alternate helmets with the primary uniforms and vice versa.

In 1964, Tex Schramm started the tradition of the Cowboys wearing their white jersey at home, contrary to an unofficial rule that teams should wear colored jerseys at home. Schramm did this because he wanted fans to see a variety of opponents' colors at home games.[^cite_note-2013BlueJerseys-204][^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208][^cite_note-DaSilvaCowboys-205][^cite_note-209][^cite_note-Morgan-191]

According to current Cowboys' Equipment Director, Mike McCord, another reason why the team chose to wear white uniforms at home was because of the intense Texas heat during the early part of the season at [Texas Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Stadium).[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211]

Throughout the years, the Cowboys' blue jersey has been popularly viewed to be "[jinxed](11347008.md)" because the team often seemed to lose when they wore them. This purported curse drew attention after the team lost Super Bowl V with the blue jerseys.[^cite_note-212] However, the roots of the curse likely date back earlier to the 1968 divisional playoffs, when the blue-shirted Cowboys were upset by the [Cleveland Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Cleveland_Browns_season) in what turned out to be Don Meredith's final game with the Cowboys. Another example was a 1976 regular season road game against the [St. Louis Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_St._Louis_Cardinals_\(NFL\)_season), in which the Cardinals elected to wear white as the home team and promptly defeated the then-undefeated Cowboys 21–17 for their first loss in six games.[^cite_note-213]

Since the white home uniform tradition began in 1964, the only season Dallas never wore blue uniforms in a regular season game was in the [1972 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Dallas_Cowboys_season), even though they wore them thrice in the preseason.[^cite_note-214] The only other times Dallas wore blue in one regular season game came in [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Dallas_Cowboys_season),[^cite_note-215] [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Dallas_Cowboys_season),[^cite_note-216] [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Dallas_Cowboys_season),[^cite_note-217] [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Dallas_Cowboys_season),[^cite_note-218] [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Dallas_Cowboys_season),[^cite_note-219] [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Dallas_Cowboys_season),[^cite_note-220] and [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Dallas_Cowboys_season).[^cite_note-221] Conversely, the [2019 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Dallas_Cowboys_season) saw Dallas wear their blue uniforms eight times, the most of any season.[^cite_note-222][^cite_note-223]

Since the 1970 NFL-AFL merger, league rules were changed to allow the Super Bowl home team to pick their choice of jersey. Most of the time, Dallas will wear their blue jerseys when they visit Washington, Philadelphia (sometimes), Miami, or one of the handful of other teams that traditionally wear their white jerseys at home during the first half of the season due to the hot climates in their respective cities or other means. Occasionally opposing teams will wear their white jerseys at home to try to invoke the curse,[^cite_note-224] such as when the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles) hosted the Cowboys in the [1980 NFC Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980%E2%80%9381_NFL_playoffs#Conference_championships),[^cite_note-225] as well as their November 4, 2007, meeting. Various other teams followed suit in the 1980s.

Although Dallas has made several tweaks to their blue jerseys over the years, Schramm said he did not believe in the curse.[^cite_note-226] Since the league began allowing teams to use an alternate jersey, the Cowboys' alternates have been primarily blue versions of past jerseys and the Cowboys have generally had success when wearing these blue alternates. With the implementation of the 2013 NFL helmet rule for alternate jerseys, the team decided instead to wear their regular blue jerseys for their Thanksgiving game, something they have not done at home since Schramm started the white-jersey-at-home tradition.[^cite_note-2013BlueJerseys-204]

As of the [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_NFL_season) season, the Cowboys have a cumulative 101–104–3 regular season record in their blue uniforms. They are also 17–14 at home while wearing the blue uniforms since 2001. The Cowboys also sport a 9–5 record when wearing the primary blue uniform/white pants combination since its 2017 debut.

The Cowboys are 2–6 in playoff games while wearing the blue uniforms. The only victories with the blue uniforms came in the [1978 NFC Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978%E2%80%9379_NFL_playoffs#NFC:_Dallas_Cowboys_28,_Los_Angeles_Rams_0) against the [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Los_Angeles_Rams_season), and the [2022 NFC Wild Card Round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_NFL_playoffs#NFC:_Dallas_Cowboys_31,_Tampa_Bay_Buccaneers_14) against the [Tampa Bay Buccaneers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Tampa_Bay_Buccaneers_season).[^cite_note-227]



[Roger Staubach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Staubach) and [Bob Lilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Lilly) jerseys shown at the [Pro Football Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame) in [Canton, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton,_Ohio)



Quarterback [Dak Prescott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dak_Prescott) handing the ball off to running back [Ezekiel Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezekiel_Elliott), both in the blue jerseys during a [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Dallas_Cowboys_season) game

#### Thanksgiving Day uniforms

With the Dallas Cowboys traditionally hosting [Thanksgiving Day games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_on_Thanksgiving_Day), the team donned new uniforms when they unveiled their white "Double-Star" jersey for the first time on November 24, 1994.[^cite_note-228] This game later became synonymous with future Cowboys Head Coach (2010–2019); then 3rd string Quarterback [Jason Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Garrett) as he led a come-from-behind victory against the Green Bay Packers.[^cite_note-229]

In the 2004 season, the team went further into Cowboys history by choosing to don blue jerseys worn in their first 4 years of existence, which included white helmets and pants. However, keeping consistent with modern marketing, navy blue was used for this version as opposed to the original 1960-1963 royal color jersey.[^cite_note-HanzusCowboys-4] Aside from the 2007 and 2008 seasons, the Cowboys continued to use this "throwback" uniform through Thanksgiving Day 2012.

Before the start of the 2013 season, the NFL announced a "One-helmet" rule to help prevent potential player concussions.[^cite_note-230] This regulation also prevented the Cowboys from pairing the white helmets with the throwback uniforms, as the team will often use the traditional silver-blue as their primary helmets throughout the season.

In the 2015 season, the Cowboys chose to wear a variation of the 1994 "Double-Star" jersey as their [Color Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_Rush) on Thanksgiving Day against the Carolina Panthers on November 26, 2015.[^cite_note-231] Since then, the Color Rush was only used again on Thanksgiving against the [Washington Football Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Washington_Football_Team_season) on November 26, 2020.[^cite_note-l353-232] In all other seasons, the team opted to wear their standard white or blue uniforms.

In 2022, the NFL restored the use of alternate helmets and the Cowboys reinstated the white helmet and navy 'throwback" uniforms on November 24, 2022, against the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants).[^cite_note-r502-233]

## Stadiums

### Cotton Bowl

Main article: [Cotton Bowl (stadium)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_Bowl_\(stadium\))



The main entrance to the [Cotton Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_Bowl_\(stadium\)) in [Dallas](53838.md)

The Cotton Bowl is a stadium which opened in 1932 and became known as "The House That Doak Built" due to the immense crowds that former SMU running back [Doak Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doak_Walker) drew to the stadium during his college career in the late 1940s. Originally known as the Fair Park Bowl, it is located in [Fair Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Park), site of the [State Fair of Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Fair_of_Texas). Concerts or other events using a stage allow the playing field to be used for additional spectators. The Cotton Bowl was the longtime home of the annual [Cotton Bowl Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_Bowl_Classic) college football bowl game, for which the stadium is named. (Beginning with the January 2010 game, the Cotton Bowl Classic has been played at [AT\&T Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Stadium) in Arlington.) The Dallas Cowboys called the Cotton Bowl home for 11 years, from the team's formation in 1960 until 1971, when the Cowboys moved to [Texas Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Stadium). It is the only Cowboys stadium within the Dallas [city limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_limits). The Cowboys hosted the Green Bay Packers for the [1966 NFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_NFL_Championship_Game) at the Cotton Bowl.[^cite_note-k235-234]

### Texas Stadium

Main article: [Texas Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Stadium)



The outside of [Texas Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Stadium) in [Irving, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving,_Texas)

For the majority of the franchise's history the Cowboys played their home games at Texas Stadium. Just outside the city of Dallas, the stadium was located in [Irving, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irving,_Texas). The stadium opened on October 24, 1971, at a cost of \$35 million and with a [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) of 65,675. The stadium was famous for its hole-in-the-roof dome. The roof's worn paint had become so unsightly in the early 2000s that it was repainted in the summer of 2006 by the City of Irving. It was the first time the famed roof was repainted since Texas Stadium opened. The roof was structurally independent from the stadium it covered. The Cowboys lost their final game at Texas Stadium to the [Baltimore Ravens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_Ravens), 33–24, on December 20, 2008. After Cowboys Stadium was opened in 2009, the Cowboys turned over the facility to the City of Irving.

In [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_NFL_season), it was replaced as home of the Cowboys by [Cowboys Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Stadium), which officially opened on May 27, 2009, in [Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Texas).[^cite_note-235] Texas Stadium was demolished by implosion on April 11, 2010.[^cite_note-b784-236]

### AT\&T Stadium

Main article: [AT\&T Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Stadium)



[AT\&T Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Stadium) (located in [Arlington, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Texas)) during a game

AT\&T Stadium, previously named Cowboys Stadium, is a [domed stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domed_stadium) with a retractable roof in [Arlington, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Texas). After failed negotiations to build a new stadium on the site of the Cotton Bowl, Jerry Jones, along with the city of Arlington, funded the stadium at a cost of \$1.3 billion.[^cite_note-s595-237] The stadium is located in [Tarrant County, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarrant_County,_Texas), the first time the Cowboys has called a stadium home outside of [Dallas County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_County,_Texas). It was completed on May 29, 2009, and seats 80,000, but is expandable to seat up to 100,000. AT\&T Stadium is among the largest domed stadiums in the world.[^cite_note-238]

A highlight of AT\&T Stadium is its gigantic, center-hung [high-definition television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_television) screen, at one point the largest in the world.[^cite_note-\:1-239] The 160 by 72 feet (49 by 22 m), 11,520-square-foot (1,070 m<sup>2</sup>) scoreboard surpassed the 8,736 ft<sup>2</sup> (812 m<sup>2</sup>) screen that opened in 2009 at the renovated [Kauffman Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kauffman_Stadium) in [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri) as the world's largest.[^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241][^cite_note-x819-242] In 2011, [Charlotte Motor Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Motor_Speedway) unveiled its plans for a new HDTV screen larger than the one in AT\&T Stadium;[^cite_note-\:1-239] that larger screen has since been completed.[^cite_note-243]

At the debut pre-season game of Cowboys Stadium, a punt by Tennessee Titans kicker, [A. J. Trapasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._J._Trapasso), hit the 2,100 in. screen above the field. The punt deflected and was ruled in-play until Titans coach [Jeff Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Fisher) informed the officials that the punt struck the [scoreboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoreboard). (Many believe Trapasso was trying to hit the suspended scoreboard, based on replays and the angle of the kick.) The scoreboard is, however, within the regulation of the NFL guidelines – hanging approximately five feet above the minimum height. No punts hit the scoreboard during the entire 2009 regular season during an actual game. Also, on August 22, 2009, the day after AJ Trapasso hit the screen, many fans touring the facility noted that half of the field was removed with large cranes re-positioning the screen. According to some fans, a tour guide explained that Jerry Jones invited a few professional soccer players to drop kick soccer balls to try to hit the screen. Once he observed them hitting it consistently he had the screen moved up another 10 feet.

The first regular season home game of the 2009 season was against the New York Giants. A league record-setting 105,121 fans showed up to fill Cowboys Stadium for the game before which the traditional "blue star" at the 50-yard line was unveiled for the first time; however, the Cowboys lost in the final seconds, 33–31.[^cite_note-244]

The Cowboys got their first regular-season home win on September 28, 2009. They beat the Carolina Panthers 21–7 with 90,588 in attendance. The game was televised on ESPN's *[Monday Night Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monday_Night_Football)* and marked a record 42nd win for the Cowboys on *Monday Night Football*.[^cite_note-245]

On July 25, 2013, the Cowboys announced that [AT\&T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T) would be taking over the rights to the name of the stadium.[^cite_note-246]

## Nationwide fanbase

### Fan support

Ever since the team joined the NFL in 1960, the franchise have garnered strong fan support in both the [Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas%E2%80%93Fort_Worth_metroplex) and the state of Texas. With its strong fanbase across the country, including the notable presence of fans at road games, the Cowboys are often referred to as "[America's Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Team)".[^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250]

### Criticism

Despite the historical success of the franchise and a large Cowboys' fanbase, many fans of other NFL teams have come to dislike the Cowboys.[^cite_note-251][^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253][^cite_note-254] Over the years, the Cowboys' fanbase had been labeled as the most annoying in all of sports.[^cite_note-255] [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) host and commentator [Stephen A. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_A._Smith) has validated this claim.[^cite_note-256]

## Rivalries

The [NFC East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFC_East), composed of the Cowboys, [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles), the [Washington Commanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Commanders) and [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants), is one of the least-changed divisions of the original six formed in the wake of the [NFL-AFL merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL-AFL_merger) (its only major changes being the relocation of the [Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Cardinals) franchise from St. Louis to Arizona and its subsequent move to the [NFC West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFC_West) in the league's [2002 realignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_NFL_season)). Three of the four teams have been division rivals since the Cowboys' entry into the NFL. As such, the Cowboys have some of the longest and fiercest rivalries in the sport.

### Divisional

#### Washington Commanders

Main article: [Commanders–Cowboys rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commanders%E2%80%93Cowboys_rivalry)

The [Washington Commanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Commanders) and the Dallas Cowboys enjoy what has been called by *[Sports Illustrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Illustrated)* the top NFL rivalry of all time and "one of the greatest in sports". Some sources trace the enmity to before the Cowboys were even formed, due to a longstanding disagreement between Washington owner [George Preston Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Preston_Marshall) and Cowboys founder [Clint Murchison, Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Murchison_Jr.) over the creation of a new football team in the South, due to Marshall's TV monopoly in that region. The two teams' storied on-field rivalry goes back to 1960 when the two clubs first played each other, resulting in a 26–14 Washington victory. Since that time, the two teams have met in 126 regular-season contests and two NFC Championships. Dallas leads the regular season all-time series 78–46–2, and Washington leads the all-time playoff series 2–0.[^cite_note-257] The Cowboys currently have a 14–7 advantage over Washington at FedEx Field. Some notable moments in the rivalry include Washington's victory over Dallas in the 1982 NFC Championship and the latter's 1989 win over Washington for their only victory that season. The last Cowboys game with Tom Landry as coach was a win over Washington on December 11, 1988. In the 2010s, Washington has struggled to consistently compete for the Division title, but still play the Cowboys particularly tough, posting an impressive upset victory against Dallas in 2014, despite being outclassed by the Cowboys in the overall standings. The 2010s also included an important game in week 17 of 2012 which saw Washington defeat Dallas 28–18 to win the NFC East.

#### Philadelphia Eagles

Main article: [Cowboys–Eagles rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboys%E2%80%93Eagles_rivalry)

See also: [Bounty Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounty_Bowl)



A game between the Cowboys and the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles) in December 2007

The competition between the Cowboys and the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles) has been particularly intense since the late 1970s, when the long-moribund Eagles returned to contention.[^cite_note-258] In January 1981, the two teams faced off in the NFC Championship, with Philadelphia winning 20–7. A series of other factors heightened tensions during the 1980s and 1990s, including several provocative actions by Philadelphia fans and Eagles head coach [Buddy Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_Ryan). Among these were the 1989 [Bounty Bowls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounty_Bowl) in which Ryan allegedly placed a bounty on Dallas kicker [Luis Zendejas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Zendejas) and [Veterans Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterans_Stadium) fans pelted the Cowboys with snowballs and other debris.

A 1999 game in [Philadelphia](50585.md) saw Eagles fans cheering as [Michael Irvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Irvin) lay motionless on the field at Veterans Stadium. In 2008, the rivalry became more intense when in the last game of the year in which both teams could clinch a playoff spot with a victory, the Philadelphia Eagles defeated the Cowboys 44–6. The following season, the Cowboys avenged that defeat by beating the Eagles three times: twice during the regular season to claim the title as NFC East champions and once more in a wild-card playoff game by a combined score of 78–30, including a 24–0 shutout in week 17. That three-game sweep was Dallas' first over any opponent and the longest winning streak against the Eagles since 1992–1995 when Dallas won seven straight matches against Philadelphia.

During the 2013 season, Dallas won the first meeting 17–3 at [Lincoln Financial Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Financial_Field) in Philadelphia. The two teams met again in Week 17 at AT\&T Stadium with the winner clinching the 2013 NFC East title. The Cowboys came into the game at a disadvantage with starting quarterback [Tony Romo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Romo) out with a season-ending back injury, which put backup [Kyle Orton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Orton) as the starter. It was a tight game with the Eagles up 24–22 with less than 2 minutes to go in regulation. Orton got the ball and started driving down the field when he was intercepted by the Eagles defense, which ended the game and the Cowboys season. In 2014, the Cowboys and Eagles both won against each other on the road with Philadelphia posting a dominant 33–10 win on [Thanksgiving Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League_on_Thanksgiving_Day) in Dallas, and Dallas returning the favor two weeks later by defeating the Eagles 38–27 at Lincoln Financial Field in Philadelphia. The second game between these rivals clinched a playoff spot for Dallas and led to formerly first-place Philadelphia missing out on the post-season. Dallas leads the all-time series 73–56.[^cite_note-259]

#### New York Giants

Main article: [Cowboys–Giants rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboys%E2%80%93Giants_rivalry)

The first game ever played between the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants) and Cowboys was a 31–31 tie on December 4, 1960. Dallas logged its first win in the series on October 29, 1961, and New York's first was on November 11, 1962. Among the more notable moments in the rivalry was the Giants' defeat of Dallas in the 2007 playoffs en route to their victory in [Super Bowl XLII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLII) and winning the first regular-season game played at [Cowboys Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboys_Stadium) in 2009. Dallas currently leads the all-time series 75–47–2.[^cite_note-footballdb1-260]

### Conference

#### San Francisco 49ers

Main article: [49ers–Cowboys rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49ers%E2%80%93Cowboys_rivalry)

The bitter rivalry between the Dallas Cowboys and [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_49ers) has been going on since the 1970s.[^cite_note-261][^cite_note-262] The [NFL Top 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Top_10) ranked this rivalry to be the tenth best in the history of the NFL. San Francisco has played Dallas in seven postseason games. The Cowboys defeated the 49ers in the 1970 and 1971 NFC Championship games, and again in the 1972 Divisional Playoff Game. The 1981 NFC Championship Game in San Francisco, which saw the 49ers' [Joe Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Montana) complete a game-winning pass to [Dwight Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_Clark) in the final minute (now known as [The Catch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Catch_\(American_football\))) is one of the most famous games in NFL history. The rivalry became even more intense during the 1992–1994 seasons. San Francisco and Dallas faced each other in the NFC Championship Game three separate times. Dallas won the first two match-ups, and San Francisco won the third. In each of these pivotal match-ups, the game's victor went on to win the Super Bowl. Both the Cowboys and the 49ers are tied for third all-time in [Super Bowl](27718.md) victories to the [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers) and New England Patriots, with five each. The 49ers-Cowboys rivalry is also part of the larger cultural rivalry between California and Texas. The 49ers lead the all-time series with a record of 20–19–1.[^cite_note-263]

#### Green Bay Packers

Main article: [Cowboys–Packers rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboys%E2%80%93Packers_rivalry)

The rivalry between the Dallas Cowboys and the [Green Bay Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers)[^cite_note-264] is one of the best known intra-conference [rivalries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League_rivalries) in the NFL. The two teams do not play every year; instead, they play once every three years due to the NFL's rotating division schedules, or if the two teams finish in the same place in their respective divisions, they would play the ensuing season. The rivalry has also resulted in notable [playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playoff) games.[^cite_note-265][^cite_note-266]

The all-time regular seasons series record is 22–17–1 in favor of the Packers, and the postseason series is also in favor of the Packers at 5–4.[^cite_note-267]

#### Los Angeles Rams

Main article: [Cowboys–Rams rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboys%E2%80%93Rams_rivalry)

The Cowboys also had a fierce rivalry with the [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams), particularly during the 1970s and 1980s. The two teams played eight postseason games during this period, including two NFC championship games. Between 1975 and 1980, the Cowboys faced the Rams in the playoffs five times in a six-year period. In both 1975 and 1978, the Cowboys won the NFC championship on the road in blowout fashion, only to be followed by close defeats at home in next year's divisional round. The 1980 Wild Card Round saw Dallas follow up last year's playoff defeat with another blowout victory. As of 2022, the Cowboys and Rams tied the all-time regular season series 18–18, but the Rams lead the all-time playoff series 5–4, having recently defeated the Cowboys in the 2018 Divisional Round.[^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269]

#### Minnesota Vikings

Main article: [Cowboys–Vikings rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboys%E2%80%93Vikings_rivalry)

Between the Dallas Cowboys and [Minnesota Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Vikings),[^cite_note-270]. The teams have met seven times in the post-season, the Cowboys third most played playoff opponent. The rivalry is home to many key memories, including the famous 1975 Hail Mary pass against the Vikings, the [Herschel Walker trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herschel_Walker_trade), the [Randy Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Moss) Thanksgiving game, and [Brett Favre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Favre) torching the Cowboys in what would be his last playoff win of his career in 2009. As of the 2025 season, the Cowboys lead the all-time series 19–16.[^cite_note-271]

### Interconference

#### Houston Oilers/Houston Texans

Main article: [Governor's Cup (Texas)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor's_Cup_\(Texas\))

The Cowboys have an intrastate interconference rivalry with the [Houston Texans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Texans) for which they compete in either a preseason or regular season game for bragging rights in [Texas](29810.md), a tradition started between the teams prior to the Oilers relocating to [Nashville, Tennessee](22018.md) to become the [Tennessee Titans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Titans). The Texans defeated the Cowboys in the team's inaugural season in 2002. The Cowboys lead the all-time series 4–2.[^cite_note-272]

#### Kansas City Chiefs

Main article: [Preston Road Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preston_Road_Trophy)

Due to the Cowboys driving his Dallas Texans out of the city, Chiefs owner Lamar Hunt held a grudge against the NFL franchise during the 1960s. In 2005, Jones compared Hunt to a [wolf in sheep's clothing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_in_sheep's_clothing) because of his interest in the Cowboys despite acting otherwise.[^cite_note-Winning_against_hometown_team-273]

Still, Jones and Hunt were close friends and neighbors who lived by Preston Road. In 1998, Hunt created a trophy for their teams called the Preston Road Trophy that would be given to whoever won when they met.[^cite_note-274] They often pranked each other when passing the trophy,[^cite_note-Winning_against_hometown_team-273] while Jones quipped in 2025 that he would put it by the foot of his bed after reclaiming it from Kansas City.[^cite_note-275]

[CBS11 Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTVT) wrote in 2017 that "it may not be Cowboys–Redskins, or Cowboys–Eagles, or Cowboys–Giants, but \[the] Cowboys game against the Kansas City Chiefs is an old school rivalry."[^cite_note-276]

#### Pittsburgh Steelers

Main article: [Cowboys–Steelers rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboys%E2%80%93Steelers_rivalry)

The two teams met in the first regular-season game the Cowboys ever played in 1960 (a 35–28 loss to the [Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers)), the first-ever regular-season victory for the expansion Cowboys in 1961, and would later meet in three Super Bowls, all of them closely contested events. The Steelers-Cowboys is to date the Super Bowl matchup with the most contests. The Steelers won [Super Bowl X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_X) and [Super Bowl XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XIII); both games were decided in the final seconds, first on a last-second throw by [Roger Staubach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Staubach), then as a fourth-quarter rally by Dallas fell short on an [onside kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onside_kick).[^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278] The Cowboys won [Super Bowl XXX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXX) in January 1996.[^cite_note-279] It is said that the rivalry was fueled in the 1970s due to the stark contrast of the teams: the Cowboys, being more of a "flashy" team with Roger Staubach's aerial attack and the "flex" [Doomsday Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doomsday_Defense); while the Steelers were more of a ["blue-collar"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-collar_worker) team with a strong running game and the 1970s-esque [Steel Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Curtain) defense, a contrast that still exists today.[^cite_note-280] In addition, both teams have national fan bases rivaled by few NFL teams, and both come from areas with a strong following for football at all levels. Dallas leads the all-time series 17–16 including the playoffs.[^cite_note-footballdb1-260]

### Ring of Honor

Main article: [Dallas Cowboys Ring of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Cowboys_Ring_of_Honor)

Unlike many NFL teams, the Cowboys do not retire jersey numbers of past standouts as a matter of policy. Instead, the team has a ["Ring of Honor"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Cowboys_Ring_of_Honor), which is on permanent display encircling the field. Originally at Texas Stadium, the ring is now on display at [AT\&T Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Stadium) in Arlington. The first inductee was Bob Lilly in 1975 and by 2005, the ring contained 17 names, all former Dallas players except for one head coach and one general manager/president.

The Ring of Honor has been a source of controversy over the years. Tex Schramm was believed to be a "one-man committee" in choosing inductees and many former Cowboys players and fans felt that Schramm deliberately excluded linebacker [Lee Roy Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Roy_Jordan) because of a bitter contract dispute the two had during Jordan's playing days. When Jerry Jones bought the team he inherited Schramm's Ring of Honor "power" and immediately inducted Jordan.

Jones also has sparked controversy regarding his decisions in handling the "Ring of Honor". For four years he was unsuccessful in convincing Tom Landry to accept induction. Meanwhile, he refused to induct Tex Schramm (even after Schramm's induction to the Pro Football Hall of Fame). In 1993, thanks in part to the efforts of Roger Staubach as an intermediary, Landry accepted induction and had a ceremony on the day of that year's Cowboys-Giants game (Landry had played and coached for the Giants). In 2003, Jones chose to induct Tex Schramm. Schramm and Jones held a joint press conference at Texas Stadium announcing the induction. Schramm did not live to see his ceremonial induction at the Cowboys-Eagles game that fall.

Troy Aikman, all-time NFL leading rusher Emmitt Smith, and Michael Irvin, known as "The Triplets", were inducted into the Ring of Honor during halftime at a Monday Night Football home game against the archrival Washington Redskins on September 19, 2005.[^cite_note-309]

Defensive end [Charles Haley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Haley), offensive lineman [Larry Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Allen), and wide receiver [Drew Pearson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Pearson_\(American_football\)) were inducted into the Ring of Honor during halftime of the Cowboys' game vs. the [Seattle Seahawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Seattle_Seahawks_season) on November 6, 2011.[^cite_note-j961-310]

Safety [Darren Woodson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Woodson) was inducted on November 1, 2015.[^cite_note-h811-311] Executive [Gil Brandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gil_Brandt) was inducted on November 29, 2018.[^cite_note-v788-312]

The most recent inductees were [DeMarcus Ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeMarcus_Ware), who was inducted on October 29, 2023, and [Jimmy Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Johnson_\(American_football_coach\)), who was inducted on December 30, 2023.[^cite_note-x244-313][^cite_note-u575-314]

### Retired numbers

The Dallas Cowboys do not officially retire jersey numbers; however, some are kept "unofficially inactive".[^cite_note-316][^cite_note-p981-317] As of 2022, six numbers have been kept out of circulation: [Troy Aikman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_Aikman)'s No. 8, [Roger Staubach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Staubach)'s No. 12, [Bob Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Hayes)' and [Emmitt Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmitt_Smith)'s No. 22,[^cite_note-d724-318] [Bob Lilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Lilly)'s No. 74, and [Jason Witten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Witten)'s No. 82.[^cite_note-complic-319] The Cowboys are one of three NFL teams that do not officially retire numbers, with the other two being the [Atlanta Falcons](2103.md) and [Las Vegas Raiders](22312.md).

## Radio and television

See also: [Dallas Cowboys Radio Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Cowboys_Radio_Network)

As of 2010, the Cowboys' flagship radio station is [KRLD-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KRLD-FM).[^cite_note-k163-338] [Brad Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Sham) is the team's longtime [play-by-play voice](1662121.md).[^cite_note-h439-339] Working alongside him is former Cowboy quarterback [Babe Laufenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babe_Laufenberg), who returned in 2007 after a one-year absence to replace former safety Charlie Waters.[^cite_note-b391-340] The Cowboys, who retain rights to all announcers, chose not to renew Laufenberg's contract in 2006 and brought in Waters. However, Laufenberg did work as the analyst on the "Blue Star Network", which televises Cowboys preseason games not shown on national networks. The anchor station is [KTVT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTVT), the [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) owned and operated station in Dallas. Previous stations which aired Cowboys games included [KVIL-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KVIL-FM), [KRLD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KRLD_\(AM\)), and [KLUV-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSPF). Kristi Scales is the sideline reporter on the radio broadcasts.[^cite_note-y846-341]

During his tenure as Cowboys coach, Tom Landry co-hosted his own coach's show with late veteran sportscaster [Frank Glieber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Glieber) and later with Brad Sham. Landry's show was famous for his analysis of raw game footage and for him and his co-host making their NFL "predictions" at the end of each show. Glieber is one of the original voices of the Cowboys Radio Network, along with [Bill Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Mercer), famous for calling the *Ice Bowl* of 1967 and both Super Bowl V and VI. Mercer is perhaps best known as the ringside commentator of [WCCW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Class_Championship_Wrestling) in the 1980s. Upon Mercer's departure, [Verne Lundquist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verne_Lundquist) joined the network, and became their play-by-play announcer by 1977, serving eight years in that capacity before handing those chores permanently over to Brad Sham, who joined the network in 1977 as the color analyst and occasional fill-in for Lundquist.

Longtime [WFAA-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WFAA-TV) sports anchor [Dale Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dale_Hansen) was the Cowboys color analyst with Brad Sham as the play-by-play announcer from 1985 to 1996.[^cite_note-x852-342]

Dave Garrett served as the Cowboys' play-by-play announcer from 1995 to 1997, when Brad Sham left the team and joined the Texas Rangers' radio network team as well as broadcast Sunday Night Football on Westwood One.

Seeking to expand its radio broadcasting scope nationally, the Cowboys began a five-year partnership with [Compass Media Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_Media_Networks) on February 2, 2011. The result was the America's Team Radio Network, a supplement to the franchise's regional one.[^cite_note-343] Beginning with the 2011 season, [Kevin Burkhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Burkhardt) and [Danny White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_White) handled the broadcasts, with [Jerry Recco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Recco) as the studio host.[^cite_note-344]

## Fight song

The Dallas Cowboys fight song, "Cowboys Stampede March" by Tom Merriman Big Band was the official fight song of the Dallas Cowboys.[^cite_note-f569-345] The Cowboys now play [We Dem Boyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Dem_Boyz) by [Wiz Khalifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiz_Khalifa) for starting defensive line, because of the saying "How Bout Dem Cowboys". For every touchdown scored by the Cowboys at a home game the song "Cowboys and Cut Cigars" by The Burning of Rome is played after a train horn.[^cite_note-k900-346]
