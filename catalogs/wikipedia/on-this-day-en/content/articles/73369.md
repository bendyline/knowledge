**Montpellier** ([UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English): [/mɒntˈpɛlieɪ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/ˌmoʊnpɛlˈjeɪ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English))[^cite_note-5] is a city in southern France near the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md). One of the largest urban centres in the region of [Occitania](45093609.md), Montpellier is the prefecture of the [department](38523.md) of [Hérault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9rault). As of 2023, 310,240 people live in the city proper,[^cite_note-pophist-6] while its [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_area_\(France\)) has a population of 842,923.[^cite_note-AAV_pop-7] Montpellier is the third-largest French city near the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) coast, behind [Marseille](40888948.md) and [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice), and [the seventh-largest city of France](76050805.md) overall. The inhabitants are called *Montpelliérains* and *Montpelliéraines*.

In the Middle Ages, Montpellier was an important city of the [Crown of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon) (and was the birthplace of [James I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_I_of_Aragon)), and then of [Majorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Majorca), before its sale to France in 1349. Established in 1220, the [University of Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montpellier) is one of the [oldest universities in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_universities_in_continuous_operation) and has the oldest medical school still in operation, with notable alumni such as [Petrarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarch), [Nostradamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nostradamus) and [François Rabelais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Rabelais). Above the medieval city, the ancient [citadel of Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel_of_Montpellier) is a stronghold built in the seventeenth century by [Louis XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIII).

Since the 1990s, Montpellier has experienced one of the strongest economic and [demographic growths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_France) in the country. Its urban area has experienced the highest population growth in France since the year 2000. Numbering 70,000, students comprise nearly one-fourth of its population, one of the highest such proportions in Europe.[^cite_note-8] Its living environment, with one of Europe's largest pedestrian areas,[^cite_note-9] along with its rich cultural life and [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate), explains the enthusiasm for the city, which is nicknamed the "Gifted". Montpellier was nominated for "Best Emerging Culture City of the Year 2017" by the think tank LCD.[^cite_note-10] It is ranked as a Sufficiency city by the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network).

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Montpellier).

### Medieval period



Tour de la Babote

In the [Early Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Middle_Ages), the nearby episcopal town of Maguelone was the major settlement in the area, but raids by [pirates](50715.md) encouraged settlement a little farther inland. In 737 [Charles Martel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Martel) destroyed [Maguelone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villeneuve-l%C3%A8s-Maguelone#History).[^cite_note-EBMontpellier-11]

Montpellier, first mentioned in a document of 985, was founded under a local [feudal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudal) dynasty, the Guilhem, who combined two hamlets and built a castle and walls around the united settlement. The name is from medieval Latin *mons pisleri*, "Woad Mountain" referring to the [woad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woad) (Latin *pastellus*, *pestellus*) used for dyeing locally. There is no real "mountain" in the area, with the *mons* referring to a pile of stones.[^cite_note-12] In 986 the [Lords of Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lords_of_Montpellier) begin with [William I of Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_I_of_Montpellier). In the 10th century the town consisted of two portions, Montpellier and Montpelliéret.[^cite_note-EBMontpellier-11] In 1160 the law school was active.[^cite_note-EBMontpellier-11]



Peyrou water tower

The two surviving towers of the city walls, the *Tour des Pins* and the *Tour de la Babotte*, were built later, around the year 1200. Montpellier came to prominence in the 12th century—as a trading centre, with trading links across the Mediterranean world, and a rich Jewish cultural life that flourished within traditions of tolerance of [Muslims](19541.md), Jews and [Cathars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathar)—and later of its Protestants. [William VIII of Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_VIII_of_Montpellier) gave freedom for all to teach medicine in Montpellier in 1180. The city's faculties of law and medicine were established in 1220 by Cardinal [Conrad of Urach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrad_of_Urach), legate of [Pope Honorius III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Honorius_III); the medical faculty has, over the centuries, been one of the major centres for the teaching of medicine in Europe. This era marked the high point of Montpellier's prominence. The city became a possession of the [Kings of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_of_Aragon) in 1204 by the marriage of [Peter II of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_II_of_Aragon) with [Marie of Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_of_Montpellier), who was given the city and its dependencies as part of her [dowry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dowry).

Montpellier gained a charter in 1204 when Peter and Marie confirmed the city's traditional freedoms and granted the city the right to choose twelve governing consuls annually. Under the Kings of Aragon, Montpellier became a very important city, a major economic centre and the primary centre for the spice trade in the Kingdom of France. It was the second or third most important city of France at that time, with some 40,000 inhabitants before the [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death). Montpellier remained a possession of the crown of Aragon until it passed to [James III of Majorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_III_of_Majorca), who sold the city to the French king [Philip VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_VI_of_France) in 1349, to raise funds for his ongoing struggle with [Peter IV of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_IV_of_Aragon).

From the middle of the 14th century until the French Revolution (1789), Montpellier was part of the [province of Languedoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languedoc).

### Renaissance

In the 14th century, [Pope Urban VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Urban_VIII) gave Montpellier a new monastery dedicated to [Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter), noteworthy for the very unusual porch of its chapel, supported by two high, somewhat rocket-like towers. With its importance steadily increasing, the city finally gained a bishop, who moved from [Maguelone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguelone) in 1536, and the huge monastery chapel became a cathedral. In 1432, [Jacques Cœur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_C%C5%93ur) established himself in the city and it became an important economic centre, until 1481 when Marseilles overshadowed it in this role.

### After the Reformation

At the time of the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation) in the 16th century, many of the inhabitants of Montpellier became [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestants) (or [Huguenots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenots) as they were known in France) and the city became a stronghold of Protestant resistance to the Catholic French crown.[^cite_note-Davis-13] Montpellier was among the most important of the 66 *villes de sûreté* ('cities of protection' or 'protected cities') that the [Edict of Nantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Nantes) granted to the Huguenots. The city's political institutions and the university were all handed over to the Huguenots.

Increasing tension with Paris led to King [Louis XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIII) [besieging the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Montpellier) in 1622.[^cite_note-Davis-13] A truce ended 40 days of bombardment on 12 October 1622, with the Huguenots agreeing to the peace settlement on 18 October.[^cite_note-Davis-13] Peace terms called for the dismantling of the city's fortifications and the building of the royal [Citadel of Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel_of_Montpellier) to secure the city for the government. The university and consulate were taken over by the Catholic party. Even before the [Edict of Alès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edict_of_Al%C3%A8s) in 1629, Protestant rule was dead and the *ville de sûreté* was no more.

[Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV) made Montpellier capital of [Bas Languedoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas_Languedoc?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the town started to embellish itself, by building the [Promenade du Peyrou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promenade_du_Peyrou), the Esplanade and a large number of houses in the historic centre.

### French Revolution

After the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), the city became the capital of the much smaller Department of [Hérault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A9rault).

### Modern history

During the 19th century the city thrived on the wine culture that it was able to produce due to the abundance of sun throughout the year. The wine consumption in France allowed Montpellier's citizens to become very wealthy until in the 1890s the [phylloxera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylloxera) induced fungal disease had spread amongst the vineyards and the people were no longer able to grow the grapes needed for wine.[^cite_note-14]

During the [repression of January and February 1894](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repression_of_January_and_February_1894), the police conducted raids targeting the [anarchists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchists) living there, without much success.[^cite_note-\:12-15][^cite_note-\:22-16][^cite_note-\:32-17]

After this the city grew because it welcomed French repatriates from Algeria and other parts of northern Africa after [Algeria's independence from France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria's_independence_from_France). In the 21st century Montpellier is between France's number seventh and eighth largest city. The city had another influx in population more recently, largely due to the student population, who make up about one-fourth of Montpellier's population. The school of medicine kickstarted the city's thriving university culture,[^cite_note-18] though many other universities have been well established there. The coastal city also benefited in the past 40 years from major construction programs such as [Antigone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigone,_Montpellier), Port Marianne and Odysseum districts.

## Geography

The city is situated on hilly ground 10 km (6 mi) inland from the Mediterranean coast, on the river [Lez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lez_\(river\)).

Montpellier is located 170 km (106 mi) from [Marseille](40888948.md), 242 km (150 mi) from [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse), and 748 km (465 mi) from Paris. The city is relatively close to [Nîmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%AEmes), being just 50 km (31 mi).

Montpellier's highest point is the Place du Peyrou, at an altitude of 57 m (187 ft). The city is built on two hills, Montpellier and Montpelliéret, thus some of its streets have great differences of altitude. Some of its streets are also very narrow and old, which gives it a more intimate feel.

### Climate

Montpellier has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Csa*), with cool, damp winters, and hot, rather dry summers. The monthly mean ranges from 7.2 °C (45.0 °F) in January to 24.1 °C (75.4 °F) in July. Precipitation is around 630 millimetres (24.8 in), and is greatest in fall and winter, but not absent in summer, either. Extreme temperatures have ranged from −17.8 °C (−0.04 °F) recorded on 5 February 1963 and up to 43.5 °C (110.3 °F) on 28 June 2019.

## Demographics

The whole [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_area_\(France\)) had a population of 813,272 at the 2020 census.[^cite_note-AAV_pop-7] In a study made by INSEE from 2017 to 2023 Montpellier saw the strongest population growth of France's cities with more than 100,000 inhabitants (+1.4%). For most of its history, and even today, Montpellier has been known for its significant Spanish population, heritage and influence. Montpellier also houses significant Moroccan, Algerian, and Italian communities.

- The main focus point of the city is the [Place de la Comédie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_de_la_Com%C3%A9die), with the Opéra Comédie built in 1888.

- The [Musée Fabre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_Fabre).

- In the historic centre, a significant number of *[hôtels particuliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotels_of_Montpellier)* (i.e. mansions) can be found. The majority of the buildings of the historic centre of Montpellier (called the Écusson because its shape is roughly that of an [escutcheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escutcheon_\(heraldry\))) have medieval roots and were modified between the 16th and the 18th centuries. Some buildings, along Rue Foch and the Place de la Comédie, were built in the 19th century.

- The Rue du Bras de Fer (Iron Arm Street) is very typical of the medieval Montpellier.

- The mikve, ritual Jewish bath, dates back to the 12th century and is one of very few old [mikves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikveh) preserved in Europe.

- The [Jardin des plantes de Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jardin_des_plantes_de_Montpellier)—oldest [botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_garden) in France, founded in 1593.

- The [La Serre Amazonienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Serre_Amazonienne), a tropical rain forest greenhouse

- The 14th-century [Saint Pierre Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier_Cathedral)

- The [Porte du Peyrou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porte_du_Peyrou), a [triumphal arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triumphal_arch) built at the end of the 17th century, and the Place Royal du Peyrou built in the 17th century, are the highest point of the Ecusson.

- The Tour des Pins, the only remaining of 25 towers of the medieval city walls, built around 1200.

- The Tour de la Babote, a medieval tower which was modified in the 18th century to house an observatory.

- The [Saint Clément Aqueduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Cl%C3%A9ment_Aqueduct?action=edit\&redlink=1), built in the 18th century.

- The [Antigone District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigone_District) was designed by the postmodern architect [Ricardo Bofill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Bofill) from [Catalonia](6822.md), Spain

- A [number of *châteaux*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier_follies) (such as [Château de Flaugergues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Flaugergues), [Château de la Mogère](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_de_la_Mog%C3%A8re) or [Château d'O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_d'O)), so-called [follies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folly), built during the 18th century by wealthy merchants surround the city

### History

The [University of Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montpellier) is one of the oldest in the world, founded in 1160, and having been granted a charter in 1220 by Cardinal [Conrad von Urach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrad_von_Urach) and confirmed by [Pope Nicholas IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_IV) in a [papal bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_bull) of 1289. It was suppressed during the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) but was re-established in 1896.

It is not known exactly at what date the schools of literature were founded which developed into the Montpellier faculty of arts; it may be that they were a direct continuation of the [Gallo-Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallo-Roman) schools. The school of law was founded by [Placentinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placentinus), a doctor from [Bologna University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_University), who came to Montpellier in 1160, taught there during two different periods, and died there in 1192. With regard to the school of medicine, there were excellent physicians at Montpellier. The statutes given in 1220 by Cardinal Conrad, legate of [Honorius III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorius_III), which were completed in 1240 by Pierre de Conques, placed this school under the direction of the [Bishop of Maguelonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Maguelonne). [Pope Nicholas IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_IV) issued a Bull in 1289, combining all the schools into a university, which was placed under the direction of the bishop, but which in fact enjoyed a large measure of autonomy.

Theology was at first taught in the convents, in which [St. Anthony of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Anthony_of_Padua), [Raymond Lullus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Lullus), and the Dominican [Bernard de la Treille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_de_la_Treille) lectured. Two letters of King John prove that a faculty of theology existed at Montpellier independently of the convents, in January 1350. By a Bull of 17 December 1421, [Martin V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_V) granted canonical institution to this faculty and united it closely with the faculty of law. In the 16th century the faculty of theology disappeared for a time, when [Calvinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism), in the reign of [Henry II of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_II_of_France), held complete possession of the city. It resumed its functions after [Louis XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIII) had reestablished the royal power at Montpellier in 1622; but the rivalries of [Dominicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order) and [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits) interfered seriously with the prosperity of the faculty, which disappeared at the Revolution. The faculty numbered among its illustrious pupils of law [Petrarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarch), who spent four years at Montpellier, and among its lecturers [Guillaume de Nogaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_de_Nogaret), chancellor to [Philip the Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_the_Fair), [Guillaume de Grimoard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_de_Grimoard), afterwards pope under the name of Urban V, and [Pedro de Luna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_de_Luna), [antipope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipope) as Benedict XIII. But after the 15th century this faculty fell into decay, as did also the faculty of arts, although for a time, under [Henry IV of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_IV_of_France), the latter faculty had among its lecturers [Isaac Casaubon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Casaubon).

The Montpellier school of medicine owed its success to the ruling of the Guilhems, lords of the town, by which any licensed physician might lecture there; there was no fixed limit to the number of teachers, lectures were multiplied, and there was a great wealth of teaching. [Rabelais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabelais) took his medical degrees at Montpellier. It was in this school that the biological theory of [vitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitalism), elaborated by [Barthez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Joseph_Barthez) (1734–1806), had its origin. The French Revolution did not interrupt the existence of the faculty of medicine.

The faculties of science and of letters were re-established in 1810; that of law in 1880. It was on the occasion of the sixth centenary of the university, celebrated in 1889, that the Government of France announced its intention—which has since been realized—of reorganizing the provincial universities in France.

## Transport

Main article: [Transport in Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Montpellier)



Line 1 of the tramway network, at the [Corum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corum_\(Montpellier\)) stop

Montpellier is served by railway, including [TGV](57212.md) highspeed trains. Montpellier's main railway station is [Saint-Roch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier-Saint-Roch_station). Since 2018, there is also a station on the [high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contournement_N%C3%AEmes_%E2%80%93_Montpellier) linking [Nîmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%AEmes) and Montpellier with the [LGV Méditerranée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGV_M%C3%A9diterran%C3%A9e), called [Montpellier-Sud de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier_Sud_de_France_station).[^cite_note-28]



Montpellier tramway map

The [Montpellier – Méditerranée Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier_%E2%80%93_M%C3%A9diterran%C3%A9e_Airport) is located in the area of Fréjorgues, in the town of [Mauguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauguio), southeast of Montpellier.

The *[Transports de l'agglomération de Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transports_de_l'agglom%C3%A9ration_de_Montpellier)* (TaM) manages the city's public transportation, including its 56-kilometre (35 mi) [tramway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Montpellier) consisting of five lines and several parking facilities.[^cite_note-Montpellier-29] Line 1 runs from Mosson in the west to Odysseum in the east. Line 2 runs from [Jacou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacou) in the northeast to [St. Jean-de-Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Jean-de-V%C3%A9das) in the southwest. Line 3 and Line 4 opened in April 2012. Line 3, which is 22.4-kilometre (13.9 mi) long, links [Juvignac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juvignac) and [Pérols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A9rols) with a branch to [Lattes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattes,_H%C3%A9rault) and serves 32 stations. Line 4 circles the centre and serves as a connector line between the various arms of tram system. They intersect at Gare St. Roch station, Rives du Lez and Corum.

Since 2019, €440 million were invested into the construction of a 5th tramway line, linking the south from [Lavérune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lav%C3%A9rune) to [Clapiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clapiers), up north. It was inaugurated on December 20, 2025.[^cite_note-30]

Since 21 December 2023, the public transport is free for all residents. Previously, all residents under 18 and over 65 years of age have been transported free of charge since 1 September 2021.[^cite_note-31]

The TaM also manages the large [bike sharing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike_sharing) scheme [Vélomagg'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A9lomagg'), started in June 2007, comprising 1200 bicycles and 50 stations.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

## Culture

The city is a centre for cultural events as there are many students. Montpellier has two large concert venues: Le Zénith Sud (7.000 seats) and L'Arena (14.000 seats). [Le Corum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corum_\(Montpellier\)) cultural and conference centre contains three auditoriums.

- The [Festival de Radio France et Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_de_Radio_France_et_Montpellier) is a summer festival of opera and other music held in Montpellier. The festival concentrates on classical music and jazz with about 150 events, including opera, concerts, films, and talks. Most of these events are free and are held in the historic [courtyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtyard) of the city or in the modern [concert halls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concert_hall) of Le Corum near historical city center.

- The annual Cinemed, the [International Mediterranean Film Festival Montpellier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpellier_Film_Festival), held in the fall, is the second-largest French film festival after the [Cannes Film Festival](101460.md). Held since 1979, it offers screenings of over 200 long and short films, documentaries, animated films, trailers, and a special program of student films.

  [^cite_note-35]

  &#x20;Other events include panel discussions, exhibitions, and gatherings. Venues include Le Corum and cinema halls.

## Other locations named after Montpellier

The name Montpellier is used for towns and streets in as many as four continents.[^cite_note-all-61] Many places in the United Kingdom and Ireland carry the name Montpellier. Often they are in resort locations claiming some of the healthy attributes for which the French city was renowned in earlier centuries. The variant spelling "Montpelier" is common, and is of quite early provenance. [Brewer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewer's_Dictionary_of_Phrase_and_Fable) uses that spelling. The first example was the early 19th-century suburb of [Montpelier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpelier,_Brighton) in [Brighton](49796.md).[^cite_note-MC1-62]

The capital of the American state of [Vermont](32578.md) was named [Montpelier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpelier,_Vermont) because of the high regard in which the Americans held the French[^cite_note-63] who had aided their [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) against the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain). Several other American cities are also named Montpelier.

Places named Montpellier/Montpelier are also found in Australia, Canada, South Africa, and the Caribbean.

James Madison, the United States fourth president, named his plantation [Montpelier (Orange, Virginia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montpelier_\(Orange,_Virginia\)), after the resort-like properties associated with the city at the time.
