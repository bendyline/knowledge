**Right-wing politics**, or **rightism**, is the range of [political ideologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideology#Political_ideologies) that view certain [social stratifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stratification) and [orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_order) as inevitable, natural, normal, or desirable,[^cite_note-Johnson-2005-1][^cite_note-Bobbio-1996-2][^cite_note-Goldthorpe-1985a-3] typically supporting this position in favour of [conservatism](6675.md), [natural law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_law), [economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics), [authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Authority), [property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property), [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion), or [tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tradition).[^cite_note-EB_online-4][^cite_note-Carlisle-5][^cite_note-T._Alexander_Smith_2003._p._30-6][^cite_note-Allan_Cameron_pg._37-7][^cite_note-Fuchs,_D._1990._p._203-8][^cite_note-Lukes-9][^cite_note-Clark-10] Hierarchy and [inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_inequality) may be seen as natural results of traditional social differences[^cite_note-Smith-2003b-11][^cite_note-Gidron-2019a-12] or competition in [market economies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy).[^cite_note-Scruton-1996-13][^cite_note-Goldthorpe-1985b-14][^cite_note-Gidron-2019b-15]&#x20;

Right-wing politics are considered the counterpart to [left-wing politics](18499.md), and the [left–right political spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left%E2%80%93right_political_spectrum) is the most common [political spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_spectrum).[^cite_note-16] The right includes [social conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatives), as well as *[laissez-faire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laissez-faire)* economic policies.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] "Right" and "right-wing" have been variously used as compliments and [pejoratives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pejorative) describing [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberal) and conservative economic and social ideas.[^cite_note-\:0-20]&#x20;

## Historical overview



[Joseph Maistre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_de_Maistre), a historically influential right-wing and conservative ideologue

[^cite_note-21]

The political terms [*Left* and *Right*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left%E2%80%93right_political_spectrum) were first used in the 18th century, during the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), referencing the seating arrangement of the new [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(French_Revolution\)). Those who sat to the right of the chair of the presiding officer (*le président*) were generally supportive of the institutions of the [monarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchism) [Old Regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancien_R%C3%A9gime).[^cite_note-Parliaments_1988_pp._287–302-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-Knapp-25] The original "Right" in France was formed in reaction to the "Left" and comprised those supporting hierarchy, tradition, and [clericalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clericalism).[^cite_note-Carlisle-5]<sup>: 693 </sup> The expression *la droite* ('the right') increased in use after the [restoration of the monarchy in 1815](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon_Restoration_in_France#Second_Restoration), when it was applied to the [ultra-royalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra-royalist).[^cite_note-26]&#x20;

[Edmund Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Burke) is regarded as one of the most influential conservative thinkers and political writers of the 18th century,[^cite_note-27] and his writings played a significant role in influencing opinions regarding conservatism following the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution) in 1789, and he remains a major figure in modern conservative and right-wing circles.[^cite_note-28] Burke postulated the importance of religious institutions for the moral stability and good of the state.[^cite_note-29] He expressed his views in a satirical book, *[A Vindication of Natural Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Vindication_of_Natural_Society)* (1756). He also criticized the actions of the British government towards the [American colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies), including its taxation policies. Burke supported the American colonists' right to resist metropolitan authority. He also supported [Catholic emancipation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_emancipation).&#x20;

While in the 19th century, Burke was praised by both conservatives and [liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism),[^cite_note-30] in the 20th century, Burke became widely regarded in the [United States](3434750.md) and the [United Kingdom](31717.md) as the philosophical founder of conservatism.[^cite_note-auto-31][^cite_note-32] along with his [ultra-royalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra-royalist) and [ultramontane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultramontanism) counterpart [Joseph de Maistre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_de_Maistre).[^cite_note-33] His publications influenced British conservative thought and helped establish the earliest foundations for modern conservatism.[^cite_note-34]&#x20;

The chief ideologue of French ultra-royalists, [Joseph de Maistre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_de_Maistre), who was lawyer, diplomat, and [political philosopher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_philosophy), is regarded as one of intellectual forefathers of modern [conservatism](6675.md).[^cite_note-35] Maistre was noted for his advocacy of [monarchism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchism) and [social stratification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stratification) in the period immediately following the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] Maistre is also regarded as one of the intellectual founders of the right-wing thought.[^cite_note-38]&#x20;

[William F. Buckley Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_F._Buckley_Jr.) was an American [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States) writer and [intellectual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_intellectual),[^cite_note-39] who in 1955, founded *[National Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Review)*, a magazine for the development of the conservative movement in the United States.[^cite_note-40] Buckley is widely considered to have been one of the most influential figures in the [conservative movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States) and right-wing politics in the United States.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]&#x20;

## Positions

### Anti-communism

Early [communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) used the term "right-wing" in reference to [conservatives](6675.md), placing the conservatives on the right, the [liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) in the [centre](39619035.md) and the communists on the [left](18499.md). Both the conservatives and the liberals were strongly [anti-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-communism), but the conservatives' anti-communism was much stronger than the liberals'. The history of the use of the term *right-wing* in reference to anti-communism is complicated.[^cite_note-43]&#x20;



[Anti-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-communism) propaganda poster depicting the [White movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement) which says "For a united Russia", 1919

By [World War I](4764461.md), in most [European monarchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchies_in_Europe), the [divine right of kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_right_of_kings) had become discredited and was replaced by [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) and [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) movements. Most European monarchs became figureheads, or they yielded some of their power to elected governments. The most conservative European monarchy, the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), was replaced by the communist [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). The [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution) inspired a series of other [communist revolutions across Europe from 1917–1923](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1917%E2%80%931923). Many of these revolutions, such as the [German Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Revolution), were crushed by nationalist and monarchist military units. During this period, nationalism was first considered right-wing, especially when it opposed the [internationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internationalism_\(politics\)) of the communists.[^cite_note-sticks-44][^cite_note-45]&#x20;

After [World War II](32927.md), communism became a global phenomenon and anti-communism became an integral part of the domestic and foreign policies of the [United States](3434750.md) and its [NATO](21133.md) allies. Conservatism in the post-war era abandoned most of its monarchist and aristocratic roots, shifting its focus to patriotism, religious values, and nationalism. Throughout the [Cold War](325329.md), postcolonial governments in [Asia](689.md), [Africa](5334607.md), and [Latin America](18524.md) turned to the United States for political and economic support. Communists were also enemies of capitalism, portraying [Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street) as the oppressor of the masses. The United States made anti-communism the top priority of its [foreign policy](7564.md), and many American conservatives sought to combat what they saw as communist influence at home. This situation led to the adoption of several domestic policies that are collectively referred to as *[McCarthyism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarthyism)*. While both liberals and conservatives were anti-communist, the followers of [Senator McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_McCarthy) were called *right-wing* and those on the right called liberals who favoured free speech, even free speech for communists, *leftist*.[^cite_note-sticks-44][^cite_note-46]&#x20;

Climate friendly policies are generally supported across the political spectrum, though there have been many exceptions among voters and politicians leaning towards the right, and even politicians on the left have rarely made addressing climate change a top priority.[^cite_note-53] In the 20th century, right wing politicians led much significant action against climate change, both internationally and domestically, with [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) and [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher) being prominent examples.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] Yet by the 1990s, especially in some English speaking countries and most especially in the US, the issue began to be polarized.[^cite_note-Politics_of_climate_change_Kamarck2019-56][^cite_note-Politics_of_climate_change_Dessler_2020_loc=_Chpt._1,_4,_5-57] Right wing media started arguing that climate change was being invented or at least exaggerated by the left to justify an expansion in the size of government.[^cite_note-58] As of 2020, some right wing governments have enacted increased climate friendly policies. Various surveys indicated a slight trend for even U.S. right wing voters to become less skeptical of global warming, and groups like American Conservation Coalition indicate young Republican voters embrace climate as a central policy field. Though in the view of Anatol Lieven, for some right wing US voters, being skeptical of climate change has become part of their identity, so their position on the matter cannot easily be shifted by rational argument.[^cite_note-Politics_of_climate_change_Lieven_2020_loc=_Passim,_esp._Chpt._1-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-Politics_of_climate_change_Unherd2021-61] [^cite_note-62]&#x20;

A 2014 study from the [University of Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_University_of_Dortmund) concluded that countries with center and left-wing governments had higher emission reductions than right-wing governments in OECD countries during 1992–2008.[^cite_note-Politics_of_climate_change_Garmann2014-63] Historically, nationalist governments have been among the worst performers in enacting policies. Though according to Lieven, as climate change is increasingly seen as a threat to the ongoing existence of nation states, nationalism is likely to become one of the most effective forces to drive determined mitigation efforts. The growing trend to [securitize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securitization_\(international_relations\)#As_a_tactic) the climate change threat may be especially effective for increasing support among nationalist and conservatives.[^cite_note-Politics_of_climate_change_Lieven_2020_loc=_Passim,_esp._Chpt._1-59][^cite_note-Politics_of_climate_change_PewSurvey_2020-48][^cite_note-Politics_of_climate_change_Dessler_2020_loc=_Chpt._1,_4,_5-57]&#x20;

A 2024 analysis found 100 U.S. representatives and 23 U.S. senators—23% of the 535 members of Congress—to be climate change deniers, all the deniers being Republicans.[^cite_note-Politics_of_climate_change_AmerProgress_20240718-64]&#x20;

### Economics

See also: [Fiscal conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiscal_conservatism)

In post-revolutionary France, the Right fought against the rising power of those who had grown rich through commerce, and sought to preserve the rights of the hereditary nobility. They were uncomfortable with capitalism, the Enlightenment, individualism, and industrialism, and fought to retain traditional social hierarchies and institutions.[^cite_note-Parliaments_1988_pp._287–302-22][^cite_note-Appleby-65] In Europe's history, there have been strong [collectivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectivism_and_individualism) right-wing movements, such as in the social Catholic right, that have exhibited hostility to all forms of [liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) (including [economic liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_liberalism)) and have historically advocated for paternalist class harmony involving an organic-hierarchical society where workers are protected while class hierarchy remains.[^cite_note-66]&#x20;

In the 19th century, the Right had shifted to support the newly rich in some European countries (particularly Britain) and instead of favouring the nobility over industrialists, favoured capitalists over the working class. Other right-wing movements—such as [Carlism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlism) in Spain and nationalist movements in France, Germany, and Russia—remained hostile to capitalism and industrialism. Nevertheless, a few right-wing movements—notably the French [Nouvelle Droite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouvelle_Droite), [CasaPound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CasaPound), and American [paleoconservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleoconservatism)—are often in opposition to capitalist ethics and the effects they have on society. These forces see capitalism and industrialism as infringing upon or causing the decay of social traditions or hierarchies that are essential for social order.[^cite_note-Fascism-67]&#x20;

#### *Laissez-faire* schools

See also: [Austrian school of economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_school_of_economics) and [Chicago school of economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_school_of_economics)







L–R: [Milton Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Friedman), [Ludwig von Mises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_von_Mises) and [Friedrich Hayek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Hayek), 20th century economists belonging to the [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_school_of_economics) and [Austrian schools of economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_school_of_economics)

In modern times, "right-wing" is sometimes used to describe [*laissez-faire* capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laissez-faire#capitalism). In Europe, capitalists formed alliances with the Right during their conflicts with workers after 1848. In 1871, the Austrian school came to be with the work of [Carl Menger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Menger), [Eugen von Böhm-Bawerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugen_von_B%C3%B6hm-Bawerk), [Friedrich von Wieser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_von_Wieser), and others,[^cite_note-68] originating from methodologically opposition to the [Historical school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_school_of_economics), in a dispute known as *[Methodenstreit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodenstreit)*.[^cite_note-69] The Austrian school opposition to be [heterodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heterodox_economics),[^cite_note-Boettke_and_Leeson-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] advocating strict adherence to [methodological individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodological_individualism), the concept that social phenomena result primarily from the motivations and actions of individuals along with their [self interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self_interest). Austrian-school theorists hold that economic theory should be exclusively derived from basic principles of human action.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-Mises_Action-75]&#x20;

In France, the Right's support of capitalism can be traced to the late 19th century.[^cite_note-Knapp-25] The so-called [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism) Right, popularised by [US President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_President) [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) and [UK Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Prime_Minister) [Margaret Thatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Thatcher), combines support for [free markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_markets), [privatisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privatisation), and [deregulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deregulation) with traditional right-wing support for social conformity.[^cite_note-Lukes-9]&#x20;

### Nationalism

Main article: [Nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism)



1940 postcard marking the 2600th anniversary of the [mythical foundation of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Jimmu). At the time, Japan was [governed by an ultranationalist political regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokkashugi).

In France, [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) was originally a left-wing and republican ideology.[^cite_note-76] After [the period of *boulangisme*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Ernest_Boulanger) and the [Dreyfus affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreyfus_affair), nationalism became a trait of the right wing.[^cite_note-77] Right-wing nationalists sought to define and defend a "true" national identity from elements which they believed were corrupting that identity.[^cite_note-Knapp-25] Some were [supremacists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supremacism), who in accordance with [scientific racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_racism) and [social Darwinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Darwinism) applied the concept of "[survival of the fittest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_of_the_fittest)" to [nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nations) and [races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(human_categorization\)).[^cite_note-78]&#x20;

Right-wing nationalism was influenced by [Romantic nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_nationalism) in which the state derives its political legitimacy from the organic unity of those who it governs. This generally includes the language, race, culture, religion, and customs of the nation, all of which were "born" within its culture. Linked with right-wing nationalism is [cultural conservatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_conservatism), which supports the preservation of the heritage of a nation or culture and often sees deviations from cultural norms as an existential threat.[^cite_note-79]&#x20;

In the 21st century, [neo-nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-nationalism) came to prominence after the [Cold War](325329.md) in the [Western world](21208200.md). It is typically associated with cultural conservatism, [populism](8354382.md), [anti-globalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-globalization_movement), and [nativism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativism_\(politics\)) and is [opposed to immigration](6491626.md). The [ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideology) takes historical association in determining membership in a nation, rather than [racial concepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism).[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]&#x20;

### Natural law and traditionalism

Right-wing politics typically justifies a hierarchical society based on [natural law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_law) or [tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tradition).[^cite_note-T._Alexander_Smith_2003._p._30-6][^cite_note-Allan_Cameron_pg._37-7][^cite_note-Fuchs,_D._1990._p._203-8][^cite_note-Lukes-9][^cite_note-Clark-10][^cite_note-autogenerated68-82]&#x20;

Traditionalism was advocated by a group of United States university professors (labelled the "New Conservatives" by the popular press) who rejected the concepts of [individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualism), [liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism), [modernity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernity), and [social progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_progress), seeking instead to promote what they identified as cultural and educational renewal.[^cite_note-83]&#x20;

### Populism

Main article: [Right-wing populism](8354382.md)









21st century right-wing populists seen from left to right, top to bottom: [Jair Bolsonaro](46603496.md), [Alice Weidel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Weidel), [Nigel Farage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Farage), [Jarosław Kaczyński](637440.md) and [Viktor Orbán](750582.md)

[Right-wing populism](8354382.md) is a combination of [civic-nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_nationalism), [cultural-nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_nationalism) and sometimes [ethno-nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_nationalism), [localism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localism_\(politics\)), along with [anti-elitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-elitism), using populist rhetoric to provide a critique of existing political institutions.[^cite_note-84] According to Margaret Canovan, a right-wing populist is "a charismatic leader, using the tactics of politicians' populism to go past the politicians and intellectual elite and appeal to the reactionary sentiments of the populace, often buttressing his claim to speak for the people by the use of referendums".[^cite_note-Canovan-85]&#x20;

In Europe, right-wing populism often takes the form of distrust of the [European Union](9317.md) (commonly known as [Euroscepticism](10037.md)), and of politicians in general, combined with [anti-immigrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-immigrant) rhetoric and a call for a return to traditional, national values.[^cite_note-86] Daniel Stockemer states, the radical right is, "Targeting immigrants as a threat to employment, security and cultural cohesion".[^cite_note-Daniel_Stockemer_2016-87]&#x20;

In the United States, the [Tea Party movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea_Party_movement) stated that the core beliefs for membership were the primacy of individual liberties as defined by the Constitution of the United States, preference for a small federal government, and respect for the rule of law. Some policy positions included opposition to illegal immigration and support for a strong national military force, the right to individual gun ownership, cutting taxes, reducing government spending, and balancing the budget.[^cite_note-88]&#x20;

In Indonesia, Islamic populism has a significant impact on right-wing politics.[^cite_note-Hadiz2018-89] This is largely due to the historical context which Islamic organisations had during the 1960s in destroying the Indonesian Communist Party.[^cite_note-Hadiz2018-89] Whilst the party is adopting democratic processes with neo-liberal market economies, socially pluralist positions are not necessarily adopted.[^cite_note-Hadiz2018-89] The Islamic populism in Indonesia has boosted its influence in 1998 after the demise of the Suharto authoritarian regime.[^cite_note-Hadiz2018-89] Islamic populism in Indonesia has similar properties with Islamic populist regimes like in the Middle East, Turkey and North Africa (MENA).[^cite_note-Hadiz2018-89] The emphasis on social justice, pluralism, equality and progressive agendas could be potentially mobilised by Islamic cultural resources.[^cite_note-Hadiz2018-89]&#x20;



Flag of the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md) in India

In India, supporters of the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md) (BJP) have more authoritarian, nativist, and populist ideas than other Indian citizens. Under Narendra Modi, populism is a core part of the party's ideology. The party's rhetoric reflects the idea is that the ordinary, "good" individuals are continuously under attack from "bad" political forces, the media, etc. Since Narendra Modi became the leader of the BJP, it has increasingly been considered a populist radical right party (PRR) and has also been considered a Hindu nationalist party.[^cite_note-AmmassariFossatiMcDonnell2023-90]&#x20;

American right-wing media outlets oppose sex outside marriage and [same-sex marriage](92656.md), and they sometimes reject scientific positions on [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) and other matters where science is perceived to disagree with the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible).[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]&#x20;

Outside the [West](21208200.md), the [Hindu nationalist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_nationalism) has attracted privileged groups which fear encroachment on their dominant positions, as well as "plebeian" and impoverished groups which seek recognition around a majoritarian rhetoric of cultural pride, order, and national strength.[^cite_note-97]&#x20;

### Social stratification



The American political philosopher [Russell Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Kirk) in 1962

Right-wing politics involves, to varying degrees, the rejection of some [egalitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egalitarianism) objectives of [left-wing politics](18499.md), based on the belief that [social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_inequality) or [economic inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality) is natural and inevitable or the belief that it is beneficial to society.[^cite_note-autogenerated68-82] Right-wing ideologies and movements support [social order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_order). The original French right-wing was called "the party of order" because it believed that France needed to be ruled by a strong political leader who would keep order.[^cite_note-Knapp-25]&#x20;

The conservative British scholar R. J. White, who rejects egalitarianism, wrote: "Men are equal before God and the laws, but they are unequal in all else; hierarchy is the order of nature, and privilege is the reward of honourable service".[^cite_note-autogenerated2003-106] American conservative [Russell Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Kirk) also rejected egalitarianism as imposing sameness, stating: "Men are created different; and a government that ignores this law becomes an unjust government for it sacrifices nobility to mediocrity".[^cite_note-autogenerated2003-106] Italian scholar [Norberto Bobbio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norberto_Bobbio) argued that the right-wing is inegalitarian compared to the left-wing, because he argued that equality is a relative, not an absolute, concept.[^cite_note-107]&#x20;

[Right-libertarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-libertarianism) reject [collective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_organization) or [state-imposed equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state) as undermining rewards for [personal merits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meritocracy), initiatives, and enterprise.[^cite_note-autogenerated2003-106] In their view, such imposed equality is unjust, limits personal freedom, and leads to social uniformity and mediocrity.[^cite_note-autogenerated2003-106]&#x20;

In the view of philosopher [Jason Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Stanley) in *[How Fascism Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_Fascism_Works)*, the "politics of hierarchy" is one of the hallmarks of [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism), which refers to a "glorious past" in which members of the rightfully dominant group sat atop the hierarchy, and attempt to recreate this state of being.[^cite_note-108]&#x20;

## History

According to *[The Cambridge History of Twentieth-Century Political Thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cambridge_History_of_Political_Thought)* (2003), the Right has gone through five distinct historical stages:[^cite_note-109]&#x20;

1. The reactionary right sought a return to [aristocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy) and [established religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion).

2. The moderate right [distrusted intellectuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-intellectualism) and advocated the establishment of limited governments.

3. The [radical right](221220.md) espoused a [romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_nationalism) and an [aggressive form of nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultranationalism).

4. The extreme right explicitly advocated the implementation of [anti-immigration](6491626.md) policies and it implicitly espoused [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism).

5. The [neo-liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberalism) right sought to combine a market economy and economic deregulation with the traditional right-wing beliefs in [patriotism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotism), elitism and law and order.

   [^cite_note-Clark-10]

From the 1830s to the 1880s, the [Western world](21208200.md)'s [social class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_class) structure and economy shifted from [nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobility) and [aristocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristocracy) towards [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism).[^cite_note-110] This shift affected [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics) movements such as the [British Conservative Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)), which responded supporting capitalism.[^cite_note-111]&#x20;

The populations of [English-speaking countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-speaking_countries) did not apply the terms *right* and *left* to their politics until the 20th century.[^cite_note-112] The term *right-wing* was originally applied to [traditional conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditionalist_conservatism), [monarchists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchism), and reactionaries; a revision of this which occurred sometime between the 1920s and 1950s considers the *[far-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_right-wing) to* denote [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism), [Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism), and [racial supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_supremacy).[^cite_note-113]&#x20;

Rightist regimes were common in Europe during the [Interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period), 1919–1938.[^cite_note-114]&#x20;

### Hungary

The dominance of the political right of [inter-war Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_between_the_two_world_wars), after the collapse of a short-lived Communist regime, was described by historian [István Deák](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istv%C3%A1n_De%C3%A1k):&#x20;

Between 1919 and 1944 Hungary was a rightist country. Forged out of a counter-revolutionary heritage, its governments advocated a ["nationalist Christian"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_nationalism) policy; they extolled heroism, faith, and unity; they despised the French Revolution, and they spurned the liberal and socialist ideologies of the 19th century. The governments saw Hungary as a bulwark against [bolshevism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevism) and bolshevism's instruments: [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism), [cosmopolitanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitanism), and [Freemasonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasonry). They perpetrated the rule of a small clique of aristocrats, civil servants, and army officers, and surrounded with adulation the head of the state, the counterrevolutionary [Admiral Horthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikl%C3%B3s_Horthy).

### India

In the United States, following the [Second World War](32927.md), social conservatives joined forces with right-wing elements of the [Republican Party](32070.md) to gain support among traditionally [Democratic](5043544.md) voting populations like [white southerners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_southerners) and [Catholics](410868.md). [Ronald Reagan](25433.md)'s election to the presidency in 1980 cemented the alliance between the [religious right in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_right_in_the_United_States) and social conservatives.[^cite_note-159]&#x20;

In 2019, the United States populace leaned [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics), with 37% of Americans self-identifying as [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States), compared to 35% moderate and 24% [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_liberalism_in_the_United_States). This was continuing a decades-long trend of the country leaning centre-right.[^cite_note-160]&#x20;

The [United States Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) defines right-wing extremism in the United States as "broadly divided into those groups, movements, and adherents that are primarily hate-oriented (based on hatred of particular religious, racial or ethnic groups), and those that are mainly anti-government, rejecting federal authority in favour of state or local authority, or rejecting government authority entirely. It may include groups and individuals that are dedicated to a single issue, such as opposition to abortion or immigration".[^cite_note-161]&#x20;

## Types

The meaning of right-wing "varies across societies, historical epochs, and political systems and ideologies".[^cite_note-162] According to *The Concise Oxford Dictionary of Politics*, in liberal democracies, the political right opposes [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism) and [social democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy). Right-wing parties include [conservatives](6675.md), [Christian democrats](328969.md), [classical liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_liberalism), and [nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism), as well as [fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) on the [far-right](221220.md).[^cite_note-163]&#x20;

British academics [Noël O'Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No%C3%ABl_O%27Sullivan) and [Roger Eatwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Eatwell) divide the right into five types: reactionary, moderate, radical, extreme, and new.[^cite_note-164] [Chip Berlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chip_Berlet) wrote that each of these "styles of thought" are "responses to the left", including liberalism and socialism, which have arisen since the 1789 French Revolution.[^cite_note-Berlet,_p._117-165]&#x20;

1. The reactionary right looks toward the past and is "aristocratic, religious and authoritarian".

   [^cite_note-Berlet,_p._117-165]

2. The [moderate right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics), typified by the writings of [Edmund Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Burke), is tolerant of change, provided it is gradual and accepts some aspects of liberalism, including the rule of law and capitalism, although it sees radical *[laissez-faire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laissez-faire)* and individualism as harmful to society. The moderate right often promotes nationalism and social welfare policies.

   [^cite_note-166]

3. [Radical right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_right_\(disambiguation\)) is a descriptive term that was developed after World War II and it was applied to groups and ideologies such as [McCarthyism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCarthyism), the [John Birch Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Birch_Society), [Thatcherism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thatcherism), and the [Republikaner Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republikaner_Party). Eatwell stresses that this usage of the term has "major typological problems" because it "has also been applied to clearly democratic developments".

   [^cite_note-167]

   &#x20;The radical right includes [right-wing populism](8354382.md) and various other subtypes.

   [^cite_note-Berlet,_p._117-165]

4. The [extreme right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_Right) has four traits: "1) [anti-democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-democracy), 2) [ultranationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultranationalism), 3) [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism), and 4) the strong state".

   [^cite_note-168]

5. The [New Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Right) consists of the [liberal conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_conservatism), who stress [small government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_government), [free markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_market), and individual initiative.

   [^cite_note-169]

Other authors make a distinction between the centre-right and the far-right.[^cite_note-170]&#x20;

- Parties of the centre-right generally support [liberal democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_democracy), [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism), the [market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy) (though they may accept government regulation to control monopolies), [private property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_property) rights, and a limited welfare state (for example, government provision of education and medical care). They support conservatism and economic liberalism and oppose socialism and communism.

- By contrast, the phrase "far-right" is used to describe those who favour an absolutist government, which uses the power of the state to support the dominant ethnic group or religion and criminalise other ethnic groups or religions.
