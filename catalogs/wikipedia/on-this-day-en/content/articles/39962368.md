**Nabil Fahmy** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): نبيل فهمي; born 5 January 1951) is an [Egyptian](31912046.md) diplomat and politician who has served as the [Secretary-General](32386686.md) of the [Arab League](52625.md) since July 2026. He also served as the [minister of foreign affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Minister_of_Egypt) of Egypt from June 2013 to July 2014.

## Early life and education

Nabil Fahmy was born in New York on 5 January 1951.[^cite_note-Blog-2][^cite_note-blscc-3] His father, [Ismail Fahmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_Fahmy), was [Anwar Sadat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_Sadat)'s foreign minister from 1973 to 1977.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-bbc16july-5]

He holds a Bachelor of Science degree in physics and mathematics and a master's degree in management, both of which he received from the [American University in Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_University_in_Cairo) in 1974 and 1976, respectively.[^cite_note-jmcns-6][^cite_note-weforum-7]

## Career

Fahmy is a career diplomat. He served in the Egyptian cabinet from 1974 to 1978 in various posts, including deputy foreign minister.[^cite_note-weforum-7] He also assumed the post of advisor to the [Vice President of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_Egypt) and was the secretary of the president for external communications from February 1974 to August 1976.[^cite_note-blscc-3] He worked at the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Egypt\)) in different capacities, including member of the Egyptian mission to the [United Nations Office at Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_at_Geneva) and New York and political advisor from August 1993 to September 1997.[^cite_note-acsis-8] He served as the Ambassador of Egypt to Japan from September 1997 to September 1999.[^cite_note-9] Next he served as the Ambassador of Egypt to the United States from October 1999 to September 2008.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-acsis-8]

From 1999 to 2003 he was also among the members of the UN Secretary General's advisory board on disarmament issues and he was appointed chairman of the board in 2001.[^cite_note-11] Upon returning to Cairo he was named [Ambassador-at-Large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador-at-Large) at the ministry.[^cite_note-acsis-8]

After leaving his diplomatic post, he entered politics. He was a member of the [Constitution Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Party_\(Egypt\)) headed by [Mohamed ElBaradei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_ElBaradei).[^cite_note-12] He also joined [The American University in Cairo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_University_in_Cairo) as a faculty member.[^cite_note-13] He is also the founding dean of university's school of public affairs.[^cite_note-aucairo-14][^cite_note-15] In addition, he worked as the dean at the faculty.[^cite_note-16] He was named non-resident chair of the Middle East project carried out by the James Martin center for nonproliferation studies in 2009.[^cite_note-jmcns-6] He was also a board member of McLarty associates.[^cite_note-17]

On 14 July 2013, he announced that he had accepted a proposal to become minister of foreign affairs in the [interim government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beblawi_Cabinet) of Egypt led by [Hazem Al Beblawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazem_Al_Beblawi).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] He accepted the post after [Mohamed Kamel Amr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Kamel_Amr) had declared his intention not to continue in the post.[^cite_note-egind-20] On 16 July Fahmy's term as foreign minister began.[^cite_note-bbc16july-5][^cite_note-21] Fahmy suspended his membership at the Constitution Party when he began to serve as foreign minister.[^cite_note-22]

In March 2026, Fahmy was nominated as the secretary-general of the [Arab League](52625.md), succeeding [Ahmed Aboul Gheit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Aboul_Gheit)[^cite_note-23] on 1 July 2026.[^cite_note-24]

## Personal life

Fahmy is married to Nermin Fahmy and has three children.[^cite_note-WashDiplomat-1][^cite_note-blscc-3] He publishes various articles in his blog at *[The Huffington Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Huffington_Post)*.[^cite_note-Blog-2] He is the author of *Egypt’s Diplomacy in War, Peace and Transition* which was published by [Palgrave Macmillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palgrave_Macmillan) in 2020.[^cite_note-25]

He was given an honorary PhD by the [Monterey Institute of International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterey_Institute_of_International_Studies), [Middlebury College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlebury_College), in May 2009.[^cite_note-aucairo-14]
