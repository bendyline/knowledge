# 2025 India–Pakistan floods

In August and September 2025, widespread flooding affected parts of eastern Pakistan and northern India. The floods were caused by particularly heavy monsoon rains in mountainous areas of the Indian state of [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md), and the Indian-administered union territory of [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md). The resulting flash floods forced Indian authorities to open dams on several rivers flowing into both Indian and [Pakistani Punjab](24751.md), leading to widespread flooding in downstream areas. Over 1400 villages in Pakistan's Punjab province have been flooded, while 1400 villages in the Indian state of Punjab were also affected,[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-\:0-2] displacing over 1.2 million people in Pakistan and 300,000 in India.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-\:0-2]&#x20;

## Regions affected

Heavy rains had been falling in Indian-administered Kashmir throughout August, with a [massive cloudburst in Kishtwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Kishtwar_district_flash_flood) on 14 August, killing 68 people. In late August, more rivers in Jammu and Kashmir began to rise. Two days later, a cloudburst hit [Kathua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathua), leaving 7 dead and several injured.[^cite_note-4] On 25 August, the Indian government notified Pakistan of water releases along dams on the [Tawi river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawi_River), which flows through [Jammu](43066157.md) into [Sialkot district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sialkot_District) of Punjab, Pakistan.[^cite_note-5]&#x20;

Pakistan then evacuated at least 125,000 people from northeastern regions of the province.[^cite_note-6] In Kasur, 75,000 people were evacuated while in Bahawalnagar along the Sutlej, 35,000 people were evacuated. [Jammu city](43066157.md) was then affected by flooding, which displaced at least 3500 people.[^cite_note-7] The [Sutlej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutlej) and [Beas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beas) rose rapidly by August 27, causing Indian Punjab and [Jammu Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_Division) to declare schools closed. 13 districts were initially affected, all the border districts as well as districts along the Beas and Sutlej.[^cite_note-8]&#x20;

These floods also affected Punjab and its neighbouring states like [Himachal Pardesh](14190.md), [Jammu & Kashmir](61449812.md), [Uttarakhand](1429154.md), [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh), [Bihar](442482.md), [Chhattisgarh](47734.md), [West Bengal](34040.md) and also in state of [Punjab in Pakistan](24751.md).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]&#x20;

### Punjab



Flooding at Hussainwala Ganda Singh Wala near the international border during the 2025 flood

In August 2025, around 1400 villages in more than 13 districts of [Punjab, India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India) faced a devastating [flood](50482.md) crisis, regarded as the worst in nearly four decades since 1988.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] There were about 55 fatalities.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] The floods, caused by unusually heavy [monsoon](57630.md) rains in the upper catchment areas (particularly [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md) and [Jammu & Kashmir](61449812.md)), along with surplus water releases from several dams — notably [Pong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pong_Dam), [Ranjit Sagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranjit_Sagar_Dam), and [Bhakra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhakra_Dam) — which intensified flooding in downstream districts, particularly along the [Ravi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_River), [Beas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beas_River) and [Sutlej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutlej) rivers.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]&#x20;

The severely affected districts of Punjab were [Gurdaspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurdaspur_district), [Amritsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar_district), [Ferozepur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferozepur), [Pathankot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathankot_district), [Kapurthala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapurthala_district) and [Fazilka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fazilka_district). Other districts like [Tarn Taran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarn_Taran_district), [Hoshiarpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoshiarpur_district), [Kapurthala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapurthala_district), [Rupnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupnagar_district), [Moga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moga_district), [Sangrur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangrur_district), [Barnala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnala), [Patiala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patiala) and SAS Nagar (Mohali) also been heavily impacted by the floods suffering crop damage, displacement, and infrastructure loss.[^cite_note-24]&#x20;

Over 1,400 villages across Punjab were inundated, and more than 3,71,475 acres of farmland were submerged, particularly in districts such as [Gurdaspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurdaspur_district), [Kapurthala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapurthala_district), [Ferozepur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferozepur), [Pathankot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathankot_district), [Fazilka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fazilka), [Amritsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar_district) and [Barnala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnala_district).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The floods affected approximately 3.5 lakh residents in the state with more than 29 dead,[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] prompting large-scale evacuations, widespread disruption and school closures.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] About 30 km of fencing along the zero line on the Indo-Pak international border got damaged. The BSF post near the Kartarpur Corridor got submerged, and BSF personnel temporarily relocated to Gurdwara Darbar Sahib in [Dera Baba Nanak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dera_Baba_Nanak_Assembly_constituency).[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]&#x20;

## Special Health Campaign

The Punjab state government (India) launched a clean-up and fogging drive in flood-hit livestock shelters to protect around 253,000 animals across 713 villages from water-borne and vector-borne diseases. Measures included the distribution of disinfectants such as potassium permanganate, mass vaccination campaigns against hemorrhagic septicaemia, and emergency veterinary care.[^cite_note-35]&#x20;

## Response and relief efforts in Punjab

### Rescue operations

- National and state-level agencies — [NDRF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Disaster_Response_Force), SDRF, Army, BSF, and local NGO's and authorities—mounted extensive rescue operations. In Gurdaspur, the Army evacuated 27 individuals via helicopter airlift from Lassian.

  &#x20;Around 11,330 persons were evacuated to safer places with the combined efforts of the Army, NDRF, BSF and the district authorities.

  [^cite_note-37]

  &#x20;Drones were deployed to deliver medicines and food in remote areas, and amphibious vehicles facilitated evacuations in Ramdas, Amritsar.

  [^cite_note-38]

  &#x20;Relief camps provided shelter to thousands of displaced residents.
