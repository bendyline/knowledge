**Lake Ontario** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): ***Lac Ontario***) is one of the five [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) of North America. It is bounded on the north, west, and southwest by the [Canadian province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_province) of [Ontario](22218.md), and on the south and east by the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [New York](8210131.md). The [Canada–United States border](630260.md) spans the centre of the lake. On the Canadian side, the major cities are [Kingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_Ontario), [Mississauga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississauga), [Toronto](64646.md), [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario), and [St. Catharines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Catharines). On the American side, the major cities are [Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York) and [Watertown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watertown,_New_York).

As the last in the Great Lakes chain of freshwater lakes, Lake Ontario serves as the outlet to the Atlantic Ocean via the [St. Lawrence River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_River), comprising the western end of the [St. Lawrence Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_Seaway). Its primary inlet is the [Niagara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_River) from [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie). The Long Sault control dam, primarily along with the [Moses-Saunders Power Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses-Saunders_Power_Dam) regulates the water level of the lake.[^cite_note-10]

## Name

See also: [Lake Ontario naming dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ontario_naming_dispute)

The French explorer [Samuel de Champlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_de_Champlain) named the lake "Lac S. Louis" (in English: Lake Saint Louis) in 1632, a name that remained in use until the end of the 17th century.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

The name Ontario is likely derived from the [Iroquoian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iroquoian) word *Kaniatarí\:io* or *Oniatarí\:io* meaning "lake of shining waters" or "beautiful lake".[^cite_note-13] It has been in use since at least 1641.[^cite_note-\:0-14] During [French colonial Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_\(New_France\)), various other names for the lake were also used, including Frontenac and St. Louis, though Ontario ultimately became the common term.[^cite_note-\:0-14]

Amidst [an ongoing trade war with Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%932026_United_States_trade_war_with_Canada), United States president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) signed an [executive order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_order_\(United_States\)) in August 2026 directing US federal agencies to [refer to the lake as *Lake America*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ontario_naming_dispute).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The name was rejected by Canadian officials[^cite_note-17] and numerous US state officials,[^cite_note-18] but was adopted by a number of private companies offering mapping services, including [Apple](856.md) and [Google](1092923.md), when serving their U.S. users.

## Geography



Lake Ontario's [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin)

Lake Ontario is the easternmost of the Great Lakes and the smallest in surface area (19,000 square kilometres \[7,340 mi<sup>2</sup>]),[^cite_note-EPAphysical-5] of which about 10,000 square kilometres (3,980 mi<sup>2</sup>) is in Canada and 9,000 square kilometres (3,560 mi<sup>2</sup>) in the United States.[^cite_note-CSDEBR1927-7] Lake Ontario exceeds [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie) in volume (1,640 km<sup>3</sup> \[393 mi<sup>3</sup>] to 480 km<sup>3</sup> \[116 cu mi]). It is the 13th-largest lake in the world. When its islands are included, the lake's shoreline is about 1,146 kilometres (712 mi) long. As the last lake in the Great Lakes' hydrologic chain, Lake Ontario has the lowest mean surface elevation of the lakes at 74 metres (243 ft)[^cite_note-EPA-6] above sea level; 99 metres (326 ft) lower than its neighbor upstream. Its maximum length is 311 [kilometres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilometre); 168 [nautical miles](21289.md) (193 [statute miles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_mile)), and its maximum width is 85 kilometres; 46 nautical miles (53 mi).[^cite_note-EPA-6] The lake's average depth is 47 [fathoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fathom) 1 foot (86 metres \[283 ft]), with a maximum depth of 133 fathoms 4 feet (244 metres \[802 ft]) in the [Rochester Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_Basin).[^cite_note-EPA-6][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWright200664-8]

The lake's primary source is the Niagara River, draining Lake Erie, with the Saint Lawrence River serving as the outlet. The [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) covers 64,030 square kilometres (24,720 mi<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-EPAphysical-5][^cite_note-19] As with all the Great Lakes, water levels change both within the year (owing to seasonal changes in water input) and among years (owing to longer-term trends in precipitation). These water level fluctuations are an integral part of [lake ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_ecosystem) and help create and maintain extensive wetlands.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] The lake also has an important freshwater fishery, although it has been negatively affected by factors including [overfishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overfishing), water pollution and [invasive species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasive_species_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-Christie,_W\._J._1974-22]

[Baymouth bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baymouth_bar) built by prevailing winds and currents have created a significant number of [lagoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagoon) and sheltered harbours, mostly near (but not limited to) [Prince Edward County, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward_County,_Ontario), and the easternmost shores. Perhaps the best-known example is [Toronto Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Harbour), chosen as the site of the [Upper Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Canada) capital for its strategic harbour. Other prominent examples include [Hamilton Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Harbour), [Irondequoit Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irondequoit_Bay), [Presqu'ile Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presqu'ile_Provincial_Park), and [Sodus Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodus_Bay). The bars themselves are the sites of long beaches, such as [Sandbanks Provincial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandbanks_Provincial_Park) and [Sandy Island Beach State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandy_Island_Beach_State_Park). These sand bars are often associated with large [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland), which support large numbers of plant and animal species, as well as providing important rest areas for migratory birds.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] Presqu'ile, on the north shore of Lake Ontario, is particularly significant in this regard. One unique feature of the lake is the Z-shaped [Bay of Quinte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_of_Quinte) which separates Prince Edward County from the Ontario mainland, save for a 3.2-kilometre (2 mi) isthmus near Trenton; this feature also supports many wetlands and aquatic plants, as well as associated fisheries.

Major rivers draining into Lake Ontario include the [Niagara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_River), [Don River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River_\(Toronto\)), [Humber River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humber_River_\(Ontario\)), [Rouge River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rouge_River_\(Ontario\)), [Trent River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent_River_\(Ontario\)), [Cataraqui River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cataraqui_River), [Genesee River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genesee_River), [Oswego River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswego_River_\(New_York\)), [Black River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_River_\(New_York\)), [Little Salmon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Salmon_River_\(Lake_Ontario\)), [Credit River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_River), [Ganaraska River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganaraska_River) and the [Salmon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon_River_\(New_York\)).

On the Canadian side, the [Welland Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welland_Canal) connects Lakes Ontario and Erie[^cite_note-25] and is used for maritime navigation between the two lakes.[^cite_note-26]

## Geology

The lake basin was carved out of soft, weak [Silurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silurian)-age rocks by the [Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_glaciation) ice sheet during the [last ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period). The action of the ice occurred along the pre-glacial [Ontarian River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontarian_River) valley, which had approximately the same orientation as today's basin. [Material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Till) that was pushed southward by the ice sheet left landforms such as [drumlins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drumlin), [kames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kame), and [moraines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moraine), both on the modern land surface and the lake bottom,[^cite_note-27] reorganizing the region's entire drainage system. As the ice sheet retreated toward the north, it still dammed the St. Lawrence Valley outlet, so the lake surface was at a higher level. This stage is known as [Lake Iroquois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_Lake_Iroquois). During that time, the lake drained through present-day [Syracuse, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_New_York), into the [Mohawk River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohawk_River), thence to the [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River) and the Atlantic. The shoreline created during this stage can be easily recognized by the (now dry) beaches and wave-cut hills 16 to 40 kilometres (10 to 25 mi) from the present shoreline.

When the ice finally receded from the St. Lawrence valley, the outlet was below [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level), and for a short time, the lake became a [bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_sea_\(geology\)) of the Atlantic Ocean, in association with the [Champlain Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champlain_Sea). Gradually the land [rebounded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-glacial_rebound) from the release of the weight of about 2,000 metres (6,500 ft) of ice that had been stacked on it. It is still rebounding about 30 centimetres (12 in) per century in the St. Lawrence area. Since the ice receded from the area last, the most rapid rebound still occurs there. This means the lake bed is gradually tilting southward, inundating the south shore and [turning river valleys into bays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ria). Both north and south shores experience shoreline erosion, but the tilting amplifies this effect on the south shore, causing loss to property owners.

## Climate



[Hamilton Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Harbour) frozen over. Ice sheets can form along the shoreline of Lake Ontario during the winter.

The lake has a natural [seiche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seiche) rhythm of eleven minutes. The seiche effect normally is only about 1.9 centimetres (3⁄4 in) but can be greatly amplified by earth movement, winds, and atmospheric pressure changes. Because of its great depth, the lake as a whole does not completely freeze in winter, but an ice sheet covering between 10% and 90% of the lake area typically develops, depending on the severity of the winter. Ice sheets typically form along the shoreline and in slack water bays, where the lake is not as deep. During the winters of 1877 and 1878, the ice sheet coverage was up to 95–100% of the lake. [During the War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engagements_on_Lake_Ontario#Operations_in_1812), the ice cover was stable enough the American naval commander stationed at [Sackets Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sackets_Harbor,_New_York) feared a British attack from Kingston, over the ice. The lake has completely frozen over on five recorded occasions: in 1830,[^cite_note-28] 1874, 1893, 1912, and 1934.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMay2008-29]

When the cold winds of winter pass over the warmer water of the lake, they pick up moisture and drop it as [lake-effect snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake-effect_snow). Since the prevailing winter winds are from the northwest, the southern and southeastern shoreline of the lake is referred to as the [snowbelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowbelt). In some winters, the area between Oswego and [Pulaski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulaski,_New_York) may receive twenty or more feet (600 cm) of snowfall. Also impacted by lake-effect snow is the [Tug Hill Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tug_Hill), an area of elevated land about 32 kilometres (20 mi) east of Lake Ontario. The "Hill", as it is often referred to, typically receives more snow than any other region in the [eastern United States](509486.md). As a result, Tug Hill is a popular location for winter enthusiasts, such as snow-mobilers and cross-country skiers. Lake-effect snow often extends inland as far as [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_New_York), with that city often recording the most winter snowfall accumulation of any large city in the United States. Other cities in the world receive more snow annually, such as [Quebec City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_City), which averages 340 centimetres (135 in), and [Sapporo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapporo), Japan, which receives 480 centimetres (189 in) each year and is often regarded as the snowiest city in the world.

Foggy conditions (particularly in fall) can be created by thermal contrasts and can be an impediment for recreational boaters. Lake breezes in spring tend to slow fruit bloom until the frost danger is past, and in the autumn delay the onset of fall frost, particularly on the south shore. Cool onshore winds also slow the early bloom of plants and flowers until later in the spring season, protecting them from possible frost damage. Such [microclimatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microclimate) effects have enabled tender fruit production in a continental climate, with the southwest shore supporting a major fruit-growing area. [Apples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple), [cherries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry), [pears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pear), [plums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plum), and [peaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peach) are grown in many commercial orchards around Rochester. Between [Stoney Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoney_Creek,_Ontario) and [Niagara-on-the-Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara-on-the-Lake) on the [Niagara Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_Peninsula) is a major fruit-growing and wine-making area. The [wine-growing region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_Escarpment_AVA) extends over the international border into [Niagara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niagara_County,_New_York) and [Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orleans_County,_New_York) counties in New York. Apple varieties that tolerate a more extreme climate are grown on the lake's north shore, around [Cobourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobourg).

## Ecology



Profile of coastal wetlands for Lake Ontario

The Great Lakes watershed is a region of high biodiversity, and Lake Ontario is important for its diversity of birds, fish, reptiles, amphibians, and plants. Many of these special species are associated with shorelines, particularly sand dunes, lagoons, and wetlands. The importance of wetlands to the lake has been appreciated, and many of the larger wetlands have protected status. These wetlands are changing, partly because the natural water level fluctuations have been reduced. Many wetland plants are dependent upon low water levels to reproduce.[^cite_note-30] When water levels are stabilized, the area and diversity of the marsh is reduced. This is particularly true of meadow marsh (also known as [wet meadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_meadow) wetlands); for example, in Eel Bay near Alexandria Bay, regulation of lake levels has resulted in large losses of wet meadow.[^cite_note-31] Often this is accompanied by the invasion of [cattails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typha), which displace many of the native plant species and reduce plant diversity. [Eutrophication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eutrophication) may accelerate this process by providing nitrogen and phosphorus for the more rapid growth of competitively dominant plants.[^cite_note-32] Similar effects are occurring on the north shore, in wetlands such as Presqu'ile, which have interdunal wetlands called [pannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_pannes_and_pools), with high plant diversity and many unusual plant species.[^cite_note-33]



Lake Ontario's food web

Most of the forests around the lake are [deciduous forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_deciduous_forest) dominated by trees including maple, oak, beech, ash and basswood. These are classified as part of the [Mixedwood Plains Ecozone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixedwood_Plains_Ecozone_\(Canada\)) by [Environment and Climate Change Canada](272329.md), or as the [Eastern Great Lakes and Hudson Lowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Great_Lakes_and_Hudson_Lowlands_\(ecoregion\)) by the [United States Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md), or as the Great Lakes Ecoregion by The Nature Conservancy.[^cite_note-34] Deforestation in the vicinity of the lake has had many negative impacts,[^cite_note-35] including loss of forest birds, extinction of native salmon, and increased amounts of sediment flowing into the lake. In some areas, more than 90 per cent of the [forest cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_cover) has been removed and replaced by agriculture. Certain tree species, such as hemlock, have also been particularly depleted by past logging activity.[^cite_note-36] Guidelines for restoration stress the importance of maintaining and restoring forest cover, particularly along streams and wetlands.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

By the 1960s and 1970s, the increased pollution caused frequent [algal blooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algal_bloom) to occur in the summer.[^cite_note-Christie,_W\._J._1974-22] These blooms killed large numbers of fish, and left decomposing piles of filamentous algae and dead fish along the shores.[^cite_note-39]

### Water pollution

Lake Ontario is the most downstream lake of the Great Lakes, so the pollution from all the other lakes flows into it. Lake Ontario was ranked as the most environmentally stressed amongst the five Great Lakes in a 2015 ecological study. Some of the stresses on the lake include excess application of fertilizers in agriculture running into the lake, spillover from obsolete municipal [sewage systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_systems), toxic chemicals from industries along the rivers that drain into the lake, and metropolitan drainage from big cities like Toronto, Rochester, and Hamilton.[^cite_note-40]

[Randle Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randle_Reef), the westernmost part of Lake Ontario, has been identified as one of the most contaminated areas on Lake Ontario alongside other [areas of concern on Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Areas_of_Concern#List_of_areas_of_concern). However, a \$150-million clean-up project had begun in 2016 and is expected to be completed by 2025.[^cite_note-41]

## Human history

The first archaeological evidence of humans in the Lake Ontario region dates to circa 11,000 BP. These peoples were mobile foragers who based much of their movement and settlement on the seasonal migrations of caribou, but who also used smaller animals and plant resources.[^cite_note-42] These early settlers likely inhabited the lake margins, but those early lake shores are now either far from the current lake representing earlier, higher waters, or submerged under the modern lake, representing periods of lower lake levels. Lake Ontario reached its modern level circa 4000 BP.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] The lake was a border between the [Wendat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyandot_people) people and the [Haudenosaunee Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haudenosaunee) (Iroquois) in the [pre-Columbian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era). In the 17th century, the Haudenosaunee committed genocide against the Wendat in southern Ontario and [settled the northern shores of Lake Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haudenosaunee_settlement_of_the_north_shore_of_Lake_Ontario).[^cite_note-45] When the Haudenosaunee withdrew and the [Anishinaabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anishinaabe) moved in from the north to southern Ontario, they retained the Iroquoian name.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESmith198710-46]



A map depicting the [Haudenosaunee settlement of the north shore of Lake Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haudenosaunee_settlement_of_the_north_shore_of_Lake_Ontario) during the late-17th century

It is believed the first European to reach the lake was [Étienne Brûlé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89tienne_Br%C3%BBl%C3%A9) in 1615. European colonization of Lake Ontario was slow and uneven due to the power and organization of the Haudenosaunee, Wendat, and Ojibwe nations. From 1688 to 1694, the Haudenosaunee largely excluded the French from Lake Ontario.[^cite_note-47]

As was their practice, the French explorers introduced other names for the lake. In 1632 and 1656, the lake was referred to as Lac de St. Louis or Lake St. Louis by [Samuel de Champlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_de_Champlain) and cartographer [Nicolas Sanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Sanson), respectively.[^cite_note-48] In 1660, [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) historian [Francis Creuxius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Creuxius?action=edit\&redlink=1) coined the name *Lacus Ontarius*. In a map drawn in the *Relation des Jésuites* (1662–1663), the lake bears the legend *"Lac Ontario ou des Iroquois"* with the name *"Ondiara"* in smaller type. A French map produced in 1712 (currently in the [Canadian Museum of History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Museum_of_History)[^cite_note-49]), created by military engineer [Jean-Baptiste de Couagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_de_Couagne?action=edit\&redlink=1), identified Lake Ontario as "Lac Frontenac" named after Louis de Buade, Comte de Frontenac et de Palluau. He was a French soldier, courtier, and Governor General of [New France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_France) from 1672 to 1682 and from 1689 to his death in 1698.

In the 17th century, sightings of an alleged creature named [Gaasyendietha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaasyendietha), similar to the so-called [Loch Ness Monster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loch_Ness_Monster), were reported in the lake. The creature is described as large with a long neck, green in colour, and generally causes a break in the surface waves.[^cite_note-MorningPost-50][^cite_note-GlasgowHerald-51][^cite_note-52]

A series of trading posts were established by both the British and French, such as [Fort Frontenac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Frontenac) in 1673, [Fort Oswego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Oswego) in 1722, and [Fort Rouillé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Rouill%C3%A9) in 1750. As the easternmost and nearest lake to the Atlantic seaboard of Canada and the United States, population centres here are among the oldest in the Great Lakes basin, with [Kingston, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_Ontario), formerly the [capital of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Canada), dating to the establishment of Fort Frontenac in 1673.&#x20;



[USS *General Pike*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_General_Pike) and [HMS *Wolfe*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Wolfe_\(1813\)) prepare for action on September 28, 1813. The battle was one of several [engagements that took place on Lake Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engagements_on_Lake_Ontario) during the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812).

After the [French and Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_War), all forts around the lake were under British control. During the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution), the British controlled Lake Ontario from their bases at [Carleton Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carleton_Island), [Navy Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy_Hall), and [Oswego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswego,_New_York), allowing them to largely exclude the Americans from the lake.[^cite_note-53] The United States took possession of the forts along the American side of the lake at the signing of the [Jay Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Treaty) in 1794. Permanent, non-military European settlement began during the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution) with the influx of [Loyalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Empire_Loyalists) settlers.

Following the American Revolution, sailing commerce slowly returned to Lake Ontario. There was one private commercial vessel, the *Good Intent*, on the lake in 1788. Several more vessels were launched prior to 1800, primarily by Canadian merchants.[^cite_note-54] Commerce continued to expand until the [Embargo of 1807](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embargo_Act_of_1807). There was significant smuggling across Lake Ontario during the embargo.[^cite_note-55]

During the War of 1812, the Royal Navy and US Navy operated substantial shipyards at Kingston, Ontario, and [Sackets Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sackets_Harbor,_New_York), New York, respectively, and [fought in several engagements for control of Lake Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engagements_on_Lake_Ontario). The Great Lakes, including Lake Ontario, were largely demilitarized after the [Rush–Bagot Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rush%E2%80%93Bagot_Treaty) was ratified in 1818. As a result, most of the naval vessels were sold or abandoned.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

The lake became a hub of commercial activity following the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812) with canal building on both sides of the border and significant sailing and steamer commerce.

Sailing commerce on Lake Ontario was initially significant but declined relative to the other Great Lakes after the mid-19th century as the centres of colonialist agriculture, timbering, and mining moved farther west.[^cite_note-58] After the [Erie Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_Canal) opened, much of the movement of people and commodities between the East Coast and the Great Lakes circumvented Lake Ontario. [Oswego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswego,_New_York) remained an important port on Lake Ontario during this time because the [Oswego Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswego_Canal) allowed shippers to connect to the Erie Canal while avoiding bottlenecks at Buffalo, New York.[^cite_note-59] [Kingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston,_Ontario), [Clayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clayton,_New_York), and [Oswego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswego,_New_York) were major 19th century wooden shipbuilding centres on Lake Ontario.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] Oil and coal were important commodities shipped on Lake Ontario through the early 20th century.[^cite_note-62] In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, a type of scow known as a *stone hooker* was in operation on the northwest shore, particularly around [Port Credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Credit,_Ontario) and [Bronte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronte,_Ontario). [Stonehooking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonehooking) was the practice of raking flat fragments of Dundas [shale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale) from the shallow lake floor of the area for use in construction, particularly in the growing city of Toronto.[^cite_note-63]

The *[Frontenac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PS_Frontenac)* was the first steamer on Lake Ontario and the Great Lakes. This ship was launched in September 1816 from Finkle's Point (now [Bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyalist,_Ontario)) near Kingston, ON. *Frontenac* was constructed by two shipbuilders who had worked for the Americans during the War of 1812 and was captained by a former Royal Navy sailing master.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-\:02-65] The Americans at Sackets Harbor launched a smaller steamship, the *[Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_\(steamboat\)), in* March 1817.[^cite_note-\:02-65][^cite_note-\:1-66]

As bulk cargoes became increasingly important in Great Lakes commerce and the number of improved harbours increased, so did propeller ships.[^cite_note-\:1-66][^cite_note-\:2-67] The number of propeller ships exceeded the number of side-wheel steamers by circa 1857.[^cite_note-68] The *Vandalia* was the first propeller ship on the Great Lakes and the second in the United States. Launched at Oswego in 1841, *Vandalia*'s propeller was designed by [John Ericsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ericsson).[^cite_note-\:1-66][^cite_note-\:2-67] Steamer activity peaked in the mid-19th century before competition from railway lines.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

Iron hull construction developed on Lake Ontario during the mid-19th century but did not become widespread until the early 20th century. The first iron-hulled ship on the Great Lakes, the British paddle-wheel gunboat HMS *Mohawk*, was launched at Kingston in 1843. The *Passport* and the *Magnet*, both built for the Royal Mail Line, were launched at Kingston in 1846 and at Niagara in 1847, respectively.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

### Swims across the lake



Marilyn Bell Park in Toronto

As of 2026, more than 60 people have successfully swum across the lake.[^cite_note-73] The first person who accomplished the feat was a Canadian long-distance swimmer [Marilyn Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marilyn_Bell), who did it in 1954 at age 16. Toronto's Marilyn Bell Park is named in her honour. The park opened in 1984 and is east of the spot where Bell completed her swim.[^cite_note-74] In 1974, [Diana Nyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diana_Nyad) became the first person who swam across the lake against the current (from north to south).[^cite_note-75] On August 28, 2007, 14-year-old Natalie Lambert from Kingston, Ontario, made the swim, leaving Sackets Harbor, New York, and reaching Kingston's Confederation basin less than 24 hours after she entered the lake.[^cite_note-76] On August 19, 2012, 14-year-old [Annaleise Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annaleise_Carr) became the youngest person to swim across the lake. She completed the 52-kilometre (32 mi) crossing from Niagara-on-the-Lake to Marilyn Bell Park in just under 27 hours.[^cite_note-77]

## Settlements

A large [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation) called the [Golden Horseshoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horseshoe) occupies the lake's westernmost shores, anchored by the cities of Toronto and Hamilton. Ports on the Canadian side include St. Catharines, Oshawa, Cobourg and Kingston, near the St. Lawrence River outlet. Close to 9 million people, or over a quarter of Canada's population, live within the watershed of Lake Ontario. The American shore is largely rural, with the exception of Rochester and the much smaller ports at Oswego and Sackets Harbor. The city of Syracuse is 64 kilometres (40 mi) inland, connected to the lake by the New York State Canal System. Over 2 million people live in Lake Ontario's American watershed.

## Navigation



Canal Lock 1 at the [Welland Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welland_Canal) in Lake Ontario. The canal forms a part of the [Great Lakes Waterway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Waterway), which connects Lake Ontario with the [St. Lawrence River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_River) and other Great Lakes.

The [Great Lakes Waterway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Waterway) connects the lake sidestream to the Atlantic Ocean via the Saint Lawrence Seaway and upstream to the other rivers in the chain via the [Welland Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welland_Canal) and to Lake Erie. The [Trent-Severn Waterway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent-Severn_Waterway) for pleasure boats connects Lake Ontario at the Bay of Quinte to [Georgian Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Bay) (Lake Huron) via [Lake Simcoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Simcoe). The [Oswego Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswego_Canal) connects the lake at Oswego to the [New York State Canal System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State_Canal_System), with outlets to the Hudson River, Lake Erie, and [Lake Champlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Champlain). The [Rideau Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rideau_Canal), also for pleasure boats, connects Lake Ontario at Kingston to the Ottawa River in downtown [Ottawa](22219.md), Ontario.

Several lighthouses exist throughout the lake to help with navigation. Notable historic examples include:

- [Braddock Point Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braddock_Point_Light)

- [Charlotte-Genesee Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte-Genesee_Lighthouse)

- [Gibraltar Point Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar_Point_Lighthouse)

- [Oswego Harbor West Pierhead Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswego_Harbor_West_Pierhead_Light)

- [Presqu'ile Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presqu'ile_Provincial_Park)

- [Selkirk Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selkirk_Lighthouse)

- [Sodus Point Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodus_Point_Light)

- [Stony Point Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stony_Point_\(Henderson\)_Light)

- [Thirty Mile Point Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Mile_Point_Light)

A land-based trail that roughly follows the lake's shoreline also exists, the [Great Lakes Circle Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Circle_Tour) and [Seaway Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaway_Trail). The designated scenic road systems connects all of the Great Lakes and the St. Lawrence River.[^cite_note-78] As the Seaway Trail is posted on the US side only, Lake Ontario is the only of the five [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) to have no posted bi-national circle tour.

## National marine sanctuary



A 2024 map of the [Lake Ontario National Marine Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ontario_National_Marine_Sanctuary)

The [Lake Ontario National Marine Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ontario_National_Marine_Sanctuary) covers 1,300 square nautical miles; 4,460 square kilometres (1,722 sq mi) in US waters in southeastern Lake Ontario. Designated on September 6, 2024, the [national marine sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Marine_Sanctuary) protects historic [shipwrecks](272778.md) and an area of great cultural, historical, and spiritual importance to the [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) peoples of the [Haudenosaunee Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haudenosaunee_Confederacy). The [National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration](37876.md)'s [Office of National Marine Sanctuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_National_Marine_Sanctuaries) and the [Government of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_New_York_\(state\)) jointly administer the sanctuary.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

## Economic impact



Cargo ships in Oshawa Harbour

Lake Ontario is the site of several major commercial ports including the [Port of Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Toronto) and the [Port of Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton-Oshawa_Port_Authority). [Hamilton Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Harbour) has major steel production facilities.

The [government of Ontario](2670174.md), which holds the lakebed rights of the Canadian portion of the lake under the Beds of Navigable Waters Act,[^cite_note-trilliumsco-81] does not permit [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power) to be generated offshore.[^cite_note-tsoffshore-82] In *[Trillium Power Wind Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trillium_Power_Wind_1) v. Ontario (Natural Resources)*,[^cite_note-trilliumsco-81] the [Superior Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Superior_Court_of_Justice) held Trillium Power—since 2004 an "Applicant of Record" who had invested \$35,000 in fees and, when in 2011 the Crown made a policy decision against offshore windfarms, claimed an injury of \$2.25 billion—disclosed no reasonable cause of action.

The Great Lakes once supported an industrial-scale fishery, with record hauls in 1899; overfishing later blighted the industry.[^cite_note-glearb-83] However, only recreational fishing activities exist in the 21st century.
