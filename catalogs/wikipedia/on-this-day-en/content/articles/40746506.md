**James Edward Chalmers** (born 2 March 1978) is an Australian politician who has served as the [treasurer](579169.md) in the [Albanese government](70837765.md) since 2022. A member of the [Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party), he has been the [member of Parliament](61565.md) (MP) for the [Queensland](59051.md) division of [Rankin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Rankin) since 2013.

Born in [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane), Chalmers graduated from [Griffith University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffith_University) in 1999 with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) and a [Bachelor of Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Communications), and later from the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University) in 2004 with a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate) in political science. He worked as a research officer and manager for the [Queensland government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensland_Government) and the national Labor Party from 1999 to 2001, and 2002 to 2004 respectively, and held senior advisory positions in the staffs of [Wayne Swan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Swan), [Kim Beazley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Beazley), and [Morris Iemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Iemma) from 2005 to 2007. He was elected to the [House of Representatives](61565.md) at the [2013 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Australian_federal_election), winning the southeast Brisbane seat of Rankin. He was appointed to the [shadow ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorten_shadow_ministry) by [Bill Shorten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Shorten) in 2013, becoming a frontbencher in 2015, and later to the [shadow ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanese_shadow_ministry) of [Anthony Albanese](556745.md) after Shorten's retirement. Chalmers became the treasurer after the Labor Party's victory at the [2022 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_federal_election).

## Early life and education

Chalmers was born on 2 March 1978 in [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane),[^cite_note-aph-1] the youngest of three children born to Graham and Carol Chalmers. His father worked as a courier and his mother worked as a nurse. They divorced when he was 14, by which time his older sisters had left home.[^cite_note-walker-2]

Chalmers was raised in [Logan City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_City) in Brisbane's south.[^cite_note-3] He attended Catholic schools before going on to [Griffith University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffith_University), where he completed the degrees of Bachelor of Arts and Bachelor of Communications and attained a First Class honours degree in public policy.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] He went on to complete a PhD in political science at the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University), writing his doctoral thesis on the prime ministership of [Paul Keating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Keating),[^cite_note-aph-1][^cite_note-walker-2] titled "Brawler statesman: Paul Keating and prime ministerial leadership in Australia".[^cite_note-6]

## Early political involvement

From 1999 to 2001, Chalmers worked as a research officer in the Queensland Department of Premier and Cabinet. He was the ALP's national research manager from 2002 to 2004, media adviser to Shadow Treasurer [Wayne Swan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Swan) from 2005 to 2006, deputy chief of staff to Opposition Leader [Kim Beazley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Beazley) in 2006, and a senior adviser to New South Wales premier [Morris Iemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Iemma) from 2006 to 2007. After Labor won the [2007 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Australian_federal_election), Chalmers returned to work for Wayne Swan, as deputy chief of staff and principal adviser (2007–2010) and then as chief of staff (2010–2013).[^cite_note-aph-1] He briefly served as the executive director of the [Chifley Research Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chifley_Research_Centre) in 2013.[^cite_note-7] In the same year he published *Glory Daze*, a book about the disconnect between Australia's strong economic performance and popular discontent with government.[^cite_note-8]

## Member of Parliament

### Opposition (2013–2022)



Chalmers in 2016

Chalmers was elected to parliament at the [2013 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Australian_federal_election), replacing the retiring ALP member [Craig Emerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Emerson) in the [Division of Rankin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Rankin). He defeated former MP [Brett Raguse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Raguse) for Labor preselection.[^cite_note-9] Chalmers was made a shadow [parliamentary secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_secretary) in October 2013, a shadow minister in October 2015, and promoted to the [shadow cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Cabinet_of_Australia) after the [2016 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Australian_federal_election) as Shadow Minister for Finance.[^cite_note-aph-1]

After the [2019 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Australian_federal_election), Chalmers publicly considered running to succeed [Bill Shorten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Shorten) as party leader and [Leader of the Opposition](579586.md). His relative youth and status as a Queenslander were seen as potential assets, as well as his membership of the [Labor Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Right) faction. However, some within his faction had already chosen to support the [Labor Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Left) candidate [Anthony Albanese](556745.md).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Chalmers eventually chose not to stand for the position, allowing Albanese to become leader unopposed. He subsequently also ruled out standing for the deputy leadership.[^cite_note-12] He was subsequently appointed Shadow Treasurer in Albanese's cabinet.[^cite_note-13]

### Albanese government (2022–present)

Labor was victorious in the [2022 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_federal_election),[^cite_note-14] and two days later, Albanese had himself, Chalmers and three other senior Labor frontbenchers sworn in as an interim five-person government, with Chalmers becoming the [Treasurer of Australia](579169.md). He was also the interim [Minister for Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Home_Affairs_\(Australia\)) until the full ministry was sworn in after the [Quadrilateral Security Dialogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadrilateral_Security_Dialogue).[^cite_note-15] In October 2022, Chalmers handed down his [first budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2022_Australian_federal_budget).[^cite_note-16]

In May 2023, Chalmers handed down his [second budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_federal_budget). After being initially predicted to deliver a surplus of over \$4 billion, the budget ended up significantly exceeding expectations by delivering a surplus of \$22.1 billion (equivalent to 0.9% of Australia's GDP); this was Australia's first budget surplus in 15 years, and the largest ever Australian budget surplus.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] In January 2024, Chalmers played a key role in championing the Albanese government's changes to the [stage three tax cuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_three_tax_cuts).[^cite_note-23] Chalmers handed down his [third budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_federal_budget) in May 2024, which saw the government post a second consecutive surplus of \$9.3 billion.[^cite_note-24] He handed down the [2025 budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_federal_budget), which saw the budget return to a \$42.1 billion deficit.[^cite_note-25] The budget included a further \$17 billion in personal income tax cuts for the lowest earners.[^cite_note-26] During the [2025 federal election campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_federal_election), Chalmers participated in several [debates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_federal_election_debates_and_forums) with shadow treasurer [Angus Taylor](40520884.md).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

In 2026, Chalmers handed down the [2026 Australian federal budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Australian_federal_budget).[^cite_note-29]

## Political positions

Chalmers is a member of the [Labor Right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_Right) faction.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] In 2016 he co-founded the Courtyard Group, a roundtable linking Labor MPs with "Australia's leading progressive thinkers from business, academia, media, and consumer advocacy bodies".[^cite_note-32]

According to political scientist [Carol Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Johnson_\(academic\)), Chalmers' 2013 book *Glory Daze* "defined Labor as standing for [intergenerational mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intergenerational_mobility), aspiration and the Fair Go, while emphasising the importance of sound economic management". His 2017 book *Changing Jobs: The fair go in the new machine age*, co-authored with telecommunications executive [Mike Quigley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Quigley_\(businessman\)), argued that future governments should consider a [robot tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot_tax) to reduce the impact of [technological unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_unemployment). He has also expressed positive views on the concept of a [universal basic income](28310828.md). In the lead-up to the 2022 election, Chalmers stated that an ALP government would not increase taxes other than on multinational corporations. He also rejected new taxes. His campaigning focused on [cost of living](522934.md) issues but also emphasised the need to work co-operatively with business.[^cite_note-33]

In a long-form essay published in *[The Monthly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Monthly)* in 2023, Chalmers argued for more active involvement by the government in capital markets to collaborate with enterprises that suit the government's social and environmental priorities, describing it as "values-based capitalism".[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

## Personal life

In March 2013, Chalmers married Laura Anderson, a journalist and writer who worked as a staffer to [Penny Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_Wong) and [Julia Gillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Gillard). The couple have three children. Their wedding, attended by Gillard and [Wayne Swan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Swan) among others, occurred two days after [an ALP leadership spill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2013_Australian_Labor_Party_leadership_spill). While in attendance Gillard "convened a council of war in a specially set-aside room to frame a new ministry".[^cite_note-walker-2]

Chalmers identifies as a [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic), though more "tribal than Bible", his three children having been baptised in the same church as himself.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] His interests have been listed to include running, [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) and basketball.[^cite_note-44] He is a fan of [hip-hop music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop_music), with some of his favourite artists including [Tupac Shakur](31341.md), [The Notorious B.I.G.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Notorious_B.I.G.) and [Nas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nas).[^cite_note-45]

In the [National Rugby League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rugby_League) Chalmers supports the [Brisbane Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane_Broncos).[^cite_note-46]
