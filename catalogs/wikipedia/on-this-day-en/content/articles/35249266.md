**Ludwig Emil Tomas Göransson** ([/ˈɡɔːrənsən/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-2] Swedish: [\[ˈlɵ̌dːvɪɡ ˈjœ̂ːranˌsɔn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Swedish); born 1 September 1984) is a Swedish musician, composer, and record producer. Based in the United States, he is often regarded as one of the most successful composers in [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles) and of the 21st century.[^cite_note-3] For his work in music, film and television, he has received [numerous accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Ludwig_G%C3%B6ransson) including three [Academy Awards](324.md), two [British Academy Film Awards](150200.md), two [Emmy Awards](6579025.md), two [Golden Globe Awards](142724.md), and six [Grammy Awards](44635.md).

As a record producer, he has frequently collaborated with [Childish Gambino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Glover), producing his studio albums *[Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_\(album\))* (2011), *[Because the Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Because_the_Internet)* (2013) and *["Awaken, My Love!"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%22Awaken,_My_Love!%22)* (2016), among others. Göransson's producing work on Gambino's 2018 single "[This Is America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_America_\(song\))" was met with acclaim and accolades, including two Grammy Awards (for [Record of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Record_of_the_Year) and [Song of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Song_of_the_Year)). He has also produced for other recording artists, such as [Adele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adele), [Alicia Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicia_Keys), [Rihanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihanna), [Chance the Rapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chance_the_Rapper), [Haim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haim_\(band\)), [Justin Timberlake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Timberlake), [Kendrick Lamar](29909823.md), [Travis Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Scott) and [Moses Sumney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Sumney).

For television, he received his first break as a composer for the [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) comedy series *[Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_\(TV_series\))* (2009–2015), followed by the [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Broadcasting_Company) comedy series *[New Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Girl)* (2011–2018). Göransson transitioned into dramatic work, scoring the [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney+) series *[The Mandalorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mandalorian)* (2019–2020), which earned him two [Primetime Emmy Awards for Outstanding Music Composition for a Series (Original Dramatic Score)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Music_Composition_for_a_Series_\(Original_Dramatic_Score\)). He also composed the main theme for *[The Book of Boba Fett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Book_of_Boba_Fett)* (2021–2022).

For his work in film, he is primarily known for his work with his longtime friend [Ryan Coogler](38348303.md), scoring all his films, and [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan). He has earned the [Academy Award for Best Original Score](83065.md) three times, for Coogler's superhero film *[Black Panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_\(film\))* (2018), Nolan's biopic *[Oppenheimer](66850554.md)* (2023) and Coogler's horror film *[Sinners](75895455.md)* (2025). He was also nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song) for "[Lift Me Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lift_Me_Up_\(Rihanna_song\))" performed by [Rihanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihanna) featured in *[Black Panther: Wakanda Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther:_Wakanda_Forever)* (2022) and for "[I Lied to You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Lied_to_You)" from *Sinners*. Aside from his collaborations with Coogler and Nolan, his other prominent scores include the superhero film *[Venom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venom_\(2018_film\))* (2018), the Pixar film *[Turning Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_Red)* (2022), and the *Mandalorian* follow-up film *[The Mandalorian and Grogu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mandalorian_and_Grogu)* (2026).

## Early life and education

Göransson was born and raised in [Linköping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link%C3%B6ping), [Östergötland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96sterg%C3%B6tland). His mother Maria, a florist, is from [Warsaw](32908.md), Poland, and his father Tomas, a guitar teacher,[^cite_note-Washington_Post-4][^cite_note-NPR-5] is Swedish. He has an older sister named Jessika.[^cite_note-latimes-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] He is named after [Ludwig van Beethoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_van_Beethoven).[^cite_note-latimes-6]

He began music lessons at a young age and went on to graduate from the [Stockholm Royal College of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_College_of_Music,_Stockholm). In 2007, he moved to [Los Angeles](18110.md) to study at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) Scoring for Motion Picture and Television program.[^cite_note-9] It was there that Ludwig met [Ryan Coogler](38348303.md), with whom Ludwig collaborated on numerous projects.[^cite_note-Washington_Post-4]

## Career

### 2008–2017: *Community*, *"Awaken, My Love!"*, and *Fruitvale Station*

Göransson met [Ryan Coogler](38348303.md) at the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) (USC) where both were students.[^cite_note-Washington_Post-4] He composed the score for the award-winning short film *locks* in 2009, which Coogler wrote and directed while at USC.[^cite_note-Washington_Post-4] Soon after graduating from USC, Göransson began work assisting [Theodore Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Shapiro), a composer known for films such as *[Along Came Polly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Along_Came_Polly)*, *[The Devil Wears Prada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Devil_Wears_Prada_\(film\))*, *[Idiocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiocracy)*, and *[Tropic Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropic_Thunder)*. Göransson's first break came in 2009 as the composer for the sitcom [*Community*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_\(TV_series\)).[^cite_note-NPR-5]

In 2010, when [Donald Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Glover) first approached Göransson about his side project, Childish Gambino, the two were working on the set of *[Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_\(TV_series\))*.[^cite_note-NPR-5] Since then they have released four albums: *[Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_\(album\))*, *[Because the Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Because_the_Internet)*, *["Awaken, My Love!"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%22Awaken,_My_Love!%22)*, and *[3.15.20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3.15.20)*. Göransson served as producer and writer for every song on the album *"Awaken, My Love!"*, inspired by [Parliament-Funkadelic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament-Funkadelic) and black power music of the '70s. They earned four Grammy nominations for their work on *"Awaken, My Love!"*, including Album of the Year and Record of the Year for "[Redbone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redbone_\(song\))".[^cite_note-10] Overall, his work with Gambino earned him six Grammy Award nominations. In 2012, Göransson produced [Haim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haim_\(band\))'s debut EP and helped establish the band with a new sound on the song "[Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forever_\(Haim_song\))". He also produced two songs on the band's debut album *[Days Are Gone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Days_Are_Gone)*.[^cite_note-11]

In 2013, Göransson's first feature film came from fellow USC graduate [Ryan Coogler](38348303.md). Göransson composed the score to Coogler's critically acclaimed drama *[Fruitvale Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruitvale_Station)*, based on the fatal shooting of 22-year-old [Oscar Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_of_Oscar_Grant). The film won the Grand Jury Prize and Audience awards in the Dramatic category at the [2013 Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Sundance_Film_Festival), also taking home the Avenir Prize and Un Certain Regard awards at the [Cannes Film Festival](101460.md). In August 2013, Göransson signed a management deal as a composer for [Jay-Z](162870.md)'s label, [Roc Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roc_Nation).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-latimes-6] That year he also composed the music for the movie *[We're the Millers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We're_the_Millers)*. In 2015, Coogler and Göransson again worked together this time on the critically acclaimed [Rocky franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_\(film_series\)) film [*Creed*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_\(film\)). By 2016–17, he was scoring *[True Memoirs of an International Assassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Memoirs_of_an_International_Assassin)*, *[Central Intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Intelligence)* and *[Everything, Everything](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything,_Everything_\(film\))*.

### 2018–2022: *Black Panther*, "This is America", and *The Mandalorian*

In 2018, Göransson and Coogler came together for their third collaboration, [*Black Panther*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_\(film\)).[^cite_note-13] Göransson set out to create a score that would sit somewhere between traditional African instrumentation and a traditional superhero score. In order to do this he travelled to Senegal to research traditional African music and instruments. There he met musician [Baaba Maal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baaba_Maal) whose voice is featured in the score, singing in his native Fula language. He later traveled to the [International Library of African Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Library_of_African_Music) in South Africa, founded by [Hugh Tracey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Tracey), where he listened to recordings of traditional musicians to ensure the score had a culturally traditional sound.[^cite_note-14] In 2019, Göransson's score for *Black Panther* won the [Academy Award for Best Original Score](83065.md) and the [Grammy Award for Best Score Soundtrack for Visual Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Score_Soundtrack_for_Visual_Media).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-\:0-16]

On 5 May 2018, "[This Is America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_America_\(song\))" was released. Produced by Gambino and Ludwig Göransson, the song features a [gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_music)-style choir and background contributions from various American rappers [Young Thug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Thug), [Slim Jxmmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slim_Jxmmi), [BlocBoy JB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlocBoy_JB), [21 Savage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21_Savage), [Quavo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quavo) and [Offset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offset_\(rapper\)). "This Is America" debuted at number one on the US [*Billboard* Hot 100](423161.md), becoming the 31st song to do so in the chart's history. The song won in all four of its nominated categories at the [61st Annual Grammy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/61st_Annual_Grammy_Awards) for [Record of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Record_of_the_Year), [Song of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Song_of_the_Year), [Best Rap/Sung Performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Rap/Sung_Performance), and [Best Music Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Music_Video).[^cite_note-grammy-17]

On 22 May 2019, it was announced that Göransson was composing the film score for [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan)'s *[Tenet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenet)*.[^cite_note-18] Göransson was chosen after Nolan's frequent collaborator and first choice, [Hans Zimmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Zimmer), turned down the offer in favor of the 2021 film *[Dune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune_\(2021_film\))*.[^cite_note-Stolworthy-May2019-19][^cite_note-20] Göransson experimented with [retrograde composition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrograde_composition) and [sampling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_\(music\)) to match the themes and tone of the film. The first scoring session was held in November 2019, and sessions continued into early 2020.[^cite_note-21] He worked on original songs and music with [Justin Timberlake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Timberlake) for the 2020 film *[Trolls World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolls_World_Tour)*.[^cite_note-latimes-6]

Since 2019, Göransson has composed the score for the [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney+) television series *[The Mandalorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mandalorian)*, which is set in the *[Star Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars)* universe, as well as the fanfare for the franchise's brand logo.[^cite_note-latimes-6] He won the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Music Composition for a Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Music_Composition_for_a_Series) for both [season 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapter_8:_Redemption)[^cite_note-22] and [season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapter_16:_The_Rescue)'s[^cite_note-23] finales. In 2021, he started scoring sessions for *The Mandalorian'*&#x73; spinoff series, *[The Book of Boba Fett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Book_of_Boba_Fett)*,[^cite_note-NME-24] which premiered on 29 December 2021. In 2021, he was chosen to be the composer of the new fanfare for [Warner Bros. Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Pictures), which combines a new arrangement of "[As Time Goes By](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_Time_Goes_By_\(song\))" with elements of the original [Max Steiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Steiner) fanfare. In 2022, he worked on the scores of the [Pixar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixar) film *[Turning Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_Red)* and the second *Black Panther* installment, *[Wakanda Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther:_Wakanda_Forever)*.[^cite_note-\:1-25][^cite_note-26] For the latter, he co-wrote the song "[Lift Me Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lift_Me_Up_\(Rihanna_song\))" performed by [Rihanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihanna), which earned him a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song).[^cite_note-27]

### 2023–present: Further Coogler, Nolan, and *Star Wars* collaborations

He worked for a second time with Christopher Nolan on his 2023 film *[Oppenheimer](66850554.md)*.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] [Its accompanying soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppenheimer_\(soundtrack\)) received critical acclaim and earned Göransson the [Grammy Award for Best Score Soundtrack for Visual Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Score_Soundtrack_for_Visual_Media),[^cite_note-30] the [BAFTA Award for Best Original Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Original_Music),[^cite_note-31] the [Critics' Choice Movie Award for Best Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critics'_Choice_Movie_Award_for_Best_Score),[^cite_note-32] the [Golden Globe Award for Best Original Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award_for_Best_Original_Score),[^cite_note-33] and the [Academy Award for Best Original Score](83065.md).[^cite_note-34]

In 2024, he composed the [Disney+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney+) mnemonic as part of the streaming service's rebrand.[^cite_note-Disney+_Logo_2024-35]

For his work on Ryan Coogler's *[Sinners](75895455.md)*, he earned his third Oscar win at the [98th Academy Awards](79369474.md). The soundtrack, which he based on his experience with American [blues music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues), was also inspired by his father's love of the genre.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

In 2026, Göransson composed two major film scores. He returned for the theatrical follow-up to *The Mandalorian*, *[The Mandalorian and Grogu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mandalorian_and_Grogu)*.[^cite_note-38] He reunited with Nolan for *[The Odyssey](78079604.md)*.[^cite_note-39]

## Personal life

Göransson married American violinist Serena McKinney in 2018.[^cite_note-40] They have two sons.[^cite_note-latimes-6][^cite_note-41]

For *[The Mandalorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mandalorian)*, Göransson earned the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Music Composition for a Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Music_Composition_for_a_Series) for both [season 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapter_8:_Redemption) and [season 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapter_16:_The_Rescue)'s finales. In film, his score for *[Black Panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_\(film\))* won the [Academy Award for Best Original Score](83065.md) and the [Grammy Award for Best Score Soundtrack for Visual Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Score_Soundtrack_for_Visual_Media). He scored the second *Black Panther* film, 2022's [*Wakanda Forever*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther:_Wakanda_Forever), achieving a nomination for the [Academy Award for Best Original Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Original_Song) for "[Lift Me Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lift_Me_Up_\(Rihanna_song\))" performed by [Rihanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihanna). His work on [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan)'s epic biopic *[Oppenheimer](66850554.md)* (2023) earned his first wins at the [BAFTA Awards](150200.md) and [Golden Globe Awards](142724.md), and a second win at the [Academy Awards](324.md). For *[Sinners](75895455.md)*, he won his third Academy Award.[^cite_note-43]
