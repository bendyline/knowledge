The **Aviation Industry Corporation of China** (**AVIC**) is a Chinese state-owned, [publicly-traded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicly-traded) [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_manufacturer) and [defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_industry) conglomerate headquartered in [Beijing](18603746.md). AVIC is overseen by the [State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission of the State Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned_Assets_Supervision_and_Administration_Commission_of_the_State_Council). It is ranked 140th in the [*Fortune* Global 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_Global_500) list as of 2021,[^cite_note-Fortune_500,_AVIC-2] and has over 100 subsidiaries, 27 listed companies and 500,000 employees across the globe.[^cite_note-4] AVIC is also the sixth largest defense contractor globally as of 2022 and second largest Chinese defense contractor with total revenue of \$79 billion (from both defense and non-defense services).[^cite_note-5]

## History

Since being established on 1 April 1951 as the **Aviation Industry Administration Commission**,[^cite_note-6] the aviation industry of the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) has been through 12 systemic reforms.

In 1994, Avic was among the large industrial [state-owned enterprises of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned_enterprises_of_China) which were selected for a pilot program of restructuring as state holding companies, thereby enabling partial public listings of its subsidiaries' assets.[^cite_note-\:1-7]<sup>: 49 </sup>

AVIC purchased American aircraft engine manufacturer [Continental Motors, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Motors,_Inc.) in 2010, aircraft manufacturer [Cirrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cirrus_Aircraft) in 2011, and specialized parts supplier [Align Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Align_Aerospace) in 2015.[^cite_note-align-8] In 2015, AVIC and [BHR Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BHR_Partners) acquired U.S. automotive supplier [Henniges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henniges_Automotive), through a joint venture structure.[^cite_note-9]

In 2016, [Aero Engine Corporation of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_Engine_Corporation_of_China) was formed, capitalized with US\$7.5 billion by Aviation Industry Corporation of China (AVIC) and Commercial Aircraft Corporation of China, Ltd. (COMAC) in order to consolidate aero-engine and related technologies.

### Split and re-merger

China Aviation Industry Corporation was split into two separate entities, China Aviation Industry Corporation I and China Aviation Industry Corporation II in 1999. Both retained civilian and military aircraft production capabilities, along with a number of unrelated business ventures. The split was intended to foster [competitiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_\(economics\)) in the Chinese aerospace industry.[^cite_note-11]

In 2008, AVIC I and AVIC II officially merged back together. The previous separation resulted in split resources and led to redundant projects. The goal of the merger was to eliminate this redundancy and spin off pursuits unrelated to aerospace, such as motorcycle and automobile parts manufacturing.[^cite_note-12]

### Espionage allegations

Further information: [Chinese espionage in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_espionage_in_the_United_States)

In April 2009, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* reported that computer spies, allegedly Chinese, "had penetrated the database of the [Joint Strike Fighter program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Strike_Fighter_program) and acquired terabytes of secret information about the fighter, possibly compromising its future effectiveness."[^cite_note-13] AVIC allegedly "incorporated the stolen know-how into China's [Chengdu J-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-20) and [Shenyang FC-31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenyang_FC-31) fighters."[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

### U.S. sanctions

Further information: [United States sanctions against China](64972789.md) and [Military-civil fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military-civil_fusion)

In November 2020, [Donald Trump](4848272.md) issued an [executive order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_13959) prohibiting any American company or individual from owning shares in companies that the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md) has listed as having links to the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md), which included AVIC.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### Russian invasion of Ukraine

Further information: [China and the Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_and_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine)

In February 2023, the [Center for Advanced Defense Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Advanced_Defense_Studies) reported that customs data showed that AVIC shipped parts for [Sukhoi Su-35](314292.md) fighter jets to a subsidiary of sanctioned Russian defense company [Rostec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostec) following the 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-21]

### Myanmar civil war

See also: [Myanmar civil war (2021–present)](69807451.md)

AVIC has provided weapons and aircraft to the [Myanmar junta](66589972.md).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]
