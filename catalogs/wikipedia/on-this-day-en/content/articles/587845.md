The **Sava**[^cite_note-6] is a [river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River) in [Central](5188.md) and [Southeast Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Europe), a right-bank and the longest tributary of the [Danube](21209639.md). From its source in [Slovenia](27338.md), it flows through [Croatia](5573.md) and along its border with [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), and finally reaches [Serbia](29265.md), feeding into the [Danube](21209639.md) in its capital, [Belgrade](55904.md).

The Sava is 990 kilometres (615 miles) long, including the 45-kilometre (28 mi) [Sava Dolinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sava_Dolinka) [headwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headwater) rising in [Zelenci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelenci), Slovenia. It is the largest [tributary of the Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tributaries_of_the_Danube) by volume of water, and the second-largest after the [Tisza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tisza) in terms of catchment area (97713km²) and length. It drains a significant portion of the [Dinaric Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinaric_Alps) region, through the major tributaries of [Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina), [Bosna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosna_\(river\)), [Kupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kupa), [Una](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Una_\(Sava\)), [Vrbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrbas_\(river\)), [Lonja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonja), [Kolubara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolubara), [Bosut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosut_\(river\)) and [Krka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krka_\(Sava\)). The Sava is one of the longest [rivers in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivers_in_Europe) and among the longest tributaries of another river.

The population in the Sava River basin is estimated at 8,176,000, and is shared by three capital cities: [Ljubljana](42374.md), [Zagreb](55906.md) and Belgrade. The Sava is about 2⁄3-navigable for larger vessels: from the confluence of the Kupa in [Sisak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisak) a few kilometers below Zagreb.

## Etymology

The name is believed to be derived from the Proto-Indo-European root *\*sewh<sub>1</sub>-* 'to press, push (forth); to take liquid, water' (whence the English word *sup*) and the ending *\*eh<sub>2</sub>*, so that it literally means 'that which waters \[the ground]'.[^cite_note-7] The [ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greeks) called it Saos ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Σάος).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo) writes in *Geographica* 4.6.10 (composed between 20 BC and AD 20) of the *Saüs*,[^cite_note-10] and the Romans used the name *Savus*. Another name, used by Strabo for the Sava in its entirety or its lower part, is *Noarus*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEŠašel_Kos200942–43-11]

The Sava River is formed from the [Sava Dolinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sava_Dolinka) and the [Sava Bohinjka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sava_Bohinjka) [headwaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headwater) in northwest [Slovenia](27338.md). The drainage basin has other key [tributaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary), including the 52-kilometre (32 mi) [Sora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sora_\(river\)), the 27-kilometre (17 mi) [Tržič Bistrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%C5%BEi%C4%8D_Bistrica) and the 17-kilometre (11 mi) [Radovna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radovna_\(river\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) rivers—flowing into the Sava at [confluences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence) as far east downstream as [Medvode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvode).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVrhovecPristovHočevar1996123-12][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESURS200247-13]

The Sava Dolinka rises at the [Zelenci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zelenci) Pools near [Kranjska Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kranjska_Gora), Slovenia, in a valley separating the [Julian Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Alps) from the [Karawanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karawanks) [mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_range).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECareyClark200550-14] The spring is near the Slovene-[Italian](14532.md) border at 833 metres (2,733 feet) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESURS200247-13][^cite_note-FOOTNOTETocknerUehlingerRobinson2009chapter_3.9.6.-15] in a [drainage divide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_divide) between the [Adriatic](23275478.md) and [Danube basins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_basin). The Sava Dolinka [spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_\(hydrology\)) is fed by [groundwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater), possibly exhibiting [bifurcation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_bifurcation) of source [karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst) [aquifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifer) to the Sava and [Soča](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/So%C4%8Da) basins.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETrišičBatPolajnarPristov1997295–298-16] [Nadiža](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadi%C5%BEa_\(creek\)) creek, a short [losing stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Losing_stream) flowing nearby, is the source of Zelenci Pools water.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECareyClark200550-14] The Sava Dolinka is considered the Sava's initial[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESURS200247-13] 45-kilometre (28 mi) segment.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCSeptember_200912-17]

The Sava Bohinjka originates in [Ribčev Laz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rib%C4%8Dev_Laz), at the confluence of the [Jezernica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezernica?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOrožen190196-18] a short watercourse flowing out from [Lake Bohinj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Bohinj), and the [Mostnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostnica?action=edit\&redlink=1) River.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWFFC2012-19] Some sources define the Jezernica as a part of the Sava Bohinjka, specifying the latter as flowing directly out of the lake,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFallon2010133-20] while another group of sources include the [Savica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sava_Bohinjka), rising at the southern flank of [Triglav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triglav) as the 78-metre (256 ft) [Savica Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savica_Falls),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcKelvieMcKelvie2008111-21] downstream from [Triglav Lakes Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triglav_Lakes_Valley), and flowing into the lake, as a part of the Sava Bohinjka.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingleton19853-22] The watercourse flows 41 kilometres (25 miles)—including the length of the Savica—east to [Radovljica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radovljica),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESURS200247-13] where it discharges into the Sava Dolinka. Downstream from the confluence, the river is referred to as the Sava.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETrišičBatPolajnarPristov1997295–298-16]

## Course



The Sava's [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin)



The Sava at [Litija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litija)

The Sava spans Central-[Southeast Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Europe), flowing through Slovenia, Croatia, Serbia and along the Bosnia-Herzegovina border. Its total length is 990 kilometres (615 miles), including the 45-kilometre (28 mi) Sava Dolinka and the 945-kilometre (587 mi) Sava proper. As a right tributary of the [Danube](21209639.md), the river belongs to the [Black Sea](3386.md) drainage basin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCSeptember_2009113-23][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPrimožičKoboldBrilly20081-24] When the Sava Dolinka headwater is excluded from its course, the Sava River is the [third longest tributary of the Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tributaries_of_the_Danube),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCFebruary_2009-25] slightly shorter than the 966-kilometre (600 mi) [Tisza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tisza) and the 950-kilometre (590 mi) [Prut](30876764.md)—the Danube's two longest tributaries.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEICPDR-26][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBostanGheorgheDulgheruBostan2011127-27] It is also the largest tributary of the Danube by [discharge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discharge_\(hydrology\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCFebruary_2009-25] The river's course is sometimes used to describe the northern boundary of the [Balkans](4829.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETodorova200930-28] and the southern border of the [Central Europe](5188.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPromitzerHermanikStaudinger200910-29] Before the [breakup of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakup_of_Yugoslavia) in 1991 the Sava was the longest river lying completely within the country.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELampe200013-30]

### From the source to the Sutla



The Sava Gorge between [Ljubljana](42374.md) and [Trbovlje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trbovlje)

The Sava Dolinka rises in the Zelenci Pools, west of [Podkoren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podkoren) in the [Upper Carniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Carniola) region of Slovenia at 833 metres (2,733 feet) above sea level (a.s.l.),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESURS200247-13][^cite_note-FOOTNOTETocknerUehlingerRobinson2009chapter_3.9.6.-15] and flows east, past Kranjska Gora to [Jesenice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesenice,_Jesenice), where it turns southeast. At [Žirovnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDirovnica,_%C5%BDirovnica), the river enters the [Ljubljana Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljana_Basin) and encounters the first [hydroelectric dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_dam)—[Moste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moste,_%C5%BDirovnica) plant—before proceeding to the east of the glacial [Lake Bled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Bled) towards Radovljica and confluence of the Sava Bohinjka,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHSE_(Moste)-31] at 411 metres (1,348 feet) a.s.l.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESURS200247-13] Downstream of Radovljica, the Sava proceeds southeast towards [Kranj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kranj). Between Kranj and Medvode, its course comprises the [Lake Trboje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Trboje) and the [Lake Zbilje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Zbilje?action=edit\&redlink=1) [reservoirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCSeptember_200953-32] built for the [Mavčiče](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mav%C4%8Di%C4%8De) and the Medvode power plants.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHSE_(Mavčiče)-33][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHSE_(Medvode)-34]

The Sava then flows through the capital of Slovenia, [Ljubljana](42374.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMunicipality_of_Ljubljana-35] where another reservoir is on the river, adjacent to the [Tacen Whitewater Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacen_Whitewater_Course).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEECRR200681–83-36] There the river's course turns east and leaves the Ljubljana Basin via [Dolsko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolsko),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMunicipality_of_Dol_pri_Ljubljani2007-37] at 261 metres (856 feet) a.s.l. (at confluence of the [Ljubljanica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ljubljanica) and the [Kamnik Bistrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamnik_Bistrica)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESURS200247-13] The course continues through the [Sava Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sava_Hills), where it passes the [Litija Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litija_Basin?action=edit\&redlink=1) with the mining and industrial town of [Litija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litija), the [Central Sava Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Sava_Valley) with the mining towns of [Zagorje ob Savi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagorje_ob_Savi), [Trbovlje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trbovlje), and [Hrastnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrastnik), turns to the southeast and runs through the [Lower Sava Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Sava_Valley) with the towns of [Radeče](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rade%C4%8De), [Sevnica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevnica), and [Krško](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kr%C5%A1ko). The course through the Sava Hills forms the boundary of traditional regions of Lower Carniola and [Styria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Styria_\(Slovenia\)),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMunicipality_of_Laško-38] At Radeče, the [Vrhovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrhovo,_Rade%C4%8De) hydroelectric dam reservoir stands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHSE_(Vrhovo)-39] The latter is site of the [Krško Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kr%C5%A1ko_Nuclear_Power_Plant), which uses the Sava River water to dissipate excess heat.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKrško_NPP-40] The easternmost stretch of the Sava's course in Slovenia runs to the south of [Brežice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre%C5%BEice), where it is joined by the Krka, and the river ultimately becomes a border river between Slovenia and [Croatia](5573.md), marking 4 kilometres (2.5 miles) of their border near confluence of the [Sutla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutla) ([Slovene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovene_language): *Sotla*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCSeptember_2009170-41] At that point, the Sava reaches 132 metres (433 feet) a.s.l. after flowing 221 kilometres (137 miles) through Slovenia and along its border.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESURS200247-13]

The westernmost part of the 562-kilometre (349 mi) Sava River's course in Croatia,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStatistical_Yearbook_of_the_Republic_of_Croatia_201549-44] takes the river east, through the western part of the [Zagreb County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagreb_County), between [Samobor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samobor) and [Zaprešić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapre%C5%A1i%C4%87). The area encompasses forests interspersed by [marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsh) and lakes formed in [gravel pits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravel_pit).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZagreb_County_Tourist_Board-45] As the Sava approaches the capital of Croatia, [Zagreb](55906.md), the marshes give way to urban landscape, but there are surviving examples of the gravel pit lakes, such as the [Jarun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarun),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAničićTreer1997162-46] and the [Bundek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundek) within the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENacional22_May_2006-47] At the western outskirts of Zagreb, there is the western terminus of the 32-kilometre (20 mi) [Sava–Odra flood-relief canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sava-Odra_Canal) connecting the Sava to the [Odra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odra_\(Kupa\)) River plain which is intended to act as [flood control](20913655.md) [retention basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retention_basin).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEŠterc197997-48] The canal has been built in response to the most destructive [flooding of the river that occurred in Zagreb in 1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Zagreb_flood), when one third of the city was flooded and 17 people were killed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIndex.hr26_October_2011-49] The city itself marks the western extent of the Sava River basin area especially prone to flooding, spanning from Zagreb to confluence of the river in [Belgrade](55904.md), [Serbia](29265.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCSeptember_2009187-50]

East of Zagreb, the river turns southeast again further through the [Central Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Croatia), to the [Sisak-Moslavina County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisak-Moslavina_County), the city of [Sisak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisak), reaching 91.3 metres (300 feet) a.s.l. The city of Sisak marks the westernmost extent of the Sava River navigable to larger vessels. Navigation conditions on the river are poor due to limited [draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_\(hull\)) and fairway width, [meandering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meander) of the river, [bridge clearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_gauge) restrictions, poor [fairway markings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigation_mark) as well as presence of sunken vessels and other objects, including [unexploded ordnance](406804.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCSeptember_2009160–161-51] The ordnance is left over from various conflicts including the [World War II](32927.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENova_TV4_January_2012-52] [Croatian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_War_of_Independence), [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEtportal.hr7_July_2011-53] and the [1999 NATO bombing of Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NATO_bombing_of_Yugoslavia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIndex.hr19_January_2011-54] Before reaching confluence of [Una](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Una_\(Sava\)) at [Jasenovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasenovac,_Sisak-Moslavina_County) and 86.8 metres (285 feet) a.s.l,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBC201137-55] the Sava River traces [Lonjsko polje Nature Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonjsko_polje_Nature_Park),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELonjsko_Polje_NP_(a)-56] encompassing marshes frequently flooded by the Sava and its tributaries in the area.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELonjsko_Polje_NP_(b)-57]

### From the Una to the Drina



The Sava as a border river between [Brod, Republika Srpska, BiH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brod,_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) (left) and [Slavonski Brod](149285.md), [Croatia](5573.md) (right)&#x20;

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENadilo2000183-58]

Downstream of confluence of the Una River, the Sava is once again tracing an international border—between Croatia and [Bosnia-Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia-Herzegovina). Its meandering course runs generally eastwards along [Gradiška](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradi%C5%A1ka,_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina), and [Slavonski Brod](149285.md) to [Županja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDupanja), where it turns south to [Brčko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%8Dko). There, the river resumes its predominantly eastward course towards [Sremska Rača](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sremska_Ra%C4%8Da) and confluence of the [Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina) River. The right bank of the Sava, in this segment of its course, belongs to Bosnia-Herzegovina (with Bosnia's all three administrative entities, [Republika Srpska](347422.md), [Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and the [Brčko District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%8Dko_District), having gateway to the river), while the opposite bank belongs to Croatia and its Sisak-Moslavina, [Brod-Posavina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brod-Posavina_County) and [Vukovar-Srijem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vukovar-Srijem_County) counties, except in the area of [Jamena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamena) and further downstream—which belongs to Serbia and the province of [Vojvodina](176801.md). No cities in this segment of the course span the river. It is an international frontier, three times seeing adjacent key settlements facing one another: Gradiška, Brod, and Brčko in Bosnia and Herzegovina, opposite [Stara Gradiška](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stara_Gradi%C5%A1ka), Slavonski Brod, and [Gunja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunja,_Croatia) in Croatia.

The 337.2-kilometre (209.5 mi) segment between the Una and the Drina confluences, corresponding to the Sava flowing along the border of Bosnia-Herzegovina, exhibits small change of elevation, such as from 86.8 metres (285 feet) [ASL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_Ordnance_Datum) at Jasenovac to 76.6 metres (251 feet) ASL at Brčko [gauges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream_gauge): over 287.5 kilometres (178.6 miles) of the river between them.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBC201169-59] The river below Zagreb has a 0.4‰ slope (gradient) on average, much less steep than the course in Slovenia, where the average slope exceeds 0.7‰. This results in the Sava's meandering course running through a wide plain bordered by [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETocknerUehlingerRobinson2009chapter_3.9.6.-15]

### From the Drina to the Danube



The confluence of the Sava (river on right side) into the [Danube](21209639.md) (river on left) near the right bank of the [Great War Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_War_Island) and [Kalemegdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrade_Fortress)



The Sava and the historical center of [Belgrade](55904.md)

Downstream from the confluence of the Drina, the Sava River changes its eastward course to northeast, until it reaches [Sremska Mitrovica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sremska_Mitrovica), whence it flows southeast and then south to [Šabac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0abac), before finally turning east towards Belgrade. Most of the river's course in Serbia represents a border between province of Vojvodina, on the left bank, and [Central Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Serbia), on the right bank. Exceptions to that are in area around Sremska Mitrovica, where both banks are in Vojvodina, and downstream of [Progar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progar) suburb of Belgrade where both banks are in Central Serbia. The river meanders and forms wetlands there as well—the most significant centering on [Obedska bara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obedska_bara) [oxbow lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxbow_lake).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCSeptember_200912-17] The Sava River forms several large [islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_island) in this segment of the course, with the largest among them—800-hectare (2,000-acre) [Ada Ciganlija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada_Ciganlija) in Belgrade—connected to the right bank by a pair of artificial [embankment dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embankment_dam) forming [Lake Sava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Sava) since 1967.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBC2011103-60][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJP_Ada_Ciganlija-61]

The Sava discharges into the Danube, after reaching 68.3 metres (224 feet) a.s.l. as its right tributary at the [Great War Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_War_Island) off the easternmost tip of [Syrmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrmia) in Belgrade, 1,169.9 kilometres (726.9 miles) away from the Danube's confluence and the Black Sea.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBC2011109-62]

The Sava River basin covers a total area of 97713.2km² making it the second largest Danube tributary catchment by area size, surpassed only by the Tisza basin,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETocknerUehlingerRobinson2009chapter_3.9.6.-15] and it encompasses 12% of the Danube basin, draining into the Black Sea. The Sava represents the third longest tributary of the Danube and its largest tributary by discharge.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCFebruary_2009-25] The catchment area borders the remainder of the Danube basin to the north and east, and the Adriatic Sea basin to the west and south. The river basin generally consists of parts of Bosnia-Herzegovina, Croatia, [Montenegro](20760.md), Serbia and Slovenia, with a very small part of the catchment area belonging to [Albania](738.md). Topography of the basin varies significantly. Upstream portion of the basin is more rugged than downstream one, but asymmetry of the basin topography is particularly apparent when comparing right and left bank areas—the former dominated by the [Alps](981.md) and the [Dinarides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinarides) reaching elevations in excess of 2000m a.s.l, while the latter is dominated by the [Pannonian Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Plain). The mean elevation of the basin is 545m a.s.l.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCSeptember_20095–8-77]

### Major tributaries



The confluence of the Sava and [Drina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drina)

The most important tributaries of the Sava River found in its upper basin are characterized by relatively steep grades of flow, high flow velocities and [rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapids). Those are left tributaries: the [Kokra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokra), the Kamnik Bistrica and the [Savinja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savinja); and right tributaries: the Sora, the Ljubljanica and the [Krka (Sava)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krka_\(Sava\)). Further downstream larger rivers empty into the Sava, as the right bank of the basin grows steadily. Right tributaries in this lower segment of the basin start as fast flowing courses, only to slow down as they enter the [Pannonian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Basin). They include the [Kupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kupa), the Una, the [Vrbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrbas_\(river\)), the [Ukrina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrina), the [Bosna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosna_\(river\)), the [Brka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brka), the Tinja, the Drina and the [Kolubara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolubara). Left tributaries in the lower segment drain plains consequently exhibiting less steep course grades, lower flow rates and meandering. They include the Sutla, the [Krapina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krapina_\(river\)), the [Lonja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonja), the [Ilova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilova_River_\(Croatia\)), the [Orljava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orljava) and the [Bosut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosut_\(river\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCSeptember_200912–13-79]

The 346km Drina is the largest tributary of the Sava, flowing in Bosnia-Herzegovina and along border of the country and Serbia. It is formed by the headwaters of the [Tara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tara_\(Drina\)) and the [Piva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piva_\(river\)) at the border of Bosnia-Herzegovina and Montenegro, near [Šćepan Polje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0%C4%87epan_Polje). Its 20319.9km² catchment extends across parts of four countries—reaching as far south as Albania. The Bosna and the Kupa river basins are the second and third largest catchments of the Sava tributaries, each surpassing 10000km² in size.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCSeptember_200912–13-79]

## Hydrology



Lake Zbilje upstream from [Medvode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvode)

The average annual [flow rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discharge_\(hydrology\)) of the Sava River at Radovljica, immediately downstream of the Sava Dolinka and the Sava Bohinjka confluence, stands at 44.9 cubic metres (1,590 cubic feet) per second.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOikos200820-81] Downstream of the Krka confluence the average flow rate reaches 317 cubic metres (11,200 cubic feet) per second,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMayer199631-82] gradually increasing as tributaries discharge along the course—340 cubic metres (12,000 cubic feet) per second downstream of the Sutla, 880 cubic metres (31,000 cubic feet) per second following discharge of the Kupa and the Una, 990 cubic metres (35,000 cubic feet) per second downstream of the Vrbas confluence, 1,180 cubic metres (42,000 cubic feet) per second after the Bosna river empties into the Sava,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMayer199632-83] and finally of 1,564 cubic metres (55,200 cubic feet) per second at confluence of the Sava in Belgrade.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCFebruary_2009-25] The highest flow rate of 6,007 cubic metres (212,100 cubic feet) per second was recorded by Slavonski Šamac [gauging station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauging_station) in May 2014.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETutiš20143-84]

Seven out of eight largest reservoirs in the Sava River basin are in the Drina catchment, the largest among them being the 0.88-cubic-kilometre (0.21 cu mi) [Lake Piva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Piva) on the eponymous river in Montenegro, created after construction of [Mratinje Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mratinje_Dam). Overall, there are 22 reservoirs holding more than 5,000,000 cubic metres (180,000,000 cubic feet) of water in the basin, with four of them *on* the Sava, including one on the Sava Dolinka. Most of the reservoirs are used primarily, or even exclusively, for [electricity generation](9540.md), but they are also used as supply of [drinking water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_water), [industrial water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_water) source, for [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) and [food production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_production).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCSeptember_200953-32]

Groundwater is a very important resource in the Sava River basin, generally used for public water supply of potable water, as a source of water for industrial use, but also as the mainstay of [aquatic ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_ecosystem). There are 41 identified significant groundwater bodies in the Sava River basin of basin-wide importance,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCNovember_2011_(a)3-85] ranging in area size from 97 to 5,186 square kilometres (37 to 2,002 square miles), as well as numerous minor ground water bodies. Even though most of them are [transboundary waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transboundary_waters), eleven are considered to be largely in Slovenia, fourteen in Croatia, seven in Bosnia-Herzegovina, five in Serbia and four in Montenegro.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCNovember_2011_(a)5–6-86]

## Geology

The course of the Sava River runs through several diverse [geological units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_unit) and [orographic regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orography). The uppermost course of the river and its headwaters in the Karawanks area, is in the [Southern Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Alps_\(geology\)), tracing the Sava [Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\))—itself running parallel to the [Periadriatic Seam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periadriatic_Seam). [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) and [Upper Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Triassic) rocks are exposed in the region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlacer2008207-92] The Ljubljana Basin represents the boundary of the Southern Alps and the Dinarides.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlacer2008209-93] Valleys of the Sava Dolinka and the Sava Bohinjka are [glacial valleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_valley), carved out by the Sava Dolinka and Bohinj [glaciers](12463.md) advancing down Karawanks range to vicinity of present-day Radovljica. In the [late Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pleistocene), [Bohinj Glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohinj_Glacier?action=edit\&redlink=1) was the largest glacier in the territory of present-day Slovenia, up to 900 metres (3,000 feet) thick.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBavecVerbič2001385-94][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStepišnik2012300–303-95] Sava Folds, southeast and east of the Ljubljana Basin are thought of as a part of the Dinarides,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlacer2008206-96] separating the Ljubljana and [Krško Basins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kr%C5%A1ko_Basin?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlacer2008207-92] and forming the Sava Hills.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETopole200353-97] The east–west oriented folds are younger than the [Miocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miocene) and the folding is considered to have taken place in the [Pliocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliocene) and the [Quaternary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary), but it is possible that the tectonic activity continues in the present day.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETopole200346-98] The Sava Folds largely exhibit [Paleozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleozoic) and [Triassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic) rocks,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKolar-JurkovšekJurkovšek2012324-99] and [clastic sediments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clastic_sediment).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERamovšSremaćKulenović1987398-100]

The lower course of the Sava in the Pannonian Basin—first reached by the Sava River in the Krško Basin on the western rim of the Pannonian Basin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlacer2008208-101] The Pannonian Basin took shape through Miocenian thinning and [subsidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidence) of crust structures formed during Late Paleozoic [Variscan orogeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variscan_orogeny). The Paleozoic and Mesozoic structures are visible in [Papuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papuk) and other Slavonian mountains. The processes also led to the formation of a [stratovolcanic](162404.md) chain in the basin 17–12 [Mya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mya_\(unit\)) (million years ago) and intensified subsidence observed until 5 Mya as well as [flood basalts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_basalt) about 7.5 Mya. Contemporary uplift of the [Carpathian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Mountains) prevented water flowing to the Black Sea, and the [Pannonian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Sea) formed in the basin. Sediments were transported to the basin from uplifting Carpathian and Dinaric mountains, with particularly deep fluvial sediments being deposited in the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) during the uplift of the [Transdanubian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transdanubian_Mountains).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaas201214–18-102] Ultimately, up to 3,000 metres (9,800 feet) of the sediment was deposited in the basin, and the Pannonian sea eventually drained through the [Iron Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gate_\(Danube\)) gorge.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHilbers201116-103] In the southern Pannonian Basin, the [Neogene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neogene) to Quaternary sediment depth is normally lower, averaging 500 to 1,500 metres (1,600 to 4,900 feet), except in central parts of depressions formed by [subduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction). A subduction zone formed in the present-day Sava River valley, and approximately 4,000 metres (13,000 feet) deep sediments were deposited in the Slavonia-Syrmia depression and 5,500 metres (18,000 feet) in the Sava depression.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESaftićVelićSztanóJuhász2003108-104] The results of those processes are large [plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain) in the Sava River valley and the Kupa River valley. The plains are interspersed by the [horst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horst_\(geology\)) and [graben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graben) structures, believed to have broken the Pannonian Sea surface as [islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_island_mountains),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalvićVelić2011221-105] which became watershed between [Drava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drava) and Sava River basins extending along [Ivanščica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan%C5%A1%C4%8Dica)–[Kalnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalnik_\(mountain\))–[Bilogora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilogora)–Papuk mountain chain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHrvatske_vodesection_2.1-106] The Papuk Mountain is flanked by the [Krndija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krndija) and the [Dilj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilj) Hills on the eastern rim of the [Požega Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po%C5%BEega_Valley). The Bilogora, Papuk and Krndija Mountains consist mostly of Paleozoic rocks which are 300–350 million years old, while the Dilj consists of much more recent Neogene rocks, 2–18 million years old.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPamićRadonićPavić20036-107] Further east of the chain, the watershed runs through the [Đakovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90akovo)–[Vinkovci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinkovci) and [Vukovar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vukovar) Plateau.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBačaniŠparicaVelić1999149-108] The [loess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loess) plateau, extending eastward from Dilj and representing the watershed between the [Vuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vuka_\(river\)) and Bosut rivers, gradually rises to the [Fruška Gora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fru%C5%A1ka_Gora) south of Ilok.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVukovar-Syrmia_County20066-109]

## Economy

### Electric power generation

There are 18 hydroelectric power plants with power generation capacity exceeding 10 [Megawatts](21347693.md) in the Sava River basin. In Slovenia, most of them harness the Sava itself. In other countries, the hydroelectric power plants are on its tributaries. Total power generation capacity of the 18 power plants, and additional smaller plants largely found in Slovenia, amounts to 41542 megawatts, and their annual production capacity stands at 2,497 [gigawatt-hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigawatt-hour). Approximately 3.3 cubic kilometres (0.79 cubic miles) of water per year in the river's basin is used to cool thermoelectric and nuclear power plants. Power plant cooling represents the main type of use of the Sava River waters.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCDecember_201025-110]

As of October 2012, there are six existing hydroelectric power plants built along the Sava River. Upstream of Ljubljana there are Moste, Mavčiče and Medvode power plants, while Vrhovo, [Boštanj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bo%C5%A1tanj,_Sevnica) and [Blanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blanca,_Sevnica) are downstream of the capital. There is one additional plant under construction near Krško. The Krško hydroelectric power plant, as well as two additional plants planned on the Sava River course downstream of Ljubljana—Brežice and Mokrice—should be completed by 2018. The power plants downstream of Ljubljana, except Vrhovo, are developed as a chain of five Slovenia's Lower Sava Valley plants since 2002.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCDecember_201015-111][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHESS_(Boštanj)-112] They will have production capacity of 2,000 gigawatt-hours per year and 570 megawatts of [installed capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Installed_capacity). Completion of the five power plants is expected to cost 700  million [euros](9472.md). There are also plans for construction of ten new powerplants in the middle Sava valley HE Suhadol, HE Trbovlje, HE Renke, HE Ponovice, HE Kresnice, HE Jevnica, HE Zalog, HE Šentjakob, HE Ježica and HE Tacen. Croatia is planning the construction of four hydroelectric power plants on the Sava River in the Zagreb area. The four plants—[Podsused](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podsused_%E2%80%93_Vrap%C4%8De), Prečko, Zagreb and [Drenje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drenje_Brdove%C4%8Dko)—are scheduled to be completed by 2021 at a cost of 800  million euros. The four power plants will have an installed capacity of 122  megawatts and an annual production capacity of 610 gigawatt-hours.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJalušić20119-113]

### Water supply and food production

Use of water for public [water supply](684928.md) in the Sava River basin is estimated at 783,000,000 cubic metres (2.77×10<sup>10</sup> cubic feet) per year, and another 289,000,000 cubic metres (1.02×10<sup>10</sup> cubic feet) of water per year is used for industrial production purposes. Use of water for [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) in the Sava River basin is relatively high, but most of it is applied in non-consumptive uses, such as [fish farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_farming). Use of water for irrigation is relatively low, estimated at 30,000,000 cubic metres (1.1×10<sup>9</sup> cubic feet) per year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCDecember_201025-110] [Commercial fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_fishing) on the Sava River is in decline since the middle of the 20th century. In 1978, there were only 97  commercial fishermen there, while [recreational fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_fishing) became dominant.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHabekovićHomenFašaić19909-116] The decline became more rapid during the wars in Croatia and Bosnia-Herzegovina, reducing the quantity of fish caught in the river to approximately one-third of the pre-war catches which ranged from 719 to 988 tonnes (708 to 972 long tons; 793 to 1,089 short tons) between 1979 and 1990.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHabekovićSafnerAničićTreer199799-117] The [International Sava River Basin Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Sava_River_Basin_Commission) (ISRBC), a cooperative body established by Bosnia-Herzegovina, Croatia, Slovenia and [Serbia and Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro) in 2005,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBC_History-118] is tasked with the establishment of sustainable management of surface water and groundwater resources in the Sava River basin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBC_Mission-119]

### Navigation and ports

The Sava is navigable to larger vessels for 593.8 kilometres (369.0 miles) between its confluence with the Danube in Belgrade, Serbia and Galdovo Bridge in [Sisak, Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisak,_Croatia), 2.8 kilometres (1.7 miles) upstream from confluence of Sava and Kupa rivers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBC201127-120] The confluence marks the westernmost point of the river course designated as a Class IV international [waterway](174851.md) in compliance with the [United Nations Economic Commission for Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Economic_Commission_for_Europe)'s European Agreement on Main Inland Waterways of International Importance (AGN).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMMATI200819-121] The classification means that the river course between Sisak and Belgrade is navigable to ships of the maximum length of 80 to 85 metres (262 to 279 feet), the maximum beam of 9.5 metres (31 feet), the maximum draught of 2.5 metres (8 feet 2 inches) and tonnage up to 1,500 tonnes (1,500 long tons; 1,700 short tons).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUNECE199684-122] The Sava River downstream of Sisak, is designated as European waterway E 80-12, branching off from the E 80 waterway spanning the Danube and [Le Havre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Havre) via the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUNECE199616-123] The largest ports on the Sava River are Brčko and Šamac in Bosnia-Herzegovina,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBMG-124] Sisak and Slavonski Brod in Croatia,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMMATI200818-125] and Šabac and Sremska Mitrovica in Serbia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDanube_Strategy_in_Serbia-126]

As of 2008, 24.5 kilometres (15.2 miles) of the river course between [Slavonski Šamac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavonski_%C5%A0amac) and [Oprisavci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oprisavci), as well as additional 219.8 kilometres (136.6 miles) between Slavonski Brod and Sisak, are considered by [Croatia's Ministry of Maritime Affairs, Transport and Infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Maritime_Affairs,_Transport_and_Infrastructure_\(Croatia\)) to fail the Class IV criteria, permitting navigation of vessels up to 1,000 tonnes (980 long tons; 1,100 short tons) only, complying with the AGN's Category III.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMMATI200819-121] The Slavonski Šamac–Oprisavci section is especially troublesome for navigation as it offers 250 centimetres (98 inches) draught in less than 50% of an average hydrological year, causing navigation to cease each summer. Similar interruptions are less frequent elsewhere on the river, occurring 30 days a year on average upstream from Oprisavci, and even more rarely downstream from Slavonski Šamac.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMMATI2010section_1.1.3.-127]

The restricted draft and fairway is compounded with a meandering of the river's course—limiting the length of vessels—and low bridge clearance. Further problems are incurred through poor transport infrastructure along the route, including poor navigation markings, and presence of sunken vessels and unexploded [munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munitions).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCSeptember_2009160–161-51] Navigation along further 68 kilometres (42 miles) of the river upstream to [Rugvica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugvica) near Zagreb is possible for vessels with [tonnage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonnage) below 1,000 tonnes (980 long tons; 1,100 short tons), and the section of the river belongs to the AGN's Category II. There are plans for the restoration of the Category IV compliant waterway downstream of Sisak and betterment of navigation infrastructure between Sisak and Rugvica,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMMATI200835-128] as well as upgrading of the waterway between Brčko and Belgrade to Category Va, matching that of the Danube, with uninterrupted navigation through the year. The plan is planned to be supported by the [European Union](9317.md) and as of October 2012, an agreement to implement the plan was signed by Bosnia-Herzegovina and Croatia, while Serbia is invited to join the project. The plan aims to increase the safety and volume of river transport, which declined by about 70% since the breakup of Yugoslavia, largely because of poor maintenance of the route.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAl_Jazeera_Balkans22_April_2012-129] The ISRBC is tasked with the establishment of an international regime of navigation on the river since 2005.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBC_Mission-119]

### Road, rail, and pipeline transport

The Sava Valley is also a route for road and rail traffic. The river valley routes are a part of the [Pan-European Corridor X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_Corridor_X), and forming junctions with [Pan-European Corridors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_Corridors) V, Vb, [Vc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_Corridor_Vc), Xa and Xb in area of Ljubljana (V), Zagreb (Vb, Xa), Slavonski Šamac (Vc), and Belgrade (Xb).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEU9_September_2002-131] The motorways forming the Pan-European Corridor X in the area—[Slovenia's A2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A2_motorway_\(Slovenia\)), [Croatia's A3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A3_\(Croatia\)) and [Serbia's A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_\(Serbia\)) motorways—represent a part of [European route E70](8130337.md) [Bordeaux](4097.md)–[Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin)–Ljubljana–Zagreb–Belgrade–[Bucharest](36877.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEECOSOC200211–12-132] and the [European route E61](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E61) [Villach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villach)–Ljubljana–[Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste)–[Rijeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijeka).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEECOSOC200217-133] A largely [double track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_track) and [electrifried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_electrification_system) railway is also a part of the Corridor X.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrnjacAbramovićMaslarić2010303-134] The railway was a part of the [Simplon-Orient-Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient_Express) and Direct-Orient-Express routes.[^cite_note-135] The navigable river course between Belgrade and Galdovo north of Sisak is spanned by 25 bridges.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBC_Bridges201124-136] The Sava River valley east of Sisak is also used as a route for the [Jadranski naftovod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jadranski_naftovod), a [crude oil](23195.md) pipeline. The system connects the [Port of Rijeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Rijeka) oil terminal to [oil refineries](195137.md) in Rijeka and Sisak, to Brod in Bosnia-Herzegovina, as well as [Novi Sad](113288.md) and [Pančevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan%C4%8Devo) in Serbia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJANAF-137]

## Environmental issues

### Pollution

The main pressure on the Sava River basin environment is generated by the activities of the urban population in the basin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCNovember_2011_(b)38-138] Even though nearly all population centres generating pollution above 10,000 [population equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_equivalent) (PE) have some sort of [sewage treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_treatment) in place, less than a quarter of them are adequate.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCNovember_2011_(b)41-139] Wastewater from 86% of Sava River basin settlements, generating more than 2,000 PE, goes untreated. Pollution levels vary along the river. The best conditions in terms of wastewater treatment are found in Slovenia, although the existing facilities are inadequate.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCNovember_2011_(b)42-140]

In Serbia, on the other hand, 68% of population centres have no wastewater treatment facilities at all.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCNovember_2011_(b)41-139] Population centres exceeding 2,000 PE directly discharge into the Sava River basin's surface waters 11112 tonnes of [nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen) and 2,642 tonnes of [phosphorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorus).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCNovember_2011_(b)50-141]

Agriculture is another significant source of the Sava River basin surface water pollution, specifically through [livestock](25160767.md) [manure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manure) production. It is estimated that the nutrient pollution levels generated by manure production equal 32,394 tonnes of nitrogen and 3,784 tonnes of phosphorus per year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCNovember_2011_(b)68-142] As a consequence, the Sava River is microbiologically polluted in areas affected by the nutrient pollution. One such part of the river is the lowermost part of its course between Šabac and Belgrade, where acceptable freshwater bacterial counts are exceeded.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVukovićMarkovićRadenkovićVuković201113-143]

Levels of industrial pollution vary significantly throughout the basin. In 2007, significant sources of industrial pollution were identified in Slovenia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, and Serbia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCNovember_2011_(b)66-144] Levels of lead, cadmium and arsenic measured in the Sava River at Zagreb in 2003 did not exceed permitted concentrations, but measured levels of [mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_\(element\)) exceeded permitted levels in four out of 216 samples.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBošnirPuntarićŠkesKlarić200334-145] Levels of [heavy metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metals), specifically zinc, copper, lead and cadmium, measured in sediments in the Sava River near Belgrade were assessed as representing little to no risk, and the conclusion drawn was that in order to "reduce the existing bacterial contamination of the Sava River it is necessary to control faecal discharge near cities like Belgrade."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVukovićMarkovićRadenkovićVuković201114-146] The two countries (Croatia and Montenegro) with the greatest direct access to the Adriatic showed by far the least polluted basin surface waters, although other factors, such as demography, agricultural/environmental development and, especially, investment (internal and external), play a role.

### Protected areas

The Sava River basin is very significant because of its [biological diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_diversity), and it contains large [alluvial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alluvial) wetlands and lowland forests. This led to the designation of six protected areas under provisions of the [Ramsar Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsar_Convention) by the countries in the basin. Those are [Lake Cerknica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Cerknica) in Slovenia, [Lonjsko Polje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonjsko_Polje) and [Crna Mlaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crna_Mlaka) in Croatia, [Lake Bardača](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Barda%C4%8Da) in Bosnia-Herzegovina, and Obedska and [Zasavica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zasavica_\(bog\)) bogs in Serbia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEISRBCDecember_201013-147]

## Sport and recreation



The [Tacen Whitewater Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacen_Whitewater_Course) in Ljubljana, Slovenia

There are several sports and recreational grounds on the river banks and gravel pits and artificial lakes adjacent. Tacen Whitewater Course, on the right bank of the Sava in [Tacen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacen), a suburb of Ljubljana, was built as a permanent [kayaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayaking) course in 1948.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECanoe_Federation_of_Slovenia_(a)-148] It hosts a major international competition almost every year, examples being the [ICF Canoe Slalom World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICF_Canoe_Slalom_World_Championships) in [1955](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_ICF_Canoe_Slalom_World_Championships), [199](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_ICF_Canoe_Slalom_World_Championships)1[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECanoe_Federation_of_Slovenia_(b)-149] and [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_ICF_Canoe_Slalom_World_Championships).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESinfoOctober_201035-150] In Zagreb, Jarun complex of lakes along the river course offers a range of facilities for swimming, water sports and cycling.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZagreb_Holding-151] The island of Ada Ciganlija in Belgrade is the major recreational zone of the city, gathering as many as 100,000 visitors daily in the summer months.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPolitika14_July_2008-152][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPress2_July_2012-153]

The Sava River is the site of several [regattas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regatta). Those include the International Sava Tour [rowing](25736.md) regatta taking place between Zagreb and Brčko,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVasićMandićNedeljkov2011103-154] and the Belgrade Regatta ([sailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing_\(sport\)) regatta).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTE24_sata8_August_2011-155]

The river is also the site of the [Šabac Swimming Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0abac_Swimming_Marathon?action=edit\&redlink=1)—an [open water swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_water_swimming) competition, running on an 18.8-kilometre (11.7 mi) course between the village of [Jarak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarak) and the city of Šabac in Serbia. The competition is held annually since 1970, and was included in [FINA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FINA) international calendar from 1984 to 2012.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEŠabac_Marathon2013-156]

Recreational and [sport fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_fishing) is a popular activity along the Sava River course.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHabekovićHomenFašaić19909-116] There is a 700 metres (2,300 feet) long sport fishing competition ground near [Hotemež](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoteme%C5%BE), Slovenia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESlovenian_Tourist_Board-157]

## Tradition

Worship of various river gods in the area dates to the [Late Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Bronze_Age),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERendić-Miočević2012300-158] when the first settlements were founded along the Sava River.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVasićMandićNedeljkov201178-159] [Taurisci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurisci) associated their river goddess [Adsullata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adsullata) with the *Savus*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEŠašel_Kos200946-160][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERendić-Miočević2012300-158] Altars or inscriptions dedicated to the river-god Savus have been found at a number of locations along the river course, including at the Zelenci Pools where the Sava Dolinka rises, and a number of [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) settlements and [castra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castra) built along the Via Pannonia, the [Roman road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_road) running from [Aquileia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquileia) to the Danube.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDelukaDragčevićRukavina2003739-161] The settlements include [Emona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emona), [Andautonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andautonia) and [Siscia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siscia) (near modern-day Ljubljana, [Velika Gorica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velika_Gorica) and Sisak respectively) upstream of the Kupa River confluence, and [Marsonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsonia), itself built atop a [prehistoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory) settlement,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuzov2011367-162] [Cibalae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cibalae), [Sirmium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirmium) and [Singidunum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singidunum) (in modern-day Slavonski Brod, Vinkovci, Sremska Mitrovica and Belgrade) downstream of the Kupa.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBuzov2011369-163] Besides the altar found at the Zelenci Pools, inscriptions and sites dedicated to Savus have been found in remains of Emona,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEŠašel_Kos200943-164] Andautonia and Siscia.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERendić-Miočević2012294-165] Several years after 1751 completion of the [Robba Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robba_Fountain) in Ljubljana, the three male figures sculpted as parts of the fountain became identified with the river gods of Sava, Krka and Ljubljanica. In the early 20th century, the fountain was named the Fountain of Three [Carniolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carniola) Rivers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENational_Gallery_of_Slovenia-166]

The [Romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_poetry) poet [France Prešeren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_Pre%C5%A1eren) wrote *[The Baptism on the Savica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Baptism_on_the_Savica)* (Slovene: *Krst pri Savici*), the [Slovene](246311.md) [national epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_epic), in 1835. The poem, referring in its title to a headwater of the Sava River, helped to inspire the design of the [coat of arms of Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Slovenia) of 1991:



However, the two wavy lines at the base of the [blazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blazon) officially represent rivers of Slovenia and the Adriatic Sea rather than the Savica or the Sava specifically.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGCOJune_20119-167]

The Sava River also appears symbolically in the coat of arms of the former [Kingdom of Slavonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Slavonia):



The design, approved by the [Vladislaus II of Bohemia and Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladislaus_II_of_Bohemia_and_Hungary) in 1496, incorporates two [bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(heraldry\)) symbolising the Sava and the Drava rivers tracing the borders of the kingdom. The design inspired the arms of several present-day [counties of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_Croatia) in the region of [Slavonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavonia) and itself forms a part of the [coat of arms of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Croatia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrunčić200344-168] The poem *Horvatska domovina*, written by [Antun Mihanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antun_Mihanovi%C4%87) in 1835 as a national symbol of Croatia, also refers to the Sava River. Modified lyrics of the poem later became the [Croatian anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_anthem).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMVPEI-169]
