**Tareq Badawi Kamleh** (born 1985), known by his *[nom de guerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nom_de_guerre)* **Abu Yousef al-Australie**, is an [Australian](11197621.md) citizen and medical doctor who went to [Syria](7515849.md) to join the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md). After joining ISIL, he worked in paediatrics in their Syrian capital of [Raqqa](858965.md), in the [Islamic State Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_Health_Service). He appeared in two ISIL propaganda videos, in April 2015 and July 2017. Kamleh is currently wanted by the [Australian Federal Police](341221.md) for terrorism offenses. Kamleh was believed to have been killed in the final battle for Raqqa in September or October 2017.[^cite_note-\:10-3] In 2026, it was reported that Kamleh is in fact alive and imprisoned in [Baghdad, Iraq](4492.md).

## Early life, education and career

Kamleh was born in 1985 in [Perth, Western Australia](24355.md). His father was born and raised in [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)), before migrating to [Australia](4689264.md). His mother was born in [Germany](11867.md), before migrating to Australia. She was raised [Catholic](606848.md) and converted to [Islam](6037917.md).[^cite_note-Hatch-4] Kamleh's parents met in [Iraq](7515928.md) while his mother was working for a German company there. He has an older sister.[^cite_note-\:8-5] He attended the Al-Hidayah Islamic School and [Lynwood Senior High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynwood_Senior_High_School).[^cite_note-\:0-6]

At 20, Kamleh travelled to [New Zealand](4913064.md) to attend the [University of Otago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Otago). Kamleh's goal was to get into the medical school there. After a year, he failed and left the country. He went back Australia where he was admitted to the [University of Adelaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Adelaide), graduating with a [Bachelor of Medicine and Surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Medicine,_Bachelor_of_Surgery) in 2010.[^cite_note-Jonhston-7]

After graduating, Kamleh was assigned to paediatric unit in the [Women and Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_and_Children's_Hospital_\(Australia\)) in [Adelaide](1148.md) in February 2011. After two years, Kamleh transferred to [Mackay Base Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackay_Base_Hospital) in [Queensland](59051.md) from January 2013.[^cite_note-Mills-8] During this time, Kamleh also worked at Alice Springs Hospital.[^cite_note-\:1-9] His co-workers called him "lazy".[^cite_note-\:2-10] He was a registered doctor in Western Australia prior to 2015. Consequent to Kamleh's appearance in an ISIL recruitment video, the Medical Board of Australia confirmed on 21 June 2015 that it would be suspending his medical registration to "manage serious risk to public health and safety".[^cite_note-MBA-11]

According to media reports, Kamleh’s peers saw him as somewhat of a playboy. He was a practicing Muslim, but up until 2014 his lifestyle was not consistent with the teachings of Islam; he had multiple romantic affairs and drank alcohol regularly.[^cite_note-Sheikh-12][^cite_note-CETP-13] As an intern, he won [Royal Adelaide Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Adelaide_Hospital)'s "Golden Speculum", a joke award for the intern who sleeps with the most members of staff.[^cite_note-14]

Starting in 2014, Kamleh told friends he had reconnected to his faith and given up alcohol and women. He became increasingly religious.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-\:0-6] He stopped drinking, avoided his friends and began growing a beard; one acquaintance said he turned into a "straight hard-line fundamentalist".[^cite_note-16] Kamleh later said he had once suggested to his mother that he might travel to Turkey to help with Syrian refugees, and his mother threatened to call the authorities and have his passport canceled if he did.[^cite_note-\:9-17] His parents, who are practicing Muslims, said they were unaware that he had had any [extremist](8734632.md) beliefs. He lived near their home in Perth and when he said goodbye to them, he told them he was travelling to [Melbourne](17306237.md) for a two-week vacation.[^cite_note-\:8-5]

## ISIL

Kamleh left Australia on 10 March 2015,[^cite_note-\:1-9] flying to [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md) in [Malaysia](3607937.md), then to [Turkey](11125639.md), before crossing the border illegally into [Syria](7515849.md). This was first brought to the Australian government's attention after Kamleh appeared in a recruitment video for ISIL on 29 April 2015. In June, a warrant was issued for his arrest for membership in a terrorist organization.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-West-19] Kamleh appeared in three minutes of the 15-minute video, which was called "health services in the Islamic state" and appeared to be filmed in a medical facility. He appealed to Muslim medical personnel to join him in Raqqa because, he said, "Muslims are really suffering from not lack of equipment or medicine but lack of qualified medical care."[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-\:3-21]

He posted an open letter to the [Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Health_Practitioner_Regulation_Agency) on his Facebook page, saying he did not care if his medical registration or passport were canceled because he no longer considered himself to be an Australian. He denied the reports about his having been a womaniser and a drinker and denied having "ever taken part in unprofessional conduct which would have jeopardised my doctor-patient relationship" and said, "I have come here as there are locals suffering from normal medical conditions despite being surrounded by war, with an overt lack of qualified medical care."

Consequent to arriving in Syria, Kamleh met [Ariel Bradley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Bradley), an American woman who left [Tennessee](30395.md) to fight for ISIL and was previously married to Yasin Mohamad, who was killed in an [airstrike](323167.md) in 2015. Bradley believed it was her civic duty to marry an ISIL fighter to support the cause. She and Kamleh married after Mohamad's death.[^cite_note-CETP-13] Bradley brought two children into the marriage.[^cite_note-22] She and Kamleh had a son in 2016.[^cite_note-\:4-23] Kamleh also had another wife, a Somali nurse who worked with him at the hospital.[^cite_note-us_born_girl-24][^cite_note-\:4-23] She used the [nom de guerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nom_de_guerre) Umm Yakeen.[^cite_note-\:6-25] Kamleh used the nom de guerre "Abu Yousef Al-Australie", aka "Abu Yusuf". He was also nicknamed "Dr. Jihad" by the media.[^cite_note-Wahlquist-26]

In July 2017, Kamleh appeared in a second ISIL propaganda video, which first showed him tending to a child who had been wounded in an airstrike, then showed him sitting in a dark tunnel dressed in combat gear with a tactical vest and carrying an [AK-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-47), fighting for ISIL.[^cite_note-Wroe-27][^cite_note-\:2-10][^cite_note-\:5-28] In the video he said ISIL fighters "love death more than you love life."[^cite_note-29] He directly addressed [Donald Trump](4848272.md), claiming ISIL could not wait to battle American forces. He also addressed the [world's Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummah), saying, "We are under continuous bombing here and we are fighting hard to try to hold the lands of Islam... What more will it take for it to be a justified cause for you to come here to fight for Allah’s sake?"[^cite_note-\:5-28]

Kamleh reportedly kept a journal during his time in Raqqa, which was found by a British soldier during the raid of an abandoned ISIL safe house in 2017; it had Kamleh's name on it. The journal included Kamleh's feelings about the war, claiming he was unhappy with ISIL's refusal to support an orphanage he was involved with and also because of the way ISIL fighters treated animals. He wrote, "I despair for the future of the [caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate)."[^cite_note-Wroe-27][^cite_note-30]

## Possible death and fate of family

Kamleh is currently wanted by the [Australian Federal Police](341221.md) for crimes of terrorism and faces up to 25 years in jail if he returns to Australia. An arrest warrant has been filed for him in Adelaide, claiming offences he apparently committed in Raqqa in 2015 after leaving Australia, including joining and aiding a terrorist organization.[^cite_note-Wroe-27]

Kamleh was reported to have died during the final battle for Raqqa in September or October 2017.[^cite_note-\:10-3] Bradley and their son were reportedly killed in an airstrike in a village near [Hajin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajin) in November 2018.[^cite_note-\:4-23]

After her death, Kamleh’s Somali second wife, Umm Yakeen, took in Bradley’s young daughter from a previous marriage.[^cite_note-us_born_girl-24][^cite_note-\:6-25][^cite_note-\:4-23] After ISIL lost the last of its territory at the [Battle of Baghuz Fawqani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Baghuz_Fawqani), Bradley’s daughter and Umm Yakeen were sent to the [Al-Hawl refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Hawl_refugee_camp), then to the [Al-Roj refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Roj_refugee_camp). The woman was hiding her because she did not want her to be taken to the United States, where the girl had been born. In July 2021, the [Syrian Democratic Forces](48205248.md) raided the section of Al-Roj where Umm Yakeen and the child were living, and rescued the by then eight-year-old girl. She was sent to a rehabilitation center pending transfer to the United States, where her relatives live in the [Chattanooga, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chattanooga,_Tennessee) area.[^cite_note-\:4-23]

In July 2026, reports emerged that Kamleh was alive, imprisoned in Baghdad and in ill health.[^cite_note-31] He is believed to have retreated during the [Battle of Raqqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Raqqa_\(2017\)) with other ISIL members and survived for two years undetected, working as a field doctor, until he eventually surrendered to or was arrested by the [Syrian Democratic Forces](48205248.md) in March 2019. He was then extradited to Iraq and has been imprisoned there ever since. He is currently one of 13 men linked to ISIL who are seeking repatriation to Australia.[^cite_note-\:7-32][^cite_note-33] The Australian government had reportedly been aware he was alive since at least 2022, and told no one, including Kamleh's parents and sister back in Australia.[^cite_note-\:8-5]

After it was revealed that he was alive, Kamleh was identified as the incarcerated man in Iraq whose anonymous interview with a German media outlet was published in July 2025. In the interview, Kamleh denied having ever formally joined ISIL, trained with them or pledged allegiance to [Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr_al-Baghdadi), and said he’d only lived and worked in the ISIL-occupied area as a pediatrician. He said he was troubled by the Syrian civil war and wanted to help, and to that end he tried to join [Doctors Without Borders](20498.md). When the charity told him they were not sending any volunteers to Syria, Kamleh said, he decided to go on his own. The place where he entered Syria happened to be ISIL territory, he said, and ISIL provided housing for himself and his family. He said ISIL had forced him to record the propaganda videos he was in, and that he did not know why he had been arrested and detained.[^cite_note-\:9-17]
