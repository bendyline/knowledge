The **Buffalo Sabres** are a professional [ice hockey](14790.md) team based in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York). The Sabres compete in the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL) as a member of the [Atlantic Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Division_\(NHL\)) in the [Eastern Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Conference_\(NHL\)). The team was established in [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970%E2%80%9371_NHL_season), along with the [Vancouver Canucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks), when the league expanded to 14 teams. The Sabres have played their home games at [KeyBank Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KeyBank_Center) since [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_NHL_season), having previously played at the [Buffalo Memorial Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Memorial_Auditorium) since their inception. The Sabres are owned by [Terry Pegula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Pegula), who purchased the club in 2011 from [Tom Golisano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Golisano).

The team has twice advanced to the [Stanley Cup Final](3314237.md), losing to the [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers) in [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Stanley_Cup_Final) and to the [Dallas Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Stars) in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Stanley_Cup_Final). The Sabres, along with the Canucks, are the oldest active NHL franchises to have never won the Stanley Cup.[^cite_note-4] The Sabres held the [NHL record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NHL_franchise_post-season_droughts) longest playoff drought in the NHL at 14 seasons, which occurred between the [2010–11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_NHL_season) and [2025–26](79321758.md) seasons.[^cite_note-14thseason-5]

## History

### Early years and the French Connection (1970–1981)



[Floyd Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floyd_Smith_\(ice_hockey\)) served as the first team captain in 1970. Four years later, he became head coach, with his first season seeing the team reach the [Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Stanley_Cup_Final) for the first time in franchise history.

The Sabres, along with the [Vancouver Canucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks), joined the NHL in the [1970–71 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970%E2%80%9371_NHL_season).[^cite_note-6] Their first owners were [Seymour H. Knox III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seymour_H._Knox_III) and [Northrup Knox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrup_Knox), scions of a family long prominent in [Western New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_York) and grandsons of the co-founders of the [Woolworth's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._W._Woolworth_Company) variety store chain; along with [Robert O. Swados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_O._Swados), a Buffalo attorney.[^cite_note-7] On the team's inaugural board of directors were [Robert E. Rich Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_E._Rich_Jr.), later the owner of the [Buffalo Bisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bisons) minor league baseball team;[^cite_note-8] and [George W. Strawbridge Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_W._Strawbridge_Jr.), an heir to the [Campbell's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campbell's) fortune.[^cite_note-9] Buffalo had a history of professional hockey; immediately prior to the Sabres' establishment, the [Buffalo Bisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bisons_\(AHL\)) were a pillar of the [American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) (AHL), having existed since 1940 (and before that, another [Bisons hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bisons_\(IHL\)) played from 1928 to 1936), winning the [Calder Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calder_Cup) in their final season.[^cite_note-10]

Wanting a name other than "[bison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bison)" (a generic stock name used by Buffalo sports teams for decades), the Knoxes commissioned a name-the-team contest. With names like "[Mugwumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mugwumps)", "Buzzing Bees" and "Flying Zeppelins" being entered,[^cite_note-11] the winning choice, "Sabres", was chosen because Seymour Knox felt a [saber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabre) was a weapon carried by a leader, and could be effective on both offense and defense.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The Knoxes tried twice before to get an NHL team, first when the NHL [expanded in 1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_NHL_Expansion), and again when they attempted to purchase the [Oakland Seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Seals) with the intent of moving them to Buffalo. Their first attempt was thwarted when [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers) owner [Art Rooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Rooney) persuaded his [horse racing](74711.md) friends [James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_D._Norris) and [Bruce Norris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Norris_\(ice_hockey\)) to select [Pittsburgh](25101.md) over Buffalo.[^cite_note-15] The second attempt was due to the NHL not wanting an expansion market to give up on a team so soon, nor isolate the [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings) (the only NHL team other than the Seals west of St. Louis at the time) from the rest of the NHL entirely.[^cite_note-16] At the time of their creation, the Sabres created their own AHL [farm team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farm_team), the [Cincinnati Swords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati_Swords).[^cite_note-17] Former [Toronto Maple Leafs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs) general manager and head coach [Punch Imlach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch_Imlach) was hired in the same capacity with the Sabres.[^cite_note-18]

The Sabres' debut in 1970 also coincided with the [AFL–NFL merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL%E2%80%93NFL_merger) in which the [Buffalo Bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bills) joined the [National Football League](21211.md),[^cite_note-19] and the [National Basketball Association](22093.md)'s [Buffalo Braves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Braves) also began to play.[^cite_note-20] The city of Buffalo went from having no teams in the established [major professional sports leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_of_the_United_States_and_Canada) to three in one off-season. Between the Braves and the Sabres, the Sabres would prove to be by far the more successful of the two; [Paul Snyder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Snyder_\(basketball\)), the *[nouveau riche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouveau_riche)* Braves owner, publicly feuded with the [old money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_money) Knoxes and the local [college basketball](768856.md) scene, eventually losing those feuds and being forced to sell his team in 1976.[^cite_note-Ownersfeud-21] Subsequent owners of the Braves, in a series of convoluted transactions tied to the [ABA–NBA merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABA%E2%80%93NBA_merger), moved the team out of Buffalo.[^cite_note-Ownersfeud-21]

When the Sabres debuted as an expansion team, they used [Aram Khachaturian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aram_Khachaturian)'s Armenian war dance "[Sabre Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabre_Dance)" as their entrance song.[^cite_note-22] The music has been associated with the team ever since, typically at the beginning of each period.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

The consensus was that the first pick in the [1970 NHL amateur draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_NHL_amateur_draft) would be [junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_ice_hockey) phenomenon [Gilbert Perreault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Perreault). Either the Sabres or the Canucks would get the first pick, to be determined with the spin of a [wheel of fortune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Six_wheel). Perreault was available to the Sabres and Canucks as this was the first year the [Montreal Canadiens](42966.md) did not have a priority right to draft [Quebec](7954867.md)-born junior players. The Canucks were allocated numbers 1–10 on the wheel, while the Sabres had 11–20. When league president [Clarence Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Campbell) spun the wheel, he initially thought the pointer landed on one. While Campbell was congratulating the Vancouver delegation, Imlach asked Campbell to check again. As it turned out, the pointer was on 11, effectively handing Perreault to the Sabres.[^cite_note-25] Perreault scored 38 goals in his rookie season of 1970–71, at the time a record for most goals scored by an NHL rookie, and he received the [Calder Memorial Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calder_Memorial_Trophy) as the NHL's rookie of the year.[^cite_note-26]



A statue of the [French Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_French_Connection_\(ice_hockey\)) line stands outside [KeyBank Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KeyBank_Center). Consisting of [Gilbert Perreault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Perreault), [Rick Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Martin), and [Rene Robert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Robert), they played together from 1972 to 1979.

During the team's [second season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_Buffalo_Sabres_season), rookie [Rick Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Martin), drafted fifth overall by Buffalo in [1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_NHL_amateur_draft),[^cite_note-RickMartin-27] and recently acquired [Rene Robert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Robert)[^cite_note-ReneRobert-28] joined Perreault to become one of the league's top forward [lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_\(ice_hockey\)) in the 1970s. Martin broke Perreault's goal-scoring record as a [rookie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rookie) with 44 goals.[^cite_note-RickMartin-27] They were nicknamed "[The French Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_French_Connection_\(hockey\))" after [the movie of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_French_Connection_\(film\)) and in homage to their [French-Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Canadians) roots.[^cite_note-29] The Sabres made the playoffs for the first time in [1972–73](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972%E2%80%9373_NHL_season), just the team's third year in the league, but lost in the quarterfinals in six games to the eventual [Stanley Cup](66968.md) champion Montreal Canadiens.[^cite_note-30]

After the [1973–74 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973%E2%80%9374_Buffalo_Sabres_season), which saw them miss the playoffs (as well as defenseman [Tim Horton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Horton)'s death in a [DUI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driving_under_the_influence)-induced car accident[^cite_note-31]), the Sabres did an odd moment at the [1974 NHL amateur draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_NHL_amateur_draft). General manager [Punch Imlach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch_Imlach), annoyed at the length of the draft, decided to draft a fictitious Japanese player named [Taro Tsujimoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taro_Tsujimoto); the pick was subsequently invalidated.[^cite_note-32] [That season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_Buffalo_Sabres_season), they tied for the best record in the NHL in the regular season.[^cite_note-33] Buffalo advanced to the [Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Stanley_Cup_Final) for the first time in team history to play against the [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers), a series which included the legendary [Fog Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Stanley_Cup_Final#Fog_and_the_bat) (game three of the series).[^cite_note-Fog_Game-34] Due to unusual heat in Buffalo in May 1975 and the lack of air conditioning in the [Buffalo Memorial Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Memorial_Auditorium), parts of the game were played in heavy fog that made players, officials, and the puck invisible to many spectators.[^cite_note-Fog_Game-34] During a face-off and through the fog, Sabres center [Jim Lorentz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Lorentz) spotted a [bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat) flying across the rink, swung at it with his stick, killing it. It was the only time that any player killed an animal during an NHL game. The Sabres won that game thanks to Rene Robert's goal in [overtime](568092.md). However, Philadelphia would wind up taking the Stanley Cup in six games, winning the series 4–2.[^cite_note-Fog_Game-34]

Although [Danny Gare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Gare) scored 50 goals in the [1975–76 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975%E2%80%9376_Buffalo_Sabres_season),[^cite_note-35] the team lost in the quarterfinals to the [New York Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Islanders). The team did not return to the Final with the French Connection despite a [Wales Conference championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Wales_Trophy) in [1979–80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979%E2%80%9380_NHL_season) and being the first team to beat a team from the Soviet Union during the [Super Series '76](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Series_'76).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] The French Connection era ended with Robert's trade to the [Colorado Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Rockies_\(ice_hockey\)) in 1979 and Martin's trade to the Los Angeles Kings in 1981.[^cite_note-ReneRobert-28][^cite_note-RickMartin-27] All three players have had their jersey numbers (11, 7 and 14, respectively) retired and a statue erected in their honor at KeyBank Center in 2012.[^cite_note-38]

### Adams/Northeast Division rivalries (1981–1996)

In [1981–82](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981%E2%80%9382_NHL_season), the NHL realigned its conferences and adopted an intra-divisional playoff format for the first two rounds.[^cite_note-39] It was the beginning of an era in which the Sabres would finish in the middle of the Adams Division standings with regularity, and then face the near-certainty of having to get past either the [Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bruins) or Canadiens to make it to the conference finals. Aside from first-round victories over Montreal in [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) and Boston in [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Stanley_Cup_playoffs),[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-1993G4-41] the era saw the Sabres lose to division rivals Boston, the [Quebec Nordiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Nordiques) and Montreal in the Adams Division semifinals (first round) a combined eight times, and miss the playoffs altogether in 1985–86 and 1986–87—only third and fourth times out of the playoffs in franchise history.[^cite_note-42] Perrault reached the 500-goal mark in the 1985–86 season and retired after playing 20 games in 1986–87, 17 years after joining the Sabres as their first draft pick.[^cite_note-43]

The Sabres drafted [Pierre Turgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Turgeon) with the first pick in the [1987 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_NHL_entry_draft), and he quickly made an impact with the team.[^cite_note-44] During his rookie season in 1987–88, he helped the Sabres reach the [playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) for the first time in three years. He was joined in 1989 by [Alexander Mogilny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Mogilny), who with the help of Sabres officials became the first Soviet player to defect to the NHL.[^cite_note-45] In the 1989–90 season, the Sabres would improve to finish with 98 points—third-best in the NHL,[^cite_note-46] but the playoff futility continued with a first-round loss to Montreal.[^cite_note-47] The Sabres traded Turgeon to the New York Islanders in 1991 as part of a blockbuster seven-player trade that brought [Pat LaFontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_LaFontaine) to Buffalo.[^cite_note-48]

In 1992–93, goaltender [Dominik Hašek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominik_Ha%C5%A1ek) joined the team in a trade from the [Chicago Blackhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Blackhawks).[^cite_note-49] In the [1993 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), the Sabres upset the Bruins in a four-game sweep in the Adams Division semifinals, their first playoff series victory in ten years.[^cite_note-1993G4-41] [Brad May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_May)'s series-winning goal in overtime of game four in Buffalo was made famous by Rick Jeanneret's "May Day!" call.[^cite_note-50] However, the eventual Stanley Cup champion Montreal Canadiens swept the Sabres in the division final, with the Sabres losing all four games by a 4–3 score.[^cite_note-51]

With the NHL adopting a conference playoff format for the [1993–94 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993%E2%80%9394_NHL_season), the Sabres faced the [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils) in the Eastern Conference playoffs' first round. Despite Hašek winning a 1–0 quadruple overtime goaltending duel with the Devils' [Martin Brodeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Brodeur) in game six,[^cite_note-52] Buffalo would lose the series in seven games.[^cite_note-53] Another first-round playoff loss to the Philadelphia Flyers in the lockout-shortened [1994–95 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_NHL_season) was followed by a fifth-place finish in the Northeast Division in 1995–96,[^cite_note-54] as the team missed the playoffs for the first time in nine years. It was the first season under head coach [Ted Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Nolan) and the last for the Sabres at Buffalo Memorial Auditorium. During his coaching tenure, Buffalo was referred to as the "hardest-working team in hockey".[^cite_note-BuffNews-55] This season also featured the debut of veteran [Randy Burridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Burridge), who earned a spot on the roster after he attended training camp on a try-out basis. He scored 25 goals that season and was second in team scoring to Pat LaFontaine.[^cite_note-56] Burridge also earned the Tim Horton Award for being the unsung hero and was voted team [most valuable player](235000.md).[^cite_note-57]

The final game in Memorial Auditorium was played on April 14, 1996, a 4–1 victory over the [Hartford Whalers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford_Whalers).[^cite_note-58] Sabres principal owner Seymour Knox died a month later, on May 22, 1996.[^cite_note-59]

### Final trip and ownership turnover (1996–2005)

Ted Nolan and the Sabres rebounded in [1996–97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_NHL_season), their first at [Marine Midland Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Midland_Arena), by winning their first division title in 16 years, with Nolan winning the [Jack Adams Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Adams_Award) as the NHL's top coach,[^cite_note-60] Dominik Hašek winning both the [Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hart_Memorial_Trophy) and [Vezina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vezina_Trophy) Trophies (the first goaltender to do so since Montreal's [Jacques Plante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Plante) in 1962),[^cite_note-61] [Michael Peca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Peca) taking home the [Frank J. Selke Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_J._Selke_Trophy) as the best defensive forward in the NHL and general manager [John Muckler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Muckler) honored as Executive of the Year.[^cite_note-62] The team also acquired [Miroslav Šatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miroslav_%C5%A0atan) in a trade with the [Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Oilers).[^cite_note-63]

However, the regular season success was overshadowed by what had taken place during the playoffs. Tensions between Nolan and Hašek had been high for most of the season.[^cite_note-NolanFeud-64] After being scored upon in game three of the first round against the [Ottawa Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Senators), Hašek left the game, forcing backup [Steve Shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Shields_\(hockey_player\)) to step in. Hašek claimed he felt his knee pop, and the team doctor pronounced him day-to-day. *[The Buffalo News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Buffalo_News)* columnist [Jim Kelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Kelley) wrote a column that night for the next day's newspaper questioning the severity of the injury,[^cite_note-65] which irked Hašek. After the Senators won game five, Hašek came out of the Sabres' training room and attacked Kelley, tearing his shirt.[^cite_note-66] Despite the fact Hašek issued an apology, things went downhill after the incident. Shields started as the Sabres goaltender for the remainder of the series as the team rallied to defeat Ottawa. However, before the next series against the Philadelphia Flyers, the NHL announced Hašek had been suspended for three games, with the Sabres informing the NHL Hašek was healthy.[^cite_note-67] Set to return in game four with the Sabres down by three games to none, Hašek told the Sabres' coaching staff he felt a twinge in his knee and left the ice after the pre-game skate.[^cite_note-68] Although the Sabres staved off elimination in game four, they lost in game five.[^cite_note-69]

Team president [Larry Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Quinn_\(ice_hockey\)) fired general manager John Muckler, who had a noted feud with Nolan.[^cite_note-70] Hašek, who supported Muckler, openly told reporters at the NHL Awards Ceremony he did not respect Nolan.[^cite_note-NolanFeud-64] The new general manager, [Darcy Regier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darcy_Regier), offered Nolan just a one-year contract, but Nolan refused. Regier then pulled the contract off the table and did not offer another one, ending Nolan's tenure as Sabres coach.[^cite_note-71] Former Sabres captain [Lindy Ruff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindy_Ruff) was hired as head coach on July 21, 1997, agreeing to a three-year contract.[^cite_note-72]

#### New owners and return to the Final

During the [1997–98 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_NHL_season), the Sabres, which had lost \$32 million over the previous three seasons and nearly missed payroll in December 1997, were sold by Northrop Knox to [John Rigas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rigas), owner of [Adelphia Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelphia_Communications).[^cite_note-73] Shortly thereafter, Quinn was dismissed and replaced by John's son, Timothy Rigas.[^cite_note-74] The Sabres reached the conference finals that season, but lost to the [Washington Capitals](73125.md) in six games.[^cite_note-75]



The Sabres playing a game during the [1998–99 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_NHL_season). The Sabres were later crowned the Eastern Conference champions following the [1999 Stanley Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Stanley_Cup_playoffs).

In the [1998–99 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_NHL_season), Šatan scored 40 goals,[^cite_note-76] doing so during the [dead puck era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_National_Hockey_League_\(1992%E2%80%932017\)#Dead_puck_era) where goal-scoring had dropped to 5.19 goals per game.[^cite_note-77] The Sabres also added centers [Stu Barnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stu_Barnes) from the Pittsburgh Penguins and [Joe Juneau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A9_Juneau) from the Capitals.[^cite_note-78] The team returned to the [Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Stanley_Cup_Final), this time against the [Presidents' Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents'_Trophy)-winning [Dallas Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Stars).[^cite_note-79] In game six, [Brett Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Hull)'s triple-overtime goal ended the series, and the Stars were awarded the Cup. In 1999, it was illegal to score a goal if an offensive player's skate entered the crease before the puck did. However, NHL officials maintained that Hull's two shots in the crease constituted a single possession of the puck since the puck deflected off Hašek. The rule was changed for the following season, allowing players to be inside the goaltender's crease as long as they do not interfere with the goaltender.[^cite_note-80]

In the [1999–2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999%E2%80%932000_NHL_season), [Doug Gilmour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Gilmour) was acquired from the Chicago Blackhawks at the trade deadline.[^cite_note-81] Hašek was also sidelined for three months due to a torn groin, but returned with two months left in the regular season.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] Gilmour on the other hand was stricken by stomach flu just before the postseason.[^cite_note-84] The Sabres finished as the eighth seed in the Eastern Conference, clinching a playoff spot on the final day of the regular season with a 1–1 tie against the Capitals.[^cite_note-85] Like the previous playoffs season, there would be an officiating controversy. In game two, Flyers left wing [John LeClair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_LeClair) put the puck in the net through a hole in the mesh. While replays appeared to show the puck entering through the "side" of the net, the goal was allowed to stand.[^cite_note-86] The Flyers would win the game 2–1 and go on to win the series four games to one.[^cite_note-87]

[Captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_\(hockey\)) Michael Peca sat out the [2000–01 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_NHL_season) due to a contract dispute, and was later traded to the New York Islanders in June 2001 for [Tim Connolly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Connolly) and [Taylor Pyatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Pyatt).[^cite_note-88] Even so, the Sabres still defeated Philadelphia in six games during the first round of the playoffs, winning 8–0 in the final game.[^cite_note-89] In the second round, they faced the Pittsburgh Penguins but lost via a seventh-game overtime goal scored by defenseman [Darius Kasparaitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_Kasparaitis).[^cite_note-90] After lengthy and failed negotiations with their star goaltender, the Sabres traded Hašek to the [Detroit Red Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Red_Wings) in the summer of 2001,[^cite_note-91] closing out his career with Buffalo.[^cite_note-92]

#### Ownership turmoil and lockout

In May 2002, John Rigas and his sons were indicted for bank, wire and securities fraud for embezzling more than \$2 billion from Adelphia. Rigas was later convicted and served eight years of a 15-year sentence before his release in 2016.[^cite_note-93] The NHL took control of the team, though the Rigas family remained owners on paper.[^cite_note-94] After the two-year period of uncertainty, including rumors of relocating to another city or even outright folding,[^cite_note-95] the team was sold to a consortium led by [Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York) billionaire, and former New York gubernatorial candidate, [Tom Golisano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Golisano) and former Sabres president [Larry Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Quinn_\(ice_hockey\)).[^cite_note-96] Golisano was introduced as team owner on March 19, 2003.[^cite_note-97]

With the [2002–03 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_NHL_season) starting under NHL control, general manager Darcy Regier could make only minimal moves. However, with the consultations of impending new ownership, the team began their rebuilding process around the March 2003 trade deadline by clearing out veteran players. The first to go was winger [Rob Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Ray), who was sent to the Ottawa Senators.[^cite_note-RayBarnes_trade-98] The team then sent center and team captain Stu Barnes to the Dallas Stars in exchange for winger [Michael Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Ryan_\(ice_hockey\)) and a draft pick.[^cite_note-RayBarnes_trade-98] A third deal sent center [Chris Gratton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Gratton) to the [Phoenix Coyotes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Coyotes) with a draft pick for [Daniel Briere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Bri%C3%A8re) and a draft pick.[^cite_note-99] Over the summer, Regier also completed a three-team trade that landed them [Chris Drury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Drury).[^cite_note-100] Although the team improved during the [2003–04 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_NHL_season), they did not make the playoffs.[^cite_note-101] The next season was then curbed by the [2004–05 NHL lockout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_NHL_lockout).[^cite_note-04-05_lockout-102] Many of the players would play overseas or for the [Rochester Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_Americans) during the lockout.[^cite_note-04-05_lockout-102]

### Post-lockout success (2005–2010)



[Lindy Ruff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindy_Ruff) was awarded the [Jack Adams Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Adams_Award) in 2006. He was the second Sabres coach to win the award.

In 2005, the Sabres lost their main cable television broadcaster, as the [Empire Sports Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Sports_Network), which had been on the air since 1991, ceased operations during the Adelphia scandal and reorganization.[^cite_note-103] Like the Sabres, Empire had been owned by Adelphia prior to the NHL's seizure of the franchise. Adelphia sold their rights to Sabres telecasts and for the 2005–06 campaign [Madison Square Garden Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_Network) (MSG), a New York City-based channel which broadcasts [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers), New York Islanders and [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils) games, took over the rights to broadcast Sabres games to television viewers in [western New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_New_York), with the Sabres controlling all aspects of the broadcast. The agreement was later extended through 2017, then again through 2027.[^cite_note-104]

In the 2005–06 season, the Sabres finished with their best record in over 20 years and clinching their first playoff berth since the 2000–01 season. The team finished the regular season with 52 wins, surpassing the 50-win mark for the first time in franchise history. They also finished with 110 points, their first 100-point season in 23 years and tied the 1979–80 club for the second-best point total in franchise history.[^cite_note-105] The Sabres tied the Ottawa Senators and [Carolina Hurricanes](70523.md) for the most wins in the Eastern Conference. They finished with the fifth-best record in the NHL, behind Detroit, Ottawa, Dallas and Carolina.[^cite_note-106]

Buffalo defeated the Philadelphia Flyers in the first round of the [2006 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) in six games, then the top-seeded Ottawa in five games in the second round.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] The Sabres advanced to play Carolina in their first conference finals since [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Stanley_Cup_playoffs). However, injuries began to mount. They were forced to play without four of their top defensemen, [Teppo Numminen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teppo_Numminen),[^cite_note-2006injuries-109] [Dmitri Kalinin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitri_Kalinin),[^cite_note-2006injuries-109] [Jay McKee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_McKee),[^cite_note-110] and [Henrik Tallinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Tallinder),[^cite_note-2006injuries-109] and their top [powerplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_play_\(sporting_term\)) scorer, Tim Connolly, for much of the series.[^cite_note-111] Despite this, the Sabres forced the series to seven games before falling to the eventual Stanley Cup champions Carolina.[^cite_note-112] The Sabres' impressive season was recognized on June 22, 2006, at the NHL Awards Ceremony when Lindy Ruff edged Hurricanes coach [Peter Laviolette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Laviolette) to win the Jack Adams Award as coach of the year in the closest vote in the award's history.[^cite_note-113] Ruff was the second Sabres coach to win the award.



[Thomas Vanek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Vanek) was re-signed in 2007 after the [Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Oilers) offered him a seven-year offer sheet.

The Sabres started the [2006–07 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_NHL_season) 10–0, setting a new franchise record for consecutive wins to start a season, and becoming just the second team in NHL history, after the 1993–94 Toronto Maple Leafs, to open a season with a ten-game winning streak.[^cite_note-114] They also set a new NHL record for consecutive road wins to start a season (eight), which was extended to ten games (tying the team record for consecutive road wins) after a 7–4 win over the Carolina Hurricanes on November 13, 2006.[^cite_note-115] This record would be beaten by the [2023–24 Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Los_Angeles_Kings_season).[^cite_note-116] The team reached the 50-win plateau for the second time in franchise history. The Sabres won the [Presidents' Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents'_Trophy) for the first time in franchise history, giving them the home-ice advantage for their entire run in the [2007 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Stanley_Cup_playoffs). They also tied the [1974–75 team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_Buffalo_Sabres_season)'s franchise record for points in a season.[^cite_note-117] The team defeated the New York Islanders and the New York Rangers to reach their second consecutive conference finals.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] However, on May 19, they were eliminated by the Ottawa Senators after five games.[^cite_note-120]

In the April 9, 2007, issue of *[ESPN the Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_the_Magazine)*, the team ranked first of 122 major professional sports franchises in North America. The Sabres were cited for their player accessibility, low ticket prices and exciting brand of hockey.[^cite_note-121]

#### Post-Briere–Drury era

On July 1, 2007, the Sabres lost both co-captains, with Daniel Briere going to the Philadelphia Flyers and [Chris Drury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Drury) going to the New York Rangers as free agents.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123] The team also nearly lost [Thomas Vanek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Vanek) to the [Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Oilers), which offered him a seven-year, \$50 million offer sheet, but the Sabres matched the offer on July 6.[^cite_note-124] After these events, the team changed its policy of not negotiating contracts during the regular season.[^cite_note-125] Long-time Sabres broadcast color commentator [Jim Lorentz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Lorentz) announced his retirement during the preseason.[^cite_note-126] *[Hockey Night in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_Night_in_Canada)'*&#x73; [Harry Neale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Neale) took over the position in October 2007.[^cite_note-Neale-127]



In 2008, the Sabres hosted the [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) at [Ralph Wilson Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Wilson_Stadium) in the inaugural [Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_NHL_Winter_Classic).

During the [2007–08 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_NHL_season), the Sabres hosted a game against the Pittsburgh Penguins on January 1, 2008, which was played outdoors at [Ralph Wilson Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Wilson_Stadium), home of the [Buffalo Bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bills) of the [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL).[^cite_note-128] The [2008 Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_NHL_Winter_Classic), known colloquially as the "Ice Bowl" due to it taking place at the same time as [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football)'s [bowl games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowl_game).[^cite_note-129] The Sabres lost 2–1 in a shootout.[^cite_note-130] The Sabres failed to qualify for the [2008 playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) and became only the third team in NHL history to go from finishing first overall in the regular season standings to finishing out of the playoffs the following year.[^cite_note-131]

On June 10, the Sabres officially announced their new AHL affiliate, beginning in the [2008–09 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_AHL_season), would be the [Portland Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_Pirates) from [Portland, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Maine). This ended their 29-year affiliation with the [Rochester Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_Americans). They signed with the Pirates for two seasons, with a parent club option for a third.[^cite_note-Buffalo_Sabres_News-132] In the offseason, the Sabres acquired [Craig Rivet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Rivet) from the [San Jose Sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Sharks) in exchange for a second-round draft pick in each of the next two drafts.[^cite_note-133] The Sabres also extended the contracts of some players including [Ryan Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Miller) (five years) and [Jason Pominville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Pominville) (five years).[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135] Miller was slated to become an unrestricted free agent the upcoming season while Pominville was set to become a restricted free agent. On October 8, the Sabres named defenseman Craig Rivet team captain, the first single full-time captain since [Stu Barnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stu_Barnes)' term from 2001 to 2003.[^cite_note-136] The team was also active at the trade deadline. First, they signed Tim Connolly to a two-year, \$4.2 million extension, then acquired [Mikael Tellqvist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikael_Tellqvist) from the Phoenix Coyotes for a fourth-round pick in the [2010 draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_NHL_entry_draft).[^cite_note-137] [Dominic Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Moore) came from the Toronto Maple Leafs during the [2009 draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_NHL_entry_draft).[^cite_note-138] On April 9, the Buffalo Sabres were eliminated from the playoffs.[^cite_note-139]



[Mikael Tellqvist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikael_Tellqvist) was acquired by the Sabres on March 4, 2009. He was their backup goaltender for the remainder of the [2008–09 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_NHL_season).

General manager Darcy Regier announced on the first day of free agency for the [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_NHL_season) the Sabres had signed unrestricted free agent defenseman [Steve Montador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Montador) to a two-year contract.[^cite_note-140] After only playing two games with Buffalo that season, [Daniel Paille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Paille) was traded to the [Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bruins) on October 20, 2009, in exchange for a third-round and a conditional fourth-round draft selection. Paille's move to Boston marked the first ever trade of a player under contract between the two division rivals in their common 39 years in the NHL.[^cite_note-141] [Tyler Ennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Ennis_\(ice_hockey\)), who was selected 26th in the [2008 draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_NHL_entry_draft), made his NHL debut this season.[^cite_note-142] On January 1, the Sabres became the first team to win consecutive games when trailing by three or more goals since the Dallas Stars did it in 2005–06; Buffalo defeated the [Atlanta Thrashers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_Thrashers) 4–3 in overtime. It was Buffalo's second straight win in a game it trailed 3–0, following a 4–3 victory over the Pittsburgh Penguins.[^cite_note-143] On March 27, the Sabres clinched their first playoff berth since 2006–07 with a 7–1 rout of the Tampa Bay Lightning.[^cite_note-144] On April 6, the Sabres clinched the Northeast Division title by defeating the New York Rangers by a score of 5–2.[^cite_note-145] However, they were defeated in six games by the Boston Bruins in the first round of the playoffs.[^cite_note-146]

The 2010–11 roster did not have many significant changes in the offseason. One of the most notable was the team's decision to waive center [Tim Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Kennedy_\(ice_hockey\)), a Buffalo native, to avoid paying the award he won in arbitration.[^cite_note-147] Defensemen [Henrik Tallinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Tallinder) and [Toni Lydman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Lydman) were allowed to leave as free agents,[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] while the team signed veterans [Jordan Leopold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Leopold) and [Shaone Morrisonn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaone_Morrisonn) to replace them.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151] Additionally, center [Rob Niedermayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Niedermayer) was added.[^cite_note-152]

### The Pegula era (2010–present)



On February 18, 2011, the sale of the Sabres franchise to [Terrence Pegula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrence_Pegula) was finalized.

On November 30, 2010, Ken Campbell of *[The Hockey News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hockey_News)* reported a story that billionaire [Terry Pegula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Pegula) had signed a letter of intent to purchase the Sabres for US\$150 million. Pegula was the founder, president and CEO of [East Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Resources), one of the largest privately held companies in the United States before he sold the company.[^cite_note-153] After the report was released, Sabres managing partner Larry Quinn claimed it was "untrue" but refused further comment.[^cite_note-154] The \$150 million was later determined to be an undervalued amount, as *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* magazine had valued the team at just under \$170 million in 2010. In December, Pegula officially expressed interest in buying the Sabres for \$170 million and submitted a letter of intent to the NHL. In January, Golisano reportedly issued a counteroffer with an asking price of US\$175 million.[^cite_note-155] Pegula and Golisano reached an agreement to sell the team on January 29, 2011, with Pegula purchasing the team for \$189 million (\$175 million with \$14 million in debt included) with the Sabres and Golisano officially making an announcement in a press conference on February 3, 2011.[^cite_note-156] NHL owners approved the sale on February 18.[^cite_note-157]

In the conference, it was stated that an unnamed bidder submitted a much higher bid than Pegula's, but made the bid contingent upon moving the team.[^cite_note-158] The description is consistent with that of [Jim Balsillie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Balsillie), who has made public his efforts to move a team to [Hamilton, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario), a move the Sabres have actively opposed. Terry Pegula named former Pittsburgh Penguins executive [Ted Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Black) to be team president.[^cite_note-159] Pegula was introduced as the Sabres' owner in a public ceremony at [HSBC Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KeyBank_Center) on February 23, accompanied by what would be the final appearance of all three members of The French Connection before Rick Martin's death three weeks later.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] Around the 2010–11 trade deadline, the team attempted to trade Craig Rivet, but was unsuccessful. After initially clearing waivers, Rivet entered re-entry waivers and was claimed by the Columbus Blue Jackets.[^cite_note-162] Late on February 27, the team acquired [Brad Boyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Boyes) from the [St. Louis Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Blues) in exchange for a second-round draft pick.[^cite_note-163] This was the Sabres' sole trade of the deadline. After Pegula's official takeover of the team, the Sabres finished the regular season 16–4–4, never losing two consecutive games in that span, and landed the seventh seed in the Eastern Conference.[^cite_note-164] Pegula's approach was credited by players, fans and the public with bringing new energy to the team, sparking a run to the playoffs that seemed improbable only months earlier.[^cite_note-165] On April 8, the Sabres clinched a playoff berth for the second consecutive season, defeating the Philadelphia Flyers 4–3 in overtime.[^cite_note-166] The Sabres clinched the seventh seed and faced Philadelphia in the first round. The Sabres had a three games to two lead but lost the series in seven games.[^cite_note-167]

#### Playoff drought

The Sabres began the [2011–12 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_NHL_season) as part of the [2011 NHL Premiere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_NHL_Premiere) series for the first time, playing games in Finland and Germany. The team was particularly well-received during a game against [Adler Mannheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adler_Mannheim) in [Mannheim](99627.md), the hometown of Sabres forward [Jochen Hecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jochen_Hecht).[^cite_note-168] Prior to the first game, Lindy Ruff named Jason Pominville the Sabres' 13th full-time captain in team history.[^cite_note-169] The Sabres began the season relatively strong but collapsed after a Boston Bruins game in which Bruins forward [Milan Lucic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Lucic) hit and injured goaltender Ryan Miller;[^cite_note-170] the subsequent months saw the Sabres collapse to last place in the Eastern Conference. Despite a two-month rally that began in February along with the emergence of rookie forward [Marcus Foligno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Foligno),[^cite_note-171] the Sabres lost the last two games of the regular season and fell three points short of a playoff spot.[^cite_note-172]



[Jason Pominville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Pominville) was named the 13th Sabres team captain before the start of the [2011–12 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_NHL_season).

The [2012–13 NHL lockout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_NHL_lockout) eliminated the first part of the [2012–13 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_NHL_season), which ultimately began with a scheduled 48 games.[^cite_note-173] After a 6–10–1 start to the season, Lindy Ruff's contract was terminated by general manager Darcy Regier on February 20, 2013, ending 16 seasons as head coach. Ruff was replaced by [Ron Rolston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Rolston) first on an interim basis, then full-time after the season ended.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175] In the days leading up to the trade deadline, the Sabres were active in trades. On March 15, the Sabres' first trade sent [T. J. Brennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._J._Brennan) to the [Florida Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Panthers) in exchange for a fifth-round pick (originally owned by the [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings)) in the [2013 draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_NHL_entry_draft).[^cite_note-176] On March 30, the Sabres traded [Jordan Leopold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Leopold) to the St. Louis Blues in exchange for a second-round pick and a conditional fifth-round pick in the 2013 draft.[^cite_note-177] On April 1, the Sabres traded [Robyn Regehr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robyn_Regehr) to the Los Angeles Kings in exchange for two-second round draft choices (one in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_NHL_entry_draft) and the other in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_NHL_entry_draft)).[^cite_note-178] The final trade came on the day of the trade deadline, April 3, where the Sabres sent Jason Pominville to the [Minnesota Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Wild) for [Johan Larsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Larsson_\(ice_hockey,_born_1992\)) and [Matt Hackett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Hackett).[^cite_note-179] It was later revealed that draft picks were also involved in the deal: the Wild would receive a fourth-round pick in the 2014 draft, and the Sabres would receive a first-round pick in the 2013 draft and a second-round pick in the 2014 draft.[^cite_note-180]

The [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_NHL_season), on November 13, 2013, the team dismissed general manager Darcy Regier and head coach Ron Rolston.[^cite_note-RolstonFired-181] Former Sabres head coach Ted Nolan was named interim head coach for the remainder of the season. He later signed a three-year contract extension. [Pat LaFontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_LaFontaine) was named president of hockey operations.[^cite_note-RolstonFired-181] On January 9, 2014, [Tim Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Murray_\(ice_hockey_executive\)), the assistant general manager of the [Ottawa Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottawa_Senators), was named general manager.[^cite_note-182] On February 28, 2014, Murray made his first major trade, sending star goaltender Ryan Miller and captain [Steve Ott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Ott) to the St. Louis Blues in exchange for goaltender [Jaroslav Halak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaroslav_Hal%C3%A1k), forwards [Chris Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Stewart_\(ice_hockey,_born_1987\)) and [William Carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Carrier_\(ice_hockey\)) and two draft picks.[^cite_note-183] After just over three months as president of hockey operations, Pat LaFontaine resigned from the Sabres to return to his previous position with the NHL on March 1, 2014.[^cite_note-184] Among highlights in the otherwise bad 2013–14 season included the "butt goal" in which a severely short-staffed Sabres won their December 23 contest against the Phoenix Coyotes when Coyotes goaltender [Mike Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Smith_\(ice_hockey,_born_1982\)) backed into his [own goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_goal) with the puck lodged in his pants,[^cite_note-185] and the lone NHL appearance of former [Lancaster High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster_High_School_\(New_York\)) goaltender [Ryan Vinz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Vinz), who was working as a videographer in the Sabres organization, to suit up as a backup goaltender in the wake of the Ryan Miller trade.[^cite_note-186] The Sabres finished the 2013–14 season last in the NHL and again missed the playoffs.[^cite_note-187]



The Sabres selected [Jack Eichel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Eichel) with the second overall pick in the [2015 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_NHL_entry_draft).

Despite winning two more games than the previous season, the [2014–15 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_NHL_season) was much like the previous one, with the team sitting near the bottom of the standings the entire season, and finishing last in the NHL.[^cite_note-188] On March 26, 2015, during a 4–3 overtime loss to the Arizona Coyotes, spectators at the game, ostensibly fans of the Sabres, cheered after a game-winning goal by Coyotes center [Sam Gagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Gagner).[^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190] The Sabres clinched last place with a loss to the Columbus Blue Jackets on April 10 and got the second pick in the draft which they used to select [Jack Eichel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Eichel).[^cite_note-191] Murray fired Nolan at the end of the season, citing a lack of chemistry between them.[^cite_note-192] On May 28, 2015, [Dan Bylsma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Bylsma) was hired as the 17th head coach in franchise history.[^cite_note-193]

The hiring of Bylsma, the drafting of Eichel and 2014 second overall pick [Sam Reinhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Reinhart), the acquisition of star centerman [Ryan O'Reilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_O'Reilly) in the offseason,[^cite_note-194] and the rising performance of youngsters [Zemgus Girgensons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zemgus_Girgensons), [Jake McCabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_McCabe) and [Rasmus Ristolainen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasmus_Ristolainen) resulted in an improved season in [2015–16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_NHL_season). Even though the Sabres again missed the playoffs for the fifth consecutive season, the team managed to finish just under .500 in points percentage with a 27-point improvement over the previous season.[^cite_note-195]

In mid-2016, the team announced that its television broadcasts would be spun off to their own [regional sports network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_sports_network), [MSG Western New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSG_Western_New_York). The new network continues to operate under the MSG banner but under [Pegula Sports and Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegula_Sports_and_Entertainment) control and features additional programs centered around the Sabres and the Buffalo Bills, which the Pegulas purchased separately in 2014.[^cite_note-196] The team failed to make significant progress, and in fact slightly regressed, in 2016–17, missing the playoffs for the sixth consecutive season, leading to the firings of both head coach Dan Bylsma and general manager Tim Murray on April 20, 2017.[^cite_note-197]



[Phil Housley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Housley) during the [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_NHL_season). Housley was named the Sabres' head coach the preceding offseason. He would be fired following the [2018–19 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_NHL_season).

During the 2017 offseason, the Sabres hired two of their former players as head coach and general manager: [Jason Botterill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Botterill) as general manager and [Phil Housley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Housley) as head coach.[^cite_note-198][^cite_note-199] Among the more notable roster changes for this season was the return of former scoring leader Jason Pominville to the team in a trade that brought him and defenseman [Marco Scandella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Scandella) to Buffalo in exchange for sending forwards [Tyler Ennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Ennis_\(ice_hockey\)) and Marcus Foligno to the Minnesota Wild.[^cite_note-200]

In the [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_NHL_season), the Sabres participated in the [2018 Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NHL_Winter_Classic), losing 3–2 in overtime to the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers).[^cite_note-201] Buffalo finished in last place in the NHL for the third time in five seasons and won the draft lottery for the [2018 NHL entry draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NHL_entry_draft) for the first time since 1987, using the pick to select [Rasmus Dahlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasmus_Dahlin) from [Frölunda HC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%B6lunda_HC) of the [Swedish Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Hockey_League).[^cite_note-202]

On November 27, 2018, the Sabres became the first team in NHL history to lead the league in points after the first 25 games of the [season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_NHL_season) after finishing last in the league the previous season.[^cite_note-203] The team won 10 games in a row for the first time since the 2006–07 season and tied the franchise record.[^cite_note-204] [Jeff Skinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Skinner) became the seventh player in franchise history to score 20 or more goals in less than 27 games, and only the second player to score 20 goals before December.[^cite_note-205] The team then collapsed and missed the playoffs, leading to Housley's firing at the end of the season.[^cite_note-206]

On March 21, 2019, it was announced that the Sabres would play the [Tampa Bay Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Lightning) in the 2019 NHL Global Series at [Ericsson Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ericsson_Globe) in [Stockholm](26741.md), Sweden.[^cite_note-207] [Ralph Krueger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Krueger) was named the Sabres head coach in May 2019.[^cite_note-208]

In the [2019–20 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_NHL_season), the Lightning defeated the Sabres 5–3 in the Global Series on November 9, 2019.[^cite_note-209] The team traded a draft pick for [Jimmy Vesey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Vesey) and during the season traded Scandella for a draft pick.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211] Even with 24 teams making the expanded playoffs due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the Sabres finished with a 30–31–8 record and missed the postseason for the ninth consecutive season.[^cite_note-212]

On June 16, 2020, despite [Kim Pegula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Pegula)'s vote of confidence three weeks prior, Botterill was fired and replaced by [Kevyn Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevyn_Adams).[^cite_note-213]

On March 17, 2021, in the midst on a twelve-game losing streak, head coach Ralph Krueger was fired.[^cite_note-214] [Don Granato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Granato) was named interim head coach. After continuing to lose games, on March 29, 2021, the Sabres tied the [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins)' all-time NHL losing streak of 18 games from the 2003–04 season after blowing a 3–0 lead in the third period against the [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers) and subsequently losing in overtime; it is the longest losing streak since the shootout was introduced.[^cite_note-215] Granato was named the full-time head coach at the end of the season.[^cite_note-216]

With their last-place finish in the [2020–21 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_NHL_season), they won the draft lottery and selected [Owen Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Power) with the first overall pick in the [2021 draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NHL_entry_draft).[^cite_note-217] Longtime play-by-play announcer Rick Jeanneret also announced the upcoming season would be his final one.[^cite_note-218] Prior to the [2021–22 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_NHL_season), the Sabres traded away [Sam Reinhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Reinhart) and [Rasmus Ristolainen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasmus_Ristolainen).[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220] During the season, the team traded away [Jack Eichel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Eichel) for [Peyton Krebs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton_Krebs), [Alex Tuch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Tuch) and two draft picks.[^cite_note-221] Leading to the trade, Eichel had suffered a herniated disc and sought an independent specialist who recommended an artificial total disc. Eichel's lawyers also told the Sabres organization that the surgery would be required or he wouldn't be able to play the following season. However, the Sabres organization placed him on injured reserve and stripped him of the captaincy prior to the season. The [Vegas Golden Knights](47712000.md) funded his surgery following the trade, winning the Stanley Cup with Eichel in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Stanley_Cup_Final).[^cite_note-222] The team ended up missing for the 11th consecutive season, setting the record for consecutive missed playoff seasons.[^cite_note-223]

The Sabres improved in the [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_NHL_season), going 42–33–7 and finishing above .500 for the first time since 2011 and only finishing one point below the playoff line.[^cite_note-224] With the [Sacramento Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_Kings) clinching a spot in the [2023 NBA playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NBA_playoffs), it left both the [New York Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Jets) and Sabres as the teams among the [major professional leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada#%22Big_Four%22_leagues) with the longest active postseason drought with twelve seasons.[^cite_note-225]

On August 17, 2023, Rick Jeanneret died.[^cite_note-Rick-226] The Sabres honored his legacy by renaming Perry Street, the street adjacent to KeyBank Center, to "RJ Way" on October 13.[^cite_note-227] The team regressed slightly in the [2023–24 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_NHL_season). They ended up trading captain [Kyle Okposo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Okposo) to the [Florida Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Panthers)—he would end up winning the Stanley Cup with Florida.[^cite_note-228][^cite_note-229] On April 9, they were eliminated from postseason potential with a loss to the Dallas Stars.[^cite_note-230] On April 16, 2024, one day after playing the final game of the season, head coach Granato was fired, marking his ousting as the seventh coach the Sabres have employed since the playoff drought began.[^cite_note-231] One week later, on April 22, former coach [Lindy Ruff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindy_Ruff) was named as the new head coach, returning for a second tenure.[^cite_note-RuffReturn-232]

In the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_NHL_season), after beginning the season 11–9–1, the Sabres went on a 13-game losing streak.[^cite_note-233] During the streak, owner Terry Pegula visited the team while they were in Montreal to express his support in the team and leadership.[^cite_note-234] On April 8, 2025, the team was eliminated from postseason potential for the 14th time.[^cite_note-14thseason-5] Shortly after the end of that season, the government of [Erie County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_County,_New_York) announced plans to end its 30-year operational lease of KeyBank Center when it expires in September 2026, ostensibly placing the arena into the responsibility of the city of Buffalo, who indicated an inability to pay for the maintenance of said arena.[^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236] In a statement to the press, Sabres chief operating officer Pete Guelli insisted that the team did not intend to relocate.[^cite_note-237] On July 30, the Sabres and [KeyBank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KeyBank) announced a new arena naming rights contract that will keep the team in the arena through the 2035–36 season.[^cite_note-238]

#### Return to the playoffs

General manager Kevyn Adams was fired December 15, 2025, succeeded by [Jarmo Kekäläinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarmo_Kek%C3%A4l%C3%A4inen).[^cite_note-espn_fire-239] This coincided with a sudden turnaround in the team's fortunes, as the team won 20 out of 24 games from December 2025 through the end of January 2026.[^cite_note-240] On April 4, 2026, the Sabres clinched a playoffs spot, ending their 14-year drought.[^cite_note-241] They clinched the Atlantic Division on April 13, winning their first division title since 2010.[^cite_note-242] The season ended with a game seven overtime loss to the Montreal Canadiens in the second round of the playoffs.[^cite_note-243]

## Team information

### Logo and uniforms

The Sabres have had, for the most part, used a primary logo featuring a [bison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bison) atop two crossed [sabers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabre) in a blue circle with gold trim. This logo was first used from 1970 to 1996 and was restored in 2020 after the Sabres 50th anniversary season was complete.[^cite_note-244]

Throughout their 26-year tenure at the Buffalo Auditorium, the Sabres have worn white uniforms with a blue and gold shoulder yoke and alternating gold, white and blue stripes. On the road they wore blue uniforms with gold stripes. In 1978 the primary logo was added on the shoulders.[^cite_note-bufjersey-245]

Upon moving to what is now KeyBank Center (formerly Marine Midland and HSBC Arena, later First Niagara Center) in 1996, the Sabres changed their logo and colors. Red, black and silver replaced blue and gold while the primary "bison head" logo was unveiled as the primary logo. Black road (later home) and white home (later road) uniforms were also released featuring a bull's head silhouette design in front and the "Sabre B" alternate logo on the shoulders.[^cite_note-bufjersey-245]

The first third jersey of the Buffalo Sabres was created in 2000. The primary color was Sabre red, with black and silver stripes on the sleeves. It also featured the word "Buffalo" written on a black stripe outlined by silver near the waist. The logo was a black circle with two sabers crossing each other (a nod to the original logo).[^cite_note-bufjersey-245]

On October 7, 2001, the Sabres wore a modified version of their white jerseys in a road game against the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers). The uniform replaced the "bison head" with the "NEW YORK" diagonal wordmark as a tribute to the state of New York in the wake of the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md).[^cite_note-246]

On September 16, 2006, the Sabres unveiled new home and away jerseys featuring midnight blue, maize (gold), silver and white colors. Front chest numbers were also added. The new logo, a stylized bison, was widely reviled, drawing unfavorable comparisons to a [banana slug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_slug) (hence the nickname "Buffaslug").[^cite_note-247] Despite the criticism, five of the top ten player jerseys sold in the first two months of the 2006–07 season were Sabres "slug" designs.[^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249] Nevertheless, the Sabres brought back the classic blue jerseys as a third jersey, but continued to pair the look with the navy helmet and pants. When the [Reebok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reebok) Edge template was unveiled in 2007, the Sabres kept their "Buffaslug" uniforms, but the following season, they released a new third jersey featuring the classic look depicted in the navy, gold, silver and white colors.[^cite_note-250] The Sabres also wore the original white uniforms during the [2008 Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_NHL_Winter_Classic).[^cite_note-251]

The third jersey eventually became the primary home jersey on September 18, 2010, when the Sabres released a modern version of the classic 1970–1996 logo. A corresponding road white jersey was also released, along with a third jersey featuring an alternate throwback arrangement that pays homage to the AHL's [Buffalo Bisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bisons_\(AHL\)), complete with the team's 40th Anniversary insignia (essentially the original royal blue version of the current logo with the year "1970" inside).[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253]

In 2013, the Sabres released a new third jersey, featuring a gold front and navy back design. The uniform only lasted two seasons, after which it was retired.[^cite_note-254]

The Sabres kept their uniforms largely intact when [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) took over as its supplier, save for the removal of silver sections in the armpits. They were also the only remaining NHL team to sport uniform numbers in front; teams such as the [Dallas Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Stars) and [San Jose Sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Sharks) briefly added numbers in front of their uniforms before removing them altogether.[^cite_note-255]

During the [2018 Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NHL_Winter_Classic), the Sabres broke out white uniforms with the classic blue and gold shade, albeit with a different striping scheme from the original uniforms.[^cite_note-256]

The 2019–20 season marked the final season of the navy and gold look, as the Sabres announced the return to royal blue uniforms for the following season. Also, a 50th-anniversary white third jersey was used, featuring metallic gold elements on the logo and stripes.[^cite_note-257]

On August 11, 2020, the Sabres unveiled the uniforms for the upcoming season. The style is similar to the ones worn in the early days of the franchise.[^cite_note-ReturnToRoyal-1] Jersey numbers are no longer displayed on the front. The Sabres also released a "Reverse Retro" alternate uniform, bringing back the "crossed swords" alternate from 2000 to 2006 but recolored to the current royal, gold and white scheme.[^cite_note-258]

For the [2022 Heritage Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Heritage_Classic), the Sabres again wore a variation of their classic uniforms, but without the blue shoulder yoke and with a cream base.[^cite_note-259]

On August 31, 2022, the Sabres announced that their black uniform used from 1995 to 2006 would become their new third jersey.[^cite_note-260] This same uniform also became the basis of their second "Reverse Retro" uniform, but recolored to the white, blue and gold scheme and featured white pants.[^cite_note-261]

### National anthems

The Canadian and U.S. national anthems are sung before every Sabres home game, regardless if the visiting team is Canadian or American, because Buffalo is adjacent to the Canadian border and many spectators come from Canada.[^cite_note-272] [Saint Catharines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Catharines), in particular, is noted for its residents' Sabres fandom,[^cite_note-273] and the city is included in the team's regional territory.[^cite_note-274] Doug Allen sang the [Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Canada) and [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Star-Spangled_Banner) national anthems at most home games (except in cases where there is a conflict with his charitable work for the [Wesleyan Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_Church))[^cite_note-275] until resigning in 2021 because of his refusal to take a [COVID-19 vaccine](63319438.md).[^cite_note-276] Curtis Cook is the arena's in-game organist.[^cite_note-277] During Tom Golisano's ownership, the team occasionally used the services of singer [Ronan Tynan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronan_Tynan) who sang "[God Bless America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_Bless_America)" while Allen performed the Canadian anthem (in such cases, the U.S. anthem was not performed).[^cite_note-278] As of 2024, the anthems have been performed by Cami Clune, former semi-finalist on *[The Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Voice_\(American_TV_series\)_season_19)*.[^cite_note-279]

### In-game hosting

Rich Gaenzler, morning host at [WGRF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WGRF), took over as in-game host beginning in 2018[^cite_note-280][^cite_note-Buffalo_staff-281] before he was fired in 2021 over an unrelated dispute pertaining to his WGRF show, which was canceled at the same time. [WBFO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBFO) personality Jay Moran is the current public address announcer; he succeeded Milt Ellis in the position.[^cite_note-Buffalo_staff-281]

In November 2021, the Sabres added an official team dog, named Rick, a [Newfoundland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newfoundland_dog) puppy trained as a [service animal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_animal).[^cite_note-282] Rick was graduated to daily service and succeeded by Nikki, a [Golden Retriever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Retriever) puppy.[^cite_note-283]

### Minor league affiliates

The Sabres are presently affiliated with two minor league teams, the [Rochester Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester_Americans) of the [American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League), and the [Jacksonville Icemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville_Icemen) of the [ECHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHL).[^cite_note-AHL-284][^cite_note-ECHL-285] The Americans play at the [Blue Cross Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Cross_Arena) in [Rochester, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochester,_New_York).[^cite_note-286] Founded in 1956, the Americans were previously the Sabres AHL affiliate from the 1979–80 season to the 2007–08 season.[^cite_note-AHL-284] During the original Sabres affiliation, the Americans won three [Calder Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calder_Cup) championships and finished as runners-up another six times. They finished out of the playoffs only five times in 28 years. The Sabres became re-affiliated with the Americans starting with the 2011–12 season when after buying the Sabres, Pegula purchased the Americans from former owner Curt Styres.[^cite_note-287][^cite_note-288]

The Jacksonville Icemen are based in [Jacksonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville), Florida and have been the Sabres ECHL affiliate since the 2023–24 season.[^cite_note-ECHL-285] Unlike the Americans, the Icemen are not owned by Pegula but are instead owned by SZH Hockey LLC.[^cite_note-289]

### Front office

Further information: [List of Buffalo Sabres general managers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Buffalo_Sabres_general_managers)

[Jarmo Kekäläinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarmo_Kek%C3%A4l%C3%A4inen), who previously served as general manager of the [Columbus Blue Jackets](73128.md) from 2013 to 2024, was named general manager on December 15, 2025.[^cite_note-293] He had previously been hired by the team in May 2025 as a senior advisor.[^cite_note-294]

### Head coaches

Further information: [List of Buffalo Sabres head coaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Buffalo_Sabres_head_coaches)

The Sabres named [Lindy Ruff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindy_Ruff) head coach on April 22, 2024. He returned for a second tenure with the team, after previously coaching Buffalo from 1997 to 2013.[^cite_note-RuffReturn-232]

Of the 20 head coaches the Sabres have used in their history, seven of them had previously played for the Sabres during their playing career: [Floyd Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floyd_Smith_\(ice_hockey\)), [Bill Inglis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Inglis_\(ice_hockey\)), [Jim Schoenfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Schoenfeld), [Craig Ramsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Ramsay), [Rick Dudley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Dudley), Ruff, and [Phil Housley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Housley). Two others, [Dan Bylsma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Bylsma) and [Ted Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Nolan), had played in the Sabres' farm system.

### Hockey Hall of Famers

The Buffalo Sabres has an affiliation with a number of inductees to the Hockey Hall of Fame. Sabres inductees include 14 former players and four builders of the sport.[^cite_note-medgu-295][^cite_note-296] The four individuals recognized as builders by the Hall of Fame includes former general managers, head coaches, and owners. In addition to players and builders, three broadcasters for the Buffalo Sabres were also awarded the [Foster Hewitt Memorial Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foster_Hewitt_Memorial_Award) from the Hockey Hall of Fame, [Ted Darling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Darling) in 1994, [Rick Jeanneret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Jeanneret) in 2012, and [Harry Neale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Neale) in 2013.[^cite_note-297][^cite_note-298]

Four [sports writers](558219.md) from publications based in Buffalo, and [St. Catharines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Catharines), Ontario (which is within Buffalo's media territory), were also awarded the [Elmer Ferguson Memorial Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer_Ferguson_Memorial_Award) from the Hockey Hall of Fame. Recipients of the award include Charlie Barton (*[Buffalo Courier-Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Courier-Express)*) in 1985, Dick Johnston (*[Buffalo News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_News)*) in 1986, [Jack Gatecliff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Gatecliff) (*[St. Catharines Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Catharines_Standard)*) in 1995, and [Jim Kelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Kelley) (*Buffalo News*) in 2004.[^cite_note-medgu-295][^cite_note-299]
