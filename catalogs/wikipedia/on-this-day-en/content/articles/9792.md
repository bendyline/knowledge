# Extravehicular activity

"Spacewalk" redirects here. For other uses, see [Spacewalk (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacewalk_\(disambiguation\)).



Cosmonaut [Sergey Volkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Volkov_\(cosmonaut\)) works outside the [International Space Station](15043.md) on August 3, 2011.



[Stephen Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Robinson) riding the [Canadarm2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadarm2) while conducting the first in-flight repair of the Space Shuttle during [STS-114](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-114) on August 3, 2005. The landmass in the backdrop is the [Bari region](185131.md) of [Somalia](27358.md).

**Extravehicular activity** (**EVA**) is any activity performed by an [astronaut](664.md) in [outer space](177602.md) outside of a [spacecraft](37910.md). This includes **spacewalks**, [lunar](19331.md) or [planetary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet) surface exploration (commonly known as **moonwalks**), and stand-up EVAs (SEVAs) where astronauts stand through an open hatch without fully leaving the spacecraft.[^cite_note-standup-1] EVAs have been conducted by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)/Russia, the United States, and China, with astronauts from Canada, Japan, the United Arab Emirates, and the [European Space Agency](10363.md) also participating in EVAs conducted by those nations. Since there is no [atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere_of_Earth) in space, the astronaut is completely dependent on the [space suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_suit) for environmental support.&#x20;

On March 18, 1965, [Alexei Leonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Leonov) conducted the first spacewalk, lasting 12 minutes and 9 seconds during the [Voskhod 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voskhod_2) mission. [Neil Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Armstrong) performed the first moonwalk on July 20, 1969, during the [Apollo 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11) mission, lasting 2 hours and 31 minutes. [Svetlana Savitskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svetlana_Savitskaya) became the first woman to conduct a spacewalk in 1984, spending 3 hours and 35 minutes outside the [Salyut 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salyut_7) space station. Astronauts on the last three crewed lunar landing missions, [Apollo 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_15), [16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_16), and [17](1971.md), also performed deep space EVAs to retrieve film canisters. In 1973, American astronauts [Pete Conrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Conrad), [Joseph Kerwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_P._Kerwin), and [Paul Weitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_J._Weitz) conducted an EVA to repair launch damage to [Skylab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skylab), the United States' first space station.

EVAs can be tethered, where the [astronaut](664.md) is connected to the spacecraft with an [umbilical cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbilical_cable) for oxygen and power, or untethered. Untethered spacewalks were conducted on three missions in 1984 using the [Manned Maneuvering Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manned_Maneuvering_Unit) (MMU) and in a 1994 flight test of the [Simplified Aid For EVA Rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Aid_For_EVA_Rescue) (SAFER) safety device worn on tethered U.S. EVAs.

## Development history

[NASA](18426568.md) planners invented the term *extravehicular activity* (abbreviated with the acronym EVA) in the early 1960s for the [Apollo program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program) to land humans on the Moon, because the astronauts would leave the spacecraft to collect [lunar material samples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_rock) and deploy scientific experiments. To support this, and other Apollo objectives, the [Gemini program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Gemini) was spun off to develop the capability for astronauts to work outside a two-person Earth orbiting spacecraft. However, the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) was fiercely competitive in holding the early lead it had gained in crewed spaceflight, so the [Soviet Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union), led by [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev), ordered the conversion of its single-pilot [Vostok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vostok_\(spacecraft\)) capsule into a two- or three-person craft named [Voskhod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voskhod_spacecraft), in order to compete with [Gemini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_program) and [Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_\(spacecraft\)).[^cite_note-Siddiqi_384-6-2] The Soviets were able to launch two Voskhod capsules before U.S. was able to launch its first crewed Gemini.

The Voskhod's [avionics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avionics) required cooling by cabin air to prevent any kind of overheating, therefore an [airlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airlock) was required for the spacewalking [cosmonaut](664.md) to exit and re-enter the cabin while it remained pressurized. Unusually, and by contrast, the Gemini avionics did not require air cooling, allowing the spacewalking astronaut to exit and re-enter the depressurized cabin through an open hatch. Because of this, the [American](3434750.md) and [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) space programs developed different definitions for the duration of an EVA. The Soviet (now [Russian](25391.md)) definition begins when the outer airlock hatch is open and the cosmonaut is in [vacuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum). An American EVA began when the astronaut had at least their head outside the spacecraft.[^cite_note-3] The U.S. has changed its EVA definition since.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

### First instance

The first EVA was performed on March 18, 1965, by Soviet cosmonaut [Alexei Leonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Leonov), who spent 12 minutes and 9 seconds outside the [Voskhod 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voskhod_2) spacecraft. Carrying a white metal backpack containing 45 minutes' worth of breathing and pressurization oxygen, Leonov had no means to control his motion other than pulling on his 15.35 m (50.4 ft) tether. After the flight, he claimed this was easy, but his [space suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_suit) ballooned from its internal pressure against the vacuum of space, stiffening so much that he could not activate the shutter on his chest-mounted camera.[^cite_note-portree-6]

At the end of his space walk, the suit stiffening caused a more serious problem: Leonov had to re-enter the capsule through the inflatable cloth airlock, 1.2 m (3 ft 11 in) in diameter and 2.5 m (8 ft 2 in) long. He improperly entered the airlock head-first and got stuck sideways. He could not get back in without reducing the pressure in his suit, risking "[the bends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decompression_sickness)". This added another 12 minutes to his time in vacuum, and he was overheated by 1.8 °C (3.2 °F) from the exertion. It would be almost four years before the Soviets tried another EVA. They misrepresented to the press how difficult Leonov found it to work in [weightlessness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weightlessness) and concealed the problems encountered until after the end of the [Cold War](325329.md).[^cite_note-portree-6][^cite_note-First-Spacewalk-7]

### Project Gemini



[Ed White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_White_\(astronaut\)) performs the first American spacewalk during [Gemini IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_IV).

The first American spacewalk was performed on June 3, 1965, by [Ed White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_White_\(astronaut\)) from the second crewed [Gemini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Gemini) flight, [Gemini IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_IV), for 21 minutes. White was tethered to the spacecraft, and his oxygen was supplied through a 25-foot (7.6 m) [umbilical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbilical_cable), which also carried communications and biomedical instrumentation. He was the first to control his motion in space with a [Hand-Held Maneuvering Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand-Held_Maneuvering_Unit), which worked well but only carried enough propellant for 20 seconds. White found his tether useful for limiting his distance from the spacecraft but difficult to use for moving around, contrary to Leonov's claim.[^cite_note-portree-6] However, a defect in the capsule's hatch latching mechanism caused difficulties opening and closing the hatch, which delayed the start of the EVA and put White and his crewmate at risk of not getting back to Earth alive.[^cite_note-mcdivittoh-8]

No EVAs were planned on the next three Gemini flights. The next EVA was planned to be made by [David Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Scott) on [Gemini VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_VIII), but that mission had to be aborted due to a critical spacecraft malfunction before the EVA could be conducted. Astronauts on the next three Gemini flights ([Eugene Cernan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Cernan), [Michael Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Collins_\(astronaut\)), and [Richard Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_F._Gordon_Jr.)), performed several EVAs, but none was able to successfully work for long periods outside the spacecraft without tiring and overheating. Cernan attempted but failed to test an Air Force [Astronaut Maneuvering Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut_Maneuvering_Unit) which included a self-contained oxygen system.

On November 13, 1966, [Edwin "Buzz" Aldrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_%22Buzz%22_Aldrin) became the first to successfully work in space without tiring during [Gemini XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_XII), the last Gemini mission. Aldrin worked outside the spacecraft for 2 hours and 6 minutes, in addition to two stand-up EVAs in the spacecraft hatch for an additional 3 hours and 24 minutes. Aldrin's interest in [scuba diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuba_diving) inspired the use of [underwater EVA training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_buoyancy_simulation_as_a_training_aid) to simulate weightlessness, which has been used ever since to allow astronauts to practice techniques of avoiding wasted muscle energy.

### First crew transfer

On January 16, 1969, Soviet cosmonauts [Aleksei Yeliseyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksei_Yeliseyev) and [Yevgeny Khrunov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevgeny_Khrunov) transferred from [Soyuz 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_5) to [Soyuz 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_4), which were docked together. This was the second Soviet EVA, and it would be almost another nine years before the Soviets performed their third.[^cite_note-portree-6]

### Apollo missions



[Buzz Aldrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzz_Aldrin) walks on the Moon during the pioneering [Apollo 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11) mission in 1969.

American astronauts [Neil Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Armstrong) and [Buzz Aldrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzz_Aldrin) performed the first EVA on the lunar surface on July 21, 1969 ([UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC)), after landing their [Apollo 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11) [Lunar Module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Lunar_Module) spacecraft. This first Moon walk, using self-contained [portable life support systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portable_life_support_system), lasted 2 hours and 31 minutes. A total of fifteen Moon walks were performed among six Apollo crews, including [Charles "Pete" Conrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Conrad), [Alan Bean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Bean), [Alan Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Shepard), [Edgar Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Mitchell), [David Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Scott), [James Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Irwin), [John Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Young_\(astronaut\)), [Charles Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Duke), [Eugene Cernan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Cernan), and [Harrison "Jack" Schmitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Schmitt). Cernan was the last Apollo astronaut to step off the surface of the Moon.[^cite_note-portree-6]

[Charles Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Duke) with a hammer on the lunar surface

[Apollo 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_15) [command module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_command_and_service_module) pilot [Al Worden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Worden) made an EVA on August 5, 1971, on the return trip from the Moon, to retrieve a film and data recording canister from the service module. He was assisted by Lunar Module Pilot James Irwin standing up in the Command Module hatch. This procedure was repeated by [Ken Mattingly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Mattingly) and Charles Duke on [Apollo 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_16), and by [Ronald Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Evans_\(astronaut\)) and Harrison Schmitt on [Apollo 17](1971.md).[^cite_note-portree-6]

### Post-Apollo

The first EVA repairs of a spacecraft were made by [Charles "Pete" Conrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Conrad), [Joseph Kerwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_P._Kerwin), and [Paul J. Weitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_J._Weitz) on May 26, June 7, and June 19, 1973, on the [Skylab 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skylab_2) mission. They rescued the functionality of the launch-damaged [Skylab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skylab) [space station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_station) by freeing a stuck [solar panel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panels_on_spacecraft), deploying a solar heating shield, and freeing a stuck circuit breaker relay. The Skylab 2 crew made three EVAs, and a total of ten EVAs were made by the three Skylab crews.[^cite_note-portree-6] They found that activities in weightlessness required about 21⁄2 times longer than on Earth because many astronauts suffered [spacesickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spacesickness) early in their flights.[^cite_note-reusestudy-9]

After Skylab, no more EVAs were made by the United States until the advent of the [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle) program in the early 1980s. In this period, the Soviets resumed EVAs, making four from the [Salyut 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salyut_6) and [Salyut 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salyut_7) space stations between December 20, 1977, and July 30, 1982.[^cite_note-portree-6]

When the United States resumed EVAs on April 7, 1983, astronauts started using an [Extravehicular Mobility Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extravehicular_Mobility_Unit) (EMU) for self-contained life support independent of the spacecraft. STS-6 was the first Space Shuttle mission during which a spacewalk was conducted. Also, for the first time, American astronauts used an airlock to enter and exit the spacecraft like the Soviets. Accordingly, the American definition of EVA start time was redefined to when the astronaut switches the EMU to battery power.[^cite_note-10]

Numerous EVAs were conducted during the assembly of the [ISS](15043.md), often using the [Quest Joint Airlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quest_Joint_Airlock), designed to support both U.S. EMUs, and Russian Orlan space suits.

### By China



Astronaut [Fei Junlong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fei_Junlong) performing a spacewalk on the [Tiangong Space Station](2481401.md)

[China](5405.md) became the third [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country) to independently carry out an EVA on September 27, 2008, during the [Shenzhou 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_7) mission. Chinese taikonaut [Zhai Zhigang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhai_Zhigang) completed a 22-minute spacewalk wearing the Chinese-developed [Feitian space suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feitian_space_suit), with taikonaut [Liu Boming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Boming_\(astronaut\)) wearing the Russian-derived [Orlan space suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlan_space_suit) assisting him in the process. Zhai completely exited the craft, while Liu stood by at the airlock, straddling the portal.

Since 2021, China has carried out several more EVAs lasting several hours for the construction of the [Tiangong space station](2481401.md). In December 2024 China's [Cai Xuzhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cai_Xuzhe) and [Song Lingdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_Lingdong) set the current record for the longest EVA at 9 hours and six minutes.[^cite_note-\:0-11][^cite_note-\:1-12]

### By SpaceX

American company [SpaceX](832774.md) conducted the first private sector-financed EVA on September 12, 2024. Entrepreneur [Jared Isaacman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Isaacman) and SpaceX engineer [Sarah Gillis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Gillis) briefly ventured outside a [Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon) capsule, for a stand-up EVA (SEVA) during the [Polaris Dawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polaris_Dawn) mission to conduct [spacesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_suit) mobility testing.[^cite_note-13] The other two crew members were exposed to the vacuum of space in the capsule, but did not leave it.[^cite_note-14] SpaceX plans to launch at least two more missions involving an EVA,[^cite_note-15] including one that involves SpaceX's still-in-development [Starship](59118602.md) launch vehicle.[^cite_note-16]

## Milestones

### Capability milestones

- The first untethered spacewalk was made by American [Bruce McCandless II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_McCandless_II) on February 7, 1984, during the [Space Shuttle *Challenger*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_Challenger) mission [STS-41-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STS-41-B), using the [Manned Maneuvering Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manned_Maneuvering_Unit). He was subsequently joined by [Robert L. Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_L._Stewart) during the 5-hour, 55-minute spacewalk. A self-contained spacewalk was first attempted by [Eugene Cernan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Cernan) in 1966 on [Gemini 9A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_9A), but Cernan could not reach the maneuvering unit without tiring.

- The first metalwork in open space, consisting of welding, brazing and metal spraying, was conducted by Soviet cosmonauts [Svetlana Savitskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svetlana_Savitskaya) and [Vladimir Dzhanibekov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Dzhanibekov) on July 25, 1984. A specially designed multipurpose tool was used to perform these activities during a 3-hour, 30-minute EVA outside the Salyut 7 space station.

### Personal cumulative duration records

- Russian [Anatoly Solovyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Solovyev) holds both the [record for most EVAs and for the greatest cumulative duration spent in EVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cumulative_spacewalk_records) (16 EVAs; 82 hr and 22 min over 4 Mir missions between July 1990 and January 1998).

- [Michael Lopez-Alegria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Lopez-Alegria) holds the American record (10 EVAs; 67 hr and 40 min over 2 Shuttle and 1 ISS missions between October 2000 and February 2007).

- [Thomas Pesquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Pesquet) holds the European (and non-U.S./Russian) record (6 EVAs; 39 hr and 54 min over 2 ISS missions between January 2017 and August 2021).

  [^cite_note-23]

- [Sunita Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunita_Williams) holds the record for the most cumulative duration spent for a [woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman) (62 hr and 6 min). However, [Peggy Whitson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peggy_Whitson) holds the record for most EVAs by a woman (10 EVAs over 3 ISS missions between August 2002 and May 2017).

## Commemoration

The first spacewalk, made by Soviet cosmonaut Alexei Leonov, was commemorated in 1965 with several Eastern Bloc stamps (see [Alexei Leonov#Stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Leonov#Stamps)). Since the Soviet Union did not publish details of the Voskhod spacecraft at the time, the spaceship depiction in the stamps was purely fictional.

The [U.S. Post Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Post_Office) issued a postage stamp in 1967 commemorating [Ed White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_White_\(astronaut\))'s first American spacewalk. The engraved image has an accurate depiction of the [Gemini IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_IV) spacecraft and White's [space suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_suit).[^cite_note-31]

## Camp-out procedure

For EVAs from the [International Space Station](15043.md), NASA employed a [*camp-out* procedure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quest_Joint_Airlock#Camp-out_procedure) to reduce the risk of decompression sickness.[^cite_note-tanner-34] This was first tested by the [Expedition 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedition_12) crew. During a camp-out, astronauts sleep overnight in the [airlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quest_Joint_Airlock) prior to an EVA, lowering the air pressure to 10.2 psi (70 kPa) from the normal station pressure of 14.7 psi (101 kPa).[^cite_note-tanner-34] Spending a night at the lower air pressure helps flush nitrogen from the body, thereby preventing "[the bends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decompression_sickness)".[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] More recently astronauts have been using the In-Suit Light Exercise protocol rather than camp-out to prevent decompression sickness.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]
