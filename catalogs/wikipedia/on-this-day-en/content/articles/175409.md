# Patriarch

This article is about the title in Christianity. For other uses, see [Patriarch (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_\(disambiguation\)).

























Patriarchs in different [Christian confessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_confessions), including [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy), [Oriental Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy) and [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism) respectively from left to right:\
**Eastern Orthodox**: [Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople) [Bartholomew I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartholomew_I), [Catholicos-Patriarch of All Georgia](435047.md) [Shio III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shio_III), [Patriarch of All Romania](1795665.md) [Daniel I](12545130.md) and [Patriarch of Moscow and all Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Moscow_and_all_Rus') [Kirill I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirill_I)\
**Oriental Orthodox**: [Patriarch of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Armenia) [Garegin II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garegin_II), [Coptic Orthodox Patriarch of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Patriarch_of_Alexandria) [Tawadros II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawadros_II), [Patriarch-Catholicos of All Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch-Catholicos_of_All_Ethiopia) [Mathias I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathias_I) and [Syriac Orthodox Patriarch of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox_Patriarch_of_Antioch_and_All_the_East) [Ignatius Aphrem II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignatius_Aphrem_II)\
**Catholic**: [Syriac Catholic Patriarch of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Catholic_Patriarch_of_Antioch) [Ignatius Joseph III Yonan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignatius_Joseph_III_Yonan), [Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Patriarch_of_Jerusalem) [Pierbattista Pizzaballa](54051059.md), [Coptic Catholic Patriarch of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_Catholic_Patriarch_of_Alexandria) [Ibrahim Isaac Sidrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Isaac_Sidrak) and [Maronite Patriarch of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Maronite_patriarchs_of_Antioch) [Bechara Boutros al-Rahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bechara_Boutros_al-Rahi)

The highest-ranking [bishops](4092.md) in [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy), [Oriental Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy),[^cite_note-\:0-1] the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) (above [major archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_archbishop) and [primate](24672.md)), the [Hussite Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussite_Church), [Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_East) and some [Independent Catholic Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Catholicism) are termed **patriarchs** (and in certain cases also *[popes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_\(word\))* – such as the [pope of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_of_Rome) or [pope of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_of_Alexandria)).

The word is derived from [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) πατριάρχης (*patriarchēs*),[^cite_note-2] meaning "chief or father of a family",[^cite_note-OnEtDi-3] a compound of πατριά (*patria*),[^cite_note-4] meaning "family", and ἄρχειν (*archein*),[^cite_note-5] meaning "to rule".[^cite_note-OnEtDi-3][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Originally, a *patriarch* was a man who exercised authority as a [pater familias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pater_familias) over an [extended family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_family).[^cite_note-9] The system of such rule of families by senior males is termed "[patriarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchy)". Historically, a patriarch has often been the logical choice to act as [ethnarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnarch) of the community identified with his religious confession within a state or empire of a different [creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed) (such as Christians within the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md)). The term developed an ecclesiastical meaning within [Christianity](5211.md). The office and the ecclesiastical circumscription of a Christian patriarch is termed a *[patriarchate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchate)*.

[Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham), [Isaac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac) and [Jacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob) are referred to as the three [patriarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchs_\(Bible\)) of the [people of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelite), and the period during which they lived is termed the [Patriarchal Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchal_Age). The word *patriarch* originally acquired its religious meaning in the [Septuagint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septuagint) version of the [Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible).[^cite_note-10]

## Catholic Church



Catholic Patriarchal (non [cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_\(Catholicism\))) coat of arms

### Patriarchs



Map of Justinian's Pentarchy

In the [Catholic Church](606848.md), the bishop who is head of a particular [autonomous church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_iuris), known in canon law as a church *sui iuris*, is ordinarily a patriarch, though this responsibility can be entrusted to a major archbishop, metropolitan, or other prelate for a number of reasons.[^cite_note-11]

Since the [Council of Nicaea](11118.md), the bishop of Rome has been recognized as first among patriarchs.[^cite_note-12] That council designated three bishops with this 'supra-Metropolitan' title: Rome, Alexandria, and Antioch. In the [Pentarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentarchy) formulated by [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I) (527–565), the emperor assigned to the [bishop of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome) a patriarchate covering the whole of Christianized Europe (including almost all of modern [Greece](12108.md)), except [Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrace), the areas around [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople), and the [Black Sea](3386.md) coast. He also included the western part of [North Africa](21714.md) in this patriarchate. The jurisdictions of the other patriarchates extended over Roman Asia and the rest of Africa. Justinian's system was given formal ecclesiastical recognition by the [Quinisext Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinisext_Council) of 692, which the [see of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/See_of_Rome) has, however, not recognized.

There were at the time bishops of other apostolic sees that operated with patriarchal authority beyond the borders of the Roman Empire, such as the [catholicos of Selucia-Ctesephon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Patriarchs_of_the_Church_of_the_East).

Today, the patriarchal heads of Catholic autonomous churches are:[^cite_note-13]

- The [Patriarch of Rome (Pope)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome), as head of the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church)

  [^cite_note-14]

- The [Coptic Catholic Patriarch of Alexandria (Pope)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_Catholic_Patriarchate_of_Alexandria) and head of the [Coptic Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_Catholic_Church), recognised in 1824

- The [Maronite Catholic Patriarch of Antioch and All the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronite_Catholic_Patriarchate_of_Antioch) and head of the [Maronite Church](66391078.md), recognised in 685

- The [Melkite Greek Catholic Patriarch of Antioch and All the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Catholic_Patriarchate_of_Antioch), of Alexandria and of Jerusalem, head of the [Melkite Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Church); in his case, Antioch is the actual and sole patriarchate, Alexandria and Jerusalem are just titular (once residential) patriarchates vested in his see.

- The [Syriac Catholic Patriarch of Antioch and All the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Catholic_Patriarchs_of_Antioch) and head of the [Syriac Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Catholic_Church)

- The [Chaldean Catholic Patriarch of Baghdad](14716825.md) and head of the [Chaldean Catholic Church](698821.md), recognised in 1553

- The [Armenian Catholic Patriarch of Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Cilicia) and head of the [Armenian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church), recognised in 1742

Four more of the [Eastern Catholic Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_Church) are headed by a prelate known as a "[Major Archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_Archbishop)",[^cite_note-15] a title essentially equivalent to that of Patriarch and originally created by [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) in 1963 for [Josyf Slipyj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josyf_Slipyj).[^cite_note-16]

### Minor Latin patriarchates

Minor patriarchs do not have jurisdiction over other metropolitan bishops. The title is granted purely as an honour for various historical reasons. They take precedence after the heads of autonomous churches in full communion, whether pope, patriarch, or major archbishop.&#x20;

- The [Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Patriarch_of_Jerusalem), established in 1099.

- The [Patriarch of the East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_the_East_Indies), a titular patriarchal see, united to [Goa and Daman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Goa_and_Daman), established in 1886.

- The [Patriarch of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Lisbon), established in 1716.

- The [Patriarch of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Venice), established in 1451.

#### Historical Latin patriarchates

- The [Patriarch of Aquileia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchate_of_Aquileia) – with rival line of succession moved to Grado – dissolved in 1752.

- The [Patriarch of Grado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Grado) – in 1451 merged with the Bishopric of Castello and Venice to form the Metropolitan Archdiocese of Venice (later, a residential Patriarchate itself).

- The [Patriarch of the West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_the_West_Indies) – a titular patriarchal see, vacant since 1963.

- The [Latin Patriarch of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Patriarch_of_Antioch) – title abolished in 1964.

- The titular [Latin Patriarch of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Patriarch_of_Alexandria) – title abolished in 1964.

- The [Latin Patriarch of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Patriarch_of_Constantinople) – title abolished in 1964.

- The [Latin Patriarchate of Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Patriarchate_of_Ethiopia) – from 1555 to 1663, never effective, only held by Iberian [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuits)

#### Patriarch as title *ad personam*

The pope can confer the rank of patriarch without any see, upon an individual archbishop, as happened on 24 February 1676 to [Alessandro Crescenzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Crescenzi_\(cardinal\)), of the [Somascans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somascans), former [Latin Titular Patriarch of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Titular_Patriarch_of_Alexandria) (19 January 1671 – retired 27 May 1675), who nevertheless resigned the title on 9 January 1682.

#### Patriarch of the West

Main article: [Patriarch of the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_the_West)

One of the [pope](23056.md)'s traditional titles in some eras and contexts has been "[Patriarch of the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_the_West)" ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Patriarcha Occidentis*; [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Πατριάρχης τῆς Δύσεως), highlighting the role of the bishop of Rome as the highest authority of the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church).

The title was not included in the 2006 *Annuario Pontificio*. On 22 March 2006, the [Pontifical Council for Promoting Christian Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Council_for_Promoting_Christian_Unity) offered an explanation for the decision to remove the title. It stated that the title "Patriarch of the West" had become "obsolete and practically unusable" when the term *the West* comprises Australia, New Zealand and North America in addition to Western Europe, and that it was "pointless to insist on maintaining it" given that, since the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council), the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church), for which "the West" is an equivalent, has been organized as a number of [episcopal conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_conference) and their international groupings.[^cite_note-17] The title was reintroduced in the 2024 edition of *Annuario Pontificio*. No explanation was provided for its reintroduction.[^cite_note-18]

### Motu proprio *Mutua Concordia*

Under the provision of *Mutua Concordia*, an Apostolic Letter published on August 29, 2026, [Pope Leo XIV](54520372.md) introduced for Eastern Synods the faculty to remove Patriarchs from office with a "secret ballot of at least two-thirds of the members of the Synod entitled to vote". The Pope has the sole power of confirming a Synod's decision.[^cite_note-19]

Latter-Day Saint movement

Main article: [Patriarch (Latter Day Saints)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_\(Latter_Day_Saints\))

In the [Latter-Day Saint movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Day_Saint_movement), a [patriarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_\(Latter_Day_Saints\)) is one who has been ordained to the office of patriarch in the [Melchizedek priesthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melchizedek_priesthood_\(Latter_Day_Saints\)). The term is considered synonymous with the term [evangelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelist_\(Latter_Day_Saints\)), a term favored by the [Community of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_of_Christ). In [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints), one of the patriarch's primary responsibilities is to give [patriarchal blessings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchal_blessing), as Jacob did to his twelve sons according to the Old Testament. Patriarchs are typically assigned in each [stake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stake_\(Mormonism\)) and possess the title for life.

## Manichaeism

The term patriarch has also been used for the leader of the extinct [Manichaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manichaeism) religion, initially based at [Ctesiphon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ctesiphon) (near modern-day [Baghdad](4492.md)) and later at [Samarkand](51491.md).
