**Shinjuku** ([Japanese](15606.md): 新宿区, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *Shinjuku-ku*; IPA: [\[ɕiɲdʑɯkɯ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c7/TomJ-Shinjuku.ogg/TomJ-Shinjuku.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/c/c7/TomJ-Shinjuku.ogg/TomJ-Shinjuku.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), officially called **Shinjuku City**, is one of the 23 [special wards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_wards_of_Tokyo) of [Tokyo](30057.md).

Since the end of [World War II](32927.md), Shinjuku has become a major secondary center of Tokyo (*[fukutoshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metro_Fukutoshin_Line#History)*), rivaling the original city center in [Marunouchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marunouchi). Today, it is a major commercial and administrative center and houses the northern half of [Shinjuku Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinjuku_Station), the busiest railway station in the world, as well as the [Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_Government_Building), the administrative center of the [Tokyo Metropolitan Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_Government).

As of 2025, the ward has an estimated population of 355,981,[^cite_note-4] living in a total area of 18.23 km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-5]

## History



Shinjuku at night



A commercial street in Shinjuku at night.

In 1634, during the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period), as the outer moat of the [Edo Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_Castle) was built, a number of temples and shrines moved to the Yotsuya area on the western edge of Shinjuku. In 1698, Naitō-Shinjuku had developed as a new (shin) station ([shuku or juku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shukuba)) on the [Kōshū Kaidō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dsh%C5%AB_Kaid%C5%8D), one of the major [highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaid%C5%8D) of that era. Naitō was the family name of a *[daimyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimy%C5%8D)* whose mansion stood in the area; his land is now a public park, the Shinjuku Gyoen. In 1898, the Yodobashi Water Purification Plant, the city's first modern water treatment facility, was built in the area that is now between the park and the train station.[^cite_note-6]

In 1920, the town of Naitō-Shinjuku, which comprised large parts of present-day Shinjuku (the neighborhood, not the municipality), parts of [Nishi-Shinjuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishi-Shinjuku) and [Kabukichō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabukich%C5%8D,_Tokyo) were integrated into [Tokyo City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_City). Shinjuku began to develop into its current form after the [Great Kantō Earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_Great_Kant%C5%8D_earthquake) in 1923, since the seismically stable area largely escaped the devastation. Consequently, West Shinjuku is one of the few areas in Tokyo with many [skyscrapers](29485.md).

The [Tokyo air raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Tokyo_in_World_War_II) from May to August 1945 destroyed almost 90% of the buildings in the area in and around Shinjuku Station.[^cite_note-7] The pre-war form of Shinjuku and the rest of Tokyo was retained after the war because the roads and rails, damaged as they were, remained, and these formed the heart of Shinjuku in the post-war construction. Only in Kabuki-cho was a grand reconstruction plan put into action.[^cite_note-8]

The present ward was established on March 15, 1947 with the merger of the former wards of Yotsuya, Ushigome, and Yodobashi. It served as part of the [athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1964_Summer_Olympics) 50 km walk and marathon course during the [1964 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-9] In March 1965, the Yodobashi Water Purification Plant closed and was replaced by skyscrapers in the following years.[^cite_note-10]

### The Sixties in Shinjuku

Shinjuku was the epicenter of new ideas and artistic avant-gardes in Tokyo in the 1960s, much like [Greenwich Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Village) in New York.

#### Cinema

The young directors of the [Japanese New Wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_New_Wave) used Shinjuku as the setting for several of their films,[^cite_note-11] such as [Nagisa Oshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagisa_%C5%8Cshima)'s *[Diary of a Shinjuku Thief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diary_of_a_Shinjuku_Thief)* (1969), [Koji Wakamatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dji_Wakamatsu)'s *Shinjuku Mad* (screenplay by [Masao Adachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masao_Adachi)), and *[Funeral Parade of Roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral_Parade_of_Roses)* by [Toshio Matsumoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshio_Matsumoto).

In 1967, the [Art Theatre Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Theatre_Guild) opened the Scorpio Theatre (Sasori-za) in the basement of its [Art Theatre Shinjuku Bunka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Theatre_Shinjuku_Bunka) cinema. The Scorpio Theatre quickly established itself as an influential underground venue[^cite_note-12] for [angura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angura) theater, music, dance, and film. Its name, proposed by novelist [Yukio Mishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukio_Mishima), was inspired by [Kenneth Anger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Anger)'s film *[Scorpio Rising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorpio_Rising_\(film\))* (1963). It was at the Sasori-za that Mishima held the first screening of his film *[Patriotism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotism_\(1966_film\))* (1966).

#### Theater

In 1967, the Modern Art Theater was opened in Shinjuku. Dubbed the "sensational cave," it offered nude shows, avant-garde and entertainment theater, film screenings, and performances. The theater laboratory "Ceiling Pier", which had [Tadanori Yokoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tadanori_Yokoo) as a founding member, also performed there. After the underground boom, the venue was used for striptease performances.

It was in Shinjuku, in the Hanazono shrine, that the first performances of the "Red Tent" (Jokyo Gekijo), the company founded by [Juro Kara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%ABr%C5%8D_Kara), took place.[^cite_note-13]

[Tatsumi Hijikata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatsumi_Hijikata) and the [buto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butoh) dancers were also closely associated to Shinjuku.

#### Places

Cafe Fugetsudo, founded by actor Goro Yokoyama, was a rallying point for artists, intellectuals, and homosexuals in the 1960s.[^cite_note-14] Actor [Takeshi Kitano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeshi_Kitano), painter [Taro Okamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tar%C5%8D_Okamoto), transvestite actress [Akihiro Miwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akihiro_Miwa), poet [Gozo Yoshimasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C5%8Dz%C5%8D_Yoshimasu), and filmmaker [Shuji Terayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%ABji_Terayama) frequented it.[^cite_note-15]

In the [Golden Gai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinjuku_Golden_Gai), a small block located east of [Kabukicho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabukich%C5%8D), many bars have been haunts of artists and protesters.

#### Folk Guerilla Concerts

In 1969, anti-war group [Beheiren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beheiren) organized folk concerts in Shinjuku station. A batch of Japanese anti-Vietnam War activists gathered and were termed "[folk guerrillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_guerrilla_concerts)".[^cite_note-16] In July, a fight with riot police led to the dispersal of the concerts and the arrests of musicians.

#### Demonstrations

On October 21, 1969, an International Anti-War Day demonstration was [violently suppressed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10.21_International_Anti-War_Day_Struggle),[^cite_note-17] so much so that the daily newspaper *[Asahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Asahi_Shimbun)* ran the following day's headline: "Guerrilla Warfare in the Heart of Shinjuku."

In 1991, the Tokyo Metropolitan Government moved from the [Marunouchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marunouchi) district of [Chiyoda](47836792.md) to the current building in Shinjuku (the [Tokyo International Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_International_Forum) stands at the former site vacated by the government).

## Geography



Shinjuku office buildings

Most of Shinjuku is occupied by the Yodobashi Plateau, the most elevated portion of which extends through most of the Shinjuku Station area. The Kanda River runs through the Ochiai and Totsuka areas near sea level, but the Toshima Plateau also builds elevation in the northern extremities of Totsuka and Ochiai. The highest point in Shinjuku is Hakone-san in Toyama Park, 44.6 m above sea level.[^cite_note-18]

Shinjuku is surrounded by [Chiyoda](47836792.md) to the east; [Bunkyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunkyo,_Tokyo) and [Toshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toshima,_Tokyo_\(ward\)) to the north; [Nakano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakano,_Tokyo) to the west, and [Shibuya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibuya,_Tokyo) and [Minato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minato,_Tokyo) to the south.[^cite_note-19]



[Shinjuku Gyo-en National Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinjuku_Gyo-en) and [NTT DoCoMo Yoyogi Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NTT_Docomo_Yoyogi_Building)

The current city of Shinjuku grew out of several separate towns and villages, which have retained some distinctions despite growing together as part of the Tokyo metropolis.

- East Shinjuku : the area east of [Shinjuku Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinjuku_Station) between [Higashi-Shinjuku Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higashi-Shinjuku_Station) and [Shinjuku-sanchome Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinjuku-sanchome_Station), historically known as Naito-Shinjuku, houses the city hall and the flagship [Isetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isetan) department store, as well as several famous districts :&#x20;

  - [Kabukichō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabukich%C5%8D,_Tokyo): the red-light district, renowned for its variety of bars, restaurants, and [sex-related establishments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_trade) near [Seibu-Shinjuku Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seibu-Shinjuku_Station).

  - [Golden Gai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Gai): an area of tiny shanty-style bars and clubs, where musicians, artists, journalists, actors and directors used to gather.

  - [Shinjuku Gyo-en](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinjuku_Gyo-en): A large park, 58.3 hectares, blending [Japanese traditional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_garden), [English landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_garden) and [French formal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_garden) style gardens.

  - [Shinjuku Ni-chōme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinjuku_Ni-ch%C5%8Dme): Tokyo's best-known [gay](5171128.md) district.

- [Nishi-Shinjuku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishi-Shinjuku): The area west of Shinjuku Station, historically known as Yodobashi, is home to Tokyo's largest concentration of [skyscrapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyscrapers). Several of the [tallest buildings in Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallest_buildings_in_Tokyo) are located in this area, including the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building, KDDI Building and [Park Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinjuku_Park_Tower).



Ochiai area Shinmejiro St.（2025）

- Ochiai: The northwestern corner of Shinjuku, extending to the area around [Ochiai-minami-nagasaki Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochiai-minami-nagasaki_Station) and the south side of [Mejiro Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mejiro_Station), is largely residential with a small business district around [Nakai Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakai_Station).

- [Ōkubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ckubo,_Tokyo): The area surrounding [Okubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ckubo_Station_\(Tokyo\)) and [Shin-Okubo Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin-Okubo_Station) is best known as Tokyo's historic ethnic Korean neighborhood.

- [Shinanomachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinanomachi) : A district reputed to be the headquarters of a Buddhist organization, which owns many buildings there.

- Totsuka: The northern portion of Shinjuku surrounding [Takadanobaba Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takadanobaba_Station) and [Waseda University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waseda_University), today commonly referred to as [Nishi-Waseda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishi-Waseda). The [Takadanobaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takadanobaba) area is a major residential and nightlife area for students, as well as a commuter hub.

- [Toyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyama,_Shinjuku): A largely residential and school area, in the east of Ōkubo and south of [Waseda University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waseda_University), extending to the area around [Nishi-Waseda Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishi-Waseda_Station), [Gakushuin Women's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gakushuin_Women's_College) and Toyama Park.

- [Ushigome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ushigome,_Tokyo): A largely residential area in the eastern portion of the city.&#x20;

  - [Ichigaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichigaya): A commercial area in eastern Shinjuku, site of the [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Japan\)).

  - [Kagurazaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagurazaka): A hill descending to the [Iidabashi Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iidabashi_Station) area, once one of Tokyo's last remaining *[hanamachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanamachi)* or *[geisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geisha)* districts, and currently known for hosting a sizable French community.

    [^cite_note-20]

- [Yotsuya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yotsuya): An upscale residential and commercial district in the southeast corner of Shinjuku. The Arakichō area is well known for its many small restaurants, bars, and *izakaya*.

"Shinjuku" is often popularly understood to mean the entire area surrounding [Shinjuku Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinjuku_Station), but the Shinjuku Southern Terrace complex and the areas to the west of the station and south of [Kōshū Kaidō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dsh%C5%AB_Kaid%C5%8D) are part of the [Yoyogi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoyogi) and [Sendagaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sendagaya) districts of the special ward of [Shibuya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibuya,_Tokyo).

## Government and politics



[Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_Government_Building)



Shinjuku City Office



[Shinjuku Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinjuku_Central_Park)



Shinjuku shopping street



Shinjuku shopping area



A giant 3D [calico cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calico_cat), known officially as the “Shinjuku Higashi-guchi no Neko” (The Cat of Shinjuku East Exit), projected near the east exit of Shinjuku Station

Like the other [special wards of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_wards_of_Tokyo), Shinjuku has a status equivalent to that of a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Japan). The current mayor is Kenichi Yoshizumi.

The ward council (区議会, *kugikai*) consists of 38 elected members; the [Liberal Democratic Party](164231.md) and [New Komeitō Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Komeit%C5%8D_Party) together currently hold a majority. The [Democratic Party of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_of_Japan), [Japanese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Communist_Party) and the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Japan\)) are also represented together with four [independents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independents_\(politician\)). Shinjuku's city office (区役所, *kuyakusho*) is located on the southeastern edge of Kabukichō.

Shinjuku is also the location of the [Tokyo Metropolitan Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_Government). The governor's office, the metropolitan assembly chamber, and all administrative head offices are located in the [Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Metropolitan_Government_Building).

## Transportation



A guitarist immediately south of the Shinjuku JR Station, a popular [busking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busking) location

Further information: [Transport in Greater Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Greater_Tokyo)

Shinjuku is a major urban transit hub. [Shinjuku Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinjuku_Station) sees an estimated 3.64 million passengers pass through each day, making it the busiest station in the world. It houses interchanges to three subway lines and three privately owned commuter lines, as well as several JR lines.

The southern half of this area surrounding the station and majority of the station are in fact located in the neighboring [Shibuya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shibuya) ward.
