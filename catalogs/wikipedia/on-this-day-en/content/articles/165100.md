In the Philippines, **provinces** ([Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *lalawigan* or *probinsiya*) are the country's primary political and [administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_the_Philippines). There are 82 provinces, which are further subdivided into [component cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines) and [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_the_Philippines). The [local government units](22088280.md) in the [National Capital Region](72340.md), as well as [independent cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Independent_cities), are administratively independent of any provincial government. Each province is governed by an elected [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor) and an elected legislative body known as the [Sangguniang Panlalawigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Panlalawigan).

The provinces are grouped into [18 regions](216786.md) based on geographic, cultural, and ethnolinguistic characteristics. Thirteen of these regions are numerically designated from north to south, while the National Capital Region, the [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region), the Southwestern Tagalog Region ([Mimaropa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimaropa)), the [Negros Island Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Island_Region), and the [Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](37260549.md) are identified by their names or acronyms.

Each province is a member of the [League of Provinces of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Provinces_of_the_Philippines), an organization that represents provincial governments and promotes cooperation on issues affecting provincial administration.[^cite_note-2]

## Government

A provincial government is autonomous of other provinces within the republic. Each province is governed by two main elected branches of the government: executive and legislative. Judicial affairs are separated from provincial governance and are administered by the [Supreme Court of the Philippines](1335534.md). Each province has at least one branch of a [Regional Trial Court](36842278.md).

### Executive

Main article: [List of current Philippine provincial governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_Philippine_provincial_governors)

The provincial governor is chief executive and head of each province. Provincial governors are elected to a term of three years and limited to three consecutive terms and appoint the directors of each provincial department which include the office of administration, engineering office, information office, legal office, and treasury office.

### Legislative

Main article: [Sangguniang Panlalawigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Panlalawigan)

The vice governor acts as the president for each *[Sangguniang Panlalawigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Panlalawigan)* (**SP**; "Provincial Board"), the province's legislative body. Every SP is composed of regularly elected members from provincial districts, as well as *ex officio* members. The number of regularly elected SP members allotted to each province is determined by its income class. First- and second-class provinces are provided ten regular SP members; third- and fourth-class provinces have eight, while fifth- and sixth-class provinces have six. Exceptions are provinces with more than five congressional districts, such as [Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite) with 16 regularly elected SP members, and [Cebu](160278.md), [Negros Occidental](266813.md) and [Pangasinan](1691800.md) which have twelve each.

Every SP has designated seats for *ex officio* members, given to the respective local presidents of the Association of [Barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay) Captains (ABC), Philippine Councilors' League (PCL), and *[Sangguniang Kabataan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Kabataan)* (**SK**; "Youth Council").

The vice governor and regular members of an SP are elected by the voters within the province. *[Ex officio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_officio)* members are elected by members of their respective organizations.

### Relation to other levels of government

#### National government

National intrusion into the affairs of each provincial government is limited by the Philippine Constitution. The [President of the Philippines](271126.md) however coordinates with provincial administrators through the [Department of the Interior and Local Government](4718691.md). For purposes of national representation, each province is guaranteed its own congressional district, ensuring that communities across the Philippines have a voice in the House of Representatives regardless of the population of their province.[^cite_note-3]

#### Cities and municipalities

Main articles: [Cities of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines) and [Municipalities of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_the_Philippines)

Those classified as either "highly urbanized" or "independent component" cities are [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Independent_cities) from the province, as provided for in Section 29 of the Local Government Code of 1991.[^cite_note-LGC-4] Although such a city is a self-governing second-level entity, in many cases it is often presented as part of the province in which it is geographically located, or in the case of [Zamboanga City](25167165.md), the [province it last formed part the congressional representation of](256096.md).

Local government units classified as "component" cities and municipalities are under the jurisdiction of the provincial government. In order to make sure that all component city or municipal governments act within the scope of their prescribed powers and functions, the Local Government Code mandates the provincial governor to review executive orders issued by mayors, and the [Sangguniang Panlalawigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Panlalawigan) to review legislation by the [Sangguniang Panlungsod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Panlungsod) (City Council) or [Sangguniang Bayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Bayan) (Municipal Council), of all component cities and municipalities under the province's jurisdiction.[^cite_note-LGC-4]

### Barangays

The provincial government does not have direct relations with individual barangays. Supervision over a barangay government is the mandate of the mayor and the Sanggunian of the component city or municipality of which the barangay in question is a part.[^cite_note-LGC-4]

### Classification&#x20;



Provinces based on income classification.

Provinces are classified according to average annual income based on the previous 4 calendar years. Effective July 29, 2008, the thresholds for the income classes for cities are:[^cite_note-5]

| Class | Average annual income |
| --- | --- |
| First | [₱](227672.md)450 million or more |
| Second | ₱360 million or more but less than ₱450 million |
| Third | ₱270 million or more but less than ₱360 million |
| Fourth | ₱180 million or more but less than ₱270 million |
| Fifth | ₱90 million or more but less than ₱180 million |
| Sixth | below ₱90 million |

A province's income class determines the size of the membership of its [Sangguniang Panlalawigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Panlalawigan), and also how much it can spend on certain items, or procure through certain means.[^cite_note-LGC-4]

### Former provinces

- [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_\(province\)) (until 1901) –  Incorporated into [Rizal](232458.md); portions around Manila later consolidated to form present-day [NCR](72340.md).

- [Lepanto-Bontoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepanto-Bontoc) (1902–1908) –  Incorporated into [Mountain Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Province).

- [Moro Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Province) (1903–1913) –  Converted to the [Department of Mindanao and Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Mindanao_and_Sulu), composed of seven provinces. Now part of several regions in Mindanao.

- [Ambos Camarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambos_Camarines) (1901–1908) –  Divided into [Camarines Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camarines_Norte) and [Camarines Sur](255883.md), although the wording of *Act No. 2809* implies Camarines Norte was created from *Ambos Camarines*, the remainder of which was renamed Camarines Sur. Camarines Sur retained the provincial capital of [Nueva Caceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga,_Camarines_Sur).

- [Misamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misamis_\(province\)) (1901–1939) –  Partitioned into [Misamis Occidental](260936.md) and [Misamis Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misamis_Oriental). Misamis Oriental retained the provincial capital of [Cagayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro).

- [Mindoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindoro_\(province\)) (1902–1950) –  Divided into [Occidental Mindoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occidental_Mindoro) and [Oriental Mindoro](261948.md). Oriental Mindoro retained the provincial capital of [Calapan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calapan).

- [Zamboanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_\(province\)) (1914–1952) –  Partitioned into [Zamboanga del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_del_Norte) and [Zamboanga del Sur](256096.md). The *de jure* provincial capital of [Molave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molave,_Zamboanga_del_Sur) was placed under the jurisdiction of Zamboanga del Sur which had its capital in [Pagadian](1694645.md). [Zamboanga Sibugay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Sibugay) later created from Zamboanga del Sur.

- [Lanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanao_\(province\)) (1914–1959) –  Divided into [Lanao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanao_del_Norte) and [Lanao del Sur](297370.md). Lanao del Sur retained the provincial capital of Dansalan (now [Marawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marawi)).

- [Surigao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_\(province\)) (1901–1967) –  Partitioned into [Surigao del Norte](267461.md) and [Surigao del Sur](267469.md). Surigao del Norte retained the provincial capital of [Surigao City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_City) and the provincial seal. The province of [Dinagat Islands](3185248.md) was later created from Surigao del Norte.

- [Davao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_\(province\)) (1914–1967; 1972–1998) –  Divided into [Davao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Norte), [Davao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Sur) and [Davao Oriental](296986.md). Davao del Norte was officially known as Davao from 1972 to 1998, when Compostela Valley (now [Davao de Oro](296993.md)) was later created from Davao province. Davao Occidental later created from Davao del Sur.

- [Agusan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_\(province\)) (1907–1967) –  Partitioned into [Agusan del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Norte) and [Agusan del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agusan_del_Sur). Agusan del Norte retained the former provincial capital of [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan). The provincial capital was officially transferred to Cabadbaran in 2000 but the provincial government services and functions are yet to be completely transferred to the new capital.

  [^cite_note-69]

- [Negros del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_del_Norte) (1985–1986) –  *Batas Pambansa Blg. 885*,

  [^cite_note-ndn-70]

  &#x20;which created a new province out of the northern part of [Negros Occidental](266813.md), took effect on December 23, 1985, with a plebiscite to ratify the law held on January 3, 1986. The province comprised the present-day cities of [Cadiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadiz,_Negros_Occidental) (which was to serve as the capital), [Escalante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escalante,_Negros_Occidental), [Sagay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagay,_Negros_Occidental), [San Carlos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Carlos,_Negros_Occidental), [Silay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silay) and [Victorias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorias,_Negros_Occidental), as well as the municipalities of [Calatrava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calatrava,_Negros_Occidental), [Don Salvador Benedicto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Salvador_Benedicto), [Enrique B. Magalona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_B._Magalona,_Negros_Occidental), [Manapla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manapla,_Negros_Occidental), and [Toboso](2051335.md). Despite voters ratifying *Batas Pambansa Blg. 885*, on July 11, 1986, the [Supreme Court](1335534.md) declared the law and the proclamation of the province null and void. The ruling states the enabling law was unconstitutional for, among other things, not including the rest of Negros Occidental in the plebiscite, and the proposed province not meeting the 3,500 square kilometer land area requirement of the 1983 Local Government Code.

  [^cite_note-ndn2-71]

- [Kalinga-Apayao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga-Apayao) (1966–1995) –  Divided into [Apayao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apayao_\(province\)) and [Kalinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_\(province\)). Kalinga retained the provincial capital of [Tabuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabuk,_Kalinga).

- [Shariff Kabunsuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shariff_Kabunsuan) (2006–2008) –  *Republic Act No. 9054* conferred to the [Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Region_in_Muslim_Mindanao) expanded powers, especially the capacity to create provinces (Article VI, Section 19).

  [^cite_note-72]

  &#x20;Based on this, the [ARMM Regional Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARMM_Regional_Legislative_Assembly) enacted *Muslim Mindanao Autonomy Act No. 201* on August 28, 2006. The Act created a new province, comprising all the municipalities in the [first congressional district of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_Maguindanao#1st_District) (except [Cotabato City](298955.md)), with its capital at [Datu Odin Sinsuat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu_Odin_Sinsuat). The province's creation was approved on October 28, 2006, by a majority vote in a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Shariff_Kabunsuan_creation_plebiscite). Responding to requests for clarification as to which congressional districts form Shariff Kabunsuan for the [2007 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Philippine_general_election) (specifically whether Cotabato City was part of the representation of the new province), [COMELEC](1847922.md) issued *Resolution No. 7845*, which initially held Cotabato City to be the sole remaining [LGU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_unit) in the First District of Maguindanao. COMELEC later amended this with *Resolution No. 7902*, which maintained the *status quo* before the province's creation. The COMELEC resolutions became the subject of [a case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sema_v._COMELEC_and_Dilangalen) in which the Supreme Court opined that because "*the power to create new a province or city inherently involves the power to create a legislative district*"—a power that [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Philippines) did not explicitly delegate to the ARMM Regional Assembly—the creation of a province by a lower legislative body (the ARMM Regional Assembly) will necessarily entail the creation of a legislative district for a higher legislative body (Congress). Therefore, on July 16, 2008, the Supreme Court declared Section 19, Article VI of *RA No. 9054* unconstitutional, *MMA Act No. 201* void, and *COMELEC Resolution No. 7902* valid.

  [^cite_note-svc-73]

- [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao) (1973–2022) – *Republic Act No. 11550*

  [^cite_note-74]

  &#x20;divided the province into Maguindanao del Norte and Maguindanao del Sur through a plebiscite. Maguindanao del Norte resembles the former province of Shariff Kabunsuan however adds the municipality of Sultan Sumagka into its territory. The capital of Maguindanao del Norte is [Datu Odin Sinsuat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu_Odin_Sinsuat) while the capital of Maguindanao del Sur is [Buluan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buluan).

### Timeline

The [Revolutionary Government of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Government_of_the_Philippines) and later the [First Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic) continued the original Spanish divisions, establishing civil government on the provincial as well as municipal scale in areas liberated from Spanish sovereignty. The United States, asserting ownership over the Philippines, divided the islands into four *gobiernos* (governments), which were further subdivided into provinces and districts. The American administration likewise inherited the Spanish divisions and placed them under military government. Civil government instituted under Filipino authority was gradually replaced with American civil government as towns fell to the Americans during the [Philippine–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine%E2%80%93American_War).

## Formally proposed/renamed provinces

### Proposed provinces with enacted law

This section only lists the proposals that reached the stage where a legislation was enacted for the purpose of establishing a province or a sub-province but never achieved, or yet to achieve, corporate existence.



Map of the Philippines showing the proposed provinces

- **Occidental Leyte and Oriental Leyte** (1923) – [Leyte](194750.md) was divided into two new provinces by *Act No. 3117* on March 27, 1923.

  [^cite_note-181]

  &#x20;The division never took place, however, as no proclamation was issued by the [Governor-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_the_Philippines).&#x20;

  - The province of **Oriental Leyte** would have covered the present-day territories of the entire province of Biliran, the municipalities of Abuyog, Alangalang, Babatngon, Barugo, Burauen, Calubian, Capoocan, Carigara, Dagami, Dulag, Jaro, Javier, Julita, La Paz, Leyte, MacArthur, Mahaplag, Mayorga, Palo, Pastrana, San Isidro, San Miguel, Santa Fe, Tabango, Tabontabon, Tanauan, Tolosa, Tunga and Tacloban (which was designated as the provincial capital).

  - The province of **Occidental Leyte** would have covered the present-day territories of the entire province of Southern Leyte, the municipalities of Albuera, Bato, Hilongos, Hindang, Inopacan, Isabel, Kananga, Matag-ob, Matalom, Mérida, Palompon, Villaba and the cities of Baybay and Ormoc. The province capital of Occidental Leyte "**SEC. 2.***&#x20;... shall be designated by the Governor-General, until determined by a plurality vote of the electors of the new province at the next general election.*"

- **Samal** (1969) – The sub-province of Samal was created by *Republic Act No. 5999*

  [^cite_note-samal-182]

  &#x20;and covered the area of the present-day [Island Garden City of Samal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samal,_Davao_del_Norte). However, the sub-province was never inaugurated.

- **Maranaw** (1971) – *Republic Act No. 6406*,

  [^cite_note-maranaw-183]

  &#x20;which sought to create a new province out of eastern [Lanao del Sur](297370.md) (now corresponding to the province's [first congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_Lanao_del_Sur#1st_District)), was approved on October 4, 1971. The province was to consist of the municipalities of [Bubong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubong,_Lanao_del_Sur), [Ditsaan-Ramain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ditsaan-Ramain,_Lanao_del_Sur) (including what is now [Buadiposo-Buntong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buadiposo-Buntong,_Lanao_del_Sur)), [Kapai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapai,_Lanao_del_Sur), [Lumba-Bayabao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumba-Bayabao,_Lanao_del_Sur) (including what is now [Maguing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguing,_Lanao_del_Sur)), [Marantao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marantao,_Lanao_del_Sur), [Masiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masiu,_Lanao_del_Sur), [Mulondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulondo,_Lanao_del_Sur), [Saguiaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saguiaran,_Lanao_del_Sur), [Piagapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piagapo,_Lanao_del_Sur), [Poona Bayabao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poona_Bayabao,_Lanao_del_Sur), [Tamparan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamparan,_Lanao_del_Sur), [Taraka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taraka,_Lanao_del_Sur) and [Wao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wao,_Lanao_del_Sur) (including what is now [Bumbaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumbaran,_Lanao_del_Sur)), with the chartered city of [Marawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marawi) serving as the new province's capital. Lanao del Sur was to retain the remaining municipalities, with [Malabang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabang,_Lanao_del_Sur) serving as its new capital. Section 4 of RA 6406 provided that "*The new provinces as provided in this Act shall come into existence upon the election and qualification of their first elective provincial officials, who shall be elected in a special election simultaneously with the general elections of November, nineteen hundred and seventy-three*." The division never took place due to the [declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_No._1081) of [martial law in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_the_Philippines) on September 21, 1972, which disrupted the scheduled general elections for 1973 and paved the way for the adoption of a new [Constitution](19554336.md) and the establishment of the [Fourth Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Philippine_Republic). A legacy of this unimplemented division is the existence of two [ZIP code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZIP_codes_in_the_Philippines) series for Lanao del Sur: the 93- series was retained by what were to be the remaining towns of the province (with Malabang, the new capital, being reassigned the code 9300), while a new series (97-) was assigned to what was supposed to be the province of Maranaw (with Marawi getting the new code 9700).

- **Isabela del Norte and Isabela del Sur** (1995) – On February 20, 1995, *Republic Act No. 7891*,

Isabela del Sur was to consist of the [third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_Isabela#3rd_District) and [fourth congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_Isabela#4th_District) (excluding the independent component city of [Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago,_Isabela)), with [Cauayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauayan,_Isabela) as the capital. - **Quezon del Norte and Quezon del Sur** (2007) – The act dividing the province of [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon) into two, *Republic Act No. 9495*,

  [^cite_note-qds-185]

  &#x20;lapsed into law without [the President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo)'s signature on September 7, 2007. Quezon del Norte (which would be renamed from Quezon) was to be composed of the [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_Quezon#1st_District) and [second congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_Quezon#2nd_District) of the province, with [Lucena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucena,_Philippines) as its capital. Quezon del Sur, with its capital at [Gumaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gumaca,_Quezon), would have been composed of the [third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_Quezon#3rd_District) and [fourth congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_Quezon#4th_District). The COMELEC held the [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Quezon_del_Sur_creation_plebiscite) on December 13, 2008, and the majority of the votes rejected the division. Notwithstanding the voters' rejection, all the representatives of Quezon's four legislative districts—Wilfrido Mark Enverga, Irvin Alcala, [Danilo Suarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danilo_Suarez), and [Lorenzo Tañada III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Ta%C3%B1ada)—reintroduced the measure as House Bill No. 3839 on December 8, 2010. They explained that “the wisdom, logic and bare facts behind what is driving this bill to be filed again in this Congress remain,” citing the persistence of poverty in far-flung coastal municipalities and small islands. Yet, the bill did not make it out of the committee level this time.

- **Palawan del Norte**, **Palawan Oriental**, and **Palawan del Sur** (2021) –  On April 5, 2019, President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) signed Republic Act No. 11259 that proposed the division the province of Palawan, with the exception of the independent city of [Puerto Princesa](2171719.md), into three separate provinces. A [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Palawan_division_plebiscite) was originally scheduled for the second Monday of May 2020

  [^cite_note-188]

  &#x20;but was postponed to March 2021 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_Philippines).

  [^cite_note-189]

  &#x20;Palawan del Norte would have been composed of [Taytay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taytay,_Palawan) and municipalities north of it. Palawan del Sur would have been composed of the municipalities west and south of Puerto Princesa, including [Kalayaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalayaan,_Palawan) (which administers the country's claims in the [Spratly Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spratly_Islands)), while the rest were proposed to form Palawan Oriental. The proposed provincial capitals would have been [Taytay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taytay,_Palawan) (Palawan del Norte), [Brooke's Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooke's_Point) (Palawan del Sur), and [Roxas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roxas,_Palawan) (Palawan Oriental).

  [^cite_note-palawan-190]

  &#x20;The COMELEC held the plebiscite on March 13, 2021, and the majority of the votes rejected the division.

### Proposed renaming

- [Southern Leyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Leyte) to **Leyte del Sur** (2007)  –  [Southern Leyte Lone District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Leyte's_at-large_congressional_district) Rep. Roger Mercado introduced bills to rename the province of [Southern Leyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Leyte) to Leyte del Sur, citing a desire to “enhance the identity of \[the province] as geographically a part of the [Leyte Island](194750.md) with a distinct personality of its own” and to have the province alphabetically listed closer to the neighboring province of [Leyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leyte_\(province\)), from which it was separated in 1959.

  [^cite_note-235]

  &#x20;Mercado first filed the bill in 2007,

  [^cite_note-236]

  &#x20;and this attempt gained traction during the 14th Congress, as House Bill No. 6650 was passed by the House of Representatives on September 8, 2009, with 170 votes in its favor and none against it. The Senate, however, did not act on the measure.

  [^cite_note-237]

  &#x20;Mercado's second attempt in 2017 did not prosper, as House Bill No. 1602 died in the committee level during the 17th Congress.
