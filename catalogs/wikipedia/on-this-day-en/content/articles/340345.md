# Doctor of the Church



[Isidore of Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isidore_of_Seville), a seventh-century Doctor of the Church, depicted by [Murillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartolom%C3%A9_Esteban_Murillo) (c. 1628) with a book, which is a common iconographical [attribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblem) for a doctor



[Hildegard von Bingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildegard_von_Bingen) was a twelfth-century Doctor of the Church, depicted by Marshall with a book, the common iconographical attribute for a doctor

**Doctor of the Church** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *doctor*, 'teacher'), also referred to as **Doctor of the Universal Church** (*Doctor Ecclesiae Universalis*), is a title given by the [Catholic Church](606848.md) to saints recognized as having made a significant contribution to theology or doctrine through their research, study, or writing.[^cite_note-1]

As of 2025, the Catholic Church has named 38 Doctors of the Church. Of these, the 18 who died before the [Great Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East-West_Schism) of 1054 are also held in high esteem by the [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md), although it does not use the formal title *Doctor of the Church*.

Among the 38 recognised Doctors, 29 are from the West and nine from the East; four are women and 34 are men; one is an abbess, three are nuns, and one is a tertiary associated with a religious order; two are popes, 19 are bishops, 13 are priests, and one is a deacon; and 28 are from Europe, three are from Africa, and seven are from Asia. More Doctors (twelve) lived in the fourth century than any other; eminent Christian writers of the first, second, and third centuries are usually referred to as the [Ante-Nicene Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ante-Nicene_Fathers). The shortest period between death and nomination was that of [Alphonsus Liguori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphonsus_Liguori), who died in 1787 and was named a Doctor in 1871 – a period of 84 years; the longest was that of [Irenaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irenaeus), which took more than 18 centuries.

Some other churches have similar categories with various names.

## Before the 16th century

In the [Western church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_church) four outstanding "Fathers of the Church" attained this honour in the early Middle Ages: [Gregory the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_the_Great), [Ambrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrose), [Augustine of Hippo](2030.md), and [Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome). The "four Doctors" became a commonplace notion among [scholastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholasticism) theologians. In 1298, pope [Boniface VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boniface_VIII) decreed that they should be honoured with [double feasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_feast) throughout the Latin Church, in his sixth book of Decretals (cap. "Gloriosus", de relique. et vener. sanctorum, in Sexto, III, 22)<sup>\[*[needs translation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia\:Template_index/Translation#not_English-inline)*]</sup>.[^cite_note-CathEn-2]

In the [Byzantine Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Rite), three Doctors were pre-eminent: [John Chrysostom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Chrysostom), [Basil the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_the_Great), and [Gregory of Nazianzus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_of_Nazianzus). The feasts of these three saints were made obligatory throughout the Eastern Empire by [Leo VI the Wise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_VI_the_Wise). A common feast was later instituted in their honour on 30 January, called "the feast of the [three Hierarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Hierarchs)". In the Menaea for that day it is related that the three Doctors appeared in a dream to [John Mauropous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mauropous), Bishop of [Euchaita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euchaita), and commanded him to institute a festival in their honour, in order to put a stop to the rivalries of their votaries and panegyrists.[^cite_note-CathEn-2]

This was under [Alexius Comnenus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexius_Comnenus) (1081–1118; see "Acta SS.", 14 June, under St. Basil, c. xxxviii). But sermons for the feast are attributed in manuscripts to [Cosmas Vestitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmas_Vestitor), who flourished in the tenth century. The three are as common in Eastern art as the four are in Western. [Durandus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaume_Durand_\(nephew\)) (i, 3) remarks that Doctors should be represented with books in their hands. In the West analogy led to the veneration of four Eastern Doctors, [Athanasius of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria) being added to the three hierarchs.[^cite_note-CathEn-2]

## Catholic Church



The Four Great Doctors of the Western Church were often depicted in art, here by [Pier Francesco Sacchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_Francesco_Sacchi), c. 1516. From the left: [Saint Augustine](2030.md), [Pope Gregory I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I), [Saint Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome), and [Saint Ambrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrose), with their [attributes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblem)

The details of the title *Doctor of the Church* vary from one [autonomous ritual church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particular_church) to another.

### Latin Church

In the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church), the four Latin Doctors ([Ambrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrose), [Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustine), [Jerome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerome), and [Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_the_Great)) had been given a special pre-eminence since the eighth century, but in 1298 [Pope Boniface VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Boniface_VIII) declared them Doctors of the Church.[^cite_note-3] [Pope Pius V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_V) recognized the four Great Doctors of the Eastern Church ([John Chrysostom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Chrysostom), [Basil the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_the_Great), [Gregory of Nazianzus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_of_Nazianzus), and [Athanasius of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria)) in 1568.[^cite_note-4]

To these names others have subsequently been added. There are three requisite conditions: eminent learning, a high degree of sanctity, and proclamation by the church. [Benedict XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_XIV) stated that the declaration must be made by the [supreme pontiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_pontiff) or a [general council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_council).[^cite_note-CathEn-2]

The decree is issued by the [Dicastery for the Causes of Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_the_Causes_of_Saints) and approved by the pope, after a careful examination, if necessary, of the saint's writings. It is not an *[ex cathedra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_cathedra)* decision, nor does it amount to a declaration that no error is to be found in the teaching of the Doctor. Doctors of the Church are not considered wholly immune from error. Previously, no [martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyr) were on the list, since the Office and the Mass had been for [Confessors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessor_of_the_Faith). Hence, as Benedict XIV pointed out during his pontificate, [Ignatius of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignatius_of_Antioch), [Irenaeus of Lyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irenaeus), and [Cyprian of Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprian) were not called Doctors of the Church.[^cite_note-CathEn-2] This changed in 2022 when Pope Francis declared Irenaeus of Lyons the first martyred Doctor.

The Doctors' works vary greatly in subject and form. Augustine of Hippo was one of the most prolific writers in Christian antiquity and wrote in almost every genre. Some, such as Pope [Gregory the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I) and Ambrose of Milan, were prominent writers of letters. Pope [Leo the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_I), Pope Gregory the Great, [Peter Chrysologus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Chrysologus), [Bernard of Clairvaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux), [Anthony of Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_of_Padua) and [Lawrence of Brindisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_of_Brindisi) left many homilies. [Catherine of Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena), [Teresa of Ávila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teresa_of_%C3%81vila), [John of the Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_the_Cross) and [Therese of Lisieux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Therese_of_Lisieux) wrote works of [mystical theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystical_theology). Athanasius of Alexandria and [Robert Bellarmine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bellarmine) defended the church against [heresy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heresy). [Bede the Venerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bede) wrote biblical commentaries and theological treatises. Systematic theologians include the Scholastic philosophers [Anselm of Canterbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anselm_of_Canterbury), [Albertus Magnus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albertus_Magnus), and [Thomas Aquinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas).

In the 1920 encyclical *Spiritus Paraclitus*, [Pope Benedict XV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XV) refers to Jerome as the church's "Greatest Doctor".[^cite_note-5]

Until 1970, no woman had been named a Doctor of the Church. Since then four additions to the list have been women: [Teresa of Ávila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teresa_of_%C3%81vila) (also known as Saint Teresa of Jesus) and [Catherine of Siena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_of_Siena) by [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI); [Therese of Lisieux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Therese_of_Lisieux)[^cite_note-6] by [Pope John Paul II](23805.md); and [Hildegard of Bingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildegard_of_Bingen) by [Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_XVI). Teresa and Thérèse were both [Discalced Carmelites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discalced_Carmelite), Catherine was a [Dominican tertiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Order_of_Saint_Dominic), and Hildegard was a [Benedictine](4240.md).

Traditionally, in the [Liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgy), the Office of Doctors was distinguished from that of [Confessors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessor_of_the_Faith) by two changes: the Gospel reading *Vos estis sal terrae* ("You are the salt of the earth"), Matthew 5\:13–19, and the eighth Respond at Matins, from Sirach 15\:5, *In medio Ecclesiae aperuit os ejus, \* Et implevit eum Deus spiritu sapientiae et intellectus. \* Jucunditatem et exsultationem thesaurizavit super eum.* ("In the midst of the Church he opened his mouth, \* And God filled him with the spirit of wisdom and understanding. \* He heaped upon him a treasure of joy and gladness.") The [Nicene Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicene_Creed) was also recited at Mass, which is normally not said except on Sundays and the highest-ranking feast days. The 1962 revisions to the Missal dropped the Creed from feasts of Doctors and abolished the title and the Common of Confessors, instituting a distinct Common of Doctors.

On 20 August 2011, [Pope Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) announced that he would soon declare [John of Ávila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_%C3%81vila) a Doctor of the Church.[^cite_note-7] It was also reported in December 2011 that Pope Benedict intended to declare [Hildegard of Bingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hildegard_of_Bingen) as a Doctor of the Church, though she had not been formally canonized.[^cite_note-8] Pope Benedict XVI declared Hildegard of Bingen a saint on 10 May 2012, clearing the way for her to be named a Doctor of the Church,[^cite_note-9] then declared both John of Ávila and Hildegard of Bingen Doctors of the Church on 7 October 2012.[^cite_note-10]

[Pope Francis](1687680.md) declared the 10th-century Armenian monk [Gregory of Narek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_of_Narek) the 36th Doctor of the Church on 21 February 2015.[^cite_note-11] The decision was somewhat controversial, as Gregory was a monk of the [Armenian Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church), a [non-Chalcedonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Chalcedonian_Christianity) church that was not in communion with the Catholic Church during Gregory's life and has sometimes been described as [monophysite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monophysite). However, the Armenian Apostolic Church does not accept monophysitism, and in 1996, Pope [John Paul II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Paul_II) and [Catholicos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicos) [Karekin I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karekin_I), patriarch of the Armenian Apostolic Church, signed a joint declaration which said that the division between the two churches was due to historical misunderstandings, not a real difference in Christology. Further, Gregory had been recognized as a saint by the Catholic Church since it received the [Armenian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church) into full communion.[^cite_note-12]

Saint Cardinal [John Henry Newman](83895.md) was proclaimed a doctor of the Church on All Saints Day, Saturday, November 1, 2025 by [Pope Leo XIV](54520372.md) at [Saint Peter's Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Square) at [Vatican City](32408.md).[^cite_note-13] He was named co-patron of Catholic education joining [Saint Thomas Aquinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Aquinas). On 31 July 2025, a statement from the [Holy See](13393.md) Press Office reported that during an audience granted to Cardinal Marcello Semeraro, Prefect of the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, [Pope Leo XIV](54520372.md) had "confirmed the affirmative opinion of the Plenary Session of Cardinals and Bishops, Members of the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, regarding the title of Doctor of the Universal Church, would be conferred on Saint [John Henry Newman](83895.md)."[^cite_note-14] In November 2023, the [United States Conference of Catholic Bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Conference_of_Catholic_Bishops) had voted to support a petition by the [Catholic Bishops' Conference of England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Bishops'_Conference_of_England_and_Wales) for the Vatican to name John Henry Newman a Doctor of the Church.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

#### Proposed Doctors

In April 2000, a Capuchin writer on the subject suggested that [John Bosco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bosco) and [Louis de Montfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_de_Montfort) would be the next doctors of the Church.[^cite_note-\:0-28]

In October 2018, on the occasion of the canonization of [Oscar Romero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Arnulfo_Romero_y_Galdamez), martyred Archbishop of [San Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Salvador), [José Luis Escobar Alas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Luis_Escobar_Alas), the current Archbishop of San Salvador, petitioned Pope Francis to name Romero a Doctor of the Church.[^cite_note-rom-29]

In October 2019, the Polish Catholic Bishops Conference formally petitioned Pope Francis to consider making [Pope John Paul II](23805.md) a Doctor of the Church in an official proclamation, in recognition of his contributions to theology, philosophy, and Catholic literature, as well as the formal documents of his papacy.[^cite_note-jp2-30]

In January 2023, [Cardinal](6221.md) [Angelo Bagnasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelo_Bagnasco) proposed that [Pope Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) be declared a doctor of the Church "as soon as possible", in view of his theological intelligence and contribution to the formation of current doctrine of the [Catholic Church](606848.md), such as the new [catechism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catechism_of_the_Catholic_Church).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] In January 2024, [Archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop) [Georg Gänswein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_G%C3%A4nswein) also spoke in favor of the pontiff's [canonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonization) and his elevation to the status of doctor of the church.[^cite_note-papa-33]

In April 2024, during a private audience Pope Francis received a formal request from the [superior general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_general) of the [Discalced Carmelites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discalced_Carmelites), Miguel Márquez Calle, to declare [Teresa Benedicta of the Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edith_Stein) a Doctor of the Church. The Discalced Carmelites first launched an international commission to gather the necessary documentation required for the declaration in 2022, in commemoration of the 100th anniversary of her conversion to Catholicism and the 80th anniversary of her martyrdom.[^cite_note-stein-34]

#### Other recognised Doctors

In addition, parts of the Catholic Church have recognised other individuals with this title. In [Spain](26667.md), [Fulgentius of Cartagena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulgentius_of_Cartagena),[^cite_note-35] [Ildephonsus of Toledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ildephonsus_of_Toledo)[^cite_note-36] and [Leander of Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leander_of_Seville)[^cite_note-CathEn-2] have been recognized with this title. In 2007 Pope [Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_XVI), in his encyclical *[Spe Salvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spe_Salvi)*, called [Maximus the Confessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximus_the_Confessor) "the great Greek Doctor of the Church",[^cite_note-37] though the [Dicastery for the Causes of Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_the_Causes_of_Saints) considers this declaration an informal one.[^cite_note-38]

#### Scholastic epithets

Main article: [Scholastic accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholastic_accolades)

Though not named Doctors of the Church or even canonized, many of the more celebrated doctors of theology and law of the Middle Ages were given an epithet which expressed the nature of their expertise. Among these are Bl. [John Duns Scotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Duns_Scotus), *Doctor subtilis* ("subtle doctor"); [Alexander of Hales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_of_Hales), *Doctor irrefragabilis* ("unanswerable doctor"); [Roger Bacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Bacon), *Doctor mirabilis* ("wondrous doctor"); [William of Ockham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_of_Ockham), *Doctor singularis et invincibilis* ("valuable and invincible doctor"); [Jean Gerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Gerson), *Doctor christianissimus* ("most Christian doctor"); and [Francisco Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Su%C3%A1rez), *Doctor eximius* ("exceptional doctor").[^cite_note-39]

### Syro-Malabar Catholic Church

The [Syro-Malabar Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syro-Malabar_Catholic_Church) recognises Ambrose, Jerome, Gregory, Augustine, Athanasius, Basil, Gregory of Nazianzus and John Chrysostom, as well as [Ephrem the Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephrem_the_Syrian), [Pope Leo I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_I), [John of Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_Damascus), [Cyril of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_of_Alexandria), [Cyril of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_of_Jerusalem), [Epiphanius of Salamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphanius_of_Salamis) and [Gregory of Nyssa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_of_Nyssa).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-\:0-28]

## Eastern Orthodox Church

The [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md) honors many of the pre-schism saints as well, but the term *Doctor of the Church* is not applied in the same way. One consistent use of the category is the trio of [Basil the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_the_Great), [Gregory of Nazianzus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_of_Nazianzus) and [John Chrysostom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Chrysostom), recognized as universal teachers and known as the [Three Holy Hierarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Holy_Hierarchs).[^cite_note-46] The church also recognizes three saints with the title *Theologos* ([Theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theologian)): [John the Evangelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Evangelist), [Gregory of Nazianzus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_of_Nazianzus) and [Symeon the New Theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symeon_the_New_Theologian).[^cite_note-47]

### Russian Orthodox Church

The Russian Orthodox Church commemorates on 19 July the feast of Three Holy Russian Hierarchs: [Demetrius of Rostov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demetrius_of_Rostov), [Mitrophan of Voronezh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitrophan_of_Voronezh) and [Tikhon of Zadonsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikhon_of_Zadonsk).[^cite_note-48]
