**Luís Roberto Barroso** (Portuguese pronunciation: [\[ɫwis, ɫwiʃ ʁoˈbeɾtu baˈʁɔzu\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese); born 11 March 1958) is a Brazilian law [professor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor), [jurist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurist), and retired [Justice](44719.md) of the [Supreme Federal Court](3359076.md) of Brazil, having been nominated to the position by President [Dilma Rousseff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilma_Rousseff) in 2013. Between 2020 and 2022, Barroso also served as President of the [Superior Electoral Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_Electoral_Court).[^cite_note-1]

Barroso graduated in law from the [Universidade do Estado do Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidade_do_Estado_do_Rio_de_Janeiro) (UERJ), holds a [Master's Degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_Degree) in law from [Yale University](34273.md), and a [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) from [UERJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_de_Janeiro_State_University). He has done [post-doctoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-doctoral) studies at [Harvard Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Law_School) and he is a professor of [Constitutional Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Law) at UERJ.

He is considered a [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism) and [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism) Justice,[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] providing landmark votes on the legalization of [abortion](765.md) in pregnancies originated from [rape](7980471.md)[^cite_note-4] and the criminalization of [homophobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophobia) and [transphobia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transphobia) in Brazil.[^cite_note-5]

On 18 October 2025, Justice Barroso officially retired from the Supreme Court.[^cite_note-6]

## Life and career

Born in the city of [Vassouras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassouras), Barroso received a bachelor's degree in law from the [State University of Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_Rio_de_Janeiro) (UERJ) in 1980, and an [LL.M.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LL.M.) from [Yale Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_Law_School) in 1987. He received a doctorate in [public law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_law) from UERJ in 2008 and is a tenured professor of constitutional law at the university.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] In 2011, Barroso was a visiting scholar at [Harvard Law School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Law_School), and while in the United States published the paper "Here, there and everywhere: human dignity in contemporary law and in the transnational discourse".[^cite_note-Yale-9]

At 2011, Barroso became nationally known for being the lawyer of the militant [Cesare Battisti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Battisti_\(militant\)) in Brazil. Barroso implemented a defense that argued that his client's actions were justified because the actions were part of Battisti's fight against an authoritarian Italian regime.[^cite_note-10]

Barroso owned the law firm Luís Roberto Barroso & Associados in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), which specialized in public law and Supreme Court litigation.[^cite_note-Yale-9] Prior to being nominated to the Supreme Federal Court by Dilma Rousseff in May 2013 to replace Justice [Carlos Ayres Britto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Ayres_Britto), Barroso served as a state attorney in [Rio de Janeiro](229419.md) state. He was the fourth Supreme Court nominee of Rousseff, who had previously nominated the justices [Luiz Fux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luiz_Fux), [Rosa Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosa_Weber) and [Teori Zavascki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teori_Zavascki).[^cite_note-11] He was confirmed by the [Federal Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Senate) in early June, and was sworn into office on 26 June 2013.[^cite_note-12]

Barroso has been invited to lecture in various universities around the world, including the [New York University School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_University_School_of_Law), in the United States, and [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics) and [Oxford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_University), in England.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

Barroso is an advocate for drug legalization, starting with [decriminalizing the possession of marijuana](168917.md) for private consumption.[^cite_note-15] His judicial views have been described as [progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism).[^cite_note-16]

On 9 October 2025, Barroso announced his retirement from the Supreme Federal Court, eight years before the mandatory retirement age of 75.[^cite_note-17]

## Controversies

On 13 November 2022, while in [New York City](645042.md) attending a conference by Grupo LIDE near [Harvard Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Club_of_New_York_City), Barroso was approached by Brazilian demonstrators, and in response he uttered the phrase "Perdeu mané, não amola" (Brazilian Portuguese pronunciation: [\[peʁˈdew maˈnɛ nɐ̃w̃ aˈmɔlɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese)), meaning: You lost, man, don't bother me.[^cite_note-18] It has become a notorious expression in Brazilian politics, having been defaced into a statue at the [Praça dos Três Poderes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pra%C3%A7a_dos_Tr%C3%AAs_Poderes) by Débora Rodrigues, a far-right bolsonarist demonstrator during the [8 January 2023 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8_January_Bras%C3%ADlia_attacks).[^cite_note-19]

On 13 July 2023, while attending an event at [UNE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Union_of_Students_\(Brazil\)), he uttered the phrase "Nós derrotamos o bolsonarismo", meaning: We defeated [Bolsonarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsonarism).[^cite_note-20] The phrase has been used by conservative Brazilians to accuse Barrroso of bias. He apologized for the phrase and later expressed regret.[^cite_note-21]
