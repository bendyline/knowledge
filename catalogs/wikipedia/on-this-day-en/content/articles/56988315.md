This article contains a list of political parties in [France](5843419.md).

France has a [multi-party political system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system): one in which the number of competing [political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) is sufficiently large as to make it almost inevitable that, in order to participate in the exercise of power, any single party must be prepared to negotiate with one or more others with a view to forming [electoral alliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_alliance) and/or [coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition) agreements.

The dominant French political parties are also characterised by a noticeable degree of intra-party [factionalism](40362512.md), making each of them effectively a coalition in itself.

Until 2017, the government of France had alternated between two rather stable [coalitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition):

- on the [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left), one led by the [Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(France\)) and with minor partners such as [The Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Greens_\(France\)) and the [Radical Party of the Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Party_of_the_Left).

- on the [centre-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right), one led by [The Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_R%C3%A9publicains) (and previously its predecessors, the [Union for a Popular Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_a_Popular_Movement), [Rally for the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rally_for_the_Republic)) and the [Union of Democrats and Independents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Democrats_and_Independents).

This was the case until the [2017 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_French_presidential_election), when [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) of the [centrist](39619035.md) [La République En Marche!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_R%C3%A9publique_En_Marche!) defeated [Marine Le Pen](572897.md) of the [far-right](221220.md) [National Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rally_\(France\)) in the second round. This was the first time in which a third party had won the presidency, as well as the first time that neither of the major coalitions had appeared in the second round of a presidential election. This was followed shortly by a significant victory for [LREM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LREM) in the [2017 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_French_legislative_election), winning a majority of 350 seats. Both the traditional coalitions suffered major defeats.

In the [2022 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_presidential_election), the same scenario repeated, with [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) being again victorious. Both traditional parties ([Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(France\)) and [The Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_R%C3%A9publicains)) scored less than 5% each, with [Jean-Luc Mélenchon](20298042.md)'s [La France Insoumise](52520313.md) emerging as the dominant [left-wing](18499.md) party, ranking third in the first round.

The National Rally (previously known as the National Front before a name change in 2018) has also experienced significant successes in other elections. Since 2014, the party has established itself as a major party in France, finishing in first place in the [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_European_Parliament_election_in_France) and [2019 European elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_European_Parliament_election_in_France) as well as in the 2015 local elections,[^cite_note-1] though the party failed to win government in any regions due to the last-ditch alliance between the centre-left and the centre-right coalitions in [Hauts-de-France](45093681.md) and [Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provence-Alpes-C%C3%B4te_d'Azur).[^cite_note-2]
