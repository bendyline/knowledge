**Życzyn** [\[ˈʐɨt͡ʂɨn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish) is a [village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village) in the administrative district of [Gmina Trojanów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gmina_Trojan%C3%B3w), within [Garwolin County](5461537.md), [Masovian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masovian_Voivodeship), in east-central Poland.[^cite_note-TERYT-1]

Życzyn was a royal village of the Kingdom of Poland,[^cite_note-sgk-2] administratively located in the [Sandomierz Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandomierz_Voivodeship) in the [Lesser Poland Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland_Province,_Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland).[^cite_note-4]

According to the 1921 census, the village had a population of 689, entirely [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) by nationality and 99.0% [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) by confession.[^cite_note-gus-3]

21 Polish citizens were murdered by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) in the village during [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-5]

On 16 November 2025 the railway in the village was demolished by an act of [sabotage](29462.md), for which Poland blamed two Russian-linked Ukrainian nationals.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]
