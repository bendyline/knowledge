**Munich**[^cite_note-5] ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *München* [\[ˈmʏnçn̩\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/78/Muenchentitle.ogg/Muenchentitle.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/78/Muenchentitle.ogg/Muenchentitle.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, [Bavarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_language): *Minga* [\[ˈmɪŋ(ː)ɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/61/Bar-M%C3%BCnchen.ogg/Bar-M%C3%BCnchen.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/61/Bar-M%C3%BCnchen.ogg/Bar-M%C3%BCnchen.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is the capital and most populous city of the German state of [Bavaria](3764.md). As of 30 November 2024, its population was 1,604,384,[^cite_note-stadtbevoelkerung-6] making it the [third most-populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Germany_by_population) in Germany after [Berlin](3354.md) and [Hamburg](13467.md). Munich is the largest city in Germany that is not [a state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) of [its own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City-state), and it ranks as the 11th-largest city in the [European Union](9317.md) (EU). The metropolitan area has around 3 million inhabitants, and the broader [Munich Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Metropolitan_Region) is home to about 6.2 million people.[^cite_note-mmr_official_site-7] It is the [third largest metropolitan region by GDP in the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_EU_metropolitan_regions_by_GDP#2021_ranking_of_top_four_German_metropolitan_regions). Munich is located on the river [Isar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isar) north of the [Alps](981.md). It is the seat of the [Upper Bavarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Bavaria) administrative region. With 4,800 people per km<sup>2</sup>, Munich is Germany's most densely populated municipality. It is also the second-largest city in the [Bavarian dialect area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_language) after [Vienna](55866.md).

The first record of Munich dates to 1158. The city has played an important role in Bavarian and German history. During the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation), it remained a Catholic stronghold. Munich became the capital of the [Kingdom of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria) in 1806 and developed as a centre for arts, architecture, culture, and science. The [House of Wittelsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Wittelsbach) ruled until 1918, when the [German revolution of 1918–1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_revolution_of_1918%E2%80%931919) ended their reign and saw the short-lived [Bavarian Soviet Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Soviet_Republic). In the 1920s, Munich became a centre of political movements, including the rise of the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party). The city was known as the "Capital of the Movement". During [World War II](32927.md), Munich was heavily bombed, but much of its historic architecture has since been restored.[^cite_note-englund-8][^cite_note-zensus-9] After the war, the city's population and economy grew rapidly. Munich hosted the [1972 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Summer_Olympics) and the [1974 FIFA World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup_final).

Munich is a major centre for science, technology, finance, innovation, business, and tourism. It has a high standard of living, ranking first in Germany and third worldwide in the 2018 [Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercer_\(consulting_firm\)) survey.[^cite_note-mercer-10] It was named the world's most liveable city by [*Monocle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocle_\(brand\))'s [Quality of Life Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_quality_of_life_indices) 2018.[^cite_note-forbes-11] Munich is the wealthiest city in the EU by GDP per capita among cities with over one million inhabitants[^cite_note-12] and is among the most expensive German cities for real estate and rents.[^cite_note-biimmobilien-13][^cite_note-hausimmobilien-14] In 2023, 30.1% of residents were foreigners, and 19.4% were German citizens with a [migration background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_background) from abroad.[^cite_note-migr2023-15] Munich's economy is based on [high tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_tech), automobiles, the service sector, information technology, biotechnology, engineering, and electronics. Multinational companies such as [BMW](3772.md), [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens), [Allianz SE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allianz_SE), and [Munich Re](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Re) are headquartered there. The city has two research universities and many scientific institutions.[^cite_note-nature-16] Munich is known for its architecture, cultural venues, sports events, exhibitions, and the annual [Oktoberfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktoberfest), the world's largest *[Volksfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volksfest)*.[^cite_note-tourismus-17]

## History

Main article: [History of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Munich)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Munich).

### Etymology

Munich was a tiny 10th-century [monastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monasticism) settlement, which was named *zu den Munchen* ("to the monks"). The Old High German *Munche* served as the base for the modern German city name *München*.[^cite_note-18]

### Early history

The river [Isar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isar) was a prehistoric [trade route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route) and in the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) Munich was among the largest [raft ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raft) in Europe.[^cite_note-Bernan_Associates_UNESCO-19] Bronze Age settlements up to four millennia old have been discovered.[^cite_note-20] Evidence of [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celt) settlements from the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) have been discovered in areas around [Ramersdorf-Perlach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramersdorf-Perlach).[^cite_note-21]

The ancient Roman road Via Julia, which connected [Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg) and [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg), crossed over the Isar south of Munich, at the towns of [Baierbrunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baierbrunn) and [Gauting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauting).[^cite_note-22] A Roman settlement north-east of Munich was excavated in the neighborhood of [Denning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denning_\(Munich\)).[^cite_note-23]

Starting in the 6th century, the [Baiuvarii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiuvarii) populated the area around what is now modern Munich, such as in [Johanneskirchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johanneskirchen), [Feldmoching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldmoching), [Bogenhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogenhausen) and [Pasing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasing).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The first known Christian church was built ca. 815 in Fröttmanning.[^cite_note-26]

### Middle Ages



Munich in a 1493 woodcut from [Hartmann Schedel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartmann_Schedel)'s *[Nuremberg Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Chronicle)*

The first medieval bridges across the river Isar were located in current city areas of Munich and [Landshut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landshut).[^cite_note-Bernan_Associates_UNESCO-19] [Henry the Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_the_Lion), the Duke of Saxony and Bavaria, founded the town of Munich in his territory to control the [salt trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_trade), after having burned down the town of Föhring and its bridges over the Isar.[^cite_note-27] Historians date this event at about 1158.[^cite_note-freed-28]<sup>: 167 </sup>

Henry built a new toll bridge, customs house and a coin market closer to his home, somewhat upstream, at a settlement around the area of modern old town Munich. This new toll bridge most likely crossed the Isar where the Museuminsel and the modern Ludwigsbrücke are now located.[^cite_note-29]

[Otto of Freising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_of_Freising) protested to his nephew, [Emperor Frederick Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa). However, on 14 June 1158, in [Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg), the conflict was settled in favor of Duke Henry. The *[Augsburg Arbitration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg_Arbitration)* mentions the name of the location in dispute as *forum apud Munichen*. Although Bishop Otto had lost his bridge, the arbiters ordered Duke Henry to pay a third of his income to the Bishop in Freising as compensation.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

14 June 1158 is considered the official founding day of the city of Munich. Archaeological excavations at Marienhof Square (near [Marienplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marienplatz)) in advance of the expansion of the S-Bahn (subway) in 2012 discovered shards of vessels from the 11th century, which prove again that the settlement of Munich must be older than the Augsburg Arbitration of 1158.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] The old [St. Peter's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Church,_Munich) near Marienplatz is also believed to predate the founding date of the town.[^cite_note-35]

In 1175, Munich received city status and fortification. In 1180, after Henry the Lion's fall from grace with Emperor Frederick Barbarossa, including his trial and exile, [Otto I Wittelsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_I_of_Wittelsbach,_Duke_of_Bavaria) became Duke of Bavaria, and Munich was handed to the [Bishop of Freising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Munich_and_Freising). In 1240, Munich was transferred to [Otto II Wittelsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_II_Wittelsbach,_Duke_of_Bavaria) and in 1255, when the [Duchy of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Bavaria) was split in two, Munich became the ducal residence of [Upper Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Bavaria).

Duke [Louis IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), a native of Munich, was elected [German king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_king) in 1314 and crowned as [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor) in 1328.[^cite_note-muenchen-stadtgeschichte-36] He strengthened the city's position by granting it the salt monopoly with [staple rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stapelrecht), thus assuring it of additional income.[^cite_note-37]

The growth of Munich was aided by its location on a gravel plain, where the Isar branched into multiple streams that in turn provided drinking water, defensive advantages, and power for many mills and industries within the city.[^cite_note-38]

In the 15th century, Munich underwent a revival of [Gothic arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_art): the Old Town Hall was enlarged, and Munich's largest [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) church – the [Frauenkirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Frauenkirche) – now a cathedral, was constructed in only 20 years, starting in 1468.

### Capital of reunited Bavaria



The Renaissance *Antiquarium* of the [Munich Residenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Residenz)

When Bavaria was reunited in 1506 after the [War of the Succession of Landshut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Succession_of_Landshut) against the [Duchy of Bavaria-Landshut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Bavaria-Landshut), Munich became its capital.[^cite_note-muenchen-stadtgeschichte-36] During the 16th century Munich became a centre of the [Counter-Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Reformation).[^cite_note-muenchen-stadtgeschichte-36] The [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) movement beset Munich and the Bavarian branch of the [House of Wittelsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Wittelsbach) under Duke [Albrecht V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_V,_Duke_of_Bavaria) who bolstered their prestige by conjuring up a lineage that reached back to [classical antiquity](252905.md). In 1568 Albrecht V built the Antiquarium to house the [Wittelsbach collection of Greek and Roman antiquities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatliche_Antikensammlungen) in the [Munich Residenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residenz,_Munich).[^cite_note-39] Albrecht V appointed the composer [Orlando di Lasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_di_Lasso) as director of the court orchestra and tempted numerous Italian musicians to work at the Munich court, establishing Munich as a hub for late [Renaissance music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_music).[^cite_note-40] During the rule of [Duke William V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_V,_Duke_of_Bavaria) Munich began to be called the "German Rome" and William V began presenting Emperor [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) as ancestor of the Wittelsbach dynasty.[^cite_note-41]

Duke William V further cemented the Wittelsbach rule by commissioning the [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) [Michaelskirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Michael's_Church,_Munich). He had the sermons of his Jesuit court preacher [Jeremias Drexel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremias_Drexel) translated from Latin into German and published them to a greater audience.[^cite_note-42] William V was addressed with the epithet "the Pious" and like his contemporary Wittelsbach dukes promoted himself as "father of the land" (*Landesvater*), encouraged pilgrimages and [Marian devotions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneration_of_Mary_in_the_Catholic_Church).[^cite_note-43] William V had the [Hofbräuhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofbr%C3%A4uhaus) built in 1589.[^cite_note-muenchen-stadtgeschichte-36] It would become the prototype for [beer halls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_hall) across Munich.

The [Catholic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_League_\(German\)) was founded in Munich in 1609. In 1623, during the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War) (1618–1648), Munich became an electoral residence when Duke [Maximilian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_I,_Elector_of_Bavaria) was invested with the [electoral dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_dignity) of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), but in 1632 the city was occupied by King [Gustavus Adolphus of Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavus_Adolphus_of_Sweden).[^cite_note-44] In 1634, Swedish and Spanish troops advanced on Munich. Maximilian I published a plague ordinance to halt an epidemic escalation.[^cite_note-45] The [bubonic plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague) nevertheless ravaged Munich and the surrounding countryside in 1634 and 1635.[^cite_note-46] Troops again converged on Munich in 1647 and precautions were taken, so as to avoid another epidemic.[^cite_note-47] The city remained physically untouched during the war.[^cite_note-englund-8]



[Marienplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marienplatz), c. 1650

During its time as the capital of the [Electorate of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Bavaria), Munich was an important centre of [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) life, but also had to suffer under [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg) occupations in 1704 after Bavaria's defeat in the [War of the Spanish Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Spanish_Succession) and again in 1742 during the [War of the Austrian Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Austrian_Succession).[^cite_note-muenchen-stadtgeschichte-36] When Elector [Maximilian III Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_III_Joseph) died in 1777, the succession empowered the [Palatinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_palatine) branch within the House of Wittelsbach.[^cite_note-48] In 1785, Elector [Karl Theodor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Theodore,_Elector_of_Bavaria) invited Count Rumford ([Benjamin Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Thompson)) to Munich to implement social reforms, including [workhouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workhouses) for the poor and army restructuring with improved conditions for soldiers.[^cite_note-49] In the 1790s, Munich became the largest German city to remove its [fortifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications), starting in 1791 under Karl Theodor and Rumford.[^cite_note-50] After 1793, citizens built new structures outside the former city walls.[^cite_note-51]

After making an alliance with Napoleonic France, the city became the capital of the new [Kingdom of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria) in 1806 with Elector [Maximilian IV Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_I_Joseph_of_Bavaria) becoming its first king. The state parliament (the *[Landtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag)*) and the new [archdiocese of Munich and Freising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Munich_and_Freising) were also located in the city.[^cite_note-52]

The establishment of Bavarian state sovereignty made Munich the centre of a modernising kingdom. In 1802, King Maximilian Joseph [secularised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularised) Bavaria, dissolving [monasteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monasteries) and selling church lands to generate state revenue. He also took control of the right to brew beer, granting a monopoly to Munich's wealthiest brewers in exchange for substantial taxes. In 1807, he abolished restrictions on the number of brewery workers, allowing brewers to meet growing demand.[^cite_note-53]

In October 1810, a beer festival was held on the meadows outside Munich to celebrate the crown prince's wedding to Princess [Therese of Saxe-Hildburghausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Therese_of_Saxe-Hildburghausen). The event, featuring parades in regional dress, later evolved into the annual [Oktoberfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktoberfest), now held at [Theresienwiese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresienwiese).[^cite_note-54]



[Neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) buildings erected under [Ludwig I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_I), such as the [Propylaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propylaea_\(Munich\)), earned Munich the nickname 'Athens on the Isar'.

The first Munich railway station was built in 1839, with a line going to [Augsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg) in the west. By 1849 a newer Munich Central Train Station ([München Hauptbahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnchen_Hauptbahnhof)) was completed, with a line going to [Landshut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landshut) and [Regensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regensburg) in the north.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] In 1825 [Ludwig I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_I_of_Bavaria) had ascended to the throne and commissioned leading architects such as [Leo von Klenze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_von_Klenze) to design a series of public museums in [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) style. Between 1856 and 1861 the court gardener [Carl von Effner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_von_Effner) landscaped the banks of the river [Isar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isar) and established the *Maximilian Gardens*. Leo von Klenze supervised the construction of the [Propylaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propylaea_\(Munich\)) between 1854 and 1862.[^cite_note-57] In 1857 the construction of the [Maximilianeum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilianeum) was begun.[^cite_note-58] The grand building projects of Ludwig I gave Munich the endearment "Athens on the Isar" (German: *Isar-Athen*) and "Monaco di Bavaria".[^cite_note-59]

By the time [Ludwig II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_II_of_Bavaria) became king in 1864, he remained mostly aloof from his capital and focused more on his fanciful castles in the Bavarian countryside. In 1876 Munich hosted the first German Art and Industry Exhibition, which showcased the northern [Neo-Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Renaissance) fashion that came to be the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire)'s predominant style. Munich based artists put on the German National Applied Arts Exhibition in 1888, showcasing [Baroque Revival architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_Revival_architecture) and [Rococo Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rococo_Revival) designs.[^cite_note-60]



[Jugendstil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jugendstil) style house at Leopoldstr. 77. At the beginning of the 20th century, Munich was a hub for the Jugendstil movement.

The Prince Regent [Luitpold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luitpold,_Prince_Regent_of_Bavaria)'s reign from 1886 to 1912 was marked by tremendous artistic and cultural activity in Munich.[^cite_note-61] At the dawn of the 20th century Munich was an epicenter for the [Jugendstil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jugendstil) movement, combining a liberal magazine culture with progressive [industrial design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_design) and architecture. The German art movement took its name from the Munich magazine *Die Jugend* (*The Youth*).[^cite_note-62] Prominent Munich Jugendstil artists include [Hans Eduard von Berlepsch-Valendas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Eduard_von_Berlepsch-Valendas), [Otto Eckmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Eckmann),[^cite_note-63] [Margarethe von Brauchitsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margarethe_von_Brauchitsch?action=edit\&redlink=1), [August Endell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Endell), [Hermann Obrist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Obrist), [Wilhelm von Debschitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_von_Debschitz),[^cite_note-64] and [Richard Riemerschmid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Riemerschmid). In 1905 two large department stores opened in Munich, the Kaufhaus Oberpollinger and the [Warenhaus Hermann Tietz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Tietz), both having been designed by the architect [Max Littmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Littmann).[^cite_note-65] In 1911 the [expressionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expressionist) group [Der Blaue Reiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Blaue_Reiter) was established in Munich. Its founding members include [Gabriele Münter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriele_M%C3%BCnter).[^cite_note-66]

### World War I to World War II

Following the outbreak of World War I in 1914, life in Munich became very difficult, as the [Allied blockade of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_Germany_\(1914%E2%80%931919\)) led to food and fuel shortages. During a French air raid in 1916, three bombs fell on Munich.[^cite_note-67]

In 1916, the 'Bayerische Motoren Werke' ([BMW](3772.md)) produced its first [aircraft engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_engine) in Munich.[^cite_note-68] The public limited company BMW AG was founded in 1918, with [Camillo Castiglioni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camillo_Castiglioni) owning one third of the share capital. In 1922 BMW relocated its headquarters to a factory in Munich.[^cite_note-69]

After World War I, the city was at the centre of substantial political unrest. In November 1918, on the eve of the German revolution, [Ludwig III of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_III_of_Bavaria) and his family fled the city. After the murder of the first republican [premier of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ministers-President_of_Bavaria) [Kurt Eisner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Eisner) in February 1919 by [Anton Graf von Arco auf Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Graf_von_Arco_auf_Valley), the [Bavarian Soviet Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Soviet_Republic) was proclaimed.[^cite_note-70] The November 1918 revolution ended the reign of the Wittelsbach in Bavaria.[^cite_note-City_Halls_and_Civic_Materialism-71] In *[Mein Kampf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mein_Kampf)* [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md) described his political activism in Munich after November 1918 as the "Beginning of My Political Activity". Hitler called the short-lived Bavarian Soviet Republic "the rule of the Jews".[^cite_note-72]



Unrest during the 1923 [Beer Hall Putsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_Hall_Putsch), a failed [coup d'état](25914154.md) led by [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md), [Erich Ludendorff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_Ludendorff), and other *[Kampfbund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampfbund)* leaders

In 1923 [Gustav von Kahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_von_Kahr) was appointed Bavarian prime minister and immediately planned for the expulsion of all Jews who did not hold German citizenship. Chief of Police [Ernst Pöhner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_P%C3%B6hner) and [Wilhelm Frick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Frick) openly indulged in antisemitism, while Bavarian judges praised people on the political right as patriotic for their crimes and handed down mild sentences.[^cite_note-73] In 1923, Adolf Hitler and his supporters, who were concentrated in Munich, staged the [Beer Hall Putsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_Hall_Putsch), an attempt to overthrow the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic) and seize power. The revolt failed, resulting in Hitler's arrest and the temporary crippling of the [Nazi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party) (NSDAP).[^cite_note-74]

The unofficial city anthem of Munich, recorded in 1929

Munich was chosen as capital for the [Free State of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_State_of_Bavaria) and acquired increased responsibility for administering the city itself and the surrounding districts. Offices needed to be built for bureaucracy, so a 12-story office building was erected in the southern part of the historic city centre in the late 1920s.[^cite_note-City_Halls_and_Civic_Materialism-71]

Munich again became important to the Nazis when they took power in Germany in 1933. The party created its first [concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camp) at [Dachau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp), 16 km (10 mi) north-west of the city. Because of its importance to the rise of National Socialism, Munich was referred to as the *Hauptstadt der Bewegung* ("Capital of the Movement").[^cite_note-75]

The NSDAP headquarters and the documentation apparatus for controlling all aspects of life were located in Munich. Nazi organizations, such as the [National Socialist Women's League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Socialist_Women's_League) and the [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo), had their offices along [Brienner Straße](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brienner_Stra%C3%9Fe_\(Munich\)) and around the [Königsplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6nigsplatz,_Munich). The party acquired 68 buildings in the area and many *Führerbauten* ("*Führer* buildings") were built to reflect a new aesthetic of power.[^cite_note-76] Construction work for the [Führerbau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BChrerbau) and the party headquarters (known as the [Brown House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_House,_Munich)) started in September 1933.[^cite_note-77] The *[Haus der Kunst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haus_der_Kunst)* (House of German Art) was the first building to be commissioned by Hitler. The architect [Paul Troost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Troost) was asked to start work shortly after the Nazis had seized power because "the most German of all German cities" was left with no exhibition building when in 1931 the [Glass Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaspalast_\(Munich\)) was destroyed in an arson attack.[^cite_note-78]

The city was the site where the 1938 [Munich Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Agreement) was signed between [Nazi Germany](21212.md), the United Kingdom, [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_French_Republic) and [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy).[^cite_note-79] On 8 November 1939, shortly after the Second World War had begun, [Georg Elser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Elser) planted a bomb in the [Bürgerbräukeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%BCrgerbr%C3%A4ukeller) in Munich in an attempt to assassinate Adolf Hitler, who held a political party speech. Hitler, however, had left the building minutes before the bomb went off.[^cite_note-80]



View of Munich city centre in 1945 after the devastation of World War II, and in 1989 after reconstruction, featuring the restored [Old Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_Hall,_Munich), [Talburgtor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talburgtor), and [Heilig-Geist-Kirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heilig-Geist-Kirche,_Munich)

During the war, Munich was the location of multiple [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) camps, including two *Polenlager* camps for Polish youth,[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] and 40 subcamps of the [Dachau concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp), in which men and women of various nationalities were held.[^cite_note-83] With up to 17,000 prisoners in 1945, the largest subcamp of Dachau was the [Munich-Allach concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich-Allach_concentration_camp). By mid 1942 the majority of Jews living in Munich and the suburbs had been deported.[^cite_note-84]

Munich was the base of the [White Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Rose), a student [resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_movement). The core members were arrested and executed after [Sophie Scholl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie_Scholl) and her brother [Hans Scholl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Scholl) were caught distributing leaflets on the campus of the [Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig-Maximilians-Universit%C3%A4t_M%C3%BCnchen), calling upon the youth to rise against Hitler.[^cite_note-85]

Around 50% of city were heavily damaged by the [bombing of Munich in World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Munich_in_World_War_II), with 71 air raids over five years. After the war, large reconstruction projects restored most of the historically relevant buildings in the old town, that had suffered damage. US troops captured Munich on 30 April 1945.[^cite_note-86]

### Postwar

In the aftermath of World War II, Germany was subject to [US Military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Military) occupation.[^cite_note-87] Due to Polish annexation of the [former eastern territories of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_eastern_territories_of_Germany) and [expulsion of Germans from all over Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_and_expulsion_of_Germans_\(1944%E2%80%931950\)), Munich operated over a thousand refugee camps for 151,113 people in October 1946.[^cite_note-88] After US occupation Munich was completely rebuilt following a meticulous plan, which preserved its pre-war street grid, bar a few exceptions owing to then-modern traffic concepts. In 1957, Munich's population surpassed one million. The city continued to play a highly significant role in the [West German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_German) economy, politics and culture, giving rise to its nickname *Heimliche Hauptstadt* ("secret capital") in the decades after World War II.[^cite_note-89]

The [Free State of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_State_of_Bavaria) used the [arms industry](608296.md) as kernel for its [high tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_tech) development policy.[^cite_note-Making_Competitive_Cities-90] Since 1963, Munich has been hosting the [Munich Security Conference](1464620.md), a conference on international [security policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_policy) held annually in the [Hotel Bayerischer Hof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Bayerischer_Hof,_Munich).[^cite_note-91] Munich also became known on the political level due to the strong influence of Bavarian politician [Franz Josef Strauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Josef_Strauss) from the 1960s to the 1980s. The [Munich Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport), which commenced operations in 1992, was named in his honor.[^cite_note-92]



[Olympiapark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiapark) was the main venue for the [1972 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Olympic_Games) and also hosted the [1974 FIFA World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup_final).

Munich hosted the [1972 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Summer_Olympics). After winning the bid in 1966 the [Mayor of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Munich), [Hans-Jochen Vogel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Jochen_Vogel), accelerated the construction of the [U-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_U-Bahn) subway and the [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_S-Bahn) metropolitan commuter railway. In May 1967 the construction work began for a new U-Bahn line connecting the city with the [Olympic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiapark_\(Munich\)) (*Olympiapark*). The Olympic Park subway station was built near the [BMW Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_Headquarters) and the line was completed in May 1972, three months before the opening of the 1972 Summer Olympics. Shortly before the opening ceremony, Munich also inaugurated a sizable pedestrian priority zone between [Karlsplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsplatz_\(Stachus\)) and [Marienplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marienplatz).[^cite_note-93] In 1970 the Munich city council released funds so that the [gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) facade and [Glockenspiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rathaus-Glockenspiel) of the [New City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neues_Rathaus_\(Munich\)) (*Neues Rathaus*) could be restored.[^cite_note-94] During the 1972 Summer Olympics 11 Israeli athletes were murdered by Palestinian terrorists in the [Munich massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_massacre), when gunmen from the Palestinian "[Black September](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September_\(group\))" group took hostage members of the Israeli Olympic team.[^cite_note-95] In 1974, the [FIFA World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup_final) was held at the [Olympic Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Stadium,_Munich).

Munich and its [urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl) emerged as the leading German high tech region during the 1980s and 1990s. The urban economy of Munich became characterized by a dynamic labour market, low unemployment, a growing [service economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_economy) and high per capita income.[^cite_note-Making_Competitive_Cities-90]

Munich was one of the host cities for the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) and both the [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020) – postponed by a year because of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) – and [UEFA Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024).[^cite_note-96]

## Geography

### Topography

Munich lies on the elevated plains of [Upper Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Bavaria) in the Northern [Alpine Foreland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foothills), about 50 km north of the [Alps](981.md) and at about 520 m [above mean sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level). The rivers [Isar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isar) and [Würm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrm_\(Amper\)) flow through the city.



Aerial view of Munich city centre (facing southeast)

The northern part of this sandy plateau includes a highly fertile [flint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flint) area no longer affected by Alpine tectonic processes, while the southern part is covered with [morainic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moraine) hills. Between them are [fluvio-glacial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluvio-glacial) outwash plains around Munich. Where these deposits become thinner, [groundwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater) can rise through the gravel surface, causing marsh formation in northern Munich.

### Climate



View of Munich with the [Alps](981.md) in the background

According to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), the climate is [oceanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) (*Cfb*) bordering on [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (*Dfb*), with features like warm to hot summers and cold winters, but without permanent snow cover.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] The city centre lies between both climates, while the [airport of Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport) has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate). The warmest month, on average, is July. The coolest is January.

The proximity to the [Alps](981.md) brings higher volumes of rainfall and consequently greater susceptibility to [flood problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_flooding). Studies of [adaptation to climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_adaptation) and extreme events are carried out; one of them is the Isar Plan of the [EU](9317.md) Adaptation Climate.[^cite_note-99]

Showers and thunderstorms bring the highest average monthly precipitation in late spring and throughout the summer. The most precipitation occurs in July, on average. Winter tends to have less precipitation, the least in February.

The higher elevation and proximity to the Alps cause the city to have more rain and snow than many other parts of Germany. The Alps affect the city's climate in other ways too; for example, the warm downhill wind from the Alps ([föhn wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%B6hn_wind)), which can raise temperatures sharply within a few hours even in the winter.

Being at the centre of Europe, Munich is subject to many climatic influences, so that weather conditions there are more variable than in other European cities, especially those further west and south of the Alps.

Munich is near the Alps. Annual variation in temperature can be significant, because there are no large bodies of water nearby. The winter in Munich is generally cold and overcast, and some Munich winters have significant snow. January is the coldest month. While winter averages remain only moderately cold, and relatively mild for an elevated inland location of Munich's latitude, [inversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inversion_\(meteorology\)) from the nearby Alps causes cold air to sink and result in temperatures below −15 °C (5 °F). In Munich the summer is usually pleasantly warm, with daytime temperatures averaging 25 °C (77 °F).

Munich is subject to active [convective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convective) seasons and sometimes damaging events. The Alpine [thunderstorm system](70807.md) moves along the mountain range, or detaches, heading east-north-east over the foothills of the Alps.[^cite_note-100]

At Munich's official [weather stations](206018.md), the highest and lowest temperatures ever measured are 37.6 °C (100 °F), on 27 June 2026 in Munich City, and −31.6 °C (−24.9 °F), on 12 February 1929 in the Botanic Garden of the city.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

#### Climate change

In Munich, the general trend of [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) with a rise of medium yearly temperatures of about 1 °C (1.8 °F) in Germany between 1900 and 2020 can be observed as well. In November 2016 the city council concluded officially that a further rise in medium temperature, a higher number of heat extremes, a rise in the number of hot days and nights with temperatures higher than 20 °C ([tropical nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_night)), a change in [precipitation patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation), as well as a rise in the number of local instances of heavy rain, is to be expected as part of the ongoing climate change. The city administration decided to support a joint study from its own Referat für Gesundheit und Umwelt (department for health and environmental issues) and the [German Meteorological Service](3372074.md) that will gather data on local weather. The data is supposed to be used to create a plan for action for adapting the city to better deal with climate change as well as an integrated action program for climate protection in Munich. With the help of those programs issues regarding [spatial planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spatial_planning) and settlement density, the development of buildings and green spaces as well as plans for functioning [ventilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventilation_\(architecture\)) in a cityscape can be monitored and managed.[^cite_note-107]

From only 24,000 inhabitants in 1700, the city population doubled about every 30 years. It was 100,000 in 1852, 250,000 in 1883 and 500,000 in 1901. Since then, Munich has become Germany's third-largest city. In 1933, 840,901 inhabitants were counted, and in 1957 over 1 million. Munich has reached 1.5 million in 2022.

### Immigration

In December 2023, Munich had 1.58 million inhabitants; 477,855 foreign nationals resided in the city as of 31 December 2023 with 42.88% of these residents being citizens of EU member states, and 29.66% citizens in European states not in the EU (including Kosovo and Turkey).[^cite_note-108] Along with the Turks, the Croats are one of the two largest foreign minorities in the city, which is why some Croats refer to Munich as their "second capital".[^cite_note-109] The largest groups of foreign nationals were [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) (38,947), [Croats](69801.md) (37,541), [Italians](385155.md) (28,142), [Greeks](42056.md) (24,843), [Bosnians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnians) (24,161), [Ukrainians](145144.md) (21,899), and [Austrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrians) (21,944).

### Religion

About 45% of Munich's residents are not affiliated with any religious group; this ratio represents the fastest growing segment of the population. As in the rest of Germany, the Catholic and Protestant churches have experienced a continuous decline in membership. As of 31 December 2017, 31.8% of the city's inhabitants were Catholic, 11.4% Protestant, 0.3% Jewish (see: [History of the Jews in Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Munich)),[^cite_note-110] and 3.6% were members of an Orthodox Church ([Eastern Orthodox](10186.md) or [Oriental Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy)).[^cite_note-111] About 1% adhere to other Christian denominations. There is also a small [Old Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Catholic) parish and an English-speaking parish of the [Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_\(United_States\)) in the city. According to Munich Statistical Office, in 2013 about 6.9% of Munich's population was Muslim.[^cite_note-112] Munich has the largest [Uyghur](31783.md) population with about 800 (whole Germany about 1,600) people with Uyghur diaspora. Many of them fled to Munich due to the Chinese government and are exiled in Munich. Munich is also home to [World Uyghur Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Uyghur_Congress), which is an [international organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_organisation) of exiled Uyghurs.[^cite_note-113]

## Government and politics



The [Bavarian State Chancellery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_State_Chancellery) serves as the executive office of the [Minister-President of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister-President_of_Bavaria).

As the capital of Bavaria, Munich is an important political centre for both the state and country as a whole. It is the seat of the [Landtag of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag_of_Bavaria), the [State Chancellery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayerische_Staatskanzlei), and all state departments. Several national and international authorities are located in Munich, including the [Federal Finance Court of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Finance_Court_of_Germany), the [German Patent Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Patent_Office) and the [European Patent Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Patent_Office). Bavaria has been dominated by the [Christian Social Union in Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Social_Union_in_Bavaria) (CSU) on a federal, state, and local level since the establishment of the Federal Republic in 1949.

### Old Town

At the centre of the old town is the [Marienplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marienplatz) with the [Old Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_Hall,_Munich) and the [New Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Town_Hall,_Munich). Its tower contains the [Rathaus-Glockenspiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rathaus-Glockenspiel). The [Peterskirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peter's_Church,_Munich) is the oldest church of the inner city. Nearby St. Peter, the Gothic hall-church [Heiliggeistkirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiliggeistkirche_\(Munich\)) was converted to baroque style from 1724 onwards and looks down upon the [Viktualienmarkt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktualienmarkt). Three gates of the demolished medieval fortification survive; these are the [Isartor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isartor), the [Sendlinger Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sendlinger_Tor), and the [Karlstor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlstor). The Karlstor leads up to the [Stachus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlsplatz_\(Stachus\)), a square dominated by the [Justizpalast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justizpalast_\(Munich\)) (Palace of Justice).

The [Frauenkirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Frauenkirche) serves as the cathedral for the [Catholic Archdiocese of Munich and Freising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Munich_and_Freising). The nearby [Michaelskirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Michael's_Church,_Munich) is the largest [renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance) church north of the Alps, while the [Theatinerkirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatine_Church,_Munich) is a [basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica) in Italianate high baroque, which had a major influence on southern German [baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) architecture. Its dome dominates the [Odeonsplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeonsplatz).

### Palaces and castles

Schloss Nymphenburg ([Nymphenburg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nymphenburg_Palace), construction started 1664) is a museum open to the public for tours.[^cite_note-Zen_2020_i476-119][^cite_note-Lonely_Planet_2010_b951-120]

The smaller Schloss Fürstenried ([Fürstenried Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BCrstenried_Palace), construction 1715–1717) is used by the [Archdiocese of Munich and Freising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Munich_and_Freising) as a conference location.[^cite_note-muenchen.de_2022_v258-121]

Schloss Blutenburg ([Blutenburg Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blutenburg_Castle)) opened as a children's library in 2024,[^cite_note-Hordych_2024_l233-122] but visitors may tour the late-Gothic Blutenburg Castle Church built on the same grounds.[^cite_note-muenchen.de_2022_i705-123]

The large [Munich Residenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Residenz) complex on the edge of Munich's Old Town now ranks among Europe's most significant museums of interior decoration. Within the Residenz is the splendid [Cuvilliés Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuvilli%C3%A9s_Theatre) and next door is the [National Theatre Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_Munich). Among the mansions that still exist in Munich are the [Palais Porcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_Porcia), the [Palais Preysing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_Preysing), the [Palais Holnstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_Holnstein) and the [Prinz-Carl-Palais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prinz-Carl-Palais). All mansions are situated close to the Residenz, so is the [Alter Hof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alter_Hof), the first residence of the [House of Wittelsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Wittelsbach).

### Modernist architecture

Despite Munich being the breeding ground for German [Jugendstil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jugendstil), starting with the architect [Martin Dülfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_D%C3%BClfer), Munich Jugendstil style was quickly submerged as historic trash. While the modernist architect [Theodor Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Fischer) was based in Munich, his influence on Munich underwhelmed. Prior to 1914 the city of Munich was under-industrialized. During the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic), the Munich establishment was hostile to [modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism). The TUM professor [German Bestelmeyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Bestelmeyer) favored a conservative style, and [Jacobus Oud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobus_Oud) was rejected for the post of city building chief. Modernist exceptions include a series of post offices by [Robert Vorhoelzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Vorhoelzer) built in the late 1920s and early 1930s. Examples of [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde) temporary constructions include the *Wohnmaschine* (*Housing Machine*) by [Robert Vorhoelzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Vorhoelzer), as well as the *Flachdachhaus* (*Flat Roof House*) by [Fritz Norkauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Norkauer?action=edit\&redlink=1). [Paul Schultze-Naumburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Schultze-Naumburg), and the [Kampfbund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kampfbund) enjoyed particular popularity.[^cite_note-124]

### High rise buildings

Several high-rise buildings are clustered at the northern edge of Munich in the skyline, like the [HVB Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypo-Haus), the [Arabella High-Rise Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabella_High-Rise_Building), the [Highlight Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highlight_Towers), [Uptown Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uptown_Munich), Münchner Tor and the [BMW Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_Headquarters) next to the [Olympic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiapark_\(Munich\)). Further high-rise buildings are located in the [Werksviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werksviertel?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Berg am Laim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berg_am_Laim).

### Long-term residential development

Munich is subject to a long-term residential development plan that is established by the city administration of Munich. The LaSie ("Langfristige Siedlungsentwicklung") was passed in 2011 in response to the acute housing crisis. LaSie is aligned with the strategic development plan passed for Munich in 1998 ("Perspektive München"). LaSie defines three priorities for the construction of residential housing in Munich. Existing [housing estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_estate), post-war low-density developments, and the suburban area are subject to [densification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Densification) ("Nachverdichtung"). Non-residential industrial areas are subject to conservation and will be turned into residential and mixed-use areas. On greenfield sites in the Munich periphery medium and large-scale housing estates are to be built so as to extend Munich's urban center.[^cite_note-125]

## Parks



*[Englischer Garten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Englischer_Garten)* with Munich skyline

[Friedrich Ludwig von Sckell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Ludwig_von_Sckell) became famous for designing the [Englischer Garten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Englischer_Garten) between 1789 and 1807. Besides planning the first public garden in Europe, Sckell also redesigned Baroque gardens as landscape gardens, including the parks of [Nymphenburg Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nymphenburg_Palace) and the [Botanischer Garten München-Nymphenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanischer_Garten_M%C3%BCnchen-Nymphenburg).[^cite_note-126]

Other large green spaces are the [Olympiapark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiapark,_Munich), the [Westpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westpark_\(Munich\)) and the [Ostpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostpark_\(Munich\)). The city's oldest park is the [Hofgarten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofgarten_\(Munich\)), near the Residenz, dating back to the 16th century. The site of the largest beer garden in town, the former royal Hirschgarten, was founded in 1780.[^cite_note-127]

## Culture

### Language

Main article: [Bavarian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_language)

German is spoken and understood in and around Munich. While the German language has many dialects, so-called "[Standard German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_German)" or "High German" is learned in schools and spoken among Germans, Austrians and in some parts of Switzerland. A speaker of a [Low German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_German) dialect in Hamburg may find it difficult to understand the dialect of a Bavarian mountaineer.[^cite_note-128] The [Bavarian dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_language) are recognized as regional languages and continue to be spoken alongside Standard German.[^cite_note-129]

### Museums



The [Deutsches Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Museum) is the world's largest museum of [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_museum) and [technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_museum).

#### Technical Museums and Natural History Museums

In 1903 [Oskar von Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_von_Miller) assembled a group of engineers and industrialists, who chartered the [Deutsches Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Museum). The Museum was built with the financial support of the German business and imperial nobility community, as well as the blessing of [Wilhelm II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_II), German Emperor.[^cite_note-130] The Deutsches Museum had its grand opening in 1925; it is the world's largest museum of [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_museum) and [technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_museum).[^cite_note-131] The Deutsches Museum now operates three locations, with exhibition space at [Flugwerft Schleissheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Museum_Flugwerft_Schleissheim) and a transportation museum near Theresienwiese. The original site in central Munich continues to expand its exhibits.[^cite_note-132] Further transportation and technical museums include the [MVG Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MVG_Museum) and the [BMW Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_Museum).

There are several natural history museums in Munich. Some are located in the area known as the "Apfelstrudel District". These include the [Museum of Man and Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Man_and_Nature), the [Palaeontological Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeontological_Museum,_Munich), the [Museum Reich der Kristalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Reich_der_Kristalle), the [Bavarian State Archaeological Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_State_Archaeological_Collection), the [Bavarian State Collection of Zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_State_Collection_of_Zoology), the [Geologisches Museum München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologisches_Museum_M%C3%BCnchen), the [Botanische Staatsammlung München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanische_Staatssammlung_M%C3%BCnchen), and the [German Hunting and Fishing Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Hunting_and_Fishing_Museum).

#### Art Museums and Galleries



[Bavarian National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_National_Museum)

City guides published in the early 1860s directed tourists to Munich's architecture and art collections, which at the time were unique in Germany and are a legacy mainly of [Ludwig I of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_I_of_Bavaria), with contributions from [Maximilian II of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_II_of_Bavaria).[^cite_note-133] After the first German art exhibition in the [Glaspalast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaspalast_\(Munich\)) for an international audience in 1869, Munich emerged as a focal point for the arts. Men of distinction from around the world visited the [Academy of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Fine_Arts,_Munich) under the directorship of [Karl von Piloty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_von_Piloty) and later [Wilhelm von Kaulbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_von_Kaulbach).[^cite_note-134]

The city has several important art museums and galleries, many of which can be found in the [Kunstareal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunstareal). This neighborhood includes several of the galleries of the [Bavarian State Painting Collections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_State_Painting_Collections), most notably the three "Pinakotheken" ([Alte Pinakothek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alte_Pinakothek), [Neue Pinakothek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neue_Pinakothek), and [Pinakothek der Moderne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinakothek_der_Moderne)). The [Alte Pinakothek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alte_Pinakothek) contains works of European masters between the 14th and 18th centuries. Major displays include [Albrecht Dürer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer)'s [*Self-Portrait* (1500)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-Portrait_\(D%C3%BCrer,_Munich\)), his *[Four Apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Four_Apostles)*, [Raphael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael)'s paintings *[The Canigiani Holy Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canigiani_Holy_Family_\(Raphael\))* and *[Madonna Tempi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempi_Madonna_\(Raphael\))* as well as [Peter Paul Rubens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Paul_Rubens) large *Judgment Day*. The [Neue Pinakothek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neue_Pinakothek), like other German museums, was wholly reconstructed from 1974 until 1981.[^cite_note-135] The [Pinakothek der Moderne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinakothek_der_Moderne) exhibits modern art, architecture, design, and works on paper. The principal focus of its permanent collection is Classical Modernism, but the displays are enhanced continuously with gifts from private collections.[^cite_note-136] Also included in the Bavarian State Painting Collections are the [Museum Brandhorst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Brandhorst), a modern and contemporary art museum in the Kunstareal, and the [Schackgalerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schackgalerie), an important gallery of German 19th-century paintings.[^cite_note-137]



The [Glyptothek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyptothek) houses King [Ludwig I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_I)'s collection of Greek and Roman sculptures.

Several further museums are located in the Kunstareal neighborhood. The [Lenbachhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenbachhaus) displays works of the movement [Der Blaue Reiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Blaue_Reiter) (The Blue Rider), a Munich-based modernist art.[^cite_note-138] Another museum is the [Staatliche Sammlung für Ägyptische Kunst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatliche_Sammlung_f%C3%BCr_%C3%84gyptische_Kunst) (the State Collection of Egyptian Art).[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141] An extensive collection of Greek and Roman art is held in the [Glyptothek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyptothek)[^cite_note-142] and the [Staatliche Antikensammlungen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatliche_Antikensammlungen) (the State Antiquities Collections). Works on display include the [Medusa Rondanini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medusa_Rondanini), the [Barberini Faun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barberini_Faun) and figures from the [Temple of Aphaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Aphaea) on [Aegina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegina) for the Glyptothek.[^cite_note-143] The [Staatliche Graphische Sammlung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatliche_Graphische_Sammlung_M%C3%BCnchen), the [Zentralinstitut für Kunstgeschichte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zentralinstitut_f%C3%BCr_Kunstgeschichte), and the [Museum für Abgüsse Klassischer Bildwerke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_f%C3%BCr_Abg%C3%BCsse_Klassischer_Bildwerke) are also housed in the building of the former [Munich Central Collecting Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Central_Collecting_Point) at [Königsplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6nigsplatz,_Munich).

Elsewhere in the city are many other notable public art collections, such as the [Haus der Kunst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haus_der_Kunst), the [Bavarian National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_National_Museum), and the [Villa Stuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Stuck). There are also several private collections and art exhibition spaces, such as the [Goetz Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goetz_Collection), the [Museum of Urban and Contemporary Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Urban_and_Contemporary_Art), the [Kunsthalle der Hypo-Kulturstiftung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunsthalle_der_Hypo-Kulturstiftung?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Lothringer13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothringer13?action=edit\&redlink=1).

#### Cultural Museums and Memorial Sites

The [Munich City Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Stadtmuseum) is located the old gothic arsenal building in the inner city and contains a variety of cultural collections and museums, such as the [Munich Film Archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Film_Archive). The main museum building is closed for renovations and is scheduled to reopen in summer 2031.[^cite_note-144] Across the Sankt-Jakobs-Platz from the city museum is the [Jewish Museum Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Museum_Munich), located next to the [Ohel Jakob Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohel_Jakob_synagogue_\(Munich\)).

The [Museum Five Continents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Five_Continents) is the second largest collection in Germany of artefacts and objects from outside Europe, while the [Bavarian National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_National_Museum) and the adjoining [Bavarian State Archaeological Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_State_Archaeological_Collection) display regional art and cultural history. Throughout the city are several smaller museums focused on various historical and cultural aspects, such as the [Sudeten German Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudeten_German_Museum), the [Beer and Oktoberfest Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_and_Oktoberfest_Museum), the [Deutsches Brauereimuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Brauereimuseum), the [Deutsches Theatermuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Theatermuseum), the [Valentin-Karlstadt-Musäum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentin-Karlstadt-Mus%C3%A4um?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the [Alpine Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_Museum).

Several former residences of the [Wittelsbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Wittelsbach) monarchs of Bavaria located in the city can be visited. The [Residenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Residenz), the former royal palace, houses a museum, treasury, the [Cuvilliés Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuvilli%C3%A9s_Theatre), and the Bavarian state coin collection. The royal family's former summer residence, the Nymphenburg Palace, also includes space for the [Marstallmuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marstallmuseum) and the [Nymphenburg Porcelain Manufactory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nymphenburg_Porcelain_Manufactory). Just outside the city limits is an additional summer place complex, [Schleissheim Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schleissheim_Palace).

Several museums and memorials attest to the city's complicated history under National Socialism. In May 2015, the [Munich Documentation Centre for the History of National Socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Documentation_Centre_for_the_History_of_National_Socialism) opened to the public, and the memorial museum of the former [Dachau concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dachau_concentration_camp) is just outside the city. The [Erinnerungsort Olympia-Attentat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erinnerungsort_Olympia-Attentat) further commemorates the victims of the [Munich massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_massacre) during the [1972 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Summer_Olympics).

### Music

Munich is a major international musical centre and has played host to many prominent composers including [Orlande de Lassus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlande_de_Lassus), [Orlando di Lasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_di_Lasso), [Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart), [Carl Maria von Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Maria_von_Weber), [Richard Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Wagner), [Gustav Mahler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Mahler), [Richard Strauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Strauss), [Max Reger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Reger) and [Carl Orff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Orff).[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146] Some notable classical music compositions created in and around Munich include Richard Strauss's tone poem *[Also sprach Zarathustra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Also_sprach_Zarathustra_\(Strauss\))*[^cite_note-147] or Carl Orff's *[Carmina Burana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmina_Burana_\(Orff\))*.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149]

#### Opera and Orchestral Music



The [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_Munich) is home to the [Bavarian State Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_State_Opera) and the [Bavarian State Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_State_Orchestra).

While [Richard Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Wagner) was also a supporter of [William I, German Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_I,_German_Emperor), he was generously supported by [Ludwig II of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_II_of_Bavaria).[^cite_note-150] Ludwig II sponsored the successful Munich premiere of Wagner's *[Die Meistersinger von Nürnberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Meistersinger_von_N%C3%BCrnberg)* (*The Mastersingers of Nuremberg*) at the Hoftheater, now the [National Theatre Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_Munich).[^cite_note-151]



[Staatstheater am Gärtnerplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatstheater_am_G%C3%A4rtnerplatz)

The National Theatre Munich is now home to the [Bavarian State Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_State_Opera) and the [Bavarian State Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_State_Orchestra). Next door, the modern [Residenz Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residenz_Theatre) was erected in the [former royal palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Residenz) that also houses the [Cuvilliés Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuvilli%C3%A9s_Theatre). The [Staatstheater am Gärtnerplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatstheater_am_G%C3%A4rtnerplatz) is a state theater while another opera house, the [Prinzregententheater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prinzregententheater), has become the home of the Bavarian Theater Academy and the [Munich Chamber Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Chamber_Orchestra).

The modern [Gasteig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gasteig) centre houses the [Munich Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Philharmonic). The third orchestra in Munich with international importance is the [Bavarian Radio Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_Radio_Symphony_Orchestra). Its primary concert venue is the Herkulessaal in the former city royal residence, the [Munich Residenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Residenz). Many important conductors have been attracted by the city's orchestras, including [Felix Weingartner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Weingartner), [Hans Pfitzner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Pfitzner), [Hans Rosbaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Rosbaud), [Hans Knappertsbusch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Knappertsbusch), [Sergiu Celibidache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergiu_Celibidache), [James Levine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Levine), [Christian Thielemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Thielemann), [Lorin Maazel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorin_Maazel), [Rafael Kubelík](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Kubel%C3%ADk), [Eugen Jochum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugen_Jochum), Sir [Colin Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Davis), [Mariss Jansons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariss_Jansons), [Bruno Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Walter), [Georg Solti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Solti), [Zubin Mehta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zubin_Mehta) and [Kent Nagano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_Nagano). A stage for shows, big events and musicals is the [Deutsche Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsches_Theater_M%C3%BCnchen). It is Germany's largest theatre for guest performances.[^cite_note-152]

#### Pop and electronica

In the late-1960s Munich was the centre of [Krautrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krautrock) in southern Germany, with bands such as [Amon Düül II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amon_D%C3%BC%C3%BCl_II), [Embryo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryo_\(band\)) or [Popol Vuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popol_Vuh_\(band\)) hailing from the city.

In the 1970s, the [Musicland Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musicland_Studios) in Munich became a world-renowned recording studio, with bands such as the [Rolling Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stones), [Led Zeppelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Led_Zeppelin), [Deep Purple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Purple) and [Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_\(band\)) recording albums there.[^cite_note-mjunikdisco-153] Musicland Studios also played a role in the development of electronic music through the work of genre pioneer [Giorgio Moroder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Moroder), who invented [synth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synth) [disco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco) and [electronic dance music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_dance_music), and the recordings of disco musician [Donna Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Summer).[^cite_note-mjunikdisco-153]

Several bands associated with the [Neue Deutsche Welle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neue_Deutsche_Welle) of the late 1970s and 1980s were founded in Munich, including [Spider Murphy Gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider_Murphy_Gang), [Münchener Freiheit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnchener_Freiheit_\(band\)), and [FSK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FSK_\(band\)).

In the late 1990s, [DJ Hell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Hell) brought together international pioneers of the developing musical genre of [Electroclash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroclash) through his [International DeeJay Gigolo Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_DeeJay_Gigolo_Records) label in Munich.[^cite_note-mjunikdisco-153]

Other notable musicians from Munich include the singer-songwriter [Konstantin Wecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Wecker), singer [Lou Bega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Bega), known for his cover of the song "[Mambo No. 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mambo_No._5#Lou_Bega_version)", as well as the contemporary rock bands [Colour Haze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colour_Haze) and [Sportfreunde Stiller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportfreunde_Stiller).

The biggest club in the city by capacity, [Neuraum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuraum), also regularly books [electronic dance music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_dance_music) acts.[^cite_note-sz2010-154]

### Theatre

The [Munich Kammerspiele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Kammerspiele) is one of the most important German-language theatres. Several notable playwrights have lived and worked in Munich, including [Christian Friedrich Hebbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Friedrich_Hebbel), [Henrik Ibsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Ibsen), [Bertolt Brecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertolt_Brecht), and [Frank Wedekind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wedekind).

Munich was also the site of a vibrant [cabaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabarett) performance scene during the Weimar era, epitomized by the short films and performances of [Karl Valentin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Valentin) and [Liesl Karlstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liesl_Karlstadt). Their work is commemorated at the [Valentin-Karlstadt-Musäum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentin-Karlstadt-Mus%C3%A4um?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Isartor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isartor).[^cite_note-155]

The [Circus Krone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circus_Krone), based in Munich, is one of the largest circuses in Europe.[^cite_note-156] It was the first and still is one of only a few in Western Europe to also occupy a [building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circus_Krone_Building) of its own.

### Art and Literature

Painters and sculptors based in Munich in the 15th and 16th century included [Erasmus Grasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Grasser) and [Jan Polack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Polack).

#### Baroque and Rococo

During the Baroque and Rococo eras, several sculptors, architects, and painters were based in Munich, including [Hans Krumpper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Krumpper), [Johann Baptist Zimmermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Baptist_Zimmermann), [Cosmas Damian Asam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmas_Damian_Asam), [Johann Michael Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Michael_Fischer), [Egid Quirin Asam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egid_Quirin_Asam), [François de Cuvilliés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_de_Cuvilli%C3%A9s), [Johann Baptist Straub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Baptist_Straub) and [Ignaz Günther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignaz_G%C3%BCnther).

#### Biedermeier

The [Biedermeier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biedermeier) era of the early 19th century was named after a character that regularly appeared in the satire magazine *[Fliegende Blätter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fliegende_Bl%C3%A4tter)*, which was published by [Adolf Kussmaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Kussmaul) and [Ludwig Eichrodt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Eichrodt) in Munich between 1855 and 1857. The Biedermeier era painters [Ferdinand Georg Waldmüller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Georg_Waldm%C3%BCller), [Moritz von Schwind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moritz_von_Schwind), and [Carl Spitzweg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Spitzweg) are shown in the [Neue Pinakothek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neue_Pinakothek).[^cite_note-157]

Further artists of the Biedermeier era based in Munich include sculptor [Ludwig von Schwanthaler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Michael_Schwanthaler) and the painters [Carl Rottmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Rottmann), [Wilhelm von Kaulbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_von_Kaulbach), [Karl Piloty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Piloty).

#### Prinzregentenzeit

&#x20;Celebrity literary figures worked in Munich especially during the final decades of the Kingdom of Bavaria, the so-called *Prinzregentenzeit* from 1886 to 191&#x32;*,* under the reign of [Luitpold, Prince Regent of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luitpold,_Prince_Regent_of_Bavaria). This includes the authors [Thomas Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Mann), [Heinrich Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Mann), [Paul Johann Ludwig von Heyse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Johann_Ludwig_von_Heyse), [Rainer Maria Rilke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainer_Maria_Rilke), [Ludwig Thoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Thoma), [Fanny zu Reventlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanny_zu_Reventlow), [Gustav Meyrink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Meyrink), and [Erich Mühsam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_M%C3%BChsam) as well as the playwrights [Oskar Panizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Panizza), [Frank Wedekind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Wedekind), and [Max Halbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Halbe). [Thomas Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Mann) wrote in his novella *Gladius Dei* about this period: "München leuchtete" (literally "Munich shone").&#x20;



Wassily Kandinsky's *Houses in Munich* (1908)

At the turn of the 20th century the neighbourhood of [Schwabing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwabing) became known as a bohemian cultural epicenter for both literature and the fine arts.[^cite_note-158] Central to Schwabing's bohemian scene were *Künstlerlokale* (*Artist's Cafés*) such as [Café Stefanie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caf%C3%A9_Stefanie) or [Simpl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simpl_\(Munich\)), whose liberal ways differed fundamentally from Munich's more traditional localities. The Simpl, which survives to this day, was named after Munich's anti-authoritarian satirical magazine *[Simplicissimus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplicissimus)*, founded in 1896 by [Albert Langen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Langen) and [Thomas Theodor Heine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Theodor_Heine). [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin) authored the pamphlet *[What Is to Be Done?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Is_to_Be_Done%3F)* while living in Schwabing.

Artists of the late-19th and early-20th century based in Munich include [Franz von Lenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_von_Lenbach), [Wilhelm Leibl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Leibl), [Lovis Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovis_Corinth), and [Franz Stuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Stuck).

#### Weimar Republic



Portrait of Oskar Maria Graf by [Georg Schrimpf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Schrimpf) (1927)

Munich remained a centre of cultural life during the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic), with writers such as [Lion Feuchtwanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Feuchtwanger), [Bertolt Brecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertolt_Brecht), [Ricarda Huch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricarda_Huch), [Oskar Maria Graf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oskar_Maria_Graf), [Annette Kolb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annette_Kolb), [Ernst Toller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Toller), [Hugo Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ball), and [Klaus Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Mann) living in the city.[^cite_note-159]

#### Post-war literature

After World War II, Munich soon again became a focal point of the German literary scene and remains so to this day. Writers such as [Wolfgang Koeppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Koeppen), [Erich Kästner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_K%C3%A4stner), [Eugen Roth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugen_Roth), [Alfred Andersch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Andersch), [Elfriede Jelinek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elfriede_Jelinek), [Hans Magnus Enzensberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Magnus_Enzensberger), [Michael Ende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Ende), [Franz Xaver Kroetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Xaver_Kroetz), [Gerhard Polt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Polt) and [Patrick Süskind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_S%C3%BCskind) have called the city their home.

### Cinema

Munich has been home to many of the directors active in the [New German Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_German_Cinema) movement, including [Rainer Werner Fassbinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainer_Werner_Fassbinder), [Werner Herzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Herzog), [Edgar Reitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Reitz) and [Herbert Achternbusch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Achternbusch). New German Cinema was a significant shift in post-war German filmmaking that rebuffed traditional funding structures and kitschy subject matter in favour of cinematic experimentation and political expression.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] In 1971, the [Filmverlag der Autoren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmverlag_der_Autoren) was founded in Munich as an independent co-operative that produced and distributed the work of several of these directors.[^cite_note-162] Munich also served as the setting for many of Fassbinder's films, including *[Ali: Fear Eats the Soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali:_Fear_Eats_the_Soul)* (1974). Fassbinder and his "clan" of actors were also regulars at local sites, such as the Hotel [Deutsche Eiche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Eiche?action=edit\&redlink=1) near Gärtnerplatz.

In 1919, the film producer and distributor [Bavaria Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria_Film) was founded in [Grünwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%BCnwald,_Bavaria) as Münchner Lichtspielkunst (Emelka), which developed into one of Europe's largest film studios. Among the internationally well-known films produced at the associated Bavaria Studios are *[The Pleasure Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pleasure_Garden_\(1925_film\))* (1925) by [Alfred Hitchcock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Hitchcock), *[The Great Escape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Escape_\(film\))* (1963) by [John Sturges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Sturges), *[Paths of Glory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paths_of_Glory)* (1957) by [Stanley Kubrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Kubrick), *[Willy Wonka & the Chocolate Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willy_Wonka_&_the_Chocolate_Factory)* (1971) by [Mel Stuart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Stuart) and both *[Das Boot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Boot)* (1981) and *[The Neverending Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Neverending_Story_\(film\))* (1984) by [Wolfgang Petersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Petersen).

Other significant companies based in the city include the motion picture film equipment manufacturer [Arri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arri) and the film production company [Constantin Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Film). The [University of Television and Film Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Television_and_Film_Munich) (HFF) is also a publicly funded film school based in the city and the alma mater of [Maren Ade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maren_Ade), [Wim Wenders](32902.md), [Caroline Link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Link), and [Hito Steyerl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hito_Steyerl).

Munich remains one of the centres of the German film and entertainment industry.[^cite_note-163] In addition to those above, directors such as [Billy Wilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Wilder), [Orson Welles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orson_Welles), [John Huston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Huston), [Ingmar Bergman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingmar_Bergman), [Claude Chabrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Chabrol), and [Fritz Umgelter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Umgelter) and have made films in Munich.

### Cuisine and culinary specialities



Weißwurst with sweet mustard and a pretzel

Munich cuisine is a part of the broader [Bavarian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_cuisine). Munich [Weißwurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei%C3%9Fwurst) ("white sausage", *German: Münchner Weißwurst*) was invented in the city in 1857 and is a Munich speciality. Traditionally Weißwurst is served in pubs before noon and is served with [sweet mustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_mustard) and freshly baked [pretzels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretzel).

Munich has 11 restaurants that have been awarded one or more [Michelin Guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelin_Guide) stars in 2021.[^cite_note-MichelinGuide-2021-164]

In 1971 [Eckart Witzigmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eckart_Witzigmann) teamed up with a Munich building contractor to finance and open the *Tantris* restaurant in Schwabing. Witzigmann is credited for starting the German *Küchenwunder* (*kitchen wonder*).[^cite_note-165]

#### Beers and breweries



Beer garden in Munich

Munich is known for its breweries and [Weissbier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weissbier) (*wheat beer*). [Helles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helles), a [pale lager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_lager) with a translucent gold color, is the most popular contemporary Munich beer. Helles has largely replaced Munich's dark beer, known as [Dunkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunkel), which gets its color from roasted malt. It was the typical beer in Munich in the 19th century. Starkbier is the strongest Munich beer, with a high alcohol content of 6%–9%. It is dark amber in color and has a heavy malty taste. The beer served at [Oktoberfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktoberfest) is a special type of beer with a higher alcohol content.

Wirtshäuser are traditional Bavarian pubs, many of which also have small outside areas. Biergärten (*[beer gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_garden)*) are a popular fixture in Munich's gastronomic landscape. They are central to the city's culture, and are an overt melting pot for members of all walks of life, regardless of social class. There are many smaller beer gardens, but some beer gardens have thousands of seats. Large beer gardens can be found in the [Englischer Garten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Englischer_Garten), on the Nockherberg, and in the Hirschgarten.

The six main breweries in Munich are [Augustiner-Bräu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustiner-Br%C3%A4u), [Hacker-Pschorr Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacker-Pschorr_Brewery), [Hofbräuhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofbr%C3%A4uhaus), [Löwenbräu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%B6wenbr%C3%A4u), [Paulaner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulaner), and [Spaten-Franziskaner-Bräu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaten-Franziskaner-Br%C3%A4u). Smaller breweries are becoming more prevalent in Munich.

### Festivals



The [Oktoberfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktoberfest) is the world's largest [beer festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_festival), attracting around seven million visitors every year.

#### Coopers' Dance



Schäfflertanz in [Neuhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuhausen-Nymphenburg), 2012

The [Coopers' Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coopers'_Dance) (German: *Schäfflertanz*) is a [guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_guild) dance of [coopers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_\(profession\)) originally started in Munich. Beginning in the early 1800s the custom spread via the travels of [journeymen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journeyman), and it is now a common tradition throughout the [Old Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Bavaria) region. The dance was supposed to be held every seven years.[^cite_note-myth-166]

#### Strong Beer Festival

Munich is home to the famous [Nockherberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nockherberg) Strong Beer Festival ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Starkbierfest*) during the Lenten fasting period (usually in March). Its origins go back to the 17th/18th century, but has become popular when the festivities were first televised in the 1980s. The festival includes comical speeches and a mini-musical in which numerous German politicians are parodied by look-alike actors.[^cite_note-167]

#### Frühlingsfest

Held for two weeks at the [Theresienwiese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresienwiese) from the end of April to the beginning of May, celebrating and serving the new local spring beers.[^cite_note-168]

#### Auer Dult

Main article: [Auer Dult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auer_Dult)

A regular event combining a [market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marketplace) and a German style [folk festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volksfest) on the [Mariahilfplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariahilfplatz). The Auer Dult can be up to 300 stalls, selling handmade crafts, [household goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_goods), and [local foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_food).[^cite_note-169]

#### Kocherlball

Munich's Kocherlball (*Cooks' Ball*) is an annual event to commemorate all servants, ranging from kitchenhands to cooks. The tradition started in the 19th century.[^cite_note-170]

#### Tollwood



[Tollwood Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tollwood_Festival)

Usually held annually in July and December at Olympia Park. The [Tollwood Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tollwood_Festival) showcases fine and performing arts with live music, and several lanes of booths selling handmade crafts, as well as [organic food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_food), mostly [fusion cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_cuisine).[^cite_note-171]

#### Oktoberfest

At [Theresienwiese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresienwiese), Munich's [Oktoberfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktoberfest), the largest [beer festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_festival) and *[Volksfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volksfest)* in the world, runs for 16–18 days from the end of September through early October. In the last 200 years the festival has grown to span 85 acres and now welcomes over six million visitors every year. Beer is served from the six major Munich [breweries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewery): [Augustiner-Bräu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustiner-Br%C3%A4u), [Hacker-Pschorr Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacker-Pschorr_Brewery), [Löwenbräu Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%B6wenbr%C3%A4u_Brewery), [Paulaner Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulaner_Brewery), [Spaten-Franziskaner-Bräu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaten-Franziskaner-Br%C3%A4u), and [Staatliches Hofbräuhaus in München](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatliches_Hofbr%C3%A4uhaus_in_M%C3%BCnchen). Food must be bought in each tent.[^cite_note-172]

#### Christkindlmarkt

The Munich [Christkindlmarkt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christkindlmarkt) started to evolve in the 14th century. The German Christkindlmarkt reached the desired accomplishment in the 17th century in [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg).[^cite_note-173]

### Nightlife

[Nightlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightlife) in Munich is located mostly in the boroughs [Ludwigsvorstadt-Isarvorstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwigsvorstadt-Isarvorstadt), [Maxvorstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxvorstadt), [Au-Haidhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Au-Haidhausen), [Berg am Laim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berg_am_Laim) and [Sendling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sendling). Between [Sendlinger Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sendlinger_Tor) and Maximiliansplatz, on the edge of the central [Altstadt-Lehel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altstadt-Lehel) district, there is also the so-called Feierbanane (party banana), a roughly banana-shaped unofficial party zone spanning 1.3 km (0.8 mi) along Sonnenstraße, characterized by a high concentration of clubs, bars and restaurants, which became the center of Munich's nightlife in the mid-2000s.[^cite_note-sueddeutsche_20130706-174]



[Bahnwärter Thiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahnw%C3%A4rter_Thiel_\(club\))

In the 1960s and 1970s, [Schwabing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwabing) was considered a center of nightlife in Germany, with internationally known clubs such as [Big Apple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Apple_\(club\)), PN hit-house, Domicile, Hot Club, Piper Club, Tiffany, Germany's first large-scale discotheque [Blow Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blow_Up_\(club\)) and the underwater nightclub [Yellow Submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Submarine_\(club\)),[^cite_note-mjunikdisco-153][^cite_note-ertl-175][^cite_note-mitvergnuegen_schauberger-176] and Munich has been called "New York's big disco sister" in this context.[^cite_note-mjunikdisco-153][^cite_note-abendzeitung_20081121-177] Bars in the Schwabing district of this era include, among many others, [Schwabinger 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwabinger_7) and Schwabinger Podium. Since the 1980s, however, Schwabing has lost much of its nightlife activity due to [gentrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentrification) and the resulting high rents, and the formerly wild artists' and students' quarter developed into one of the city's most coveted and expensive residential districts, attracting affluent citizens with little interest in partying.[^cite_note-mitvergnuegen_nisslmüller-178]

Beginning in the 1960s, the Rosa Viertel (pink quarter) developed in the [Glockenbachviertel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwigsvorstadt-Isarvorstadt#Glockenbachviertel) and around Gärtnerplatz, which in the 1980s made Munich "one of the four gayest metropolises in the world" along with San Francisco, New York City and Amsterdam.[^cite_note-stankiewitz-179] In particular, the area around [Müllerstraße](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCllerstra%C3%9Fe) and Hans-Sachs-Straße was characterized by numerous gay bars and nightclubs. One of them was the [travesty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travesti_\(theatre\)) nightclub Old Mrs. Henderson, where [Freddie Mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freddie_Mercury), who lived in Munich from 1979 to 1985, filmed the music video for the song "[Living on My Own](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_on_My_Own)" at his 39th birthday party.[^cite_note-stankiewitz-179][^cite_note-mitvergnuegen_schauberger-176][^cite_note-BR_20211004-180]

Munich hosted several *Love Parades* and *Mayday Party* [rave](43488.md) events throughout the 1990s. Munich continues to rave, the local youth scenes are active.[^cite_note-181]

Since the mid-1990s, the Kunstpark Ost and its successor Kultfabrik, a former industrial complex that was converted to a large party area near [München Ostbahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_East_station) in [Berg am Laim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berg_am_Laim), hosted more than 30 clubs and was especially popular among younger people from the metropolitan area surrounding Munich and tourists.[^cite_note-stankiewitz-179][^cite_note-munichfoundcom-182] The Kultfabrik was closed at the end of the year 2015 to convert the area into a residential and office area. Apart from the Kultfarbik and the smaller Optimolwerke, there is a wide variety of establishments in the urban parts of nearby [Haidhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haidhausen_\(Munich\)). Before the Kunstpark Ost, there had already been an accumulation of internationally known nightclubs in the remains of the abandoned former [Munich-Riem Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich-Riem_Airport#Reuse).[^cite_note-mjunikdisco-153][^cite_note-mitvergnuegen_022017-183][^cite_note-ard_072022-184]



Blitz Club on Museumsinsel

Munich nightlife tends to change dramatically and quickly. Establishments open and close every year, and due to gentrification and the overheated housing market many survive only a few years, while others last longer. Beyond the already mentioned venues of the 1960s and 1970s, nightclubs with international recognition in recent history included Tanzlokal Größenwahn, [The Atomic Café](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atomic_Caf%C3%A9_\(club\)) and the techno clubs [Babalu Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babalu_Club), [Ultraschall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraschall), [KW – Das Heizkraftwerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KW_%E2%80%93_Das_Heizkraftwerk), [Natraj Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natraj_Temple), [MMA Club (Mixed Munich Arts)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMA_Club), Die Registratur and Bob Beaman.[^cite_note-zeitmagazin-185] From 1995 to 2001, Munich was also home to the [Union Move](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Move), one of the largest [technoparades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technoparade) in Germany.[^cite_note-ertl-175]

Munich has the highest density of music venues of any German city, followed by Hamburg, Cologne and Berlin.[^cite_note-fazemag_20210920-186][^cite_note-clubstudy_2021-187] Within the city's limits are more than 100 nightclubs and thousands of bars and restaurants.[^cite_note-Munich_bars-188][^cite_note-Munich_nightclubs-189]

Popular techno nightclubs are [Blitz Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitz_Club), Harry Klein, Rote Sonne, [Bahnwärter Thiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahnw%C3%A4rter_Thiel_\(club\)), Pimpernel, Charlie, Palais and Pathos.[^cite_note-sueddeutsche_20161016-190][^cite_note-theclubmap-191] Popular mixed music clubs are Call me Drella, Wannda Circus, Tonhalle, Backstage, Muffathalle, Ampere, Pacha, P1, [Zenith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenith_\(building\)), Minna Thiel and the party ship [Alte Utting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alte_Utting).

## Sports

Main article: [Sport in Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Munich)

### Football

[Allianz Arena](1363716.md) is the second-largest stadium in Germany and the home stadium of [FC Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich).

### Olympics



Olympiasee in the [Olympiapark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympiapark)

Munich hosted the [1972 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Summer_Olympics); the [Munich massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_massacre) took place in the [Olympic village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Village,_Munich). It was one of the host cities for the [2006 Football World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), which was not held in Munich's [Olympic Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Stadium_\(Munich\)), but in a new [football specific stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soccer-specific_stadium), the [Allianz Arena](1363716.md). In October 2025, Munich citizens voted heavily (66%)[^cite_note-194] in favor of their city's bid to host the 2036, 2040, or 2044 Olympic Games. The pro-bid campaign[^cite_note-195] focused on ecological and sustainable solutions, social housing, and leveraging existing infrastructure.

## Economy



[BMW Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_Headquarters) building (one of the few buildings that has been built from the top to the bottom) and the bowl-shaped [BMW Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMW_Museum)



The [HypoVereinsbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HypoVereinsbank) tower

Munich has the strongest economy of any German city according to a study[^cite_note-Icm-muenchen.de-201] and the lowest unemployment rate (5.4% in July 2026[^cite_note-202]) of any German city of more than a million people (the others being [Berlin](3354.md), Hamburg and [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne)).[^cite_note-203][^cite_note-204] Munich [ranks third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_cities_by_GDP) on the list of German cities by gross domestic product (GDP). In addition, it is one of the most attractive business locations in Germany.[^cite_note-Icm-muenchen.de-201] The city is also the economic centre of [southern Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Germany). Munich topped the ranking of the magazine *Capital* in February 2005 for the economic prospects between 2002 and 2011 in 60 German cities.

Munich is a financial, business and commercial center and [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) that holds the headquarters of many companies. This includes more companies listed by the [DAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAX) than any other German city, as well as the German or European headquarters of many foreign companies such as [McDonald's](2480627.md) and [Microsoft](19001.md). One of the best-known newly established Munich companies is [Flixbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flixbus).

### Manufacturing

Munich holds the headquarters of [Siemens AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens) (electronics), [BMW](3772.md) (car), [Traton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traton) (truck manufacturer, engineering), [MTU Aero Engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTU_Aero_Engines) (aircraft engine manufacturer), [Linde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Linde_Group) (gases) and [Rohde & Schwarz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohde_&_Schwarz) ([electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics)). Among German cities with more than 500,000 inhabitants, purchasing power is highest in Munich (€26,648 per inhabitant) as of 2007.[^cite_note-205] In 2006, Munich blue-collar workers enjoyed an average hourly wage of €18.62 (ca. \$20).[^cite_note-206]

The breakdown by cities proper (not metropolitan areas) of Global 500 cities listed Munich in 8th position in 2009.[^cite_note-207] Munich is also a centre for [biotechnology](4502.md), software and other [service industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy). Furthermore, Munich is the home of the headquarters of many other large companies such as the [injection moulding machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injection_moulding_machine) manufacturer [Krauss-Maffei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krauss-Maffei), and its arms manufacturing branch [Krauss-Maffei & Wegmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krauss-Maffei_Wegmann), the camera and lighting manufacturer [Arri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arri), the semiconductor firm [Infineon Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infineon_Technologies) (headquartered in the suburban town of [Neubiberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neubiberg)), lighting giant [Osram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osram), as well as the German or European headquarters of many foreign companies such as [Microsoft](19001.md).

### Finance

Munich has significance as a [financial centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich's_financial_community) (second only to [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main)), being home of [HypoVereinsbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HypoVereinsbank) and the [Bayerische Landesbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayerische_Landesbank). It outranks [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main) though as home of insurance companies such as [Allianz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allianz) (insurance) and [Munich Re](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Re) ([re-insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinsurance)).[^cite_note-208]

### Media

Munich is the largest publishing city in Europe[^cite_note-209] and home to the *[Süddeutsche Zeitung](30873120.md)*, one of Germany's biggest daily newspapers. The city is also the location of the programming headquarters of Germany's largest public broadcasting network, [ARD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARD_\(broadcaster\)), while the largest commercial network, [Pro7-Sat1 Media AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ProSieben), is headquartered in the suburb of [Unterföhring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unterf%C3%B6hring). The headquarters of the German branch of [Random House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_House), the world's largest publishing house, and of [Burda publishing group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert_Burda_Media) are also in Munich.

The [Bavaria Film Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavaria_Film_Studios) are located in the suburb of [Grünwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%BCnwald,_Bavaria). They are one of Europe's biggest film production studios.[^cite_note-210]

## Transport



ZOB Muenchen (long-distance bus terminal)

Munich has an extensive public transport system consisting of an underground metro, trams, buses and high-speed rail. In 2015, the transport [modal share](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_share) in Munich was 38 percent public transport, 25 percent car, 23 percent walking, and 15 percent bicycle.\[^cite_note-[www.mvg.de-216](http://www.mvg.de-216/)] Its public transport system delivered 566 million passenger trips that year.[^cite_note-217]

Munich is the hub of a developed regional transportation system, including the second-largest airport in Germany and the [Berlin–Munich high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin%E2%80%93Munich_high-speed_railway), which connects Munich to the German capital city with a journey time of about 4 hours. [Flixmobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flixmobility) which offers intercity coach service is headquartered in Munich.

The trade fair [Transport Logistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_Logistic) is held every two years at the *Neue Messe München* (Messe München International).

### Public transport



A class R2 *Straßenbahn* (*Tram*) on route 19 at Ostbahnhof



[Munich's S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_S-Bahn) at the [Marienplatz station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Marienplatz_station)

For its urban population of 2.6 million people, Munich and its closest suburbs have a comprehensive network of public transport incorporating the [Munich U-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_U-Bahn), the [Munich S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_S-Bahn), trams and buses. The system is supervised by the [Munich Transport and Tariff Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Transport_and_Tariff_Association) (*Münchner Verkehrs- und Tarifverbund*). The [Munich tramway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_tramway) is the oldest existing public transportation system in the city, which has been in operation since 1876. Munich also has an extensive network of bus lines. The average amount of time people spend commuting to and from work with public transit in Munich on a weekday is 56 min.

The extensive network of subway and tram lines assists and complement pedestrian movement in the city centre. The 700m-long Kaufinger Straße, which starts near the Main train station, forms a pedestrian east–west spine that traverses almost the entire centre. Major spines and many smaller streets cover an extensive area of the centre that can be enjoyed on foot and bike. These attributes result from applying the principle of [filtered permeability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permeability_\(spatial_and_transport_planning\)). Pedestrian and bike paths, which permeate the entire Munich city centre, go through public squares and open spaces for enjoyment. Munich city centre was subject to [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning) and has a comprehensive model for laying out neighborhoods and districts according to [grid plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_plan).[^cite_note-218]

### Cycling

### Munich Central Train Station

Main article: [München Hauptbahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnchen_Hauptbahnhof)

Munich Central Train Station (*München Hauptbahnhof*) is the central railway station located in the city centre and is the long-distance station in Munich.[^cite_note-220]

Munich Central Train Station serves about 450,000 passengers a day, which puts it on par with other large stations in Germany. Munich Central Train Station alongside [München Ost railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnchen_Ost_railway_station) are two of the 21 stations in Germany classified by [Deutsche Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bahn) as a [category 1 station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_railway_station_categories).

The central mainline station is a [terminal station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_station) with 32 [platforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_platform). The subterranean [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_S-Bahn) with 2 platforms and [U-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_U-Bahn) stations with 6 platforms are through stations.

[Intercity-Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity-Express) (ICE) and [EuroCity-Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroCity-Express) (ECE; Munich–[Bregenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bregenz)–[Zurich](40334603.md)) trains – and starting in 2026 also [TGV](57212.md) trains (Munich–Paris)[^cite_note-221] – stop at Munich Central Train Station. [InterCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCity) (IC) and [EuroCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroCity) (EC) trains to destinations east of Munich also stop at the München Ost railway station. Munich is connected to [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg) via [Ingolstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingolstadt) by the [Nuremberg–Munich high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg%E2%80%93Munich_high-speed_railway) and [Berlin–Munich high-speed railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin%E2%80%93Munich_high-speed_railway).

The old air raid shelter next to platform 11 of Munich Central Train Station was an important distribution point for [guest workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guest_worker) (*Gastarbeiter*) between 1960 and 1973. At peak more than 1,000 guest workers arrived per day, in total 1.8 million guest workers passed through Munich Central Train Station.[^cite_note-222]

### Autobahns



Munich [motorway](1764276.md) network

Munich is an integral part of the [Autobahn](1764276.md) network of southern Germany. Motorways from [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) (W), [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg), [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main) and Berlin (N), [Deggendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deggendorf) and [Passau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passau) (E), [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg) and [Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innsbruck) (SE), [Garmisch Partenkirchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garmisch-Partenkirchen) (S) and [Lindau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindau) (SW) terminate at Munich, allowing direct access to the different parts of Germany, Austria and Italy.

Traffic is often very heavy in and around Munich. [Traffic congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion) are commonplace at the beginning and end of major Bavarian holidays. There are few "green waves" or [roundabouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roundabout), and an abundance of construction sites.

Munich has introduced an [environmental zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_and_Environmental_Zone) and was among the first German cities to require a [green sticker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_sticker) for vehicles, these are a requirement when entering the city or driving in the wider surrounding area.[^cite_note-223]

### Air

#### Munich International Airport



[Munich International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport) (MUC)

[Franz Josef Strauss International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Airport) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Air_Transport_Association): MUC, [ICAO](14985.md): EDDM) is the second-largest airport in Germany and seventh-largest in Europe after [London Heathrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Heathrow_Airport), [Paris Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle_Airport), [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_International_Airport), [Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Airport_Schiphol), [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barajas_Airport) and [Istanbul Atatürk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atat%C3%BCrk_International_Airport). It is used by about 46 million passengers a year, and lies some 30 km (19 mi) north east of the city centre. It replaced the smaller [Munich-Riem Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich-Riem_Airport) in 1992. The airport can be reached by suburban train lines from the city. From the [main railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnchen_Hauptbahnhof) the journey takes 40–45 minutes. A [magnetic levitation train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maglev_\(transport\)) (called [Transrapid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transrapid)), which was to have run at speeds of up to 400 km/h (249 mph) from the central station to the airport in a travel time of 10 minutes, had been approved,[^cite_note-224] but was cancelled in March 2008 because of cost escalation and after heavy protests.[^cite_note-225] [Lufthansa](97733.md) opened its second hub at the airport when Terminal 2 was opened in 2003.

#### Other airports

In 2008, the Bavarian state government granted a licence to expand Oberpfaffenhofen Air Station located west of Munich, for commercial use. These plans were opposed by many residents in the Oberpfaffenhofen area as well as other branches of local government, including the city of Munich, which took the case to court.[^cite_note-226] However, in October 2009, the permit allowing up to 9725 business flights per year to depart from or land at Oberpfaffenhofen was confirmed by a regional judge.[^cite_note-227]

Despite being 110 km (68 mi) from Munich, [Memmingen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memmingen_Airport) has been advertised as Airport Munich West. After 2005, passenger traffic of nearby [Augsburg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg_Airport) was relocated to Munich Airport, leaving the Augsburg region of Bavaria without an air passenger airport within close reach.

## Around Munich

### Nearby towns

The Munich agglomeration sprawls across the plain of the [Alpine foothills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foothills) comprising about 2.6 million inhabitants. Several smaller traditional Bavarian towns and cities like [Dachau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dachau,_Bavaria), [Freising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freising), [Erding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erding), [Starnberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starnberg), [Landshut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landshut) and [Moosburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moosburg) are today part of the Greater Munich Region, formed by Munich and the surrounding districts, making up the [Munich Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Metropolitan_Region), which has a population of about 6 million people.[^cite_note-mmr_official_site-7]

### Recreation

South of Munich, there are numerous nearby freshwater lakes such as [Lake Starnberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Starnberg), [Ammersee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammersee), [Chiemsee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiemsee), [Walchensee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walchensee), [Kochelsee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kochelsee), [Tegernsee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tegernsee_\(lake\)), [Schliersee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schliersee_\(lake\)), [Simssee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simssee), [Staffelsee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffelsee), [Wörthsee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%B6rthsee), [Kirchsee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirchsee_\(Bavaria\)) and the [Osterseen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osterseen) (Easter Lakes), which are popular among Munich residents for recreation, swimming and watersports and can be quickly reached by car and a few also by Munich's [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_S-Bahn).[^cite_note-229]
