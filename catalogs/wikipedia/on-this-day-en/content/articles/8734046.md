# Brazil at the FIFA World Cup

For the women's team, see [Brazil at the FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_at_the_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup).



[Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9) celebrating Brazil's win of the [1970 FIFA World Cup](157227.md) in [Mexico](3966054.md)

This article summarizes the results and overall performance of [Brazil](149286.md) at the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md), including the [qualification phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) and the final phase, officially called the World Cup finals. The qualification phase, which currently takes place over the three years preceding the finals, is used to determine which teams qualify for the finals. The current format of the finals involves 48 teams competing for the title, at venues within the host country.

Brazil is the most successful national team in the history of the World Cup, having won five titles and earned second place, third place, and fourth place finishes twice each. Brazil is one of four countries along with Argentina, Spain, and Germany to win a FIFA World Cup away from its continent ([Sweden 1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup), [Chile 1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup), [Mexico 1970](157227.md), [United States 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup), and [South Korea/Japan 2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup)). Brazil is also the only national team to have played in all [FIFA World Cup editions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_team_appearances_in_the_FIFA_World_Cup#Comprehensive_team_results_by_tournament) without any absence or need for play-offs. Brazil also has the best overall performance in World Cup history in both proportional and absolute terms, with a record of 76 victories in 114 matches played, 129 [goal difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_difference), 247 points, and only 19 losses across 22 tournaments.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

Traditionally, Brazil's [greatest rival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Brazil_football_rivalry) is [Argentina](454699.md). The two countries have met each other four times in the history of the FIFA World Cup, with two wins for Brazil ([West Germany 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup) and [Spain 1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup)), one for Argentina ([Italy 1990](157232.md)), and a draw ([Argentina 1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup)). The country that played the most matches against Brazil at the World Cup is [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team): seven times, with five wins for Brazil and two draws. Three other historical rivals are [Italy](362466.md), which lost two World Cup finals against Brazil and eliminated the Brazilians in two tournaments ([France 1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup) and Spain 1982), [France](250197.md), which defeated Brazil on three occasions ([Mexico 1986](157231.md), [France 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), and [Germany 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup)), and the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), which has eliminated Brazil at two of their five meetings ([West Germany 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup) and [South Africa 2010](168079.md)), were themselves eliminated at two others ([USA 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) and [France 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup)) and won the match for third place in [Brazil 2014](656933.md).

  - Youngest tournament winner (1958, aged 17 years and 249 days)

  - Youngest goalscorer (1958 v Wales, aged 17 years and 239 days)

  - Youngest hat-trick scorer (1958 v France, aged 17 years and 244 days)

  - Youngest goalscorer in a final (1958 v Sweden, aged 17 years and 249 days)

- Youngest Golden Ball winner: [Ronaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldo_\(Brazilian_footballer\)) (1998, aged 21 years and 297 days)

- Most appearances in the [All-Star Team](3482503.md#All-Star_Team_and_Dream_Team): [Djalma Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djalma_Santos) (3, 1954–1962) *(shared with [Franz Beckenbauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Beckenbauer) and [Philipp Lahm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipp_Lahm))*

- Most tournament wins as player and coach: [Mário Zagallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1rio_Zagallo) (3, 1958 and 1962 as player, 1970 as coach)

- Only player to appear in three consecutive FIFA World Cup finals: [Cafu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cafu) (1994, 1998 and 2002)

- Most team awards won: Cafu (4, 1994–2006)
