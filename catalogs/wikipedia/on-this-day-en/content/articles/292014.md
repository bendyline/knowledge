**Sisaket province** ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): ศรีสะเกษ, [RTGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Thai_General_System_of_Transcription): *_Si Sa Ket_*,[^cite_note-5] pronounced [\[sǐː sàʔ kèːt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Thai); [Northern Khmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Khmer_dialect): ซีซะเกด) is one of Thailand's seventy-six [provinces](204637.md) (*changwat*). It lies in [lower northeastern Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isan#Administrative_divisions), a region called [Isan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isan). Neighboring provinces are (from west clockwise): [Surin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surin_province), [Roi Et](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roi_Et_province), [Yasothon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasothon_province), and [Ubon Ratchathani](242147.md). To the south it borders [Oddar Meancheay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oddar_Meancheay) and [Preah Vihear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preah_Vihear_province) of [Cambodia](334751.md).

## Geography

The province is in the valley of the [Mun River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mun_River), a tributary of the [Mekong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong). The [Dângrêk mountain chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A2ngr%C3%AAk_Mountains), which forms the border with [Cambodia](334751.md), is in the south of the province. The total forest area is 1,025 km<sup>2</sup> (396 sq mi) or 11.5 percent of provincial area.[^cite_note-AREA-1] [Khao Phra Wihan National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khao_Phra_Wihan_National_Park) covers an area of 130 km<sup>2</sup> of the Dângrêk mountains in the southeast of the province. Established on 20 March 1998, it is named after the Khmer Empire temple [Preah Vihear](2367753.md) (anglicised in [Thailand](30128.md) as *Phra Wihan*), now in [Cambodia](334751.md), which has been the subject of a border dispute. The temple faces north and was built in dedication to the Hindu god Shiva.

Access to the temple is still principally from the Thai side, as the ruins are difficult to reach from the Cambodian plains at the bottom of a sheer cliff several hundred meters below.

## History

&#x20;The many [Khmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Empire) ruins found in the province show the area must have been important to the [Khmer empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_empire) at least by the 12th century, although it was apparently sparsely populated. According to local tradition, it was known as *Sri Nakorn Lamduan* (ศรีนครลำดวน.) It was later called *Khukhan*, after a town built in the late-15th century CE during the reign of King [Boromaratcha III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boromaratcha_III) of [Ayutthaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayutthaya_\(city\)). Ethnic [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_people) began settling the northern portion of the province, and in 1786 the town Sisaket was formed, subject to Khukhan. In 1904, Sisaket was renamed Khukhan, while the original Khukhan was designated Huai Nua.



Rasi Salai Dam

[Monthon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monthon) [Udon Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udon_Thani) was created in 1912, and assumed the administration of the most of region. In 1933 the monthon system was ended, and the province of Khukhan was administered directly from Bangkok. Five years later, the name of the town and province were restored to Sisaket, with the district containing Huai Nua being called *Khukhan*.

The [Rasi Salai Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasi_Salai_Dam) built here in 1994 was unofficially decommissioned in July 2000, following devastation of local farming villages.

## Demographics

The province is populated by four main ethnic groups: [Kui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kui_people), [Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_people), [Khmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_people), and [Yer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yer_people?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-8] Sisaket is one of the provinces where there is a sizable [northern Khmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Khmer_people) population. In the 2000 census it was reported that 26.2 percent of the population are capable of speaking [Khmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_language). This is down from the 1990 census when it was reported that 30.2 percent of the population were capable of speaking Khmer.[^cite_note-9] The majority are [Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_language) speaking people.

## Symbols

The [provincial seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seals_of_the_provinces_of_Thailand) shows Prasat Hin Ban Samo, a Khmer temple about 1,000 years old, in the Prang Ku District.

The [provincial tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Thai_provincial_trees) and flower of the province is the [lamduan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamduan). The six leaves of the flower symbolise the six original districts of the province: Khukhan, Kantharalak, Uthumphon Phisai, Kanthararom, Rasi Salai, and Khun Han. The provincial aquatic life is the [Chinese edible frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_edible_frog) (*[Hoplobatrachus rugulosus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoplobatrachus_rugulosus)*).

## Economy

Sisaket is largely agricultural. Among other agricultural produce, it is known for its [garlic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlic) and its [shallots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shallot), for which it was rewarded with [GI registration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_indication) in 2020.[^cite_note-10]

### Local government

As of 26 November 2019 there is one Sisaket Provincial Administration Organisation (*_ongkan borihan suan changwat_*) and 37 municipal (*thesaban*) areas in the province.[^cite_note-11] Sisaket and Kantharalak have town (*[thesaban mueang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesaban_mueang)*) status. There are a further 35 subdistrict municipalities (*[thesaban tambon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesaban_tambon)*). The non-municipal areas are administered by 179 Subdistrict Administrative Organisations, SAO (*ongkan borihan suan tambon*).

## Transportation

Sisaket is on the [northeastern railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Railway_of_Thailand#Northeastern_Line) from [Bangkok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krung_Thep_Aphiwat_Central_Terminal) (กรุงเทพอภิวัฒน์) to [Warin Chamrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warin_Chamrap_District) (วารินชำราบ). Sisaket's main station is [Sisaket Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Si_Sa_Ket_Railway_Station). Sisaket has frequent bus service to and from Bangkok's [Northern Bus Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Bus_Terminal_\(Bangkok\)) ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): หมอชิดใหม่; [RTGS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Thai_General_System_of_Transcription): *_mo chit mai_*)

## Health

Sisaket's main hospital is [Sisaket Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisaket_Hospital), a regional hospital operated by the [Ministry of Public Health](23194319.md).
