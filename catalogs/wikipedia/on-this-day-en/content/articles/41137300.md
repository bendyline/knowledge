# Science and technology in South Korea



[Hyundai Motor Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Motor_Group), one of the top three car brands in the world



[LG Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LG_Electronics), the No. 1 TV market player in the world



[Samsung Electronics](46445426.md), the world's No. 1 smartphone market share as of 2022



[SK Hynix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Hynix), the world's fourth-largest chipmaker in sales as of 2023

**Science and technology in South Korea** has advanced throughout the decades. The advancement of science and technology has become an integral part of [economic planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_planning) in [South Korea](27019.md).[^cite_note-\:1-1] Fast-growing industries have created a massive demand for new and more advanced technologies.[^cite_note-\:1-1] Additionally, Korean engineers and scientists propose that the advancement of science and technology in partnership with North Korea could help facilitate the peaceful reunification of North and South Korea.[^cite_note-\:3-2]

In the pursuit of advancement, South Korea has taken a centralized approach.[^cite_note-\:1-1] South Korea was ranked 4th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2025.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] As of 2022, South Korea has the sixth largest private investment in [artificial intelligence](1164.md).[^cite_note-5]

## Korean Federation of Science and Technology Societies (KOFST)

The [Korean Federation of Science and Technology Societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Federation_of_Science_and_Technology_Societies?action=edit\&redlink=1) was established in 1966 and consists of 780 organizations as of 2012.[^cite_note-\:3-2] The goals of the KOFST are to make the sharing of technology and research data between these societies easier and to encourage and facilitate the cooperation of [Pacific Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Rim) countries.[^cite_note-\:3-2]

## Aerospace engineering

Main article: [South Korean space program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korean_space_program)



A replica of the [Naro-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naro-1)

South Korea has sent up 10 satellites from 1992, all using foreign rockets and overseas launch pads, notably [Arirang-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arirang-1) in 1999, and [Arirang-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arirang-2) in 2006 as part of its space partnership with Russia.[^cite_note-6] Arirang-1 was lost in space in 2008, after nine years in service.[^cite_note-7]

In April 2008, [Yi So-yeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_So-yeon) became the first Korean to fly in space, aboard the Russian [Soyuz TMA-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_TMA-12).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

In June 2009, the first [spaceport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceport) of South Korea, [Naro Space Center](3584260.md), was completed at [Goheung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goheung), [South Jeolla Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Jeolla_Province).[^cite_note-10] The launch of [Naro-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naro-1) in August 2009 resulted in a failure.[^cite_note-11] The second attempt in June 2010 was also unsuccessful.[^cite_note-12] However, the third launch of the Naro 1 in January 2013 was successful.[^cite_note-13] The government plans to develop Naro-2 by the year 2018.[^cite_note-14]

Companies involved in the aerospace and defense markets of South Korea are primarily large multinational companies.[^cite_note-\:2-15] These large companies have been further strengthened by a push towards consolidation in the market.[^cite_note-\:2-15] This results in a difficulty for newer companies to enter the South Korean aerospace market.[^cite_note-\:2-15]

With the final successful launch of the [Nuri](31909917.md), which South Korea launched for the third time in 2023, South Korea has become the seventh country in the world to have a [medium-sized liquid-propellant rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid-propellant_rocket) engine over 75 tons.[^cite_note-16]

Two years after South Korea officially expressed its intention to join [Horizon Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon_Europe) in 2022, South Korea and the [European Union](9317.md) signed a Horizon Europe agreement, which is expected to allow Korea to participate in that from 2025.[^cite_note-17]

[National Aerospace Industrial Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aerospace_Industrial_Complex) is being built in [South Gyeongsang Province](319775.md), bringing together aerospace-related companies and agencies, with the goal of completion in June 2025.[^cite_note-18]

## Defense technology

Due to the growing perceived threat of North Korea, South Korea is increasing defense spending.[^cite_note-\:2-15] In 2019, South Korea is expected to increase spending by the largest amount in 11 years.[^cite_note-\:2-15]

[Hanwha Techwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanwha_Techwin) is a leading South Korean company manufacturing defense products, security devices, engines, digital solutions, and integrated control systems in South Korea.[^cite_note-\:2-15] [Hanwha Techwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanwha_Techwin) produces video recorders, surveillance software, access control products, and intelligent machinery and solutions.[^cite_note-\:2-15]

## Biotechnology

See also: [Traditional Korean medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Korean_medicine)

Since the 1980s, the South Korean government has invested in the development of a domestic [biotechnology](4502.md) industry, and the sector is projected to grow to \$6.5 billion by 2010.[^cite_note-19] The medical sector accounts for a large part of the production, including production of [hepatitis vaccines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_A_vaccine) and [antibiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotic).

Recently, research and development in [genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics) and [cloning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloning) has received increasing attention, with the first successful cloning of a dog, [Snuppy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snuppy) (in 2005), and the cloning of two females of an endangered species of wolves by the [Seoul National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_National_University) in 2007.[^cite_note-20]

The rapid growth of the industry has resulted in significant voids in regulation of ethics, as was highlighted by the [scientific misconduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwang_Woo-Suk#Controversies) case involving [Hwang Woo-Suk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hwang_Woo-Suk).[^cite_note-21]

In January 2025, the country launched the [National Bio Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bio_Committee), an advisory committee directly under the president.[^cite_note-22]

## Robotics

Main article: [South Korean robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korean_robotics)



Airport cleaning robot by [LG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LG)

[Robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics) has been included in the list of main national R\&D projects in South Korea since 2003.[^cite_note-23] In 2009, the government announced plans to build robot-themed parks in [Incheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incheon_Free_Economic_Zone) and [Masan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masan) with a mix of public and private funding.[^cite_note-24]

In 2005, [Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Advanced_Institute_of_Science_and_Technology) (KAIST) developed the world's second walking [humanoid robot](48180.md), [HUBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HUBO). A team in the Korea Institute of Industrial Technology developed the first South Korean [android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(robot\)), [EveR-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EveR-1) in May 2006.[^cite_note-25] EveR-1 has been succeeded by more complex models with improved movement and vision.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

Plans of creating English-teaching robot assistants to compensate for the shortage of teachers were announced in February 2010, with the robots being deployed to most preschools and kindergartens by 2013.[^cite_note-28] Robotics are also incorporated in the entertainment sector as well; the *Korean Robot Game Festival* has been held every year since 2004 to promote science and robot technology.[^cite_note-29]
