The **2026 Romanian political crisis** is an ongoing constitutional and governmental crisis in [Romania](25445.md) that began on 5 May 2026, when the [bicameral Parliament](4462971.md) passed a [motion of no confidence](208101.md) against the government of [Prime Minister](266262.md) [Ilie Bolojan](78639092.md), forcing his cabinet to resign.[^cite_note-1] [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Nicu%C8%99or_Dan) [Nicușor Dan](35543359.md) has been challenged to provide stability amid the crisis.[^cite_note-2] The crisis has been characterised by the inability to form a new parliamentary majority, a failed prime ministerial nomination,[^cite_note-3] deepening fiscal uncertainty, and warnings from international financial institutions regarding the country's economic stability.[^cite_note-4] Former President [Traian Băsescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traian_B%C4%83sescu) described the situation as "the greatest political crisis since the [Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Revolution)".[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

## Background

In 2024, political parties struggled to form a government after the [2024 Romanian parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Romanian_parliamentary_election).[^cite_note-7] In 2024, there were reports of a political crisis developing.[^cite_note-8] The constitutional court intervened to cancel the second round of the [2024 Romanian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Romanian_presidential_election) after [Călin Georgescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C4%83lin_Georgescu) lead in the first round.[^cite_note-9]

## Events

The crisis was precipitated in April 2026 by Prime Minister Ilie Bolojan's proposal to list minority stakes of several [state-owned enterprises](204768.md) (SOEs), including [CEC Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEC_Bank), [Hidroelectrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidroelectrica), and [Romgaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romgaz), on the [Bucharest Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest_Stock_Exchange).[^cite_note-10] The plan was part of a broader restructuring initiative intended to enforce [financial transparency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_\(market\)) and market discipline while retaining state control.[^cite_note-roins-11][^cite_note-12] Bolojan claimed the plan aligned with Romania's [EU](9317.md)-backed [National Recovery and Resilience Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NextGenerationEU), with the opposition from coalition partner [Social Democratic Party](407177.md) (PSD) supposedly coming due to the plan's threat to its "political clientele"; Bolojan further accused parties of treating SOE positions as "[sinecures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinecures) for party loyalists" to extract rents.[^cite_note-agerpres2-13] On 20 April, PSD announced a bill to ban such listings for two years,[^cite_note-roins-11] withdrew its ministers from the government, and, alongside the far-right [Alliance for the Union of Romanians](64666536.md) (AUR), filed a no-confidence motion accusing Bolojan of "selling state assets".[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-agerpres-15]

On 5 May 2026, Parliament passed the motion with 281 votes in favour, the largest such vote since the [1989 revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Revolution), toppling the cabinet.[^cite_note-16] This meant that a new government would have to be formed.[^cite_note-17] The [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Romania) subsequently passed the PSD-sponsored [moratorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moratorium_\(law\)).[^cite_note-roins-11] Bolojan accused that the listing directly threatened PSD's ability to use these enterprises as tools for political [rent extraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent-seeking), a dynamic he explicitly identified as the crisis's underlying cause.[^cite_note-agerpres2-13]

On 4 June 2026, [Eugen Tomac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugen_Tomac) was rejected as a potential prime minister after talks broke down.[^cite_note-18] On 22 June 2026, [Adrian Veștea](74616916.md) was rejected by parliament as the proposed new prime minister.[^cite_note-19] On 30 September 2026, [Siegfried Mureșan](45320900.md) failed to secure parliamentary approval to become new prime minister.[^cite_note-20]

The political crisis has occurred amid an economic crisis.[^cite_note-21]
