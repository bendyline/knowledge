**Alpes-Maritimes** (French: [\[alp(ə)maʁitim\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); [Occitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_language): *Aups Maritims*; [Monégasque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mon%C3%A9gasque_dialect): *Alpes Marìtime*; [Italian](14708.md): *Alpi Marittime*; lit. 'Maritime Alps') is a [department of France](38523.md) located in the country's southeast corner, on the [Italian border](47329029.md) and [Mediterranean](19006.md) coast. Part of the [Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provence-Alpes-C%C3%B4te_d'Azur) [region](58846.md), it encompasses the [French Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Riviera) alongside neighbouring [Var](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Var_\(department\)). Alpes-Maritimes had a population of 1,128,418 in 2023.[^cite_note-pophist-3] Its [prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_in_France) (and largest city) is [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice), with [Grasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grasse) as the sole [subprefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subprefectures_in_France).

Alpes-Maritimes has become one of the world's most attractive tourist destinations in recent decades, featuring renowned cities and towns such as [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice), [Grasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grasse), [Cannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes), [Antibes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibes), [Menton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menton), [Èze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%88ze), [Roquebrune-Cap-Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roquebrune-Cap-Martin) and [Sainte-Agnès](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sainte-Agn%C3%A8s,_Alpes-Maritimes), as well as numerous alpine ski resorts.[^cite_note-4] It also entirely surrounds the [Principality of Monaco](19261.md). The department's inhabitants are called *Maralpins* (masculine) or *Maralpines* (feminine) but are more commonly referred to as "Azuréens"; its arms are those of the City of Nice. In terms of politics, Alpes-Maritimes is one of France's most right-wing departments, as the majority led by [The Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republicans_\(France\)) in the [departmental council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departmental_council_\(France\)) holds all but two of the 54 seats following the [2021 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_departmental_elections).

### Overview

The Alpes-Maritimes department is surrounded by the departments of [Var](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Var_\(department\)) in the southwest, [Alpes-de-Haute-Provence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpes-de-Haute-Provence) in the northwest; Italy to the north and east; and the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) to the south. It surrounds the [Principality of Monaco](19261.md) on the west, north and east.



Geography of Alpes-Maritimes

Its topography is very mixed. As its name suggests, most of the department is a constituent part of the overall topographic Alps – including the [Maritime Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Alps) – but it also has the distinction of being a coastal district with its Mediterranean coast. The coastal area, urbanized and densely populated (shaded in red on the map), includes all the cities in an almost continuous conurbation from [Cannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes) to [Menton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menton), while the larger but sparsely populated mountainous area (light green) is fully rural with the exception of the three large resorts of [Valberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valberg_\(ski_resort\)) (created in 1936), [Auron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auron_\(ski_resort\)) (created in 1937) and [Isola 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isola_2000) (created in 1971).

#### Summits and passes

The highest point of the department is the [Cime du Gélas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cime_du_G%C3%A9las) (3,143 metres, 10,312 ft) on the Franco-Italian border which dominates the [Vallée des Merveilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vall%C3%A9e_des_Merveilles) further east. The summit of [Monte Argentera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Argentera) is higher at 3,297 metres (10,817 ft) above sea level, but it is located in Italy. There is also [Mount Mounier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Mounier?action=edit\&redlink=1) (2,817 metres, 9,242 ft), which dominates the south of the vast [Dôme de Barrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%B4me_de_Barrot?action=edit\&redlink=1), formed of a mass more than 900 metres (3,000 ft) thick of red mudstones deeply indented by the gorges of [Daluis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daluis) and [Cians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cians). Except in winter, four passes allow passage to the north of the [Mercantour-Argentera massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massif_du_Mercantour-Argentera) whose imposing 62-kilometre-long (39 mi) barrier is covered in winter snow which is visible from the coast. From the west, the [Route des Grandes Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_des_Grandes_Alpes) enters the [Cayolle Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayolle_Pass) (2,326 metres, 7,631 ft) first on the way to the Alps and the sources of the [Var](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Var_\(river\)) in the commune of [Entraunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entraunes). Then the route follows the [Col de la Bonette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_de_la_Bonette) – the highest pass in Europe at 2,715 metres (8,907 ft) – to connect to the valley of the [Tinée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin%C3%A9e) then the Ubaye. Further east, the [Col de la Lombarde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_de_la_Lombarde) (2,350 metres, 7,710 ft) above [Isola 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isola_2000) allows access to the shrine of Saint-Anne de [Vinadio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinadio) in Italy. Finally, at its eastern end, the [Col de Tende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_de_Tende) (1,871 metres, 6,138 ft) links with [Cuneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneo) in Italy.

It is the climate that made the [Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Riviera) famous. The current department of Alpes-Maritimes, however, does not have only one climate, the complex terrain and high mountains divide the department between those who are well exposed (the south-facing side) and those which are less (the north-facing side) and even with the mild Mediterranean climate there can be violent storms and prolonged droughts.

The coastal area has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) (rainfall in autumn November and spring February especially, summer drought, mild winter and dry summer). The interior, especially in the north, has a [mountain climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_climate) (winter quite bright, summer storms). Around Cannes is a particularly warm micro-climate due to the high hills warming the air which descends on the city.

One of the attractions of the department is its level of sunshine: over 300 days per year. Despite this the department is also the most stormy of France with an average of 70 to 90 thunderstorm days per year, arising from the differences in temperature due to a warm sea in autumn.

As soon as one moves away from the coast, towards the west of the department, the interior plains (in particular near Grasse) the climate is a little less temperate but just as sunny. In summer, the temperature very easily exceeds 30 °C (86 °F), while the average is only 27 °C (81 °F) along the Nice coast during July and August. Occasional frost is possible in the interior during winter, unlike in Nice and rest of the coast, where frost is very rare.

In the east of the department, unlike the west, there are no plains. In the [Menton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menton) region, the altitude increases very rapidly inland, so the sea tempers the atmosphere much more: the maximum in summer is on average 25 °C (77 °F) and the winters are milder than in the interior and frost is very rare.

Snow is also rare along the coast, however, it happens that rare snowfall surprises the Côte d'Azur residents (on average every 5–7 years), as was the case in the winter of 2004–2005 when the city of Nice woke up with a few centimetres of snow, often creating traffic problems. More recently, in February 2010, more than 10 centimetres (3.9 in) of snow was measured in Cannes and nearly 30 centimetres (12 in) in the Grasse region.

In the north of the department the climate in the Alps is mountainous, and there is decent snow cover from end of November until late April.

The [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) military district of [Alpes Maritimae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpes_Maritimae) was created by [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus) in 14 BC. It became a full [Roman province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) in the middle of the 1st century AD, with its capital first at [Cemenelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cemenelum) (today [Cimiez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimiez), a suburb north of [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice)) and subsequently at [Embrun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embrun,_Hautes-Alpes). At its greatest extent in AD 297, the province reached north to [Digne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digne) and [Briançon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian%C3%A7on).

Conquered by the [French First Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_First_Republic) during the [French Revolutionary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars), a [department](38523.md) of Alpes-Maritimes was first set up in 1793 with different boundaries from those of the modern department. In 1793, Alpes-Maritimes included [Monaco](19261.md) ([Port Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Hercules)), but not [Grasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grasse), which was then part of the department of [Var](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Var_\(department\)) as a historical part of France. In 1805, [San Remo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanremo) (San Rème) was included after the disbandment of the [Ligurian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligurian_Republic). In 1812, the department had three [arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_France) with the following [cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_\(administrative_division\)):[^cite_note-Gallica-10]

- [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice), cantons: [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice) (2 cantons), [Aspremont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspremont,_Alpes-Maritimes), [La Brigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Brigue), [Menton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menton), [Monaco](19261.md), [Roquebillière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roquebilli%C3%A8re), [Saint-Sauveur-sur-Tinée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Sauveur-sur-Tin%C3%A9e), [Saorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saorge), [L'Escarène](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Escar%C3%A8ne), [Sospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sospel), [Utelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utelle) and [Villefranche-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villefranche-sur-Mer).

- [Sanremo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanremo), cantons: [Sanremo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanremo), [Bordighera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bordighera), [Dolceacqua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolceacqua), [Pigna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigna,_Liguria), [Taggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taggia), [Triora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triora) and [Ventimiglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventimiglia).

- [Puget-Théniers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puget-Th%C3%A9niers), cantons: [Puget-Théniers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puget-Th%C3%A9niers), [Beuil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beuil), [Gilette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilette), [Guillaumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillaumes), [Roquestéron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roquest%C3%A9ron), [Saint-Étienne-de-Tinée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-%C3%89tienne-de-Tin%C3%A9e) and [Villars-sur-Var](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villars-sur-Var).

The population of the department in 1812 was 131,266, and its area was 3,226.74 square kilometres (1,245.85 sq mi), covering both present-day [Arrondissement of Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissement_of_Nice), [Principality of Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Monaco) and [Province of Imperia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Imperia).[^cite_note-Gallica-10]

In 1814, at the close of the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars), the territory was restored to the [Crown of Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Savoy) by the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna).

In 1860 [Camillo Benso, Count of Cavour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camillo_Benso,_Count_of_Cavour), one of the architects of Italian unity with the [Kingdom of Piedmont-Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Piedmont-Sardinia), negotiated support for [Napoleon III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_III) in exchange for [Savoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy) and the [County of Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Nice) as set forth in the [Treaty of Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turin_\(1860\)). The annexation was confirmed on 15 and 16 April 1860 by 30,712 male electors enrolled in the 89 communes of the County of Nice who, for the first time, had universal male suffrage by [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite). The "Yes" vote for reunification with France was 83.8% of registered voters and 99.2% of votes.[^cite_note-11]

The new department of Alpes-Maritimes consisted of the former County of Nice, divided into an [Arrondissement of Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissement_of_Nice) and an Arrondissement of [Puget-Théniers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puget-Th%C3%A9niers) (both arrondissements existed in the former Department (1793–1814)), and a portion of the [Var department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Var_department), which formed the [Arrondissement of Grasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissement_of_Grasse). However, the County of Nice did not include [Tende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tende) and [La Brigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Brigue), which were still part of Piedmont-Sardinia.

For economic reasons, the Arrondissement of Puget-Théniers was merged into the Arrondissement of Nice in 1926. Since that time, the department has had two [arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements_of_France).

In 1947, in accordance with the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Peace_Treaties,_1947) and as a referendum result favourable to their attachment to France, the communes of [Tende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tende) and [La Brigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Brigue) (also parts of communes in the high valleys of Vésubie and Tinée: part the commune of [Isola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isola,_Alpes-Maritimes)) which had not been ceded to France in 1860, were attached to the department.

## Administration

### Politics



Constituencies of Alpes-Maritimes for the National Assembly of France. Blue: [The Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republicans_\(France\)). Yellow: [La République En Marche!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_R%C3%A9publique_En_Marche!) ([2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_French_legislative_election)).

Since the end of [World War II](32927.md), Alpes-Maritimes has generally voted to the right. It has nine constituencies for the [National Assembly](299616.md). Following the [2017 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_French_legislative_election), six constituencies are represented by members of [The Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republicans_\(France\)) (LR) right-wing party and three by members of the [La République En Marche!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_R%C3%A9publique_En_Marche!) (REM) centrist party. Of the five members of the [Senate](567323.md) who represent the department in the upper house of Parliament, four are right-wing (LR) and one is left-wing ([Socialist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Party_\(France\)), PS). Of the 54 departmental councillors, 52 are aligned with the right-wing coalition and two are in the left-wing opposition.

In the [2022 French presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_presidential_election), [Marine Le Pen](572897.md) of the [National Rally](67119.md) won a plurality in Alpes-Maritimes in the first round; incumbent [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) of [La République En Marche!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_R%C3%A9publique_En_Marche!) won a majority in the second round.

In the 1980s and 1990s, Alpes-Maritimes experienced [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_France) problems with its politicians, which led to several criminal convictions, including those of Nice Mayor [Jacques Médecin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_M%C3%A9decin) and Cannes Mayor [Michel Mouillot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Mouillot), as well as that of Antibes Mayor Pierre Merli, criminally indicted in 1995 for a real estate scam and convicted four years later.

### Budget

In 2011, the draft departmental budget amounted to €1.3 billion of which 498 million (38.3%) was devoted to social action and 346 million to operations (26.6%).[^cite_note-CG06-budget-14] Capital expenditures was just over 250 million euros (19.2%).[^cite_note-CG06-budget-14]

In 2010, the department was the third most indebted in France with €942 million of debt or 68.4% of the annual budget.[^cite_note-jdn-dette-15] This debt amounted to 2,460 euros per [fiscal tax unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_splitting) (household) and 859 euros per person.[^cite_note-jdn-dette-15] The trend of change in debt over the last decade has been a sharp increase: + 440% between 2001 and 2009 and 26% between 2009 and 2010.[^cite_note-jdn-dette-15] There was only €43 million in debt in 2003.

When [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice) became part of France in 1860, it was still a small town; the department had fewer than 200,000 inhabitants. However, the population grew quickly from 300,000 at the beginning of the 20th century to over a million. The population is aging because of the number of retirees who move to the coast.

The population is now concentrated in the urban region that includes [Antibes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibes), [Cannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes), [Grasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grasse), Nice, and [Menton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menton), and which constitutes 90% of the total population.

The department had 1,128,418 inhabitants in 2023, making it the 20th most populated department in France. There are 163 communes including 107 under 2000 inhabitants (representing a total of 60,376 inhabitants), 36 from 2000 to 9999 inhabitants (total 159,291 inhabitants), 15 between 10,000 and 49,999 inhabitants (total 294,703 inhabitants), 4 between 50,000 and 199,999 inhabitants (Antibes, Cannes, Cagnes-sur-Mer and Grasse, total 256,311 inhabitants), and one with over 200,000 inhabitants (Nice, with 357,737 inhabitants).[^cite_note-pop2023-7] The population density was 263 inhabitants per square kilometre in 2023.

According to INSEE 39.5% of children born in 2011 in the department of Alpes-Maritimes have at least one parent born abroad (regardless of nationality), 15.4% have a father born in North Africa.[^cite_note-18]

The area is also known for its extremely large population of people of Italian descent. About 40% of the population of the Alpes-Maritimes claim their ancestry as being solely Italian, and as many as 80% of the population can trace some degree of ancestry back to Italy before it was annexed by France in the 1860s.[^cite_note-19]

## Economy

The economy of the Alpes-Maritimes is characterized by the importance of the [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector). The department has, in addition to tourism and traditional services, a relatively high level of corporate research and higher level of services. Agriculture is of little importance and [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_sector) plays a relatively small role although it has diversified into activities with high technological value. The construction and public works sector is quite important. The economy is very sensitive to changes in the national and international situation. The rate of unemployment is 9.1%.

According to the INSEE, in 2005 the GDP per capita of the Alpes-Maritimes was 27,723 euros which ranked it as the thirteenth highest department in France.[^cite_note-insee_2005-20] GDP was 29.6 billion euros.[^cite_note-insee_2005-20] According to Eurostat, *GDP per capita at market prices in 2008*, the department had a GDP per capita of 30,700 euros, which is also ranked it thirteenth in France.[^cite_note-21]

On the coast, thanks to the mild climate, towns are [resort destinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort_town) all year round. ## Tourism

### Seaside



The [French Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Riviera), centred on [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice), attracts millions of international tourists every year.



[Villefranche-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villefranche-sur-Mer) between Nice and Monaco

The presence of the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) and the [French Alps](961317.md) under a mild sky has favoured one dominant activity: tourism, which accounts for 64,000 jobs directly in the Alpes-Maritimes. For only the city of Nice the tourism turnover represents a 12 to 13% share of the whole tourism market in France. The capital of the [Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Riviera) is the fifth most populous city in France. The city of Nice also has the second largest airport in France ([Nice Côte d'Azur Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice_C%C3%B4te_d'Azur_Airport)), after Paris and its three airports at [Roissy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle_Airport), [Orly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orly_Airport) and [Le Bourget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris%E2%80%93Le_Bourget_Airport). There are nearly 13.5 million passengers per year passing through Nice Airport.

The seaside where the majority of the population resides is one of the most popular parts of the world with many attractions:

- [Seaside resorts](293990.md) ([Théoule-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%A9oule-sur-Mer), [Mandelieu-la-Napoule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandelieu-la-Napoule), [Cannes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes), [Golfe-Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golfe-Juan), [Juan-les-Pins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan-les-Pins), [Antibes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibes), [Cagnes-sur-Mer](3546923.md), Nice, [Villefranche-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villefranche-sur-Mer), [Beaulieu-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaulieu-sur-Mer), [Èze-sur-Mer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%88ze-sur-Mer), [Cap d'Ail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap_d'Ail), [Roquebrune-Cap-Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roquebrune-Cap-Martin) and [Menton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menton))

- Convention cities that spread their business throughout the year are Cannes, with its [Palais des Festivals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_des_Festivals_et_des_Congr%C3%A8s), and Nice, with its [Acropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_des_Congr%C3%A8s_Acropolis).

The area inland from the busy French Riviera is a base for many outdoor sports: cycling, [mountain biking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_biking), [skiing](28478.md), walking, [rock climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_climbing), [canyoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canyoning), [canoeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoeing), [rafting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafting), fishing, [horse riding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_riding), [Adventure parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventure_park), [caving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caving) and the area has the first ever underground *[via ferrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_ferrata)*. The area has internationally renowned [paragliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paragliding) and [hang gliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_gliding) flying sites at Col-de-Bleyne, [Gourdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gourdon,_Alpes-Maritimes), [Gréolières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%A9oli%C3%A8res) and Lachens.

### Mountainside



[Mercantour National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercantour_National_Park) in the commune of [Saint-Martin-Vésubie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Martin-V%C3%A9subie), which primarily covers a small town in the Alps and tourist destination

In the mountains, [skiing](28478.md) and hiking bring life to [Saint-Étienne-de-Tinée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-%C3%89tienne-de-Tin%C3%A9e) ([Auron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auron_\(ski_resort\))), [Beuil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beuil), [Péone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A9one) ([Valberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valberg_\(ski_resort\))), [Saint-Martin-Vésubie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Martin-V%C3%A9subie), [Isola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isola,_Alpes-Maritimes), [Gréolières](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%A9oli%C3%A8res), Peïra-Cava, [Col de Turini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Col_de_Turini), and Turini-Camp d'argent in the Authion mountains.

### Second homes

As of 2020, 25.3% of available housing in the department were second homes.[^cite_note-24]

## Education

### Primary and secondary education

The department has 222 nursery schools, 357 primary schools and one special school. It also hosts 72 colleges, 14 vocational schools and 22 high schools, to which must be added 65 private schools.

### Higher education

The [University of Nice Sophia Antipolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nice_Sophia_Antipolis), in Nice and neighbouring areas, was founded in 1965. In 2019 it was merged into the new [Université Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A9_C%C3%B4te_d'Azur).

Higher education is relatively underdeveloped in the department. The urban area of Nice has 35,000 students,[^cite_note-25] while [Rennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rennes) and [Bordeaux](4097.md) each have 60,000.

A campus of the [Paris Institute of Political Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Institute_of_Political_Studies) (Sciences Po) was established in [Menton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menton) in October 2005. The Menton campus is dedicated to the relationship between the northern and southern shores of the Mediterranean and the relationship of Europe with the Middle East.

Several engineering schools are also located in [Sophia Antipolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophia_Antipolis):

- [Eurecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurecom) (School of Engineering and Research Center in communication systems)

- [Mines ParisTech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mines_ParisTech)

- [Polytech Nice Sophia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytech_Nice_Sophia)

In addition, two major business schools are located in the region:

- [EDHEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDHEC) in Nice

- [Skema Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skema_Business_School) in Sophia Antipolis

There are the [French Institute for Research in Computer Science and Automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Institute_for_Research_in_Computer_Science_and_Automation) (INRIA) and the [French National Centre for Scientific Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_National_Centre_for_Scientific_Research) (CNRS) in Sophia Antipolis.
