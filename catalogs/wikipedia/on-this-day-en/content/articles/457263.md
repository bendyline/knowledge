**Nangarhar** (also spelled *Nangrahar*, *Ningrahar*, and *Ningarhar*, [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): د ننګرهار ولایت, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Pashto): *Da Nangarhār Wilāyat* and [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari_language): ولایت ننگرهار, romanized: *Wilāyat-e Nangarhār*) is one of the major eastern provinces of [Afghanistan](737.md) and serves as a key political, economic, and cultural gateway between Afghanistan and [Pakistan](23235.md). It borders Pakistan's [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) province to the east and south, while internally it is adjacent to the Afghan provinces of [Kunar](2159600.md), [Laghman](457257.md), [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_Province), and [Logar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logar_Province). The provincial capital is [Jalalabad](16274.md), a lowland city located along the [Kabul River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_River) that functions as the principal administrative, commercial, and educational center of the region.

Covering an area of approximately 7,700 square kilometers and hosting an estimated population of around 1.8 million people (as of 2023), Nangarhar is defined by its fertile river valleys, semi-arid plains, and the lower reaches of the [Spin Ghar mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_Ghar), which forms the natural frontier with Pakistan.[^cite_note-nps-3] The province's landscape is shaped by the [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_River), [Kunar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunar_River), and [Surkh Rod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surkhab_River) rivers, which support extensive agricultural production and sustain some of Afghanistan's most densely populated rural districts.[^cite_note-npp-4]

Historically part of the greater eastern Afghan cultural sphere, Nangarhar has long been an important crossroads linking [Central Asia](6742.md), [South Asia](21566765.md), and the [Iranian Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Plateau). The region has served as a strategic corridor since antiquity, positioned along ancient routes connecting [Kabul](16826.md) with [Peshawar](66230.md). Jalalabad and its surroundings have hosted various dynasties, including the [Timurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_dynasty), [Mughals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire), and the early Afghan state under the [Durrani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire). The province is also home to the ancient Buddhist complex of [Hadda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadda,_Afghanistan), one of Afghanistan's most significant archaeological sites, which flourished as a center of [Greco-Buddhist art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Buddhist_art) and learning.[^cite_note-hadda-5]

In the modern era, Nangarhar remains a region of both strategic importance and complex social dynamics. Its proximity to the [border](203570.md), diverse [tribal landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_tribes), and major transit routes have shaped its political, economic, and security environment.[^cite_note-euaa-6] While the provincial center has experienced significant urban growth, rural districts face challenges related to infrastructure, governance, and economic development. Despite these disparities, Nangarhar retains a strong regional identity rooted in its historical heritage, agricultural traditions, and central role in cross-border exchange.

## Etymology

The name *Nangarhar* is derived from ancient regional and linguistic traditions and reflects the province's deep historical roots. The most widely accepted interpretation traces the name to the ancient term *[Nagarahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagarahara)* ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_language): नगर, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Sanskrit): *nagara*), which also appears in early [Brahmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi_script) sources related to the broader region of [Gandhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhara). In this context, *Nagara* is often associated with the idea of a settlement, city, or populated place, while *har* or *ghar* is interpreted as a geographic or territorial suffix, together suggesting a meaning close to 'land of settlements' or 'inhabited region'.[^cite_note-7] Some scholars have additionally proposed that the name could originate from the term *nava-vihara* ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_language): नव-विहार, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Sanskrit): *nava-vihāra*), meaning "nine [viharas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vihara)", reflecting the historical presence of Buddhist monastic centers in the region.[^cite_note-8]

## History

Further information: [History of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Afghanistan)

### Antiquity



The [Bimaran casket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bimaran_casket) is a small gold [reliquary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliquary) for [Buddhist](3267529.md) [relics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Aar%C4%ABra) from around the 1st century CE that was removed at [Bimaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bimaran), near [Jalalabad](16274.md) in eastern [Afghanistan](737.md)

Nangarhar has been inhabited for thousands of years and was an integral part of the ancient [Gandhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhara) civilization. Archaeological sites such as [Hadda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadda,_Afghanistan), [Bara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bara,_Punjab), and [Begram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begram) reveal early urban settlements with Buddhist monasteries, stupas, and extensive sculpture workshops dating back to at least the 1st millennium BCE.[^cite_note-hadda-5][^cite_note-buddhist-9] The region's fertile river valleys and strategic position along trade routes connecting the [Kabul Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_Valley) with the [Peshawar Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshawar_Valley) and [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation) facilitated commerce, cultural exchange, and the spread of [Buddhism](3267529.md).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-buddhist-9]



Relief sculpture from the archaeological site of [Hadda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadda,_Afghanistan) around the [2nd century CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_century_CE)

The province was historically influenced by several empires and cultures. During the Achaemenid period, it formed part of the eastern satrapies, providing revenue and troops to the empire. Following the conquest by [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great), Greek cultural influence spread, as evidenced by Hellenistic coins and inscriptions. After Alexander, the region fell under [Seleucid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire) and later [Mauryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire) control, promoting Buddhist institutions and integration into the broader South Asian cultural sphere.[^cite_note-11] Inscriptions in [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek), [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic), [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit), and [Brahmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi_script) highlight the multicultural and multilingual character of early Nangarhar. Archaeologists have found terracotta figurines, coins, and pottery showing trade links with [Central Asia](6742.md) and India.&#x20;

### Medieval period

During the early medieval era, Nangarhar came under successive Islamic dynasties, including the [Ghaznavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavid_Empire) and [Ghurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghurid_Empire).[^cite_note-Farishta-2-12][^cite_note-Houtsma-13] The region's cities, especially [Jalalabad](16274.md), served as commercial and administrative hubs connecting the Indian subcontinent with Central Asia.[^cite_note-ei-14] Islamic architecture, including mosques, caravanserais, and fortifications, expanded during this period, some of which remain today. Local Pashtun tribes preserved their autonomy in rural areas, while regional empires sought control over strategic trade routes.

The Mongol invasions of the 13th century under [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan) caused widespread devastation, destroying settlements and displacing populations. Under the [Timurid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire), Nangarhar's cities were rebuilt, fortifications strengthened, and trade networks reestablished.[^cite_note-ei-14][^cite_note-15] Timurids appointed governors from trusted family members to maintain local authority. The province remained a key junction for trade, culture, and military campaigns, linking Kabul, Peshawar, and the wider Indian subcontinent.

### Early modern period



[Wazir Akbar Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazir_Akbar_Khan) during the [Battle of Jalalabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jalalabad_\(1842\)) in 1842

From the 16th century onward, Nangarhar was at the center of repeated conflicts between regional powers. The province was contested between the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) and the [Safavid dynasty of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty), reflecting both strategic and religious rivalries between [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) and [Shia](26961.md) rulers.[^cite_note-mughal-16][^cite_note-17] During this period, fortifications were rebuilt multiple times to withstand sieges, and the province functioned as a provincial administrative and military center.



A village in [Jalalabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalalabad_District) in 1879

In 1709, local leaders successfully rebelled against the Safavids, establishing semi-independent rule and consolidating authority over eastern Afghan territories. Nangarhar later became part of the [Durrani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire) under [Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Durrani), who established the empire in 1747. The province's fertile valleys and strategic location along trade and military routes made it a critical part of the eastern administration of the empire. Its strategic location also made it a site of contest during the [First Anglo-Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Afghan_War) (1839–1842) and the [Second Anglo-Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Afghan_War) (1878–1880), when British forces briefly occupied parts of eastern Afghanistan, including key routes through Nangarhar, to secure their interests in India and counter Russian influence.[^cite_note-caseof-18]

Throughout the 19th and early 20th centuries, Nangarhar continued to serve as a commercial and cultural hub, benefiting from fertile valleys, river access, and long-established trade networks. Infrastructure, agriculture, and local governance expanded under various Afghan administrations, though periodic tribal conflicts and regional instability constrained growth and development.[^cite_note-caseof-18]

### During war times (1979–2021)



An Afghan [Humvee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humvee) next to the [Kabul–Jalalabad Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul%E2%80%93Jalalabad_Road) (2010)

The [Soviet invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Afghanistan) in 1979 triggered decades of conflict in Nangarhar. The province became a stronghold for [mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen), with its mountains and valleys like in [Tora Bora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tora_Bora) providing natural defense and access to supply routes from [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-19] Multiple local commanders, including influential tribal leaders, organized resistance against Soviet and later Afghan communist forces. After the Soviet withdrawal in 1989, Nangarhar was controlled by competing warlords and local militias.[^cite_note-20] The province was strategically important for both [Taliban](30635.md) and [al-Qaeda](1921.md) networks during the 1990s due to its border with Pakistan and the Khyber Pass.[^cite_note-21] Various insurgent bases and training camps were established in the region, making it a focal point of military and intelligence operations.

Following the [United States invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Afghanistan) in 2001, Nangarhar was among the first provinces to experience coalition military operations. U.S. and allied forces targeted insurgent networks while working to rebuild infrastructure and establish local governance. Nevertheless, insurgent activity persisted, and the province remained contested throughout the 2000s and 2010s.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-Bangladeshis,_Indians_among_militants_killed_by_MOAB-23][^cite_note-24]

### Today (2021–)

In August 2021, as part of the [U.S. withdrawal from Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._withdrawal_from_Afghanistan), the Taliban advanced rapidly across eastern Afghanistan and captured Jalalabad along with surrounding districts.[^cite_note-25] With the fall of Nangarhar, the Taliban gained control over key transport routes linking Afghanistan to Pakistan, including roads towards the [Khyber Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pass).[^cite_note-26] Since returning to power, the Taliban have issued multiple edicts imposing severe restrictions on women and girls, including bans on secondary education beyond a certain grade, limitations on employment, travel, and public life, and strict dress and guardianship rules.[^cite_note-27] Consequently, Nangarhar remains under Taliban control while continuing to occupy a strategic and economic position, owing to its fertile valleys, historic trade routes, and geographic proximity to the border with Pakistan and the Khyber Pass, thus preserving its long‑standing role as a cultural and geographic crossroads of eastern Afghanistan.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

## Geography

### Landscape



View on the [Spin Ghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_Ghar) from Jalalabad

Nangarhar is located in eastern Afghanistan and shares borders with [Kunar](2159600.md) and [Laghman](457257.md) to the north, the [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) region of [Pakistan](23235.md) to the east and south, and [Paktia](457274.md) to the west. The province covers a mixture of fertile river valleys, arid plains, and mountainous terrain. The most prominent valley is the one near [Jalalabad](16274.md), through which the [Kabul River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_River) flows, supporting the majority of agricultural production and human settlement. Smaller valleys branch from the main river basin, providing additional arable land and linking the mountainous areas to the plains.[^cite_note-npp-4]

The mountains in the province are primarily extensions of the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush) and [Spin Ghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_Ghar) ranges, forming natural barriers along the border with Pakistan.[^cite_note-nps-3] These mountains include peaks reaching over 3,000 m and are interspersed with lower hills and rocky outcrops, which create natural districts and influence settlement patterns. The region's terrain has historically shaped trade routes and military movements due to the strategic passes and rugged corridors.

### Flora and fauna



[Geckos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gecko) are widely found in Nangarhar

Nangarhar's vegetation varies from fertile riverine lands to arid plains and rocky mountain slopes. In the Jalalabad valley and along irrigated riverbanks, crops such as wheat, rice, [pomegranate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranate), [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape), and vegetables are cultivated extensively. Orchards and gardens flourish in irrigated zones, supporting local diets and commerce. Semi-arid and mountainous areas are covered by shrubs, grasses, and scattered trees like [acacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia) and [tamarisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamarisk).[^cite_note-31] Wildlife includes foxes, jackals, wild goats, and various hare, amphibia, and lizard species, while birds such as partridges, doves, and migratory waterfowl inhabit rivers, wetlands, and cultivated fields.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] The diverse habitats created by valleys, rivers, and hills allow for localized biodiversity, though human activity and limited water resources affect the abundance of wildlife.[^cite_note-35]

### Climate

Nangarhar experiences a variety of climate types, with most areas classified as [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) (BSh) and [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) (BWh), while smaller regions exhibit [hot-summer Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean) (Csa), cold and warm [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) (Dsa, Dsb), [cold semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_semi-arid_climate) (BSk), and [warm-summer Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm-summer_Mediterranean) (Csb) climates.[^cite_note-36] Summers in valleys often exceed 40 °C, while mountainous regions remain cooler. Winter temperatures in low-lying areas generally range from 5–15 °C. Annual rainfall is low, averaging 200–400 mm, mostly in winter and early spring, with seasonal snow in the highlands contributing to river flow and irrigation. Irrigation and river valleys create localized microclimates supporting agriculture. Nangarhar is also prone to floods and periodic droughts, which periodically affect agriculture and settlements.[^cite_note-mdpi-37][^cite_note-fao-38]

## Government and politics

Further information: [Politics of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Afghanistan)

### Local governance

Further information: [List of governors of Nangarhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Nangarhar)



Local elders gather at a local police station during a traditional *[jirga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jirga)* at [Hesarak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesarak_District), Nangarhar

Local governance in Nangarhar has historically been shaped by its position as eastern Afghanistan's primary gateway to Pakistan, its dense population, and the strong political influence of powerful Pashtun tribes such as the Shinwari, Mohmand, and Khogyani. Unlike many more remote provinces, Nangarhar has long been closely integrated into national trade, border administration, and security policy due to the strategic importance of the [Khyber Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pass) and the [Torkham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torkham) border crossing.[^cite_note-39]

Throughout the 20th century, governance in Nangarhar was nominally exercised through governors appointed by Kabul.[^cite_note-40] However, in practice, local tribal *[khans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_\(title\))*, *maliks* and other informal authorities often held significant influence, and administration was shaped by Jalalabad's historical role as a trade and border city.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

During the period following the Soviet withdrawal and throughout the Afghan civil wars, governance fragmented among mujahideen factions and tribal power holders, while parts of Achin, Kot, and Deh Bala intermittently fell outside effective provincial control. After 2001, the Islamic Republic re-established provincial institutions, but governance remained heavily affected by corruption, contested land ownership, drug trafficking routes, and the presence of militant groups including the Taliban and [IS-K](48029263.md).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-caseof-18]

Since the [Fall of Kabul](68481047.md), governance in Nangarhar has been fully integrated into the administrative system of the [Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan). The governor and all district officials are appointed directly by Taliban leadership. Civil administration, courts, customs revenue at Torkham, and border enforcement are now managed under Taliban structures.[^cite_note-45] Despite centralized control, informal influence by tribal elders, religious authorities, and cross-border commercial networks remains significant, especially in rural districts.[^cite_note-caseof-18]

As of December 2025, the governor of Nangarhar is [Haji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haji) [Gul Mohammad Barich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naim_Kuchi).[^cite_note-mei-1]

### Administrative divisions



Map of the districts of Nangarhar as of January 2004, prior to the redrawing of provincial and district boundaries later that year

Nangarhar is divided into 22 districts, each led by a district chief responsible for local administration and coordination with the provincial government.[^cite_note-nps-3] The provincial capital, [Jalalabad](16274.md), serves as the political, economic, and administrative hub of the province. Other notable districts include [Behsud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behsud_District), [Hesarak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesarak_District), [Achin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achin_District), and [Rodat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodat_District).

### Security



Members of an [Afghan Local Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Local_Police) in Nangarhar (2013)

Nangarhar's security situation is heavily influenced by its strategic location along the [Durand Line](203570.md) with [Pakistan](23235.md) and by complex tribal dynamics.[^cite_note-understandingwar-72] During the era of the [Islamic Republic of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Afghanistan), the province was a key site for counter‑insurgency operations by Afghan and international forces.[^cite_note-understandingwar-72] Since the 2021 Taliban takeover, the province is formally under Taliban control, who maintain order particularly in urban centers such as Jalalabad, while many rural districts continue to experience sporadic insurgent activity, criminal networks, and localized conflict.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] Meanwhile, informal local power actors, such as tribal elders, religious leaders, and community councils, continue to play a role in mediating disputes and influencing local law enforcement and governance.[^cite_note-75]

## Economy

Nangarhar is one of eastern Afghanistan's most economically significant provinces due to its fertile river valleys, direct access to [Pakistan](23235.md) through the [Torkham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torkham) border crossing, and its role as a regional commercial hub centered on [Jalalabad](16274.md).[^cite_note-76] The provincial economy is based on a combination of [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), cross-border [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade), small-scale [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_\(manufacturing\)), and [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport) services, with strong economic linkages to the Pakistani economy.[^cite_note-77]

### Agriculture and animal husbandry



Agriculture is the main source of income in Nangarhar

Agriculture forms the backbone of Nangarhar's rural economy, particularly in the fertile valleys of the [Kabul River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_River) and its tributaries.[^cite_note-agr-78] The province is one of Afghanistan's leading producers of [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), [vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetables), and [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus) fruits, especially [lemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemon), [oranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(fruit\)), [olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive), [peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut) and [dates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm) from the Jalalabad area.[^cite_note-agr-78][^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] Irrigated farming dominates in districts such as [Surkh Rod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surkh_Rod_District), [Behsud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behsud_District), [Kama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_District), and [Kuz Kunar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuz_Kunar_District), while rain-fed cultivation is more common in mountainous districts such as [Deh Bala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dih_Bala_district) and [Pachir wa Agam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachir_wa_Agam_District). Nangarhar also plays an important role in Afghanistan's [horticulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horticulture), with widespread production of [pomegranates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranate), [olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive), and [almonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond).[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

Livestock breeding in Nangarhar commonly includes [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle), [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep) and [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat), supplying both subsistence needs and markets.[^cite_note-npp-4] Until recently, parts of southern and mountainous Nangarhar were affected by [opium‑poppy cultivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_production_in_Afghanistan), but after a 2022 ban by the Taliban, poppy farming dropped sharply, and many farmers reportedly shifted toward legal crops.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

### Mining and industry



[Diopside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diopside) found in [Khogyani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khogyani_District)

Nangarhar possesses a wide range of exploitable [mineral resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_resource), although most extraction remains small-scale or semi-industrial. The province is particularly known for deposits of [jasper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasper), [gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum), [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble), [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), and [nephrite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nephrite),[^cite_note-86] as well as various [gemstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemstone) and rare-metal bearing [pegmatite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegmatite) minerals, which include [beryllium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryllium)-, [lithium](17561.md)-, [tantalum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantalum)-, and [tourmaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourmaline)-bearing formations.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] These mineral deposits are mainly concentrated in the mountainous districts of eastern and southern Nangarhar, especially in areas such as [Achin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achin_District), Deh Bala, and [Pachir wa Agam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pachir_wa_Agam_District).[^cite_note-talc-89] Marble and limestone extraction support the local [construction industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_industry) and cross-border export to [Pakistan](23235.md). [Talc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talc) and [chromite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromite) have historically played an important role in regional mineral trade networks.[^cite_note-talc-89]

[Industrial activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_activity) is concentrated around [Jalalabad](16274.md), where food processing, [flour milling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flour_mill), [edible oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edible_oil) production, [brick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick) kilns, [plastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic) manufacturing, [textile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile) workshops, and [beverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverage) bottling facilities operate.[^cite_note-90] The province also hosts one of Afghanistan's largest industrial zones, the [Nangarhar Industrial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nangarhar_Industrial_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1), which accommodates dozens of medium-sized manufacturing and processing enterprises. However, unstable [electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power) supply, limited access to [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(economics\)), and weak transport logistics continue to restrict large-scale industrial development.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

### Trade



A local vendor selling fresh fruits at the [Torkham border crossing](15844053.md)

[Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade) is the most dynamic sector of Nangarhar's economy due to the strategic importance of the [Torkham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torkham) border crossing, which connects Afghanistan to Pakistan's [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md).[^cite_note-93] A significant share of Afghanistan's [imports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Import), including [fuel](21188370.md), [food products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_product), [construction materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_material), [pharmaceutical drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical_drug), and [consumer goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_good), passes through Nangarhar.[^cite_note-94] [Jalalabad](16274.md) functions as a major [wholesale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wholesale) and redistribution center for eastern and northeastern Afghanistan. Informal cross-border trade and [smuggling](28645.md) have historically played a major economic role, particularly in fuel, [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics), and [agricultural commodities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodity_\(type_of_good\)). [Customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs) revenue from Torkham represents one of the most important income sources for both provincial and central authorities under successive Afghan governments.[^cite_note-95]

### Energy and irrigation



An irrigated field in Nangarhar

Nangarhar's irrigation system is primarily based on water from the [Kabul River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_River) via the [Darunta Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darunta_Dam), which provides irrigation water and hydroelectric power for Jalalabad and surrounding areas.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] The province historically supported up to 39,000 ha under formal irrigation schemes.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] In practice, however, electricity supply remains unreliable, as many households and businesses are dependent on generators or alternative means. It was especially reported when power from the dam proved insufficient.[^cite_note-100] While canal networks distribute water to agricultural zones, recurring maintenance problems, siltation and drought or flood‑induced damage continue to challenge irrigation and agricultural productivity in rural districts.[^cite_note-mdpi-37][^cite_note-fao-38]

### Tourism

Nangarhar is often cited for its scenic valleys, mild winter climate and river landscapes around [Jalalabad](16274.md) and the [Darunta Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darunta_Dam). Historically, Jalalabad has long-served as a winter retreat for the Kabul elite.[^cite_note-ei-14][^cite_note-101] Domestic tourism, especially during holidays like Eid, has reportedly increased, with many Afghans visiting green areas, historical sites and mountainous districts such as [Spin Ghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_Ghar).[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] However, international tourism remains minimal, and formal accommodation or tourism‑infrastructure is still largely underdeveloped.[^cite_note-105]

### Communication



Antennae to receive TV channels on a roof in Jalalabad

Telecommunications infrastructure in parts of Nangarhar, especially around urban areas like Jalalabad, has traditionally included mobile‑network coverage and internet access, enabling communication and media consumption.[^cite_note-106] However, since 2021 the situation has become unstable, as fibre‑optic and WiFi internet access has been repeatedly shut down by the government, and media‑licenses for several outlets in Nangarhar have been revoked, restricting independent media and broadcasting.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108]

### Transportation and infrastructure



Typical street in Jalalabad

Nangarhar's transportation network is anchored by the [Kabul–Jalalabad Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul%E2%80%93Jalalabad_Road), which leads to the [Torkham border crossing](15844053.md), Afghanistan's primary corridor to Pakistan.[^cite_note-109] This route carries the bulk of the transit and [freight traffic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight_traffic) of eastern Afghanistan.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111] Secondary roads, improved in recent years with international assistance, now connect Jalalabad with many districts, facilitating transport of agricultural products and goods.[^cite_note-112] However, despite these developments, many rural roads remain in fragile condition and prone to disruption by floods or heavy rains, which periodically interrupts transport and trade flows.[^cite_note-113]

## Demographics

Further information: [Demographics of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Afghanistan)

### Population



Local boys in [Achin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achin_District) (2011)

As of 2023, Nangarhar has an estimated population of approximately 1.8 million people, distributed across urban centers, towns, and rural villages.[^cite_note-nsia-46] The provincial capital [Jalalabad](16274.md) contains the largest share of the urban population, followed by district centers such as [Kama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_District) and [Surkh Rod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surkh_Rod_District). The majority of the population lives in rural areas, where livelihoods depend largely on [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) and cross-border trade. Poverty remains widespread, particularly in mountainous districts, with a [multidimensional poverty index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multidimensional_Poverty_Index) of 0.266 and 23.0% of the population living in [severe poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_poverty) as of 2023.[^cite_note-ophi-114] Many households rely on small-scale farming, casual labor, or remittances. Rapid population growth, seasonal displacement, and return migration have placed additional strain on housing, infrastructure, and public services.[^cite_note-115]

### Ethnicity, languages and religion

The population of Nangarhar is ethnically diverse but overwhelmingly [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns), who make up the clear majority of the province's inhabitants.[^cite_note-nps-3][^cite_note-understandingwar-72] Prominent Pashtun tribal confederations include both [Ghilzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghilji) and [Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani), alongside numerous smaller tribal groupings. Significant minorities include the [Pashai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashayi_people), especially in eastern and northeastern mountain districts, as well as smaller communities of [Tajiks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajiks), [Arabs](2185.md), and other groups.[^cite_note-understandingwar-72] [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto) is the dominant language and serves as the main *[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca)* of the province, while [Pashayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashayi_languages) is widely spoken in eastern highland districts.[^cite_note-116] [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari) is used in administration, education, and interprovincial communication. Many residents are bilingual or multilingual due to long-standing trade and migration links with the [Kabul Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_Valley) and [Khyber Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pass) region. The population is overwhelmingly [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam).[^cite_note-nps-3] Small minority communities of other religious groups formerly existed in urban areas, but their presence has declined significantly due to decades of conflict and emigration. Religious life remains closely tied to local [mosques](19894.md), *[madrasas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasa)*, and tribal traditions.[^cite_note-nps-3]

### Education

Further information: [Education in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Afghanistan), [List of universities in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Afghanistan), and [List of schools in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Afghanistan)



Local school girls in Nangarhar

[Nangarhar University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nangarhar_University) is located in the provincial capital, [Jalalabad](16274.md), and is one of the largest public universities in eastern Afghanistan. It provides higher education to several thousand students in fields such as medicine, agriculture, engineering, economics, and Islamic studies. Primary and secondary education is provided through a network of government and community-based [schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Afghanistan) distributed across the province.[^cite_note-130] Educational access varies widely between urban and rural districts, with mountainous areas facing shortages of school buildings, trained teachers, and learning materials. Literacy rates remain low compared to national and regional averages, particularly among women, with the most recent estimates from 2011 indicating an overall literacy rate of 31% and an overall net enrolment rate for school-age children of approximately 51%.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-131]

Since 2021, educational policy has been reshaped under the [Taliban](30635.md) administration.[^cite_note-132] While male education continues across all levels, female access to [secondary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education) and [higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education) has been severely restricted.[^cite_note-133] At the same time, the number of religious *madrasas* has expanded throughout the province.[^cite_note-134] [NGO](46539.md)-supported literacy and vocational programs operate in limited form, mainly focusing on basic skills and humanitarian education.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

### Health

Further information: [Health in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Afghanistan) and [Healthcare in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Afghanistan)



Doctors in the local hospital of [Kama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_District) (2011)

Healthcare services in Nangarhar are concentrated primarily in [Jalalabad](16274.md), where the main provincial and regional hospitals are located, including the [Nangarhar Regional Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nangarhar_Regional_Hospital?action=edit\&redlink=1). In addition to public facilities, a number of private clinics and pharmacies operate in urban districts.[^cite_note-137] Rural populations often depend on small health posts or must travel long distances for treatment.[^cite_note-138] Major public health challenges include [maternal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_death) and [infant mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality), [malnutrition](258979.md), limited access to [clean drinking water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_drinking_water), and outbreaks of [infectious diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_disease), with the most recent available estimates from 2011 indicating that 8% of households had access to clean drinking water and 60% of births were attended by a skilled birth attendant.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-131] Healthcare delivery is supported by [NGOs](46539.md) and international humanitarian organizations, which provide vaccinations, emergency treatment, and basic medical services, especially in underserved districts.[^cite_note-139] Ongoing economic difficulties, medical staff shortages, and restricted funding continue to challenge the long-term development of the provincial health system.[^cite_note-140]

## Culture

### Music and dances

Traditional music and dance in Nangarhar are closely linked to eastern [Pashtun tribal culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_tribal_culture). The *[attan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attan)* is the most widely performed communal dance, traditionally featured at weddings and tribal celebrations, and often accompanied by instruments such as the *[dhol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhol)* and *[rubab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubab_\(instrument\))*.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142] Folk songs and performances commonly reflect [Pashtun cultural themes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_culture), including community life, seasonal events, and tribal identity.[^cite_note-143] Public performances of music and dance have largely disappeared under Taliban rule, and many artists report that venues are closed or fear reprisals.[^cite_note-144]

### Dresses and attire



Nangarhari men in typical Afghan attire

In Nangarhar, as in much of Pashtun regions of Afghanistan, traditional clothing remains common.[^cite_note-145] Men typically wear a *[perahan o tunban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perahan_o_tunban)*, often paired with a *[turban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turban)*, *[pakol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakol)*, or *[shawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawl)*.[^cite_note-146] Women often wear long, loose dresses over wide trousers, sometimes with colourful embroidery and head scarves. For special occasions like weddings or festivals, more ornate outfits are used, with decorative embroidery and bright fabrics.[^cite_note-147]

### Cuisine

Nangarhar's cuisine is shaped by its fertile river valleys and agricultural production. [Wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) bread, [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_and_mutton), and [dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_product) form the basis of daily meals.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] Signature dishes include *[qabeli palaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qabeli_palaw)*, *[qormas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korma)* with vegetables or meat, grilled [kababs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kababs), and local specialties based on fish and [karahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karahi) dishes.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151] Tea, especially [green tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_tea), is consumed throughout the day. Seasonal fruits such as [pomegranates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranates), [apples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apples), and [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grapes) complement meals and are commonly served during festive occasions.[^cite_note-npp-4][^cite_note-152]

### Architecture, art, and literature



A child weaving an embroidered [cot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_bed) (2011)

Nangarhar's traditional architecture adapts to its river valley plains and surrounding hills. Homes are built from [mudbrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudbrick), stone, and timber, often with flat or gently sloped roofs.[^cite_note-153] Courtyards are common, allowing for family gatherings and livestock shelter. Villages often cluster along rivers or roads for access and defense. [Mosques](19894.md) serve as religious and social centers.[^cite_note-154] Local crafts include [carpet weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpet_weaving), [embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery), wood carving, pottery, and traditional jewelry.[^cite_note-155] Oral literature, including [Pashto poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_poetry), epic storytelling, and folk tales, remains central, with elders and local poets preserving histories and tribal genealogies through recitations during social and religious events.[^cite_note-156]

### Media, entertainment, and festivities

Radio remains an important source of information for many people in rural areas of Nangarhar, while access to television and mobile internet tends to be stronger in urban centers such as Jalalabad and other major towns, although overall media freedom and content diversity have been heavily restricted since 2021 under Taliban rule.[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158] Restrictions include the suspension of broadcast licenses for local stations and new directives limiting content that can be aired, which has led to reduced programming and increased self-censorship by journalists and outlets.[^cite_note-159]

Cultural celebrations such as [Eid al-Fitr](154944.md) and [Eid al-Adha](358226.md), as well as life-cycle events like weddings and other family gatherings, have traditionally played a central role in communal life across Afghanistan, bringing together relatives and neighbours for shared meals, rituals, and social interaction. Although public forms of entertainment and music have been curtailed by the current authorities, many families continue to celebrate these events within private settings, and some cultural practices persist informally despite official restrictions.[^cite_note-160]

### Places of interest



Mausoleum of [Amanullah Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanullah_Khan)

Nangarhar contains a number of cultural and natural points of interest. In [Jalalabad](16274.md), historic *[bazaars](556904.md)*, mosques, and older residential quarters illustrate the city's long-established urban landscape, with notable landmarks like the [mausoleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum) of Amanullah Khan, the *Bahraabad* [stupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stupa), and the *Las Pa Las Baba* [shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrine).[^cite_note-arch1-161][^cite_note-arch2-162] The province also features additional religious shrines and Sufi sites, as well as natural areas such as the [Sardeh Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardeh_Band) reservoir, the [Khyber Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khyber_Pass) and its surrounding foothills, and the vicinity of the [Spin Ghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_Ghar) mountain range, the [Darunta Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darunta_Dam) and the [Kabul River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_River), which are used for local visits and seasonal activities.[^cite_note-163] Archaeological surveys have documented previously unrecorded historical sites across the province, including ancient structural remains and settlement traces dating to the pre-Islamic and [Kushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire) periods.[^cite_note-arch1-161][^cite_note-arch2-162]

### Sports

Further information: [Sport in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Afghanistan)



The Sherzai Cricket Stadium under construction in June 2011

In recent decades, [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_in_Afghanistan) has become the most popular modern sports in Nangarhar, especially among youth, and [Jalalabad](16274.md) is widely viewed as the center of Afghan cricket development.[^cite_note-icc-164][^cite_note-165] Many prominent [national team players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_national_cricket_team), including [Rashid Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Khan), have roots in the province.[^cite_note-icc-164] In national cricket competitions such as the [Shpageeza Cricket League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shpageeza_Cricket_League), Nangarhar is represented by the [Speenghar Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speenghar_Tigers) franchise, which covers the eastern provinces of the country. The [Ghazi Amanullah International Cricket Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazi_Amanullah_International_Cricket_Stadium), located in [Ghazi Amanullah Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazi_Amanullah_Town) near Jalalabad, was Afghanistan's first international-standard cricket stadium, with a seating capacity of 14,000, making it the largest stadium in the country at the time of its inauguration.[^cite_note-PAN-166] Subsequently, the *[Najeeb Tarakai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najeeb_Tarakai) Cricket Ground* was constructed in Jalalabad, making Nangarhar the first province to feature two cricket stadiums.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168]



Local men playing [basketball](3921.md) in their freetime

[Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Afghanistan) is also widely played in Nangarhar. During the period of the [Islamic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Afghanistan), the province was represented in national competitions by [De Spin Ghar Bazan FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Spin_Ghar_Bazan_FC), which served as the regional team for Nangarhar alongside [Laghman](457257.md), [Kapisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapisa_Province), [Kunar](2159600.md), and [Nuristan](457267.md) within the [Afghan Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Premier_League). Other modern sports practiced in the province include [volleyball](32558.md) and [basketball](3921.md), which are commonly played due to minimal equipment requirements. Private gyms in urban areas provide facilities for [boxing](4243.md), [taekwondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taekwondo), and general fitness training. Traditional sports continue to play a role in cultural life, particularly local forms of wrestling and horse riding, which are closely associated with community festivals, tribal prestige, and social gatherings.[^cite_note-169]
