**Meningococcal vaccine** refers to any [vaccine](32653.md) used to prevent infection by *[Neisseria meningitidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neisseria_meningitidis)*.[^cite_note-WHO2011-15] Different versions are effective against some or all of the following types of meningococcus: A, B, C, W-135, and Y.[^cite_note-WHO2011-15][^cite_note-FDA2014-16] The vaccines are between 85 and 100% effective for at least two years.[^cite_note-WHO2011-15] They result in a decrease in [meningitis](21009963.md) and [sepsis](158400.md) among populations where they are widely used.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] They are given either by [injection into a muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramuscular) or [just under the skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subcutaneous_injection).[^cite_note-WHO2011-15]

The [World Health Organization](33583.md) recommends that countries with a moderate or high rate of disease or with frequent outbreaks should [routinely vaccinate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccination_schedule).[^cite_note-WHO2011-15][^cite_note-WHO2015A-19] In countries with a low risk of disease, they recommend that high-risk groups should be immunized.[^cite_note-WHO2011-15] In the [African meningitis belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_meningitis_belt) efforts to immunize all people between the ages of one and thirty with the meningococcal A [conjugate vaccine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjugate_vaccine) are ongoing.[^cite_note-WHO2015A-19] In [Canada](5042916.md) and the [United States](3434750.md), vaccines against four types of meningococcus (A, C, W, and Y) are recommended routinely for teenagers and others who are at high risk.[^cite_note-WHO2011-15] Saudi Arabia requires vaccination with the quadrivalent vaccine for international travellers to [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) for [Hajj](23487999.md).[^cite_note-WHO2011-15][^cite_note-20]

Meningococcal vaccines are generally safe.[^cite_note-WHO2011-15] Some people develop pain and redness at the injection site.[^cite_note-WHO2011-15] Use in pregnancy appears to be safe.[^cite_note-WHO2015A-19] [Severe allergic reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaphylaxis) occur in less than one in a million doses.[^cite_note-WHO2011-15]

The first meningococcal vaccine became available in the 1970s.[^cite_note-21] It is on the [World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Model_List_of_Essential_Medicines).[^cite_note-WHO24th-22]

Inspired by the response to the 1997 outbreak in Nigeria, the WHO, [Médecins Sans Frontières](20498.md), and other groups created the International Coordinating Group on Vaccine Provision for Epidemic Meningitis Control, which manages global response strategy. ICGs have since been created for other epidemic diseases.[^cite_note-23]

## Types

*[Neisseria meningitidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neisseria_meningitidis)* has 13 clinically significant [serogroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotype), classified according to the antigenic structure of their polysaccharide capsule. Six serogroups, A, B, C, Y, W-135, and X, are responsible for virtually all cases in humans. Serogroup B is a major cause of [meningococcal disease](3868809.md) in younger children and adolescents.

#### Menactra and Menveo

The first meningococcal conjugate vaccine (MCV-4), Menactra, was licensed in the US in 2005, by [Sanofi Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanofi_Pasteur); Menveo was licensed in 2010, by [Novartis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novartis). Both MCV-4 vaccines are approved by the [Food and Drug Administration](11632.md) (FDA) for people 2 through 55 years of age. Menactra received FDA approval for use in children as young as 9 months in April 2011,[^cite_note-32] while Menveo received FDA approval for use in children as young as two months in August 2013.[^cite_note-33] The [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](6811.md) (CDC) has not made recommendations for or against its use in children less than two years.[^cite_note-cdc1-34] In November 2024, the [European Commission](9974.md) (EC) approved Menveo to protect individuals aged two years and older against [invasive meningococcal disease](3868809.md)**.**[^cite_note-35]

#### Menquadfi



MenQuadfi solution for injection

Menquadfi, manufactured by [Sanofi Pasteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanofi_Pasteur), was approved by the US [Food and Drug Administration](11632.md) in April 2020, for use in individuals two years of age and older.[^cite_note-FDA_Menquadfi-9]

#### Menomune

Meningococcal polysaccharide vaccine (MPSV-4), Menomune, has been available since the 1970s. It may be used if MCV-4 is not available, and is the only meningococcal vaccine licensed for people older than 55. Information about who should receive the meningococcal vaccine is available from the CDC.[^cite_note-cdc1-34]

#### Nimenrix



Nimenrix (Pfizer). Meningococcal group A, C, W-135, and Y conjugate vaccine

Nimenrix (developed by GlaxoSmithKline and later acquired by [Pfizer](62304.md)), is a quadrivalent conjugate vaccine against serogroups A, C, W-135, and Y.[^cite_note-Torresi2019-36] In April 2012 Nimenrix was approved as the first quadrivalent vaccine against invasive meningococcal disease to be administered as a single dose in those over the age of one year, by the European Medicines Agency.[^cite_note-Assaf2016-37] In 2016, they approved the vaccine in infants six weeks of age and older, and it has been approved in other countries including Canada and Australia, among others.[^cite_note-Nimenrix_EPAR-14][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] It is not licensed in the United States.[^cite_note-40]

#### Mencevax

Mencevax ([GlaxoSmithKline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GlaxoSmithKline)) and [NmVac4-A/C/Y/W-135](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NmVac4-A/C/Y/W-135) (JN-International Medical Corporation) are used worldwide but have not been licensed in the United States.

#### Limitations

The duration of immunity mediated by Menomune (MPSV-4) is three years or less in children aged under five because it does not generate [memory T cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_T_cell).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] Attempting to overcome this problem by repeated immunization results in a diminished, not increased, [antibody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibody) response, so boosters are not recommended with this vaccine.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] As with all polysaccharide vaccines, Menomune does not produce [mucosal immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mucosal_immunity), so people can still become colonised with virulent strains of meningococcus, and no [herd immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herd_immunity) can develop.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] For this reason, Menomune is suitable for travellers requiring short-term protection, but not for national public health prevention programs.

Menveo and Menactra contain the same antigens as Menomune, but the antigens are conjugated to a [diphtheria toxoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diphtheria_toxin) polysaccharide-protein complex, resulting in anticipated enhanced duration of protection, increased immunity with booster vaccinations, and effective herd immunity.[^cite_note-auto-47]

#### Endurance

A study published in March 2006, comparing the two kinds of vaccines found that 76% of subjects still had passive protection three years after receiving MCV-4 (63% protective compared with controls), but only 49% had passive protection after receiving MPSV-4 (31% protective compared with controls).[^cite_note-Vu_2006-48] As of 2010, there remains limited evidence that any of the current conjugate vaccines offer continued protection beyond three years; studies are ongoing to determine the actual duration of immunity, and the subsequent requirement of booster vaccinations. The CDC offers recommendations regarding who they feel should get booster vaccinations.[^cite_note-auto-47][^cite_note-49]

### Bivalent (serogroups C and Y)

In June 2012, the FDA approved a combination vaccine against two types of meningococcal disease and [Hib disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hib_disease) for infants and children 6 weeks to 18 months old. The vaccine, Menhibrix, prevents disease caused by *[Neisseria meningitidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neisseria_meningitidis)* [serogroups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotypes) C and Y and *[Haemophilus influenzae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haemophilus_influenzae)* type b. This was the first meningococcal vaccine that could be given to infants as young as six weeks old.[^cite_note-50] Menhibrix is [indicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indicated) for active immunization to prevent invasive disease caused by *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroups C and Y and *Haemophilus influenzae* type b for children 6 weeks of age through 18 months of age.[^cite_note-51]

### Serogroup A

A vaccine called [MenAfriVac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MenAfriVac) has been developed through a program called the [Meningitis Vaccine Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meningitis_Vaccine_Project) and has the potential to prevent outbreaks of group A meningitis, which is common in sub-Saharan Africa.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

### Serogroup B

Main article: [Meningitis B](82738811.md)



Meningococcal B vaccine

There are two licensed vaccines against serotype B meningococcal disease; BEXSERO (GSK) 4-component MenB vaccine (4CMenB) and Trumenba (Pfizer)[^cite_note-\:0-54], a bivalent factor H binding protein vaccine (MenB-FHbp). In the UK, the MenB vaccine (BEXSERO) is given routinely as part of childhood vaccines as infants are at greatest risk from meningitis B.[^cite_note-55] The protection offered is short (around 3 years). In the US, both vaccines are only approved for ages 10-25 and only given to certain risk groups.[^cite_note-\:0-54]

### Serogroup X

The occurrence of serogroup X has been reported in North America, Europe, Australia, and West Africa.[^cite_note-Gagneux_2002-56] Until recently, there was no vaccine to protect against serogroup X *N. meningitidis* disease.[^cite_note-WHO2011-15]

However, a new pentavalent vaccine that protects against serogroups A, C, W, X and Y (MenFive; Serum Institute of India) has become available and has been recommended by the WHO in 2023 for use in endemic countries in Africa[^cite_note-57] In 2024, Nigeria became the first country in the world to roll out the new vaccine[^cite_note-58]

## Side effects

Common side effects include pain and redness around the site of injection (up to 50% of recipients). A small percentage of people develop a mild fever. A small proportion of people develop a severe allergic reaction.[^cite_note-59] In 2016 Health Canada warned of an increased risk of anemia or [hemolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemolysis) in people treated with [eculizumab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eculizumab) (Soliris). The highest risk was when individuals "received a dose of Soliris within 2 weeks after being vaccinated with Bexsero".[^cite_note-60]

Despite initial concerns about [Guillain-Barré syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillain-Barr%C3%A9_syndrome), subsequent studies in 2012 have shown no increased risk of GBS after meningococcal conjugate vaccination.[^cite_note-61]

Travellers who wish to enter or leave certain countries or territories must be vaccinated against meningococcal meningitis, preferably 10–14 days before crossing the border, and be able to present a vaccination record/certificate at the border checks.[^cite_note-WHO_Travel_and_Health-62]<sup>: 21–24 </sup> Countries with required meningococcal vaccination for travellers include [The Gambia](65656.md), Indonesia, Lebanon, Libya, the Philippines and, most importantly and extensively, Saudi Arabia for Muslims visiting or working in [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca) during the [Hajj](23487999.md) or [Umrah](1591421.md) pilgrimages.[^cite_note-WHO_country_list-63] For some countries in [African meningitis belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_meningitis_belt), vaccinations prior to entry are not required, but highly recommended.[^cite_note-WHO_Travel_and_Health-62]<sup>: 21–24 </sup>
