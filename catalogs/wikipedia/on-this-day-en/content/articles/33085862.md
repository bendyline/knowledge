**Corruption in Ukraine** since [the country became an independent state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_Ukraine) in 1991 is a [societal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Societal) issue rooted in the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) that year.[^cite_note-1] Following independence, Ukrainian politicians from across the political spectrum, criminal bosses, and [Ukrainian oligarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_oligarchs) exploited [corruption](3608404.md) within the [National Police of Ukraine](44656231.md), [the country's political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_parties_in_Ukraine), and [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_in_Ukraine) to gain power.[^cite_note-2] While Ukraine is still considered one of the more corrupt countries in Europe, it has made steady progress in improving its standing since 2015.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-Guardian-Ukraine-Corruption-4]

## History

The modern period of [corruption](3608404.md) in Ukraine can be traced back to when it formed part of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), prior to 1991. During the 1980s, individuals linked to [Soviet organised crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thief_in_law) became integrated into the [Soviet ruling elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomenklatura).[^cite_note-5] The American academic [Louise Shelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Shelley), writing in 1998, commented that corruption in Ukraine followed similar patterns to organized crime and political parties across the post-Soviet space.[^cite_note-She-6]

For two decades following the establishment of [Ukraine as an independent state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_Ukraine) in 1991, the country experienced a period of violent corruption.



[Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych) in April 2010

During this period, corruption in industry and tourism was particularly acute in [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md).[^cite_note-7] In 2005, [mass graves](1349803.md) containing businesspersons, judges, lawyers, and investigators were discovered in the region.[^cite_note-8] A former governor, [Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych), and his [Party of Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Regions) were among those accused of maintaining close ties to [organised crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_mafia).[^cite_note-9] Despite this, Yanukovych won the [2010 Ukrainian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Ukrainian_presidential_election).

According to the [United States Agency for International Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Agency_for_International_Development) (USAID) in 2006, the main causes of corruption in Ukraine included a compromised [justice system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_system_of_Ukraine), a centralised and non-transparent government, close ties between [business and political elites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_oligarchs) and Russia, and a weakened [civil society](209443.md).[^cite_note-USAID2006-10] Corruption has been a frequent topic of [Ukrainian media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_media) coverage.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-KPJune252009-12]

A 2013 survey of Ukrainians found the country's most corrupt officials were believed to be the [judiciary](1238343.md), the [police](44656231.md), public servants, the health service, and parliament.[^cite_note-UWBU9713-13] Yanukovych was overthrown during the [Maidan Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maidan_Uprising), which began in 2013 after his government—viewed by many of the protestors as corrupt—refused to sign a [trade agreement with the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_agreements_of_the_European_Union).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] Yanukovych's [pro-Russian](2541825.md) tendencies caused [Ukrainian nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_nationalism) to link corruption in Ukraine with the country's relationship with Russia.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] In the aftermath of the uprising, [law enforcement](11137543.md) reforms were introduced to reduce waste, public procurement procedures were changed, and some state-owned industries were dissolved, but critics argued that the progress made in tackling corruption was too limited.[^cite_note-23]

In 2008, [Transparency International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transparency_International) (TI) estimated that 30–50 percent of Ukrainians had experienced government corruption.[^cite_note-TI-GCP-2008-p225-24] Juhani Grossmann, when working in 2009 for the ACTION project (Promoting Active Citizen Engagement in Combating Corruption in Ukraine), which was funded by the USAID, claimed that "Ukrainians pay roughly ₴3.5 billion, or more than US\$400 million, in bribes annually."[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-KPJune252009-12] The previous year, he claimed that the figure was US\$700 million.[^cite_note-26]

In 2011, Yanukovych announced that corruption was costing his government an estimated US\$2.5 billion each year, and that corrupt practices in public [procurement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procurement) alone resulted in 10–15% of the state budget (approximately US\$7.4 billion) ending up "in the pockets of officials".[^cite_note-Yanusays4June2011-27] According to the [economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist) Ararat Osipian, Ukraine failed to sustain its [economic growth trends](31829.md#History) in the 2000s due to endemic corruption.[^cite_note-28] Corrupt practices marked Yanukovich's period as president; it contributed to [his removal from office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych#Removal_from_presidency) in 2014, and left the [Ukrainian Ground Forces](7433327.md) ill-equipped to counter [Russia's invasion of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) that year.[^cite_note-29]

In 2016, the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) (IMF) stated that a reduction in corruption was a key condition for continued international support for Ukraine.[^cite_note-reuters-20160518-30] Some Western analysts argued that large foreign [loans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan) were not encouraging reform, but instead were enabling the extraction of funds from the country through corrupt practices.[^cite_note-wp-20160505-31] The then U.S. Assistant Secretary of State, [Victoria Nuland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Nuland), called for the prosecution of corrupt officials, stating: "It's time to start locking up people who have ripped off the Ukrainian population for too long and it is time to eradicate the cancer of corruption."[^cite_note-voa-20160427-32]

A 2025 [Gallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallup,_Inc.) opinion poll found that 85% of Ukrainians believed corruption was widespread throughout their government, a figure that had hardly varied since 2007.[^cite_note-gallup-20250807-33]

## Bribery

In the 2000s, [bribes](260979.md) were used in Ukraine to ensure that public services were delivered,[^cite_note-corupt-34] with many Ukrainians considering their payment to be customary and expected.[^cite_note-corupt-34][^cite_note-Gfk2001-35] Some of the largest recorded bribes exceeded US\$1 million.[^cite_note-36]

In a 2001 survey conducted by the German [market research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_research) company [GfK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GfK), 43% of respondents stated that they had never personally paid a bribe.[^cite_note-Gfk2001-35]

According to a Management Systems International survey conducted in 2008, the highest levels of corruption were identified in [vehicle inspection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_inspection) services (58%), the police (54%), health care (54%), the courts (49%), and in higher education (44%).[^cite_note-TI-GCP-2008-p225-24] In another 2008 survey, 21% of those questioned answered that they—or someone living in their household—had paid a bribe during the previous year.[^cite_note-CorruptionBarometer2009p33-37] Surveys conducted between 2000 and 2010 showed that a majority of Ukrainians self-reported engaging in corrupt transactions with government authorities.[^cite_note-corupt-34][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-UkraineTCI2011p36-39][^cite_note-40]

In the [Global Corruption Barometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Corruption_Barometer) 2013 produced by TI, Ukrainians reported that the institutions to which they most commonly paid bribes during the preceding two years were the [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_police) (49% of responders), [medical and health services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Ukraine) (41%), and the [education system](5231751.md) (33%).[^cite_note-UWBU9713-13]

### National politics

In the years following Ukrainian independence, [electoral fraud](382165.md) was widespread, mainly through the [administrative resource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_resource) available to some candidates.[^cite_note-41] According to the British political analyst [Taras Kuzio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Kuzio), the fraud accounted to more than five percent of the total vote.[^cite_note-42]

[Pavlo Lazarenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlo_Lazarenko), who was the prime minister of Ukraine from 1996 to 1997, [allegedly embezzled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlo_Lazarenko#Corruption) between US\$114 million and 200 million,[^cite_note-43] After the [initial round of voting](25932.md) in the [2004 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Ukrainian_presidential_election), the [Supreme Court of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Ukraine) ruled that the scale of the electoral fraud made it impossible to determine the election results, and it ordered a revote.[^cite_note-Supr-44] Instances of [vote rigging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_rigging) have since declined,[^cite_note-45] although politicians continued to allege election fraud, and some political parties persisted in using tactics to secure votes,[^cite_note-46] and the Ukrainian electorate remained sceptical of the integrity of the electoral process.[^cite_note-47]

In the 2000s, [United States diplomats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Diplomatic_Service) claimed that the [privatization](24661.md) of several Ukrainian state enterprises was rigged by politicians in favour of their political allies.[^cite_note-USPrivatization-48] In the early 2010s [Ukrainian media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_in_Ukraine), particularly the *[Ukrayinska Pravda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrayinska_Pravda)*, regularly unveiled millionaire lifestyles of Ukrainian politicians and public servants,[^cite_note-49] utterly at odds with their declared official incomes.[^cite_note-novyi_golova_tsvk-50] In 2010, it was reported that Ukrainian politicians regularly accused each other of corruption while claiming to fight it themselves.[^cite_note-51] According to information revealed by means of the 2010 [United States diplomatic cables leak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_diplomatic_cables_leak), US officials described Ukraine under presidents [Leonid Kuchma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Kuchma) and [Viktor Yushchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yushchenko) as a [kleptocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleptocracy).[^cite_note-USPrivatization-48]

[Minister of Internal Affairs](1400321.md) [Vitaliy Zakharchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaliy_Zakharchenko) stated in 2012 that since 2010, about 400 politicians had faced criminal charges in connection with corruption; most of them from the Party of Regions, followed by [Bloc Yulia Tymoshenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloc_Yulia_Tymoshenko) and [Our Ukraine–People's Self-Defense Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Ukraine%E2%80%93People's_Self-Defense_Bloc) members.[^cite_note-52] Since 2011, the [President](20688479.md), [Chairman of the Verkhovna Rada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Verkhovna_Rada), Prime Minister, [Prosecutor General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosecutor_General_of_Ukraine), [ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Ministers_of_Ukraine) and other Ukrainian top officials have been liable for prosecution for corruption.[^cite_note-Reuters100611-53] Since 2010, the Ukrainian press brought up thousands of examples of criminal cases in which state officials, as well as politicians and businessmen linked to the then-ruling Party of Regions, were shown leniency unprecedented for the general population of suspects.[^cite_note-genprokuratura_ne_zahotila-54] After [going undercover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_operation) in the *[Reforms for the Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reforms_for_the_Future)* [parliamentary](844993.md) faction in early 2012, the Rada deputy [Roman Zabzaliuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Zabzaliuk) claimed this faction "bought" its members, paying US\$500,000 \[for a 'defection' from other parliamentary groups, and then paying a salary of \$20,000–25,000 a month. In contrast, according to Reforms for the Future, Zabzalyuk had pretended he was "suffering a very serious disease" and the group had managed to raise some \$100,000 for Zabzalyuk to undergo surgery in Israel.[^cite_note-Zabzalyuk-55]

A 2015 survey showed that 72% of adults blamed "corruption of power" for the lack of progress in reform.[^cite_note-nw-20160214-56] According to historian [Andrew Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Wilson_\(historian\)), as of 2016, progress in reducing corruption was poor.[^cite_note-nw-20160214-56] A 2016 requirement for [members of the Ukrainian parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Deputy_of_Ukraine) (deputies) to declare their wealth led to a declared cumulative wealth of about \$460 million for the 413 deputies.[^cite_note-foreignpolicy-20161104-57] Reacting to public criticism, deputies cancelled a salary rise that would have doubled their monthly salary.[^cite_note-ft-20161101-58] This measure was part of an Anti-Corruption Package passed into law in 2014, which was a requirement of international financial support for Ukraine and a prerequisite to eligibility for visa-free travel within the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-foreignpolicy-20161104-57][^cite_note-kp-20141023-59]

### Local politics

In the early 21st century, several [Ukrainian mayors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Ukraine) were suspected of using their posts to serve their own business interests.[^cite_note-60] In 2009, regional corruption was discovered in connection with land allocation.[^cite_note-61]

In 2013, Serhiy Odarych, former mayor of [Cherkasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherkasy), was suspected of causing a [₴](722696.md)600,000 loss to the city budget.[^cite_note-62]

### Judiciary

In the early 2010s, [Ukrainian politicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Ukraine) and analysts described the country's justice system as "rotten to the core",[^cite_note-rotten-63][^cite_note-Moskal-64] highlighting the political pressure put on judges.[^cite_note-65] Ukraine's court system was widely regarded as corrupt;[^cite_note-66] independent lawyers and [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Ukraine#The_right_to_fair_trial) activists have complained that judges are pressurised to hand down a particular verdict.[^cite_note-KPMakar1042012-67] A [Ukrainian Justice Ministry](30520978.md) survey in 2009 revealed that 10% of respondents trusted the nation's court system, and fewer than 30% believed that it was possible to get a [fair trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_a_fair_trial).[^cite_note-rotten-63] [Judicial independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_independence) exists in principle, but judges were subjected to pressure from political and business interests.[^cite_note-68] In the 2010s, Ukrainian judges were arrested while taking bribes;[^cite_note-69] and during the early part of the decade, it was noticeable that officials and their children (or *[mazhory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazhory)*[^cite_note-70]) received favourable sentences compared with common citizens.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

In 2012, Volodymyr Rokytskyi, Deputy Head of Ukraine's [Security Service](709222.md), was photographed in public wearing a US\$32,000 [luxury](858665.md) [wristwatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wristwatch)—a price equally his yearly official income—at a [joint Ukrainian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine%E2%80%93United_States_relations) event dedicated to fighting [illegal drugs](218449.md).[^cite_note-73] When a 2017 [Reuters](18998750.md) article quoted the deputy [Volodymyr Groysman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volodymyr_Groysman) as saying that "the weakest link in our fight against corruption is the Ukrainian court"; the article gave an example of 30 judges who received salaries ranging from US\$10,000–13,000 and who owned [Porsches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche).[^cite_note-74]

The *[Kyiv Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Post)* reported in 2018 that several candidates for a post in the new [High Anti-Corruption Court of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Anti-Corruption_Court_of_Ukraine) were themselves suspected of, or were associated with, corruption.[^cite_note-75] [AutoMaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AutoMaidan), Dejure, and the [Anti-corruption Action Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-corruption_Action_Center) all criticised decisions made in June 2023 by the [Ethics Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethics_Council_\(Ukraine\)) relating to appointments onto the [High Council of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Council_of_Justice_\(Ukraine\)). The decisions including the nomination of judges tainted by corruption, and the unfair exclusion of [Larysa Golnyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larysa_Golnyk), an anti-corruption [whistleblower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistleblower) judge.[^cite_note-KyivIndep_watchdogs_say_UA_judicial_reform-76]

In May 2023, on the orders of the [National Anti-Corruption Bureau of Ukraine](33096574.md) (NABU) and the [Specialized Anti-Corruption Prosecutor's Office](57303521.md) (SAPO), the President of the [Supreme Court of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Ukraine) [Vsevolod Kniaziev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vsevolod_Kniaziev) was detained while allegedly receiving a bribe.[^cite_note-СудуКнязєваGuardian2023-05-16-77] NABU and SAPO reported on their social media accounts about allegations of corruption in the country's Supreme Court of Ukraine. Anti-Corruption officials said that, "NABU and SAP have uncovered massive corruption in the Supreme Court, specifically plans to profit unfairly from the Supreme Court leadership and judges."[^cite_note-78]

## Corruption in the public sector

### Highways

In 2016, many of Ukraine's [major provincial highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roads_in_Ukraine) were in very poor condition, with an [Ukravtodor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukravtodor) official stating that 97% of roads needed repair. The road repair budget was set at about ₴20 billion, but corruption caused the budget to be poorly spent.[^cite_note-foreignpolicy-20160616-79]

### Nuclear energy

In 2015, corruption allegations were made against [Energoatom](5712443.md), Ukraine's state [nuclear power operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_in_Ukraine).[^cite_note-wnn-20150812-80] In 2016, Energoatom's assets and bank accounts were frozen by Ukrainian courts over allegedly unpaid debts, a decision that Energoatom appealed against.[^cite_note-nei-20160607-81][^cite_note-82] In June 2016, its bank accounts were unfrozen.[^cite_note-83]

#### Operation Midas

On 12 November 2025, a NABU investigation code-named [Operation Midas](81589122.md) into an alleged Energoatom US\$100 million [kickback scheme](35545035.md) led to the resignation of Justice Minister [German Galushchenko](70897877.md), and the Energy Minister [Svitlana Hrynchuk](79004452.md), and the dismissal of Energoatom's supervisory board. Grynchuk had been Galushchenko's deputy Energy Minister, and succeeded him in the post in July 2025. The investigation involved 1,000 hours of audio recordings and about 70 search operations. When President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) called for the resignation of the ministers, they quickly complied.

Among the seven suspects named by prosecutors was an [entrepreneur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrepreneur) Timur Mindich, who had co-owned the [*Kvartal 95* Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kvartal_95_Studio) with Zelenskyy prior to his election to the presidency, and Dmytro Basov, an executive director at Energoatom.

The Economy Ministry announced that a new supervisory board for Energoatom would be named within a week, that the [G7](1196634.md) countries would be consulted, and that Energoatom would undergo an audit to be reviewed by law enforcement officials.[^cite_note-reuters-20251111-84][^cite_note-wp-20251112-85]

On November 28, 2025, investigators raided the home of [Andriy Yermak](63086056.md), the head of the [Office of the President of Ukraine](19362024.md). Zelenskyy dismissed him later that day. Reports indicated that Yermak had played a central role in July 2025 efforts to curtail the independence of anti-corruption bodies, allegedly to obstruct the Energoatom investigation; protests forced the government to reverse course within days. Internal polling shared with *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* suggested that trust in Zelenskyy had fallen by half since the NABU revelations.[^cite_note-economist-20251128-86]

### Higher education

In the 2000s and 2010s, [higher education in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education_in_Ukraine) was plagued by bribery.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-pUuj16-89] In 2011, 33% of all students claimed they had encountered corruption in their school, 29% heard about cases of corruption from other students, while 38% had not encountered corruption.[^cite_note-90] According to TI research undertaken in 2008, 47.3% of university students stated that a bribe had been demanded from them; of those, 29% had paid this bribe freely.[^cite_note-TI-GCP-2008-p225-24] Students paid bribes for admission to a college, for exam results, and to get their doctoral or master's theses evaluated.[^cite_note-TI-GCP-2008-p225-24][^cite_note-pUuj16-89]

Bribes ranged from US\$10 to US\$50 for an exam pass to several thousand for entry to a university.[^cite_note-TI-GCP-2008-p225-24] According to government sources, bribes varied from US\$80 to US\$21,500.[^cite_note-TI-GCP-2008-p225-24] Salaries of teachers and professors were low in Ukraine compared with other professions; this may have tempted them to demand bribes.[^cite_note-TI-GCP-2008-p225-24][^cite_note-pUuj16-89] According to Ararat Osipian, entire corruption hierarchies formed in Ukraine's colleges and universities.[^cite_note-91] These hierarchies evolved from the 1990s as a result of uncontrolled and rampant corruption.[^cite_note-92] Ararat claimed in 2010 that corrupt ruling regimes control corrupt universities and force them into compliance,[^cite_note-93] including during the elections.[^cite_note-ararat.osipian-94] This was aided by universities largely still having Stalinist-type bureaucracies, unable to transform.[^cite_note-ararat.osipian-94]

Until 2015, university autonomy was nonexistent.[^cite_note-95] In 2015, the [Ukrainian parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_parliament) passed a new law on higher education to give universities more autonomy, including control over their own finances.[^cite_note-pUuj16-89] The aim was to encourage private investment, fundraising and the creation of [endowments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_endowment).[^cite_note-pUuj16-89]

In the 2000s, Ukrainian government officials were caught with fake university diplomas.[^cite_note-96]

### Social security

In 2012, Yanukovych reported that only about 23 percent of social service funds go to those who actually need them.[^cite_note-KP87P-97] The Ukrainian media featured many stories revealing that even parliamentarians illegally received social benefits, fraudulently claiming to be war and [Chornobyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster) veterans.[^cite_note-KP87P-97]

### Healthcare

Though medical care in state-run hospitals is theoretically free for Ukrainians, during the 2000s, patients commonly paid [out-of-pocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out-of-pocket_expense) to ensure they receive any treatment required.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-Abel2008-99] In 2012, [advocacy groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advocacy_group) accused [Health Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Healthcare_\(Ukraine\)) officials of [embezzling money](209411.md) that should have been used to treat Ukrainian [AIDS patients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS_in_Ukraine), by buying AIDS drugs at inflated prices.[^cite_note-100]

## Corruption in the business sector

In 2011, the [Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development](33853117.md) stated corruption was a "significant obstacle" to doing business in Ukraine.[^cite_note-BBCNews7March2011-101] Research conducted by [Ernst & Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_&_Young) in 2011 and 2012 showed that the practice of top business managers accepting bribes increased by 9 percent in 2011 and 15 percent in 2012. Another 4 percent were ready to pay bribes in order to hide the details of their financial performance.[^cite_note-KP87P-97]

In the early 2010s, the representative of a UK-based company claimed non-Ukrainian companies often lost [contracts](19280537.md) if they did not pay bribes or failed to "out-bribe" their competitors. Ukrainians and business representatives claimed that "Business ventures above a certain level require palm-greasing of some functionary at some level."[^cite_note-KPMakar1042012-67]

## Anti-corruption procedures and institutions

See also: [European Union Anti-Corruption Initiative in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Anti-Corruption_Initiative_in_Ukraine), [2020 Ukrainian constitutional crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Ukrainian_constitutional_crisis), and [Law enforcement in Ukraine § Anticorruption](11137543.md#Anticorruption)

In a survey in 2001, 80% of respondents were in agreement with the statement: "The present \[Ukrainian] government has no real interest in punishing corruption."[^cite_note-Gfk2001-35] After his election in late 2004, Yushchenko promised a "War on Corruption".[^cite_note-VICORWAR-102] Several politicians were investigated the following year, among them [Borys Kolesnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borys_Kolesnikov) and [Yuri Boyko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Boyko).[^cite_note-KP1-103][^cite_note-CoEar-104][^cite_note-105][^cite_note-arrest-106] [Oleksandr Turchynov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandr_Turchynov), the former chairman of the [Security Service of Ukraine](709222.md), claimed that in the summer of 2005, Yushchenko prevented a fraud investigation relating to the transport of [Turkmen](198149.md) [natural gas](22131.md) to Ukraine, and prevented Boyko's arrest for an [abuse of office](4308572.md) when he was the director of [Naftogaz](3614879.md):[^cite_note-GosLobby-107][^cite_note-arrest-106] In a 2005 interview, Turchynov said Yushchenko had told him to stop "persecuting my men", and that the investigation of [RosUkrEnergo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RosUkrEnergo) was "creating a conflict with Russian President Vladimir Putin".[^cite_note-arrest-106] Ukraine joined the [Group of States Against Corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_States_Against_Corruption) in 2006.[^cite_note-108]

Like his predecessor Yushchenko,[^cite_note-VICORWAR-102] Yanukovych (and his Azarov Government[^cite_note-program-109]) made the fight against corruption a spearhead in his domestic policies.[^cite_note-CorruNo-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-misinform-112] Political opponents of Yanukovych accused him of using his anti-corruption campaign for politically motivated trials; the general public in Ukraine largely shared this view.[^cite_note-BBCCTimeline_Ukraine-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-KPMakar1042012-67][^cite_note-115] Yanukovych denied this.[^cite_note-misinform-112] In September 2011 the [National Anti-Corruption Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Anti-Corruption_Committee) was introduced.[^cite_note-CorruNo-110]

The [International Association of Anti-Corruption Authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Anti-Corruption_Authorities) spoke in April 2011 of "remarkable successes in fighting corruption in 2010".[^cite_note-116] The [EU Ambassador to Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassadors_of_the_European_Union), Jose Manuel Pinto Teixeira, stated at an investment conference on February 28, 2012, that Yanukovych's pledges of reform "have regrettably produced no such results".[^cite_note-KPMakar1042012-67]

In May 2014, an Anti-Corruption Initiative was established. In December, it appointed Lithuanian economist and former [European Commissioner for Taxation and Customs Union, Audit and Anti-Fraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Commissioner_for_Taxation_and_Customs_Union,_Audit_and_Anti-Fraud) [Algirdas Šemeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algirdas_%C5%A0emeta) as Business [Ombudsman](26289198.md).[^cite_note-117] NABU was established in the same year[^cite_note-118] after its predecessor, the National Anti-Corruption Committee, was considered a failure. For designing and implementing systematic change tending to prevent corruption, the [National Agency for Prevention of Corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Agency_for_Prevention_of_Corruption) was created in 2015.[^cite_note-network4integrity_NACP-119]

In 2015, President [Petro Poroshenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petro_Poroshenko) sacked [Ihor Kolomoisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ihor_Kolomoyskyi) — the billionaire governor of the key industrial region of [Dnipropetrovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipropetrovsk) — after armed men suspected of links to Kolomoisky briefly occupied the offices of [a state-owned oil firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrnafta) in [Kyiv](585629.md).[^cite_note-120]

In 2018, a law came into force requiring that cases concerning corruption be brought directly to the High Anti-Corruption Court of Ukraine.[^cite_note-IUacc511967-121][^cite_note-122] In June 2018 President Poroshenko expected the court to be established before the end of 2018.[^cite_note-IUacc511967-121] In 2019 the High Anti-Corruption Court did start to work.[^cite_note-2773908-eu-welcomesB-123] Due to the IMF concern that oligarchs would use the courts to seize bailout money, parliament passed a bill in 2020 to prevent courts from reversing bank nationalizations. The bill would combat a lawsuit by oligarch Ihor Kolomoisky and others seeking to regain control of PrivatBank, the recipient of a \$5.5 billion 2016 bailout and an alleged "money laundering machine" under Kolomoisky.[^cite_note-124]

In 2020, the [Constitutional Court of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Ukraine) triggered [a constitutional crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_crisis_in_Ukraine_in_2020) when it ruled that anti-corruption legislation, including the mandatory electronic declaration of income, was unconstitutional.[^cite_note-unian-20201113-125] Zelensky warned that if parliament did not restore these anti-corruption laws, foreign aid, loans and [visa-free travel to the European Union](5612265.md#Visa_liberalisation_dialogue_and_visa-free_regime) were at risk. The governor of the [National Bank of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Ukraine) reported that because of the issue, Ukraine would not receive a scheduled \$700 million [IMF loan](43158902.md) before the end of 2020.[^cite_note-reuters-20201102-126][^cite_note-intellinews-20201114-127] On December 4, 2020, the Ukrainian parliament restored the legislation, reauthorising criminal penalties for officials who lied about their incomes.[^cite_note-2020104ukraineparliamentCUWA-128] On December 29, 2020, Zelenskyy suspended the Constitutional Court's chairperson [Oleksandr Tupytskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandr_Tupytskyi) for two months.[^cite_note-judgeCCU11271860UccCUWA-129] On July 19, 2022, [Oleksandr Klymenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandr_Klymenko_\(detective\)) was announced as the new head of SAPO, following an open competition for the post.[^cite_note-TI_election_SAPO_head_finish_line-130][^cite_note-KyivIndep_selection_panel_declares_winner-131][^cite_note-TI_detective_Klymenko_wins-132]

## Comparisons with other countries

A 1995 national [poll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survey_\(human_research\)) initiated by the [Ukrainian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_government) showed that 42% of the survey's respondents viewed corruption as "a shameful phenomenon that has no objective grounds", while 36% viewed it as "a component of social traditions".[^cite_note-pravdaua7270036-133]

In the 2010s, the Ukrainian economist [Oleh Havrylyshyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleh_Havrylyshyn?action=edit\&redlink=1) compared Ukrainian corruption to global trends, using data from TI. His research estimated that corruption levels in Ukraine were comparable to those in [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md), identifying Uganda as the country's closest counterpart.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135]

In 2012, Ernst & Young ranked Ukraine among the three most corrupt nations out of 43 surveyed.[^cite_note-KP87P-97][^cite_note-ukraine-corruption-ernst-136] In 2015, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* labelled Ukraine as "the most corrupt nation in Europe".[^cite_note-Guardian-Ukraine-Corruption-4] By 2017, an Ernst & Young poll of experts ranked Ukraine ninth-most corrupt out of 53 surveyed nations.[^cite_note-137]

### Corruption Perceptions Index

Transparency International's annual [Corruption Perceptions Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_Perceptions_Index) (CPI) measures perceptions of corruption, as seen by business people and country analysts, in the public sector of many countries of the world. Transparency International then scores the countries on a scale from "highly corrupt" to "very clean" and ranks them by score. The country with the highest score is ranked first and is perceived to have the most honest public sector.[^cite_note-CPI-ABCs-138]

#### Recent performance

The 2025 CPI scored Ukraine at 36 on a scale from 0 ("highly corrupt") to 100 ("very clean"). When ranked by score, Ukraine ranked 104th among the 182 countries in the Index, where the country ranked first is perceived to have the most honest public sector. For comparison with regional scores, the best score among Eastern European and Central Asian countries[^cite_note-140] was 50, the average score was 34 and the worst score was 17.[^cite_note-CPI2025-139] For comparison with worldwide scores, the best score was 89 (ranked 1), the average score was 42, and the worst score was 9 (ranked 181 in a two-way tie).[^cite_note-141]

Discussing Ukraine in 2025, TI commented that the significant procurement and defence-sector scandals which emerged that year showed at once that Ukraine's anti-corruption agencies were working successfully and that much corruption remained for them to uproot. TI concluded, "The challenge now is to move from reactive clean-ups after each scandal to structural reforms that close loopholes and better protect public resources."[^cite_note-Tra1-142] TI had struck a similar note in discussing Ukraine's score in 2024: it had improved steadily for 11 years, but the high number of corruption cases that were detected and prosecuted in 2024 was an indication that significant work remained to be done.[^cite_note-\:0-143]

Berlin: Transparency International. December 1, 2011. 2_CPI2011_FAQS_EN.pdf. Retrieved June 17, 2025. Can country/territory scores in the 2011 CPI be compared to those in past indexes? The CPI is not designed to allow for country scores to be compared over time. This is because the index draws on a country's rank in the original data sources, rather than its score. A rank will always deliver only relative information – and therefore a ranking is a one off assessment. A country's rank in a given data source can change a) if perceptions of corruption in other countries included in that source change or b) if countries are added or removed from that data source.
