On 18 September 2026, armed insurgents attacked and occupied the Old Police Lines complex in [Kohat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohat), Pakistan. After an initial attack on the complex's [mosque](19894.md), a battle and clearance operation continued for about 22 hours, resulting in at least 31 deaths and 103 injuries.[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-1]

It is the second attack on the Old Police Lines complex; it had previously been targeted in a [suicide car bombing](155282.md#Suicide_usage) on 7 September 2010 that resulted in 20 deaths and over 90 injuries. It is the deadliest attack against a police complex in [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) since the [2023 bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Peshawar_mosque_bombing) of the [Peshawar](66230.md) Police Lines mosque.[^cite_note-dawn-2031248-2]

After the attack, Pakistan significantly strengthened counterterrorism operations along the [Afghan border](203570.md), alleging that the perpetrators had originated from [Afghanistan](737.md). Pakistan launched [air strikes](323167.md) in retaliation for the attack, breaking a three-month lull in the [2026 Afghanistan‍–‍Pakistan war](82482902.md).[^cite_note-ap-3] Hostilities quickly grew, with additional air strikes, artillery fire, and [drone attacks](38212231.md) being exchanged after supposed Afghan incursions.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

## Attack

People outside the mosque reacting to the car bomb explosion

On the afternoon of 18 September, police officers and civilians were attending [Friday prayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_prayer) at the mosque of the Old Police Lines complex in Kohat, whose [Counter Terrorism Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter_Terrorism_Department_\(Pakistan\)) (CTD) and [Special Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Branch_\(Pakistan\)) had participated in counterterrorism operations in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.[^cite_note-Khan-2026a-5] At around 13\:15 [PKT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Standard_Time) ([UTC+5\:00](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+05\:00)), a suicide bomber rammed an explosives-laden car through the gate of the police complex and into the entrance of the mosque before it exploded.[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-1][^cite_note-BBC-2026-6] Gunfire broke out when seven heavily armed militants with hand grenades, sniper rifles, and other weaponry launched an assault on the complex,[^cite_note-Ahtesham-7] taking up positions in the complex's Special Branch building.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-Khan-2026b-9] During the engagement with security personnel, a suicide bomber detonated himself near the Special Branch building. Six of the militants were quickly killed in the battle.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

Militants also targeted a police post at the main gate of Kohat's district courts, damaging it but causing no casualties. All offices and district courts near the Old Police Lines complex were then closed as authorities declared an emergency and implemented additional security measures.[^cite_note-Geo_News-2026b-12][^cite_note-13]

On the morning of 19 September, the [Pakistan Army](1965925.md) was deployed and a [joint operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_warfare) was initiated to clear the two remaining militants who had hidden themselves within the complex, utilising [drone](58900.md) technology to monitor their movements.[^cite_note-Ahtesham-7]

The militants were eventually defeated by the Kohat Police, [Elite Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite_Police), CTD, and Pakistan Army after about 22 hours of fighting,[^cite_note-Ali-2026-14][^cite_note-Khan-2026b-9] in which all of the attackers,[^cite_note-Geo_News-2026b-12][^cite_note-15] including suicide bombers and a sniper, died.[^cite_note-Khan-2026a-5][^cite_note-Geo_News-2026a-16]

The police complex was extensively damaged. The explosions created a crater outside the mosque, trapped people in rubble, caused offices to collapse, and damaged nearby structures, including government and private property.[^cite_note-Geo_News-2026b-12][^cite_note-Geo_News-2026a-16][^cite_note-17] [Rescue 1122](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_1122) provided relief efforts at the site with more than 60 personnel and 24 emergency vehicles, some from neighbouring districts.[^cite_note-Farooq-2026a-18]

## Casualties

Thirty-one people died in the attack, identified as fifteen government personnel, six civilians, eight attackers, and two government personnel who later died of injuries.[^cite_note-Ali-2026-14][^cite_note-19] Seventy-three government personnel and thirty civilians were injured,[^cite_note-20] four of whom were in critical condition and required [laparotomies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laparotomy).[^cite_note-Geo_News-2026a-16][^cite_note-Farooq-2026a-18] Ninety of those wounded were received at DHQ Hospital Kohat while the other thirteen were sent to Liaqat Memorial Hospital.[^cite_note-Khan-2026c-21] Funeral prayers were held for the killed officers later in the day while the remains of the civilians were released to their families for burial.[^cite_note-Khan-2026b-9]

## Investigation

Pakistani president [Asif Ali Zardari](993655.md) initially implicated the [Pakistani Taliban](18953699.md), who denied carrying out the attack.[^cite_note-DW-22] [Ittihad-ul-Mujahideen Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ittihad-ul-Mujahideen_Pakistan), an alliance of Pakistani Taliban offshoots led by [Hafiz Gul Bahadur](77984933.md), claimed responsibility for the attack.[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-1][^cite_note-BBC-2026-6]

An investigation was conducted to determine the planning, supplies, training, and potential links to other actors involved in the attack. On 20 September, a [first information report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_information_report) was registered by Kohat Police with the CTD against "unidentified terrorists", charging them with terrorism, murder, and attempted murder, among other offences. Investigators believed the attackers planned for a prolonged siege at the complex for several hours.[^cite_note-Ahtesham-7]

The Kohat Police, CTD, and Elite Force jointly investigated the identities of the attackers and potential links to foreign actors. Pakistani authorities claimed to have identified one of the attackers as an Afghan national named Mutiullah, which prompted further scrutiny regarding the perpetrators' alleged links to Afghanistan.[^cite_note-23]

## Reactions

### Domestic

Asif Ali Zardari expressed "regret" over the attack, giving "his best wishes for the swift recovery of those injured in the explosion" in a statement, and called for the "complete elimination" of foreign-backed terrorists.[^cite_note-BBC-2026-6] Prime Minister [Shehbaz Sharif](6502561.md) condemned the attack and gave condolences to the victims. He directed authorities to accelerate rescue operations. The [Ministry of Interior](26905116.md) said that the "cowardly actions" of the attackers would not weaken "the nation's resolve."[^cite_note-DW-22] Chief Minister of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa [Sohail Afridi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohail_Afridi) visited those affected by the attack and paid tribute to responders.[^cite_note-Farooq-2026a-18][^cite_note-Khan-2026c-21]

### International

[United Nations](31769.md) spokesperson [Stéphane Dujarric](10302459.md) stated that Secretary-General [António Guterres](471806.md) strongly condemned the terrorist attack targeting the mosque and police complex in Kohat, saying "attacks targeting civilians and places of worship are unacceptable".[^cite_note-BBC-2026-6] UN Under-Secretary-General [Miguel Ángel Moratinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_%C3%81ngel_Moratinos) also offered condolences and condemned the Kohat attack, saying "Places of worship must remain protected spaces where people feel safe."[^cite_note-24] United Nations Security Council president [Jérôme Bonnafont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%A9r%C3%B4me_Bonnafont) condemned the attack "in the strongest terms", describing it as "heinous and cowardly". Security Council members obliged all states to cooperate with Pakistan to bring the perpetrators to justice and for adherence to [international law](8195726.md).[^cite_note-25]

The attack drew condemnation from several [Islamic terrorist](566821.md) groups, including the Pakistani Taliban, [Jamaat-ul-Ahrar](44284567.md), and [al-Qaeda in the Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Indian_subcontinent).[^cite_note-26]

The [General Secretariat of the Organisation of Islamic Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretariat_of_the_Organisation_of_Islamic_Cooperation) offered condolences and condemned the Kohat attack,[^cite_note-Geo_News-2026a-16] as did Afghanistan,[^cite_note-27] Australia, China, Iran,[^cite_note-28] Jordan, Kuwait, Palestine, Poland, Qatar, Russia, Saudi Arabia,[^cite_note-29] Turkey,[^cite_note-30] and the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-Geo_News-2026a-16] Pakistan rejected Afghanistan's statement, calling it a "pretence of dialogue", and demanded they act on dismantling "terrorist sanctuaries, alongside their recruitment and support infrastructure in Afghanistan" instead of "deflecting responsibility" onto Pakistan.[^cite_note-Geo_News-2026b-12][^cite_note-Arab_News_Pakistan-2026-31]

## Aftermath

On 19 September, Pakistan's [Ministry of Information & Broadcasting](32715412.md) (MoIB) accused Afghanistan of [sponsoring terrorism](317645.md#Afghanistan) in Pakistan, demanding its [Taliban government](68866725.md) take "credible, verifiable and sustained measures" to dismantle militant sanctuaries and terrorist recruitment and training networks on its soil, warning they have the "will and the capability to eliminate terrorist threats wherever they are".[^cite_note-Geo_News-2026b-12][^cite_note-Arab_News_Pakistan-2026-31][^cite_note-32]

[Ahmed Sharif Chaudhry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Sharif_Chaudhry) of the Pakistan Army declared that the Taliban's inaction constituted a violation of the [Doha Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States%E2%80%93Taliban_deal), which requires that the Taliban prevent terrorist groups from being hosted in Afghan territory. Asserting Pakistan's "right to defend itself", he warned that Pakistan was "left with no other option" than to take any necessary steps to resolve the issue if the Taliban did not comply. Afghanistan denied the accusations, threatening "retaliatory action" and a "crushing response" if Pakistan violated Afghan sovereignty.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] Aziz Maraj, a former diplomat, suggested that Afghanistan should file a complaint to the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) to protect "Afghanistan's dignity and reputation" against Pakistani defamation should Pakistan fail to provide evidence of the Afghan involvement. [^cite_note-35]

Pakistan increased counterterrorism operations in provinces along its border with Afghanistan under its ongoing counterterrorism campaign, Operation Ghazab lil Haq. On 20 September, a [Pakistani Security Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Security_Forces) convoy attacked a group of terrorists supposedly "belonging to [Indian proxy Fitna Al Khwarij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_and_state-sponsored_terrorism)" in a "well-planned intelligence-based ground operation" in Kohat, [Hangu](3620736.md), and [Thal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thall,_Hangu_District,_Khyber_Pakhtunkhwa). Nine of the terrorists and six Pakistani personnel died in the exchange and a number were injured.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-dawn-37] Similar intelligence-based operations continued in the region, with six terrorists killed and nine suspects "belonging to [Fitna al-Hindustan](6010135.md)" being arrested in Saran Tangi, [Balochistan](8431743.md) on 22 September, in a mission named Operation Shaban‑2.[^cite_note-38]

On 21 September, Pakistani fighter jets launched overnight air strikes on [Nurgal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurgal_District) and [Barmal District](14912368.md) in eastern Afghanistan, breaking a three-month period of reduced conflict. Afghan officials reported that strikes in Barmal destroyed a grocery store and an empty house in the Rakhah and Tor Kandi areas with no casualties, while strikes in Nurgal hit a family home, killing three and injuring four family members.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] The casualties were corroborated by the [United Nations Assistance Mission in Afghanistan](1234277.md), who reported that "a man, woman and a girl were killed and four injured in apparent strikes".[^cite_note-ap-3][^cite_note-41] Pakistan denied attacking civilians, claiming the buildings were militant hideouts and that the strikes hit their targets with "\[m]aximum precautions" against collateral damage;[^cite_note-42] targets purportedly included a suicide bomber training centre and weapons depot, and 28 militants and multiple suicide bombers were killed according to the Pakistani MoIB.[^cite_note-dawn-37] Afghanistan condemned the air strikes as acts of “aggression and criminality”, alleging the [Pakistan Air Force](377433.md) carried them out as a distraction from Pakistan's security failures.[^cite_note-43] The Afghan [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](8980088.md) summoned Pakistan’s [chargé d’affaires](856252.md) in [Kabul](16826.md) and handed him a formal protest over the violation of Afghan [airspace](353557.md) and civilian attacks.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

On 22 September, Pakistani officials reported that several Pakistani Taliban militants attempted to infiltrate the border at [Kurram](5053207.md) from [Nangarhar](457263.md) and [Paktia](457274.md) in Afghanistan. After being identified by Pakistani troops, the militants and [Afghan Army ](212681.md)reportedly began firing [mortars](324499.md) and heavy weapons on Pakistani checkposts unprovoked. In retaliation, Pakistani troops launched multiple artillery strikes on Afghan checkposts and defensive positions, reportedly causing several to flee their posts as a result.[^cite_note-\:1-4] Afghanistan denied that such an attack occurred.[^cite_note-46]

On 23 September, eight rudimentary drones were launched from Afghanistan and entered Pakistani airspace but then were shot down over [Torkham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torkham) and Kohat by Pakistani air defences and armed forces.[^cite_note-47] In retaliation, Pakistan carried out ten "deliberate, precise, calibrated, and proportionate" air strikes and drone attacks on Afghan military sites in [Kandahar](457241.md), Paktia, and [Khost](457249.md) early on 24 September, claiming they were used to store and launch drones.[^cite_note-48] The air strikes killed at least four civilians according Afghan officials. Afghanistan denied launching the drones and called the air strikes "indiscriminate and cowardly", warning of "dire consequences".[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

On 25 September, Pakistani officials reported that several Afghan Taliban members were killed while attempting to enter [Gulistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulistan,_Balochistan), Balochistan. Intermittent exchange of fire between security forces and the Afghan Taliban continued after midnight, with thirteen Taliban personnel reportedly dying in the clash.[^cite_note-51] Afghanistan's [Ministry of Defence](1289906.md) denied that its forces suffered any casualties.[^cite_note-52] From 27 to 30 September, Afghanistan claimed that its forces killed 41 insurgents and captured 62 others in [Nuristan](457267.md) allegedly belonged to [ISIS](9087364.md) and Pakistani militias. Pakistan’s MoIB dismissed the claim as a fabrication, describing it as “frivolous, baseless and devoid of any truth”.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]
