**Akita Prefecture** (秋田県, *Akita-ken*; Japanese pronunciation: [\[aꜜ.kʲi̥.ta, a.kʲi̥.taꜜ.keɴ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese)[^cite_note-2]) is a [prefecture](74517.md) of [Japan](15573.md) located in the [Tōhoku region](202954.md) of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu).[^cite_note-nussbaum780-3] Its population is estimated 915,691 as of 1 August 2023 and its geographic area is 11,637 square kilometres (4,493 sq mi). Akita Prefecture is bordered by [Aomori Prefecture](61732.md) to the north, [Iwate Prefecture](183477.md) to the east, [Miyagi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyagi_Prefecture) to the southeast, and [Yamagata Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamagata_Prefecture) to the south.

[Akita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akita,_Akita) is the capital and largest city of Akita Prefecture. Other major cities include [Yokote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokote,_Akita), [Daisen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisen,_Akita), and [Yurihonjō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yurihonj%C5%8D).[^cite_note-4] Akita Prefecture is located on the coast of the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan) and extends east to the [Ōu Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cu_Mountains), the longest [mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_range) in Japan, at the border with Iwate Prefecture. Akita Prefecture formed the northern half of the historic [Dewa Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewa_Province) with Yamagata Prefecture.

## History

See also: [Historic Sites of Akita Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Sites_of_Akita_Prefecture)

The region of Akita was created from the ancient provinces of [Dewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewa_Province) and [Mutsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutsu_Province).[^cite_note-5]

Separated from the principal Japanese centres of commerce, politics, and population by several hundred kilometres and by the [Ōu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cu_Mountains) and Dewa mountain ranges to the east, Akita remained largely isolated from Japanese society until after the year 600. Akita was a region of hunter-gatherers and principally nomadic tribes.

The first historical record of what is now Akita Prefecture dates to 658, when [Abe no Hirafu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abe_no_Hirafu) conquered the native [Ezo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezo) tribes at what are now the cities of Akita and [Noshiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noshiro,_Akita). Abe, then governor of [Koshi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koshi_Province_\(Japan\)) (the northwestern part of Honshū bordering the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan)), established a fort on the [Mogami River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogami_River), and thus began the Japanese settlement of the region.

In 733, a new military settlement (later renamed [Akita Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akita_Castle)) was built in what is now the Takashimizu area of Akita, and more permanent roads and structures were developed. The region was used as a base of operations for the Japanese empire as it drove the native Ezo people from northern Honshū.

Governance of the region shifted hands several times. During the [Tokugawa shogunate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate) it was appropriated to the [Satake clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satake_clan), who ruled the region for 260 years and developed the agriculture and mining industries that are still predominant today. Throughout this period, it was classified as part of [Dewa Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewa_Province).[^cite_note-nussbaum780-3] In 1871, during the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration), Dewa Province was reshaped and the old [daimyō domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_\(administrative_division\)) were abolished and administratively reconstructed, resulting in the modern-day borders of Akita.

The famous [Heian period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heian_period) [waka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waka_\(poetry\)) poet, [Ono no Komachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ono_no_Komachi), is said to have been born in Yuzawa City, Ogachi Town, located in the southeast of the prefecture.

Located on the north-western side of Honshu, Akita Prefecture is adjacent to the [Sea of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan) to the west and borders four other prefectures: [Aomori](61732.md) in the north, [Iwate](183477.md) in the east, [Miyagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyagi_Prefecture) in the southeast, and [Yamagata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamagata_Prefecture) in the south.

The borders of Akita Prefecture roughly form a rectangle that is 169 kilometres (105 miles) from north to south and 86 kilometres (54 miles) from west to east. The [Oga Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oga_Peninsula) is a prominent feature of the western edge, while the [Ōu Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cu_Mountains) mark the eastern border and the higher [Dewa Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewa_Mountains) run parallel through the center. Like much of northern Japan, the prefecture has cold winters, particularly in areas farther from the sea.

As of 31 March 2019, 11% of the total land area of the prefecture is designated as [Natural Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan), namely the [Towada-Hachimantai National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Towada-Hachimantai_National_Park); [Chōkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%8Dkai_Quasi-National_Park), [Kurikoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurikoma_Quasi-National_Park), and [Oga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oga_Quasi-National_Park) Quasi-National Parks; and [Akita Shirakami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akita_Shirakami_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Hachimori Iwadate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachimori_Iwadate_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Kimimachizaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimimachizaka_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Magi Mahiru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magi_Mahiru_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Moriyoshizan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moriyoshizan_Prefectural_Natural_Park), [Taiheizan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiheizan_Prefectural_Natural_Park_\(Akita\)), [Tashirodake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashirodake_Prefectural_Natural_Park), and [Tazawako Dakigaeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tazawako_Dakigaeri_Prefectural_Natural_Park) Prefectural Natural Parks.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## Economy and population



Akita prefecture population pyramid in 2020



**Note:** Data in the chart above was taken over the course of five years (2003-2008). The graph shows how many people migrated to Akita City from other prefectures. Overall the net gain of new residents was 4,981 people, or 1.5%.

[^cite_note-\:0-9]

Like much of the [Tōhoku Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dhoku_Region), Akita's economy remains dominated by traditional industries such as agriculture, fishing, and forestry. This has led many young people to migrate to Tokyo and other large cities. Akita Prefecture has seen some of the most severe population decline in Japan: it is one of four prefectures in Japan registering declines in population since 1955. Its population also has the lowest percentage of children (defined as under the age of 15), at 9.3% in 2022, down from 11.2% in 2010.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-\:1-11] 38.6% of residents are 65 or older, the highest percentage of any Japanese prefecture.[^cite_note-\:1-11] As of October 1, 2022, it has an estimated population of 944,902 people.[^cite_note-\:1-11]

The high rate of depopulation in Akita Prefecture has led smaller communities to merge with each other, which has affected the smallest of these merged communities. As depopulation in these communities continues, educational and health facilities have closed in some areas, encouraging families to migrate to larger cities for better access to health and educational opportunities and perpetuating the decline in population. This decline, [combined with an aging population](15015085.md), has been concerning for rural communities.[^cite_note-\:0-9]

## Culture



Kawatsura Shikki lacquerware in the Prince Consort Gallery, [V\&A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V\&A), London (2024)

[^cite_note-ELLE_Decoration-2025-12]

[^cite_note-LDF-13]

Akita, 秋田, meaning autumn rice paddy, is famous for rice farming and its [sake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sake) breweries.[^cite_note-14] It is well known for having the highest consumption of [sake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sake) in Japan[^cite_note-15] and is thought to be the origin of the [Akita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akita_dog) breed of dog which carries the prefecture's name. The women of the region, referred to as *Akita Bijin* ([秋田美人](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%A7%8B%E7%94%B0%E7%BE%8E%E4%BA%BA?action=edit\&redlink=1), *'Beauties of Akita'*), have also gained widespread renown for their white skin, rounded faces and high voices, all of which are considered highly desirable.[^cite_note-16] [Ono no Komachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ono_no_Komachi) is a famous example of an *Akita bijin*.

## Tourism



[Samurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samurai) house in Kakunodate

Recently there have been efforts to revitalize rural communities facing depopulation with different forms of green tourism and agritourism.[^cite_note-19] These efforts are primarily aimed at attracting urbanites and foreign tourists to Akita Prefecture, advertising its pristine forests, sprawling rice fields, and range of cultures.[^cite_note-\:0-9] There has been a push for home stays, farmers markets for locally produced foods, and the integration of outsiders into local cultural practices. The [Namahage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namahage) ritual in [Oga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oga,_Akita) on New Year's Eve draws a large number of tourists to Akita Prefecture every year.[^cite_note-20]

Near [Lake Tazawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Tazawa), there are a number of hot springs resorts (*[onsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onsen)*). These are popular with tourists from all over Japan. In addition, numerous seasonal festivals (*[matsuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsuri)*) offer a glimpse of rural or traditional Japan. Some famous examples are the Akita Kantō, the [Omagari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omagari) Fireworks, [Namahage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namahage) Festival, and the [Yokote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokote) Kamakura Festivals.

[Kakunodate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakunodate,_Akita), known as the little [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto), features many preserved samurai houses. The Aoyagi house is the former residence of Odano Naotake, who illustrated Japan's first modern guide to human anatomy. The house is now a museum and gallery of medical illustrations and traditional crafts.

Starting in 2009, Akita began experiencing a huge surge in [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans) tourism after the airing of the popular drama [*Iris*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iris_\(South_Korean_TV_series\)), which featured several scenes shot in Akita, most notably at Lake Tazawa and Oga's GAO Aquarium.[^cite_note-21]
