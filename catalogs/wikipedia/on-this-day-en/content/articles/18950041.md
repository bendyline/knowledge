**150 metres** is a [sprint](89276.md) event in [track and field](194904.md). It is a very rarely contested non-championship event, and it is not recognised by [World Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics). Given the proportion of standard [running tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_track), the event typically incorporates a bend when held in a [track and field stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Track_and_field_stadium), although some specially-built tracks allow it to take place entirely on a straight.

&#x20;The event was given a high-profile outing in 1997 as an [intermediate contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailey%E2%80%93Johnson_150-metre_race) between two 1996 Olympic champions: [Donovan Bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donovan_Bailey) ([100 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_metres)) and [Michael Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Johnson_\(sprinter\)) ([200 metres](1833981.md)).[^cite_note-1] Johnson pulled up mid-race, allowing Bailey to win the \$1 million prize.[^cite_note-hurriyetdailynews-2] This race coincided with a period of similar 150 m meetings between Bailey and the 1992 Olympic 100 m champion [Linford Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linford_Christie); the pair raced over three years for high cash prizes in [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield), England, in 1995, 1996 and 1997, with Christie winning the first two outings and Bailey winning the last.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-hurriyetdailynews-2]



Usain Bolt lining up for his 150 m world best run in Manchester in 2009

The [Manchester City Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_City_Games) in England – a competition featuring a long, raised track on one of the city's major streets – has provided many of the event's highlights since 2009, including the men's world best of 14.35 seconds, set by [Usain Bolt](4764846.md) in 2009.[^cite_note-4] [Allyson Felix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allyson_Felix) ran the fastest ever female 150 m race in 2013 (16.36 seconds),[^cite_note-5] although faster times have been recorded at intermediate stages of the 200 m event. The [Great North City Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_City_Games) (held variously in [Newcastle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne) and [Gateshead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateshead)) feature a similar set-up to the Manchester event and have hosted several of the best men's and women's times.[^cite_note-6] The British events typically attract American, British and Caribbean competitors, and athletes from these places account for nearly all the top 25 best times for men and women. A one-off 150 m race on [Copacabana Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copacabana_Beach) in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md) was held in 2013 and Bolt finished in a time close to his own world best.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

The 150 m had some significance as a regular indoor event in the 1960s and 1970s as a result of indoor tracks matching that distance. [Wales](69894.md) held a national championship over the distance up to 1972, and [Finland](10577.md) briefly had a women's national championship in the mid-1960s.[^cite_note-\:0-7][^cite_note-8] A relay version of the distance (4 × 150 metres) was contested at the [1967 European Athletics Indoor Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_European_Athletics_Indoor_Championships) and was won by the Soviet Union's women's team.[^cite_note-9] The distance attracted the attention of 1980 Olympic 200 m champion [Pietro Mennea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Mennea), whose hand-timed run of 14.8 seconds in [Cassino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassino), Italy, in 1983 stood as a world-best time for over a quarter of a century.[^cite_note-10] Italy also provided a women's 150 m best that same decade, with Jamaican [Merlene Ottey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlene_Ottey) setting a time of 16.46 seconds in [Trapani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapani) in 1989 – a world-best mark which was unbeaten for over two decades.[^cite_note-11]
