**Southampton**[^cite_note-9] is a [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_the_United_Kingdom) and [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority) in [Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire), England. It is located approximately 80 miles (130 km) southwest of [London](17867.md), 20 miles (32 km) west of [Portsmouth](24207.md), and 20 miles (32 km) southeast of [Salisbury](72119.md).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Southampton had a population of 248,922 at the 2021 census, making it one of the most populous cities in southern England.[^cite_note-12]

Southampton forms part of the larger [South Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Hampshire) [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation) which includes the city of [Portsmouth](24207.md) and the boroughs of [Havant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Havant), [Eastleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Eastleigh), [Fareham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Fareham) and [Gosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosport). A major port,[^cite_note-13] and close to the [New Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Forest), Southampton lies at the northernmost point of [Southampton Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Water), at the confluence of the [River Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Test) and [Itchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Itchen,_Hampshire),[^cite_note-britannica.com-14] with the [River Hamble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Hamble) joining to the south. Southampton is classified as a Medium-Port City.[^cite_note-15]

Southampton was the departure point for the [RMS *Titanic*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic)[^cite_note-16] and home to 500 of the people who died in the sinking.[^cite_note-17] The [Spitfire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermarine_Spitfire) was built in the city,[^cite_note-18] and Southampton has a strong association with the *[Mayflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayflower)*, being the departure point before the vessel was forced to return to [Plymouth](23508.md). In the past century the city was one of Europe's main ports for [ocean liners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_liner). More recently, Southampton is the home port of some of the largest cruise ships in the world.[^cite_note-19] The [Cunard Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cunard_Line) maintains a regular transatlantic service to [New York](645042.md) from the city. Southampton is also one of the largest retail destinations in the South of England.[^cite_note-20]

Southampton was heavily bombed during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) during what was known as the [Southampton Blitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Blitz). It was one of the major embarkation points for [D-Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Day). In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) Southampton was where troops left England for the [Battle of Agincourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Agincourt). It was itself raided by French pirates, leading to the construction of the fortified [town walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_town_walls), many of which still stand today. [Jane Austen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Austen) also lived in Southampton for a number of years. In 1964, the town of Southampton acquired [city status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_the_United_Kingdom), becoming the City of Southampton.[^cite_note-Rance-21]

Some notable employers in the city include the [University of Southampton](98078.md), [Ordnance Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordnance_Survey), [BBC South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_South), [Associated British Ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_British_Ports), and [Carnival UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_UK).[^cite_note-22]

## History

Main articles: [History of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Southampton) and [Timeline of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Southampton)

### Pre-Norman

Archaeological finds suggest that the area has been inhabited since the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age).[^cite_note-MusArch-23] Following the [Roman invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_conquest_of_Britain) of Britain in AD 43 and the conquering of the local Britons in AD 70 the fortress settlement of *[Clausentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clausentum)* was established. It was an important trading port and defensive outpost of [Winchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester), at the site of modern [Bitterne Manor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitterne_Manor). *Clausentum* was defended by a wall and two ditches and is thought to have contained a bath house.[^cite_note-24] *Clausentum* was not [abandoned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_Roman_rule_in_Britain) until around 410.[^cite_note-MusArch-23]

The [Anglo-Saxons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxons) formed a new, larger, settlement across the [Itchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Itchen,_Hampshire) centred on what is now the [St Mary's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's,_Southampton) area of the city. The settlement was known as *Hamwic*,[^cite_note-MusArch-23] which evolved into *Hamtun* and then *Hampton*.[^cite_note-25] Archaeological excavations of this site have uncovered one of the best collections of Saxon artefacts in Europe.[^cite_note-MusArch-23] It is from this town that the county of Hampshire gets its name.

[Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings) raids contributed to the decline of Hamwic in the late 9th century,[^cite_note-26] and by the 10th century a fortified settlement, which became medieval Southampton, had been established.[^cite_note-sotongovmedieval-27]

### 11th–13th centuries

Following the [Norman Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Conquest) in 1066, Southampton became the major port of transit between the then capital of England, Winchester, and [Normandy](21724.md). [Southampton Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Castle) was built in the 12th century[^cite_note-Rance-21] and surviving remains of 12th-century merchants' houses such as *King John's House* and *Canute's Palace* are evidence of the wealth that existed in the town at this time.[^cite_note-RancePP34-28] By the 13th century Southampton had become a leading port, particularly involved in the import of French wine[^cite_note-sotongovmedieval-27] in exchange for English cloth and [wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool).[^cite_note-29]

The [Franciscan friary in Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan_friary_in_Southampton) was founded circa 1233.[^cite_note-Ruddock-30] The friars constructed a water supply system in 1290, which carried water from Conduit Head (remnants of which survive near Hill Lane, [Shirley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley,_Southampton)) some 1.1 mi (1.7 km) to the site of the friary inside the town walls.[^cite_note-Davies-31] Further remains can be observed at Conduit House on Commercial Road. The friars granted use of the water to the town in 1310.[^cite_note-Davies-31]

### 14th century

Between 1327 and 1330, the King and Council received a petition from the people of Southampton. The community of Southampton claimed that Robert Batail of Winchelsea and other men of the [Cinque Ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinque_Ports) came to Southampton under the pretence that they were a part of [Thomas of Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_of_Lancaster)'s rebellion against [Edward II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_II). The community thought that they were in conspiracy with [Hugh le Despenser the Younger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_le_Despenser_the_Younger). The petition states that, the supposed rebels in the [Despenser War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Despenser_War) 'came to Southampton harbour, and burnt their ships, and their goods, chattels and merchandise which was in them, and carried off other goods, chattels and merchandise of theirs found there, and took some of the ships with them, to a loss to them of £8000 and more.'[^cite_note-32] For their petition to the King somewhere after 1321 and before 1327 earned some of the people of Southampton a prison sentence at [Portchester Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portchester_Castle), possibly for insinuating the king's advisor Hugh le Despenser the Younger acted in conspiracy with the Cinque Port men to damage Southampton, a flourishing port in the fourteenth century. When King [Edward III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_III) came to the throne, this petition was given to the king and his mother, [Queen Isabella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabella_of_France), who was in charge of the town, and the country at this stage likely organised the [writ of trespass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writ_of_trespass) that took any guilt away from the community at Southampton.



Part of Southampton's Town Walls

[The town was sacked in 1338](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Channel_naval_campaign,_1338-1339) by French, Genoese and Monegasque ships (under [Charles Grimaldi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I,_Lord_of_Monaco), who used the plunder to help found the [principality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality) of [Monaco](19261.md)).[^cite_note-internetarchive-33] On visiting Southampton in 1339, [Edward III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_III) ordered that [walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_town_walls) be built to "close the town". The extensive rebuilding — part of the walls dates from 1175 — culminated in the completion of the western walls in 1380.[^cite_note-bbc-34][^cite_note-sotongovtower-35] Roughly half of the walls, 13 of the original towers, and six gates survive.[^cite_note-bbc-34]

In 1348, the [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death) reached England via merchant vessels calling at Southampton.[^cite_note-RancePP45-36]

### 15th century

Prior to King Henry's departure for the [Battle of Agincourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Agincourt) in 1415, the ringleaders of the "[Southampton Plot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Plot)"—[Richard, Earl of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard,_Earl_of_Cambridge), [Henry Scrope, 3rd Baron Scrope of Masham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Scrope,_3rd_Baron_Scrope_of_Masham), and Sir [Thomas Grey of Heton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Grey_\(1384-1415\))—were accused of high treason and tried at what is now the [Red Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Lion_Inn,_Southampton) public house in the High Street.[^cite_note-37] They were found guilty and summarily executed outside the [Bargate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bargate).[^cite_note-RancePP48-38]

The city walls include [God's House Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God's_House_Tower), built in 1417, the first purpose-built artillery fortification in England.[^cite_note-39] Over the years it has been used as home to the city's gunner, the Town Gaol and even as storage for the Southampton Harbour Board.[^cite_note-sotongovtower-35] Until September 2011, it housed the Museum of Archaeology.[^cite_note-40] The walls were completed in the 15th century,[^cite_note-sotongovmedieval-27] but later development of several new fortifications along Southampton Water and [the Solent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Solent) by [Henry VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VIII) meant that Southampton was no longer dependent upon its own fortifications.[^cite_note-RancePP59-41]

During the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding) had become an important industry for the town. [Henry V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_V_of_England)'s famous warship [*Grace Dieu*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Dieu_\(ship\)) was built in Southampton and launched in 1418.[^cite_note-Rance-21]

The friars passed on ownership of the water supply system itself to the town in 1420.[^cite_note-Davies-31]

On the other hand, many of the medieval buildings once situated within the town walls are now in ruins or have disappeared altogether. From successive incarnations of the motte and bailey castle, only a section of the bailey wall remains today, lying just off Castle Way.[^cite_note-Stone-42]

In 1447 [Henry VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VI_of_England) granted Southampton a charter which made it a county of itself, separate for most purposes from the county of Hampshire. The town was granted its own [sheriff](89516.md), which it retains to this day.[^cite_note-auto-43]

### 16th–18th centuries

The friary was dissolved in 1538 but its ruins remained until they were swept away in the 1940s.[^cite_note-Ruddock-30]

The port was the point of departure for the [Pilgrim Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrim_Fathers) aboard *[Mayflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayflower)* in 1620.[^cite_note-bbc-34] In 1642, during the [English Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War), a Parliamentary garrison moved into Southampton.[^cite_note-RancePP71-44] The Royalists advanced as far as [Redbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redbridge,_Southampton) in March 1644 but were prevented from taking the town.[^cite_note-RancePP71-44]

Southampton became a spa town in 1740.[^cite_note-RancePP78-45] It had also become a popular site for sea bathing by the 1760s, despite the lack of a good quality beach.[^cite_note-RancePP78-45] Innovative buildings specifically for this purpose were built at West Quay, with baths that were filled and emptied by the flow of the tide.[^cite_note-RancePP78-45] Southampton engineer [Walter Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Taylor_\(Southampton\))'s 18th-century mechanisation of the [block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_\(sailing\))-making process was a significant step in the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution).[^cite_note-RancePP95-46] The port was used for military embarkation, including during 18th-century wars with the French.[^cite_note-RancePP92-47]

### 19th century

The town experienced major expansion during the [Victorian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era).[^cite_note-Rance-21] The Southampton Docks company had been formed in 1835.[^cite_note-Rance-21] In October 1838 the foundation stone of the docks was laid[^cite_note-Rance-21] and the first dock opened in 1842.[^cite_note-Rance-21] The structural and economic development of docks continued for the next few decades.[^cite_note-Rance-21] The railway link to London was fully opened in May 1840.[^cite_note-Rance-21] Southampton subsequently became known as *The Gateway to the Empire*.[^cite_note-48]

In his 1854 book *The Cruise of the Steam Yacht North Star* John Choules described Southampton thus: "I hardly know a town that can show a more beautiful Main Street than Southampton, except it be Oxford. The High Street opens from the quay, and under various names it winds in a gently sweeping line for one mile and a half, and is of very handsome width. The variety of style and color of material in the buildings affords an exhibition of outline, light and colour, that I think is seldom equalled. The shops are very elegant, and the streets are kept exceedingly clean."

The port was used for military embarkation, including the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War)[^cite_note-RancePP120-49] and the [Boer War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boer_War).[^cite_note-RancePP138-50]

A new pier, with ten landing stages, was opened by the [Duke of Connaught](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Arthur,_Duke_of_Connaught_and_Strathearn) on 2 June 1892.[^cite_note-auto-43] The [Grand Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theatre,_Southampton) opened in 1898. It was demolished in 1960.[^cite_note-51]

### 20th century



The [memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic_Engineers'_Memorial,_Southampton) to the engineers of the [RMS *Titanic*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic)

From 1904 to 2004, the [Thornycroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_I._Thornycroft_&_Company) shipbuilding yard was a major employer in Southampton,[^cite_note-Rance-21] building and repairing ships used in the two World Wars.[^cite_note-Rance-21] In 1912, the [RMS *Titanic*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic) sailed from Southampton. 497 men (four in five of the crew on board the vessel) were Sotonians,[^cite_note-52] with about a third of those who perished in the tragedy hailing from the city.[^cite_note-bbc-34] Today, visitors can see the [Titanic Engineers' Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanic_Engineers'_Memorial,_Southampton) in East Park, built in 1914, dedicated to the ship's engineers who died on board. Nearby is another Titanic memorial, commemorating the ship's musicians.

Southampton subsequently became the home port for the transatlantic passenger services operated by [Cunard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cunard_Line) with their [Blue Riband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Riband) liner [RMS *Queen Mary*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMS_Queen_Mary) and her running mate [RMS *Queen Elizabeth*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMS_Queen_Elizabeth). In 1938, Southampton docks also became home to the [flying boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_boat) of [Imperial Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Airways).[^cite_note-Rance-21] [Southampton Container Terminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Container_Terminals) first opened in 1968[^cite_note-Rance-21] and has continued to expand.



Home Secretary [John Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Anderson,_1st_Viscount_Waverley) with members of the Auxiliary Fire Service in Southampton in February 1940



Evacuee children returning to Southampton from [Australia](4689264.md) aboard the troop ship [*Andes*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMS_Andes) in September 1945

Southampton was designated No. 1 Military Embarkation port during [World War I](4764461.md)[^cite_note-Rance-21] and became a major centre for treating the returning wounded and [POWs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/POWs).[^cite_note-Rance-21] It was also central to the preparations for the [Invasion of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Europe) during [World War II](32927.md) in 1944.[^cite_note-Rance-21]

The [Supermarine Spitfire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermarine_Spitfire) was designed and developed in Southampton, evolving from the [Schneider trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schneider_trophy)-winning [seaplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplanes) of the 1920s and 1930s. Its designer, [R. J. Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._J._Mitchell), lived in the [Portswood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portswood) area of Southampton, and his house is today marked with a blue plaque.[^cite_note-53] Heavy bombing of the [Woolston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolston,_Southampton) factory in September 1940 destroyed it as well as homes in the vicinity, killing civilians and workers. World War II hit Southampton particularly hard because of its strategic importance as a major commercial port and industrial area. Prior to the [Invasion of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Europe), components for a [Mulberry harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulberry_harbour) were built here.[^cite_note-Rance-21] After [D-Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Day), Southampton docks handled military cargo to help keep the Allied forces supplied,[^cite_note-Rance-21] making it a key target of Luftwaffe bombing raids until late 1944.[^cite_note-RancePP169-54]

Some 630 people died as a result of the [air raids on Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Blitz) and nearly 2,000 more were injured, not to mention the thousands of buildings damaged or destroyed.[^cite_note-55] Pockets of Georgian architecture survived the war, but much of the city was levelled. There has been extensive redevelopment since World War II.[^cite_note-Rance-21] Increasing traffic congestion in the 1920s led to partial demolition of medieval walls around the Bargate in 1932 and 1938.[^cite_note-Rance-21] However, a large portion of those walls remain.

A Royal Charter in 1952 upgraded University College at Highfield to the University of Southampton.[^cite_note-Rance-21] In 1964 Southampton acquired [city status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_the_United_Kingdom), becoming the City of Southampton,[^cite_note-Rance-21][^cite_note-56] and because of the [Local Government Act 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972) was turned into a [non-metropolitan district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_district) within Hampshire in 1973.

Southampton City Council took over most of the functions of Hampshire County Council within the city in April 1997 (including education and social services, but not the fire service), and thus became a [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority).[^cite_note-57]

### 21st century

In the 2010s several developments to the inner-city of Southampton were completed. In 2016 the south section of West Quay, or West Quay South, originally known as West Quay Watermark, was opened to the public. Its public plaza has been used for several annual events, such as an ice skating rink during the winter season,[^cite_note-58] and a public broadcast of [the Wimbledon tennis championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Championships,_Wimbledon).[^cite_note-59] Two new buildings, the [John Hansard Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hansard_Gallery) with City Eye and a secondary site for the [University of Southampton's Nuffield Theatre](98078.md#Arts), in addition to several flats, were built in the "cultural quarter" adjacent to Guildhall Square in 2017.[^cite_note-60]

## Governance



[Southampton Civic Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Civic_Centre)

After the establishment of Hampshire County Council, following the passage of the [Local Government Act 1888](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1888), Southampton became a [county borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_borough) within the [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counties_of_England) of Hampshire, which meant that the corporation in Southampton had the combined powers of a lower-tier (borough) and an upper-tier (county) council within the city boundaries, while the new county council was responsible for upper-tier functions outside the city of Southampton. The ancient shire county, along with its associated assizes, was known as the *County of Southampton*[^cite_note-61] or *Southamptonshire*.[^cite_note-62] This was officially changed to *Hampshire* in 1959, although the county had been commonly known as Hampshire (and previously Hantescire – the origin of the abbreviation "Hants.") for centuries. In the [reorganisation of English and Welsh local government that took effect on 1 April 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972), Southampton lost its county borough when it became a [non-metropolitan district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_district) (i.e. with lower-tier local government functions only) within a modified [non-metropolitan county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_county) of Hampshire (Bournemouth and Christchurch were transferred to the neighbouring non-metropolitan county of [Dorset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorset)). From this date, Hampshire County Council became responsible for all upper-tier functions within its boundaries, including Southampton, until local government was once again reorganised in the late 1990s.

Southampton as a port and city has had a long history of administrative independence of the surrounding county; as far back as the reign of King John the town and its port were removed from the writ of the king's sheriff in Hampshire and the rights of custom and toll were granted by the King to the burgesses of Southampton over the port of Southampton and the Port of Portsmouth;[^cite_note-Edwin_Welch-63] this tax farm was granted for an annual fee of £200 in the charter dated at Orival on 29 June 1199. The definition of the port of Southampton was apparently broader than today and embraced all of the area between [Lymington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymington) and Langstone. The corporation had resident representatives in Newport, Lymington and Portsmouth.[^cite_note-British_History_Online-64] By a charter of [Henry VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_VI_of_England), granted on 9 March 1446/7 (25+26 Hen. VI, m. 52), the mayor, bailiffs and burgesses of the towns and ports of Southampton and Portsmouth became a County incorporate and separate from Hampshire. The status of the town was changed by a later charter of Charles I by at once the formal separation from Portsmouth and the recognition of Southampton as a county. The formal title of the town became "The Town and County of the Town of Southampton". These charters and royal grants, of which there were many, also set out the governance and regulation of the town and port which remained the "constitution" of the town until the local government organisation of the later Victorian period when the Local Government Act 1888 set up county councils and county borough councils across England and Wales, including Southampton County Borough Council. Under this regime, "The Town and County of the Town of Southampton" became a county borough with responsibility for all aspects of local government. On 24 February 1964 Elizabeth II, by letters patent, granted the County Borough of Southampton the title of "City", so creating "The City and County of the City of Southampton".[^cite_note-Soton,County-65] This did not, however, affect its composition or powers.

The city has undergone many changes to its governance over the centuries and once again became administratively independent from Hampshire County as it was made into a [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority) in a local government reorganisation on 1 April 1997, a result of the [Local Government Act 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1992). The district remains part of the Hampshire [ceremonial county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_counties_of_England).

In March 2026, the government announced that it would abolish the existing city council in 2028 as part of [local government reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upcoming_structural_changes_to_local_government_in_England) across Hampshire.[^cite_note-66] These proposals were withdrawn in September 2026.[^cite_note-67]

There are three members of Parliament for the city: [Darren Paffey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Paffey) (Labour) for [Southampton Itchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Itchen), the constituency covering the east of the city; [Satvir Kaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satvir_Kaur) (Labour) for [Southampton Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Test), which covers the west of the city; and [Caroline Nokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Nokes) (Conservative) for [Romsey and Southampton North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romsey_and_Southampton_North), which includes a northern portion of the city.

## Geography

The geography of Southampton is influenced by the sea and rivers. The city lies at the northern tip of the [Southampton Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Water), a deep water estuary, which is a [ria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ria) formed at the end of the last Ice Age and which opens into [The Solent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Solent). At the head of Southampton Water the rivers Test and Itchen converge.[^cite_note-southampton260-86] The Test — which has a salt marsh that makes it ideal for salmon fishing[^cite_note-87] — runs along the western edge of the city, while the Itchen splits Southampton in two—east and west. The city centre is located between the two rivers.

Town Quay is the original public quay, and dates from the 13th century. Today's Eastern Docks were created in the 1830s by land reclamation of the mud flats between the Itchen and Test estuaries. The Western Docks date from the 1930s when the Southern Railway Company commissioned a major land reclamation and dredging programme.[^cite_note-88] Most of the material used for reclamation came from dredging of Southampton Water,[^cite_note-89] to ensure that the port can continue to handle large ships.

Southampton Water has the benefit of a double high tide, with two high tide peaks,[^cite_note-90] making the movement of large ships easier.[^cite_note-91] This is not caused as popularly supposed by the presence of the Isle of Wight, but is a function of the shape and depth of the [English Channel](9230.md). In this area the general water flow is distorted by more local conditions reaching across to France.[^cite_note-92]

The city lies in the [Hampshire Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_Basin), which sits atop chalk beds.[^cite_note-southampton260-86]

The [River Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Test) runs along the western border of the city, separating it from the [New Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Forest). There are bridges over the Test from Southampton, including the road and rail bridges at [Redbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redbridge,_Hampshire) in the south and the [M27 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M27_motorway) to the north. The [River Itchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Itchen,_Hampshire) runs through the middle of the city and is bridged in several places. The northernmost bridge, and the first to be built,[^cite_note-93] is at [Mansbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansbridge), where the [A27 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A27_road) crosses the Itchen. The original bridge is closed to road traffic, but is still standing and open to pedestrians and cyclists. The river is bridged again at [Swaythling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaythling), where Woodmill Bridge separates the tidal and non tidal sections of the river. Further south is [Cobden Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobden_Bridge) which is notable as it was opened as a free bridge (it was originally named the Cobden Free Bridge), and was never a [toll bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toll_bridge). Downstream of the Cobden Bridge is the Northam Railway Bridge, then the [Northam Road Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northam_Bridge), which was the first major pre-stressed concrete bridge to be constructed in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-94] The southernmost, and newest, bridge on the Itchen is the [Itchen Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itchen_Bridge), which is a toll bridge.

Southampton is divided into council wards, suburbs, constituencies, ecclesiastical parishes, and other less formal areas. It has a number of parks and green spaces, the largest being the 148-hectare [Southampton Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Common),[^cite_note-95] parts of which are used to host the annual summer festivals, circuses and fun fairs. In 2006–2007, 1,267 residential dwellings were built in the city — the highest number for 15 years. Over 94 per cent of these were flats.[^cite_note-100]

### Energy



Southampton's geothermal power station

The centre of Southampton is located above a large hot water [aquifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifer) that provides [geothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_energy) power to some of the city's buildings. This energy is processed at a plant in the West Quay region in Southampton city centre, the only [geothermal power station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_power_in_the_United_Kingdom) in the UK. The plant provides electricity for the [Port of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Southampton) and hot water to the [Southampton District Energy Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_District_Energy_Scheme) used by many buildings including the [Westquay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westquay) shopping centre. In a 2006 survey of carbon emissions in major UK cities conducted by British Gas, Southampton was ranked as being one of the lowest [carbon-emitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_emissions) cities in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-106]

## Demography



Population pyramid of Southampton (unitary authority) in 2021

At the 2021 Census, Southampton had a population of 248,922,[^cite_note-107] while the 2016 mid-year population estimates suggests there are 254,275 people within the Southampton area.[^cite_note-SCC_2016_pop_est-108]  At the [2011 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Census_2011), the Southampton built-up area (which is a little larger than the area controlled by the city council) had a population of 253,651.[^cite_note-2011_Census_Data-109] There were 127,630 males and 126,021 females.[^cite_note-2011_Census_Data-109] The 30–44 age range is the most populous, with 51,989 people falling in this age range. Next largest is the 45–59 range with 42,317 people and then 20–24 years with 30,290.[^cite_note-2011_Census_Data-109] The ethnic mix as of 2021 is 80.7% white, 10.6% were Asian or British Asian, 3% black, 2.3% other ethnic groups, and 3.4% were multi-ethnic.[^cite_note-\:0-110]

Between 1996 and 2004, the population of the city increased by 4.9 per cent — the tenth-biggest increase in England.[^cite_note-ippr.org-111] In 2005 the Government Statistics stated that Southampton was the third most densely populated city in the country after London and Portsmouth, respectively.[^cite_note-112] The average age of a Sotonian was 37.6 years in 2016, ranking Southampton as one of the twenty most youthful cities in the UK.[^cite_note-bbc.co.uk-113]

In the 2001 census Southampton and Portsmouth were recorded as being parts of separate urban areas; however by the time of the 2021 census they had merged apolitically to become the sixth-largest built-up area in England with a population of 1,151,300.[^cite_note-114] This built-up area is part of the [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) known as [South Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Hampshire), which is also sometimes referred to as *Solent City*, particularly in the media when discussing development issues and local governance organisational changes.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] With a population of over 1.5 million this makes the region one of the [United Kingdom's most populous metropolitan areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_the_United_Kingdom).[^cite_note-espon-118]

In 2016–17, 169,700 residents of Southampton aged 16–64 were in employment, representing a rate of 71.4% – lower than the national rate of 74.4%. 6,600 were unemployed, representing 5% of the economically active population.[^cite_note-Southampton_Strategic_Assessment_2017-131]

In 2016–17, 24.8% of the city's resident population aged 16–64 were classed as economically inactive, higher than the national rate of 21.8%, although for over 40% of this group the reason was that they were students.[^cite_note-Southampton_Strategic_Assessment_2017-131]

Just over a quarter of the jobs available in the city are in the health and education sector. A further 19 per cent are property and other business and the third-largest sector is wholesale and retail, which accounts for 16.2 per cent.[^cite_note-southampton4-132] Between 1995 and 2004, the number of jobs in Southampton has increased by 18.5 per cent.[^cite_note-ippr.org-111]

In January 2007, the average annual salary in the city was £22,267. This was £1,700 lower than the national average and £3,800 less than the average for the South East.[^cite_note-133]

Inaugurated in 1843, the [Port of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Southampton) has served as a major source of employment for generations. It became particularly prominent as a passenger port in the first half of the 20th century, especially during the inter-war years when it handed nearly half of the UK's passenger traffic. Today, it remains a key hub for luxury [cruise ships](314855.md) and is the largest freight port on the Channel coast and the fourth-largest UK port by tonnage.[^cite_note-134] Unlike other UK port cities, such as [Liverpool](18081.md), [London](17867.md), and [Bristol](36741.md) - where dockside industry has largely relocated from city centres - Southampton has retained much of its inner-city industrial character. While the western docklands remain active and continue to expand, including the opening of a fourth cruise terminal in 2009, some eastern dock areas have been redeveloped. An example is [Ocean Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_Village,_Southampton), a mixed-use [marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina), residential, business and leisure development. The city is also home to the headquarters of the [Maritime and Coastguard Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_and_Coastguard_Agency), the [Marine Accident Investigation Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Accident_Investigation_Branch) (of the Department for Transport), and cruise operator [Carnival UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_Corporation_&_plc).[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

During the 20th century, a more diverse range of industry also came to the city, including aircraft and car manufacturing, cables, electrical engineering products, and petrochemicals. These developed alongside the city's older industries of the docks, grain milling and tobacco processing.[^cite_note-britannica.com-14][^cite_note-137] Later changes saw the loss of [Pirelli General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirelli) cables and [Joseph Rank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Rank)'s Solent Flour Mills.[^cite_note-138]



Westquay Shopping Centre



Westquay South

[University Hospital Southampton NHS Foundation Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Hospital_Southampton_NHS_Foundation_Trust) is one of the city's largest employers. It provides local hospital services to 500,000 people in the Southampton area and specialist regional services to more than 3 million people across the South of England. The Trust owns and manages [Southampton General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_General_Hospital), the [Princess Anne Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Anne_Hospital) and a palliative care service at Countess Mountbatten House, part of the [Moorgreen Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorgreen_Hospital) site in the village of [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End,_Hampshire), just outside the city.

Other major employers in the city include [Ordnance Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordnance_Survey), the UK's national mapping agency, whose headquarters is located in a new building on the outskirts of the city, opened in February 2011.[^cite_note-139] The [Lloyd's Register Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd's_Register) has announced plans to move its London marine operations to a specially developed site at the University of Southampton.[^cite_note-140]

Southampton's largest retail centre, and 27th-largest [in the UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_UK_shopping_centres), is the [Westquay Shopping Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westquay), which opened in September 2000 and hosts major high street stores including [John Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lewis_\(department_store\)) and [Marks and Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marks_and_Spencer). The centre was Phase Two of the West Quay development of the former [Pirelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirelli) undersea cables factory; the first phase of this was the West Quay Retail Park, while the third phase, Watermark Westquay, was put on hold due to the recession. Work resumed in 2015, with plans for this third stage including shops, housing, an hotel and a public piazza alongside the Town Walls on Western Esplanade.[^cite_note-141] Southampton has also been granted a licence for a large casino.[^cite_note-142] A further part of the redevelopment of the West Quay site resulted in a new store, opened on 12 February 2009, for Swedish home products retailer [IKEA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IKEA).[^cite_note-143] [Marlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlands_Shopping_Centre) is a smaller shopping centre, built in the 1990s on the site of the former bus station and located close to the northern side of [Westquay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westquay). In October 2014, the [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_City_Council) approved a follow-up from the Westquay park, WestQuay Watermark. Construction by [Sir Robert McAlpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Robert_McAlpine) commenced in January 2015.[^cite_note-144] Opened in 2016–2017, it has been renamed [Westquay South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westquay#Westquay_South).

In 2007, Southampton was ranked 13th for shopping in the UK.[^cite_note-145]

Southampton currently has three major city centre regeneration schemes under way. Construction on the £132m Bargate Quarter scheme, which is being built on the site of the former Bargate Centre, started in February 2022 and will provide over 500 new homes, new retail and hospitality venues and a new linear park running alongside the medieval town walls which is hoped to be completed by late 2024. A £200m redevelopment scheme opposite [Southampton Central Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Central_railway_station) is also planned and will replace the former Toys R Us store which is situated off Western Esplanade. The 4.8-acre (1.9 ha) Maritime Gateway scheme will have a new pedestrian-led public area with 600 new homes and new commercial space. Located close to the waterfront, Leisure World is Southampton's largest redevelopment project covering 13 acres (5.3 ha) and replacing the original Leisure World development which first opened in 1997. The £280m scheme will deliver 650 new homes, two hotels, a cinema, casino and catering outlets, with the first phase expected to complete by 2024/25.

PwC's Good Growth for Cities 2020 index places Southampton in the top three cities in England in terms of growth.[^cite_note-146] This strength has enabled the city to establish a reputation as a place to do business and has attracted nationally and internationally significant businesses. Prior to the pandemic, Southampton's economy was valued at £8.3 billion [GVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_value_added) and in 2021 £7.84 billion. In 2022 GVA is forecast to recover to £8.2 billion. GVA growth was 10.6% in 2021 as the economy rebounded and growth returned. In 2022 GVA growth is forecast to be just over 5%.

## Culture, media and sport

### Culture



Tudor House, City Centre



SeaCity Museum, Civic Centre

The city is home to the longest surviving stretch of medieval walls in England,[^cite_note-147] as well as a number of museums such as [Tudor House Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_House_Museum), reopened on 30 July 2011 after undergoing extensive restoration and improvement;[^cite_note-148] [God's House Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God's_House_Tower);,[^cite_note-149] an arts & heritage venue run by 'a space' arts;[^cite_note-150] the [Medieval Merchant's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Merchant's_House); and [Solent Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solent_Sky), which focuses on aviation.[^cite_note-151] The [SeaCity Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaCity_Museum) is located in the west wing of the civic centre, formerly occupied by Hampshire Constabulary and the Magistrates' Court, and focuses on Southampton's trading history and on the [*Titanic*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMS_Titanic). The museum received half a million pounds from the [National Lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lottery_\(United_Kingdom\)) in addition to interest from numerous private investors and is budgeted at £28 million.

The annual [Southampton Boat Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Boat_Show) is held in September each year, with over 600 exhibitors present.[^cite_note-152] It runs for just over a week at Mayflower Park on the city's waterfront, where it has been held since 1968.[^cite_note-153] The Boat Show itself is the climax of Sea City, which runs from April to September each year to celebrate Southampton's links with the sea.[^cite_note-154]

The largest theatre in the city is the 2,300-capacity [Mayflower Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayflower_Theatre) (formerly known as the Gaumont), which, as the largest theatre in [Southern England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_England) outside London, has hosted West End shows such as *[Les Misérables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Mis%C3%A9rables_\(musical\))*, *[The Rocky Horror Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Rocky_Horror_Show)* and *[Chitty Chitty Bang Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitty_Chitty_Bang_Bang_\(musical\))*, as well as regular visits from [Welsh National Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_National_Opera) and [English National Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_National_Ballet). There is also the Nuffield Theatre[^cite_note-155] based at the University of Southampton's Highfield campus, which is the city's primary producing theatre. It was awarded The Stage Award for Best Regional Theatre in 2015.[^cite_note-156] It also hosts touring companies and local performing societies (such as Southampton Operatic Society, the Maskers and the University Players).

There are art galleries in the city. The [Southampton City Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_City_Art_Gallery) at the [Civic Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Centre,_Southampton) is one of the best known and as well as a nationally important [Designated Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designated_Collection), houses several permanent and travelling exhibitions. The Solent Showcase at [Southampton Solent University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Solent_University), the John Hansard Gallery at Southampton University as well as smaller galleries including the Art House[^cite_note-157] in Above Bar Street provide a different view.[^cite_note-158] The city's [Bargate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bargate) contains an art gallery run by the arts organisation "a space" who also run the Art Vaults project. This uses several of Southampton's medieval vaults, chambers, halls and cellars as venues for contemporary art installations.



John Hansard Gallery and City Eye

The city has a Cultural Quarter, with galleries, museums, theatres, restaurants, bars, and cafés. It includes the Southampton O2 Guildhall, MAST (Mayflower Studies), the John Hansard Gallery (Studio 144) and City Eye, an arts centre. It is the location of Southampton Art Gallery which opened in 1939 and holds national and international quality exhibitions ranging from painting, sculpture, and drawing, to photography and film, as well as permanent collection and displays. The gallery has a partnership with the National Gallery in London which, in 2021, was celebrated with an exhibition entitled "Creating a National Collection: The Partnership Between Southampton City Art Gallery and the National Gallery."[^cite_note-159]

The city was described in *[The Daily Telegraph](23797577.md)* in 2026 as having "very limited cultural offerings".[^cite_note-t809-160]

#### Shortlisted bid for UK City of Culture 2025

In October 2021, Southampton was longlisted for the [UK City of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_City_of_Culture) 2025.[^cite_note-UKGOV-161] The final bid, submitted on 2 February 2022 was marked by lighting The Bargate red with #MakeItSO projected across it. The city's bid includes plans to celebrate Southampton's people and places, its rich heritage and diversity, the world-class sports and venues, the parks and green spaces and food and drink. On 19 March 2022 it was announced that Southampton had made the short-list of four, alongside [Bradford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Bradford), [County Durham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Durham_\(district\)), and [Wrexham County Borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrexham_County_Borough). In May 2022, it lost its bid, with the winner announced to be [Bradford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bradford_UK_City_of_Culture_2025).[^cite_note-UKGOV-161]

#### Music



The Mayflower Theatre

Southampton has two large live music venues, the Mayflower Theatre (formerly the Gaumont Theatre) and the [Guildhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Guildhall). The Guildhall has seen concerts from a wide range of popular artists, including [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd),[^cite_note-guildhall_tickets-162] [David Bowie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bowie),[^cite_note-guildhall_tickets-162] [Delirious?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delirious%3F),[^cite_note-163] [Manic Street Preachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manic_Street_Preachers),[^cite_note-guildhall_tickets-162] [the Killers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Killers),[^cite_note-guildhall_tickets-162] [Kaiser Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Chiefs),[^cite_note-guildhall_tickets-162] [Amy Winehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Winehouse), [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan), [Suede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suede_\(band\)), [Arctic Monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Monkeys), and [Oasis](161515.md).[^cite_note-guildhall_tickets-162] It also hosts classical concerts presented by the [Bournemouth Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournemouth_Symphony_Orchestra),[^cite_note-164] City of Southampton Orchestra,[^cite_note-165] Southampton Concert Orchestra,[^cite_note-166] [Southampton Philharmonic Choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Philharmonic_Choir),[^cite_note-167] Southampton Choral Society,[^cite_note-168] and the City of Southampton (Albion) Band.

The city also has several smaller music venues, including the Brook, Engine Rooms,[^cite_note-169] The 1865,[^cite_note-170] [The Joiners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Joiners),[^cite_note-171] and Turner Sims,[^cite_note-172] as well as smaller "club circuit" venues like Hampton's and Lennon's, and a number of public houses including the Platform tavern, the Dolphin, the Blue Keys and many others. The Joiners has played host to such acts as [Oasis](161515.md), [Radiohead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiohead), [Green Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Day), [Suede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suede_\(band\)), [PJ Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PJ_Harvey), [the Manic Street Preachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Manic_Street_Preachers), [Coldplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldplay), [the Verve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Verve), [the Libertines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Libertines), and [Franz Ferdinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Ferdinand_\(band\)), while Hampton's and Lennon's have hosted early appearances by [Kate Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Nash), [Scouting for Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scouting_for_Girls), and [Band of Skulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_of_Skulls).

The city is home or birthplace to a number of contemporary musicians such as popstar [Craig David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_David), [Coldplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldplay) drummer [Will Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Champion), [Alt-J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alt-J) singer Joe Newman, singer-songwriter [Aqualung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqualung_\(musician\)), former [Holloways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holloways) singer Rob Skipper, 1980s popstar [Howard Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Jones_\(British_musician\)), as well as Grammy Award-winning popstar [Foxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxes_\(singer\)). Several active rock and metal bands were formed in Southampton, including [Band of Skulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_of_Skulls), [Bury Tomorrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bury_Tomorrow), [Creeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creeper_\(band\)), and [The Delays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Delays). Southampton had a prominent [UK Garage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Garage) scene, championed by the duo [Artful Dodger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artful_Dodger_\(UK_band\)), who formed in the city in the late 1990s,[^cite_note-173] as well as the UKG, grime and bassline producer, [Royal-T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal-T), part of the TQD group formed with [DJ Q](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Q) and Flava D.[^cite_note-174] Notable bands who are now defunct include [Thomas Tantrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Tantrum) (disbanded 2011), [Kids Can't Fly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kids_Can't_Fly) (disbanded 2014), and [Heart in Hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_in_Hand_\(band\)) (disbanded 2015).

### Media

Local media include the *[Southern Daily Echo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Daily_Echo)* newspaper based in [Redbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redbridge,_Hampshire) and *[BBC South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_South)*, which has its regional headquarters in the [city centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_City_Centre) opposite the civic centre. From there the BBC broadcasts *[South Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_South_Today)*, the local television news bulletin and [BBC Radio Solent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Solent). The local ITV franchise is [Meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Meridian), which has its headquarters in [Whiteley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiteley), around 9 mi (14 km) from the city. Until December 2004, the station's studios were located in the [Northam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northam,_Hampshire) area of the city on land reclaimed from the River Itchen. [That's Solent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That's_Solent) is a local television channel that began broadcasting in November 2014, which will be based in and serve Southampton and Portsmouth.

Southampton also has four community FM radio stations, the Queens Award-winning Unity 101 Community Radio,[^cite_note-175] broadcasting full-time on 101.1 MHz since 2006 to the Asian and ethnic communities, and [Voice FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_FM_103.9),[^cite_note-176] located in St Mary's, which has been broadcasting full-time on 103.9 MHz since September 2011. A third station, Awaaz FM,[^cite_note-177] broadcasts on DAB digital to South Hampshire and on FM to Southampton. It caters for the Asian and ethnic community. As of March 2023, the most popular commercial radio station is the [adult contemporary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_contemporary_music) regional radio station [Wave 105](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_105) (13.7%), followed by the [hit music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_hit_radio) station [Capital South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_South) (2.4%) a networked station from London with local breakfast and drive shows. ### Sport



St Mary's Stadium

Southampton is home to [Southampton Football Club](81576.md), nicknamed "The Saints" since 1885; the club currently plays in the [EFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship) at [St Mary's Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's_Stadium), having relocated in 2001 from their 103-year-old former stadium, "[The Dell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dell_\(Southampton\))". They reached the top flight of English football ([First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division)) for the first time in 1966, staying there for eight years. They lifted the [FA Cup](11237.md) with a shock victory over [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) in 1976, returned to the top flight two years later, and stayed there for 27 years (becoming founder members of the [Premier League](11250.md) in 1992) before they were relegated in 2005. The club was promoted back to the Premier League in 2012 following a brief spell in the third tier and severe financial difficulties. In 2015, "The Saints" finished 7th in the Premier League, their highest league finish in 30 years, after a remarkable season under new manager [Ronald Koeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Koeman). Their highest league position came in 1984 when they were runners-up in the old First Division. They were also runners-up in the 1979 [Football League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Cup) final and 2003 [FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup_final). Notable former managers include [Ted Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Bates_\(footballer\)), [Lawrie McMenemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrie_McMenemy), [Chris Nicholl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Nicholl), [Ian Branfoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Branfoot) and [Gordon Strachan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Strachan). There is a strong rivalry with [Portsmouth F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_F.C.) ("[South Coast derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Coast_derby)") which is located only about 20 mi (30 km) away.

[Southampton Women's F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Women's_F.C.) won the inaugural [Women's FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_FA_Cup) in [1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_WFA_Cup_final) when they beat [Stewarton Thistle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilmarnock_FC_Women) 4–1 at [Crystal Palace National Sports Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Palace_National_Sports_Centre). In total they won the tournament eight times between 1971 and 1981. A separate club called [Red Star Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Saints_Girls_&_Ladies_F.C.) reached the final in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_WFA_Cup_final), losing 4–0 to [Doncaster Belles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doncaster_Belles), and their successors Southampton Saints lost the [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_FA_Women's_Cup_final) final to [Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenal_W.F.C.) before dissolving in 2019.[^cite_note-179] The current team [Southampton F.C. Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_F.C._Women) play in the [Women's Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Championship_\(England\)) as of the 2024–25 season.[^cite_note-180]

The two local Sunday Leagues in the Southampton area are the City of Southampton Sunday Football League and the Southampton and District Sunday Football League.



The Utilita Bowl hosting a [Twenty20 International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty20_International)

[Hampshire County Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_County_Cricket_Club) play close to the city, at the [Utilita Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utilita_Bowl) in [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End,_Hampshire), after previously playing at the [County Cricket Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Ground,_Southampton) and the [Antelope Ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antelope_Ground), both near the city centre. The [Southern Brave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Brave) team of [The Hundred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hundred_\(cricket\)) also play at the Ageas Bowl, being the inaugural winners in the men's competition, and two-time finalists in the women's.

The city also has a semi-professional basketball club, the [Solent Kestrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solent_Kestrels). Founded in 1998 the team currently plays at the Solent Sports Complex, on the Solent University campus. They currently play in the [NBL Division 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_League_\(England\)).

The city hockey club, Southampton Hockey Club, was founded in 1938 and currently fields seven senior men's teams and five senior women's teams on a weekly basis, along with boys' and girls' teams from age six upwards.

The city is also well provided for in amateur men's and women's rugby with a number of teams in and around the city, the oldest of which is Trojans RFC,[^cite_note-181] which was promoted to London South West 2 division in 2008/9. A notable former player is [Anthony Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Allen_\(rugby_union\)), who played with Leicester Tigers as a centre. Tottonians[^cite_note-182] are also in London South West division 2 and Southampton RFC[^cite_note-183] are in Hampshire division 1 in 2009/10, alongside Millbrook RFC[^cite_note-184] and Eastleigh RFC. Many of the sides run mini and midi teams from under sevens up to under sixteens for both boys and girls.

The city provides for [yachting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yachting) and water sports, with a number of marinas. From 1977 to 2001 the Whitbread Around the World Yacht Race, now the [Volvo Ocean Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volvo_Ocean_Race), was based in Southampton's [Ocean Village marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_Village,_Southampton).

Southampton Sports Centre is the focal point for the public's sporting and outdoor activities and includes an alpine centre with a dry ski slope, a theme park, and an athletics centre which is used by professional athletes Along with 11 other leisure venues which were formerly operated by the council leisure services, the operating rights have been sold to Park Wood Leisure.[^cite_note-185]

Southampton was named "fittest city in the UK" in 2006 by *[Men's Fitness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Men's_Fitness)* magazine. The results were based on the incidence of heart disease, the amount of junk food and alcohol consumed, and the level of gym membership.[^cite_note-186] In 2007, it had slipped one place behind London, but was still ranked first when it came to the parks and green spaces available for exercise and the amount of television watched by Sotonians was the lowest in the country. [Greyhound racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_racing_in_the_United_Kingdom) was also held at the stadium from 1928 to 1963.

Southampton is also home to two [American football](18951490.md) teams, the Solent Thrashers, who play at the Test Park Sports Ground, and the Southampton Stags, who play at the Wide Lane Sports Facility in [Eastleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastleigh).

The world's oldest surviving bowling green is the [Southampton Old Bowling Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Old_Bowling_Green), which was first used in 1299.[^cite_note-188]

The city is home to two [Octopush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octopush) (also known as underwater hockey) clubs. Bournemouth and Southampton Octopush Club [^cite_note-189] and Totton Octopush Club.[^cite_note-190] Both clubs train at Totton Leisure Centre (with Bournemouth and Southampton OC also training in [Ringwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringwood,_Hampshire)). In the 2023 Nautilus Tournament, Bournemouth and Southampton OC finished 7th (out of 7) in Division 2 with Totton finishing 7th in Division 6.[^cite_note-191]

## Emergency services



Southampton Central Police Station

Southampton's police service is provided by [Hampshire Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_Constabulary). The main base of the Southampton operation is a new, eight-storey purpose-built building which cost £30 million to construct. The building, located on Southern Road, opened in 2011 and is near to [Southampton Central railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Central_railway_station).[^cite_note-192] Previously, the central Southampton operation was located within the west wing of the Civic Centre; however, the ageing facilities and the plans of constructing a new museum in the old police station and magistrates court necessitated the move. There are additional police stations at [Portswood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portswood) and Banister Park as well as a [British Transport Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Transport_Police) station at [Southampton Central railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Central_railway_station). In the year ending June 2019, the crime rate in Southampton was higher than the average crime rate across similar areas.[^cite_note-193]

Southampton's fire cover is provided by [Hampshire & Isle of Wight Fire and Rescue Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampshire_&_Isle_of_Wight_Fire_and_Rescue_Service). There are three fire stations within the city boundaries at [St Mary's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's,_Hampshire), Hightown and [Redbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redbridge,_Hampshire).

The ambulance service is provided by [South Central Ambulance Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_Ambulance_Service), who respond from stations in [Nursling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursling) and Hightown, both on the outskirts of the city.

The national headquarters of the [Maritime and Coastguard Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_and_Coastguard_Agency) is located in Commercial Road.

## Education

See also: [List of schools in Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Southampton)



The National Oceanography Centre, Southampton



The George Thomas building at the University of Southampton

Southampton has two universities, namely the University of Southampton and Solent University.[^cite_note-194] Together, they have a student population of 40,000. Though students numbers had increased in the 80s, 90s, and up to 2011, they began to reduce due to changes in immigration rules and dropped further after 2016 due to [Brexit](41688778.md). Of these, 2,880 are from EU, and the rest are from UK, Asia and Africa.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196]

The [University of Southampton](98078.md), which was founded in 1862 and received its Royal Charter as a university in 1952, has over 22,000 students.[^cite_note-southampton.ac.uk-197] The university is ranked in the top 100 research universities in the world in the [Academic Ranking of World Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Ranking_of_World_Universities) 2010. In 2010, the [THES - QS World University Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/THES_-_QS_World_University_Rankings) positioned the University of Southampton in the top 80 universities in the world. The university considers itself one of the top 5 research universities in the UK.[^cite_note-southampton.ac.uk-197][^cite_note-198][^cite_note-199] The university has a global reputation for research into engineering sciences,[^cite_note-200] [oceanography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanography), [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), [cancer sciences](105219.md), sound and vibration research,[^cite_note-201] computer science, electronics, and [optoelectronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optoelectronics). It is also home to the [National Oceanography Centre, Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Oceanography_Centre,_Southampton) (NOCS), the focus of [Natural Environment Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Environment_Research_Council)-funded [marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_\(ocean\)) research.

[Southampton Solent University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Solent_University) has 17,000[^cite_note-202] students and its strengths are in the training, design, consultancy, research and other services undertaken for business and industry.[^cite_note-203] It is also host to the [Warsash Maritime School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsash_Maritime_School), which provides training and certification for the international shipping and off-shore oil industries.[^cite_note-w514-204]

In addition to state school sixth forms at [St Anne's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Anne's_Catholic_School_\(Southampton\)) and [Bitterne Park School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitterne_Park_School) and an independent sixth form at [King Edward's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Edward_VI_School,_Southampton), there are two sixth-form colleges: [Itchen College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itchen_College) and [Richard Taunton Sixth Form College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Taunton_Sixth_Form_College), and a [further education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Further_education) college, [Southampton City College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_City_College). ### Road

Southampton is a major UK port which has good transport links with the rest of the country. The [M27 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M27_motorway), linking places along the south coast of England, runs just to the north of the city. The [M3 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M3_motorway_\(Great_Britain\)) links the city to London and also with the Midlands and North, via a link to the [A34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A34_road_\(England\)) (part of the [European route E05](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E05)) at [Winchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester). The [M271 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M271_motorway) is a spur of the M27, linking it with the Western Docks and city centre.[^cite_note-206]

### Rail



Southampton Central railway station

Southampton is served by the [rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Great_Britain), both by freight services to and from the docks and passenger services as part of the [national rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rail) system. The main station in the city is [Southampton Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Central_railway_station). Rail routes run:[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210]

- East towards [Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_Harbour_railway_station), [Chichester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chichester_railway_station), [Worthing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worthing_railway_station) and [Brighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brighton_railway_station);

- North to [Winchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester_railway_station), the [Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midlands) and [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Waterloo_railway_station);

- West to [Bournemouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bournemouth_railway_station), [Poole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poole_railway_station), [Dorchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorchester_South_railway_station) and [Weymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weymouth_railway_station);

- North-west towards [Salisbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury_railway_station), [Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Temple_Meads_railway_station) and [Cardiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiff_Central_railway_station).

The route to London was opened in 1840, by what was to become the [London and South Western Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_and_South_Western_Railway) Company. Both this and its successor, [Southern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Railway_\(UK\)), played a significant role in the creation of the modern port following their purchase and development of the town's docks.

The town was the subject of an attempt by a separate company, the [Didcot, Newbury and Southampton Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didcot,_Newbury_and_Southampton_Railway), to open another rail route to the North in the 1880s and some building work, including a surviving embankment, was undertaken in the Hill Lane area. However an agreement was made to construct a junction with the existing LSWR main line south of Winchester.[^cite_note-Sands-211]

### Air

[Southampton Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Airport) is a regional airport located in the town of [Eastleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastleigh), just north of the city. It offers flights to UK and near European destinations, and is connected to the city by a frequent rail service from [Southampton Airport Parkway railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Airport_Parkway_railway_station)[^cite_note-212] and by bus services.[^cite_note-213]

For longer flights, [Heathrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heathrow_Airport) and [Gatwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatwick_Airport) airports are linked by regular [National Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Express_Coaches) coach services.[^cite_note-214]

### Ocean and cruise shipping



*Queen Mary 2* at the new Ocean Terminal, with Isle of Wight passenger ferry *Red Jet 3*

Southampton's tradition of luxury cruising began in the 1840s, one of the pioneers being [P\&O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P\&O), who advertised tours to Egypt.[^cite_note-215] The city was one of Europe's main ports for ocean passenger travel to North America and elsewhere, with British, French, and US liners regularly visiting.

Many of the world's largest cruise ships can regularly be seen in Southampton water, including record-breaking vessels from [Royal Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Caribbean_International) and [Carnival Corporation & plc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_Corporation_&_plc). The latter has headquarters in Southampton, with its brands including [Princess Cruises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Cruises), [P\&O Cruises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P\&O_Cruises), and [Cunard Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cunard_Line).

The city has a particular historical connection to Cunard Line and their fleet of ships. This was particularly evident on 11 November 2008, when the Cunard liner *[Queen Elizabeth 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_2)* departed the city for the final time amid a spectacular fireworks display, after a full day of celebrations.[^cite_note-216] Cunard ships are regularly christened in the city, for example [*Queen Victoria*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS_Queen_Victoria) was named by [The Duchess of Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camilla,_Duchess_of_Cornwall) in December 2007, and [the Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II) named [*Queen Elizabeth*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS_Queen_Elizabeth) in the city during October 2011. The [Duchess of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchess_of_Cambridge) performed the naming ceremony of *[Royal Princess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Princess_\(2012\))* on 13 June 2013.

At certain times of the year, the *[Queen Mary 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_2)*, *Queen Elizabeth*, and *Queen Victoria* may all visit Southampton at the same time, in an event commonly called 'Arrival of the Three Queens'. The [Cunard Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cunard_Line) maintains a regular transatlantic service to [New York City](645042.md) for most of the year, with its [ocean liner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_liner) [RMS Queen Mary 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mary_2).

The importance of Southampton to the cruise industry was indicated by [P\&O Cruises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P\&O_Cruises)' 175th-anniversary celebrations, which included all seven of the company's liners visiting Southampton in a single day. *[Adonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azamara_Pursuit)*, [*Arcadia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcadia_\(2004_ship\)), *Aurora*, *Azura*, *Oceana*, *Oriana*, and *Ventura* all left the city in a procession on 3 July 2012.[^cite_note-217]

The use of the port by cruise ships and bulk cargo ships has led to concerns over air quality.[^cite_note-218] In 2017 [Southampton City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_City_Council) estimated that the port contributed between seven and 23 per cent of air pollution in the city.[^cite_note-219] Cruise ships had to run their engines whilst docked because, unlike other cruise ship ports, Southampton did not provide shore power.[^cite_note-220] In 2019 an environmental pressure group ranked Southampton as the fifth highest in a list of 50 European ports whose air was polluted by sulphur oxide.[^cite_note-221] The cruise industry trade association [Cruise Lines International Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_Lines_International_Association) claimed that the report was inaccurate and used highly questionable methodology.[^cite_note-222] Since September 2021, [Port of Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Southampton) has shore power installed at the Horizon Cruise Terminal at berth 102 and at the Mayflower Cruise Terminal at berth 106, both situated in the port's Western Docks.[^cite_note-223] Shore power will eventually be available at all five of the port's cruise terminals.

### Ferry

While Southampton is no longer the base for any cross-channel ferries, it is the terminus for three internal ferry services, all of which operate from terminals at [Town Quay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_Quay). Two of these, a [car ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RORO) service and a fast [catamaran](195952.md) passenger ferry service, provide links to [East Cowes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowes) and [Cowes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowes), respectively, on the [Isle of Wight](15102.md) and are operated by [Red Funnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Funnel). The third ferry is the [Hythe Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hythe_Pier,_Railway_and_Ferry), providing a passenger service to [Hythe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hythe,_Hampshire) on the other side of [Southampton Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Water).

&#x20;Southampton used to be home to a number of ferry services to the continent, with destinations such as [San Sebastian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n), [Lisbon](18091.md), [Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier), and [Casablanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casablanca). A ferry port was built during the 1960s.[^cite_note-224] However, a number of these relocated to Portsmouth and by 1996, there were no longer any cross-channel car ferries operating from Southampton with the exception of services to the [Isle of Wight](15102.md). The land used for Southampton Ferry Port was sold off and a retail and housing development was built on the site. The Princess Alexandra Dock was converted into a marina. Reception areas for new cars now fill the Eastern Docks where passengers, dry docks and trains used to be.

### Bus

The main [bus](4146.md) operator is [Go South Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_South_Coast) – part of the [Go Ahead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go-Ahead_Group) group – which runs [Bluestar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluestar_\(bus_company\)) as well as [Unilink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unilink), QuayConnect, [morebus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morebus) and [Salisbury Reds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salisbury_Reds_\(bus_company\)). [First Solent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Hampshire_and_Dorset) runs services to Portsmouth via Fareham, while [Xelabus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xelabus) also runs some routes.[^cite_note-225] There is also a door-to-door minibus service called *Southampton Dial-a-Ride*, for residents who cannot access public transport. This is funded by the council and operated by SCA Support Services.[^cite_note-226]

### Tram

A "Lucy Box"

There was a [tram system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Corporation_Tramways) from 1879 to 1949. More recent proposals to reintroduce them surfaced in 2016[^cite_note-227] and 2017,[^cite_note-228] and a [monorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monorail) system was proposed in 1988.[^cite_note-229]

Throughout Southampton there are a number of what are locally called Lucy Boxes, so called because they were made by [W. Lucy & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._Lucy_&_Co.) for power distribution.[^cite_note-230] They still remain along the routes even though the tram lines have been removed. Examples remain in other cities, including [Wolverhampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton).[^cite_note-231]

### Cycling

Southampton City Council announced that it would adopt a new ten year 'Cycling Strategy' from 2017, which would include the construction of multiple cycling highways throughout the city and surrounding suburbs. As of March 2020, Southampton Cycle Network routes 1 (Western), 3 and 4 (Eastern), and 5 (Northern) are substantially complete, with work started on route 6 (Bevois Valley).[^cite_note-232]

## Notable people

Main category: [People from Southampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:People_from_Southampton)



[Craig David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_David) was brought up on the Holyrood estate in the city centre.

People hailing from Southampton are called Sotonians.[^cite_note-sotonian-233] The city has produced a large number of musicians throughout its history, ranging from [hymn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hymn) writer [Isaac Watts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Watts), who was born in Southampton in 1674[^cite_note-234] and whose composition *[O God, Our Help in Ages Past](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_God,_Our_Help_in_Ages_Past)* is played by the bells of [Southampton Civic Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Civic_Centre),[^cite_note-235] to more recent musical acts such as singer [Jona Lewie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jona_Lewie), who was born in Southampton,[^cite_note-Balls-236] singer [Craig David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_David), who grew up on the Holyrood estate,[^cite_note-237] [Coldplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldplay) drummer [Will Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Champion),[^cite_note-238] and solo popstar [Foxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxes_\(singer\)).

Television personalities from Southampton include comedian [Benny Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Hill)[^cite_note-239] and naturalist [Chris Packham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Packham);[^cite_note-240] in recent years the city has also produced a number of competitive [reality television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_television) winners such as [Matt Cardle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Cardle) ([*The X Factor*, 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_X_Factor_\(UK_series_7\))).[^cite_note-241] Radio personality [Scott Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Mills) was also born in Southampton.[^cite_note-242] Actor [Garry Chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Chalk) was also born in Southampton.

Novelist [Jane Austen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Austen) lived in Southampton for a number of years,[^cite_note-243] and the city has also been home to a number of artists, including [Edward John Gregory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_John_Gregory),[^cite_note-244] Hubert von Herkomer,[^cite_note-245] and [John Everett Millais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Everett_Millais).[^cite_note-246] The [feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminism) and [suffragist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffrage) [Emily Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Davies) was born there in 1830.[^cite_note-ODNB-247] Sir [Leon Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Simon_\(Zionist\)), President of the [Hebrew University of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_University_of_Jerusalem), was born in Southampton.

Sports people born in Southampton include [rugby union](25405.md) player [Mike Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Brown_\(rugby_union\)),[^cite_note-248] [Australian](4689264.md) [tennis](29773.md) player [Wally Masur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wally_Masur),[^cite_note-249] England Football Player [Wayne Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Bridge), and [Republic of Ireland national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_women's_national_football_team) player [Lucy Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_Quinn).

Being a port city, Southampton has been home to a number of seafarers, including [Charles Fryatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Fryatt), who rammed a German U-boat with his merchant ship during World War I;[^cite_note-250] [John Jellicoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Jellicoe,_1st_Earl_Jellicoe), who served as [Admiral of the Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_of_the_Fleet_\(Royal_Navy\)) during the same war and later became [Governor-General of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_New_Zealand);[^cite_note-251] and the last survivor of the RMS *Titanic*, [Millvina Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millvina_Dean).[^cite_note-252]

[Richard Aslatt Pearce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Aslatt_Pearce), the first deaf-mute Anglican clergyman, was born in Portswood, Southampton.[^cite_note-Roe_2009-253]

Former Governor of Buenos Aires, and ruler of Argentina, [Juan Manuel de Rosas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Manuel_de_Rosas) went into exile in [Swaythling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaythling) in 1852. He was buried in [Southampton Old Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southampton_Old_Cemetery) before his body was repatriated to [La Recoleta Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Recoleta_Cemetery) in Buenos Aires in 1989 by the then president [Carlos Menem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Menem).

Inventor [Thomas Dowding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Dowding) (1816– 1883) lived in Southampton for several years and helped stop a large fire.[^cite_note-254]

[Ally Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ally_Law), youtuber and parkour practitioner, was born in Southampton.[^cite_note-255]

Canadian pacifist [Robert Edis Fairbairn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Edis_Fairbairn) (1879–1953) was born in Southampton.

[Rishi Sunak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishi_Sunak), former UK prime minister and former Chancellor of the Exchequer, was born in Southampton in 1980.[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-Hatherley_Sunak-257]

Film director [Ken Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Russell) was born in Southampton in 1927, and [Laura Carmichael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Carmichael), actress known for [Downton Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downton_Abbey), was born and grew up in Southampton.
