**Ali Ardashir Larijani**[^cite_note-13] (3 June 1958 – 17 March 2026) was an Iranian politician, military officer, and philosopher who served as the [secretary of the Supreme National Security Council](82647047.md) from 2025 until [his assassination](82710049.md) in 2026. He had previously served in the position from 2005 to 2007. From late December 2025 until his assassination, he was widely considered to be one of the most powerful officials in the Iranian government.[^cite_note-defacto-14][^cite_note-defacto2-15][^cite_note-auto1-16]

Born in [Najaf](200512.md), Iraq, to the ethnic [Mazanderani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazanderani_people)-origin [Larijani family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larijani_family), he studied [Western philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_philosophy) at the [University of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tehran). He rose to prominence within the Islamic Republic after joining the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) in 1981. He served as the [speaker](24397880.md) of the [Parliament of Iran](383276.md) from 2008 to 2020. He was a member of the [Expediency Discernment Council](452730.md) from 2020 until his death, having previously served from 1997 to 2008. He filed for candidacy in the [2024 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Iranian_presidential_election), but was ultimately disqualified. He previously ran in [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Iranian_presidential_election), but finished in sixth place, and was also disqualified from running in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Iranian_presidential_election).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

Larijani was one of the two representatives of the [Supreme Leader](36814344.md) [Ali Khamenei](385653.md) to the council, the other being [Hassan Rouhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Rouhani). In his post as secretary, he functioned as the top negotiator on issues of national security, including the [nuclear program of Iran](721807.md). He was also a member of the [Supreme Council of the Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Council_of_the_Cultural_Revolution).

From c. 28 December 2025 until 17 March 2026,[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Larijani was described as the country's most powerful man by multiple newspapers like *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)*,[^cite_note-22] or as "*[de facto](43594.md)* leader" by *[The Australian](396952.md)* and others.[^cite_note-defacto-14][^cite_note-defacto2-15][^cite_note-auto1-16] Reportedly the [IRGC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRGC) supported him.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-defacto2-15] On 15 January 2026, following a wave of [protests](81976180.md) and the ensuing [massacres](82036668.md), the [United States](3434750.md) imposed new sanctions on Larijani for his part in repressing protesters.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] He was described as the "mastermind" of the January 2026 crackdown, leveraging his close ties to IRGC commanders and intelligence services, together with his family's longstanding connections to senior clerics, to consolidate support across rival factions and prepare to assume leadership after Khamenei's death.[^cite_note-27] On 17 March 2026, Larijani was [assassinated in an Israeli airstrike](82710049.md).

## Early life and education

Black-and-white headshot of a young Ali Larijani, with a short haircut

Larijani as a child, 1965

Ali Larijani was born on 3 June 1958 in [Najaf](200512.md), Iraq, to Iranian parents of ethnic [Mazanderani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazanderani_people) background.[^cite_note-28] He hailed from a religious [Shia](26961.md) gentry family based in [Amol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amol), a city in the [Mazandaran Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran_Province), located in northern [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-Sahimi_2009-29] His father was a leading [Twelver Shia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelver_Shia) cleric, [Hashim Larijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashim_Larijani).[^cite_note-Wehrey_2009-30] His parents moved to Najaf in 1931 due to pressure of the ruler [Reza Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Shah), but returned to Iran in 1961. During the later years of Reza Shah's reign, members of the Larijani family, including Hashim Larijani, were exiled to the [Kurdish-inhabited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Kurds) cities of [Bukan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukan) and [Sardasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardasht,_West_Azerbaijan) in northwestern Iran, where they resided for a time before eventually being allowed to return to northern Iran.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-Sahimi_2009-29]

Larijani was a graduate of the [Qom Seminary](32825717.md).[^cite_note-33] He also held a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science) degree in [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science) and [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics) from [Aryamehr University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryamehr_University_of_Technology) and held a [master's degree](170346.md) and [PhD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhD) in [Western philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_philosophy) from the [University of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tehran).[^cite_note-Sahimi_2009-29] Initially, he wanted to continue his graduate studies in computer science, but changed his subject after consultation with [Morteza Motahhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morteza_Motahhari), his future father-in-law. Larijani published books on [Immanuel Kant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immanuel_Kant), [Saul Kripke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saul_Kripke), and [David Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lewis_\(philosopher\)).[^cite_note-34] Larijani was a faculty member of the School of Literature and Humanities at the University of Tehran.[^cite_note-35]

## Political career (1981–2026)

Larijani was a commander of the [Iranian Revolutionary Guards](1285472.md) (IRGC) as well as a veteran of the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) (1980–1988).[^cite_note-Wehrey_2009-30] He served as the deputy minister of labor and social affairs. Later he was appointed deputy minister of information and communications technology.[^cite_note-Samaha_2013-8] In March 1994, he was appointed head of the [Islamic Republic of Iran Broadcasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIB) (IRIB), replacing [Mohammad Hashemi Rafsanjani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Hashemi_Rafsanjani) in the post.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] He was in office until 21 July 2004 and was succeeded by [Ezzatollah Zarghami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezzatollah_Zarghami) after serving ten years in the post. He became security adviser to Supreme Leader [Ayatollah Ali Khamenei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayatollah_Ali_Khamenei) in August 2004.

Larijani was a presidential candidate for the [2005 presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Iranian_presidential_election), where he ranked sixth, winning 5.94% of the votes. He was considered the most important presidential candidate of the conservative alliance for the 2005 presidential elections. He was supported by the [Islamic Society of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Society_of_Engineers) (ISE), among other conservative groups. He was the final choice of the conservative *[Council for Coordination of the Forces of the Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordination_Council_of_Islamic_Revolution_Forces)*, made up from representatives of some influential conservative parties and organizations. However, he proved to be the least popular of the three conservative candidates, the others being [Mahmoud Ahmadinejad](7907317.md) (second rank in the first round, winner in the second round) and [Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf](1421458.md) (fourth rank in the first round).

In 2005, Larijani was appointed the [Supreme National Security Council](6295948.md) secretary. This body helps draw up nuclear and other policies issued by the supreme leader.[^cite_note-38] He replaced [Hassan Rouhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Rouhani) in the post.[^cite_note-39] As a chief nuclear negotiator, Iranian analysts said he differed with the president over how to pursue negotiations with his European counterparts and say he backed a more pragmatic approach.

As Iran's top nuclear envoy, Larijani said on 25 April 2007 that he expected "new ideas" from senior EU official [Javier Solana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Solana) at talks on resolving the deadlock between [Tehran](57654.md)'s refusal to freeze [its nuclear programme](721807.md) and [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) demands that it do so.[^cite_note-40]



Larijani at [Munich Security Conference](1464620.md), Germany, 2007

Larijani was the secretary of the Supreme National Security Council from 15 August 2005 to 20 October 2007, replacing [Hassan Rouhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Rouhani); he was appointed to the position by President [Mahmoud Ahmadinejad](7907317.md).[^cite_note-41] Larijani's resignation from the secretary position was accepted on 20 October 2007; President Ahmadinejad had turned down his previous resignations.[^cite_note-42]

In the [March 2008 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Iranian_parliamentary_election), Larijani won a seat from [Qom](638742.md). He said he was willing to work with Ahmadinejad; according to Larijani, he did not disagree with Ahmadinejad on ideological issues and had only "differences in style". In May 2008, Larijani became speaker of the [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majlis_of_Iran). He was reelected in the next year as chairman of the parliament. He was re-elected in the [2012 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Iranian_legislative_election), receiving the highest vote of any candidate in the Qom district. He was also elected for another term as chairman of the parliament on 5 June 2012 and was sworn in on 11 June 2012.

Larijani implied on 21 June 2009 that authorities took the side of one candidate without clarifying which candidate.[^cite_note-43] Just after the election, Larijani reportedly congratulated presidential candidate [Mir Hossein Mousavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mir_Hossein_Mousavi) as he, having "access to firsthand and classified information and news", believed Mousavi had won the election.[^cite_note-44] However, on 22 October 2012, during a Q and A meeting with the students of [Iran University of Science and Technology](2073099.md), Larijani denied the allegations that he had congratulated Mousavi.[^cite_note-45] He was elected as speaker in the new Majlis in May 2016.[^cite_note-46]



Larijani meeting with Swedish prime minister [Stefan Löfven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_L%C3%B6fven), 2017



Larijani with Japanese prime minister [Shinzo Abe](2634434.md) in 2019

In May 2021, Larijani declared his bid for the presidency in the [2021 Iranian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Iranian_presidential_election). However, the vetting [Guardian Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardian_Council), in a decision that astounded both the conservatives and reformists, disqualified him from running. Considering Larijani's long career as an Islamic Republic insider who has been part of the top echelons of power since the 1979 revolution, his disqualification was a possibility even his staunch detractors could not envision.

In May 2024, Larijani submitted his application for his candidacy for the president in the [2024 Iranian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Iranian_presidential_election), but was again rejected by the Guardian Council.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

In March 2025, U.S. president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) sent a [letter to Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump's_letter_to_Ali_Khamenei) seeking to reopen nuclear weapons negotiations.[^cite_note-49] Ayatollah Ali Khamenei later said, "Some bullying governments insist on negotiations not to resolve issues but to impose their own expectations," which was seen as in response to the letter.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Following this, in late March 2025, Larijani said Iran would have no choice but to develop nuclear weapons if attacked by the United States, Israel or its allies.[^cite_note-52]

On 13 June 2025 the [Iran–Israel war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War) broke out with attacks on several nuclear facilities.[^cite_note-53] On 22 June, the [United States Air Force](32090.md) and [Navy](20518076.md) [attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_strikes_on_Iranian_nuclear_sites) the [Natanz Nuclear Facility](43648558.md), [Fordow Fuel Enrichment Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordow_Fuel_Enrichment_Plant), and the [Isfahan Nuclear Technology/Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isfahan_Nuclear_Technology/Research_Center).[^cite_note-54]

On 5 August 2025, Larijani was appointed by President [Masoud Pezeshkian](2650383.md) to become secretary of the Supreme National Security Council for a second time.[^cite_note-55] According to *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, Larijani ran Iran from January 2026 until his death in March of that same year and was in "charge of crushing, with lethal force, the recent protests demanding the end of Islamic rule".[^cite_note-56] Following the [assassination of Ali Khamenei](82537558.md), Larijani said that the Iranian government will not "leave Trump alone".[^cite_note-57]

In response to the [European Union](9317.md)'s decision to label the IRGC as a [terrorist organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_organization), Larijani [tweeted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweeted) that the military forces of any countries supporting the EU's decision against the IRGC would be considered terrorist groups and would face consequences of their actions.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

## Political positions

Larijani was considered to maintain [Motalefeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motalefeh) membership and views while in [Hashemi Rafsanjani cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Akbar_Hashemi_Rafsanjani_\(1989%E2%80%9397\)) (1992–1994).[^cite_note-60] Iranian scholar Mehdi Moslem in his 2002 book *Factional Politics in Post-Khomeini Iran*, suggests that Larijani had been a member of [Motalefeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motalefeh) and part of the 'traditional right'.[^cite_note-61] Payam Mohseni, a fellow at the [Belfer Center for Science and International Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belfer_Center_for_Science_and_International_Affairs), classified Larijani as a lead figure in the 'theocratic right' camp, whose other prominent members are [Mahmoud Hashemi Shahroudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Hashemi_Shahroudi) and [Mohammad Reza Mahdavi Kani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Reza_Mahdavi_Kani).[^cite_note-62]



Larijani with [Martin Schulz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Schulz), president of the [European Parliament](9581.md), during a meeting in Tehran on 15 October 2015

Larijani was one of the leaders of the [Principlists Pervasive Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principlists_Pervasive_Coalition) in the [2008 parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Iranian_legislative_election),[^cite_note-63] and a [United Front of Principlists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Front_of_Principlists) leader.[^cite_note-64] During the [Iranian 2016 parliamentary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Iranian_legislative_election) Larijani was the leader of the [Followers of Wilayat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Followers_of_Wilayat) faction,[^cite_note-65] although he was backed by the reformist [List of Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Hope) and said he was running as an [independent candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_candidate).[^cite_note-66]

He was also described as a [center-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center-right) or [moderate conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moderate_conservative) politician who "slowly distanced himself from the [Principlist camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Principlists)"[^cite_note-Ehsan_Bodaghi_2017-67] and a "conservative-turned-moderate".[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] He was sometimes described as taking a hardline approach regarding relations to the West, but as a pragmatist regarding internal affairs.[^cite_note-71]

Larijani was known to have close associates, including the interior minister [Abdolreza Rahmani Fazli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdolreza_Rahmani_Fazli),[^cite_note-72] [Behrouz Nemati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behrouz_Nemati), spokesman for the parliament's presiding board,[^cite_note-Ehsan_Bodaghi_2017-67] and [Kazem Jalali](5692027.md), head of the [parliament's research center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majlis_Research_Center).[^cite_note-Ehsan_Bodaghi_2017-67]



Larijani meeting with French National Assembly president [Claude Bartolone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Bartolone) in Tehran, 6 September 2016

Larijani supported [pragmatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatism)[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] and was because of this seen as a "pragmatic conservative", he was also inspired by [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping)'s model of the People's Republic of China.[^cite_note-75] However Larijani was critical of too much state involvement in the economy and supported a gradual economic and cultural opening alongside diplomatic engagement.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] According to Ali Alfoneh, Larijani was also an [Iranian nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_nationalist).[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] Larijani talked often about sanctions and the economic pressure which comes from the west.[^cite_note-87]

Larijani saw [Velayat-e faqih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velayat-e_faqih) as a form of [democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy).[^cite_note-88]

Larijani was opposed to [Mohammad Khatami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Khatami), while supporting [Akbar Hashemi Rafsanjani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar_Hashemi_Rafsanjani).[^cite_note-89] Larijani who was against Ahmadinejad supported some reforms of [Hassan Rouhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Rouhani).[^cite_note-90]

One day before his death, Larijani rebuked Islamic countries – particularly the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAE) – for "abandoning Iran" and siding with the U.S. and Israel.[^cite_note-91]

Larijani was strongly influenced by his father-in-law [Morteza Motahhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morteza_Motahhari).[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] However, it is not known whether Larijani supported Mothhari's [Third Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Position) [Islamic socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_socialism).[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]

## Role in the suppression of domestic dissent



Larijani with Iranian Supreme Leader [Mojtaba Khamenei](23324096.md)

Throughout his career, Ali Larijani held several high-ranking positions that placed him at the center of the Islamic Republic’s security and information apparatus. While often characterized as a "pragmatic conservative" in foreign policy, his domestic record was marked by the oversight of significant crackdowns on civil unrest.[^cite_note-BritannicaLarijani-96][^cite_note-GuardianLarijani-97]

### Early security and media roles

As the head of the Islamic Republic of Iran Broadcasting (IRIB) from 1994 to 2004, Larijani was criticized for utilizing state media as a tool for the "mass assassination" of the character of [intellectuals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual) and [dissidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissident).[^cite_note-ElPaisLarijani-98] During this period, the IRIB aired forced confessions of [political activists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activism), a practice that human rights organizations identified as a hallmark of the regime's efforts to delegitimize domestic opposition.[^cite_note-ElPaisLarijani-98][^cite_note-MediaLineLarijani-99]

### 2026 suppression of protests

Following his appointment as Secretary of the Supreme National Security Council (SNSC) in June 2025, Ali Larijani was identified by international monitors as a primary architect of the regime’s [lethal response](82036668.md) to the [2025–2026 Iranian protests](81976180.md).[^cite_note-Hindustan2026-100] Although often characterized as a "pragmatic conservative" in foreign policy, Larijani adopted a hardline domestic stance, reportedly modeling the regime's security response on the "[Tiananmen Square](44534.md)" method of overwhelming force.[^cite_note-IranWire2026-101][^cite_note-MediaLineLarijani-99]

Larijani was among the first senior officials to publicly advocate for the use of [lethal force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal_force) against demonstrators, labeling them "armed terrorist networks."[^cite_note-USTreasury2026-102][^cite_note-IranHRM2026-103] Human rights organizations reported that the SNSC, under Larijani's coordination, authorized the use of military-grade weaponry and [live ammunition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_ammunition), leading to the deadliest wave of repression in the history of the Islamic Republic.[^cite_note-BritannicaLarijani-96][^cite_note-IranHRM2026-103]

According to estimates from human rights monitors, between 7,000 and 36,500 Iranians were killed during the "January uprising," with a significant portion of these casualties occurring on 8 and 9 January 2026.[^cite_note-IranWire2026-101][^cite_note-IranIntl2026-104] On 12 January 2026, the [United States Department of the Treasury](53667.md) sanctioned Larijani, specifically citing his role in "coordinating the suppression of protests and issuing orders for the use of force against peaceful protesters."[^cite_note-USTreasury2026-102]

## Public image

According to a poll conducted in March 2016 by *Information and Public Opinion Solutions LLC* (iPOS) among Iranian citizens, Larijani had a 45% approval and 34% disapproval rating and thus a +11% net popularity, while 11% of voters did not [recognize the name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_recognition).[^cite_note-105]

According to the [University of Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maryland,_College_Park)'s School of Public Policy report *Iranian Public Opinion Soon After the [Twelve-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Day_War)* released in December 2025, Larijani was viewed "favorably" by 43% (including 8% "very favorably") of the population, up from 32% in March 2024.[^cite_note-CISSMIran2025Report-106]

## Personal life

Larijani was a brother of [Sadiq Larijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadiq_Larijani) (President of the [Judicature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicature)), [Mohammad-Javad Larijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad-Javad_Larijani), [Bagher Larijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagher_Larijani) (Faculty Member of [Tehran University of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_University_of_Medical_Sciences)), and [Fazel Larijani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fazel_Larijani) (Iran's former cultural attachée in [Ottawa](22219.md)).[^cite_note-Sahimi_2009-29][^cite_note-107] Larijani was also a cousin of [Ahmad Tavakkoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Tavakkoli) (Larijani's and Tavakkoli's mothers are sisters).[^cite_note-Sahimi_2009-29] Larijani was the son-in-law of Ayatollah [Morteza Motahhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morteza_Motahhari).[^cite_note-Sahimi_2009-29][^cite_note-108]

As of 2018, his daughter, Fatemeh Ardeshir-Larijani, studied at the [University Hospitals Cleveland Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Hospitals_Cleveland_Medical_Center) in the United States.[^cite_note-109] During the [2026 Iran massacres](82036668.md), [Iranian American](2234641.md) activists protested her employment at [Emory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University)'s [Winship Cancer Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winship_Cancer_Institute) in [Atlanta, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta,_Georgia).[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] [Buddy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_Carter), a Republican congressman from Georgia, demanded for Ardeshir-Larijani to be fired and her medical licensed to be revoked, labeling her a threat to national security.[^cite_note-113] She was fired two days later.[^cite_note-Iran_International_2026-114][^cite_note-115] After the start of the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), the Trump administration terminated Ardeshir-Larijani's and her husband's green cards and deported them from the U.S.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

## Assassination

Main article: [Assassination of Ali Larijani](82710049.md)

On the night between 16–17 March 2026 during the [Iran war](82533582.md), Larijani was killed in an Israeli airstrike.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] His son Morteza and the head of his office Alireza Bayat were also killed in the strike.[^cite_note-121] [Fars News Agency](5000099.md) reported that he was killed by a U.S.–Israeli strike as he was visiting his daughter in the outskirts of eastern Tehran.[^cite_note-122] Israeli intelligence officials on the other hand stated that they were able to track his movements after being tipped off by Tehran's residents and bombed him while he was meeting with other officials at a hideout in Tehran's outskirts.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] The commander of Iran's internal [Basij](2892082.md) militia, [Gholamreza Soleimani](61221350.md), was also killed in a separate Israeli airstrike on the same night.[^cite_note-125]

According to *The New York Times*, the assassination of Larijani raised anxiety among regime officials. One official described shaking when he received the call about the death of Larijani, and said that after the assassination "there was a pervasive sense of anxiety, that Israel would not stop until all of Iran's leaders were killed and the Islamic Republic toppled."[^cite_note-\:0-126] Other officials were said to be concerned about their own safety as well as that of Iran's remaining leaders, wondering about who would be targeted next.[^cite_note-\:0-126] Reacting to the confirmation of his death by Iran, Iran's supreme leader [Mojtaba Khamenei](23324096.md) released a public statement expressing regret and vows for vengeance.[^cite_note-\:1-127][^cite_note-128] According to Iran's foreign minister, [Abbas Araghchi](21951274.md), his death will not topple "Tehran's strong political structure."[^cite_note-\:1-127]

A conservative Iranian analyst with ties to the regime government, Hatef Salehi, referred to the political implications of the assassination and posted that Larijani's absence "would decrease the chances of finding a low-cost political solution to end the war."[^cite_note-\:0-126]

On 24 March 2026, he was succeeded by [Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr](54308220.md) as Secretary of the Supreme National Security Council.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130]

Larijani was buried at the [Fatima Masumeh Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatima_Masumeh_Shrine) in his hometown, [Qom](638742.md).[^cite_note-auto-7]
