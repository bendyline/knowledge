The **Canary Islands** ([/kəˈnɛəri/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b0/En-us-canary.ogg/En-us-canary.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b0/En-us-canary.ogg/En-us-canary.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Spanish](26825.md): *Canarias* [\[kaˈnaɾjas\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f3/Pronunciation_of_Canarias_in_Spanish.ogg/Pronunciation_of_Canarias_in_Spanish.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f3/Pronunciation_of_Canarias_in_Spanish.ogg/Pronunciation_of_Canarias_in_Spanish.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) or **Canaries** are an [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) in the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) and the southernmost [autonomous community](55002.md) of [Spain](26667.md), located about 100 kilometres (60 mi) off the northwest coast of [Africa](5334607.md). The archipelago has a population of approximately 2.27 million inhabitants, making it the most populous overseas [special territory of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_member_state_territories_and_the_European_Union).[^cite_note-Utreta-8][^cite_note-\:0-9][^cite_note-10]

The seven main islands, listed from largest to smallest by area, are [Tenerife](269223.md), [Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura), [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria), [Lanzarote](304185.md), [La Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Palma), [La Gomera](220016.md), and [El Hierro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Hierro). The only other inhabited island is [La Graciosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graciosa,_Canary_Islands), which is administratively dependent on Lanzarote. The archipelago also includes numerous smaller islands and islets, such as [Alegranza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alegranza), [Isla de Lobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islote_de_Lobos), [Montaña Clara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monta%C3%B1a_Clara), [Roque del Oeste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_del_Oeste), and [Roque del Este](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_del_Este), as well as various rocks, including [Garachico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_de_Garachico) and [Anaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roques_de_Anaga). Historically, the island chain was referred to as "the Fortunate Isles".[^cite_note-11] The Canary Islands are the southernmost and westernmost region of Spain as well as the largest and most populous archipelago of [Macaronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaronesia).[^cite_note-catalogo.museosdetenerife.org-12] They are also the largest and most populated archipelago in Spain. Owing to their strategic location, the Canary Islands have long been considered a link between [Africa](5334607.md), [Europe](9239.md), and the [Americas](29833.md).[^cite_note-13]

In October 2025, the Canary Islands had a population of 2,268,035,[^cite_note-14] with a density of 305 inhabitants per km<sup>2</sup>, making them the seventh most populous autonomous community in Spain and the third-highest in population density, behind only [Madrid](69802.md) and the [Basque Country](20514897.md). The population is mostly concentrated on the two capital islands, with around 43% on Tenerife and 39% on Gran Canaria. In 2026, the Canary Islands are expected to have a nominal GDP of €64.123 billion, resulting in a GDP per capita of €28,272.[^cite_note-15]

The Canary Islands, especially Tenerife, Gran Canaria, Fuerteventura, and Lanzarote, are a major tourist destination, with over 14.1 million visitors in 2023.[^cite_note-16] This is due to their beaches, [subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_climate), and important natural attractions, especially [Maspalomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspalomas) in [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria) and Mount [Teide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teide), a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in [Tenerife](269223.md). Mount Teide is the [highest peak in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mountains_in_Spain) and the [3rd tallest volcano in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_volcanoes_by_elevation), measured from its base on the ocean floor.[^cite_note-gobiernodecanarias1-17] The islands have warm summers and winters warm enough for the climate to be technically tropical at sea level.[^cite_note-18] The amount of precipitation and the level of maritime moderation vary depending on location and elevation. The archipelago includes green areas as well as semi-desert. The islands' high mountains are ideal for astronomical observation, because they lie above the [temperature inversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inversion_\(meteorology\)) layer. As a result, the archipelago has two professional astronomical observatories: the [Teide Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teide_Observatory) on Tenerife, and [Roque de los Muchachos Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_de_los_Muchachos_Observatory) on La Palma.[^cite_note-19]

In 1927, the [Province of Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Canary_Islands) was split into two provinces, [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife_\(province\)) and [Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Las_Palmas). In 1982, the autonomous community of the Canary Islands was established. The cities of [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife) and [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria) are, jointly, the capitals of the islands.[^cite_note-estatuto2018-20][^cite_note-es.wikisource.org-21][^cite_note-gobiernodecanarias.org-22] Those cities are also, respectively, the capitals of the [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_provinces) of Santa Cruz de Tenerife and Las Palmas. Las Palmas de Gran Canaria has been the largest city in the Canaries since 1768, except for a brief period in the 1910s.[^cite_note-23] Between the [1833 territorial division of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1833_territorial_division_of_Spain) and 1927, Santa Cruz de Tenerife was the sole capital of the Canary Islands. In 1927, it was ordered by decree that the capital of the Canary Islands would be shared between two cities, and this arrangement persists to the present day.[^cite_note-es.wikisource.org-21][^cite_note-Real_Decreto_Canarias-24] The third largest city in the Canary Islands is [San Cristóbal de La Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna), another World Heritage Site on Tenerife.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

During the [Age of Sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Sail), the islands were the main stopover for [Spanish galleons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_treasure_fleet) during the [Spanish colonisation of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonisation_of_the_Americas), which sailed that far south in order to catch the prevailing northeasterly [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_winds).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

## Etymology

The name *Islas Canarias* is likely derived from the Latin name *Canariae Insulae*, meaning 'Islands of the Dogs', perhaps because [monk seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monk_seals) or sea dogs were abundant, a name that was evidently generalised from the ancient name of one of these islands, *Canaria* – presumably Gran Canaria. According to the historian [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder), the island *Canaria* contained "vast multitudes of dogs of very large size".[^cite_note-30] The connection to dogs is retained in their depiction on the islands' coat-of-arms.

Other theories speculate that the name comes from the [Nukkari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nukkari) Berber tribe living in [North Africa](21714.md), named in Roman sources as *Canarii*, though Pliny again mentions the relation of this term with dogs.[^cite_note-31]

The name of the islands is not derived from the [canary bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_canary); rather, the birds are named after the islands.[^cite_note-32]

[Canary Wharf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Wharf), located on the [Isle of Dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Dogs) in London, was named after a warehouse which handled imports from the islands.[^cite_note-Brit-33]

## History

### Ancient and pre-Hispanic times

Main article: [Canary Islands in pre-colonial times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands_in_pre-colonial_times)



[Guanche mummy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanche_mummies) of a woman (830 AD). [Museo de la Naturaleza y el Hombre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museo_de_la_Naturaleza_y_el_Hombre), [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife).

Before the arrival of humans, the Canaries were inhabited by prehistoric animals including the giant lizard (*[Gallotia goliath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallotia_goliath)*), the [Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerife_giant_rat) and [Gran Canaria giant rats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria_giant_rat),[^cite_note-34] and giant tortoises, *[Geochelone burchardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geochelone_burchardi)* and *[Geochelone vulcanica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geochelone_vulcanica)*.

Although the original settlement of what are now called the Canary Islands is not entirely clear, linguistic, genetic, and archaeological analyses indicate that [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) peoples were living on the Canary Islands at least 2,000 years ago, possibly 3,000, and that they shared a common origin with the [Amazighs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazighs) on the nearby North African coast.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-\:3-36][^cite_note-37] Reaching the islands may have taken place using several small boats, landing on the easternmost islands Lanzarote and Fuerteventura. These groups came to be known collectively as the [Guanches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanches), although *Guanches* had been the name for only the indigenous inhabitants of Tenerife.[^cite_note-38]

According to a 2024 study by the University of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, there is archaeological evidence that the Romans were the first to establish contact with the islands, during the period from the 1st century BC to the 1st century AD. There was no overlap with the occupation by the people who were inhabiting the islands at the time of the Spanish conquest, who had first arrived sometime between the 1st and 3rd centuries AD.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]



A selection of artefacts unearthed from the Lomo de los Gatos site on Gran Canaria

As José Farrujia describes, "The indigenous Canarians lived mainly in natural caves, usually near the coast, 300 to 500 metres (1,000 to 1,600 ft) above sea level. These caves were sometimes isolated but more commonly formed settlements, with burial caves nearby".[^cite_note-\:4-41] Archaeological work has uncovered a rich culture visible through artefacts of [ceramics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceramic), human figures, fishing, hunting and farming tools, plant fibre clothing and vessels, as well as cave paintings. At Lomo de los Gatos on Gran Canaria, a site occupied from 1,600 years ago up until the 1960s, round stone houses, complex burial sites, and associated artefacts have been found.[^cite_note-42] Across the islands are thousands of [Libyco-Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyco-Berber) alphabet inscriptions that have been extensively documented by many linguists.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

The social structure of indigenous Canarians encompassed "a system of [matrilineal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrilineality) descent in most of the islands, in which inheritance was passed on via the female line. Social status and wealth were hereditary and determined the individual's position in the social pyramid, which consisted of the king, the relatives of the king, the lower nobility, villeins, plebeians, and finally executioners, butchers, embalmers, and prisoners". Their religion was [animist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism), centring on the sun and moon, as well as natural features such as mountains.[^cite_note-\:4-41]

### Exploration

The islands may have been visited by the [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia), the [Greeks](42056.md), and the [Carthaginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Carthage). King [Juba II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juba_II), Caesar [Augustus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus)'s [Numidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numidia) protégé, is credited with discovering the islands for the Western world. According to [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder), Juba found the islands uninhabited, but found "a small temple of stone" and "some traces of buildings".[^cite_note-Pliny_the_Elder_(77-79_AD)-45] Juba dispatched a naval contingent to re-open the dye production facility at [Mogador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essaouira) in what is now western Morocco in the early first century AD.[^cite_note-46] That same naval force was subsequently sent on an exploration of the Canary Islands, using Mogador as their mission base.

The names given by Romans to the individual islands were *Ninguaria* or *Nivaria* (Tenerife), *Canaria* (Gran Canaria), *Pluvialia* or *Invale* (Lanzarote), *Ombrion* (La Palma), *Planasia* (Fuerteventura), *Iunonia* or *Junonia* (El Hierro) and *Capraria* (La Gomera).[^cite_note-47]

From the 14th century onward, numerous visits were made by sailors from [Mallorca](59310.md), [Portugal](23033.md), and [Genoa](47332321.md). [Lancelotto Malocello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancelotto_Malocello) settled on Lanzarote in 1312. The Mallorcans established a [mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary) with a bishop in the islands that lasted from 1350 to 1400.

### Castilian conquest

Main articles: [Conquest of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_the_Canary_Islands), [Kingdom of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Canary_Islands), [Crown of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile), and [Kingdom of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Portugal)



Reconstruction of a [Guanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanches) settlement of Tenerife

In 1402, the Castilian colonisation of the islands began with the expedition of the French explorers [Jean de Béthencourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_de_B%C3%A9thencourt) and [Gadifer de la Salle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadifer_de_la_Salle), nobles and [vassals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal) of [Henry III of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_III_of_Castile), to Lanzarote. From there, they went on to conquer Fuerteventura (1405) and El Hierro. These invasions were "brutal cultural and military clashes between the indigenous population and the Castilians" lasting over a century due to formidable resistance by indigenous Canarians.[^cite_note-\:3-36] Some scholars, such as Mohamed Adhikari, describe the conquest of the islands as a [genocide](12441.md) of the [Guanches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanches).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

Béthencourt received the title King of the Canary Islands, but still recognised King Henry III as his overlord. It was not a simple military enterprise, given the aboriginal resistance on some islands. Neither was it politically, since the particular interests of the nobility (determined to strengthen their economic and political power through the acquisition of the islands) conflicted with those of the states, particularly Castile, which were in the midst of territorial expansion and in a process of strengthening of the crown against the nobility.[^cite_note-50]



[Alonso Fernández de Lugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonso_Fern%C3%A1ndez_de_Lugo) presenting the captured native Guanche kings of [Tenerife](269223.md) to the [Catholic Monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Monarchs)

Historians distinguish two periods in the conquest of the Canary Islands:

**Aristocratic conquest** (*conquista señorial*): This refers to the early conquests carried out by the nobility, for their own benefit and without the direct participation of the Crown of Castile, which merely granted rights of conquest in exchange for pacts of vassalage between the noble conqueror and the Crown. One can identify within this period an early phase known as the Betancurian or Norman conquest, carried out by [Jean de Bethencourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_de_Bethencourt) (who was originally from Normandy) and [Gadifer de la Salle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gadifer_de_la_Salle) between 1402 and 1405, which involved the islands of [Lanzarote](304185.md), [El Hierro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Hierro), and [Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura). The subsequent phase is known as the Castilian conquest, carried out by Castilian nobles who acquired, through purchases, assignments and marriages, the previously conquered islands and also incorporated the island of [La Gomera](220016.md) around 1450.

**Royal conquest** (*conquista realenga*): This defines the conquest between 1478 and 1496, carried out directly by the Crown of Castile, during the reign of the [Catholic Monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Monarchs), who armed and partly financed the conquest of those islands which were still unconquered: [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria), [La Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Palma) and [Tenerife](269223.md). This phase of the conquest came to an end in the year 1496, with the dominion of the island of Tenerife, bringing the entire Canarian Archipelago under the control of the Crown of Castile.



Casa de Colón (Las Palmas de Gran Canaria), which Christopher Columbus visited during his first trip

Béthencourt also established a base on the island of La Gomera, but it would be many years before the island was fully conquered. The natives of La Gomera, and of Gran Canaria, Tenerife, and La Palma, resisted the Castilian invaders for almost a century. In 1448, Maciot de Béthencourt sold the lordship of Lanzarote to Portugal's Prince [Henry the Navigator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_the_Navigator), an action that was accepted by neither the natives nor the Castilians. Despite [Pope Nicholas V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_V#cite_ref-8) ruling that the Canary Islands were under Portuguese control, the crisis swelled to a revolt which lasted until 1459 with the final expulsion of the Portuguese. In 1479, Portugal and Castile signed the [Treaty of Alcáçovas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Alc%C3%A1%C3%A7ovas), which settled disputes between Castile and Portugal over the control of the Atlantic. This treaty recognised Castilian control of the Canary Islands but also confirmed Portuguese possession of the [Azores](3226.md), [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira), and the [Cape Verde islands](18962637.md), and gave the Portuguese rights to any further islands or lands in the Atlantic that might be discovered.

The Castilians continued to dominate the islands, but due to the topography and the resistance of the native Guanches, they did not achieve complete control until 1496, when Tenerife and La Palma were finally subdued by [Alonso Fernández de Lugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonso_Fern%C3%A1ndez_de_Lugo). As a result of this "the native pre-Hispanic population declined quickly due to war, epidemics, and slavery".[^cite_note-\:5-51] The Canaries were incorporated into the [Kingdom of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Castile).

### After the conquest and the introduction of slavery



Maps of the Canary Islands drawn by [William Dampier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Dampier) during his voyage to [New Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_West_India_Company) in 1699



Coat of arms of the Castilian and Spanish Realm of Canary Islands

After the conquest, the Castilians imposed a new economic model, based on single-crop cultivation: first [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane); then wine, an important item of trade with [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England). Gran Canaria was conquered by the [Crown of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile) on 6 March 1480, and Tenerife was conquered in 1496, and each had its own governor. There has been speculation that the abundance of *[Roccella tinctoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roccella_tinctoria)* on the Canary Islands offered a profit motive for [Jean de Béthencourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_de_B%C3%A9thencourt) during his conquest of the islands. Lichen has been used for centuries to make dyes. This includes royal purple colors derived from *R. tinctoria*, also known as orseille.[^cite_note-52]

[Slave-driving in the interior of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Africa) in order to sell into enforced labour

The objective of the Spanish Crown to convert the islands into a powerhouse of cultivation required a much larger labour force.[^cite_note-Pérez-53] This was attained through a practice of enslavement, not only of indigenous Canarians, but large numbers of Africans who were taken from North and Sub-Saharan Africa.[^cite_note-54] Whilst the first slave plantations in the Atlantic region were across [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira), [Cape Verde](18962637.md), and the Canary Islands, it was only the Canary Islands which had an indigenous population and were therefore invaded rather than newly occupied.[^cite_note-55]

Because this agriculture industry was largely based on sugarcane, the Castilians converted large swaths of the landscape for sugarcane production and the processing and manufacturing of sugar, largely enabled by slave labour.[^cite_note-56] The cities of [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife) and [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria) became a stopping point for the Spanish traders, as well as [conquistadors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador), and missionaries on their way to the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World). This trade route brought great wealth to the Castilian social sectors of the islands and soon were attracting merchants and adventurers from all over Europe. As wealth increased, enslaved Africans were also forced into more domestic roles for the richer Castilians on the islands.[^cite_note-57]

Research on the skeletons of some of the enslaved from the burial site of Finca Clavijo on Gran Canaria has shown that "all of the adults buried in Finca Clavijo undertook extensive physical activity that involved significant stress on the spine and appendicular skeleton", a result of relentless hard labour, akin to the physical abnormalities found within other enslaved groups from sugarcane plantations around the world.[^cite_note-\:5-51]

As a result of the wealth generated, palaces and churches were built on La Palma during this busy and prosperous period. The Church of El Salvador survives as one of the island's finest examples of the architecture of the 16th century . Civilian architecture survives in forms such as [Casas de los Sánchez-Ochando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casas_de_los_S%C3%A1nchez-Ochando) or [Casa Quintana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_Quintana).

The Canaries' wealth invited attacks by [pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirate) and [privateers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privateer). [Ottoman](22278.md) [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) [admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral) and privateer [Kemal Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemal_Reis) ventured into the Canaries in 1501, while [Murat Reis the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murat_Reis_the_Elder) captured Lanzarote in 1585.

The most severe attack took place in 1599, during the [Dutch Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Revolt). A [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) fleet of 74 ships and 12,000 men, commanded by [Pieter van der Does](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_van_der_Does), attacked the capital Las Palmas de Gran Canaria (the city had 3,500 of Gran Canaria's 8,545 inhabitants). The Dutch attacked the Castillo de la Luz, which guarded the harbor. The Canarians evacuated civilians from the city, and the Castillo surrendered (but not the city). The Dutch moved inland, but Canarian cavalry drove them back to Tamaraceite, near the city.

The Dutch then laid siege to the city, demanding the surrender of all its wealth. They received 12 sheep and 3 calves. Furious, the Dutch sent 4,000 soldiers to attack the Council of the Canaries, who were sheltering in the village of Santa Brígida. Three hundred Canarian soldiers ambushed the Dutch in the village of Monte Lentiscal, killing 150 and forcing the rest to retreat. The Dutch concentrated on Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, attempting to burn it down. The Dutch pillaged [Maspalomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspalomas), on the southern coast of Gran Canaria, [San Sebastián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n_de_La_Gomera) on La Gomera, and [Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_La_Palma) on La Palma, but eventually gave up the siege of Las Palmas and withdrew.



Christian prisoners are [sold as slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_slave_trade) in a square in Algiers. [Barbary pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_pirates) captured almost 2,000 Canarians during four invasions between 1569 and 1618.

[^cite_note-Pérez-53]

In 1618 the [Barbary pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_pirates) from North Africa attacked Lanzarote and La Gomera taking 1000 captives to be sold as [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_slave_trade).[^cite_note-58] Another noteworthy attack occurred in 1797, when [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife_\(1797\)) was attacked by a British fleet under [Horatio Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horatio_Nelson,_1st_Viscount_Nelson) on 25 July. The British were repulsed, losing almost 400 men. It was during this battle that Nelson lost his right arm.

Santa Cruz de Tenerife was, especially during the first half of the 18th century, the most important [corsair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privateer) center in the [Macaronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaronesian) region.[^cite_note-B-59][^cite_note-60] The recent discovery of abundant documentary evidence of significant corsair activity, carried out primarily by natives, residents, and those living on the island, confirms this fact. At the same time, the numerous pirate ships that frequented the Canary Islands waters used landing places along its coast. Such was the case of Valle de Salazar or [San Andrés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Andr%C3%A9s,_Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife), which had acquired a reputation as a "[pirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirate) port" until the construction of its castle or defensive tower.[^cite_note-61]

Aside from the pirates and privateers who attacked the islands, the archipelago also gave rise to native privateers who defended their own coasts or those of the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire), especially in the [Caribbean](18956035.md). Among these, we can highlight the figure of [Amaro Pargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaro_Pargo), a privateer and merchant from Tenerife, who participated in the [Spanish treasure fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_treasure_fleet) (the Spanish-Hispanic American trade route) in the 18th century, attacking enemy ships of Spain both in Canary waters and along the coasts of the New World.[^cite_note-ColFar-62][^cite_note-AmaroHeroe-63]

Apart from the passage of [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus), the Canary Islands were the site of some of the most important fleets in Western history. Such as the fleet of the [Virginia Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Company) in 1606, which marked the foundation of [Fort Jamestown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown,_Virginia) -the first permanent English settlement in what is now the [United States](3434750.md)-[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] and the passage of the [First Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Fleet) through Tenerife, which marked the first European settlement in [Australia](4689264.md) in [Botany Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany_Bay).[^cite_note-66]

### 18th to 19th century: trade with Hispanic America and emigration



[Amaro Pargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaro_Pargo) (1678–1741), [corsair](50715.md) and [merchant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant) from Tenerife who participated in the [Spanish treasure fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_treasure_fleet), the Spanish-American trade route

The sugar-based economy of the islands faced stiff competition from Spain's [Caribbean](18956035.md) colonies. Low sugar prices in the 19th century caused severe recessions on the islands. A new cash crop, [cochineal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochineal) (*cochinilla*), came into cultivation during this time, reinvigorating the islands' economy. During this time the Canarian-American trade was developed, in which Canarian products such as cochineal, sugarcane and rum were sold in American ports such as [Veracruz](63125.md), [Campeche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeche), [La Guaira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Guaira) and [Havana](49719.md), among others.[^cite_note-67]

By the end of the 18th century, Canary Islanders had already emigrated to Spanish American territories, such as [Havana](49719.md), [Veracruz](63125.md), and [Santo Domingo](57638.md),[^cite_note-68] [San Antonio, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio,_Texas)[^cite_note-69] and [St. Bernard Parish, Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Bernard_Parish,_Louisiana).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] These economic difficulties spurred mass emigration during the 19th and first half of the 20th century, primarily to the Americas. Between 1840 and 1890 as many as 40,000 Canary Islanders emigrated to [Venezuela](32374.md). Also, thousands of Canarians moved to [Puerto Rico](23041.md) where the Spanish monarchy felt that Canarians would adapt to island life better than other immigrants from the mainland of Spain. Deeply entrenched traditions, such as the Mascaras Festival in the town of [Hatillo, Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatillo,_Puerto_Rico), are an example of Canarian culture still preserved in Puerto Rico. Similarly, many thousands of Canarians emigrated to the shores of [Cuba](5042481.md).[^cite_note-72] During the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War) of 1898, the Spanish fortified the islands against a possible American attack, but no such event took place.

### Romantic period and scientific expeditions



Square in the village of [Los Silos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Silos), Tenerife, by [Alfred Diston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Diston), 1827



Coast El Golfo, El Hierro

Sirera and Renn (2004)[^cite_note-73] distinguish two different types of expeditions, or voyages, during the period 1770–1830, which they term "the Romantic period":

First are "expeditions financed by the States, closely related with the official scientific Institutions. characterised by having strict scientific objectives (and inspired by) the spirit of Illustration and progress". In this type of expedition, Sirera and Renn include the following travellers:

- J. Edens, whose 1715 ascent and observations of Mt. Teide influenced many subsequent expeditions.

- [Louis Feuillée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Feuill%C3%A9e) (1724), who was sent to measure the meridian of El Hierro and to map the islands.

- [Jean-Charles de Borda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Charles_de_Borda) (1771, 1776) who more accurately measured the longitudes of the islands and the height of Mount Teide

- the [Baudin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Baudin)-Ledru expedition (1796) which aimed to recover a valuable collection of natural history objects.

The second type of expedition identified by Sirera and Renn is one that took place starting from more or less private initiatives. Among these, the key exponents were the following:

- [Alexander von Humboldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_von_Humboldt) (1799)

- [Buch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Leopold_von_Buch) and [Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christen_Smith_\(botanist\)) (1815)

- [Broussonet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broussonet)

- [Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Barker_Webb)

- [Sabin Berthelot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabin_Berthelot).

Sirera and Renn identify the period 1770–1830 as one in which "In a panorama dominated until that moment by France and England enters with strength and brio Germany of the Romantic period whose presence in the islands will increase".

### Early 20th century



The [port of Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Las_Palmas) in 1912

At the beginning of the 20th century, the British introduced a new [cash-crop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash-crop), the [banana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana), the export of which was controlled by companies such as [Fyffes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyffes).

30 November 1833 the [Province of Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Canary_Islands) had been created with the capital being declared as Santa Cruz de Tenerife.[^cite_note-74] The rivalry between the cities of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria and Santa Cruz de Tenerife for the capital of the islands led to the division of the [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) into two provinces on 23 September 1927.[^cite_note-75]

During the time of the [Second Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic), [Marxist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist) and [anarchist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anarchist) workers' movements began to develop, led by figures such as Jose Miguel Perez and [Guillermo Ascanio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Ascanio). However, outside of a few municipalities, these organisations were a minority and fell easily to Nationalist forces during the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War).

### Franco regime

Main article: [Francoist Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain)

In 1936, [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco) was appointed General Commandant of the Canaries. He joined the military revolt of 17 July which began the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War). Franco quickly took control of the archipelago, except for a few points of resistance on La Palma and in the town of [Vallehermoso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallehermoso,_Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife), on La Gomera. Though there was never a war in the islands, the post-war suppression of political dissent on the Canaries was most severe.[^cite_note-76]

During the Second World War, [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill) prepared plans for the British seizure of the Canary Islands as a [naval base](291782.md), in the event of [Gibraltar](7607314.md) being invaded from the Spanish mainland.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] The planned operation was known as [Operation Pilgrim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pilgrim).[^cite_note-79]

Opposition to Franco's regime did not begin to organise until the late 1950s, which experienced an upheaval of parties such as the [Communist Party of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Spain_\(main\)) and the formation of various nationalist, leftist parties.

During the [Ifni War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifni_War), the Franco regime set up [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_camps) on the islands to [extrajudicially imprison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_punishment) those in Western Sahara suspected of disloyalty to Spain, many of whom were colonial troops recruited on the spot but were later deemed to be potential [fifth columnists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_column) and deported to the Canary Islands. These camps were characterised by the use of forced labour for infrastructure projects and highly unsanitary conditions resulting in the widespread occurrence of [tuberculosis](30653.md).[^cite_note-80]

After the death of Franco, there was a pro-independence armed movement based in [Algeria](358.md), the [Movement for the Independence and Self-determination of the Canaries Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands_Independence_Movement) (MAIAC). In 1968, the [Organisation of African Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African_Unity) recognised the MAIAC as a legitimate [African independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_independence_movements), and declared the Canary Islands as an African territory still under foreign rule.[^cite_note-81]

### Modern history

The archipelago experienced a period of [Canarian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_nationalism) during the 1970s, aiming to achieve autonomy or independence.

Since August 16, 1982, the Canary Islands have been established as one of Spain's seventeen [autonomous communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_communities), with the capital shared between the cities of Santa Cruz de Tenerife and Las Palmas de Gran Canaria. The seat of the President of the Government alternates between the two cities with each four-year legislative term. The [Parliament of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_the_Canary_Islands) has its permanent seat in Santa Cruz de Tenerife.

Since 1992, the Canary Islands have been an [outermost region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outermost_region) of the [European Union](9317.md).

The Canary Islands have become one of the European Union's busiest migration frontiers in recent years. The archipelago is often the first European territory reached by migrants and refugees departing from countries such as [Senegal](27278.md), [Mauritania](18778516.md), [Mali](19127.md) and [Gambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambia). The Atlantic route is considered one of the world's most dangerous migration corridors. For this reason, Pope [Leo XIV](54520372.md) visited Canary Islands on June, 2026, marking the final leg of his [historic apostolic journey to Spain](83128241.md) to spotlight of irregular migrants crossing the Atlantic ocean.[^cite_note-82]

## Islands

From west to east, the Canary Islands are [El Hierro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Hierro), [La Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Palma), [La Gomera](220016.md), [Tenerife](269223.md), [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria), [Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura), [Lanzarote](304185.md), and [La Graciosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graciosa,_Canary_Islands). North of Lanzarote are the islets of [Montaña Clara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monta%C3%B1a_Clara), [Alegranza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alegranza), [Roque del Este](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_del_Este) and [Roque del Oeste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_del_Oeste), belonging to the [Chinijo Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinijo_Archipelago). Northeast of Fuerteventura is the islet of [Lobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobos_Island). There are a series of small adjacent rocks in the Canary Islands: the [Roques de Anaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roques_de_Anaga), [Garachico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_de_Garachico) and Fasnia in Tenerife, and Salmor and Bonanza in El Hierro.

### El Hierro

Main article: [El Hierro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Hierro)

El Hierro, the westernmost island, covers 268.71 km<sup>2</sup> (103.75 sq mi). It is the second smallest of the major islands, and the least populous with 10,798 inhabitants. The whole island was declared a Reserve of the Biosphere in 2000. Its capital is [Valverde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valverde,_Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife). Also known as Ferro, it was once the westernmost known land in the world. Ancient European geographers such as Ptolemy recognised the island as the [prime meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_meridian) of longitude with the [Ferro meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferro_meridian). That remained so until the 19th century, when it was displaced by the one [passing through Greenwich](12701.md).[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

### Fuerteventura

Main article: [Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura)



View from Pico de la Palma, Fuerteventura

Fuerteventura, with a surface of 1,660 km<sup>2</sup> (640 sq mi), is the second largest island of the archipelago. It has been declared a [biosphere reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Network_of_Biosphere_Reserves) by [UNESCO](21786641.md). It is the oldest of the islands being more eroded. Its highest point is the Pico de la Zarza, at a height of 807 metres (2,648 feet). Its capital is [Puerto del Rosario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_del_Rosario).

### Gran Canaria

Main article: [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria)



View of [Fataga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fataga), Gran Canaria

Gran Canaria has 846,717 inhabitants. The capital, [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](163701.md), with 377,203 inhabitants, is the most populous city and shares the status of capital of the Canaries with Santa Cruz de Tenerife. Gran Canaria's surface area is 1,560 km<sup>2</sup> (600 sq mi). Roque Nublo 1,813 metres (5,948 feet) and Pico de las Nieves ("Peak of Snow") 1,949 metres (6,394 feet) are located in the center of the island. On the south of the island are the Maspalomas Dunes (Gran Canaria).

### La Gomera

Main article: [La Gomera](220016.md)

La Gomera (informally known as 'Isla Colombina') has an area of 369.76 km<sup>2</sup> (142.77 sq mi) and is the second least populous island with 21,136 inhabitants. It has been declared a [biosphere reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Network_of_Biosphere_Reserves) by [UNESCO](21786641.md). Geologically it is one of the oldest of the archipelago. The insular capital is [San Sebastian de La Gomera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebastian_de_La_Gomera). [Garajonay National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garajonay_National_Park) is located on the island.

### Lanzarote

Main article: [Lanzarote](304185.md)

Lanzarote is the easternmost island and one of the oldest of the archipelago, and it has shown evidence of recent volcanic activity. It has a surface of 845.94 km<sup>2</sup> (326.62 sq mi), and a population of 149,183 inhabitants, including the adjacent islets of the Chinijo Archipelago. The capital is [Arrecife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrecife), with 56,834 inhabitants.

#### Chinijo Archipelago

Main article: [Chinijo Archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinijo_Archipelago)



The Chinijo Archipelago, seen from Lanzarote

The Chinijo Archipelago includes the islands [La Graciosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Graciosa), [Alegranza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alegranza), [Montaña Clara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monta%C3%B1a_Clara), [Roque del Este](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_del_Este) and [Roque del Oeste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_del_Oeste). It has a surface of 40.8 km<sup>2</sup> (15.8 sq mi), and only La Graciosa is populated, with 658 inhabitants. With 29 km<sup>2</sup> (11 sq mi), La Graciosa is the largest island of the Chinijo Archipelago and the smallest inhabited island of the Canaries.

##### La Graciosa

Main article: [La Graciosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Graciosa)

Graciosa Island or commonly La Graciosa is a volcanic island in the Canary Islands of Spain, located 2 km (1.2 mi) north of the island of Lanzarote across the Strait of El Río. It was formed by the [Canary hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_hotspot). The island is part of the Chinijo Archipelago and the Chinijo Archipelago Natural Park (Parque Natural del Archipiélago Chinijo). It is administered by the municipality of Teguise. In 2018, La Graciosa was declared as *the eighth Canary Island* by the [Spanish Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Senate),[^cite_note-Monzón2019-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] though it is not recognised as such by the Canarian administration. It is administratively dependent on the island of Lanzarote. It is the smallest and least populated of the main islands, with about 700 people.

### La Palma

Main article: [La Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Palma)

La Palma, with 81,863 inhabitants covering an area of 708.32 km<sup>2</sup> (273.48 sq mi), is in its entirety a biosphere reserve. For long it showed no signs of volcanic activity, even though the volcano [Teneguía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenegu%C3%ADa) entered into eruption last in 1971. On 19 September 2021, the volcanic [Cumbre Vieja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbre_Vieja) on the island [erupted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Cumbre_Vieja_volcanic_eruption).[^cite_note-88] It is the second-highest island of the Canaries, with the Roque de los Muchachos at 2,423 metres (7,949 feet) as its highest point. [Santa Cruz de La Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_La_Palma), known to those on the island as simply "Santa Cruz", is its capital.

Built by volcanic eruptions over roughly two million years, the volcanic structure continues several thousand metres below the ocean surface down to the ocean floor.[^cite_note-89]

### Tenerife

Main article: [Tenerife](269223.md)



[San Cristóbal de La Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna) in 1880 (Tenerife)

Tenerife is, with its area of 2,034 km<sup>2</sup> (785 sq mi), the most extensive island of the Canary Islands. With 904,713 inhabitants, it is the most populated island of the archipelago and Spain. Two of the islands' principal cities are located on it: the capital, [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife) and [San Cristóbal de La Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna), a World Heritage Site. San Cristóbal de La Laguna, the second city of the island, is home to the oldest university in the Canary Islands, the [University of La Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_La_Laguna). [Teide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teide), with its 3,715 metres (12,188 feet) is the highest peak of [Spain](26667.md) and a [World Heritage Site](44940.md). [Tenerife](269223.md) is the site of the [worst air disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerife_airport_disaster) in the history of aviation, in which 583 people were killed in the collision of two [Boeing 747s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) on 27 March 1977.

## Geography



A map of the Canary Islands



[Hacha Grande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacha_Grande), a mountain in the south of Lanzarote, viewed from the road to the Playa de Papagayo



A panoramic view of Gran Canaria, with [Roque Nublo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_Nublo) at the left and [Roque Bentayga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_Bentayga) at the center

[Tenerife](269223.md) is the largest and most populous island of the archipelago. [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria), with 865,070 inhabitants, is both the Canary Islands' second most populous island, and the third most populous one in Spain after Tenerife (966,354 inhabitants) and [Mallorca](59310.md) (896,038 inhabitants).[^cite_note-91] The island of [Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura) is the second largest in the archipelago and located 100 km (62 mi) from the African coast.

The islands form the [Macaronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaronesia) [ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion) with the [Azores](3226.md), [Cape Verde](18962637.md), [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira_Islands), and the [Savage Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savage_Isles).[^cite_note-Carracedo_Troll_North-East_Atlantic_Islands-92] The Canary Islands constitute the largest and most populated archipelago of the Macaronesia region.[^cite_note-catalogo.museosdetenerife.org-12] The archipelago has seven large and several smaller islands, all of which are volcanic in origin.[^cite_note-93]

According to the position of the islands with respect to the north-east [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_winds), the climate can be mild and wet or very dry. Several native species form [laurisilva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurisilva) forests.

The individual islands in the Canary archipelago tend to have distinct [microclimates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microclimate). Those islands such as [El Hierro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Hierro), [La Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Palma) and [La Gomera](220016.md) lying to the west of the archipelago have a climate which is influenced by the moist [Canary Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Current). They are well vegetated even at low levels and have extensive tracts of sub-tropical laurisilva forest. Travelling east toward the African coast, the influence of the current diminishes, and the islands become increasingly arid. [Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura) and [Lanzarote](304185.md), the islands which are closest to the African mainland, are effectively desert or semi-desert.

[Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria) is known as a "continent in miniature" for its diverse landscapes like [Maspalomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspalomas) and [Roque Nublo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_Nublo). The north of [Tenerife](269223.md) lies under the influence of the moist Atlantic winds and is well vegetated. The south of the island around the tourist resorts of [Playa de las Américas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playa_de_las_Am%C3%A9ricas) and [Los Cristianos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Cristianos) is arid. The island rises to almost 4,000 m (13,000 ft) above sea level. At altitude, in the cool relatively wet climate, forests of the endemic pine *[Pinus canariensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_canariensis)* thrive. Many of the plant species in the Canary Islands, like the Canary Island pine and the [dragon tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dracaena_\(plant\)), *[Dracaena draco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dracaena_draco)* are endemic, as noted by [Sabin Berthelot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabin_Berthelot) and [Philip Barker Webb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Barker_Webb) in their work, *L'Histoire Naturelle des Îles Canaries* (1835–50).[^cite_note-94]

### Climate

The Canary Islands are considered [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate) and generally [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aridity), moderated by the sea and in summer by the [trade winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_winds). It is the northernmost location with a tropical climate, having all its monthly average temperatures above 18° C/64.4° F [^cite_note-95] There are a number of [microclimates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microclimate) and the classifications range mainly from [semi-desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) to [desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate). The majority of the Canary Islands have a [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate) (*BWh*) and a [hot semi-desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) (*BSh*) within the [Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) system, caused partly due to the cool [Canary Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Current).[^cite_note-96] A subtropical humid climate, which is very influenced by the ocean, is in the middle of the islands of [La Gomera](220016.md), [Tenerife](269223.md) and [La Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Palma), where [laurisilva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurisilva) cloud forests grow.[^cite_note-97]

### Geology



A humid [laurel forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_forest) in [La Gomera](220016.md)

Main article: [Geology of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_the_Canary_Islands)

The seven major islands, one minor island, and several small islets were originally volcanic islands, formed by the [Canary hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_hotspot).[^cite_note-103] The Canary Islands is the only place in Spain where [volcanic eruptions](8921015.md) have been recorded during the [Modern Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Era), with some volcanoes still active (El Hierro, 2011).[^cite_note-104] Volcanic islands such as those in the Canary chain often have steep ocean cliffs caused by catastrophic debris avalanches and [landslides](37208.md).[^cite_note-105] The island chain's most recent eruption occurred at [Cumbre Vieja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumbre_Vieja), a volcanic ridge on La Palma, [in 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Cumbre_Vieja_volcanic_eruption).[^cite_note-106]

The [Teide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teide) [volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano) on Tenerife is the highest mountain in Spain, and the third tallest volcano on Earth on a volcanic ocean island.[^cite_note-107] All the islands except La Gomera have been active in the last million years. Four of them, Lanzarote, Tenerife, La Palma and El Hierro, have historical records of eruptions since European discovery.[^cite_note-Carracedo_Troll_North-East_Atlantic_Islands-92] The islands rise from [Jurassic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurassic) [oceanic crust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_crust) associated with the opening of the Atlantic. Underwater [magmatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magmatism) began during the [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous), and has continued to the present day. The islands were once considered as a distinct physiographic section of the [Atlas Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Mountains) province, which is part of the larger African Alpine System division, but are now recognised as being related to a magmatic [hot spot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotspot_\(geology\)).[^cite_note-Carracedo_Troll_North-East_Atlantic_Islands-92]

In the summer of 2011, a series of low-magnitude earthquakes occurred beneath El Hierro. These had a linear trend of northeast–southwest. In October a submarine eruption occurred about 2 km (1+1⁄4 mi) south of Restinga. This eruption produced gases and pumice, but no explosive activity was reported.[^cite_note-108]

[Teide National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teide_National_Park) is the oldest and largest national park in the Canary Islands and one of the oldest in Spain. Standing at an altitude of 3,715 metres (12,188 ft),[^cite_note-IGN_PhysicalMap_2012-115] it is the highest elevation in Spain and the third largest volcano on Earth from its base. In 2007, the Teide National Park was declared one of the [12 Treasures of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_Treasures_of_Spain).

### Governance

The regional executive body, the [Parliament of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_the_Canary_Islands), is presided over by [Fernando Clavijo Batlle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Clavijo_Batlle) ([Canarian Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_Coalition)), the current [President of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Canary_Islands). The members of the regional legislature, the Parliament of the Canary Islands, has 70 elected legislators. The [last regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Canarian_regional_election) took place in May 2023.

The islands have 14 seats in the [Spanish Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Spain). Of these, 11 seats are directly elected, 3 for Gran Canaria, 3 for Tenerife, and 1 each for Lanzarote (including La Graciosa), Fuerteventura, La Palma, La Gomera and El Hierro. The other 3 are appointed by the regional legislature.[^cite_note-compsenado-116]

### Political geography

The **Autonomous Community of the Canary Islands** has two [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Spain) (*provincias*), [Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Las_Palmas) and [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife), whose capitals, [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria) and [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife), are capitals of the autonomous community. Each of the seven major islands are ruled by an island council named a *Cabildo Insular*. Each island is subdivided into smaller [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Spain) (*municipios*). [Las Palmas is divided into 34 municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Las_Palmas), and [Santa Cruz de Tenerife is divided into 54 municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife).[^cite_note-117]

The international boundary of the Canaries is disputed in [Morocco–Spain relations](32865910.md). In 2022 the UN declared the Canary Island's territorial waters as being Moroccan coast and Morocco has authorised [gas and oil exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon_exploration) in what the Canary Islands states to be Canarian territorial waters and [Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Sahara) waters.[^cite_note-118] Morocco's official position is that international laws regarding territorial limits do not authorise Spain to claim seabed boundaries based on the territory of the Canaries, since the Canary Islands enjoy a large degree of autonomy. In fact, the islands do not enjoy any special degree of autonomy, as each one of the Spanish regions is considered an [autonomous community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_community), with equal status to the European ones. Under the [Law of the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_the_Sea), the only islands not granted territorial waters or an [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) (EEZ) are those that are not fit for human habitation, or do not have an economic life of their own, which is not the case of the Canary Islands.[^cite_note-119]

### Canarian nationalism

Main article: [Canarian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_nationalism)

There are some pro-independence political parties, like the [National Congress of the Canaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_of_the_Canaries) (CNC) and the [Popular Front of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_of_the_Canary_Islands). Their popular support is almost insignificant, with no presence in either the autonomous parliament or the *cabildos insulares*.[^cite_note-120] In a 2012 study by the [Centro de Investigaciones Sociológicas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centro_de_Investigaciones_Sociol%C3%B3gicas), when asked about national identity, the majority of respondents from the Canary Islands (53.8%) considered themselves Spanish and Canarian in equal measures, followed by 24% who consider themselves more Canarian than Spanish. 6.1% of the respondents considered themselves only Canarian, and 7% considered themselves only Spanish.[^cite_note-cis2956-121]

### Defence

Main article: [Military of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_Canary_Islands)

The defence of the territory is the responsibility of the [Spanish Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Armed_Forces). Components of the [Army](2142325.md), [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Navy), [Air Force](3853398.md) and the [Civil Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_Guard) are based in the territory.

After the establishment of a democratic [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_monarchy) in Spain, [autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance) was granted to the Canaries via a law passed in 1982, with a newly established autonomous devolved government and parliament. In 1983, the first autonomous elections were held. The [Spanish Socialist Workers' Party](272596.md) (PSOE) won.[^cite_note-122]

### Capitals

At present, the Canary Islands is the only autonomous community in Spain that has two capitals: [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife) and [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria), since the [Statute of Autonomy of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Autonomy_of_the_Canary_Islands?action=edit\&redlink=1) was created in 1982.[^cite_note-es.wikisource.org-21][^cite_note-gobiernodecanarias.org-22]

The political capital of the archipelago did not exist as such until the nineteenth century. The first cities founded by the [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europeans) at the time of the conquest of the Canary Islands in the 15th century were: [Telde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telde) (in [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria)), [San Marcial del Rubicón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marcial_del_Rubic%C3%B3n?action=edit\&redlink=1) (in [Lanzarote](304185.md)) and [Betancuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betancuria) (in [Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura)). These cities boasted the first European institutions present in the archipelago, including [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) [bishoprics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishoprics).[^cite_note-\:1-123] Although, because the period of splendor of these cities developed before the total conquest of the archipelago and its incorporation into the [Crown of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile) never had a political and real control of the entire Canary archipelago.



Overview of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria



View of Santa Cruz de Tenerife

The function of a Canarian city with full jurisdiction for the entire archipelago only exists after the conquest of the Canary Islands, although originally *[de facto](43594.md)*, that is, without legal and real meaning and linked to the headquarters of the [Canary Islands General Captaincy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands_General_Captaincy?action=edit\&redlink=1).

Las Palmas de Gran Canaria was the first city that exercised this function. This is because the residence of the Captain General of the Canary Islands was in this city during part of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries.[^cite_note-junta-124] In May 1661, the Captain General of the Canary Islands, [Jerónimo de Benavente y Quiñones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jer%C3%B3nimo_de_Benavente_y_Qui%C3%B1ones?action=edit\&redlink=1), moved the headquarters of the captaincy to the city of [San Cristóbal de La Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna) on the island of [Tenerife](269223.md).[^cite_note-portalcultura.mde_1-125] This was due to the fact that this island since the conquest was the most populated, productive and with the highest economic expectations.[^cite_note-mdc.ulpgc_1-126] La Laguna would be considered the *de facto* capital of the archipelago[^cite_note-ref_duplicada_2-127] until the official status of the capital of Canary Islands in the city of Santa Cruz de Tenerife was confirmed in the 19th century, due in part to the constant controversies and rivalries between the bourgeoisies of San Cristóbal de La Laguna and Las Palmas de Gran Canaria for the economic, political and institutional hegemony of the archipelago.[^cite_note-128]

Already in 1723, the Captain General of the Canary Islands [Lorenzo Fernandez de Villavicencio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Fernandez_de_Villavicencio?action=edit\&redlink=1) had moved the headquarters of the General Captaincy of the Canary Islands from San Cristóbal de La Laguna to Santa Cruz de Tenerife. This decision continued without pleasing the society of the island of Gran Canaria.[^cite_note-129] It would be after the creation of the [Province of Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Canary_Islands) in November 1833 in which Santa Cruz would become the first fully official capital of the Canary Islands (*[De jure](43594.md)* and not of *de facto* as happened previously).[^cite_note-es.wikisource.org-21][^cite_note-Real_Decreto_Canarias-24] Santa Cruz de Tenerife would be the capital of the Canary archipelago until during the Government of General [Primo de Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Primo_de_Rivera) in 1927 the province of Canary Islands was split in two provinces: [Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Las_Palmas) with capital in Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, and [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife) with capital in the homonymous city.

Finally, with the Statute of Autonomy of the Canary Islands in 1982 and the creation of the Autonomous Community of the Canary Islands, the capital of the archipelago between Las Palmas de Gran Canaria and Santa Cruz de Tenerife is fixed, which is how it remains today.

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Canary_Islands)



[Basilica of the Virgin of Candelaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Candelaria) (Patroness of the Canary Islands) in [Candelaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candelaria,_Tenerife), Tenerife

The [Catholic Church](606848.md) has been the majority religion in the archipelago for more than five centuries, ever since the [Conquest of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_the_Canary_Islands). There are also several other religious communities.

#### Roman Catholic Church



Procession of [Holy Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week) in [San Cristóbal de La Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna)

The overwhelming majority of native Canarians are [Roman Catholic](606848.md) (76.7%)[^cite_note-CIS2019Canarias-138] with various smaller foreign-born populations of other Christian beliefs such as [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestants).

The appearance of the [Virgin of Candelaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_of_Candelaria) (Patron of Canary Islands) was credited with moving the Canary Islands toward Christianity. Two Catholic saints were born in the Canary Islands: [Peter of Saint Joseph de Betancur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_of_Saint_Joseph_de_Betancur)[^cite_note-139] and [José de Anchieta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta).[^cite_note-140] Both born on the island of Tenerife, they were respectively missionaries in [Guatemala](17238567.md) and [Brazil](3383.md).

The Canary Islands are divided into two Catholic dioceses, each governed by a bishop:

- [Diócesis Canariense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Canarias): Includes the islands of the [Eastern Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Las_Palmas): Gran Canaria, Fuerteventura and Lanzarote. Its capital was San Marcial El Rubicón (1404) and [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](163701.md) (1483–present). There was a previous bishopric which was based in [Telde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telde), but it was later abolished.

- [Diócesis Nivariense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna): Includes the islands of the [western province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife_\(province\)): Tenerife, La Palma, La Gomera and El Hierro. Its capital is [San Cristóbal de La Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_de_La_Laguna) (1819–present).

#### Other religions

Separate from the overwhelming Catholic majority are a minority of [Muslims](19541.md).[^cite_note-laopinion.es-141] Among the followers of [Islam](6037917.md), the [Islamic Federation of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Federation_of_the_Canary_Islands) exists to represent the Islamic community in the Canary Islands as well as to provide practical support to members of the Islamic community.[^cite_note-142] For its part, there is also the [Evangelical Council of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Council_of_the_Canary_Islands?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the archipelago.

Other religious faiths represented include [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses), [The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) as well as [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism).[^cite_note-laopinion.es-141] Minority religions are also present such as the [Church of the Guanche People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Guanche_People) which is classified as a [neo-pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Paganism) native religion.[^cite_note-laopinion.es-141] Also present are [Buddhism](3267529.md),[^cite_note-laopinion.es-141] [Judaism](15624.md),[^cite_note-laopinion.es-141] [Baháʼí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith),[^cite_note-laopinion.es-141] [African religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_religion),[^cite_note-laopinion.es-141] and [Chinese religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_religions).[^cite_note-laopinion.es-141]

### Population genetics

Main article: [Canarian people § Population genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_people#Population_genetics)



Canarian women singing in [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria), 1972

The native inhabitants of the Canary Islands hold a gene pool that is predominantly European and native Guanche. It was found that Guanche males contributed less to the gene pool of modern Canary Islanders than Guanche females. Haplogroups typical among the Guanche have been found at high frequencies in [Latin America](18524.md), suggesting that descendants of the Guanche played an active role in the [Spanish colonisation of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas).[^cite_note-144]

In 2017, the first genome-wide data from the Guanches confirmed a North African origin and that they were genetically most similar to [ancient North African Berber peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_history_of_North_Africa) of the nearby North African mainland. It also showed that modern inhabitants of Gran Canaria carry an estimated 16%–31% Guanche autosomal ancestry.[^cite_note-cell_31257-5-145] A 2018 genetic study found that the Canarian population is, on average at an autosomal level, 75–83% European, 17–23% North African and 3% Sub-saharan.[^cite_note-146]

The economy is based primarily [on tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_the_Canary_Islands), which makes up 32% of the GDP. The Canaries receive about 12 million tourists per year. Construction makes up nearly 20% of the GDP and tropical agriculture, primarily bananas and tobacco, are grown for export to Europe and the Americas. Ecologists are concerned that the resources, especially in the more [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid) islands, are being [overexploited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overexploited) but there are still many agricultural resources like [tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato), [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato), [onions](51258.md), [cochineal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochineal), [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape), [vines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vine), [dates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_dactylifera), [oranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(fruit\)), [lemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemon), [figs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ficus), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [apricots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apricot), [peaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peach) and [almonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond).

Water resources are also being overexploited, due to the high water usage by tourists.[^cite_note-149] Also, some islands (such as Gran Canaria and Tenerife) [overexploit the ground water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundwater_extraction). This is done in such degree that, according to European and Spanish legal regulations, the current situation is not acceptable. To address the problems, good governance and a change in the water use paradigm have been proposed. These solutions depend largely on [controlling water use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_conservation) and on demand management. As this is administratively difficult and politically unpalatable, most action is currently directed at increasing the public offer of water through import from outside; a decision which is economically, politically and environmentally questionable.[^cite_note-150]

To bring in revenue for environmental protection, innovation, training and water sanitation a [tourist tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourist_tax) was considered in 2018, along with a doubling of the [ecotax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotax) and restrictions on holiday rents in the zones with the greatest pressure of demand.[^cite_note-151]

The economy is [€](9472.md) 25 billion (2001 GDP figures). The islands experienced continuous growth during a 20-year period, up until 2001, at a rate of approximately 5% annually. This growth was fueled mainly by huge amounts of [foreign direct investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_direct_investment), mostly to develop tourism real estate (hotels and apartments), and European Funds (near €11 billion in the period from 2000 to 2007), since the Canary Islands are labelled Region Objective 1 (eligible for euro structural funds).[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] Additionally, the EU allows the Canary Islands Government to offer special tax concessions for investors who incorporate under the Zona Especial Canaria (ZEC) regime and create more than five jobs.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]

Spain gave permission in August 2014 for [Repsol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repsol) and its partners to explore [oil](23195.md) and [natural gas](22131.md) prospects off the Canary Islands, involving an investment of €7.5 billion over four years, to commence at the end of 2016. Repsol at the time said the area could ultimately produce 100,000 barrels of oil a day, which would meet 10 percent of Spain's energy needs.[^cite_note-RepsolCanary-156] However, the analysis of samples obtained did not show the necessary volume nor quality to consider future extraction, and the project was scrapped.[^cite_note-157]

Despite currently having very high dependence on fossil fuels, research on the renewable energy potential concluded that a high potential for renewable energy technologies exists on the archipelago. This, in such extent even that a scenario pathway to 100% renewable energy supply by 2050 has been put forward.[^cite_note-158]

The Canary Islands have great natural attractions, climate and [beaches](40630.md) make the islands a major tourist destination, being visited each year by about 12 million people (11,986,059 in 2007, comprising 29% [Britons](31717.md), 22% Spanish from outside the Canaries, and 21% [Germans](11867.md)). Among the islands, Tenerife receives the largest number of tourists annually, followed by Gran Canaria and Lanzarote.[^cite_note-gobiernodecanarias1-17] The archipelago's principal tourist attraction is the [Teide National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teide_National_Park) in Tenerife where the highest mountain in Spain and third largest [volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano) in the world (Mount [Teide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teide)) receives over 2.8 million visitors annually.[^cite_note-webtenerife2-159]

The combination of high mountains, proximity to Europe, and clean air has made the [Roque de los Muchachos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_de_los_Muchachos) peak (on La Palma island) a leading location for [telescopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telescope) like the [Grantecan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grantecan).

The islands, as an autonomous region of Spain, are in the [European Union](9317.md) and the [Schengen Area](16565139.md). They are in the [European Union Customs Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Customs_Union) but outside the [VAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VAT) area.[^cite_note-territories-160] Instead of VAT there is a local Sales Tax (*IGIC*) which has a general rate of 7%, an increased tax rate of 13.5%, a reduced tax rate of 3% and a zero tax rate for certain basic need products and services. Consequently, some products are subject to additional VAT if being exported from the islands into mainland Spain or the rest of the EU. This VAT plus transportation costs make it very difficult for small Canarian shops to sell to the rest of Spain. The transportation costs are even higher outside of Tenerife and Gran Canaria.[^cite_note-Jiménez-161]

Canarian time is [Western European Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European_Time) (WET), or [GMT](12701.md). In summer, one hour ahead of GMT. Canarian time is one hour behind mainland Spain, and the same time as the UK, Ireland and mainland Portugal all year round.

## Transport



A [Binter Canarias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binter_Canarias) [Embraer 195 E2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_195-E2) at the Galician airport of [Vigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigo%E2%80%93Peinador_Airport). Binter is the biggest airline of the Canary Islands and labels itself as the flag carrier of the Autonomous Community (*Líneas Aéreas de Canarias*).&#x20;



Bus Station—*Estación de Guaguas* also known as *El Hoyo* (The hole), on the left, out of the image—at San Telmo Park, [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria)



[Tenerife Tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerife_Tram), is the only existing tramway or train in the Canary Islands

The Canary Islands have eight airports altogether, two of the main ports of Spain, and an extensive network of [autopistas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highways_in_Spain) (highways) and other roads. For a road map see multimap.[^cite_note-164] [Traffic congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traffic_congestion) is sometimes a problem in Tenerife and on Gran Canaria.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167]

Large [ferry](50771.md) boats and fast ferries link most of the islands. Both types can transport large numbers of passengers, cargo, and vehicles. Fast ferries are made of aluminium and powered by modern and efficient diesel engines, while conventional ferries have a steel hull and are powered by heavy oil. Fast ferries travel in excess of 30 kn (56 km/h; 35 mph); conventional ferries travel in excess of 20 kn (37 km/h; 23 mph), but are slower than fast ferries. A typical ferry ride between La Palma and Tenerife may take up to eight hours or more while a fast ferry takes about two and a half hours and between Tenerife and Gran Canaria can be about one hour.[^cite_note-168]

The largest airport is the [Gran Canaria Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria_Airport). Tenerife has two airports, [Tenerife North Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerife_North_Airport) and [Tenerife South Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerife_South_Airport).[^cite_note-169] The island of Tenerife gathers the highest passenger movement of all the Canary Islands through its two airports.[^cite_note-170] The two main islands (Tenerife and Gran Canaria) receive the greatest number of passengers.[^cite_note-171] Tenerife 6,204,499 passengers and Gran Canaria 5,011,176 passengers.[^cite_note-172]

The [port of Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Las_Palmas) is first in freight traffic in the islands,[^cite_note-173] while the [port of Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife) is the first fishing port with approximately 7,500 tons of fish caught, according to the Spanish government publication Statistical Yearbook of State Ports. Similarly, it is the second port in Spain as regards ship traffic, only surpassed by the [Port of Algeciras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Algeciras) Bay.[^cite_note-puertos.es-174] The port's facilities include a border inspection post (BIP) approved by the European Union, which is responsible for inspecting all types of imports from third countries or exports to countries outside the European Economic Area. The port of [Los Cristianos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Cristianos) (Tenerife) has the greatest number of passengers recorded in the Canary Islands, followed by the port of Santa Cruz de Tenerife.[^cite_note-www2.gobiernodecanarias.org-175] The Port of Las Palmas is the third port in the islands in passengers and first in number of vehicles transported.[^cite_note-www2.gobiernodecanarias.org-175]

The [SS America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_America_\(1939\)) was beached at the Canary islands on 18 January 1994. However, the ocean liner broke apart after the course of several years and eventually sank beneath the surface.

### Fauna

See also: [List of birds of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_birds_of_the_Canary_Islands), [List of reptiles of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reptiles_of_the_Canary_Islands), [List of Lepidoptera of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Lepidoptera_of_the_Canary_Islands), and [List of non-marine molluscs of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_non-marine_molluscs_of_the_Canary_Islands)

The bird life includes European and African species, such as the [black-bellied sandgrouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-bellied_sandgrouse), [canary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_canary), graja – a subspecies of [red-billed chough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-billed_chough) endemic to La Palma, [Gran Canaria blue chaffinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria_blue_chaffinch), [Tenerife blue chaffinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerife_blue_chaffinch), [Canary Islands chaffinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands_chaffinch), [Canary Islands chiffchaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands_chiffchaff), [Fuerteventura chat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura_chat), [Tenerife goldcrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerife_goldcrest), [Canarian Egyptian vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_Egyptian_vulture), [Bolle's pigeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolle's_pigeon), [laurel pigeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_pigeon), [plain swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_swift), and [houbara bustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houbara_bustard).

Terrestrial fauna includes the [El Hierro giant lizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Hierro_giant_lizard), [Tachina canariensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachina_canariensis), [La Gomera giant lizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Gomera_giant_lizard), and the (possibly extinct) [La Palma giant lizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Palma_giant_lizard). Mammals include the [Canarian shrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_shrew), [Canary big-eared bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_big-eared_bat), the [Algerian hedgehog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_hedgehog), and the more recently introduced [mouflon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouflon).

#### Extinct fauna



Skull of [Tenerife giant rat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerife_giant_rat) (*Canariomys bravoi*), an endemic species that is now extinct

The Canary Islands were previously inhabited by a variety of [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) animals, such as extinct giant lizards (*[Gallotia goliath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallotia_goliath)*), giant tortoises (*[Centrochelys burchardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrochelys_burchardi)* and *[C. vulcanica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrochelys_vulcanica)*),[^cite_note-181] [Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerife_giant_rat) and [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria_giant_rat) giant rats (*Canariomys bravoi* and *C. tamarani*),[^cite_note-182] and the [lava mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava_mouse) *Malpaisomys insularis*. The [Canary Islands oystercatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands_oystercatcher) went extinct in the 20th century, and extinct birds known only from [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) and [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene) age bones include the [Canary Islands quail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands_quail) (*Coturnix gomerae*), [dune shearwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune_shearwater) (*Puffinus holeae*), [lava shearwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava_shearwater) (*P. olsoni*), [Trias greenfinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trias_greenfinch) (*Chloris triasi*), [slender-billed greenfinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slender-billed_greenfinch) (*C. aurelioi*) and the [long-legged bunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-legged_bunting) (*Emberiza alcoveri*).[^cite_note-183]

### Marine life

Main article: [Marine life of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_life_of_the_Canary_Islands)



A [loggerhead sea turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loggerhead_sea_turtle), by far the most common species of marine turtle in the Canary Islands

The [marine life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_life) found in the Canary Islands is also varied, being a combination of [North Atlantic](698.md), [Mediterranean](19006.md) and [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) species. In recent years, the increasing popularity of both [scuba diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuba_diving) and [underwater photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_photography) have provided biologists with much new information on the marine life of the islands.

Fish found in the islands include many species of [shark](43617.md), [ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batoidea), [moray eel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moray_eel), [bream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparidae), [jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carangidae), [grunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grunt-fish), [scorpionfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorpionfish), [triggerfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triggerfish), [grouper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grouper), [goby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goby), and [blenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blenny). In addition, there is a variety of invertebrates, including numerous species of [sponge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponge), [jellyfish](50185.md), [anemone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_anemone), [crab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crab), [mollusc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollusc), [sea urchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_urchin), [starfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starfish), [sea cucumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holothuroidea) and [coral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral).

There are five species of [marine turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_turtle) that are sighted periodically in the islands, the most common of these being the [endangered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endangered) [loggerhead sea turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loggerhead_sea_turtle).[^cite_note-184] The other four are the [green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_sea_turtle), [hawksbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawksbill_sea_turtle), [leatherback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leatherback_sea_turtle) and [Kemp's ridley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemp's_ridley_sea_turtle) sea turtles. Currently, there are no signs that any of these species breed in the islands, and so those seen in the water are usually [migrating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_turtle_migration). However, it is believed that some of these species may have bred in the islands in the past, and there are records of several sightings of leatherback sea turtle on beaches in Fuerteventura, adding credibility to the theory.

Marine mammals include a large variety of [cetaceans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cetacean). [Hooded seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooded_seals)[^cite_note-185] have also been known to be [biologically vagrant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vagrancy_\(biology\)). The Canary Islands were also formerly home to a population of the rarest [pinniped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinniped) in the world, the [Mediterranean monk seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_monk_seal).

The most famous festivals of the Canary Islands is the carnival. It is the most famous and international festival of the archipelago. The carnival is celebrated in all the islands and all its municipalities, perhaps the two busiest are those of the two Canarian capitals; the [Carnival of Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_of_Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife) (*Tourist Festival of International Interest*) and the [Carnival of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_of_Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria). It is celebrated on the streets between the months of February and March. But the rest of the islands of the archipelago have their carnivals with their own traditions among which stand out: The Festival of the Carneros of [El Hierro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Hierro), the Festival of the Diabletes of [Teguise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teguise_\(municipality\)) in [Lanzarote](304185.md), Los Indianos de [La Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Palma), the Carnival of [San Sebastián de La Gomera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n_de_La_Gomera) and the Carnival of [Puerto del Rosario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_del_Rosario) in [Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura).

## Science and technology



[Roque de los Muchachos Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_de_los_Muchachos_Observatory), La Palma

In the 1960s, Gran Canaria was selected as the location for one of the 14 [ground stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_stations) in the [Manned Space Flight Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manned_Space_Flight_Network) (MSFN) to support the [NASA](18426568.md) space program. [Maspalomas Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspalomas_Station), located in the south of the island, took part in a number of space missions including the [Apollo 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_11) Moon landings and [Skylab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skylab). Today it continues to support satellite communications as part of the [ESA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESA) network.[^cite_note-esa-page-190]

Because of the remote location, a number of [astronomical observatories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observatory) are located in the archipelago, including the [Teide Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teide_Observatory) on Tenerife, the [Roque de los Muchachos Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_de_los_Muchachos_Observatory) on La Palma, and the [Temisas Astronomical Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temisas_Astronomical_Observatory) on Gran Canaria.

Tenerife is the home of the [Instituto de Astrofísica de Canarias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_de_Astrof%C3%ADsica_de_Canarias) (Astrophysical Institute of the Canaries). There is also an Instituto de Bio-Orgánica Antonio González (Antonio González Bio-Organic Institute) at the [University of La Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_La_Laguna). Also at that university are the Instituto de Lingüística Andrés Bello (Andrés Bello Institute of Linguistics), the Centro de Estudios Medievales y Renacentistas (Center for Medieval and Renaissance Studies), the Instituto Universitario de la Empresa (University Institute of Business), the Instituto de Derecho Regional (Regional Institute of Law), the Instituto Universitario de Ciencias Políticas y Sociales (University Institute of Political and Social Sciences) and the Instituto de Enfermedades Tropicales (Institute of Tropical Diseases). The latter is one of the seven institutions of the Red de Investigación de Centros de Enfermedades Tropicales (RICET, "Network of Research of Centers of Tropical Diseases"), located in various parts of Spain. The Instituto Volcanológico de Canarias (Volcanological Institute of the Canary Islands) is based in Tenerife.

## Sports



[Heliodoro Rodríguez López Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Heliodoro_Rodr%C3%ADguez_L%C3%B3pez) in Tenerife, the stadium with the largest field area in the Canary Islands

[Gran Canaria Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Gran_Canaria), the biggest sports venue in the Canary Islands

[^cite_note-193]

A unique form of wrestling known as [Canarian wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_wrestling) (*lucha canaria*) has opponents stand in a special area called a "terrero" and try to throw each other to the ground using strength and quick movements.[^cite_note-ctspanish.com-194]

Another sport is the "game of the sticks" (palo canario) where opponents fence with long sticks. This may have come about from the shepherds of the islands who would challenge each other using their long walking sticks.[^cite_note-ctspanish.com-194]

Furthermore, there is the [shepherd's jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salto_del_pastor) (*salto del pastor*). This involves using a long stick to vault over an open area. This sport possibly evolved from the shepherd's need to occasionally get over an open area in the hills as they were tending their sheep.[^cite_note-ctspanish.com-194]

The two main [football](10568.md) teams in the archipelago are: the [CD Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD_Tenerife) (founded in 1912) and [UD Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UD_Las_Palmas) (founded in 1949). As of the 2025/2026 season, UD Las Palmas plays in the Segunda División, the second tier of Spanish football. CD Tenerife however plays in The Primera Federación. When in the same division, the clubs contest the [Canary Islands derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Islands_derby). There are smaller clubs also playing in the mainland [Spanish football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_football_league_system), most notably [UD Lanzarote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UD_Lanzarote) and [CD Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD_Laguna_de_Tenerife), although no other Canarian clubs have played in the top flight.

The mountainous terrain of the Canary Islands also caters to the growing popularity of [ultra running](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_running) and [ultramarathons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultramarathons) as host of annual competitive long-distance events including [CajaMar Tenerife Bluetrail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CajaMar_Tenerife_Bluetrail) on [Tenerife](269223.md), [Transvulcania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transvulcania) on [La Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Palma), [Transgrancanaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgrancanaria)[^cite_note-195] on [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria), and the Half [Marathon des Sables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon_des_Sables) on [Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura). A yearly [Ironman Triathlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironman_Triathlon) has been taking place on [Lanzarote](304185.md) since 1992.[^cite_note-ironman-196][^cite_note-ironmanhistory-197]
