**Mathura** (Braj pronunciation: [\[mɐ.t̪ʰʊ.ɾäː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/41/Mathura.ogg/Mathura.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/41/Mathura.ogg/Mathura.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a city and the administrative headquarters of [Mathura district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathura_district) in the [Indian state](375986.md) of [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md). It is located 162 kilometres (101 mi) south-east of [Delhi](37756.md); and about 15 kilometres (9.3 mi) from the town of [Vrindavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrindavan). In ancient times, Mathura was an [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic) hub, located at the junction of important [caravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_\(travellers\)) routes. The [2011 Census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Census_of_India) estimated the population of Mathura at 441,894.

In [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), the birthplace of [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna), one of the main deities, is believed to be located in Mathura at the [Krishna Janmasthan Temple Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_Janmasthan_Temple_Complex).[^cite_note-Prasad-5] It is one of the [Sapta Puri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapta_Puri), the seven cities considered holy by [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus), also called the Mokshyadayni Tirth. The [Kesava Deo Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesava_Deo_Temple) was built in ancient times on the site of Krishna's birthplace (an underground [prison](19008450.md)). Mathura was the capital of the kingdom of [Surasena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surasena), ruled by [Kamsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamsa), the maternal uncle of Krishna. Mathura is part of the [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna) circuit (Mathura, Vrindavan, [Barsana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barsana), [Govardhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govardhan), [Kurukshetra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/48_kos_parikrama_of_Kurukshetra), [Dwarka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarka) and [Bhalka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhalka)). [Krishna Janmashtami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_Janmashtami) is celebrated in Mathura every year.

Mathura has been chosen as one of the heritage cities for the [Heritage City Development and Augmentation Yojana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_City_Development_and_Augmentation_Yojana) scheme of Government of India.[^cite_note-holycity-6]

## History

See also: [Mathura art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathura_art)

Traditionally it is believed that it was founded by [Shatrughna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatrughna) after killing [Yadava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yadava) [Lavana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavana) at the site of Madhuvana. According to [Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana) it was founded by [Madhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhu) (a man of the [Yadu tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yadu)). Later on Madhu's son [Lavanasura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavanasura) was defeated by Shatrughna.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Mathura, which lies at the centre of the cultural region of [Braj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braj)[^cite_note-LuciaMichelutti1-4] has an ancient history and is also believed to be the homeland and birthplace of Krishna, who belonged to the [Yadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yadu) dynasty. According to the [Archaeological Survey of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Survey_of_India) plaque at the [Mathura Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathura_Museum),[^cite_note-Britishlibrary-9] the city is mentioned in the oldest Indian epic, the *Ramayana*. In the epic, the [Ikshwaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikshwaku) prince Shatrughna slays a demon called Lavanasura and claims the land. Afterwards, the place came to be known as [Madhuvan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhuvan) as it was thickly wooded, then Madhupura and later Mathura.[^cite_note-p1-10] The most important pilgrimage site in Mathura was Katra ('market place'), now referred to as [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna) Janmasthan ('the birthplace of Krishna'). Excavations at the site revealed pottery and terracotta dating to the sixth century BCE, the remains of a large [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) complex, including a monastery called Yasha Vihara of the [Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) period, as well as [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) sculptures of the same era.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

### Ancient history

Archaeological excavations at Mathura show the gradual growth of a village into an important city during the [Vedic age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_age). The earliest period belonged to the [Painted Grey Ware culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painted_Grey_Ware_culture) (1100–500 BCE), followed by the [Northern Black Polished Ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Black_Polished_Ware) culture (700–200 BCE). Mathura derived its importance as a center of trade due to its location where the [northern trade route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Trunk_Road) of the [Indo-Gangetic Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_Plain) met with the routes to [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa) (central India) and the [west coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_India).[^cite_note-Singh2008-13] Archaeologists have discovered a fragment of Mathura red sandstone from [Rakhigarhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhigarhi) - a site of [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation) dated to third millennium BCE - which was used as a grindstone; red sandstone was also a popular material for [historic period sculptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_Mathura).[^cite_note-14]

By the sixth century BCE Mathura became the capital of the [Surasena Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surasena).[^cite_note-ImpGazetteer-15] The city was later ruled by the [Maurya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya) empire (fourth to second centuries BCE). [Megasthenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megasthenes), writing in the early third century BCE, mentions Mathura as a great city under the name Μέθορα (*Méthora*).[^cite_note-16] It seems it never was under the direct control of the following [Shunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunga_Empire) dynasty (2nd century BCE) as not a single archaeological remain of a Shunga presence were ever found in Mathura.[^cite_note-Rhie-17]

The [Indo-Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Greeks) may have taken control, direct or indirect, of Mathura some time between 180 BCE and 100 BCE, and remained so as late as 70 BCE according to the [Yavanarajya inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yavanarajya_inscription),[^cite_note-Rhie-17] which was found in [Maghera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghera), a town 17 kilometres (11 mi) from Mathura.[^cite_note-BotAI-18] The opening of the 3 line text of this inscription in [Brahmi script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmi_script) translates as: "In the 116th year of the [Yavana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yavana) kingdom..."[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] or '"In the 116th year of Yavana hegemony" ("*Yavanarajya*")[^cite_note-Rhie-17] However, this also corresponds to the presence of the native [Mitra dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitra_dynasty_\(Mathura\)) of local rulers in Mathura, in approximately the same time frame (150 BCE—50 BCE), possibly pointing to a vassalage relationship with the Indo-Greeks.[^cite_note-Rhie-17]

#### Indo-Scythians

After a period of local rule, Mathura was conquered by the [Indo-Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Scythians) during the first century BCE. The Indo-Scythian satraps of Mathura are sometimes called the "[Northern Satraps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Satraps)", as opposed to the "[Western Satraps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Satraps)" ruling in [Gujarat](53707.md) and [Malwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malwa). However, Indo-Scythian control proved to be short lived, following the reign of the Indo-Scythian *Mahakshatrapa* ("Great Satrap") [Rajuvula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajuvula), c. 10–25 CE. The Mora Well inscription of Mahakshatrapa [Rajuvula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajuvula), of the early decades of the first century CE, found in a village seven miles from Mathura, stated that images *pratima(h)* of the blessed (*bhagavatam*) five [Vrishni heroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrishni_heroes), were installed in a stone shrine of a person called Tosa.[^cite_note-auto2-21] The heroes were identified from a passage in the [Vayu Purana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vayu_Purana) as Samkarsana, [Vasudev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasudeva), Pradyumna, Samba, and Aniruddha.[^cite_note-22] The English translation of the inscription read:-

> . . . of the son of mahakṣatrapa Rāṃjūvula, svāmi . . . The images of the holy paṃcavīras of the Vṛṣṇis is... the stone shrine... whom the magnificent matchless stone house of Toṣā was erected and maintained... five objects of adoration made of stone, radiant, as it were with highest beauty...[^cite_note-23]

The Mathura inscription of the time of Mahakshatrapa [Rajuvula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajuvula)'s son, Mahakshatrapa [Sodasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodasa) recorded erection of a *torana* (gateway), *vedika* (terrace) and *chatuhsala* (quadrangle) at the *Mahasthana* (great place) of Bhagavat Vasudeva.[^cite_note-24] Several male torsos representing the Vrisni heroes were also found in a shrine in Mora dating to the time of Mahakshatrapa [Sodasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodasa).[^cite_note-auto2-21]

#### Kushan Empire

The [Kushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire) emperors took control of Mathura some time after the *[Mahakshatrapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahakshatrapa)* ("Great Satrap") [Sodasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodasa) (reigned circa 15 CE), although several of his successors ruled as Kushan vassals, such as the Indo-Scythian *Mahakshatrapa* [Kharapallana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharapallana) and the Satrap [Vanaspara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanaspara), both of whom paid allegiance to the Kushans, in an inscription at [Sarnath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarnath), dating to the third year of the reign of the Kushan emperor [Kanishka the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanishka) c. 130 CE.[^cite_note-25]

The Kushans made Mathura one of their capitals. (Preceding and/or concurrent capitals of the Kushans included [Kapisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapisa_\(city\)) \[modern Bagram, Afghanistan], [Purushapura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purushapura) \[modern Peshawar, Pakistan] and [Takshasila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirsukh) \[adjoining modern Taxila, Pakistan].)

Mathuran art and culture reached its zenith during the Kushan era.[^cite_note-singh-26] Perhaps the most famous example was the artistic style known as the [Mathura school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_Mathura). Mathura [ateliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atelier) were most active during the epoch of the great Kushan emperors Kanishka, [Huvishka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huvishka), [Vasudeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasudeva_I) whose reign represents the Golden Age of Mathura [sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculpture).[^cite_note-auto1-27]

During the 3rd century CE, following the decline of the Kushan Empire, the [Naga dynasty of Padmavati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagas_of_Padmavati) took control of Mathura.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAshvini_Agrawal198954-28]

#### Gupta Empire

In the reign of [Chandragupta Vikramaditya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandragupta_Vikramaditya), a magnificent temple of [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu) was built at the site of Katra Keshavadeva.[^cite_note-auto1-27] [Kalidasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalidasa), hailed as the greatest poet and dramatist in [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit), in the fourth-fifth century CE mentioned the groves of [Vrindavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrindavan) and [Govardhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govardhan) hill as:

> ...the king of Mathura, whose fame was acknowledged in song even by the devatas... At that moment, though still in Mathura, it appears as if Ganga has merged with Yamuna at the Sangam... In a Vrindavan garden which is superior even to Kubera's garden, known as Chaitra-ratha... You can, as well, during rains, look at the dancing peacocks, while sitting in a pleasant cave of the Goverdhan Mountain.[^cite_note-29]

Chinese Buddhist Monk [Faxian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faxian) mentions the city as a centre of Buddhism about 400 CE. He found the people were very well off, there were no taxes other than for those on farmers who tilled the royal land. He found that people did not kill animals, no one consumed wine, and did not eat onion or garlic. He found that engraved title deeds were issued to land owners. Visiting priests were provided with accommodation, beds, mats, food, drinks and clothes to perform scholarly works.[^cite_note-30]

#### Harsha Empire

[Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang), who visited the city in 634 CE, mentions it as Mot'ulo, recording that it contained twenty Buddhist monasteries and many Hindu temples.[^cite_note-rongxi103-31][^cite_note-EB1911-32] Later, he went east to [Thanesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanesar), [Jalandhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalandhar) in the eastern [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_region), before climbing up to visit predominantly [Theravada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theravada) monasteries in the [Kulu valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulu_valley) and turning southward again to [Bairat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bairat) and then Mathura, on the [Yamuna river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamuna_river).[^cite_note-33]

### Medieval History and Islamic Invasions

#### Early Middle Ages

The famous female [Alvar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvars) saint, [Andal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andal) visualised going to a pilgrimage which began at Mathura, then proceeded to [Gokul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gokul), the [Yamuna](354286.md), the pool of Kaliya, [Vrindavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrindavan), [Govardhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govardhan), and finished at [Dwarka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarka).[^cite_note-34] The eleventh century Kashmiri poet, [Bilhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilhana) visited Mathura and [Vrindavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrindavan) after leaving Kashmir en route to Karnataka.[^cite_note-35]

#### High Middle Ages

The city was sacked and many of its temples destroyed by [Mahmud of Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni) in 1018 CE.[^cite_note-EB1911-32] The capture of Mathura by [Maḥmūd Ibn Sebüktegīn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni) is described by the historian al-Utbi (Abu Nasr Muhammad ibn Muhammad al Jabbaru-l 'Utbi) in his work [Tarikh Yamini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarikh_Yamini) as follows:

> The wall of the city was constructed of hard stone, and two gates opened upon the river flowing under the city, which were erected upon strong and lofty foundations, to protect them against the floods of the river and rains. On both sides of the city there were a thousand houses, to which idol temples were attached, all strengthened from top to bottom by rivets of iron, and all made of masonry work; and opposite to them were other buildings, supported on broad wooden pillars, to give them strength.

> In the middle of the city there was a temple larger and firmer than the rest, which can neither be described nor painted. The Sultan thus wrote respecting it :— " If any should wish to construct a building equal to this, he would not be able to do it without expending an hundred thousand thousand red dinars, and it would occupy two hundred years, even though the most experienced and able workmen were employed." Among the idols there were five made of red gold, each five yards high, fixed in the air without support. In the eyes of one of these idols there were two rubies, of such value, that if any one were to sell such as are like them, he would obtain fifty thousand dinars. On another, there was a sapphire purer than water, and more sparkling than crystal; the weight was four hundred and fifty miskals. The two feet of another idol weighed four thousand four hundred miskals, and the entire quantity of gold yielded by the bodies of these idols, was ninety-eight thousand three hundred miskals. The idols of silver amounted to two hundred, but they could not be weighed without breaking them to pieces and putting them into scales. The Sultan gave orders that all the temples should be burnt with naphtha and fire, and levelled with the ground.[^cite_note-36]

The temple at Katra was sacked by [Maḥmūd Ibn Sebüktegīn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni). A temple was built to replace it in 1150 CE. The Mathura *prasasti* (Eulogistic Inscription) dated [Samvat (V.S.)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikram_Samvat) 1207 (1150 CE), said to have been found in 1889 CE at the Keshava mound by [Anton Fuhrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Anton_F%C3%BChrer), German Indologist who worked with the Archaeological Survey of India, recorded the foundations of a temple dedicated to [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu) at the Katra site:

> Jajja, who carried the burden of the varga, together with a committee of trustees (goshtijana), built a large temple of Vishnu, brilliantly white and touching the clouds.

Jajja was a vassal of the [Gahadavalas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gahadavala_dynasty) in charge of Mathura, and the committee mentioned in the *prasasti* could have been of an earlier [Vaishnava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishnavism) temple.[^cite_note-37] The temple built by Jajja at Katra was destroyed by the forces of [Qutubuddin Aibak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutb_al-Din_Aibak), though [Feroz Tughlaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firuz_Shah_Tughlaq) (r. 1351–88 CE) was also said to have attacked it.[^cite_note-38] It was repaired and survived till the reign of [Sikandar Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikandar_Lodi) (r. 1489–1517 CE).

In the twelfth century, Bhatta Lakshmidhara, chief minister of the [Gahadavala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gahadavala_dynasty) king [Govindachandra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govindachandra_\(Gahadavala_dynasty\)) (r. 1114–1155 CE), wrote the earliest surviving collection of verses in praise of the sacred sites of Mathura in his work Krtyakalpataru, which has been described as "the first re-statement of the theory of *Tirtha-yatra* (pilgrimage)".[^cite_note-39] In his Krtyakalpataru, Bhatta Lakshmidhara devoted an entire section (9) to Mathura.[^cite_note-40]

Later on the city was sacked again by Sikandar Lodi, who ruled the [Sultanate of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Delhi) from 1489 to 1517 CE.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] Sikandar Lodi earned the epithet of 'Butt Shikan', the 'Destroyer of Idols'. [Ferishta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firishta) recorded that Sikandar Lodi was a staunch Muslim, with a passion for vandalising Hindu temples:

> He was firmly attached to the Mahomedan religion, and made a point of destroying all Hindu temples. In the city of Mathura he caused masjids and bazaars to be built opposite the bathing-stairs leading to the river, and ordered that no Hindus should be allowed to bathe there. He forbade the barbers to shave the beards and heads of the inhabitants, in order to prevent the Hindus following their usual practices at such pilgrimages.[^cite_note-43]

In Tarikh-i Daudi, of 'Abdu-lla (written during the time of [Jahangir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahangir)) said of Sikandar Lodi:

> He was so zealous a Musulman that he utterly destroyed divers places of worship of the infidels, and left not a vestige remaining of them. He entirely ruined the shrines of Mathura, the mine of heathenism, and turned their principal Hindu places of worship into caravanserais and colleges. Their stone images were given to the butchers to serve them as meat-weights, and all the Hindus in Mathura were strictly prohibited from shaving their heads and beards, and performing their ablutions. He thus put an end to all the idolatrous rites of the infidels there; and no Hindu, if he wished to have his head or beard shaved, could get a barber to do it. Every city thus conformed as he desired to the customs of Islam.[^cite_note-44]

[Vallabhacharya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallabha) and [Chaitanya Mahaprabhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaitanya_Mahaprabhu) arrived in the Braj region, in search of sacred places that had been destroyed or lost. In *Shrikrsnashrayah*, that make up the *Sodashagrantha*, [Vallabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallabha) said of his age:

> The Malechchhas (non-Hindus in this context) have surrounded all the holy places with the result that they have become infected with evil. Besides, the holy people are full of sorrow. At such a time [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna) alone is my way.[^cite_note-45]

#### Late Middle Ages

The [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people), Father Antonio Monserrate (1536 CE-1600 CE), who was on a Jesuit mission at the Mughal Court during the times of [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar), visited Mathura in 1580–82, and noted that all temples built at sites associated with the deeds of [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna) were in ruins:-

> It (Mathura) used to be a great and well populated city, with splendid buildings and a great circuit of walls. The ruins plainly indicate how imposing its buildings were. For out of these forgotten ruins are dug up columns and very ancient statues, of skilful and cunning workmanship. Only one Hindu temple is left out of many; for the Musalmans have completely destroyed all except the pyramids. Huge crowds of pilgrims come from all over India to this temple, which is situated on the high bank of the Jomanis ([Yamuna](354286.md))...[^cite_note-46]

The [Keshavadeva temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_Janmasthan_Temple_Complex) was rebuilt by the [Bundela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundela) Rajput Rajah [Vir Singh Deo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vir_Singh_Deo) at a cost of thirty-three lakh rupees when the gold was priced at around ₹ 10/- per [*tola*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tola_\(unit\)).[^cite_note-47] And the grand structure of the temple in Mathura was regarded a "wonder of the age".[^cite_note-48]

The [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) [Emperor Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb), built the Shahi-Eidgah Mosque during his rule, which is adjacent to [Shri Krishna Janmabhoomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shri_Krishna_Janmabhoomi) believed to be over a [Hindu temple](2247213.md).[^cite_note-49] He also changed the city's name to Islamabad.[^cite_note-50] In 1669, Aurangzeb issued a general order for the demolition of Hindu schools and temples, in 1670, specifically ordered the destruction of the Keshavadeva temple. Saqi Mustaid Khan recorded:

> On Thursday, 27th January/15 Ramzan (27 January 1670)... the Emperor as the promoter of justice and overthrower of mischief, as a knower of truth and destroyer of oppression as the zephyr of the garden of victory and the reviver of the faith of the Prophet, issued orders for the demolition of the temple situated in Mathura, famous as the Dehra of Kesho Rai. In a short time by the great exertions of his officers, the destruction of this strong foundation of infidelity was accomplished and on its site a lofty mosque was built by the expenditure of a large sum... Praised be the august God of the faith of Islam, that in the auspicious reign of this destroyer of infidelity and turbulence, such a wonderful and seemingly impossible work was successfully accomplished.

> On seeing this instance of the strength of the emperor's faith and the grandeur of his devotion to God, the proud Rajas were stifled, and in amazement they stood like images facing the wall. The idols, large and small, set with costly jewels, which had been set up in the temple, were brought to Agra, and buried under the step of the mosque of the Begum Shahib in order to be continuously trodden upon. The name of Mathura was changed to Islamabad.[^cite_note-51]

The Muslim conquest resulted in the destruction of all [Buddhist](3267529.md), [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism), and [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) temples and monuments in and around Mathura. [Buddhism](3267529.md), already in decline, never revived, and for the next four hundred years the [Jains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) and [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus) were unable to erect any temples that were not sooner or later demolished.[^cite_note-auto-52] Many of the sites that had been places of religious importance were abandoned and gradually sank beneath the earth. But some of them were not forgotten, owing to the persistence of oral tradition, the refashioning of a temple into a mosque, or the presence of humble shrines, some of which housed sculptural fragments of earlier buildings. Several of them have survived as places of significance in the modern pilgrimage circuit.[^cite_note-auto-52]

### Early Modern History



Gate of Shet Lukhmeechund's Temple, a photo by Eugene Clutterbuck Impey, 1860s

According the biographer of Raja [Jai Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jai_Singh_II), Atmaram, when Jai Singh was campaigning against the Jat Raja [Churaman Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churaman), he bathed at Radha *kund* on the full moon of Kartik, went to Mathura in the month of Shravan in 1724, and performed the marriage of his daughter on [*Janmashtami*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_Janmashtami). He then undertook a tour of the sacred forests of [Braj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braj), and, on his return to Mathura, founded religious establishments and celebrated [Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holi).[^cite_note-54]

#### Pilgrimage by the Family of Peshwa of Maratha Empire

During the period of the expansion of [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Confederacy), pilgrimage to the holy places in the north became quite frequent. [Pilgrims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrim) required protection on the way and took advantage of the constant movement of troops that journeyed to and back from their homeland for military purposes. That is how the practice arose of ladies accompanying military expeditions. The mother of [Peshwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) [Balaji Baji Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaji_Baji_Rao), [Kashitai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashibai) performed her famous pilgrimage for four years in the north, visiting Mathura, [Prayag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prayagraj), [Ayodhya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayodhya), [Banaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varanasi), and other holy places.[^cite_note-55]

## Religious heritage



Entrance to the [Shri Krishna Janmabhoomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shri_Krishna_Janmabhoomi) temple complex

Mathura is a holy city in Hinduism and is considered the heart of [Brij Bhoomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brij_Bhoomi), the land of Krishna.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-Lucia_Michelutti00-57] The twin-city to Mathura is [Vrindavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrindavan).

There are many places of historic and religious importance in Mathura and its neighbouring towns.[^cite_note-holycity-6]

[Krishna Janmasthan Temple Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_Janmasthan_Temple_Complex) is an important group of temples built around what is considered to be the birthplace of [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna).[^cite_note-Avatar-58][^cite_note-Vemsani-59] The temple complex contains Keshav Deva temple, Garbha Griha shrine, Bhagavata Bhavan and the Rangabhoomi where the final battle between Krishna and [Kamsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kans) took place.[^cite_note-Janm-60][^cite_note-Prasad-5][^cite_note-Britishlibrary-9][^cite_note-Avatar-58]

The [Dwarkadheesh Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarkadheesh_temple_Mathura) is one of the largest temples in Mathura.[^cite_note-Prasad-5] [Vishram Ghat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishram_Ghat) at the bank of river Yamuna is said to be the place were Krishna had rested after killing [Kamsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kans).[^cite_note-Prasad-5]

Other notable Hindu religious sites and heritage locations includes the Gita Mandir,[^cite_note-Mathura1-61] Govind Dev temple,[^cite_note-Mathura1-61] [ISKCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISKCON) temple,[^cite_note-Prasad-5] [Kusum Sarovar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusum_Sarovar),[^cite_note-Mathura1-61] [Naam yog Sadhna Mandir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naam_yog_Sadhna_Mandir), Peepleshwar Mahadeo Temple[^cite_note-MathuraTT-62][^cite_note-63] and Yum Yamuna Temple[^cite_note-MathuraTT-62]

[Kankali Tila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankali_Tila) brought forth many treasures of Jain art. The archaeological findings testify the existence of two Jain temples and stupas. Numerous Jain sculptures, [Ayagapatas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayagapata) (tablet of homage),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDas1980171-64] pillars, crossbeams and lintels were found during archaeological excavations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVyas199516-65] Some of the sculptures are provided with inscriptions that report on the contemporary society and organisation of the Jain community.

Most sculptures could be dated from the second century BC to the 12th century CE, thus representing a continuous period of about 14 centuries during which [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) flourished at Mathura.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVyas199516-65] These sculptures are now housed in the Lucknow State Museum and in the Mathura Museum.

The [Mathura Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathura_Museum) is notable for archaeological artefacts, especially those from the [Kushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire) and [Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gupta_Empire) empires. It has sculptures associated with Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism.[^cite_note-Britishlibrary-9][^cite_note-66]

## Festivals

Main article: [Krishna Janmashtami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_Janmashtami)

Krishna Janmashtami is grandly celebrated every year in Mathura. Every year 3 to 3.5 million devotees celebrate Janmashtami in Mathura, with the maximum number of devotees visiting the Keshav Deva temple and the Dwarkadheesh temple.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] Devotees generally observe a fast and break it at midnight when Krishn was born. Devotional songs, dance performances, [bhog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhog) and [aartis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarti) are observed across Mathura-Vrindavan.[^cite_note-69]

## Culinary tradition

Mathura along with the twin city of [Vrindavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrindavan), (both of which are associated with Shri Krishna) are main centers of [Braj Cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braj_Cuisine).[^cite_note-70] Mathura is known for its [Mathura Peda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathura_peda),[^cite_note-71] Mathura Lassi, Mathura Heeng [Kachori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachori), Mathura [Chaat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaat),[^cite_note-72] Moong Dal Cheela, Bedmi [puri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puri_\(food\)) and Dubki Aloo Jhol.

The [2011 census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India) estimates the population of Mathura to be 441,894, with a decadal growth rate of 22.53 per cent. Males account for 54% (268,445) and females for 46% (173,449) of this population. Sex ratio of Mathura is 858 females per 1000 males, which has increased from 840 (2001). However, national sex ratio is 940. Population density in 2011 has increased from 621 per km<sup>2</sup> in 2001 to 761 per km<sup>2</sup>. Mathura has an average literacy rate of 72.65 per cent which has increased from 61.46 per cent (2001) but still lower than the national average of 74.04 per cent. This figure was 19.56 per cent in 2001 census.[^cite_note-census2011-76]

### Languages

According to the 2011 census on Mathura NPP, 95.4% of the people identified as [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) speakers, 2.6% as [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) speakers and 1.4% as speakers of [Braj Bhasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braj_Bhasha)[^cite_note-77] (the local dialect).[^cite_note-LuciaMichelutti1-4] The city also lies within the cultural region of [Braj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braj).[^cite_note-LuciaMichelutti1-4]

### Road

Mathura is well-connected by road to the rest of India. [National Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_highways_of_India) NH-19(previously [NH-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_2_\(India\))) from Delhi to Kolkata, with diversion for Chennai also passes through Mathura. ### Tram

A [tram](30733.md) network has been proposed in the city, which would make Mathura the second only city in India (besides [Kolkata](47905.md)) to have a functional tram transport. In 2017, the local MLA [Shrikant Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrikant_Sharma) announced that the trams will be operational in Mathura and [Vrindavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrindavan) by 2018.[^cite_note-80]

### Air

Currently the city has no airport. The nearest airports are the [Agra Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agra_Airport) (about 60 km away) and the [Delhi Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi_International_Airport) (about 160 km away) with major national and international air routes. The under-construction [Jewar Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewar_Airport) in [Greater Noida](4942600.md) is approximately 75 km from Mathura and is expected to be country's largest airport when fully operational. Land has been earmarked, and construction is in progress near the [Yamuna Expressway](19787093.md), with plans to open in the next five years with regular flights to major national and international air routes in future.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

In 2012, the then civil aviation minister [Ajit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajit_Singh_\(politician,_born_1939\)) suggested Mathura's name for the site of a new greenfield international airport to the then chief minister of [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md), [Akhilesh Yadav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhilesh_Yadav). Mathura's name came to note when a group of ministers terminated the plan of building [Taj International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taj_International_Airport) at Agra.[^cite_note-84]

## Strategic importance

[I Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Corps_\(India\)) (Strike Formation)[^cite_note-85] within the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army)'s [Central Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Command_\(India\)) is based in Mathura, hosting Strike I Corps headquarters in a large classified area in the outskirts of the city known as Mathura Cantonment (Central Command is headquartered in [Lucknow](392095.md)). It hosts Strike Infantry units, air defence units, armoured divisions, engineer brigades, artillery Units, and classified units of [Strategic Forces Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Forces_Command). The I Corps is primarily responsible for the western borders of India. In 2007 during Exercise Ashwamedha, all the armoured, artillery, and infantry divisions performed a simulation of an overall NBC (nuclear-chemical-biological) environment. The aim was to show operational ability in high intensity, short duration and 'sudden' battle.[^cite_note-86]

[Mathura Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathura_Refinery) located in the city is one of the biggest oil refineries of Asia with a refining capacity of 8.0 million [tonnes](31185.md) per year.[^cite_note-Iocl.com-87] This oil refinery of [Indian Oil Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Oil_Corporation) Ltd. is a technologically advanced oil refinery and provides local employment opportunities as well. Its main focus is to meet the demands from the [NCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Capital_Region_\(India\)).[^cite_note-Iocl.com-87] The refinery had undertaken projects to upgrade its diesel and gasoline units to bring Sulphur levels down by nearly 80 per cent.[^cite_note-88]
