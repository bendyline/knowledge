# Lead ship





*Left:* [USS *Essex*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Essex_\(CV-9\)), lead of the [*Essex* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex-class_aircraft_carrier) \
*Right:* [MS *Voyager of the Seas*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyager_of_the_Seas), lead ship of the [*Voyager* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyager-class_cruise_ship)

The **lead ship**, **name ship**, or **class leader** is the first of a series or [class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_class) of [ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship) that are all constructed according to the same [general design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_architecture). The term is applicable to [naval ships](53159.md) and large civilian vessels.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-\:0-4]

Large ships are very complex and may take five to ten years to build. Improvements based on experience with building and operating the lead ship are likely to be incorporated into the design or construction of later ships in the class, so it is rare to have vessels that are identical.

The second and later ships are often started before the first one is completed, launched and tested. Nevertheless, building copies is still more efficient and cost effective than building [prototypes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prototype), and the lead ship will usually be followed by copies with some improvements rather than radically different versions. The improvements will sometimes be [retrofitted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrofitting) to the lead ship. Occasionally, the lead ship will be launched and commissioned for [shakedown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakedown_cruise) testing before following ships are completed, making the lead ship a combination of template and prototype, rather than expending resources on a prototype that will never see actual use.

## Naming

Ship classes are typically named in one of two ways: echoing the name of the lead ship, such as the [*Pennsylvania*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania-class_battleship) [battleships](4054.md), whose lead ship was [USS *Pennsylvania*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Pennsylvania_\(BB-38\)),[^cite_note-\:0-4] and the [*Olympic* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic-class_ocean_liner), whose lead ship was [RMS *Olympic*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMS_Olympic), or defining a theme by which vessels in the class are named, as in the [Royal Navy](26061.md)'s [Tribal-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal-class_frigate) [frigates](11464.md), named after tribes of the world, such as [HMS *Mohawk*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Mohawk_\(F125\)). If a ship class is produced for another navy, the first active unit will become the lead ship for that navy; for example, the [*Oliver Hazard Perry*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Hazard_Perry-class_frigate) frigates are known as the [*Adelaide* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide-class_frigate) in [Royal Australian Navy](26327.md) service.
