The **Galápagos Islands**[^cite_note-4] ([Spanish](26825.md): *Islas Galápagos*) are an [archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) of [volcanic islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_island) in the [Eastern Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Pacific), located around the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator), 485 nautical miles (898 km; 558 mi) west of the mainland of [South America](26769.md). They form the [Galápagos Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_Province) of the [Republic of Ecuador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ecuador), with a population of slightly over 33,000 in 2020. The province is divided into the [cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantons_of_Ecuador) of [San Cristóbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_Island), [Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_Island_\(Gal%C3%A1pagos\)), and [Isabela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabela_Island_\(Gal%C3%A1pagos\)), the three most populated islands in the chain. The Galápagos are famous for their large number of [endemic species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism), which were studied by [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin) in the 1830s and [inspired his theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inception_of_Darwin's_theory) of [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) by means of [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection). All of these islands are protected as part of Ecuador's [Galápagos National Park](7486805.md) and [Marine Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_Marine_Reserve).

There is no firm evidence that [Polynesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_expansion) or the [Indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_South_America) of South America reached the islands before their accidental discovery by [Bishop Tomás de Berlanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fray_Tom%C3%A1s_de_Berlanga) in 1535. If some visitors did arrive, poor access to [fresh water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_water) on the islands seems to have limited settlement. The [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) similarly ignored the islands, although during the [Golden Age of Piracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Age_of_Piracy), various pirates used the Galápagos as a base to raid Spanish shipping along the [Peruvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru) coast. The [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feral_goats) and [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_rat) and [brown rats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_rat) introduced during this period [greatly damaged](67554.md) the existing ecosystems of several islands. British sailors were chiefly responsible for exploring and mapping the area. [Darwin's voyage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_voyage_of_HMS_Beagle) on [HMS *Beagle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Beagle) was part of an extensive British survey of the coasts of South America. [Ecuador](9334.md), which [won its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian_War_of_Independence) from [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Spain_\(1808%E2%80%931874\)) in 1822 and [left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_Gran_Colombia) [Gran Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Colombia) in 1830, formally occupied and claimed the islands on 12 February 1832 while the voyage was ongoing.[^cite_note-galhis-5] [José de Villamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_Villamil), the founder of the [Ecuadorian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian_Navy), led the push to colonize and settle the islands,[^cite_note-galhis-5] gradually supplanting the English names of the major islands with Spanish ones. The [United States](3434750.md) built the islands' [first airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seymour_Airport) as a [base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_military_bases) to protect the western approaches of the [Panama Canal](24849.md) in the 1930s. After [World War II](32927.md), its facilities were transferred to Ecuador. With the growing importance of [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) to the [local economy](9339.md), the airport modernized in the 2010s, using recycled materials for any expansion and shifting entirely to [renewable energy](25784.md) sources to handle its roughly 300,000 visitors each year.

## Etymology

The Galápagos Islands take their name from their native [giant tortoises](7934681.md), known in Spanish as "*galápagos*" (derived from a [pre-Roman Iberian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleohispanic_languages) word meaning '[turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turtle)' or 'tortoise').[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] The name is first attested as the Spanish/Latin hybrid *Insulae de los Galopegos* ('Islands of the Turtles') on the map of the Americas in [Abraham Ortelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Ortelius)'s *[Theater of the Lands of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_of_the_Lands_of_the_World)* ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Theatrum Orbis Terrarum*), first published in 1570.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKricherLoughlin202227-8] Early sailors to the islands also dubbed them the "Enchanted Isles" due to the difficulty in navigating the winds and currents around them.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJackson19931-9]

The islands have also been known by other names. They were officially named the Ecuador Archipelago (*Archipiélago de Ecuador*) or Archipelago of the Equator (*Archipiélago del Ecuador*) following their annexation by Ecuador in 1832.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPerry198410-10] In 1892, upon the quadricentennial of [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus)'s [first voyage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voyages_of_Columbus) they were renamed the Colón or Columbus Archipelago (*Archipiélago del Colón*).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPerry198410-10] This remains their official title in Ecuadorian maps and state documents.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKricherLoughlin202227-8]

The islands were mapped by the English buccaneer [Ambrose Cowley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrose_Cowley) in 1684 and the British naval officer [Captain James Colnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Colnett) in 1793. Both men named the archipelago's islands after [British monarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_monarch), [noblemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_nobility), and [Royal Navy](26061.md) officers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPerry198410-10] Over time, the English names were gradually replaced by Spanish names after Ecuador annexed the islands in 1832, but the English names remain in use in older sources.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKricherLoughlin202244-11]

## Geology

The Galápagos islands are located at the [Galápagos triple junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_triple_junction). The archipelago is located on the [Nazca plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazca_plate) (a tectonic plate), which is moving east/southeast, diving under the South American plate at a rate of about 2.5 inches (6.4 cm) per year.[^cite_note-12] It is also atop the [Galápagos hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_hotspot), a place where the Earth's crust is being melted from below by a [mantle plume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantle_plume), creating volcanoes. The first islands formed here at least 8 million and possibly up to 90 million years ago.[^cite_note-GalapagosGeoIntro-13]

[Volcanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanism) has been continuous on the Galápagos Islands for at least 20 million years, and perhaps even longer. The [mantle plume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantle_plume) beneath the eastward-moving [Nazca plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazca_plate) (51 km/[Myr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myr)) has given rise to a 3-kilometre-thick platform under the [island chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelago) and [seamounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seamount). Besides the Galápagos Archipelago, other key [tectonic features](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonics) in the region include the Northern Galápagos volcanic province between the archipelago and the [Galápagos spreading center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_spreading_center) (GSC) 200 km (120 mi) to the north at the boundary of the Nazca plate and the [Cocos plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_plate). This spreading center terminates at the [East Pacific Rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pacific_Rise) to the west and is bounded by the Cocos Ridge and the Carnegie Ridge to the east. Furthermore, the Galápagos [Hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotspot_\(geology\)) is at the northern boundary of the Pacific [Large Low Shear Velocity Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_low-shear-velocity_provinces) while the Easter Hotspot is on the southern boundary.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-Geist-16]

The Galápagos Archipelago is characterized by numerous contemporaneous volcanoes, some with plume magma sources, others from the [asthenosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthenosphere), possibly due to the young and thin [oceanic crust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_crust). The GSC caused structural weaknesses in this thin [lithosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithosphere) leading to eruptions forming the Galápagos Platform. Fernandina and Isabela in particular are aligned along these weaknesses. Lacking a well-defined [rift zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rift_zone), the islands have a high rate of [inflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deformation_\(volcanology\)) prior to eruption. Sierra Negra on Isabela Island experienced a 240 cm (94 in) uplift between 1992 and 1998, most recent eruption in 2005, while Fernandina on Fernandina Island indicated an uplift of 90 cm (35 in), most recent eruption in 2009. Alcedo on Isabela Island had an uplift of greater than 90 cm, most recent eruption in 1993. While the older islands have disappeared below the sea as they moved away from the mantle plume, the youngest islands, Isabela and [Fernandina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernandina_Island), are still being formed. Additional characteristics of the Galápagos Archipelago are closer volcano spacing, smaller volcano sizes, and larger [calderas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera). For instance, Isabela Island includes six major volcanoes, Ecuador, Wolf, Darwin, Alcedo, Sierra Negra and Cerro Azul, with most recent eruptions ranging from 1813 to 2018. The neighboring islands of Santiago and Fernandina last erupted in 1906 and 2009, respectively. Overall, the nine active volcanoes in the archipelago have erupted 24 times between 1961 and 2011. The shape of these volcanoes is tall and rounded as opposed to wide and smooth in the Hawaiian Islands. The Galápagos's shape is due to the pattern of radial and circumferential [fissure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fracture_\(geology\)), radial on the flanks, but circumferential near the caldera summits. It is the circumferential fissures which give rise to stacks of short [lava flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava_flow).[^cite_note-Poland-17]

The volcanoes at the west end of the archipelago are in general, taller, younger, have well-developed calderas, and are mostly composed of [tholeiitic basalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tholeiitic_basalt), while those on the east are shorter, older, lack calderas, and have a more diverse composition. The ages of the islands, from west to east are 0.05 [Ma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year#Megaannus) for Fernandina, 0.65 Ma for Isabela, 1.10 Ma for Santiago, 1.7 Ma for Santa Cruz, 2.90 Ma for Santa Fe, and 3.2 Ma for San Cristobal. The calderas on Sierra Negra and Alcedo have active [fault systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)). The Sierra Negra fault is associated with a [sill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sill_\(geology\)) 2 km (1.2 mi) below the caldera. The caldera on Fernandina experienced the largest basaltic volcano collapse in history, with the 1968 [phreatomagmatic eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phreatomagmatic_eruption). Fernandina has also been the most active volcano since 1790, with recent eruptions in 1991, 1995, 2005, and 2009, and the entire surface has been covered in numerous flows since 4.3 [Ka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year). The western volcanoes have numerous [tuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff) [cones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_cone).[^cite_note-Poland-17][^cite_note-Merlen-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-Geist-16] In late June 2018, Sierra Negra, one of five volcanoes on Isabela and one of the most active in the Galapagos archipelago, began erupting for the first time since 2005. Lava flows made their way to the coastline, prompting the evacuation of about fifty nearby residents and restricting tourist access.[^cite_note-20]

## Physical geography



[Orthographic projection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthographic_projection) centered over the Galápagos

The Galápagos Islands have a land area of about 8,000 square kilometers (3,100 sq mi) spread over 45,000 square kilometers (17,000 sq mi) of ocean.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKricherLoughlin202242-21] The archipelago is composed of 127 islands, islets, and rocks, of which 19 are large islands with an area exceeding 1 square kilometer (0.39 sq mi).[^cite_note-auto-22][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKricherLoughlin202243–44-23] The largest island by far is [Isabela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabela_Island_\(Gal%C3%A1pagos\)). Its area of 4,588 square kilometers (1,771 sq mi) makes up close to three-fifths of the Galápagos's total area. The islands of [Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_Island_\(Gal%C3%A1pagos\)) (986 square kilometers (381 sq mi)) and [Fernandina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernandina_Island) (642 square kilometers (248 sq mi)) follow.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnellStone1996620–621-24] The highest point in the islands is [Volcán Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volc%C3%A1n_Wolf) on Isabela, reaching an altitude of 1,700 meters (5,600 ft). While broad [shield volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shield_volcanoes) are found in the western islands of Isabela and Fernandina, smaller, more significantly eroded landforms are found in the eastern islands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKricherLoughlin2022112-25]

The islands are found at the coordinates 1°40'N–1°36'S, 89°16'–92°01'W. They are located in the eastern Pacific Ocean, around 1,000 km (620 mi) to the west of mainland [Ecuador](9334.md), the country to which they belong.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKricherLoughlin202242-21] Straddling the equator, islands in the chain are located in both the Northern and Southern hemispheres, with [Volcán Wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volc%C3%A1n_Wolf) and Volcán Ecuador on [Isabela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabela_Island_\(Gal%C3%A1pagos\)) being directly on the equator. [Española Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espa%C3%B1ola_Island), the southernmost island of the archipelago, and [Darwin Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_Island), the northernmost one, are spread out over a distance of 220 km (140 mi).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESnellStone1996620–621-24] Although bisected by the equator, the archipelago is considered to be wholly within the South Pacific Ocean by the [International Hydrographic Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Hydrographic_Organization).[^cite_note-26] The surrounding [Galápagos Marine Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_Marine_Reserve) encompasses 133,000 square kilometers (51,000 sq mi) of ocean within 40 nautical miles of the archipelago.[^cite_note-auto-22] The local marine environment is shaped by the convergence of several major ocean currents, including the cool [Humboldt Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Current) from the south, the warm [Panama Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_Current?action=edit\&redlink=1) from the north, and the deep, nutrient-rich [Cromwell Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cromwell_Current) upwelling from the west.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKricherLoughlin202257-27]

## Climate



Satellite maps of the concentration of [chlorophyll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorophyll) (representing abundance of [phytoplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytoplankton)) during [El Niño](317311.md) (top) and [La Niña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ni%C3%B1a) (bottom). The color scale goes from blue at the lowest concentrations to red at the highest. Currents that normally [fertilize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilisation) phytoplankton reverse during El Niño, resulting in [barren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_production) oceans. The same currents are strengthened by La Niña, resulting in an explosion of ocean life.



A satellite map of chlorophyll and phytoplankton concentration (top) paired with a map of oceanic surface temperatures at the same time (bottom). The thriving populations represented by green and yellow in the upper map correlate to areas of higher surface temperatures represented by yellow in the lower map (2 March 2009).

Although the islands are located on the equator, the [Humboldt Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humboldt_Current) brings cold water to them, causing frequent drizzles during most of the year. The weather is regularly influenced by the [El Niño](317311.md) events, which occur every 3 to 7 years and bring warmer [sea surface temperatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_surface_temperature), a rise in sea level, greater wave action, and a depletion of nutrients in the water.[^cite_note-44] This cycle can greatly affect the precipitation from one year to another. At Charles Darwin Station, the precipitation during the month of March in the particularly wet year of 1969 was 249.0 mm (9.80 in), but during March 1970 the next year it was only 1.2 mm (0.047 in).

There is also a large range in precipitation from one place to another and across the islands' two main seasons. The archipelago is mainly characterized by a mixture of a [tropical savanna climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) and a [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate), transitioning to a [tropical rainforest climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate) in the northwest. During the rainy season known as the *garúa* from June to November, the temperature near the sea is around 22 °C (72 °F), a steady cool wind blows from south and southeast, frequent drizzles (*garúas*) for days, and dense fog conceals the islands. During the warm season from December to May, the average sea and air temperatures rise to around 25 °C (77 °F), there is no wind at all, and the sun shines apart from sporadic strong downpours. Weather also changes as altitude increases on the larger islands. Temperature decreases gradually with altitude, while precipitation increases due to the condensation of moisture from clouds on the slopes. This pattern of generally wet highlands and drier lowlands affects the plant life on the larger islands. The vegetation in the highlands tends to be green and lush, with tropical woodland in places. The lowland areas tend to have arid and semi-arid vegetation, with many thorny shrubs and cacti and areas of barren volcanic rock.

Some islands also fall within the [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) of others during some seasons. During March 1969, the precipitation over Charles Darwin Station on the southern coast of Santa Cruz was 249.0 mm (9.80 in), while on nearby Baltra Island the precipitation during the same month was only 137.6 mm (5.42 in). This is because Baltra is located behind Santa Cruz when the prevailing winds are southerly, causing more moisture to fall on the Santa Cruz highlands.

## Ecology



[Pinnacle Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinnacle_Rock_\(Gal%C3%A1pagos\)) on [Bartolomé Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartolom%C3%A9_Island), with [Santiago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Island_\(Gal%C3%A1pagos\)) in the background and a ferry on the right for scale

### Terrestrial

Main article: [Galápagos Islands xeric scrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_Islands_xeric_scrub)

Most of the Galápagos is covered in semi-desert vegetation, including shrublands, grasslands, and dry forest. A few of the islands have high-elevation areas with cooler temperatures and higher rainfall, which are home to humid-climate forests and shrublands, and montane grasslands (*pampas*) at the highest elevations. There are about 500 species of native [vascular plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_plant) on the islands, including 90 species of ferns. About 180 vascular plant species are endemic.[^cite_note-wwf-46]

The islands are well known for their distinctive [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) species, including [giant tortoises](7934681.md), [finches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin's_finches), [flightless cormorants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flightless_cormorant), [Galápagos lava lizards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microlophus_albemarlensis) and [marine iguanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_iguana), which evolved to adapt to islands' environments.[^cite_note-wwf-46]

## History

### Pre-Columbian era

Whether [Polynesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesians) or the [indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_South_America) of [South America](26769.md) ever made it to the islands is disputed. [Oceanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island#Oceanic_islands) Pacific islands in the same general area as Galápagos—including [Clipperton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clipperton_Island), [Cocos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_Island), the [Desventuradas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desventuradas_Islands), the [Juan Fernández Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Fern%C3%A1ndez_Islands), and the [Revillagigedos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revillagigedo_Islands)—were all uninhabited when discovered by Europeans, with no evidence to indicate any prehistoric human activity.[^cite_note-asianpacific-47][^cite_note-press-48][^cite_note-49] The easternmost oceanic island in the South Pacific that was discovered with a human population was [Easter Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Island),[^cite_note-press-48] whose [Rapa Nui people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapa_Nui_people) are known to be [Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_people) rather than South American.[^cite_note-rapa-50]

In 1572, the Spanish chronicler [Pedro Sarmiento de Gamboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Sarmiento_de_Gamboa) claimed that [Topa Inca Yupanqui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topa_Inca_Yupanqui), the second [Sapa Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapa_Inca) of the [Inca Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca_Empire), had visited the archipelago. There is, however, little evidence for this and many experts consider it a far-fetched legend, especially since the Incas were not typically a seafaring people.[^cite_note-51] A 1952 archaeological survey by [Thor Heyerdahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor_Heyerdahl) and Arne Skjølsvold found [potsherds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsherd) and other artifacts from several sites on the islands that they claimed suggested visitation by South Americans during the [pre-Columbian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHeyerdahl_&_al.1956-52] The group located an Inca flute and shards from more than 130 pieces of ceramics, later identified as pre-Incan. However, no remains of graves, ceremonial vessels, or buildings have ever been found, suggesting no permanent settlement occurred before the Spanish arrived in the 16th century.[^cite_note-53] A 2016 reanalysis of Heyerdahl and Skjølsvold's archaeological sites rejected their conclusions. They found that—at all locations excavated by the 1952 survey—artifacts of Indian and European origin were interspersed without the distinct spatial or stratigraphic arrangement that would be expected from independent sequential deposition. (Heyerdahl and Skjølsvold had noted this in their original report while ignoring its implications.) Radiocarbon dates from the sites placed them in the post-Spanish era and preliminary paleoenvironmental analysis showed no disturbance older than 500 years [before present](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Before_present), suggesting no evidence from the survey that the islands were visited prior to their Spanish discovery in 1535. The authors suggested that native artifacts found by Heyerdahl and Skjølsvold had probably been brought as mementos or souvenirs at the time of Spanish occupation.[^cite_note-54]

A 2008 report by archeologists from the [Australian National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_National_University) stated that certain Asia–Pacific [taxa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxa) may have been growing in the Galápagos prior to 1535. This opens a direction for future research which might "constitute a strong line of evidence for accidental or deliberate landfall in the Galápagos by a Polynesian vessel",[^cite_note-press-48] although the report noted current scholarship finds no evidence that Pacific islands beyond Easter Island "play\[ed] a 'stepping stone' role in the interaction between Amerindians or Polynesians in prehistory".[^cite_note-press-48] The lack of [fresh water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_water) on the islands seems to have limited visits and settlement, if any ever occurred.

### European discovery



Detail of the Galápagos on [Abraham Ortelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Ortelius)'s 1570 map of the Americas

European discovery of the Galápagos Islands is recorded occurring on 10 March 1535, when the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_people) [bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_bishop_of_Panam%C3%A1) of [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Panam%C3%A1) [Tomás de Berlanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1s_de_Berlanga)—sailing to [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Peru) to adjudicate a dispute between [Francisco Pizarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Pizarro) and [Diego de Almagro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_de_Almagro)—drifted off course while his ship was becalmed in the [Doldrums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doldrums). They found the islands they visited uninhabited and so arid and barren that two men and ten horses died for lack of fresh water and the rest were forced to subsist on [cactus pads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cactus_pad).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJackson1993\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidTlxzU6i_LV0CpgPA1_1\]-55] The ship left them unclaimed and unsettled.[^cite_note-56] Berlanga, however, wrote a brief account of the islands, their condition, and their main wildlife for [Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Charles_V).

The Galápagos Islands first appeared on the maps of [Gerardus Mercator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerardus_Mercator) and [Abraham Ortelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Ortelius) around 1570.[^cite_note-57]

### Pirate era



The chart of the Galápagos published by the [English](18803164.md) pirate [William Ambrosia Cowley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ambrosia_Cowley) in 1684, the source of many of the islands' English names



A manuscript map of the islands from the charts drafted by [James Colnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Colnett) of the British [Royal Navy](26061.md) in 1793, adding additional names

The first English captain to visit the Galápagos Islands was [Richard Hawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Hawkins) in 1593. Until the early 19th century, the archipelago was often used as a hideout by (mostly English) pirates who attacked [Spanish treasure fleets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_treasure_fleet) carrying gold, silver, and supplies from [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Peru) to [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Panama) and [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Spain_\(1700%E2%80%931808\)).[^cite_note-58] The [English](18803164.md) pirate [William Ambrosia Cowley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Ambrosia_Cowley) thoroughly mapped the islands in 1684 while sailing on [John Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cook_\(pirate\))'s *Batchelor's* or *Bachelor's Delight* and [John Eaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Eaton_\(pirate\))'s *Nicholas* as they raided Peruvian shipping.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcEwen1988234–236-59] One cargo captured that year was 7–8 [tuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tun_\(unit\)) of [quince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quince) [marmalade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmalade), whose remains scattered pottery around the islands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJackson1993\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidTlxzU6i_LV0CpgPA2_2\]-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBenz2000\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidYPBccPXLsJ4CpgPA32_32\]-62] Publishing the first thorough chart of the islands, Cowley coined the English names for 16 of the islands, chiefly honoring English royalty, nobles, and Jamaican officials of the era who might provide future patronage.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcEwen1988234–236-59]

In 1793, during the early [French Revolutionary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars), the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Navy) [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship's_captain) [James Colnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Colnett) described the flora and fauna of Galápagos and suggested the islands could be used as a base for [whalers](33557.md) operating in the Pacific. Colnett improved upon Cowley's chart, coining additional names[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcEwen1988234–236-59] although accidentally transferring Cowley's Charles Island from Española to Floreana.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcEwen1988237-63] Whalers and [maritime fur traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_fur_trade) killed and captured thousands of the Galápagos tortoises to extract their fat. The tortoises could be kept on board ship as a means of providing fresh protein, as these animals could survive for several months on board without any food or water. The hunting of the tortoises was responsible for greatly diminishing, and in some cases eliminating, certain species. Along with whalers came the fur-seal hunters, who brought the population of this animal close to extinction.

The 1841 [Admiralty chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_chart) drafted from [FitzRoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_FitzRoy)'s survey of the islands [on HMS *Beagle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_voyage_of_HMS_Beagle)

The first known permanent human resident on Galápagos was [Patrick Watkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Watkins_\(sailor\)), an [Irish](775859.md) sailor who was [marooned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marooning) on the Floreana from 1807 to 1809. According to later accounts, Watkins managed to survive by hunting, growing vegetables and trading with visiting whalers[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPorter1822-64][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMelville1854-65] before stealing a [longboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longboat) from a [whaling](33557.md) ship, [impressing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressment) five of its crew as his "slaves", and navigating to [Guayaquil](215586.md) on the Ecuadorian mainland.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJackson1993\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidTlxzU6i_LV0CpgPA3_3\]-66] Watkins was the only one of the six to survive the journey.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJackson1993\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidTlxzU6i_LV0CpgPA3_3\]-66]

In 1818, the Nantucket whaleship *Globe* under Captain George Washington Gardner discovered a "mother lode" of sperm whales some thousand miles west of the South American coast approximately at the equator. He returned to Nantucket in 1820 with more than 2,000 barrels of sperm whale oil and the news of his discovery. This led to an influx of whaleships to exploit the new whaling ground and the Galápagos Islands became a frequent stop for the whalers both before and after visiting what came to be known as the Offshore Grounds. This led to the establishment in the Galápagos Islands of a kind of unofficial "post office" where whaleships stopped to pick up and drop off letters as well as for provisioning and repairing.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPerry197244-67]

In October 1820, the whaleship *Essex* out of Nantucket stopped at the Galápagos for these purposes on its way to the Offshore Grounds. On Colnett's Charles Island, while most of the crew were hunting tortoises one crewmember, English boatsteerer Thomas Chappel—for reasons still unclear—lit a fire which quickly burned out of control. Some of the tortoise hunters had a narrow escape and had to run a gauntlet of fire to get back to the ship. Soon almost the entire island was in flames. Crewmembers reported that after a day of sailing away they could still see the flames against the horizon. One crewmember who returned to the Galápagos several years afterward described the entire island as still a blackened wasteland.[^cite_note-68]

[Ecuador](9334.md) [won its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian_War_of_Independence) from [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Spain_\(1808%E2%80%931874\)) in 1822 and [seceded from](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_Gran_Colombia) [Gran Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Colombia) in 1830. [Gen. José de Villamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_Villamil), the founder of the [Ecuadorian Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian_Navy), led the push to colonize and settle the islands before Ecuador's neighbors or the [European empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Imperialism) could occupy them.[^cite_note-galhis-5] He formed the Galapagos Settlement Company in mid-1831[^cite_note-monty-69] and, with [President](840787.md) [Juan José Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Jos%C3%A9_Flores)'s support, sent Col. Ignacio Hernández with a dozen craftsmen to begin the settlement on [Charles Island](3010747.md)—renamed "Floriana" in the president's honour—early the next year.[^cite_note-granny-70] Hernández conducted a formal ceremony of annexation on Floreana on 12 February 1832,[^cite_note-galhis-5] now celebrated locally as [Galápagos Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_Day) or the Day of the Province. (Darwin's birthday was the same day,[^cite_note-granny2-71] as was [Francisco de Orellana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_de_Orellana)'s arrival at the headwaters of the [Amazon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River) in 1542, celebrated on the mainland as [Amazon Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Day).) Villamil arrived in September and established Haven of Peace (*Asilo de Paz* or *de la Paz*) in the island's highlands.[^cite_note-granny-70] The initial colonists who joined him were Ecuadorian soldiers—chiefly political prisoners[^cite_note-granny-70]—whose [death sentences](5902.md) were [commuted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutation_\(law\)) in exchange for their agreement to [permanently settle](9855.md) the islands with their families.[^cite_note-monty-69] They were joined in October by additional artisans and farmers, bringing the population to about 120, at which point Villamil was formally made the area's first governor.[^cite_note-monty-69] Villamil's lieutenant governor was the [Norwegian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_people)-born[^cite_note-monty-69] [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_people) and [Chilean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_people) sailor and shipwright [Nicholas Oliver Lawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Oliver_Lawson).[^cite_note-granny-70] Haven of Peace was originally successful and peaceful but, after being named a [penal colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penal_colony) in March 1833, violence and costs began to rise.[^cite_note-monty2-72] Villamil was faced with bankruptcy by 1837 and resigned his post, founding a colony of 21 on [Indefatigable Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indefatigable_Island)—renamed Bolivia—and leaving Lawson as its mayor.[^cite_note-granny2-71] Gen. Villamil's successors—incompetent, strict, or both—prompted a bloody uprising in 1841 that caused most settlers to return to the mainland.[^cite_note-monty2-72] Villamil returned to try to rebuild afterwards but was unsuccessful and abandoned the attempt in 1848.[^cite_note-monty2-72]



[Gould](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gould)'s illustration of [Darwin's finches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin's_finches)

The [second voyage of HMS *Beagle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_voyage_of_HMS_Beagle) under [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship's_captain) [Robert FitzRoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_FitzRoy) was undertaken to better survey the coasts and harbours of South America for the [British Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Navy)'s [Hydrographic Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Hydrographic_Office). It reached the Galápagos on 15 September 1835 and—while surveying its islands, channels, and bays—the captain and others on the crew observed the geology, plants, and wildlife on Floreana, Isabela, and Santiago before continuing on their [round-the-world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumnavigation) expedition on 20 October. The young naturalist [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin), primarily a geologist at the time, was struck by the many volcanic features they saw, later referring to the archipelago as "that land of craters". His study of several volcanic formations over the five weeks he stayed in the islands led to several important geological discoveries, including the first correct explanation for how volcanic [tuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuff) is formed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrant_&_al.2009-73] Darwin noticed the [mockingbirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mockingbird) differed between islands, though he thought the birds now known as [Darwin's finches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin's_finches) were unrelated to each other and did not bother labelling them by island.[^cite_note-Confessions-74] Acting as governor of the islands while Villamil was on the mainland, Nicholas Lawson met Darwin and the British crew, mentioning in passing that the tortoises of the different islands could be easily identified by their different shells.[^cite_note-monty-69][^cite_note-75] By the end of his voyage, Darwin was beginning to wonder if the distribution of the mockingbirds and the tortoises might "undermine the stability of [Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species)".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeynes2000\[httpdarwin-onlineorgukcontentframesetviewtypetextitemIDF1840pageseq328_291–293\]-76] Upon his return to England, analysis of the bird specimens he had collected showed that what had appeared to be many different species were actually [finches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finch) displaying developments unique to the islands. The voyage became crucial in Darwin's development of his theory of [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) of species by [natural selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_selection), presented in the 1859 *[On the Origin of Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Origin_of_Species)*.[^cite_note-Confessions-74]

The Englishman William Gurney became mayor of a new settlement on [Chatham Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_Island) in 1844.[^cite_note-granny2-71]

In April 1888 [USS *Albatross*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Albatross_\(1882\)), a Navy-crewed research vessel assigned to the [United States Fish Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fish_Commission), briefly touched eight islands in the [Galapagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galapagos) group for specimens;[^cite_note-77] this included Wreck Bay on Chatham Island (now [San Cristóbal Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_Island)) on 4 April and Charles Island (now [Floreana Island](3010747.md)) on 8 April.

[José Valdizán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Valdiz%C3%A1n?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Manuel Julián Cobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Juli%C3%A1n_Cobos?action=edit\&redlink=1) tried a new colonization, beginning the exploitation of a type of lichen found in the islands (*Roccella portentosa*) used as a coloring agent. After the assassination of Valdizán by some of his workers, Cobos brought from the continent to San Cristóbal Island a group of more than a hundred workers, and tried his luck at planting sugar cane. He ruled his plantation with an iron hand, which led to his assassination in 1904. In 1897, Antonio Gil began another plantation on Isabela Island.

Over the course of a whole year, from September 1904, an expedition of the Academy of Sciences of California, led by [Rollo Beck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rollo_Beck), stayed in the Galápagos collecting scientific material on geology, [entomology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entomology), [ornithology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithology), [botany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany), [zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology), and [herpetology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herpetology). Another expedition from that Academy was done in 1932 (Templeton Crocker Expedition) to collect insects, fish, [shells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exoskeleton), [fossils](10958.md), birds, and plants.

### 20th century

[Admiralty chart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiralty_chart) of the Galápagos (1953)

For a long time during the early 1900s and at least through 1929, a cash-strapped Ecuador had reached out for potential buyers of the islands to alleviate financial troubles at home. The US had repeatedly expressed its interest in buying the islands for military use as they were positioned strategically guarding the [Panama Canal](24849.md).[^cite_note-79] Besides the United States, [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan), [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) and Chile also expressed interest in establishing bases in the islands at the turn of the century.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] Chile had previously acquired the [Straits of Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Magellan)[^cite_note-82] and [Easter Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Island) for strategic reasons and lieutenant Gregorio Santa Cruz argued in 1903 that possessing an island in equatorial waters, like the Galápagos, would be of great benefit since the [geopolitical](163225.md) situation of Chile was expected to drastically change when the Panama Canal opened. Another benefit would be to widen the security radius of Chile.[^cite_note-83] Chile was alarmed by the United States plans to establish a [Guantanamo-like](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_Naval_Base) base in the Galápagos Islands since it would mean that Chile's [nitrate-rich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitratine) [northern provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte_Grande) would be within the range of [United States Navy](20518076.md).[^cite_note-Fischer2008-84] Ecuador's staunch resistance to a US purchase or bases in the islands can be credited to Chilean diplomacy, which in turn was informally backed on this issue by [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) and Germany.[^cite_note-Fischer2008-84]

In the 1920s and 1930s, a small wave of European settlers arrived in the islands. There occurred a series of unsolved disappearances on the island of Floreana in the 1930s among the largely European expatriate residents at the time, which prompted a book by [John Treherne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Treherne)[^cite_note-85] and the movies *[The Empress of Floreana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Empress_of_Floreana)*, [*The Galápagos Affair: Satan Came to Eden*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Galapagos_Affair), and *[Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_\(2024_film\))*. Ecuadorian laws provided all colonists with the possibility of receiving twenty hectares each of free land, the right to maintain their citizenship, freedom from taxation for the first ten years in Galápagos, and the right to hunt and fish freely on all uninhabited islands where they might settle.[^cite_note-86] The first European colonists to arrive were Norwegians who settled briefly on Floreana, before moving on to San Cristobal and Santa Cruz. A few years later, other colonists from Europe, America and Ecuador started arriving on the islands, seeking a simpler life.[^cite_note-87] Descendants of the Norwegian Kastdalen family and the German Angermeyer still live on the islands.

During World War II, Ecuador authorized the United States to establish a naval base in Baltra Island, and radar stations in other strategic locations. Baltra was established as a United States Army Air Force base. Baltra was given the name of "Beta Base" along with "Alpha Base" in Nicaragua and "Gamma Base" in Salinas (continental Ecuador). The Crews stationed at Baltra and the aforementioned locations established a geographic triangle of protection in charge of patrolling the Pacific for enemy submarines, and also provided protection for the [Panama Canal](24849.md). After the war, the facilities were given to the government of Ecuador. Today, the island continues as an official Ecuadorian military base. The foundations and other remains of the US base can still be seen as one crosses the island. In 1946, a penal colony was established on Isabela Island, but it was suspended in 1959.

[Galápagos National Park](7486805.md) was established in 1959,[^cite_note-Galapagos.com-88] with tourism starting to expand in the 1960s, imposing several restrictions upon the human population already living on the island. However, opportunities in the tourism, fishing, and farming industries attracted a mass of poor fishermen and farmers from mainland Ecuador. In the 1990s and 2000s, violent confrontations between parts of the local population and the Galápagos National Park Service occurred, including capturing and killing giant tortoises and holding staff of the Galápagos National Park Service hostage to obtain higher annual [sea cucumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_cucumber) quotas.[^cite_note-89]

### 21st century

In May 2023, Credit Suisse said it would buy Ecuador's debt of \$1.6 billion in a "[Debt-for-nature swap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt-for-nature_swap)". It will sell 2035 and 2040 bonds for Galapagos conservation at a reduced issue price. The [U.S. International Development Finance Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._International_Development_Finance_Corporation) insures the deal,[^cite_note-dw-90] which per Reuters was "in the works for more than a year", predating UBS takeover of Credit Suisse.[^cite_note-91]

#### US Military presence

Under the [Lenín Moreno](8187373.md) government, Ecuador signed a cooperation agreement with the United States, permitting military deployments on the island of San Cristóbal. In 2025, the island's airfield was expanded to accommodate a [Lockheed P-3 Orion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_P-3_Orion), a [Boeing E-3 Sentry](10384.md) and a [De Havilland Canada Dash 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Canada_Dash_8). US military personnel are exempt from the Galapagos islands' conservation and non-contamination policies.[^cite_note-92]

## Politics



The flag of Galápagos Province

Main article: [Galápagos Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_Province)

The islands are administered as Ecuador's [Galápagos Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_Province), established by presidential decree on 18 February 1973 during the administration of [Guillermo Rodríguez Lara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Rodr%C3%ADguez_\(politician\)). The province is divided into three [cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantons_of_Ecuador), each covering groups of islands. The capital is [Puerto Baquerizo Moreno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Baquerizo_Moreno).

## Demographics

&#x20;The Galápagos Islands have experienced significant demographic changes over the past century, marked by substantial population growth and evolving community dynamics.

### Population growth over time

- 1941: Approximately 810 inhabitants.

- 1960s: Population increased to around 3,000.

- 1972: Census recorded 3,488 residents.

- 1980s: Population exceeded 15,000.

- 2010: 25,124 inhabitants.

- 2022: Census reported 28,583 residents.

This rapid growth has been influenced by factors such as increased tourism and migration from mainland [Ecuador](9334.md). The permanent resident population has been growing at approximately 6.4% annually, compared to 2.1% on the mainland.[^cite_note-96]

### Ethnic composition

The majority of Galápagos residents are Ecuadorian Mestizos, descendants of Spanish colonists and indigenous Native Americans who migrated primarily from mainland Ecuador.[^cite_note-97] Additionally, there are descendants of early European and American settlers.[^cite_note-98]

### Urbanization

Approximately 85% of the population resides in designated human-use areas, which constitute about 3.3% of the archipelago's land mass. The remaining area is protected as part of the national park.[^cite_note-99]

#### Tourism impact

The Galápagos Islands attract a substantial number of tourists annually, significantly influencing the local economy and contributing to demographic changes. In 2022, the islands recorded 268,000 visitors, reflecting a 96% increase compared to 2021.[^cite_note-100] Prior to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), tourism accounted for nearly 80% of the Galápagos economy.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] This heavy reliance on tourism has led to economic benefits, including employment opportunities for over two thousand residents. However, the increasing number of tourists has also raised concerns about environmental sustainability and the preservation of the islands' unique ecosystems.[^cite_note-103]

## Travel



Water taxi in [Puerto Ayora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Ayora) (2011)



[Seymour Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seymour_Airport) on [Baltra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltra_Island) (2013)

Options for air travel to the Galápagos are limited to two islands: San Cristobal ([San Cristóbal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal_Airport)) and Baltra ([Seymour Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seymour_Airport)).[^cite_note-104] Private aircraft must use Baltra as it is the airport equipped with overnight plane accommodations. Seymour Airport on Baltra was renovated in 2012–2013 to accommodate larger planes.

Until 1969 the only way to visit was on a private or chartered vessel. There was no regular air service until Forrest Nelson's Hotel Galápagos began the first organized tours in April 1969. Soon other travel companies brought in tour ships and yachts, and local fishermen began converting their wooden boats for rudimentary cruising with guests. These vessels were the main source of overnight accommodations in the Galápagos.[^cite_note-105] Today there are about 85 yachts and ships equipped for overnight guests. In 2006 the Baltra military governed island, was opened up to limited overnight camping. Baltra also requires permits by the military government for overnight stays on the beach. Other inhabited islands also allow camping on the beaches designated as "recreational" use to the locals. All of these camping permits are limited to number of people and nights, with most nights not to exceed three.

Land based hotels are opening on the inhabited islands of San Cristobal, Santa Cruz, Floreana and Isabela. By 2012, more than half the visitors to Galápagos made their tours using day boats and these small hotels. Restaurants, easy access and economy make this an attractive travel option.

There are only 116 visitor sites in the Galápagos: 54 land sites and 62 scuba-diving or snorkeling sites. Small groups are allowed to visit in 2- to 4-hour shifts only, to limit impact on the area. All groups are accompanied by licensed guides.

Though the first protective legislation for the Galápagos was enacted in 1930 and supplemented in 1936, it was not until the late 1950s that positive action was taken to control what was happening to the native flora and fauna. In 1955, the International Union for the Conservation of Nature organized a fact-finding mission to the Galápagos. Two years later, in 1957, [UNESCO](21786641.md), in cooperation with the government of Ecuador, sent another expedition to study the conservation situation and choose a site for a research station.

In 1959, the centenary year of [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin)'s publication of *[The Origin of Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Origin_of_Species)*, the Ecuadorian government declared 97.5% of the archipelago's land area a [national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_park), excepting areas already colonised. The [Charles Darwin Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin_Foundation) (CDF) was founded the same year. The core responsibility of CDF, an international nongovernmental organization (NGO) constituted in Belgium, is to conduct research and provide the research findings to the government for effective management of Galápagos. CDF's research efforts began with the establishment of the [Charles Darwin Research Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin_Research_Station) on Santa Cruz Island in 1964. During the early years, conservation programs, such as eradication of introduced species and protection of native species, were carried out by research station personnel. Now much of that work is accomplished by the [Galápagos National Park](7486805.md) Service using the research findings and methodologies developed by CDF.

In 1986, the 70,000 square kilometers (27,000 sq mi) of ocean surrounding the islands was declared a [marine reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_reserve), second in size only to Australia's Great Barrier Reef. In 1990, the archipelago became a whale sanctuary. [UNESCO](21786641.md) recognized the islands in 1978 as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrant2009-106] and in 1985, as a [biosphere reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere_reserve). This was later extended in December 2001 to include the marine reserve. In July 2010, the World Heritage Committee agreed to remove the Galápagos Islands from its list of precious sites endangered by environmental threats or overuse.[^cite_note-107]

[Conservation International Ecuador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_International_Ecuador) has served as the project agency for a Global Environment Facility project in the Galápagos designed to strengthen archipelago biosecurity and support ecosystem restoration. Actions have included invasive-vertebrate eradication on [Floreana Island](3010747.md) and translocation of [giant tortoises](7934681.md) to [Santa Fe Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Island).[^cite_note-GEF9282GalapagosProdoc-108]

- [Flightless cormorant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flightless_cormorant), *Phalacrocorax harrisi*

- [Great frigatebird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_frigatebird) and [magnificent frigatebird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnificent_frigatebird)

- [Blue-footed booby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-footed_booby), *Sula nebouxii*, popular among visitors for their large blue feet which they show off in courtship

- [Galápagos penguin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_penguin), *Spheniscus mendiculus*, the only living tropical penguin

- [Waved albatross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waved_albatross), *Phoebastria irrorata*, the only living tropical [albatross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albatross)

- [Galápagos hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_hawk), *Buteo galapagoensis*, the islands' main [scavenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scavenger) (at the [top of the food chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apex_predator)) and "environmental police"

- Four [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemic_\(ecology\)) species of [Galápagos mockingbirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nesomimus), the first species Darwin noticed to vary from island to island

- Thirteen endemic species of [tanagers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanager), popularly called [Darwin's finches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin's_finches). Among them is the [sharp-beaked ground finch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp-beaked_ground_finch) *Geospiza difficilis septentrionalis* which is sometimes called the "vampire finch" for its blood-sucking habits, and the tool-using [woodpecker finch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodpecker_finch), *Camarhynchus pallidus*

- [Galápagos sea lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_sea_lion), *Zalophus wollebaeki*, closely related to the California sea lion, but smaller

- Two endemic genera of cacti, each with a single species: *[Jasminocereus thouarsii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasminocereus)*, the candelabra cactus, and *[Brachycereus nesioticus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava_cactus)*, the lava cactus

### Environmental threats

Introduced plants and animals, such as feral goats, cats, and cattle, brought accidentally or willingly to the islands by humans, represent the main threat to Galápagos.[^cite_note-109] Quick to reproduce and with no natural predators, these alien species decimated the habitats of native species. The native animals, lacking natural predators on the islands, are defenseless to introduced predators.

There are over 700 introduced plant species today. There are only 500 native and endemic species. This difference is creating a major problem for the islands and the natural species that inhabit them. These plants have invaded large areas and eliminated endemic species in the humid zones of San Cristobal, Floreana, Isabela and Santa Cruz. Some of the most harmful introduced plants are the guayaba or [guava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guava) (*Psidium guajava*), [avocado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avocado) (*Persea americana*), [cascarilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascarilla) (*Cinchona pubescens*), [balsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balsa) (*Ochroma pyramidale*), [hill raspberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_raspberry) (*Rubus niveus*), various [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus) (orange, [grapefruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grapefruit), lemon), [floripondio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floripondio), higuerilla (*[Ricinus communis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricinus_communis)*) trees and the elephant grass, *[Pennisetum purpureum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennisetum_purpureum)*.

Many species were introduced to the Galápagos by [pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirate). [Thor Heyerdahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor_Heyerdahl) quoted documents that mention the [Viceroy of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy_of_Peru), knowing that British pirates ate the goats that they themselves had released in the islands, ordered dogs to be freed there to eliminate the goats.[^cite_note-Heyerdahl-110] Also, when colonization of Floreana by José de Villamil failed, he ordered the goats, donkeys, cattle and other animals from the farms in Floreana be transferred to other islands for the purpose of later colonization.

The [black rat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_rat) (*Rattus rattus*) attacks small Galápagos tortoises when they leave the nest, so in Pinzón they stopped the reproduction for a period of more than 50 years; only adults were found on that island.[^cite_note-111] Also, where the black rat is found, the endemic rat has disappeared. Cattle and donkeys eat all the available vegetation and compete with native species for scarce water. In 1959, fishermen introduced one male and two female goats to Pinta island; by 1973, the National Park service estimated the population of goats to be over 30,000 individuals. Goats were also introduced to Marchena in 1967 and to Rabida in 1971. A goat eradication program, however, cleared the goats from Pinta and Santiago and most of the goat population from Isabela,[^cite_note-112] and, by 2006, all [feral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feral_animal) pigs, donkeys and [non-sterile goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judas_goat#Goats_tracking_feral_goats) had been eliminated from Santiago and Isabela, the largest islands with the worst problems due to non-native mammals.[^cite_note-Isabela1-113][^cite_note-Isabela2-114]

Six species of small non-native vertebrates have established self-sufficient populations in Galápagos and may become invasive: Fowler's snouted tree frog *[Scinax quinquefasciatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scinax_quinquefasciatus)*, common house gecko *[Hemidactylus frenatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_house_gecko)*, mourning gecko *[Lepidodactylus lugubris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lepidodactylus_lugubris)*, dwarf gecko *[Gonatodes caudiscutatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonatodes_caudiscutatus)*, Peters' leaf-toed gecko *[Phyllodactylus reissii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllodactylus_reissii)*, and smooth-billed ani *[Crotophaga ani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth-billed_ani)*. Domestic fowl *[Gallus gallus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken)* holds feral populations, which may have self-sufficient populations, but evidence is unclear.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]

The fast-growing poultry industry on the inhabited islands has been cause for concern from local conservationists, who fear domestic birds could introduce disease into the endemic wild bird populations.

The Galápagos marine sanctuary is under threat from a host of [illegal fishing](6346579.md) activities, in addition to other problems of development.[^cite_note-Carr2013-117] The most pressing threat to the Marine Reserve comes from local, mainland and foreign fishing targeting marine life illegally within the Reserve,[^cite_note-118] such as sharks (hammerheads and other species) for their fins,[^cite_note-Carr2013-117] and the harvest of sea cucumbers out of season. Development threatens both land and sea species. The growth of both the tourism industry and local populations fuelled by high birth rates and illegal immigration threaten the wildlife of the Archipelago. The grounding of the oil tanker *[Jessica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_\(tanker\))* in 2001 and the subsequent oil spill brought this threat to world attention.

In 2007, [UNESCO](21786641.md) put the Galápagos Islands on their [List of World Heritage in Danger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_in_Danger) because of threats posed by invasive species, unbridled tourism and overfishing.[^cite_note-119] On 29 July 2010, the [World Heritage Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Committee) decided to remove the Galápagos Islands from the list because the Committee found significant progress had been made by Ecuador in addressing these problems.[^cite_note-120]

On 28 January 2008, [Galápagos National Park](7486805.md) official Victor Carrion announced 53 sea lions (13 [pups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinniped), 25 youngsters, 9 males and 6 females) were killed at the Galápagos Islands nature reserve on [Pinta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinta_Island), with their heads caved in. In 2001, [poachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaching) killed 35 male sea lions.[^cite_note-121]

The Galápagos Islands were short-listed as a candidate to be one of the [New7Wonders of Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New7Wonders_of_Nature) by the New7Wonders of Nature Foundation. As of February 2009, the archipelago was ranked first in Group B, the category for islands.[^cite_note-122]

The islands' biodiversity is under threat from several sources. The human population is growing at a rate of 8% per year (1995). Introduced species have caused damage, and in 1996 a US\$5 million, five-year eradication plan commenced in an attempt to rid the islands of introduced species such as goats, rats, deer, and donkeys. Except for the rats, the project was essentially completed in 2006.[^cite_note-Isabela1-113][^cite_note-Isabela2-114] Rats have only been eliminated from the smaller Galápagos Islands of [Rábida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A1bida_Island) and [Pinzón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinz%C3%B3n_Island).[^cite_note-Isabela3-123]

[El Niño](317311.md) has adversely affected the marine ecosystem. In January 2001, an oil slick from a stranded tanker threatened the islands, but winds and shifting ocean currents helped disperse the oil before much damage was done. The devastating El Niño of 1982–83 saw almost six times as much rain as normal in the Galapagos and created a wildlife catastrophe.[^cite_note-Galpagos-Islands-124] The 1997–98 El Niño adversely affected wildlife in the waters surrounding the islands, as the waters were 5 °C (9 °F) warmer than normal. Corals and barnacles suffered, hammerhead sharks were driven away, and most of the island's seabirds failed to breed in 1997–98. The mortality rate of marine iguanas rose as the green algae they feed on was replaced by inedible red algae. During the 1982–83 El Niño, 70% of the marine iguanas starved to death because of this.[^cite_note-Galápagos_Islands_2010-125]
