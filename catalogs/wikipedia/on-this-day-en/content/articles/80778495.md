The **FP-5 "Flamingo"** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Фламі́нго, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Flaminho* [\[flɐˈmʲinɦɔ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/8d/LL-Q8798\_%28ukr%29-Tohaomg-%D1%84%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BC%D1%96%D0%BD%D0%B3%D0%BE.wav/LL-Q8798\_%28ukr%29-Tohaomg-%D1%84%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BC%D1%96%D0%BD%D0%B3%D0%BE.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/8d/LL-Q8798_%28ukr%29-Tohaomg-%D1%84%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BC%D1%96%D0%BD%D0%B3%D0%BE.wav/LL-Q8798_%28ukr%29-Tohaomg-%D1%84%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%BC%D1%96%D0%BD%D0%B3%D0%BE.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a Ukrainian ground-launched [cruise missile](6590.md) developed by defence firm [Fire Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_Point_\(Ukrainian_firm\)). Revealed on 18 August 2025, the missile is fitted with a 1,150 kg (2,540 lb) [warhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhead) and has a range of 3,000 km (1,900 miles). The missile was in serial production in August 2025, targeting 210 units a month by the end of the year.[^cite_note-Ukrainska-Pravda-4][^cite_note-tg-6][^cite_note-ap-5][^cite_note-kyiv_ind-7]

## Development

Fire Point, a Ukrainian defence startup founded by a group of friends from non-military backgrounds—including construction, game design, and architecture—began development of the FP-5 "Flamingo" following the 2022 [Russian full-scale invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-ap-5] The missile was first publicly revealed on 17 August 2025 by [Associated Press](18935732.md) photojournalist Efrem Lukatsky during a test launch at a hidden facility in southern Ukraine.[^cite_note-twz-8] In his address on 20 August 2025, [President of Ukraine](20688479.md) [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) announced that serial production had begun and described it as "the most successful missile we have".[^cite_note-kyiv_ind-7][^cite_note-ap-5][^cite_note-politico-2025-9] The Associated Press reported that the nickname "Flamingo" originated when early production units of the missile had a pink colour due to an error at the factory,[^cite_note-ap-5] but this was denied by Fire Point's CTO.[^cite_note-thedefender-10] *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* and *[Politico](9172097.md)* on the other hand reported that the name was chosen to highlight the role of women in senior positions at the company, with test prototypes being painted pink to support the name.[^cite_note-politico-2025-9][^cite_note-\:1-11]

Reporters and [OSINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_intelligence) analysts have noted the striking similarity between the FP-5 Flamingo and the FP-5 missile advertised by defence industry startup Milanion Group at IDEX 2025 (a defence industry conference and expo), with the advertised capabilities, design and even the model number being identical.[^cite_note-twz_20250818-12][^cite_note-\:02-13][^cite_note-AutoA9-1-14][^cite_note-\:2-15] Milanion Group is a company registered in the United Kingdom,[^cite_note-milaniongroup-privacy2-16] though media reports state it is based in the United Arab Emirates and did business in Ukraine prior to the Russian invasion.[^cite_note-janes-202502192-17][^cite_note-pitchbook_470779-18][^cite_note-dn-202108092-19] An analyst has described Fire Point as operating under Milanion's umbrella, using Milanion's technology and connections to produce the FP-5 locally.[^cite_note-ukrinform_4028627-20][^cite_note-\:02-13] *The Economist* assessed that the production process is partially conducted abroad, with Fire Point stating that over 90% of final assembly is done in Ukraine.[^cite_note-\:1-11]

According to [Ukrinform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrinform), the weapon threatens 90% of Russia's arms manufacturing capacities.[^cite_note-ui90-21] Possible target categories:

- Oil refineries and energy infrastructure – Fire Point officials noted their drones and missiles are "partly responsible for airport closures and high gasoline prices in Russia".

- Military production facilities and weapons depots – described as targets for the missile's 1,150 kg (2,540 lb) warhead.

- Command centres and strategic infrastructure – identified as priority targets given the missile's capabilities.

## Design

The missile has a fixed straight wing and a [turbofan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbofan) jet engine mounted above the fuselage. This gives it a superficial resemblance to the [V-1 flying bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-1_flying_bomb) and the Ukrainian-built Soviet [Tu-141](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-141) and [Tu-143](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-143) reconnaissance drones, although the V1 used a [pulsejet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulsejet) and the Tu-141 and Tu-143 were designed with engines mounted inside the main fuselage. The rear of the fuselage has four [flight control surfaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_control_surfaces) in an X-configuration.[^cite_note-defence-ua-22] The [monocoque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocoque) fuselage is primarily built with [filament-wound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filament_winding) (as used in ballistic missiles) [radar transparent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radar_cross_section#Material) composite materials ([fiberglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiberglass)),[^cite_note-u24-3] and the engine nacelle is built from metal to withstand high temperatures. The jet engine appears to be the [Ivchenko AI-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivchenko_AI-25), designed in the 1960s in the Soviet Union and used to power the [Aero L-39 Albatros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_L-39_Albatros) aircraft, later produced by Ukrainian manufacturer [Motor Sich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_Sich). The engine is significantly larger than those found in comparable cruise missiles. [Fire Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_Point_\(Ukrainian_firm\)) chief designer, Denys Shtilerman, said in an interview, on 8 July 2026, that an "indigenous turbojet engine" had been developed with some 10 engines being tested.[^cite_note-militarnyi-23]

Flamingo's [warhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warhead) is claimed to be 2.5 times the weight of the warhead of the American [Tomahawk Block V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_\(missile\)) cruise missile, with a stated weight of 1,150 kg (2,540 lb).[^cite_note-tg-6][^cite_note-politico-2025-9][^cite_note-\:0-24] According to a Dutch military expert interviewed by [UkrInform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UkrInform), Flamingo is a classic [aerial bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_bomb) converted into a cruise missile by adding a jet engine, fuselage, and wings.[^cite_note-ui-25] The warhead, shown in pictures with the nose cone removed, may be a repurposed [FAB-1500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAB-1500) bomb,[^cite_note-twz-8][^cite_note-ui-25][^cite_note-defence-blog-26] but based on the warhead's size the 1,500 kg (3,300 lb) FAB-1500 seems unlikely, with a more likely bomb being either a 925 kg (2,039 lb) [Mark 84](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_84_bomb) high-explosive bomb or a same-weight BLU-109/B bunker buster.[^cite_note-\:2-15][^cite_note-u24-3] If the missile uses a conventional gravity bomb, the weight of the warhead would be 925 kg (2,039 lb) but the explosive filler would only be 430 kg (950 lb), more in line with comparable cruise missile systems.[^cite_note-gulflink-27][^cite_note-defence-ua_15552-28] The Flamingo's claimed range is approximately twice the unclassified range of the Tomahawk Block V,[^cite_note-\:0-24] allowing it to hit targets anywhere in the European part of Russia and as far as [Novosibirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk), but it had not been shown to strike targets at this range as of June 2026.

The Flamingo claims a similar speed to its Western counterparts, 900 km/h, but does not have complex visual guidance systems ([TERCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TERCOM), [DSMAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSMAC)); the primary means of navigation is by [satellite navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_navigation) using a jamming-resistant, [controlled reception pattern antenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_reception_pattern_antenna) layout. Claimed accuracy is a [CEP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_error_probable) of 14 m (46 ft) in ideal conditions at any range. The simplified design is intended to speed production. The main drawbacks compared to the Tomahawk are a longer pre-launch preparation time of 20 to 40 minutes and a takeoff weight that is almost five times greater.[^cite_note-ap-5][^cite_note-rbc_production_details-29][^cite_note-defence-ua_15526-30][^cite_note-defence_ua_why_1000kg-31] Ukrainian experts state that Russian [air defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_defence) will intercept some FP-5 missiles, and emphasise the value of combined strikes by multiple weapons types.[^cite_note-ui90-21]

On 4 July 2026, five Flamingos were shot down by the Russian Air Force using an [A-50U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-50U) radar plane. With Michael Bohnert, a RAND analyst, said “Its radar, unobstructed by the curvature of the Earth, was able to detect Ukrainian FP-5 Flamingo cruise missiles from far away and defeat them,".[^cite_note-euromaidanpress_20260708-32]

## Cost and affordability

The Czech crowdfunding initiative "Gift for Putin" ("Dárek pro Putina") raised approximately \$520,000 (roughly 12.5 million Czech koruna) to purchase a Ukrainian-built Flamingo cruise missile in October 2025.[^cite_note-united24-33] The fundraiser, which was completed in approximately 48 hours after launching on 22 October 2025, was organised to purchase a single missile specifically priced by the manufacturer Fire Point for the campaign.[^cite_note-united24-33] The missile, designated DANA 1 in honour of the late Czech nuclear safety chief [Dana Drábová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Dr%C3%A1bov%C3%A1), was to be transferred to the Armed Forces of Ukraine upon completion of payment, but in November 2025, the organizers suspended the deal over a [corruption investigation](81589122.md) involving Fire Point.[^cite_note-united24-33][^cite_note-idnes_251128-34][^cite_note-novinky_40551056-35]

This fundraising campaign provides a concrete cost estimate for individual Flamingo missiles. Multiple independent sources have corroborated similar price points, with estimates ranging from approximately \$500,000 to under €1 million (\$1.09 million) per unit.[^cite_note-economist-36][^cite_note-aspi-37][^cite_note-armyrecognition-38] Fire Point CEO [Iryna Terekh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iryna_Terekh) stated cost per unit was US\$600,000,[^cite_note-39] and has emphasised that the company "purposefully aimed for low cost" to enable large-scale procurement and potentially massive strike operations.[^cite_note-aspi-37]

By comparison, the American [Tomahawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomahawk_\(missile\)) cruise missile costs between \$1.5 million and \$2.5 million per unit depending on the variant, despite having approximately half the range and a significantly smaller warhead than the Flamingo.[^cite_note-armyrecognition-38][^cite_note-economist-36][^cite_note-csis-40] The Flamingo's affordability is achieved through simplified design choices, including the use of repurposed Soviet-era AI-25TL turbofan engines from [Aero L-39 Albatros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_L-39_Albatros) jet trainers, a carbon-fiber fuselage that can reportedly be manufactured in six hours, and basic inertial navigation systems that [trade off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_off) some precision to reduce the cost.[^cite_note-armyrecognition-38]

The French company [Safran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safran) provided the guidance system in exchange for Ukraine purchasing more [AASM Hammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AASM_Hammer) bombs.[^cite_note-defence-ua_19074-41]

On 27 July 2026, Fire Point CEO Iryna Terekh said each Flamingo missile “costs just under \$600,000 per unit”, which is claimed to be six times cheaper than a [Tomahawk missile](31238.md). She also claimed a June attack on [Cheboksary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheboksary) to be possibly the “longest recorded cruise missile missions”. Current production is some 100 missiles per month.[^cite_note-42]

## Production

Serial production of Flamingo was claimed to have begun in mid-2025, initially at a rate of approximately 30 missiles per month.[^cite_note-kyiv_ind-7] According to Politico's August 2025 interview with Fire Point CEO Iryna Terekh, the missile had successfully completed battlefield trials, and entered serial production with a target capacity of 200 units monthly.[^cite_note-politico-2025-9][^cite_note-mezha-fire-point-flamingo-43] Fire Point announced in September 2025 that it had increased production of its Flamingo cruise missile from 30 units per month in August to 50, with plans to reach seven missiles per day by the end of the year. Terekh confirmed that the Flamingo had entered serial production, with scaling up being the primary focus for meeting expanded production targets.[^cite_note-mezha-fire-point-flamingo-43]

The weapon system provides Ukraine's most advanced long-range strike capability.

In September 2025, the [Danish government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksen_II_Cabinet) announced that a subsidiary of Fire Point would start production of [solid rocket fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_propellant#Solid_chemical_propellants) for the Flamingo in Denmark from 1 December 2025.[^cite_note-nyheder-44][^cite_note-reuters_20250903-45] Denmark temporarily suspended 20 laws and regulations to open the plant.[^cite_note-militarnyi_bypassed-46]

Fire Point had purchased a large number of [Ivchenko AI-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivchenko_AI-25) engines with a remaining flight time of some 10 hours from various stockpiles. They restore the engines enough for a Flamingo missile's flight time of three and a half hours, although not suitable for normal more prolonged use. Previous media reports said that the engines had been made by [Motor Sich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_Sich), but Fire Point said that they had found "thousands of such engines" at landfills in Ukraine. Titanium parts were replaced by "cheaper and easier-to-produce alternatives" adequate to make them flightworthy. Engines can also be taken from about 6,000 still in use in aircraft such as the [L-39 Albatross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-39_Albatross) and [Hongdu JL-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongdu_JL-8) jet trainers across "post-Soviet countries, Asia, and Africa". Fire Point has also established its own plant to produce missile engines; it is not clear if this is the Ivchenko AI-25 under licence.

Fire Point does not manufacture the warhead, which is said to be able to "pierce thick concrete", with ground penetration of some 10 m (33 ft) "Many types" of warhead, including cluster warheads, were reported to be in development. As of "early September 2025" the rate of production was two missiles a day, with work on an automated process that aims to "reach 200 per month by the end of this year".[^cite_note-militarnyi_localizes-47][^cite_note-aerospaceglobalnews-48]

In early December 2025, during an interview on X, Denys Shtilerman (co-founder and chief designer of Fire Point) said that a Flamingo missile had been intercepted on 9 October at a height of 114 meters. The aim was to fly at an "altitude of 30 to 40 meters above the ground", so "no one will see it from the ground". This would bring it in line with "[TERCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TERCOM) system, which has been in operation since the late 1980s". However Ukraine does not have maps of the Russian Federation suitable to allow the missile to fly so low.[^cite_note-bluewin_2992368-49]

As of 15 March 2026, Fire Point claimed to manufacture three Flamingos per day. New engines were close to completion to replace the current [AI-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI-25) engines used. According to Shtilerman, "All the engines (AI-25) we use now were previously used in civil aviation. Their peak efficiency is roughly between 6,000 and 10,000 meters, so they are not very effective at sea level". The new engines will be a dual-circuit turbojet engine with a low [bypass ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bypass_ratio), unlike most cruise missile engines; the company claims that performance will be exceeded only by the [Tomahawk missile](31238.md). In 2025 the new engines were planned to enter production by a Ukrainian manufacturer at the beginning of the following year.[^cite_note-militarnyi_newengine-50]

In June 2026 [Diehl Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diehl_Defence) announced an interest in manufacturing the missile with an uprated seeker and guidance system.[^cite_note-Pitel-51][^cite_note-nv_50615536-52]
