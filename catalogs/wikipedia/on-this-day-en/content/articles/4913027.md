In most legal [jurisdictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction), a **supreme court**, also known as a **court of last resort**, **apex court**, **high** (or **final**) **court of appeal**, and **court of final appeal**, is the highest [court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court) within the hierarchy of courts. Broadly speaking, the decisions of a supreme court are binding on all other courts in a nation and are not subject to further review by any other court. Supreme courts typically function primarily as [appellate courts](643.md), hearing [appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeal) from decisions of lower [trial courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_court), or from intermediate-level appellate courts. A supreme court can also, in certain circumstances, act as a court of [original jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_jurisdiction).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

[Civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)) states tend not to have a single highest court. Some federations, such as the United States, also do not have a single highest court. The highest court in some jurisdictions is not named the "Supreme Court", for example, the [High Court of Australia](314201.md). On the other hand, in some places the court named the "Supreme Court" is not in fact the highest court; examples include the [New York Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Supreme_Court), the [supreme courts of several Canadian provinces/territories](1124694.md#Superior-level_courts_of_the_provinces_and_territories), and the former [Supreme Court of Judicature of England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Judicature_of_England_and_Wales) and [Supreme Court of Judicature of Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Judicature_of_Northern_Ireland), which are all subordinate to higher courts of appeal.

## Single or multiple supreme courts

Some countries have multiple "supreme courts" whose respective jurisdictions have different geographical extents, or which are restricted to particular areas of law. Some countries with a [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation) system of government may have both a federal supreme court (such as the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md)), and supreme courts for each member state (such as the [Supreme Court of Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Nevada)), with the former having jurisdiction over the latter only to the extent that the federal [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution) extends [federal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_law) over [state law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_law_\(United_States\)). However, other federations, such as Canada, may have a supreme court of general jurisdiction, able to decide any question of law. Jurisdictions with a [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)) system often have a hierarchy of [administrative courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_court) separate from the [ordinary courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_courts), headed by a supreme administrative court (such as the [Supreme Administrative Court of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Administrative_Court_of_Finland), for example). A number of jurisdictions also maintain a separate [constitutional court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_court)[^cite_note-3] or other judicial or [quasi-judicial body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-judicial_body) (first developed in the [Czechoslovak Constitution of 1920](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Constitution_of_1920)), such as Austria, France, Germany, Italy, Luxembourg, Portugal, Russia, Spain and South Africa. Within the former [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire), the highest court within a colony was often called the "Supreme Court", even though appeals could be made from that court to the [United Kingdom](31717.md)'s [Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Committee_of_the_Privy_Council) (based in London). A number of [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) jurisdictions retain this system, but many others have reconstituted their own highest court as a court of last resort, with the right of appeal to the Privy Council being abolished.

### Divisions within a single court

In some cases, a single supreme court may be divided into multiple divisions or chambers with jurisdiction over different areas of law. For example, the [Supreme Court of Spain](8327853.md) is divided into five ordinary chambers dealing with [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_law), [criminal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_law), [administrative law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_law), [labour law](18657.md), and [military law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_law) respectively along with four special chambers.[^cite_note-4]

## History

The idea of a supreme court owes much to the framers of the [Constitution of the United States](31644.md). It was while debating the [division of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_powers) between the legislative and executive departments that delegates to the [1787 Constitutional Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Convention_\(United_States\)) established the parameters for the national judiciary. Creating a separate "third branch" of government was a novel idea; in the English tradition, judicial power is just one aspect of the sovereign authority of the [Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crown). It was also proposed in the Constitutional Convention that the judiciary should have a role in checking the executive power to exercise a [veto](74661.md) or to revise laws. In the end, the [Framers of the Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framers_of_the_Constitution) compromised by sketching only a general outline of the judiciary, vesting of federal judicial power in "one supreme Court, and in such inferior Courts as the Congress may from time to time ordain and establish".[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] They delineated neither the exact powers and prerogatives of the Supreme Court nor the organization of the Judicial Branch as a whole.

## Common law jurisdictions

In jurisdictions using a [common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law) system, the doctrine of *[stare decisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stare_decisis)* applies, whereby the principles applied by the supreme court in its [decisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judgment_\(law\)) are binding upon all lower courts; this is intended to apply a uniform interpretation and implementation of the law. In [civil law jurisdictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)) the doctrine of *stare decisis* is not generally considered to apply, so the decisions of the supreme court are not necessarily binding beyond the immediate case before it; however, in practice the decisions of the supreme court usually provide a very strong precedent, or *[jurisprudence constante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisprudence_constante)*, for both itself and all lower courts.

### Australia

Main article: [High Court of Australia](314201.md)

The High Court of Australia is the supreme court in the [Australian court hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_court_hierarchy) and the final [court of appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_appeal) in [Australia](4689264.md).[^cite_note-7] It has both [original](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_jurisdiction) and [appellate jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appellate_jurisdiction), the power of [judicial review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_review) over laws passed by the [Parliament of Australia](302298.md) and the parliaments of the states, and the ability to interpret the [Constitution of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Australia) and thereby shape the development of [federalism in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_Australia).

The High Court is mandated by section 71 of the Constitution, which vests in it the [judicial power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) of the Commonwealth of Australia. The Court was constituted by, and its first members were appointed under, the [Judiciary Act 1903](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_Act_1903). It now operates under sections 71 to 75 of the Constitution, the *Judiciary Act*,[^cite_note-Judiciary_Act-8] and the *High Court of Australia Act* 1979.[^cite_note-High_Court_of_Australia_Act-9] It is composed of seven Justices: the [Chief Justice of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Australia), currently [Stephen Gageler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Gageler) [AC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_of_the_Order_of_Australia), and six other Justices. They are appointed by the [Governor-General of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Australia) on the advice of the federal government, and under the constitution must retire at age 70.

### Bangladesh

This paragraph is an excerpt from [Supreme Court of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh\&action=edit)]

[Supreme Court of Bangladesh (SCB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh)[^cite_note-10] is the highest court of law in the [People's Republic of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Bangladesh). It is composed of the [High Court Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_Division,_Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh) and the [Appellate Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appellate_Division,_Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh), and was established by Part VI, Chapter I (Article 94) of the [Constitution of Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bangladesh), adopted in 1972.[^cite_note-Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh_ju-11] This is also the office of the [chief justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Bangladesh), Appellate Division judges, and High Court Division judges of Bangladesh. As of February 2026, the Appellate Division consists of 6 judges, while the High Court Division has 103 judges (77 are permanent and 26 are additional).[^cite_note-Supreme_Court_of_Bangladesh_JUDGES-12]

### Hong Kong

Main articles: [High Court of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Hong_Kong) and [Court of Final Appeal (Hong Kong)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Final_Appeal_\(Hong_Kong\))

See also: [NPCSC of the People's Republic of China](#People's_Republic_of_China)

In [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Hong_Kong), the [Supreme Court of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Hong_Kong) (now known as the [High Court of Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Hong_Kong)) was the final court of appeal during its colonial times which ended with transfer of sovereignty in 1997. The final adjudication power, as in any other British Colonies, rested with the [Judicial Committee of the Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Committee_of_the_Privy_Council) (JCPC) in London, United Kingdom. Now the power of final adjudication is vested in the [Court of Final Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Final_Appeal_\(Hong_Kong\)) created in 1997. Under the [Basic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_of_Hong_Kong), its constitution, the territory remains a common law jurisdiction. Consequently, judges from other common law jurisdictions (including [England and Wales](275187.md)) can be recruited and continue to serve in the [judiciary](1501145.md) according to Article 92 of the Basic Law. On the other hand, the power of interpretation of the Basic Law itself is vested in the [Standing Committee of the National People's Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Committee_of_the_National_People's_Congress) (NPCSC) in Beijing (without retroactive effect), and the courts are authorised to interpret the Basic Law when trying cases, in accordance with Article 158 of the Basic Law. This arrangement became controversial in light of the [right of abode issue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_abode_issue,_Hong_Kong) in 1999, raising concerns for judicial independence.

### India

This paragraph is an excerpt from [Supreme Court of India](395852.md).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Supreme_Court_of_India\&action=edit)]

The [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) is the supreme [judicial authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_India) and the highest court in India. It is the highest [appellate court](643.md) for all [civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(common_law\)) and [criminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_law) cases in India. The court is led by the [Chief Justice of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_India) and has a maximum sanctioned strength of 37 judges excluding the chief justice. It was established on 28 January 1950, two days after India became a republic, and replaced the [Judicial Committee of the Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Committee_of_the_Privy_Council) and the [Federal Court of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Court_of_India) as the highest court of appeal. It has operated from the Supreme Court building in [New Delhi](51585.md) since 1958. With expansive authority to initiate actions and wield appellate jurisdiction over all courts and the ability to invalidate amendments to the constitution, the Supreme Court of India is widely acknowledged as one of the most powerful supreme courts in the world.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

### Ireland

Main article: [Supreme Court of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Ireland)

The [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Ireland) is the highest court in the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md). It has authority to interpret the constitution, and strike down laws and activities of the state that it finds to be unconstitutional. It is also the highest authority in the interpretation of the law. Constitutionally it must have authority to interpret the constitution but its further appellate jurisdiction from lower courts is defined by law. The Irish Supreme Court consists of its presiding member, the Chief Justice, and seven other judges. Judges of the Supreme Court are appointed by the President in accordance with the binding advice of the Government. The Supreme Court sits in the [Four Courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Courts) in [Dublin](8504.md).

The [Supreme Court of the Irish Free State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_Irish_Free_State) was subordinate to the Privy Council until 1933, when the right of appeal to the Privy Council from Ireland was abolished.

### Nauru

Main article: [Supreme Court of Nauru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Nauru)

In [Nauru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Nauru), there is no single highest court for all types of cases. The [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Nauru) has final jurisdiction on [constitutional](490077.md) matters, but any other case may be appealed further to the [Appellate Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appellate_Court_of_Nauru?action=edit\&redlink=1). In addition, an agreement between Nauru and [Australia](4689264.md) in 1976 provided for appeals from the Supreme Court of Nauru to the [High Court of Australia](314201.md) in both criminal and civil cases, with the notable exception of constitutional cases.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

### New Zealand

Main articles: [High Court of New Zealand](3902269.md) and [Supreme Court of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_New_Zealand)

In [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_New_Zealand), the right of appeal to the [Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Committee_of_the_Privy_Council) was abolished following the passing of the Supreme Court Act (2003). A right of appeal to the Privy Council remains for criminal cases which were decided before the Supreme Court was created, but it was the case of [Teina Pora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teina_Pora) in 2015 was the final case to the Privy Council from New Zealand – which ruled in his favour.

The new [Supreme Court of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_New_Zealand) was officially established at the beginning of 2004, although it did not come into operation until July. The [High Court of New Zealand](3902269.md) was until 1980 known as the Supreme Court. The Supreme Court has a purely appellate jurisdiction and hears appeals from the [Court of Appeal of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Appeal_of_New_Zealand). In some cases, an appeal may be removed directly to the Supreme Court from the High Court. For certain cases, particularly cases which commenced in the District Court, a lower court (typically the High Court or the Court of Appeal) may be the court of final jurisdiction.

### Nigeria

The [Supreme Court of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Nigeria) is the highest court in Nigeria. The Supreme Court mainly regulates in disputes between states and/or the federal government. Another power of the Supreme court rests in its authority to oversee any decisions over presidential elections and term lengths.[^cite_note-17]

### Pakistan

Main article: [Supreme Court of Pakistan](843857.md)

The [Supreme Court](843857.md) has been the apex court for [Pakistan](23235.md) since the declaration of the republic in 1956 (previously the Privy Council had that function from 1947 to 1950 and the [Federal Court of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Court_of_Pakistan) from 1950 to 1956). The Supreme Court has the final say on matters of [constitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution) law, federal law or on matters of mixed federal and provincial competence. It can hear appeals on matters of provincial competence only if a matter of a constitutional nature is raised.

With respect to Pakistan's autonomous territories (i.e. Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan) the Supreme Court's jurisdiction is rather limited and varies from territory to territory; it can hear appeals only of a constitutional nature from Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan. Azad Kashmir has its own courts system and the constitution of Pakistan does not apply to it as such; appeals from Azad Kashmir relate to its relationship with Pakistan.

The provinces have their own courts system, with the High Court as the apex court, except insofar as where an appeal can go to the Supreme Court as mentioned above.

### United Kingdom

Main article: [Supreme Court of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_Kingdom)

The [Supreme Court of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_Kingdom) is the ultimate court for criminal and civil matters in England, Wales and Northern Ireland and for civil matters in Scotland. (The supreme court for criminal matters in Scotland is the [High Court of Justiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Justiciary).) The Supreme Court was established by the [Constitutional Reform Act 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Reform_Act_2005) with effect from 1 October 2009, replacing and assuming the [judicial functions of the House of Lords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_functions_of_the_House_of_Lords). [Devolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution) issues under the [Scotland Act 1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_Act_1998), [Government of Wales Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Wales_Act_1998) and [Northern Ireland Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Act_1998) were also transferred to the new Supreme Court by the Constitutional Reform Act, from the [Judicial Committee of the Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Committee_of_the_Privy_Council).

The Supreme Court shares its members and accommodation at the [Middlesex Guildhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesex_Guildhall) in London with the [Judicial Committee of the Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Committee_of_the_Privy_Council) which hears final appeals from certain smaller Commonwealth realm countries, admiralty cases, and certain appeals from the [ecclesiastical courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_court) and statutory private jurisdictions, such as professional and academic bodies.

(The Constitutional Reform Act also renamed the *Supreme Court of Judicature of Northern Ireland* to the [Court of Judicature](2647041.md#Court_of_Judicature), and the rarely cited *Supreme Court of Judicature for England and Wales* as the [Senior Courts of England and Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Courts_of_England_and_Wales)).

The Supreme Court was set up in 2009; until then the [House of Lords](13658.md) was the ultimate court [in addition to being a legislative body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_functions_of_the_House_of_Lords), and the [Lord Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Chancellor), with [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) and [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) functions, was also a senior judge in the House of Lords.

### United States

Main articles: [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md) and [State supreme court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_supreme_court)



The first four female justices of the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md): [Sandra Day O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Day_O'Connor), [Sonia Sotomayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonia_Sotomayor), [Ruth Bader Ginsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Bader_Ginsburg) and [Elena Kagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Kagan).

The [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md), established in 1789, is the highest federal court in the [United States](27552742.md). It has final appellate powers over the [federal court system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_federal_courts), and can perform [judicial review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_review) in matters involving [US federal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_federal_law) (which applies to all the states). There are currently nine members of the US Supreme Court; however, there is no number specified in the Constitution. New members are nominated to life terms by the [President of the United States](24113.md) and must be confirmed by the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_United_States). There are no specific requirements set out in the Constitution for Supreme Court nominees.

Each [U.S. state](18618239.md) also has its own [state supreme court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_supreme_court), the highest authority in interpreting that state's law and administering that state's judiciary. The courts of [Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Oklahoma) and [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Texas) have separate criminal and civil courts of last resort, while Delaware has separate courts for appellate and [equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equity_\(law\)) functions.[^cite_note-18]

The official names of state supreme courts vary, as do the titles of its members, which can cause confusion between jurisdictions. Alternative names for supreme courts includes [Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Court_of_Appeals), [Supreme Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Virginia_Supreme_Court_of_Appeals) and [Supreme Judicial Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Judicial_Court_\(disambiguation\)). However the [New York Supreme Court, Appellate Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Supreme_Court,_Appellate_Division) is a lower court, not a supreme court. The titles for judicial officeholders can cause confusion, even within the same jurisdiction. [In Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Texas), the posts of Justice, Judge, and Justices of the Peace are members of successively lower levels of courts.

## Civil law jurisdictions

The [Roman law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_law) and the [Corpus Juris Civilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corpus_Juris_Civilis) are generally held to be the historical model for civil law. From the late 18th century onwards, [civil law jurisdictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)) began to codify their laws, most of all in [civil codes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_code).

### Argentina

Main article: [Supreme Court of Argentina](2800711.md)

The Supreme Court functions as a last resort tribunal. Its rulings cannot be appealed. It also decides on cases dealing with the interpretation of the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Argentina) (for example, it can overturn a law passed by [Congress](1589508.md) if it deems it unconstitutional).

### Armenia

According to the [Armenian constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Armenia) the [Court of Cassation of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Cassation_of_Armenia) is the highest court in [Armenia](43824388.md), except in the sphere of constitutional justice, in which the [Constitutional Court of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Armenia) maintains authority.[^cite_note-19]

### Austria

In [Austria](25135167.md), the [Austrian Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Austria) of 1920 (based on a draft by [Hans Kelsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Kelsen)) introduced [judicial review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_review) of legislative acts for their [constitutionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionality). This function is performed by the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Constitutional_Court) (*Verfassungsgerichtshof*), which is also charged with the review of administrative acts on whether they violate constitutionally guaranteed rights. Other than that, administrative acts are reviewed by the [Administrative Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Administrative_Court) (*Verwaltungsgerichtshof*). The [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Supreme_Court) (*Oberste Gerichtshof (OGH)*), stands at the top of Austria's system of "[ordinary courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_courts)" (*ordentliche Gerichte*) as the final instance in issues of [private law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_law) and [criminal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_law).

### Brazil

In [Brazil](3504554.md), the [Supreme Federal Court](3359076.md) (*Supremo Tribunal Federal*) is the highest court. It is both the constitutional court and the court of last resort in Brazilian law. It only reviews cases that may be unconstitutional or final *[habeas corpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habeas_corpus)* pleads for criminal cases. It also judges, in [original jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_jurisdiction), cases involving members of [congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_of_Brazil), [senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Senate_of_Brazil), ministers of state, members of the high courts and the [President](273638.md) and [Vice-President of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-President_of_Brazil). The Superior Court of Justice (*Superior Tribunal de Justiça*) reviews State and Federal Circuit courts decisions for [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_law) and [criminal law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_law) cases, when dealing with federal law or conflicting rulings. The Superior Labour Court (*Tribunal Superior do Trabalho*) reviews cases involving [labour law](18657.md). The Superior Electoral Court (*Tribunal Superior Eleitoral*) is the court of last resort of [electoral law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_law), and also oversees [general elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Brazil). The Superior Military Court (*Tribunal Superior Militar*) is the highest court in matters of federal [military law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_law).

### Croatia

In [Croatia](5573.md), the supreme jurisdiction is given to the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Supreme_Court), which secures a uniform application of laws. The [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Constitutional_Court) exists to verify [constitutionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionality) of laws and regulations, as well as decide on individual complaints on decisions on governmental bodies. It also decides on jurisdictional disputes between the legislative, executive and judicial branches.

### Denmark

In [Denmark](6067786.md), all [ordinary courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_courts) have original jurisdiction to hear all types of cases, including cases of a constitutional or administrative nature. As a result, there exists no special constitutional court, and therefore final jurisdiction is vested with the [Danish Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courts_of_Denmark) (*Højesteret*) which was established 14 February 1661 by king [Frederik III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederik_III_of_Denmark).

### France

In [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_France), supreme appellate jurisdiction is divided among three judicial bodies:

- for judicial cases, i.e., [civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_justice) or [criminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_justice) matters: [Court of Cassation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Cassation_\(France\)) (*Cour de cassation*)

- for administrative cases: [Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_State_\(France\)) (*Conseil d'État*)

- constitutional challenges of [statutory laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutory_laws): [Constitutional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Council_of_France) (*Conseil constitutionnel*)

When there is jurisdictional dispute between judicial and administrative courts: the Court of Arbitration (*Tribunal des conflits*), which is empanelled half from the Court of Cassation and half from the Council of State and presided over by the [Minister of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Justice_\(France\)), is called together to settle the dispute or hand down a final decision.

The [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_\(France\)) (*Haute Cour*) exists only to impeach the [President of the French Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_French_Republic) in case of "breach of his duties patently incompatible with his continuing in office". Since a constitutional amendment of 2007, the [French Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Constitution) states that the High Court is composed of all members of both Houses of Parliament. As of 2023, it has never been convened.

Members of the French government, [except the President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_immunity), are subject to the same laws as other French citizens. However, since 1993, a new and different court was introduced to judge them in place of normal courts, the [Justice Court of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cour_de_Justice_de_la_R%C3%A9publique) (*Cour de Justice de la République*). It has since been highly criticized and its abolition was recommended in 2022 after a public consultation.[^cite_note-20]

### Germany

In [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Germany), there is no *[de jure](43594.md)* single supreme court. Instead, cases are decided in the final instance by one of five federal high courts (see below), depending on their nature.

Final interpretation of the German Constitution, the *[Grundgesetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_of_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany)*, is the task of the *[Bundesverfassungsgericht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Constitutional_Court_of_Germany)* (Federal Constitutional Court), which is the *[de facto](43594.md)* highest German court, as it can declare both [federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Germany) and [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) [legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislation) ineffective. In addition, it has the power to overrule decisions of all other courts, despite not being a court of appeals in the German court system. It is also the only court possessing the power and authority to outlaw political parties, should their manifests or activism prove unconstitutional.

When it comes to civil and criminal cases, the *[Bundesgerichtshof](561684.md)* (Federal Court of Justice) is at the top of the hierarchy of courts. The other branches of the German judicial system each have their own appellate systems, each topped by a high court; these are the *[Bundessozialgericht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Social_Court)* (Federal Social Court) for matters of social security, the *[Bundesarbeitsgericht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Labour_Court)* (Federal Labour Court) for employment and labour, the *[Bundesfinanzhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Fiscal_Court)* (Federal Fiscal Court) for taxation and financial issues, and the *[Bundesverwaltungsgericht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Administrative_Court_\(Germany\))* (Federal Administrative Court) for administrative law. The so-called *[Gemeinsamer Senat der Obersten Gerichtshöfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Senate_of_the_Supreme_Courts_of_Germany)* (Joint Senate of the Supreme Courts) is not a supreme court itself, but an ad-hoc body that is convened only when one high court intends to diverge from another high court's [legal opinion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_opinion). As the courts have well-defined areas of responsibility, situations like these are rather rare and the Joint Senate gathers very infrequently.

### Iceland

The [Supreme Court of Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Iceland) ([Icelandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandic_language): *Hæstiréttur Íslands*, [lit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation). *Highest Court of Iceland*) was founded under Act No. 22/1919 and held its first session on 16 February 1920.[^cite_note-21] The Court holds the highest judicial power in Iceland. The court system was transformed from a two level system to a three level system in 2018 with the establishment of Landsréttur.[^cite_note-22]

### Indonesia

[Law of Indonesia](9751594.md) at the national level is following of [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)) (commonly known as Continental European in Indonesia) from the tradition of [Roman-Dutch law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman-Dutch_law).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

Indonesian law also recognizes [customary law](218798.md) from the tradition of the traditional [Adat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adat).[^cite_note-25] The [Sharia](28840.md) law is applied in one province: [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_criminal_law_in_Aceh).[^cite_note-26] Laws in regional jurisdictions can vary from province to province as the governors and mayors may issue regulations, although the legal hierarchy of regional laws are lower than the national laws, and the Constitution is at the top.[^cite_note-27]

The [Supreme Court of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Indonesia) is the main judicial arm of the state, functioning as the final court of appeal as well as a means to re-open cases previously closed. The Supreme Court, which consists of a total of 51 justices, also oversees the regional high courts. It was founded at the same year of the country's independence in 1945.

The [Constitutional Court of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Indonesia), on the other hand, is a part of the judicial branch tasked with review of bills and government actions for constitutionality, as well as regulation of the interactions between various arms of the state. The constitutional amendment to establish the court was passed in 2001, and the court itself was established in 2003.[^cite_note-28] The Constitutional Court consists of nine justices serving nine year terms, and they're appointed in tandem by the Supreme Court, the [President of Indonesia](10011631.md) and the [People's Representative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Representative_Council).[^cite_note-29]

### Israel

Main article: [Supreme Court of Israel](1666510.md)

[Israel](8932627.md)'s [Supreme Court](1666510.md) is at the head of the court system in the [State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Israel). It is the highest judicial instance. The Supreme Court sits in [Jerusalem](16043.md). The area of its jurisdiction is the entire State. A ruling of the Supreme Court is binding upon every court, other than the Supreme Court itself. The Israeli supreme court is both an appellate court and the high court of justice. As an appellate court, the Supreme Court considers cases on appeal (both criminal and civil) on judgments and other decisions of the District Courts. It also considers appeals on judicial and quasi-judicial decisions of various kinds, such as matters relating to the legality of [Knesset](74656.md) elections and disciplinary rulings of the Bar Association. As the High Court of Justice (Hebrew: Beit Mishpat Gavoha Le'Zedek בית משפט גבוה לצדק; also known by its initials as Bagatz בג"ץ), the Supreme Court rules as a court of first instance, primarily in matters regarding the legality of decisions of State authorities: Government decisions, those of local authorities and other bodies and persons performing public functions under the law, and direct challenges to the constitutionality of laws enacted by the Knesset. The court has broad discretionary authority to rule on matters in which it considers it necessary to grant relief in the interests of justice, and which are not within the jurisdiction of another court or tribunal. The High Court of Justice grants relief through orders such as injunction, mandamus and Habeas Corpus, as well as through declaratory judgments. The Supreme Court can also sit at a further hearing on its own judgment. In a matter on which the Supreme Court has ruled – whether as a court of appeals or as the High Court of Justice – with a panel of three or more justices, it may rule at a further hearing with a panel of a larger number of justices. A further hearing may be held if the Supreme Court makes a ruling inconsistent with a previous ruling or if the Court deems that the importance, difficulty or novelty of a ruling of the Court justifies such hearing. The Supreme Court also holds the unique power of being able to order "[trial de novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_de_novo)" (a retrial).

### Netherlands

### Portugal

In [Portugal](5108599.md), there are several supreme courts, each with a specific jurisdiction:

- The [Supreme Court of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Justice_\(Portugal\)) (*Supremo Tribunal de Justiça*) – for judicial (civil and criminal) matters;

- The [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Portugal) (*Tribunal Constitucional*) – for the [constitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Portugal) matters;

Until 2003, a fifth supreme court also existed for the military jurisdiction, this being the Supreme Military Court (*Supremo Tribunal Militar*). Presently, in time of peace, the supreme court for military justice matters is the Supreme Court of Justice, which now includes four military judges.

### Republic of China (Taiwan)

In the [Republic of China (Taiwan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(Taiwan\)), there are four different courts of last resort:

- [Supreme Court of the Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_Republic_of_China) (中華民國最高法院): civil and criminal cases.

- Supreme Administrative Court of the Republic of China (中華民國最高行政法院): executive cases.

The Constitutional Court consists of 15 justices. The Council of Grand Justices was a previous Constitutional review organ until 2022.

All four courts are directly under the [Judicial Yuan](378790.md), whose president also serves as Chief Justice of the Constitutional Court.

### Spain

Main article: [Supreme Court of Spain](8327853.md)

The Spanish Supreme Court is the highest court for all cases in Spain (both private and public). Only those cases related to human rights can be appealed at the [Constitutional Court of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Spain) (which also decides about acts accordance with the Spanish Constitution). \
&#x20;In Spain, high courts cannot create binding precedents;[^cite_note-30] however, lower rank courts usually observe Supreme Court interpretations. In most private law cases, two Supreme Court judgements supporting a claim are needed to appeal at the Supreme Court.[^cite_note-31]\
&#x20;Five sections form the Spanish Supreme court:

- Section one judges private law cases (including commercial law).

- Section two decides about criminal appeals.

- Section three judges administrative cases and controls government normative powers.

- Section four is dedicated to labour law.

- Section five is dedicated to military justice.

There is also a separate constitutional court, the *[Tribunal Constitucional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Spain)*, which has a duty of the supreme interpreter of the Spanish Constitution, with the power to determine the constitutionality of acts and statutes made by any public body, central, regional, or local in Spain.

### Sweden

In [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Sweden), the [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Sweden), founded in 1789, and the [Supreme Administrative Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Administrative_Court_of_Sweden), founded in 1909, respectively function as the highest courts of the land. The Supreme Administrative Court considers cases concerning disputes between individuals and administrative organs, as well as disputes among administrative organs, while the Supreme Court considers all other cases. The judges are appointed by the [Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Sweden). In most cases, the Supreme Courts will only grant leave to appeal a case (*prövningstillstånd*) if the case involves setting a precedent in the interpretation of the law. Exceptions are issues where the Supreme Court is the court of first instance. Such cases include an application for a retrial of a criminal case in the light of new evidence, and prosecutions made against an incumbent minister of the Government for severe neglect of duty. If a lower court has to try a case which involves a question where there is no settled interpretation of the law, it can also refer the question to the relevant Supreme Court for an answer.

### Switzerland

In [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Switzerland), the [Federal Supreme Court of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Supreme_Court_of_Switzerland)[^cite_note-32] is the final court of appeals. Due to Switzerland's system of [direct democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_democracy), it has no authority to review the constitutionality of federal statutes, but the people can strike down a proposed law by referendum. According to settled case law, however, the Court is authorised to review the compliance of all Swiss law with certain categories of international law, especially the [European Convention of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Convention_of_Human_Rights).

### Sri Lanka

In [Sri Lanka](26750.md), the [Supreme Court of Sri Lanka](2830384.md) was created in 1972 after the adoption of a new Constitution. The Supreme Court is the highest and final superior court of record and is empowered to exercise its powers, subject to the provisions of the Constitution. The court rulings take precedence over all lower Courts. The Sri Lankan judicial system is complex blend of both common-law and civil-law. In some cases such as capital punishment, the decision may be passed on to the President of the Republic for clemency petitions. However, when there is 2/3 majority in the parliament in favour of president (as with present), the supreme court and its judges' powers become nullified as they could be fired from their positions according to the Constitution, if the president wants. Therefore, in such situations, Civil law empowerment vanishes.

## Mixed-system jurisdictions

### Canada

Main article: [Supreme Court of Canada](123468.md)



The courtroom of the [Supreme Court of Canada](123468.md)

Canada is a bi-jural country. Nine of the provinces use the common law, while the province of [Quebec](7954867.md) uses the civil law. Federal public law is based on the common law, but federal statute law must take into account both legal systems.[^cite_note-34]

The [Supreme Court of Canada](123468.md) was established in 1875. It is defined by the *[Constitution Act, 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_Act,_1867)* and by the *Supreme Court Act* as a "General Court of Appeal".[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] As a result, it can hear appeals on any legal issues considered by lower courts, on issues of constitutional law, federal law and provincial law. It can hear appeals involving the common law and the civil law, and has full authority to rule on those issues. The Court can hear appeals from the courts of appeal from the provinces and territories, and also appeals from the Federal Court of Appeal. The court's decisions are final and binding on the federal courts and the courts from all provinces and territories.

The court can also hear [reference questions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_question); requests directly from the federal cabinet asking about a question of law, typically the constitutionality of a major issue before it is heard in a trial court. Reference questions asked by a provincial cabinet to a province's highest court can be appealed as of right to the Supreme Court. Opinions given by the Supreme Court in answer to a reference question are not legally binding, but no government has ever ignored the opinion.

The Court is composed of the [Chief Justice of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_Canada) and eight [puisne justices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puisne). Three of the nine justices are required to come from the Bar or superior courts of Quebec, to ensure the Court has a strong membership in the civil law of Quebec. The remaining six justices come from the rest of Canada, traditionally three from [Ontario](22218.md), two from the [western provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada), and one from the [Atlantic provinces](71095.md). The Court is [institutionally bilingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_bilingualism_in_Canada). Parties can argue their cases in either English or French, and file written materials in either language. The Court will provide simultaneous interpretation for counsel and members of the public. It issues its judgments in both languages simultaneously.

Although created in 1875, the Supreme Court was not originally the final court of appeal. Canada was part of the British Empire, and appeals initially lay to the [Judicial Committee of the Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_Committee_of_the_Privy_Council) from the Supreme Court, and also from the provincial appellate courts, bypassing the Supreme Court. In 1933, the federal Parliament abolished such appeals in criminal matters. It was not until 1949 that all appeals to the Judicial Committee were abolished, although appeals which were pending could be decided by the Judicial Committee.[^cite_note-37]

### Philippines



Senior Justice of the Supreme Court of the Philippines [Marvic Leonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvic_Leonen)

The [Philippines](9208509.md) is generally considered the largest state with a hybrid [civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\))-[common law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_law) system, and the [Supreme Court](1335534.md) is heavily modeled after the [American Supreme Court](31737.md). This can be attributed to the fact that the Philippines was colonized by both [Spain](26667.md) and the [United States](3434750.md), and the system of laws of both nations strongly influenced the development of Philippine laws and jurisprudence. Even as the body of Philippine laws remain mostly codified, the Philippine Civil Code expressly recognizes that decisions of the Supreme Court "form part of the law of the land", belonging to the same class as [statutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute). The [1987 Philippine Constitution](19554336.md) also explicitly grants to the Supreme Court the power of [judicial review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_review) over laws and executive actions. The Supreme Court is composed of 1 [Chief Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_the_Philippines) and 14 [Associate Justices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_Justice_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_the_Philippines). The court sits either [en banc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/En_banc) or in divisions, depending on the nature of the case to be decided.

### South Africa

In [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_South_Africa), a "two apex" system existed from 1994 to 2013. The [Supreme Court of Appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Appeal_of_South_Africa) (SCA) was created in 1994 and replaced the [Appellate Division](643.md) of the Supreme Court of South Africa as the highest court of appeal in non-constitutional matters. The SCA was subordinate to the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_South_Africa), which is the highest court in matters involving the interpretation and application of the [Constitution](441610.md). But in August 2013 the Constitution was amended to make the Constitutional Court the country's single apex court, superior to the SCA in all matters, both constitutional and non-constitutional.

## Communist state jurisdictions

Main articles: [Communist state constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_state_constitution) and [Supreme judicial organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_judicial_organ)

In [communist states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_state), the [supreme state organ of power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_state_organ_of_power) (SSOP), as the sole repository of [unified state power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_state_power), holds ultimate judicial and interpretive authority alongside its [permanent organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_organ_of_the_supreme_state_organ_of_power). It delegates adjudication powers to a specialized organ known as the [supreme judicial organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_judicial_organ). However, the SJO remains structurally and politically subservient to the SSOP, which retains the power to issue binding legal interpretations and overrule judicial practice.

### People's Republic of China

In [the judicial system](15169660.md) of [mainland China](5405.md) the highest court of appeal is the [Supreme People's Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_People's_Court). This supervises the administration of justice by all subordinate "local" and "special" people's courts, and is the court of last resort for the whole People's Republic of China, except for Macau and Hong Kong.

The final power to interpret the law is vested in the [Standing Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_Committee_of_the_National_People's_Congress), the permanent organ of the [National People's Congress](38878740.md). This power includes the power to interpret the [basic laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_law) of [Hong Kong](13404.md) and [Macau](19068.md), the constitutional documents of the two [special administrative regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Administrative_Region_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) which are British-based [common law](#Common_law_jurisdictions) and Portuguese-based [civil law](#Civil_law_jurisdictions) jurisdictions respectively. This power is a legislative power and not a judicial one in that an interpretation by the NPCSC does not affect cases which have already been decided.

### Socialist Republic of Vietnam

The apex of the [judicial system of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_system_of_Vietnam) is made up of two institutions, the [Supreme People's Court of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_People's_Court_of_Vietnam) ([Vietnamese](32511.md): *Tòa án nhân dân tối cao*) and the [Supreme People's Procuracy of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_People's_Procuracy_of_Vietnam) ([Vietnamese](32511.md): *Viện kiểm sát nhân dân tối cao*). The Supreme People's Court is responsible for providing leadership to the Vietnamese court system, supervising the judicial process, and recommending bills to the National Assembly as appropriate under law. It is a court of final resort.[^cite_note-38] The Supreme People's Procuracy has functions such as acting as the prosecutor before the People's Courts.[^cite_note-39] The Supreme People's Procuracy has local and military subdivisions that include the district, provincial, and city levels.[^cite_note-40] Both are accountable to the [President of Vietnam](466686.md), the highest institution of government power in the country. The head of the Supreme People's Court of Vietnam is the Chief Justice, with the Council of Justices (*Hội đồng Thẩm phán*) being made up of the Chief Justice and 16 other judges.[^cite_note-41]
