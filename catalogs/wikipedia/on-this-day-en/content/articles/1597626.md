**Buddha Air Pvt. Ltd** ([Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language): बुद्ध एयर) is an [airline](1942.md) based in [Lalitpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalitpur,_Nepal), Nepal.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] It operates domestic flights within Nepal as well as international services to [Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Bahadur_Shastri_Airport) and [Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netaji_Subhas_Chandra_Bose_International_Airport) in [India](14533.md), from its main base in [Tribhuvan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_International_Airport), [Kathmandu](17168.md).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-FI-8] It was the largest domestic carrier in terms of passengers carried in 2023.[^cite_note-9]

## History



A Buddha Air [ ATR 42–300](346378.md) at [Pokhara Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhara_Airport)

The airline was established on 23 April 1996 as a [private limited company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_limited_company) by Surendra Bahadur Basnet, a retired [Supreme Court judge](2707227.md) and former government minister; and his son, [Birendra Bahadur Basnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birendra_Bahadur_Basnet).[^cite_note-Prof-10][^cite_note-Hist-11] The name of the airline is derived from the Sanskrit word *Buddha*, a title used for the much revered [Siddhartha Gautama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Buddha). Operations commenced on 11 October 1997 with a sightseeing flight to [Mount Everest](42179.md) using a brand new [Beechcraft 1900D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_1900D).[^cite_note-Hist-11] In 2001, the airline partnered with the [Bhutanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan) [flag carrier](465816.md) [Drukair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drukair) to charter flights during the peak tourist season.[^cite_note-12] Within ten years, the company had expanded to a fleet of seven 1900Ds.[^cite_note-13] In 2008, a loan from the [International Finance Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Finance_Corporation) allowed the company to expand further by purchasing two [ATR 42](346378.md) aircraft.[^cite_note-IFC-14] Buddha Air took delivery of its first 72-seat [ATR 72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_72) in June 2010.[^cite_note-Press_Release-15] In July 2026, the airline acquired 70 seater [ATR 72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_72)-600, the first of its kind in Nepal.[^cite_note-\:1-16]

Buddha Air became the first international airline to operate charter flights to [Paro Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paro_Airport) in Bhutan in August 2010.[^cite_note-17]

In 2011, Buddha Air began international flights from [Pokhara Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhara_Airport) to [Chaudhary Charan Singh International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaudhary_Charan_Singh_International_Airport) in [Lucknow](392095.md), [India](14533.md);[^cite_note-18] however, these flights were discontinued soon after.

**[Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netaji_Subhash_Chandra_Bose_International_Airport)** – Buddha Air first launched the [Kathmandu](17168.md)–[Kolkata](47905.md) route on 27 May 2019, operating three weekly flights between [Tribhuvan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_International_Airport) and [Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netaji_Subhas_Chandra_Bose_International_Airport) in [Kolkata](47905.md) with a flight duration of about 1 hour 30 minutes. The service was later suspended in December 2019 amid low demand and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic.

The airline resumed direct flights to [Kolkata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netaji_Subhash_Chandra_Bose_International_Airport) on 19 October 2025 after a six-year gap, initially operating three weekly return services (Sundays, Tuesdays and Thursdays) with ATR-72 turboprop aircraft. The flight time is approximately 1 hour 35 minutes, and this reinstatement reconnects Kathmandu and Kolkata as part of Buddha Air's international network.

## Hangar

Buddha Air is the first airline in Nepal, and one of few in South Asia, to have a closed-door hangar facility. Built at a cost of US\$2.5 million at the Tribhuvan International Airport, Buddha Air also provides aircraft maintenance facilities to other airlines, including the Bangladeshi airline [Novoair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novoair) which sends its ATR aircraft for maintenance at the hangar.[^cite_note-36]

Buddha Air is constructing a hangar that will be able to accommodate aircraft up to the size of an [Airbus A319](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A319) at [Pokhara International Airport](56868400.md).[^cite_note-37]

## Accidents and incidents

- On 11 November 2003, a scheduled Buddha Air flight from [Kathmandu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_International_Airport) enroute to [Biratnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biratnagar_Airport) mistakenly landed in [Bhairahawa Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautam_Buddha_Airport), owing to the air traffic and a subsequent slip-up in communication in Kathmandu.

  [^cite_note-38]

- On 25 September 2011, [Buddha Air Flight 103](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddha_Air_Flight_103) crashed while attempting to land at [Tribhuvan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_International_Airport) after a sightseeing flight of the Mount Everest region. All 19 passengers and crew on board the Beechcraft 1900D died.

  [^cite_note-NY-39]

  [^cite_note-BBC-40]

  &#x20;Out of the 19 passengers, 10 were Indian nationals, two were US nationals, one was Japanese and six were Nepalese. The crew of three were also Nepalese.

- On 18 December 2020, Buddha Air Flight 505, which took off from [Tribhuvan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_International_Airport) enroute to [Janakpur Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janakpur_Airport) landed in [Pokhara Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhara_Airport). According to the airline, there was a mix-up due to lapses in communication and failure to follow detailed standard operating procedures.

  [^cite_note-41]

  [^cite_note-42]

- On 16 December 2021, Buddha Air Flight 360, which took off from [Bharatpur Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatpur_Airport) to [Tribhuvan International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_International_Airport) with 19 passengers and three crew on board, made an emergency landing at [Bharatpur Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharatpur_Airport) when its left engine failed. Passengers were transferred to another flight and flown to Kathmandu.

  [^cite_note-43]

- On 11 July 2024, Buddha Air Flight 805, an [ATR 72-500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_72) on a flight from Kathmandu, skidded off the runway as it landed at Gautam Buddha Airport. All 59 passengers and all four crew members were safely evacuated from the aircraft without injuries.

  [^cite_note-44]

- On 2 January 2026, Buddha Air Flight 901, an [ATR 72-500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_72) on a flight from Kathmandu, suffered a [runway excursion](8731144.md) while landing at [Bhadrapur Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhadrapur_Airport), injuring seven people.

  [^cite_note-45]

## Sponsorships

In 2015, Buddha Air became the shirt sponsor of [Biratnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biratnagar) based football club [Morang XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morang_XI), who played in Nepal's highest football league, the [Nepal National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_National_League).[^cite_note-46]
