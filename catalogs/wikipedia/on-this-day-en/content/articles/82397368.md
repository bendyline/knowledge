General elections were held in the [Cook Islands](7067.md) on 12 August 2026 to determine the composition of the [19th Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_Cook_Islands_Parliament).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

Voting on the island of [Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nassau_\(Cook_Islands\)) in the electorate of [Pukapuka–Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pukapuka%E2%80%93Nassau) was delayed to 13 August due to adverse weather.[^cite_note-3]

## Electoral system

The 24 members of [Parliament](4504372.md) were elected from single-member electorates by [first-past-the-post voting](261709.md).[^cite_note-4]

There were approximately 11,000 registered voters for this election.[^cite_note-RNZ_260813-5][^cite_note-6]

## Results

Based on preliminary results, the Cook Islands Party looked to have won 12 out of the 24 seats of the parliament, the Cook Islands United Party won four, and the Democratic Party did not win any seats.[^cite_note-7] Cook Islands Party leader and incumbent prime minister Mark Brown won his Takuvaine-Tutakimoa seat and United Party leader Teariki Heather won his Murienua seat, but Democratic Party and opposition leader Tina Browne looked to have lost her Rakahanga seat to Cook Islands Party candidate Levi Hitarahera Teinaki.[^cite_note-RNZ_260813-5]

With the declaration of the final result on 26 August, Cook Islands Party candidate Tingika Elikana was found to have overcome a narrow deficit shown in the preliminary results to retain his seat, giving his party an outright majority of 13 seats in the parliament.[^cite_note-8] Independent candidates won seven seats, the Cook Islands United Party won four, and the Democratic Party was confirmed to have lost all of its seats.[^cite_note-RNZ_260827-9]

## Aftermath

Following these elections, the [Cook Islands Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_Islands_Party) secured an historic fifth consecutive term in government, having won a majority of 13 of the 24 seats in parliament. The party also has existing partnership agreements with the two independent candidates elected for [Atiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiu) and the candidate for [Mauke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauke_\(electorate\)). The three independent candidates elected for [Mangaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangaia) have since agreed to join the Cook Islands Party. This would give the Cook Islands Party-led government caucus a supermajority of 19 seats in parliament.[^cite_note-RNZ_260827-9][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] [Mark Brown](29659170.md) confirmed that he would retain all previous [Cabinet ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_Cook_Islands) that had won their seats in their positions.[^cite_note-RNZ_260827-9]

Opposition parties the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Cook_Islands\)) and [Cook Islands United Party](71031501.md) began talks of a potential merger.[^cite_note-15] Democratic Party leader [Tina Pupuke-Browne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Pupuke-Browne) has pledged a campaign review following the party's poor performance in the election.[^cite_note-16]

The leader of the Cook Islands United Party [Teariki Heather](25567244.md) nominated his brother Robert Stanley Heather, the MP for [Akaoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akaoa), for leader of [the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_opposition).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

Three electoral petitions challenging the results of the election in the [Avatiu–Ruatonga–Palmerston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatiu%E2%80%93Ruatonga%E2%80%93Palmerston), [Matavera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matavera_\(electorate\)) and [Pukapuka–Nassau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pukapuka%E2%80%93Nassau) electorates were filed with the [Ministry of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(Cook_Islands\)).[^cite_note-19]

In September 2026, the independent MP for [Mauke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauke_\(electorate\)), [Stephen Matapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Matapo?action=edit\&redlink=1), formally joined the Cook Islands Party, bringing their majority to 14.[^cite_note-20]

Further in September 2026, Mark Brown requested that the independent MP for [Manihiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manihiki_\(electorate\)), [George Angene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Angene), resign as a minister.[^cite_note-21] Having been a cabinet minister in the previous parliament as a Cook Islands Party MP, Angene retained his warrant following his re-election to parliament, despite having run against the party.[^cite_note-22] Angene refused to resign as minister,[^cite_note-23] though indicated that he would join the opposition.[^cite_note-24] Following this, the Cook Islands Party central committee voted unanimously to expel Angene from the party and Brown removed him from cabinet.[^cite_note-25]
