# Volhynian Bloody Sunday

On Sunday, 11 July 1943, death squads of the [Organization of Ukrainian Nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_Ukrainian_Nationalists)–[Ukrainian Insurgent Army](1045217.md) (OUN-UPA), aided by local Ukrainian peasants, simultaneously attacked at least 99 Polish settlements within [Wołyń Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wo%C5%82y%C5%84_Voivodeship_\(1921%E2%80%931939\)) of the [German-occupied Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-occupied_Poland).[^cite_note-FPAORG-1] It was a well-orchestrated attack on people gathered at Catholic churches for Sunday mass. The towns affected included [Kisielin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisielin) (the [Kisielin massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisielin_massacre)), [Poryck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poryck) (the [Poryck Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poryck_Massacre)), Chrynów (the [Chrynów massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chryn%C3%B3w_massacre)), Zabłoćce, and Krymn, while dozens of other towns were attacked on other dates; tens of churches and chapels were burned to the ground. The [Volhynian massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynian_massacres) spread over four prewar provinces, including [Wołyń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynia) with 40,000–60,000 victims, as well as [Lwów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lw%C3%B3w_Voivodeship), [Stanisławów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanis%C5%82aw%C3%B3w_Voivodeship), and [Tarnopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnopol_Voivodeship) provinces in [Lesser Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Poland) with 30,000-40,000 Poles murdered, for a total of 100,000 Polish victims of the UPA terror.[^cite_note-2]
