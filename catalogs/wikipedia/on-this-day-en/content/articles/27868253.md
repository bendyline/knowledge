The **Boko Haram insurgency** began in July 2009,[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-BBC_who-92] when the [militant](310790.md) [Islamist](15012.md) and [jihadist](7826589.md) [rebel group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rebel_groups_that_control_territory) [Boko Haram](23769768.md) started an armed [rebellion](513246.md) against the [government of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Nigeria).[^cite_note-Dowd_2015-5][^cite_note-Nossiter-93] Boko Haram targets both Muslims and Christians. Attacks are often directed at individuals perceived as supporting the Nigerian government or [Islamic State](46529902.md) after the split between them, including civil servants, local defense groups, Muslim leaders who oppose the group and other rebels like [al-Qaeda](1921.md) and Islamic State. Most attacks are not based on the religion of the victims.

[Boko Haram's initial uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Boko_Haram_uprising) failed, and its leader [Mohammed Yusuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Yusuf_\(Boko_Haram\)) was killed by the Nigerian government.[^cite_note-ju-94] He began the group in 2002 with a view of opposing western education with his followers.[^cite_note-m.guardian.ng-95] The movement consequently fractured into autonomous groups and started an insurgency, though rebel commander [Abubakar Shekau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abubakar_Shekau) managed to achieve a kind of primacy among the insurgents. Though challenged by internal rivals, such as Abu Usmatul al-Ansari's [Salafist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_jihadism) conservative faction and the [Ansaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansaru) faction, Shekau became the insurgency's *de facto* leader and mostly kept the different Boko Haram factions from fighting each other, instead focusing on overthrowing the Nigerian government.[^cite_note-in-96] Supported by other jihadist organizations including al-Qaeda and [al-Shabaab](8726463.md), Shekau's tactics were marked by extreme brutality and explicit targeting of civilians.[^cite_note-m.guardian.ng-95]

After years of fighting, the insurgents became increasingly aggressive and began to seize large areas in northeastern Nigeria. The violence escalated dramatically in 2014 with 10,849 deaths, while Boko Haram drastically expanded its territories.[^cite_note-NST-97][^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] At the same time, the insurgency spread to neighboring Cameroon, Chad, Mali, and Niger, thus becoming a major regional conflict in [sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md).[^cite_note-CTC_2021-1][^cite_note-Dowd_2015-5][^cite_note-101] Meanwhile, Shekau attempted to improve his international standing among jihadists by tacitly aligning with the Islamic State in March 2015, with Boko Haram becoming the "Islamic State's West Africa Province" (ISWAP).[^cite_note-CTC_2021-1]

The insurgents were driven back during the [2015 West African offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_West_African_offensive) by a Nigeria-led coalition of African and Western countries, forcing the Islamists to retreat into [Sambisa Forest](42612125.md) and bases at [Lake Chad](59314.md). Discontent about various issues consequently grew among Boko Haram. Dissidents among the movement allied themselves with ISWAP's central command and challenged Shekau's leadership, resulting in a violent split of the insurgents. Since then, Shekau and his group are generally referred to as "Boko Haram", whereas the dissidents continued to operate as ISWAP under [Abu Musab al-Barnawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Musab_al-Barnawi). The two factions consequently fought against each other while waging insurgencies against the local governments. After a period of reversals, Boko Haram and ISWAP launched offensives in 2018 and 2019, again growing in strength.[^cite_note-102]

When Boko Haram's insurgency was at its peak in the mid-2010s, it was the world's deadliest [terrorist organization](63578.md) in terms of the number of people it killed.[^cite_note-BBCDefeated-103][^cite_note-ITI-104][^cite_note-IEP-105] In a bid to ensure dialog between the government and the deadly sect, the [Jonathan administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Goodluck_Jonathan) set up a committee to grant amnesty to Boko Haram.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] Some details of the amnesty include the granting of pardons to Boko Haram fighters and also listening to different ethnic groups under the sect in a bid to end violence. This amnesty was rejected by the sect in an audio broadcast that was sent by its leader on the grounds that they are fighting to create an Islamic state in Nigeria's predominantly Muslim north and that it is the government that is committing atrocities against Muslims.[^cite_note-108]

In May 2021, ISWAP [attacked and overran Boko Haram militants in the Sambisa Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sambisa_Forest_\(2021\)) and Shekau was killed during the fighting, reportedly using a suicide vest.[^cite_note-109] In August, al-Barnawi was killed.[^cite_note-110] After Shekau's death masses of Boko Haram militants surrendered while others defected to ISWAP. According to the Nigerian Defence Forces, by July 2023 over 100,000 rebels and their family members surrendered.[^cite_note-111] A commander of the Joint Task Force expressed optimism that the Boko Haram crisis would end very soon, while advising the troops not to rest or give the terrorists a chance to recuperate, reorganise and start carrying out attacks, saying, "We are almost there, so let's maintain the momentum."[^cite_note-112]

### Nigerian statehood

Britain amalgamated both the northern and southern protectorates in 1914, about a decade after the defeat of the [Sokoto Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokoto_Caliphate) and other Islamic states by the British which were to constitute much of [northern Nigeria](2292852.md). Sir Frederick Lugard assumed office as governor of both protectorates in 1912. The aftermath of the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) saw Germany lose [its colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_former_German_colonies)—one of which was Cameroon—to French, Belgian and British mandates. Cameroon was divided in [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Cameroon) and [British parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Cameroons), the latter of which was further subdivided into [southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Cameroons) and northern parts. Following a [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite) in 1961, the Southern Cameroons elected to rejoin French Cameroon, while the Northern Cameroons opted to join Nigeria, a move which added to Nigeria's already large Muslim population.[^cite_note-113] The territory made up much of what is now northeastern Nigeria.

### Early religious conflict

[Religious conflict in Nigeria](43330495.md) dates [as far back as 1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_Kano_riot), and in the case of the town of [Tafawa Balewa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tafawa_Balewa,_Bauchi_State), to 1948.[^cite_note-114] The [Igbo massacre of 1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_anti-Igbo_pogrom) in the north that followed the [1966 counter-coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Nigerian_counter-coup) had as a dual cause the [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_people) officers' coup and pre-existing (sectarian) tensions between the Igbos and the local Muslims. This was a major factor in the [Biafran](44431.md) secession and the resulting [Nigerian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Civil_War).

### Maitatsine

In the late 1970s and early 1980s, there was a major Islamic uprising led by [Maitatsine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maitatsine) (Mohammed Marwa) and his followers, [Yan Tatsine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yan_Tatsine) that led to several thousand deaths. After Maitatsine's death in 1980, the movement continued for some five years more.

In the 1980s the erstwhile military ruler of Nigeria, General [Ibrahim Babangida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Babangida) enrolled Nigeria in the [Organisation of the Islamic Conference](47725.md). This was a move which aggravated religious tensions in the country, particularly among the Christian community.[^cite_note-115] In response, some in the Muslim community pointed out that [certain other African member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_Organisation_of_Islamic_Cooperation#Member_states) have smaller proportions of Muslims, as well as Nigeria's diplomatic relations with the [Holy See](13393.md).

### Establishment of sharia

&#x20; No sharia



Boko Haram in the Lake Chad Region, as of 14 March 2015

Since the return of democracy to Nigeria in 1999, [sharia](28840.md) has been instituted as a main body of civil and criminal law in 9 Muslim-majority and in some parts of 3 Muslim-plurality [states](226734.md), when [Zamfara State](2227664.md) Governor [Ahmad Rufai Sani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Rufai_Sani)[^cite_note-BBC_sharia-117] began the push for the institution of sharia at the state level of government. This was followed by controversy as to the would-be legal status of non-Muslims in the sharia system. A spate of Muslim-Christian riots soon emerged.

In the primarily Islamic northern states of Nigeria, a variety of Muslim groups and populations exist, who favour the nationwide introduction of sharia.[^cite_note-118] The demands of these populations have been at least partially upheld by the Nigerian federal government in 12 states, first in Zamfara State in 1999. The implementation has been widely attributed as being caused by the insistence of Sani.[^cite_note-BBC_sharia-117]

The 2002 death sentences of [Amina Lawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amina_Lawal) and [Safiya Hussaini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safiya_Hussaini) attracted international attention to what many saw as the harsh regime of these laws. These sentences were later overturned;[^cite_note-HRW_2002-119] the first execution was carried out in 2002.[^cite_note-HRW_2002-119]

#### Blasphemy and apostasy

Main article: [Blasphemy law in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blasphemy_law_in_Nigeria)

Twelve out of Nigeria's 36 states have [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) as the dominant religion. In 1999, those states chose to have sharia courts as well as customary courts.[^cite_note-IRF2008-120] A sharia court may treat blasphemy as deserving of several punishments up to and including execution.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] In many predominantly Muslim states, [conversion from Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_from_Islam) to another religion is illegal and often a [capital offence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Nigeria).[^cite_note-123]

### Demographic balance

According to a Nigerian study on [demographics](21386.md) and [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Nigeria), Muslims make up 50.5% of the population. Muslims mainly live in the north of the country; the majority of Nigerian Muslims are Sunnis. Christians are the second-largest religious group and make up 48.2% of the population. They predominate in the central and southern parts of the country.[^cite_note-124] For reasons of avoiding political controversy, questions of religion were forgone in the 2006 Nigerian census.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of the Boko Haram insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_Boko_Haram_insurgency).

### Beginnings and 2009 uprising

Main article: [2009 Boko Haram uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Boko_Haram_uprising)

[Mohammed Yusuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Yusuf_\(Boko_Haram\)) began Boko Haramin 2002 with a view of opposing western education with his followers.[^cite_note-m.guardian.ng-95] Boko Haram conducted its operations more or less peacefully during the first seven years of its existence.[^cite_note-CTC-127] That changed in 2009 when the Nigerian government launched an investigation into the group's activities following reports that its members were arming themselves.[^cite_note-Nigeria_accused_of_ignoring_sect_warnings_before_wave_of_killings-128][^cite_note-ju-94] Prior to that the government reportedly repeatedly ignored warnings about the increasingly militant character of the organisation, including that of a military officer.[^cite_note-Nigeria_accused_of_ignoring_sect_warnings_before_wave_of_killings-128]

Yusuf was arrested at his parent inlaws' house by the Nigerian military and subsequently handed over to the Nigerian police.[^cite_note-m.guardian.ng-95] When the government came into action, several members of the group were arrested in [Bauchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauchi_\(city\)), sparking deadly clashes with Nigerian security forces in Bauchi, [Maiduguri](2137952.md), [Potiskum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potiskum), and [Wudil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wudil), which led to the deaths of an estimated 700 people. Boko Haram fighters reportedly "used fuel-laden motorcycles" and "bows with poison arrows" to attack a police station.[^cite_note-Nossiter-93] Yusuf was killed while in police custody.[^cite_note-Nigerian_Islamist_attacks_spread-129][^cite_note-Over_100_dead_in_Nigerian_clashes-130][^cite_note-131] He was survived by four wives and 12 children, one of whom was [Abu Musab al-Barnawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Musab_al-Barnawi).[^cite_note-m.guardian.ng-95] After Yusuf's killing, [Abubakar Shekau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abubakar_Shekau) became the leader and held this position in January 2015.[^cite_note-Batolotta-132]

The movement consequently fractured into autonomous groups and started an insurgency, though rebel commander [Abubakar Shekau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abubakar_Shekau) managed to achieve a kind of primacy among the insurgents. Though challenged by internal rivals, such as Abu Usmatul al-Ansari's [Salafist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_jihadism) conservative faction and the [Ansaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansaru) faction, Shekau became the insurgency's *de facto* leader and mostly kept the different Boko Haram factions from fighting each other, instead focusing on overthrowing the Nigerian government.[^cite_note-in-96] Supported by other jihadist organizations including [al-Qaeda](1921.md) and [al-Shabaab](8726463.md), Shekau's tactics were marked by extreme brutality and explicit targeting of civilians.[^cite_note-m.guardian.ng-95]

### 2010 resurgence

Nearly six months after the death of Yusuf, the group carried out its first terrorist attack in Borno in January 2010, killing four people.[^cite_note-133] During the following few years, the violence escalated in terms of both frequency and intensity. On 7 September a [prison break in Bauchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauchi_prison_break) freed more than 700 Boko Haram militants, replenishing their force. On 24 December Boko Haram [used four bombs to kill 32 people in Jos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Jos_and_Maiduguri_attacks), Plateau State. On the same day, they killed six people in attacks against churches in Maiduguri.

### 2011

On 29 May 2011, a few hours after [Goodluck Jonathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodluck_Jonathan) was sworn in as [president](516088.md), [several bombings purportedly by Boko Haram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2011_Nigeria_bombings) killed 15 and injured 55. On 15 June the federal government began a military effort to counter the growing threat of Boko Haram's insurgency. With 21 Armoured Brigade of the [Nigerian Army](14943749.md) as its nucleus, Joint Task Force Operation Restore Order marked the start of the army's lengthy counter-insurgency campaign against Boko Haram.[^cite_note-134] Results were mixed, and the army was criticised for being too kinetic.[^cite_note-135]

On 16 June, Boko Haram claimed responsibility for the [Abuja police headquarters bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Abuja_police_headquarters_bombing), the first known [suicide attack](18835454.md) in Nigeria. On 26 August the [United Nations building in Abuja was bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Abuja_United_Nations_bombing), signifying the first time that Boko Haram attacked an international organisation. On 4 November, [Boko Haram carried out attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_2011_Nigeria_attacks) in [Damaturu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damaturu), [Yobe](2139960.md), and in Maiduguri, killing over a hundred people, and on 22 and 23 December [clashing with security forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_2011_Nigeria_clashes), resulting in at least 68 deaths. On [25 December, Boko Haram attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_2011_Nigeria_attacks) several churches with bombings and shootings.

### 2012

In [January 2012, Boko Haram attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2012_Northern_Nigeria_attacks) [Mubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubi_\(town\)), [Yola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yola,_Adamawa) and [Gombi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombi) – all in [Adamawa State](905832.md) – in addition to Maiduguri and [Kano](508782.md). During the same month, Shekau appeared in a video posted on [YouTube](3524766.md). According to [Reuters](18998750.md), Shekau took control of the group after Yusuf's death in 2009.[^cite_note-reuters20120112-136] Authorities had previously believed that Shekau died during the 2009 uprising.[^cite_note-france24-20120113-137]

By early 2012, the group was responsible for over 900 deaths.[^cite_note-nytpopular-138] On 8 March, a small [Special Boat Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Navy) team and the Nigerian Army [attempted to rescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokoto_hostage_rescue_attempt) two hostages, Briton Chris McManus and Italian Franco Lamolinara, being held in [Sokoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokoto_\(city\)) by members of Boko Haram. Both hostages were killed by their captors before or during the rescue attempt. All the hostage takers were reportedly killed.[^cite_note-139]

On 8 April, at least [38 people were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2012_Kaduna_bombings) by a suicide car bomber in [Kaduna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaduna). On 24 June, about 40 inmates escaped during a prison break in [Damaturu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damaturu). On 17 June, at least [12 people were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2012_Kaduna_church_bombings) by three bombings of churches in [Kaduna State](2227637.md). On 7 August, [19 people were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deeper_Life_Bible_Church_shooting) in a mass shooting in [Kogi State](299399.md). On 1–2 November, at least [25 men were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Polytechnic,_Mubi_attack) at the [Federal Polytechnic, Mubi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Polytechnic,_Mubi). On 25 December, [12 people were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_2012_shootings_in_Northern_Nigeria) in [Potiskum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potiskum) and Maiduguri. On 28 December, 15 people were killed in a village in northeastern Nigeria.

### 2013

On 8 February 2013 gunmen killed at least nine polio vaccinators in Kano.[^cite_note-140] On 18 March, a suicide car bomber [rammed a bus in Kano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Kano_bus_bombing), killing over 20 people. On 16 and 17 April, [dozens of civilians were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Baga_massacre) during a battle in [Baga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baga,_Borno_State) between Boko Haram and the Nigerian Army.

In May 2013, government forces launched an offensive in the Borno region in an attempt to dislodge Boko Haram fighters after a state of emergency was called. The state of emergency applied to the states of Borno, Yobe, and Adamawa.[^cite_note-141] The offensive had initial success, but the Boko Haram rebels were able to regain their strength. Several authors have cited the frailty of the Nigerian security system and corruption in the management of the military as some of the factors that allowed Boko Haram to regain their strength during this period.[^cite_note-142]

On 6 July, Boko Haram [massacred 42 students in Yobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamudo_school_massacre),[^cite_note-Telegraph-143] bringing the school year to an early end. On 5 August, Boko Haram launched dual attacks on [Bama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bama,_Nigeria) and Malam Fatori, leaving 35 dead.[^cite_note-144] On 11 August, Boko Haram [killed 44 people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konduga_mosque_shooting) in a mass shooting at a mosque in [Konduga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konduga), Borno.[^cite_note-145] On 6 October, the Nigerian Army won [a battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Damboa) against Boko Haram in [Damboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damboa), Borno. On 2 November in Borno, gunmen attacked a convoy returning from a wedding, killing over 30 people.

### 2014

#### Chibok kidnapping and ambush

On 15 April 2014, terrorists [abducted about 276 teenage female pupils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibok_schoolgirls_kidnapping) from a secondary school in [Chibok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibok), Borno.[^cite_note-146] The abduction was widely attributed to Boko Haram.[^cite_note-147] It was reported that the group had taken the girls to neighbouring Cameroon and Chad where they were to be [sold into marriages](19333662.md) at a price below a dollar each. The abduction of another eight girls was reported later. These kidnappings raised public protests, with some protesters holding placards bearing the Twitter tag #BringBackOurGirls, which had caught international attention.[^cite_note-148]

*[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* reported that the British [Royal Air Force](25679.md) (RAF)conducted [Operation Turus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Turus) in response to the Chibok kidnapping. A source involved with the operation told *The Observer* "The girls were located in the first few weeks of the RAF mission", and "We \[RAF] offered to rescue them, but the Nigerian government declined", this was because it viewed any action to be taken as a "national issue", and for it to be resolved by Nigerian intelligence and security services, the source added that the girls were then tracked by the aircraft as they were dispersed into progressively smaller groups over the following months.[^cite_note-149] Several countries pledged support to the Nigerian government and to help their military with intelligence gathering on the whereabouts of the girls and the operational camps of Boko Haram. On 13–14 May, Boko Haram [ambushed Nigerian soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibok_ambush) who were searching for the kidnapped girls.

#### Jos bombings

On 20 May, two bombs in [Jos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos), Plateau State, [were detonated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Jos_bombings), killing at least 118 people and injuring more than 56 others. The bombs exploded 30 minutes apart, one at a local marketplace at approximately 3\:00 and the second in a parking lot next to a hospital at approximately 3\:30, where rescuers responding to the first accident were killed.[^cite_note-Fox-150] Though no group or individual claimed responsibility, the attacks were attributed to Boko Haram.[^cite_note-BBC21-151]

First responders were unable to reach the scene of the accidents, as "thousands of people were fleeing the scene in the opposite direction". The bombs had been positioned to kill as many people as possible, regardless of religion, which differed from previous attacks in which non-Muslims were targeted. The bombers were reported to have used a "back-to-back blast" tactic, in which an initial bomb explodes at a central location, and another explodes a short time later with the intent to kill people working to rescue the wounded.[^cite_note-Reuters-152]

#### Maiduguri bombings

Boko Haram militants increased their attacks on cities and towns in northern Nigeria and captured part of the northeast. These attacks included bombings in Maiduguri in January, [in July](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2014_Maiduguri_bombing), and November.[^cite_note-153]

#### Escalation in fighting



Victims of a Boko Haram bombing in November 2014

The increasing intensity of the insurgency prompted the government to launch an offensive, and with the help of Chad, Niger, and Cameroon, they recaptured many areas that were formerly under the control of Boko Haram.[^cite_note-BBC_launch-154][^cite_note-155]

In late 2014, Boko Haram seized control of Bama, according to the town's residents.[^cite_note-156] In December, it was reported that "people too elderly to flee [Gwoza](24822782.md) Local Government Area were being rounded up and taken to two schools where the militants opened fire on them." Over 50 elderly people in Bama were killed.[^cite_note-157] A "gory" video was released of insurgents shooting over a hundred civilians in a school dormitory in Bama.[^cite_note-158]

### 2015

#### Baga massacre

Between 3 and 7 January 2015, Boko Haram attacked [Baga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baga,_Borno_State) and [killed up to 2,000 people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Baga_massacre),[^cite_note-159] perhaps the largest massacre by Boko Haram.[^cite_note-160]

#### Maiduguri bombing

On 10 January, 19 people were killed in [a suicide bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2015_Maiduguri_suicide_bombing) at a market in Maiduguri.[^cite_note-161] The city is at the heart of the Boko Haram insurgency.[^cite_note-162] On 12 January, Boko Haram [carried out an unsuccessful attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kolofata_\(2015\)) on a Cameroonian army base.

#### Counter-offensive against Boko Haram



Map of Boko Haram's territorial control on 10 April, over 2 months after the start of the [2015 West African offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_West_African_offensive)

On 23 January, a coalition of military forces from Nigeria, Chad, Cameroon, and Niger began a counter-insurgency [campaign against Boko Haram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_West_African_offensive).[^cite_note-163] In the early hours of 25 January, Boko Haram launched a major assault on Maiduguri.[^cite_note-164] On 26 January, CNN reported that the attack on Maiduguri by "hundreds of gunmen" had been repelled, but the nearby town of [Monguno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monguno) was captured by Boko Haram.[^cite_note-165] The Nigerian Army repelled another attack on Maiduguri on 31 January.[^cite_note-166]

On 4 February, the [Chad Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Chad) killed over 200 Boko Haram militants.[^cite_note-167] On 4 and 5 February, Boko Haram carried out [a massacre in Fotokol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fotokol_massacre), Far North Region, Cameroon, killing 81 civilians, 13 Chadian soldiers and 6 Cameroonian soldiers.[^cite_note-168] On 6 February, Boko Haram attacked [Bosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosso,_Niger) and [Diffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffa) in Niger. On 15 February, [a suicide bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2015_Nigeria_bombings) occurred in [Damaturu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damaturu). On 17 February, the Nigerian military retook [Monguno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monguno) in a coordinated air and ground assault.[^cite_note-169] On 22 February, a suicide bombing occurred in Potiskum. On 24 February, suicide bombings occurred in Potiskum and Kano. On 2 March, the Nigerian Armed Forces defeated Boko Haram in the [Battle of Konduga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Konduga_\(2015\)).

On 7 March, Shekau pledged allegiance to the [Islamic State](9087364.md) (IS) via an audio message posted on the organisation's Twitter account.[^cite_note-Boko_Haram_joins_ISIL-69][^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] Nigerian Army spokesperson Sami Usman Kukasheka said the pledge was a sign of weakness and that Shekau was like a "drowning man".[^cite_note-BBC_launch-154] That same day, [five suicide bomb blasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2015_Maiduguri_suicide_bombing) left 54 dead and 143 wounded.[^cite_note-172] On 12 March, IS spokesman [Abu Mohammad al-Adnani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Mohammad_al-Adnani) released an audiotape in which he welcomed the pledge of allegiance, and described it as an expansion of the group's caliphate to [West Africa](67393.md).[^cite_note-BokoHaramIS-43]

Following its declaration of loyalty to IS, Boko Haram was designated as the group's "West Africa Province" ([Islamic State – West Africa Province](46529902.md), or ISWAP), and Shekau was appointed as its first [vali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C4%81li) (governor). IS started to support Boko Haram but also began to interfere in its internal matters. For example, IS's central leadership attempted to reduce Boko Haram's brutality toward civilians and internal critics, as Shekau's ideology was "too extreme even for the Islamic State".[^cite_note-Province_vs_Shekau-68]



[Niger Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger_Army) soldiers during [counter-insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-insurgency) operations against Boko Haram in March

On 16 March, Boko Haram lost control of Bama.[^cite_note-BBCTide-173][^cite_note-174] On 24 March, residents of [Damasak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damasak), Borno, said that Boko Haram had taken more than 400 women and children from the town as they fled from coalition forces who [retook the area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Damasak) and [found a mass grave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damasak_massacre) of Boko Haram victims.[^cite_note-175] On 27 March, the Nigerian army captured [Gwoza](24822782.md), which was believed to be the location of Boko Haram headquarters.[^cite_note-BBCGwoza-176] On the [presidential election day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Nigerian_general_election), 28 March, Boko Haram killed 41 people, including a legislator, to discourage hundreds from voting.[^cite_note-177]

The Nigerian authorities said that they had taken back 11 of the 14 districts previously controlled by Boko Haram.[^cite_note-BBCTide-173][^cite_note-178] In April, four Boko Haram camps in the [Sambisa Forest](42612125.md) were overrun by the Nigerian military who freed nearly 300 females.[^cite_note-179] Boko Haram forces were believed to have retreated to the [Mandara Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandara_Mountains), along the [Cameroon–Nigeria border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon%E2%80%93Nigeria_border).[^cite_note-180] In May, the Nigerian military announced that they had released about 700 women from camps in Sambisa Forest.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]

On 12 July, two female suicide bombers wearing burqas killed 13 people in Fotokol. In response, the governor of Cameroon's Far North [banned the Islamic veil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burqa_by_country) and burqa. In August, it was reported that over a thousand deaths had occurred since the inauguration of President [Muhammadu Buhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammadu_Buhari).[^cite_note-183] On 10 October, suicide bombers [attacked a market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baga_Sola_bombings) and refugee camp in [Baga Sola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baga_Sola), Chad. On 28 October, it was announced that Nigerian troops had rescued 338 people from Boko Haram near the group's Sambisa Forest stronghold. Of those rescued, 192 were children and 138 were women.[^cite_note-184]

In December Buhari claimed that Boko Haram was "technically defeated,"[^cite_note-BBCDefeated-103] and it was reported that 1,000 women had been rescued from Boko Haram in January 2016.[^cite_note-Suicide-185][^cite_note-Lamb-186] On 5 December, four female suicide bombers [attacked a market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_2015_Chad_suicide_bombings) on the Chadian side of Lake Chad. On 28 December, female suicide bombers killed over 55 people in [Madagali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madagali) and Maiduguri.[^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188]

#### American military support

In early October 2015, the United States military deployed 300 troops to Cameroon, with the approval of the Cameroonian government, with the primary mission of providing intelligence support to local forces and conducting reconnaissance flights.[^cite_note-US1-78][^cite_note-US2-79] The troops also oversaw a program to transfer American military vehicles to the [Cameroonian Army](5455.md) to aid in their fight against Islamist militants.[^cite_note-189]

As of May 2016, U.S. personnel were involved in [drone](337755.md) operations from [Garoua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garoua), Far North Region, Cameroon, to help provide intelligence in the region to assist local forces. There were additional drone operations based out of Niger.[^cite_note-190] U.S. Army soldiers in Cameroon are also providing IED awareness training to the country's infantry forces.[^cite_note-191]

### 2016



[Civilian Joint Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_Joint_Task_Force) fighters of [Michika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michika) in 2016

On 25 January, four Boko Haram suicide bombers [killed over 30 people in Bodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Bodo_bombings), Far North, Cameroon.[^cite_note-192] On 30 January, at least [86 people were killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Dalori_attack) by Boko Haram in [Dalori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalori), Borno. On 9 February, two young female suicide bombers [killed at least 60 people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dikwa_suicide_bombings) at an [internally displaced persons](427408.md)' (IDP) camp in [Dikwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dikwa), Borno. On 16 March, two female suicide bombers [killed 22 people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2016_Maiduguri_bombings) on the outskirts of Maiduguri.

#### Boko Haram-IS infighting and loss of territory

As Boko Haram's power waned, Shekau's leadership was increasingly criticised among Boko Haram and IS central command. These elements repeatedly attempted to convince Shekau to change his tactics or his extreme ideas (such as considering everyone an apostate who has not openly sided with him, including all Muslims). Shekau refused to budge and openly disobeyed "Caliph" [Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr_al-Baghdadi) in regard to various matters. IS and parts of Boko Haram eventually came to the conclusion that this was no longer tolerable, whereupon Shekau was removed from his position as vali of ISWAP in August. [Abu Musab al-Barnawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Musab_al-Barnawi), a son of Boko Haram founder Mohammed Yusuf, was appointed as his successor.&#x20;

This event resulted in an open split among the Nigerian insurgents. Shekau refused to accept his dismissal, rallied a large number of supporters, and violently opposed Barnawi and IS central command. In turn, Barnawi and those who were loyal to him declared Shekau's group *[Khawarij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khawarij)*.[^cite_note-Province_vs_Shekau-68] The two insurgent factions subsequently became fully separate organizations, with Shekau's followers adopting their old name "Jamā'at Ahl as-Sunnah lid-Da'wah wa'l-Jihād" (outsiders refer to this faction as "Boko Haram"), whereas Barnawi's forces continued to operate as ISWAP. The two groups are generally hostile and fight each other, though it is possible that they occasionally cooperate against their common enemies.[^cite_note-offemsive_jan_2019-193]

On 31 August, Major General Lucky Irabor stated that the militants only controlled a few villages and towns near Lake Chad and in Sambisa Forest. He further stated that the military expected recapturing the final strongholds of the group within weeks.[^cite_note-194] On 24 December Buhari said that Boko Haram had been ousted from their last stronghold in the Sambisa Forest, effectively reducing Boko Haram to an insurgent force.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196][^cite_note-last_Boko_Haram_base_taken-197] This victory left Boko Haram without any territorial holdings.[^cite_note-not_yet_over-198]

### 2018

On 16 February, three suicide bombers [killed about 20 people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Konduga_bombings) in Konduga. On 19 February, Boko Haram [kidnapped 110 schoolgirls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dapchi_schoolgirls_kidnapping) from [Dapchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dapchi), Yobe. By March, two main insurgent factions were still active and continued to wage an insurgency campaign against the government: the followers of Shekau operated mainly in southern Borno, while the faction of al-Barnawi was mostly located around Lake Chad.[^cite_note-Dialogue_End-47]

On 26 April, Boko Haram bombers killed at least four civilians in the outskirts of Maiduguri. A subsequent gun battle and tear gas launched by security forces repelled the attackers but left two officers wounded and several others injured.[^cite_note-212] On 1 May, two suicide bombers [killed dozens of people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Mubi_suicide_bombings) in Mubi.

On 15 July, hundreds of Nigerian soldiers went missing after Boko Haram forces overran an army base in northeastern Nigeria. Less than 100 Nigerian soldiers returned after the attack. The attack came 24 hours after Boko Haram ambushed a military convoy in neighbouring Borno. The attack on the base resulted in a battle that lasted over an hour, it is unknown if there were any casualties in the assault, a local pro-government militia said the military had sustained some casualties. This attack marked Boko Haram's first major gain since 2015.[^cite_note-213]

On 8 September, ISWAP fighters captured Gudumbali in central Borno, marking their first major gain in nearly two years.[^cite_note-214] The next day, ISWAP released a video showing footage from the combat.[^cite_note-215] In late December, ISWAP launched another offensive and captured Baga.[^cite_note-offemsive_jan_2019-193]

In November, fighting in and around Lake Chad intensified, beginning the [Chad Basin campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_Basin_campaign_\(2018%E2%80%932020\)) which lasted until February 2020. On 18 November, ISWAP fighters attacked a military base in Metele, Borno, killing at least 118 soldiers while at least 153 others were missing after the attack. The militants also seized tanks, armored vehicles, artillery, weapons, and ammunition.[^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219]

Presence and influence of [ISWAP](9087364.md) and Boko Haram in northern Nigeria, Cameroon, and Niger in early 2019, after the rebels' victories during the [early Chad Basin campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_Basin_campaign_\(2018%E2%80%932020\))

Five insurgents were killed and seven hostages were rescued; several insurgents were wounded but managed to escape.[^cite_note-233] On 17 September, Boko Haram raided a village in Lake Chad region in Chad; several civilians were abducted.[^cite_note-234] On 18 September, Nigerian military carried out an air operation against Boko Haram in Kassa Kura in Maiduguri. Sixteen insurgents were killed, 38 were arrested, and their ammunition was seized. Several insurgents escaped with wounds.[^cite_note-235] On 19 September, Chadian military attacked Boko Haram in the village of Barkalam near the border with Nigeria; 15 Boko Haram fighters were killed in the fighting, and 12 hostages were rescued.&#x20;

### 2021

On 3 January 2021, [Multinational Joint Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_Joint_Task_Force) (MNJTF) carried a sweep around [Kolofata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolofata). A civilian was killed by Boko Haram in Kolofata area the same morning.[^cite_note-ha-251]

On 6 January, Boko Haram infiltrated [Geidam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geidam), Yobe. Insurgents were sighted at outskirts of Geidam at 1 pm. Boko Haram insurgents gathered at strategic locations such as Geidam market with a coordinated plan at 5\:30 pm, soon after they positioned at those locations they attacked the town. On 14 January, ISWAP militants attacked Garin Gada village in Yobe, killing at least two civilians, whilst also raiding and looting food from the village.[^cite_note-258] On 17 January, seven IEDs were activated against a Nigerian Army convoy of APCs and other vehicles, escorted by a foot patrol in Gorgi, Borno. Over 30 soldiers were killed. Three vehicles were destroyed, and an armored vehicle, weapons and ammunition were seized.[^cite_note-terrorism-info.org.il-259] On 18 January, ISWAP operatives exchanged fire and activated several IEDs against Nigerian soldiers in Matari, about 50 km west of Maiduguri. At least 20 soldiers were killed. Two ATVs were destroyed. In addition, two ATVs, an APC, weapons and ammunition were seized.[^cite_note-terrorism-info.org.il-259] On 22 January, ISWAP operatives ambushed and fired machine guns at a Nigerian Army patrol in Borno. Seven soldiers were killed and others were wounded. In addition, an ATV, weapons and ammunition were seized.[^cite_note-260] On 31 January, two attacks took place in northern Nigeria. One in the village of Chabal, leaving two policemen dead and two abducted. Two high-profile ISWAP commanders, Modu Sulum and Ameer Modu Borzogo, fled along with some fighters during intense fighting, but several other commanders and fighters were killed, and many hostages were rescued.[^cite_note-263]

On 5 February, ISWAP operatives ambushed Nigerian soldiers in the Goniri region, near the [Niger-Nigeria border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger-Nigeria_border). The two sides exchanged fire. ISIS operatives seized vehicles, weapons and ammunition.[^cite_note-gj-264] On 7 February, bandits raided two villages in northwest Kaduna State, leading to the deaths of 19 people.[^cite_note-265] On 8 February, ISWAP operatives attacked a Nigerian Army checkpoint in Monguno, about 70 km from the Nigeria-Chad-Cameroon tri-border area. The second half of February was similarly violent. On 16 February, a group of Nigerian soldiers was attacked in a village in Borno. There was an exchange of fire. ### 2022



Areas with presence of Boko haram and ISWAP in 2022

2022 saw gains for Nigerian forces, including capturing or killing several top leaders. Spring saw Nigerian forces kill a succession of insurgent leaders. On May 15 it was reported that Boko Haram commander Abubakar Sarki and several terrorists were killed during a clash with the Nigerian army in the Sambisa Forest.[^cite_note-294] On May 23 Boko Haram fighters killed around 40 farmers.[^cite_note-295] On May 30 MNJTF troops alongside Operation HADIN KAI stormed the general area of Tumbun Rago, Tumbun Dilla and Jamina settlements and succeeded in eliminating over 25 terrorists. The troops also recovered one AK-47 rifle, one anti-aircraft gun, and hundreds of assorted ammunition.[^cite_note-296]

In June, a group of hunters ambushed and killed a Boko Haram commander and his deputy while wounding several of their escaping fighters.[^cite_note-297] On 1 June a rivalry clash between ISWAP and the Jamā’at Ahl as-Sunnah lid-Da’wah wa’l-Jihād led to the killing of a commander and scores of his fighters.[^cite_note-298] On 5 June members of the ISWAP [attacked St Francis Xavier Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owo_church_attack) in [Owo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owo) killing at least 50 parishioners and injuring 87.[^cite_note-299][^cite_note-300][^cite_note-301] On 7 June it was reported that the MNJTF killed 805 jihadis on Lake Chad's islands and neighboring areas.

August saw further successes against insurgents. On 3 August it was reported that military airstrikes killed Boko Haram commander Alhaji Modu and 27 other terrorists on Mandara Mountain.[^cite_note-302] On 6 August Abdulkarim Faca-Faca, who was among the masterminds of the attack on President Muhammadu Buhari's advance convoy to [Katsina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katsina_\(city\)), was killed along with eight of his gang members by air strikes.[^cite_note-303] On 10 August it was reported that bombardments by two Nigerian Air Force fighter jets had killed many terrorists, among them leader Alhaji Shanono, operating in Kaduna State. According to a military source, the terrorists were caught off guard during a battle between ISWAP and Boko Haram.[^cite_note-304] On 16 August the Chadian army killed 10 Boko Haram terrorists during clashes around Bol in the Lake Chad area.[^cite_note-305] On 23 August the Nigerian Air Force bombarded the enclave of an ISWAP leader Fiya Ba Yuram in the Sambisa Forest. The airstrikes hit some specific targets in the Tunbuns and Sambisa, killing scores of terrorists hiding in the enclaves.[^cite_note-306] On 26 August terrorist commander Uzaifa was killed in an air raid on criminal enclaves in Sambisa Forest and the Tumbuns.[^cite_note-307] Between 30 and 31 August Nigerian fighter jets killed 49 Boko Haram fighters in separate camps.[^cite_note-308]

On 1 September, 70 suspected Boko Haram members drowned in a river while trying to escape air bombardment in Sheruri, Borno.[^cite_note-309] On 5 September Nigerian troops and aircraft killed 200 Boko Haram terrorists including five commanders by the names of Abou Hauwa, Amir Shettima, Akura Buri, Abou Zainab and Abou Idris.[^cite_note-310] On 12 September top Boko Haram commander and chief executioner Bashir Bulabuduwaye surrendered to the Nigerian army.[^cite_note-311] On 23 November dozens of soldiers were killed near Lake Chad.[^cite_note-312]

### 2023

2023 was relatively quiet, seeing limited insurgent activity. On 28 March Boko Haram's chief bomb maker, Awana Gaidam, was killed by his own IED.[^cite_note-313] On 18 September a group of terrorists held peace talks with people from Fankama, Katsina.[^cite_note-314] [Two attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Yobe_State_attacks) on 30 and 31 October in [Geidam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geidam) killed at least 37 people. The first attack was a shooting that killed 17. The second attack was executed with a land mine, which killed at least 20 people attending the burials of the victims of the shooting.[^cite_note-315] On 1 and 3 November, in Operation Hadarin Daji, the Nigerian Air Force carried out numerous airstrikes against Boko Haram hideouts, destroying two bases and killing several terrorists.[^cite_note-316]

### 2024

In the beginning of 2024, the Islamic State announced the beginning of a campaign called "kill them wherever you find them". This campaign lasted from 1 to 10 January and saw a rise of attacks claimed by ISWAP.[^cite_note-317] On 3 February ISWAP killed 4 policemen at [Nganzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nganzai), Borno. A security spokesperson who witnessed the massacre commented "Some of the police officers were also lucky to have survived the onslaught of the terror attack, but the unfortunate four officers paid the supreme price while on active duty". The terrorists left the area before the Nigerian military could arrive.[^cite_note-318]

On 29 June at least 32 people were killed in attacks thought to have been carried out by female suicide bombers in Gwoza.[^cite_note-319][^cite_note-320] On 31 July at least 19 people were killed in a suicide attack by Boko Haram at a market in Konduga.[^cite_note-321] On 22 August suspected Boko Haram gunmen killed 13 farmers in Shirore.[^cite_note-322]

[Open Doors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Doors) estimates that 4,118 Christians were killed for their faith in Nigeria in 2024, the highest number globally.[^cite_note-323]

### 2025

On 21 June 2025, a suspected female suicide bomber killed at least 12 people and injured 30 others at a Friday night fish market in Borno State.[^cite_note-324] On 5 September 63 people were killed, including seven soldiers, and others were reported missing in attacks by Boko Haram in Darul Jamal, Borno.[^cite_note-325] In October 2025, Boko Haram seized Kirawa, Borno, burning the district head's palace, a military barracks and dozens of homes and forcing more than 5,000 people to flee into Cameroon. Residents described Kirawa as deserted and under insurgent control, an attack that followed recent overruns of army positions in the region and prompted calls for urgent military reinforcements.[^cite_note-326]

Between 5 and 8 November Boko Haram fighters [launched a naval invasion](81558859.md) against ISWAP bases across the various islands in Lake Chad. The attacks were led by Hassan Buduma and Mohd Hassan.[^cite_note-327] Clashes left at least 4 Boko Haram attackers dead and 200 ISWAP members killed.[^cite_note-328]

On 25 December the United States [carried out a strike](83205369.md) against ISWAP. U.S. President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) ordered the strike to protect Christians from [religious violence in northern Nigeria](43330495.md). The U.S. strike was the first direct foreign military intervention in the insurgency.[^cite_note-nn-10]

### 2026

On 3 February 2026, gunmen of the IS-affiliated militant group [Lakurawa](78327630.md) attacked the villages of Nuku and Woro in [Kwara State](2227645.md) after residents rejected their demands to embrace sharia. A least 162 residents were killed, and several buildings were burned.[^cite_note-329][^cite_note-330][^cite_note-331][^cite_note-332][^cite_note-333] On 5 May Boko Haram militants attacked a Chadian military post on Barka Tolorom island, killing 23 and injuring 26.[^cite_note-334]

On 15 May Trump announced that U.S. and Nigerian forces eliminated ISWAP second-in-command Abu-Bilal al-Minuki during a joint mission.[^cite_note-335] On 3 July, the U.S. withdrew most of its deployed forces while continuing to provide intelligence support to Nigeria at the request of its government.[^cite_note-336]

## Other issues

### Possible causes

Northern Nigeria was made up of [Sahelian states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahelian_kingdoms) distinguished by a deep-rooted Islamic tradition that had shaped their cultural and political identity for centuries. These were feudal and conservative, with rigid [caste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste) and class systems and large slave populations.[^cite_note-337] Furthermore, the north failed until 1936 to outlaw slavery.[^cite_note-338] Possibly due to geographical factors, many (but not necessarily all) southern tribes, particularly those on the coast, had made contact with Europeans—unlike the north, which was engaged mainly with the Arab world and not Europe. Due to the system of [indirect rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indirect_rule), the British were happy to pursue a limited course of engagement with the [emirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir).[^cite_note-339]

While traditionalist elites in the north remained wary of Western education,[^cite_note-340][^cite_note-341][^cite_note-342] their southern counterparts frequently embraced it—often sending their sons overseas to study. In time, a considerable developmental and educational gap grew between the south and the north.[^cite_note-343] Even in 2014, northern states lagged behind in literacy, school attendance and educational achievement.[^cite_note-344]

Chris Kwaja, a Nigerian university lecturer and researcher, asserts "religious dimensions of the conflict have been misconstrued as the primary driver of violence when, in fact, disenfranchisement and inequality are the root causes". Nigeria, he points out, has laws giving regional political leaders the power to qualify people as 'indigenes' (original inhabitants) or not. It determines whether citizens can participate in politics, own land, obtain a job, or attend school. The system is abused widely to ensure political support and to exclude others. Muslims have often been denied indigene certificates disproportionately.[^cite_note-345] Nigerian opposition leader Buba Galadima said in 2012: "What is really a group engaged in class warfare is being portrayed in government propaganda as terrorists in order to win counter-terrorism assistance from the West."[^cite_note-voanews.com-346]

### Human rights

Main article: [Human rights in Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Nigeria)

The conflict has seen numerous human rights abuses conducted by the Nigerian security forces in an effort to control the violence,[^cite_note-347] as well as their encouragement of the formation of numerous vigilante groups (for example, the Civilian Joint Task Force). [Amnesty International](18947898.md) accused the Nigerian government of human rights abuses after 950 suspected Boko Harām militants died in detention facilities run by Nigeria's military Joint Task Force in 2013.[^cite_note-348] In 2016 Amnesty International reported at least 8,000 detainees had died in detention facilities operated by the security services.[^cite_note-349] Furthermore, the Nigerian government has been accused of incompetence and supplying misinformation about events in more remote areas.

Boko Haram has kidnapped large numbers of children on several occasions. This has led to Boko Haram members physically, psychologically and sexually abusing them, using and selling them as [sex slaves](173634.md) and/or brides of [forced marriages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_marriage) with their fighters,[^cite_note-350] for example the Chibok kidnapping in 2014. In addition to kidnapping child brides, [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) states that Boko Haram uses [child soldiers](42426471.md).[^cite_note-351] According to an anonymous source working on peace talks with the group, up to 40 percent of the fighters in the group are underage soldiers.[^cite_note-352] The group has forcibly converted non-Muslims to Islam[^cite_note-353] and is also known to assign non-[Kanuris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanuri_people) on suicide missions.[^cite_note-genesis-354]

### Response

Since the mid-2010s, the Nigerian government has constructed earthen berms and trenches around cities in northeastern Nigeria to restrict the movement of jihadist groups.[^cite_note-355]

### Rehabilitation of insurgents

Victims and affected communities accuse the Nigerian government of prioritising perpetrators over survivors as several groups of captured insurgents have gone through the government's deradicalization, reorientation and reintegration program. For example in March and April 2025 respectively, 600 and 390 "repentant Boko Haram members" publicly graduated after undergoing a 6–12-month process including vocational training upon which they received cash and startup equipment for the trade of their choice. Some have even been recruited into the security services. Meanwhile, no solid long term evaluation of the effectiveness of these problems are available.&#x20;

There is doubt in the sincerity of the former insurgents and fears of them acting as spies or reverting to militancy, especially if they again become disgruntled, disaffected or ostracized.[^cite_note-theconversation.com-356] This remains a major problem faced by local governments as the ex-militants are generally suspected by officials and civilians to still hold connections to the rebels and pose a security risk. As a result, ex-rebels are often ostracized by the community, which in turn increases the risk of them rejoining the insurgency.[^cite_note-357][^cite_note-theconversation.com-356]

### Cholera outbreak

In June 2024, the [Nigeria Centre for Disease Control](49228194.md) announced a total of 1,141 suspected and 65 confirmed cases of cholera with 30 deaths from 96 local government areas in 30 states.[^cite_note-358] At least 74 people were reported to have died following an outbreak of cholera in Borno State that began in May 2026.[^cite_note-359]

## International context

Main article: [War on terror](13425800.md)

The insurgence can be seen in the context of other conflicts nearby, for example [in northern Mali](36856978.md). The Boko Harām leadership has international connections to [Al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Islamic_Maghreb), [Al-Shabaab](8726463.md), the [Movement for Unity and Jihad in West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_for_Oneness_and_Jihad_in_West_Africa), [Mokhtar Belmokhtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokhtar_Belmokhtar)'s factions, and other militant groups outside Nigeria.[^cite_note-360] In 2014 Nigerian President Jonathan even went so far as calling Boko Harām "al-Qaeda in West Africa".[^cite_note-361] By 2012, attacks by Nigerian Islamist militias on targets beyond Nigeria's borders were still limited,[^cite_note-362] and should not be confused with the activities of other groups (for example, the responsibility of AQIM for most attacks in Niger). Despite this, there were concerns that conflict could spread to Nigeria's neighbours, especially Cameroon, where it existed at a relatively low level until 2014, subsequently escalating considerably. There are combatants from neighboring Chad and Niger.[^cite_note-363]

On 17 May 2014, the presidents of Benin, Chad, Cameroon, Nigeria and Niger met for a summit in Paris and agreed to combat Boko Harām on a coordinated basis, sharing in particular surveillance and intelligence gathering. Jonathan[^cite_note-364] and Chad President [Idriss Deby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idriss_D%C3%A9by)[^cite_note-tw-8] declared total war on Boko Harām. Western nations, including Britain, France, Israel, and the United States, pledged support including technical expertise and training.[^cite_note-365][^cite_note-366] *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported in March 2015 that hundreds of [private military contractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_military_company) from South Africa and other countries play a decisive role in Nigeria's military campaign, operating attack helicopters and armored personnel carriers and assisting in the planning of operations.[^cite_note-mercenaries-36]

In December 2025, the U.S. Army carried out targeted strikes in Sokoto State.[^cite_note-367]
