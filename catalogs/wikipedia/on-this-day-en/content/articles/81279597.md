**No Kings protests** (also called **No Kings 2**[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] and **No Kings Day 2.0**[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]) took place on October 18, 2025, as part of a series of [demonstrations](6787314.md) taking place largely in the United States against [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s policies and actions during his [second presidency](78298108.md). The demonstrations, which followed the [June 2025 No Kings protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2025_No_Kings_protests), took place in some 2,700 locations across the country, including the [National Mall](167796.md) in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), [Chicago](6886.md), and [New York City](645042.md).

Organizers of the protests estimated that the protests drew nearly 7 million attendees,[^cite_note-AJE_millions_no_kings-19][^cite_note-20] while a partnership between data journalist [G. Elliott Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G._Elliott_Morris) and the Xylom, an independent Atlanta-based science newsroom, estimated 5 million to 6.5 million participants. Either estimate would make this one of the [largest single-day protests in American history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_protests_and_demonstrations_in_the_United_States_by_size).[^cite_note-21]

In January 2026, organizers announced plans to hold a [third "No Kings" mass mobilization](82478314.md) on March 28, 2026, in response to the [killings of Alex Pretti](82203651.md) and [Renée Good](82055905.md), as well as the [2026 Minnesota general strike](82198050.md).[^cite_note-22]

## Background and organizers

The October 18, 2025, protests followed the [No Kings protests in June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Kings_protests_\(June_2025\)), the [Free America Weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_America_Weekend) on July 4, and the [Good Trouble Lives On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Trouble_Lives_On_protest) protest on July 17. [Indivisible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indivisible_movement) was the main organizer,[^cite_note-23] and more than 200 organizations worked together to organize the October protests, including [50501](79143789.md), the [American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_Liberties_Union) (ACLU), the [American Federation of Teachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Federation_of_Teachers), Common Defense, the [Human Rights Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Campaign), [Planned Parenthood Federation of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_Parenthood), [Autistic Self Advocacy Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autistic_Self_Advocacy_Network) (ASAN), the [League of Conservation Voters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Conservation_Voters), [MoveOn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MoveOn), [Public Citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Citizen), [United We Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_We_Dream), and Working Families Power.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Other organizers included [Third Act Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Act_Movement), [Social Security Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_Works), [Communications Workers of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_Workers_of_America), [Freedom From Religion Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_From_Religion_Foundation), [New York Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Civil_Liberties_Union), [League of Women Voters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Women_Voters) and [American Federation of Government Employees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Federation_of_Government_Employees).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

Outside the United States, protests were organized by [Democrats Abroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrats_Abroad). Various groups organized protests in the UK, including the [Stop Trump Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stop_Trump_Coalition_\(UK\)).[^cite_note-33]

Organizers were "adamant that the rallies remain peaceful",[^cite_note-34] according to *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today)*, and held virtual safety trainings ahead of the protests with help from the ACLU.[^cite_note-35] According to *The New York Times*, "Many had attended a similar event in June, but the months since had seen President Trump make a dizzying array of changes in quick succession."[^cite_note-36]

### Pre-emptive responses

See also: [October 2025 No Kings protests § Texas](#Texas)

In the days leading up to the protests, several administration officials said that protesters were members of [antifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifa_\(United_States\)), which Trump and his administration had labeled a "domestic terrorist organization"[^cite_note-37] on par with [ISIS](9087364.md).[^cite_note-39] On October 13, [United States transportation secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_Transportation) [Sean Duffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Duffy) said protestors are "part of antifa".[^cite_note-The_Hill_Duffy-40] [Attorney General](44002.md) [Pam Bondi](4550623.md) spoke of "people out there with thousands of signs that all match, pre-bought, pre-put together",[^cite_note-The_Guardian-41] which she attributed to antifa's high degree of organization,[^cite_note-42] and pledged to "get to the root of antifa" and "find and charge all of those people who are causing this chaos".[^cite_note-The_Guardian-41] *[The Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Independent)* wrote that experts and civil right activists had warned that Trump was using the antifa label broadly against any opposition or dissent, and critics warned that his administration defining who is antifa, meaning a terrorist, risked a dangerous escalation against Trump's enemies, including Americans who march in what the government has labeled an antifa event.[^cite_note-The_Independent_antifa-38]

[Speaker of the House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives) [Mike Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Johnson) said "It's all the pro-Hamas wing and the antifa people, they're all coming out," and that the group organizing the protest in Washington, D.C., was anti-American,[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-The_Hill_Johnson_Emmer_Scalise-44] while [House majority whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_leaders_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives#Party_whips_and_assistant_party_leaders) [Tom Emmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Emmer) said that the "terrorist wing" of the Democratic party was set to hold them.[^cite_note-45] They and [House majority leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_leaders_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives#Majority_leader) [Steve Scalise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Scalise)[^cite_note-The_Hill_Johnson_Emmer_Scalise-44] called the protests a "Hate America" rally.[^cite_note-The_Guardian-41] Johnson also attacked the participants with: "Let's see who shows up for that. I bet you see pro-Hamas supporters. I bet you see Antifa types. I bet you see the Marxists in full display, the people who don't want to stand and defend the foundational truths of this republic."[^cite_note-46]

The [Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Donald_Trump) said it would take anti-terrorist measures during the protests such as mobilizing the [FBI](11127.md),[^cite_note-47] and [White House press secretary](284101.md) [Karoline Leavitt](71739774.md) said to Fox News that participants and the Democratic Party's main constituents were "made up of [Hamas](13913.md) terrorists, illegal aliens, and violent criminals".[^cite_note-48]

Duffy and senators [Roger Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Marshall) and [Ted Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Cruz) accused No Kings of using [paid protesters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paid_protester).[^cite_note-The_Hill_Duffy-40][^cite_note-The_Independent_antifa-38][^cite_note-Chron_Cruz-49] According to *The Independent*, Marshall [baselessly laid the blame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Soros_conspiracy_theories) on [George Soros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Soros), saying, "We'll have to get the National Guard out. Hopefully it will be peaceful. I doubt it."[^cite_note-The_Independent_antifa-38] Cruz also blamed Soros and urged to "cut off the money" behind "these rallies that may well turn into riots".[^cite_note-Chron_Cruz-49] He spoke of legislation he had introduced that would allow use of the [RICO Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racketeer_Influenced_and_Corrupt_Organizations_Act) to prosecute the money he alleged was behind No Kings for "supporting rioting and violence", and said he had urged Attorney General Bondi and [FBI director](5861065.md) [Kash Patel](62202727.md) to prosecute.[^cite_note-Chron_Cruz-49]

### Alabama

About 12 events were planned in [Alabama](303.md). In [Birmingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham,_Alabama), 5,000 people gathered in [Railroad Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad_Park), with speakers including former U.S. senator [Doug Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Jones_\(politician\)).[^cite_note-abc3340-55] 3,000 people demonstrated in [Huntsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntsville,_Alabama), and 400 people demonstrated in [Guntersville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guntersville,_Alabama).[^cite_note-abc3340-55] 600 people demonstrated in [Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery,_Alabama),[^cite_note-reflector-56] over 200 people demonstrated in [Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford,_Alabama),[^cite_note-reflector-56] and 40 people demonstrated in [Selma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selma,_Alabama) at the [Edmund Pettus Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Pettus_Bridge).[^cite_note-reflector-56] Demonstrations were also seen in [Athens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens,_Alabama)[^cite_note-57] and [Mobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile,_Alabama).[^cite_note-58] Additional protests were planned in [Dadeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadeville,_Alabama), [Dothan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dothan,_Alabama), [Fairhope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairhope,_Alabama), [Phenix City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenix_City,_Alabama), and [Tuscaloosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscaloosa,_Alabama).[^cite_note-59]

### Alaska

More than two dozen protests occurred in [Alaska](624.md).[^cite_note-\:0-60] 2,000 people gathered in Town Square Park in [Anchorage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchorage,_Alaska), with speakers including Alaska State Senator [Löki Tobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%B6ki_Tobin) and 2026 gubernatorial candidate [Tom Begich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Begich).[^cite_note-61] 2,000 people also protested in [Fairbanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairbanks,_Alaska),[^cite_note-62] and 1,500 in [Juneau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juneau,_Alaska).[^cite_note-63] Protests also occurred in [Dillingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dillingham,_Alaska), [Girdwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girdwood,_Anchorage,_Alaska), [Gustavus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavus,_Alaska), [Haines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haines,_Alaska), [Healy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healy,_Alaska), [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer,_Alaska), [Kenai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenai,_Alaska), [Ketchikan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketchikan,_Alaska), [Kodiak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodiak,_Alaska), [Kotzebue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotzebue,_Alaska), [Nome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nome,_Alaska), [Seward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seward,_Alaska), [Sitka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitka,_Alaska), [Soldotna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldotna,_Alaska), [Talkeetna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talkeetna,_Alaska), [Valdez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdez,_Alaska), and [Wasilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasilla,_Alaska).[^cite_note-\:0-60]

### Arizona

Over 60 protests occurred in [Arizona](21883824.md) from places like [Tuba City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuba_City,_Arizona) to [Nogales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nogales,_Arizona).[^cite_note-\:7-64] About two dozen demonstrations were planned in the [Phoenix metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_metropolitan_area), including in [Anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthem,_Arizona), [Buckeye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckeye,_Arizona), [Fountain Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain_Hills,_Arizona), [Maricopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maricopa,_Arizona), [San Tan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Tan_Valley,_Arizona), and [Sun City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_City,_Arizona).[^cite_note-65] Arizona's largest protest was in Phoenix, with around 15,000 protestors. This was three times the size of its June No Kings protest. An estimated 900 protestors met in [Tempe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempe,_Arizona) at Cole Park, and organizers counted more than 4,300 protestors in [Scottsdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottsdale,_Arizona).[^cite_note-\:7-64] Over 5,000 protested at [Flagstaff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagstaff,_Arizona)'s city hall, and organizers estimated that 2,500 protested along a mile of State Route 69 in [Prescott Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescott_Valley,_Arizona) while 1,600 protested along State Route 89A in [Sedona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedona,_Arizona).[^cite_note-66]

### U.S. territories

Protests took place in [Puerto Rico](23041.md), [Guam](11974.md), the [Northern Mariana Islands](21402.md), and the [United States Virgin Islands](32135.md).[^cite_note-327]

## Response by Trump

AI-generated video of Donald Trump flying a jet and dropping fecal matter on protesting crowds posted to Truth Social on the same day as the protests

[^cite_note-328]

On October 15, Trump predicted that few people would show up for the protests. On October 17, Trump told [Fox News](11121.md), "They say they're referring to me as a king. I'm not a king." That same day, a Trump campaign social media account posted an AI video showing Trump dressed as a monarch, wearing a crown and waving from a balcony.[^cite_note-auto-329]

On the night of the protests, Trump released a video generated with artificial intelligence showing himself wearing a crown in a fighter jet marked "King Trump", dropping brown liquid resembling [feces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feces) on the protesters.[^cite_note-330][^cite_note-331] The video included the [Kenny Loggins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Loggins) song "[Danger Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danger_Zone_\(song\))" from the 1986 movie *[Top Gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Gun_\(movie\))*; Loggins asked Trump to remove his song from the video and told a reporter, "I can't imagine why anybody would want their music used or associated with something created with the sole purpose of dividing us."[^cite_note-332]

## Aftermath

[The American Civil Liberties Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_Civil_Liberties_Union) (ACLU) highlighted the No Kings protests as a significant demonstration of civic participation and the exercise of First Amendment rights in the United States. The ACLU stated that these protests demonstrate that many Americans are actively asserting their rights and will not remain silent when freedoms such as speech, assembly, and the press are perceived to be under threat.[^cite_note-333]
