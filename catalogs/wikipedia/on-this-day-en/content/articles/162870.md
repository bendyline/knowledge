**Shawn Corey Carter** (born December 4, 1969), known professionally as **Jay-Z**,[^cite_note-8] is an American rapper, businessman, and record executive.[^cite_note-9] In 2023, *[Billboard](18309966.md)* and *[Vibe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibe_\(magazine\))* ranked him as [the greatest rapper of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_and_Vibe's_50_Greatest_Rappers_of_All_Time).[^cite_note-\:6-10] Rooted in [East Coast hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_hip-hop), Jay-Z is known for his complex lyricism that often uses [double entendres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_entendre), [wordplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordplay), and [braggadocio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braggadocio_\(rap\))—built on a [rags to riches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rags_to_riches) narrative. According to *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)*, he is the [wealthiest music artist in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_music_artists_by_net_worth), worth an estimated US\$2.8 billion as of 2026.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

A protégé of fellow New York City-based rapper [Jaz-O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaz-O), Jay-Z began recording music in the late 1980s. He co-founded [Roc-A-Fella Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roc-A-Fella_Records) in 1994 to release his first two studio albums, *[Reasonable Doubt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reasonable_Doubt_\(album\))* (1996) and *[In My Lifetime, Vol. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_My_Lifetime,_Vol._1)* (1997), both of which were critically acclaimed. His 11 subsequent albums, including *[The Blueprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blueprint)* (2001), *[The Black Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Black_Album_\(Jay-Z_album\))* (2003), *[American Gangster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Gangster_\(album\))* (2007), and *[4\:44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4\:44)* (2017), have each debuted atop the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200). Jay-Z has a total of 14 number-one [albums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay-Z_albums_discography) on the chart, the [third-most in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200#Artist_milestones). He topped the [*Billboard* Hot 100](423161.md) on four occasions: once as a lead artist with his 2009 single "[Empire State of Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_State_of_Mind)" (with [Alicia Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicia_Keys)), and three times with his guest performances on the singles "[Heartbreaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbreaker_\(Mariah_Carey_song\))" (1999) by [Mariah Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariah_Carey), "[Crazy in Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crazy_in_Love)" (2003) by [Beyoncé](83688.md), and "[Umbrella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbrella_\(song\))" (2007) by [Rihanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihanna).[^cite_note-13]

Jay-Z served as president and chief executive officer of [Def Jam Recordings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Def_Jam_Recordings) from 2004 to 2007,[^cite_note-14] during which time he helped launch the careers of acts including [Kanye West](523032.md), Rihanna, and [Ne-Yo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ne-Yo). He co-founded [Rocawear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocawear), a clothing retailer, in 1999,[^cite_note-Luxury-15] and founded the [40/40 Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40/40_Club_\(venue\)), a luxury bar chain, in 2003. As both grew into multi-million-dollar businesses, he launched [Roc Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roc_Nation), a multi-disciplinary entertainment agency, in 2008. In 2015, he acquired the technology company [Aspiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspiro) and led the expansion of its media streaming service [Tidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_\(service\)).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Through his business ventures, Jay-Z became the first [hip-hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip-hop) [billionaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_billionaire) in 2019.[^cite_note-networth-19][^cite_note-Billionaire-20] After [Block, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block,_Inc.) (then Square, Inc.) acquired a majority stake in Tidal in March 2021, Jay-Z joined its [board of directors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_directors).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

One of the [best-selling music artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_best-selling_music_artists) with 125 million records sold, Jay-Z has won 25 [Grammy Awards](44635.md), the [eighth-most of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_records#Most_Grammys_won) and the second-most of any hip-hop artist.[^cite_note-23] He is the recipient of the [NAACP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAACP)'s [President's Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAACP_Image_Award_%E2%80%93_President's_Award) and four [Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Awards) (including three [Primetime Emmy Awards](6579025.md)), in addition to being nominated for a [Tony Award](54741.md). Jay-Z was the first rapper to be inducted into the [Songwriters Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songwriters_Hall_of_Fame)[^cite_note-24] and the eighth hip-hop act to be inducted into the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](58902.md). *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* named him one of the [100 most influential people in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100) in 2013.[^cite_note-\:3-25]

## Early life and education

Shawn Corey Carter was born on December 4, 1969, in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), New York City.[^cite_note-26] He was raised in [Marcy Houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcy_Houses), a public housing project in Brooklyn's [Bedford–Stuyvesant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford%E2%80%93Stuyvesant,_Brooklyn) neighborhood.[^cite_note-allmusicbio-27] His father, Adnis Reeves,[^cite_note-28] abandoned the family when Jay-Z was 11 years old, and Jay-Z and his three older siblings were raised by their mother, Gloria Carter. Reeves later met and reconciled with Jay-Z prior to his death in 2003. Jay-Z claims in his lyrics to "You Must Love Me", the closing track on his 1997 album *In My Lifetime, Vol. 1*, that in 1982, at age 12, he shot his older brother Eric in the shoulder for stealing his jewelry.[^cite_note-CBSnews-29][^cite_note-aboutjayz-30]

He attended Eli Whitney High School and [George Westinghouse Career and Technical Education High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Westinghouse_Career_and_Technical_Education_High_School), both in Brooklyn, and then [Trenton Central High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenton_Central_High_School) in [Trenton, New Jersey](57843.md). He did not graduate, dropping out during his sophomore year at Trenton Central High School.[^cite_note-31] According to his interviews and lyrics, he sold [crack cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_cocaine)[^cite_note-aboutjayz-30] and was shot at three times during this period.[^cite_note-CBSnews-29] His former friend was sentenced to prison for possessing drugs and weapons.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] Known as "Jazzy" around the neighborhood, he later adopted the stage name "Jay-Z" in homage to his mentor [Jaz-O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaz-O).[^cite_note-allmusicbio-27][^cite_note-hyphen-34]

## Career

### 1986–1995: Early career and recordings

Jay-Z can be heard contributing some of his earliest verses on several of Jaz-O's early recordings in the late 1980s and early 1990s, including "[H. P. Gets Busy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._P._Gets_Busy)", "The Originators" and "It’s That Simple".[^cite_note-EdwardsRap14-35][^cite_note-36] Jaz-O's record label [EMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMI_Records) came up with the idea to create an album with a concept reminiscent of [DJ Jazzy Jeff & the Fresh Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Jazzy_Jeff_&_the_Fresh_Prince), featuring Jaz-O as the rapper, Jay-Z as the [hype man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hype_man), and [DJ Irv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irv_Gotti_Presents:_The_Remixes) as the DJ. While working on the album *[Word to the Jaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_to_the_Jaz)* in London in 1988, Jay-Z and DJ Irv began to build their relationship on the trip.[^cite_note-m750-37][^cite_note-a513-38][^cite_note-y078-39][^cite_note-y360-40][^cite_note-r035-41] Jay-Z became embroiled in several battles with rapper [LL Cool J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LL_Cool_J) in the early 1990s.[^cite_note-42] He first became known to a wide audience on the [posse cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posse_cut) "Show and Prove" on the 1994 [Big Daddy Kane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Daddy_Kane) album *[Daddy's Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daddy's_Home_\(Big_Daddy_Kane_album\)).* Jay-Z has been referred to as Big Daddy Kane's hype man during this period,[^cite_note-EdwardsRap316-43] although Kane explains that he did not fill the traditional hype man role, and was instead basically making cameo appearances on stage. "When I would leave the stage to go change outfits, I would bring out Jay-Z and [Positive K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_K) and let them freestyle until I came back to the stage."[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

According to his second verse on "[99 Problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/99_Problems)", released in 2003, Jay-Z was allegedly stopped by an NYPD detective in 1994 while en route to [I-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_95), possibly for a search of drugs in his car. [Detection dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detection_dogs) were called, but another police car had passed; Jay-Z was let go soon after.[^cite_note-46] Jay-Z appeared on a popular song by [Big L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_L), "Da Graveyard", and on [Mic Geronimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mic_Geronimo)'s "Time to Build", which also featured early appearances by his former [Murder Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_Inc._\(rap_group\)) colleagues [Ja Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ja_Rule) and [DMX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DMX) in 1995. His first official rap single was "[In My Lifetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_My_Lifetime_\(song\))", which was released with an accompanying music video in 1995. An unreleased music video was also produced for the B-side "I Can't Get with That".[^cite_note-47]

### 1995–2000: *Reasonable Doubt*, *In My Lifetime, Vol. 1*, *Vol. 2...*, *Vol. 3...*, and *The Dynasty*

With no major label to give him a record deal, Jay-Z sold [burned CDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD-R) out of his car,[^cite_note-CBSnews-29] and with [Damon "Dame" Dash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Dash) and [Kareem "Biggs" Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kareem_Burke), created [Roc-A-Fella Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roc-A-Fella_Records) as an independent label in 1995. After striking a distribution deal with [Priority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priority_Records), Jay-Z released his 1996 debut album *[Reasonable Doubt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reasonable_Doubt_\(album\))* with [beats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_hop_production) from acclaimed producers such as [DJ Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Premier) and [Super DJ Clark Kent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Clark_Kent) and an appearance by [The Notorious B.I.G.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Notorious_B.I.G.) The album reached number 23 on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200) and went gold that year.[^cite_note-allmusicbio-27] After reaching a new label distribution deal with [Def Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Def_Jam_Recordings) in 1997, Jay-Z released his follow-up *[In My Lifetime, Vol. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_My_Lifetime,_Vol._1).* Featuring production by [Sean Combs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Combs), DJ Premier, and [Ski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_Beatz), it earned platinum status in the United States.[^cite_note-riaacertification-48]

In 1998, Jay-Z released *[Vol. 2... Hard Knock Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vol._2..._Hard_Knock_Life)* which spawned the biggest hit of his career at the time, "[Hard Knock Life (Ghetto Anthem)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Knock_Life_\(Ghetto_Anthem\))". He relied more on [flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_\(rapping\)) and wordplay, and he continued with his penchant for mining beats from the popular producers of the day such as [Swizz Beatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swizz_Beatz), an upstart in-house producer for [Ruff Ryders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruff_Ryders), and [Timbaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbaland).[^cite_note-49] Other producers included [DJ Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Premier), [Erick Sermon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erick_Sermon), [The 45 King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_45_King), and [Kid Capri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kid_Capri). Charting hits from this album included "[Can I Get A...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Can_I_Get_A...)", featuring [Ja Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ja_Rule) and [Amil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amil_\(rapper\)), and "[Nigga What, Nigga Who](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigga_What,_Nigga_Who_\(Originator_99\))", featuring Amil and [Jaz-O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaz-O). *Vol. 2* would eventually become Jay-Z's most commercially successful album; it was certified 6× Platinum in the United States and has to date sold over six million copies.[^cite_note-riaacertification-48] The album went on to win a Grammy Award, although Jay-Z boycotted the ceremony protesting DMX's failure to garner a Grammy nomination and the academy's decision to not broadcast urban music categories.[^cite_note-50] In 1999, Jay-Z collaborated with [Mariah Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariah_Carey) on "[Heartbreaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heartbreaker_\(Mariah_Carey_song\))", a song from her seventh album, *[Rainbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_\(Mariah_Carey_album\))*. The song became Jay-Z's first chart-topper in the U.S., spending two weeks atop the *[Billboard](18309966.md)* [Hot 100](423161.md).[^cite_note-51] In that same year, Jay-Z released *[Vol. 3... Life and Times of S. Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vol._3..._Life_and_Times_of_S._Carter)*. The album proved successful and sold over 3 million copies.[^cite_note-riaacertification-48] *Vol. 3*'s most successful single was "[Big Pimpin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Pimpin')", featuring [UGK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UGK).[^cite_note-52]

In 2000, Jay-Z released *[The Dynasty: Roc La Familia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dynasty:_Roc_La_Familia)*, which was originally intended to become a compilation album for Roc-A-Fella artists but Def Jam turned into a Jay-Z album.[^cite_note-53] The album helped to introduce newcomer producers [The Neptunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Neptunes), [Just Blaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Blaze), [Kanye West](523032.md), and [Bink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bink_\(producer\)), which have all gone on to achieve notable success. This is also the first album where Jay-Z utilizes a more soulful sound than his previous albums. *The Dynasty* sold over two million units in the U.S. alone.[^cite_note-riaacertification-48]

### 2001–2002: Feud with Nas, Prodigy, *The Blueprint* and *The Blueprint<sup>2</sup>*

In 2001, Jay-Z spoke out against [Prodigy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prodigy_\(rapper\)) after he took an issue with Jay-Z's "[Money, Cash, Hoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money,_Cash,_Hoes)." Prodigy felt that the lyrics alluded disparagingly to his shared dispute with Mobb Deep against [Tupac Shakur](31341.md), [Snoop Dogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snoop_Dogg), and [Death Row Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Row_Records). He later performed the song "[Takeover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeover_\(Jay-Z_song\))", at [Summer Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Jam_\(festival\)) 2001, a [diss-track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diss_\(music\)) about Nas, Prodigy, and Mobb Deep. At the same concert, Jay-Z brought Michael Jackson up on stage with him.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] A line at the end of "Takeover" referred to [Nas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nas), who criticized him on "We Will Survive".[^cite_note-56] Nas responded with a diss of his own, and Jay-Z straightaway added a verse to "Takeover" which dissed Nas and would start a feud between the two rappers. The feud had ended by 2005; Jay-Z stated that record producer [Mark Pitts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Pitts) had helped them settle their differences.[^cite_note-57]

On September 11, 2001, Jay-Z released his sixth studio album, *[The Blueprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blueprint)*, which received a [five-mic review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Source_\(magazine\)) from hip-hop magazine *The Source.* Written in just two days,[^cite_note-58] the album sold more than 427,000 copies, debuted at number one on the [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200)[^cite_note-59] and reached double platinum certification by the [Recording Industry Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_Industry_Association_of_America).[^cite_note-riaacertification-48] [Eminem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eminem) guest performed and produced its song, "[Renegade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renegade_\(Jay-Z_song\))". Four tracks were produced by [Kanye West](523032.md). *The Blueprint* includes the songs "[Izzo (H.O.V.A.)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izzo_\(H.O.V.A.\))", "[Girls, Girls, Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girls,_Girls,_Girls_\(Jay-Z_song\))", "[Jigga That Nigga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jigga_That_Nigga)", and "[Song Cry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_Cry)". As of February 2012, the album had sold 2.7 million copies worldwide.[^cite_note-60] In 2019, *The Blueprint* was selected by the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress) for preservation in the [National Recording Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Recording_Registry) for being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".[^cite_note-61]

In October 2001, Jay-Z pleaded guilty to aggravated assault for stabbing record producer [Lance Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Rivera) at the Kit Kat Klub in New York City in 1999.[^cite_note-62] Despite Jay-Z's sentence of three years probation for the crime, Rivera later recanted the allegations in 2023.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

Jay-Z then collaborated with Chicago singer [R. Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Kelly) to release collaborative studio album, *[The Best of Both Worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Best_of_Both_Worlds_\(Jay-Z_and_R._Kelly_album\))* in March 2002. In November of that year, Jay-Z released his seventh studio album *[The Blueprint<sup>2</sup>: The Gift & The Curse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blueprint_2:_The_Gift_&_The_Curse)*—a double album. The album debuted on the *Billboard* 200 at number one, selling over 3 million units solely in the U.S.[^cite_note-riaacertification-48] and surpassing *The Blueprint*.[^cite_note-65] It yielded a single-disc re-issue, *[The Blueprint 2.1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blueprint_2:_The_Gift_&_The_Curse#Blueprint_2.1)*, which retained half of the songs from the original. Its original release spawned two hit singles, "[Excuse Me Miss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excuse_Me_Miss)" and "['03 Bonnie & Clyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'03_Bonnie_&_Clyde)", which features Jay-Z's then-girlfriend, [Beyoncé](83688.md), and contained the track "A Dream", featuring [Faith Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_Evans) and the late [Notorious B.I.G.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Notorious_B.I.G.); the re-issue spawned the single "[La-La-La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La-La-La_\(Jay-Z_song\))", which was a sequel to "Excuse Me Miss" and failed to match its commercial success.

### 2003–2005: *The Black Album* and initial retirement



Jay-Z in 2003

After visiting the south of France,[^cite_note-retirement-66] Jay-Z announced work on his eighth studio album, *[The Black Album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Black_Album_\(Jay-Z_album\))* at the opening of the 40/40 Club.[^cite_note-67] He worked with several producers including [Just Blaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Blaze), [The Neptunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Neptunes), [Kanye West](523032.md), [Timbaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbaland), [Eminem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eminem), [DJ Quik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJ_Quik), [9th Wonder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Wonder), [The Buchanans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Buchanans_\(production_team\)), and [Rick Rubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Rubin). Notable songs on the album included "[What More Can I Say](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_More_Can_I_Say)", "[Dirt off Your Shoulder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirt_off_Your_Shoulder)", "[Change Clothes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Change_Clothes)", and "[99 Problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/99_Problems)". *The Black Album* has sold over 4 million copies in the U.S.[^cite_note-riaacertification-48]

On November 25, 2003, Jay-Z held a concert—billed as a "retirement party" at [Madison Square Garden](75129.md) in [New York City](645042.md), which was later the focus of his 2004 documentary, *[Fade to Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fade_to_Black_\(documentary\))*. All proceeds went to charity. Other performers included collaborators [the Roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Roots) (in the form of his backing band), [Missy Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missy_Elliott), [Memphis Bleek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis_Bleek), [Beanie Siegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beanie_Siegel), Freeway, [Mary J. Blige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_J._Blige), Beyoncé, [Twista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twista), [Ghostface Killah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghostface_Killah), [Foxy Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxy_Brown_\(rapper\)), [Pharrell Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharrell_Williams) and R. Kelly, with appearances by Voletta Wallace and [Afeni Shakur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afeni_Shakur), the mothers of [the Notorious B.I.G.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Notorious_B.I.G.) and [Tupac Shakur](31341.md), respectively.[^cite_note-retireparty-68] While Jay-Z had attested to a retirement from making new studio albums, various side projects and appearances soon followed. Included in these were a greatest hits record, as well as the release and tour of *[Unfinished Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfinished_Business_\(Jay-Z_and_R._Kelly_album\))*, the second collaborative album between Jay-Z and R. Kelly.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

### 2005–2007: *Kingdom Come* and *American Gangster*



Jay-Z performing in 2006

On October 27, 2005, Jay-Z headlined New York's [Power 105.1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_105.1) annual concert, Powerhouse. The concert's title, "I Declare War," led to intense speculation in the preceding weeks on whom exactly Jay-Z would declare war. As he had previously "declared war" on other artists taking lyrical shots at him at other events, many believed that the concert's title represented an all-out assault by Jay-Z upon his rivals.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] The theme of the concert was Jay-Z's position as president and CEO of [Def Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Def_Jam_Recordings), complete with an on-stage mock-up of the [Oval Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Office). Many artists made appearances such as the old roster of [Roc-A-Fella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roc-A-Fella) records artists, as well as [Ne-Yo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ne-Yo), [Teairra Marí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teairra_Mar%C3%AD), [T.I.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T.I.), [Young Jeezy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Jeezy), [Akon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akon), [Kanye West](523032.md), [Paul Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Wall), [the LOX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_LOX), and [Sean Combs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Combs).[^cite_note-allhiphop-76]

At the conclusion of the concert, Jay-Z put many arguments to rest to the surprise of hip-hop fans. The most significant development in this show was closure to the infamous hip-hop rivalry between Jay-Z and [Nas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nas). The two former rivals shook hands and shared the stage together to perform Jay-Z's "Dead Presidents" blended with Nas's song "The World is Yours".[^cite_note-mtvnews2-77]

Jay-Z returned with his comeback album on November 21, 2006, titled *[Kingdom Come](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Come_\(Jay-Z_album\))*.[^cite_note-78] Jay-Z's comeback single, "[Show Me What You Got](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Show_Me_What_You_Got)", was leaked on the Internet in early October 2006. The album, which was scheduled for release later on that month, received heavy air-play after its leak, causing the [FBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI) to step in and investigate.[^cite_note-79] Jay-Z worked with video director [Hype Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hype_Williams), and the single's video was directed by [F. Gary Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Gary_Gray). The album features production from [Just Blaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Blaze), [Pharrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharrell), [Kanye West](523032.md), [Dr. Dre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Dre) and [Coldplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldplay)'s [Chris Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Martin) (single entitled "Beach Chair").[^cite_note-sfgate-80][^cite_note-mtvnews-81] The first week saw 680,000 sales of the CD, enough to be his "biggest sales week ever" as *Billboard* reported.[^cite_note-billboardkingdom-82] This album has sold 2 million copies in the U.S. and is certified double platinum.[^cite_note-riaacertification-48][^cite_note-83]

Jay-Z released his tenth album entitled *[American Gangster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Gangster_\(album\))* on November 6, 2007. After viewing the Ridley Scott [film of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Gangster_\(film\)), Jay-Z was heavily inspired to create a new "concept" album that depicts his experiences as a street-hustler.[^cite_note-newyorktimes-84] The album is not the film's official soundtrack, although it was distributed by [Def Jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Def_Jam_Recordings).[^cite_note-hiphopdx-85] Jay-Z's *American Gangster* depicts his life in correlation to the movie *American Gangster*. At the start of the album's first single, "[Blue Magic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Magic_\(song\))", Jay-Z offers a dealer's manifesto while making references to political figures of the late 1980s with the lyric: "Blame [Reagan](25433.md) for making me to into a monster, blame [Oliver North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_North) and [Iran-Contra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran-Contra), I ran contraband that they sponsored, before this rhymin' stuff we was in concert."[^cite_note-86] Also notable about the "Blue Magic" music video was Jay-Z flashing €500 notes; [Harvard Business School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Business_School) professor [Rawi Abdelal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawi_Abdelal) called this a "turning point in American pop culture's response to globalization." The album has sold 1 million copies in the U.S.[^cite_note-riaacertification-48] On January 1, 2008, Jay-Z resigned as president of Def Jam.[^cite_note-87]

### 2008–2011: *The Blueprint 3* and *Watch the Throne*



Jay-Z performing in 2008

In winter 2008, it was announced that Jay-Z would become the first major hip-hop artist to headline Britain's [Glastonbury Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glastonbury_Festival_2008).[^cite_note-glastonbury-88][^cite_note-89] Tickets sold out quickly. Former headliner [Noel Gallagher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noel_Gallagher) of [Oasis](161515.md) condemned the festival organizers for choosing Jay-Z to headline a traditionally guitar-driven festival. "I'm sorry, but Jay-Z?" Gallagher asked, swearing. "No chance. Glastonbury has a tradition of, kind of, guitar music, do you know what I mean? And even when they throw the odd curve balls in on a Sunday night—you go, 'Kylie Minogue? I don't know about that', do you know what I mean?—but I'm not having hip hop at Glastonbury, no way, no, no. It's wrong."[^cite_note-90] As controversy mounted, Jay-Z replied, "We don't play guitars, Noel, but hip hop has put in its work like any other form of music. This headline show is just a natural progression. Rap music is still evolving. We have to respect each other's genre of music and move forward."[^cite_note-91] Jay-Z opened his Glastonbury set with a tongue-in-cheek cover of Oasis's iconic song "[Wonderwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonderwall_\(song\))",[^cite_note-glastonbury3-92] and went on to deliver a performance heralded as a successful response to pre-festival criticism.[^cite_note-93] The guitar used in this performance was later shown at the Book of HOV exhibit in the Brooklyn Library.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

He also headlined many other summer festivals in 2008, including [Roskilde Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roskilde_Festival) in Denmark,[^cite_note-97] [Hove Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hove_Festival) in Norway[^cite_note-98] and [O2 Wireless Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O2_Wireless_Festival) in London.[^cite_note-99] During Kanye West's concert of August 6, 2008, at Madison Square Garden, Jay-Z came out to perform a new song and he and West proclaimed that it was to be on *The Blueprint 3*.[^cite_note-100] On May 21, 2009, Jay-Z announced he would be parting ways with Def Jam, and had struck a multi-million-dollar deal to sign with [Live Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Nation), with whom he would start his [Roc Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roc_Nation) imprint which would serve as a record label, talent/management agency, and music publishing company[^cite_note-hitsdailydouble-101] and also partnered with production team [Stargate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_\(production_team\)) to start a record label called [StarRoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarRoc).[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] Jay-Z's 11th studio album *[The Blueprint 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blueprint_3)* was originally to be released on September 11, 2009,[^cite_note-rollingstonerocnation-104] but was instead released in North America on September 8, 2009, due to increasing anticipation.[^cite_note-rocnationblueprint3-105] Its international release followed on September 14.[^cite_note-106] It is his 11th album to reach No. 1 on the Billboard 200[^cite_note-107] and surpassed Elvis Presley's previous record.[^cite_note-accesshollywood-108] On October 9, 2009, Jay-Z kicked off his tour for *The Blueprint 3*, during which he supported his new album in North America. In a *Shave Magazine* review of his performance at [Rexall Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rexall_Place) in [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton), Jake Tomlinson expressed that "It was the type of smooth performance you would expect from the hip-hop superstar." The review gave this performance 4 stars. His North American tour continued until November 22, 2009.[^cite_note-shavemagazine-109] At his concert on November 8, 2009, at [UCLA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA)'s [Pauley Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauley_Pavilion), [Rihanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rihanna) joined him on stage and performed "Hard" for the first time, then performed "Run This Town" with Jay-Z.[^cite_note-110] Among his success, Jay-Z has ventured into producing Broadway shows. Along with Jada Pinkett Smith and Will Smith, Jay-Z helped produce the play *Fela!*, a musical celebrating the work of the late Nigerian star [Fela Kuti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fela_Kuti).[^cite_note-fela-111] Jay-Z said he was inspired by the power of Kuti's work and his life story, which resulted in his interest to help produce the musical.[^cite_note-fela-111] *Fela!* is a story about an African pioneer and political activist who made his first moves on the scene during the 1970s.[^cite_note-fela-111]



Jay-Z performing at the [Coachella Valley Music and Arts Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella_Valley_Music_and_Arts_Festival) in April 2010

On January 23, 2010, Jay-Z released a track, "[Stranded (Haiti Mon Amour)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranded_\(Haiti_Mon_Amour\))", with Rihanna, and [U2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U2)'s [Bono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bono) and [The Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Edge), and performed it at the [Hope For Haiti Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_For_Haiti_Now) telethon.[^cite_note-112] In June 2010, Eminem and Jay-Z announced they would perform together in a pair of concerts in Detroit and New York. The event was dubbed [The Home & Home Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Home_&_Home_Tour). The first two concerts rapidly sold out, prompting the scheduling of an additional show at each venue.[^cite_note-113] Jay-Z was the supporting act for U2 on the Australian and New Zealand leg of their [U2 360° Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U2_360%C2%B0_Tour), beginning in [Auckland](18660332.md), New Zealand, in November 2010, followed by Melbourne, Sydney, [Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane) and [Perth](24355.md) in December.[^cite_note-114]

Jay-Z would later appear with Kanye West on *[Watch the Throne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watch_the_Throne)*, a full-length LP with origins as a five-track EP. Recording sessions for the album took place at various recording locations and began in November 2010. The first single released for the project was "[H•A•M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%E2%80%A2A%E2%80%A2M)". The track was co-produced by [Lex Luger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lex_Luger_\(producer\)) and West himself. The track ended up being on the deluxe edition of the album. The follow-up to that was the second single "[Otis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otis_\(song\))", which premiered on [Funkmaster Flex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funkmaster_Flex)'s [Hot 97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_97) radio show, and was later released to the [iTunes Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITunes_Store) eleven days later. The song's existence, along with several other tracks from the album, was confirmed during a listening session hosted by Jay-Z.[^cite_note-115] The album was first released on the iTunes Store, five days prior to its being released in physical format, a strategy Jay-Z later said was used to block an [internet leak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_leak). It debuted at No. 1 on the iTunes Store in 23 countries. It also broke [Coldplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coldplay)'s record for most albums sold in one week on the online retailer, selling 290,000 copies on iTunes alone. It held that record until [Lil Wayne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lil_Wayne)'s *[Tha Carter IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tha_Carter_IV)* was released twenty-one days later, selling 10,000 copies more. It debuted on the U.S. [*Billboard* 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_200) chart at No. 1, selling 436,000 copies in its first week.[^cite_note-116] The album received generally positive reviews. Jay-Z and West later gave a surprise performance of "Otis" at the [2011 MTV Video Music Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_MTV_Video_Music_Awards).[^cite_note-117]

### 2017–present: *4\:44*, *Everything Is Love* and Book of HOV exhibit



Jay-Z announcing the *[Everything Is Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything_Is_Love)* album in London in 2018

In early June 2017, posters were displayed in New York City and Los Angeles, and banner ads were placed on the Internet promoting a [Tidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_\(service\))-related project titled *[4\:44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4\:44)*.[^cite_note-Jay_Z_Details_New_Album_'4\:44'-133] A teaser ad was aired during the [NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_NBA_Finals) on June 7 featuring actors [Mahershala Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahershala_Ali), [Lupita Nyong'o](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupita_Nyong'o) and [Danny Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Glover) in a one-minute video, ending in "4\:44 – 6.30.17, Exclusively on Tidal". ## Musical style

Video of Jay-Z being interviewed by [Dean Baquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Baquet) for *[The New York Times](30680.md)* in 2017, where he discusses his music, family, and politics&#x20;

### Influences

Jay-Z says his earliest exposure to music was through his parents' record collection, which was mostly of [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_music) artists such as [Marvin Gaye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Gaye) and [Donny Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donny_Hathaway). He says "I grew up around music, listening to all types of people... I'm into music that has soul in it, whether it be rap, R\&B, pop music, whatever. As long as I can feel their soul through the wax, that's what I really listen to."[^cite_note-MTV_News-165] He often uses excerpts from these artists as [samples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_\(music\)) in his work, particularly in the Kanye West productions included on *The Blueprint.*[^cite_note-MTV_News-165]

### Rapping technique

Known for his lyrical prowess which often utilises [double entendres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_entendre), witty [wordplays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Word_play), and mixing messages with [braggadocio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braggadocio_\(rap\)), his music often describes his rise from poverty to vast wealth.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170]

[Royce da 5'9"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royce_da_5'9%22) and [Fredro Starr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fredro_Starr) of [Onyx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onyx_\(hip_hop_group\)) both describe Jay-Z's emphasis on [flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_\(music\)) in the book *[How to Rap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_to_Rap)*—Starr says that Jay-Z is "a master of the flow—he can flow fast, he can flow slow".[^cite_note-EdwardsRap65-171] The book describes how Jay-Z uses 'rests' to provide structure to a verse[^cite_note-EdwardsRap129-172] and how he uses 'partial linking' to add more [rhymes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyme) to a verse.[^cite_note-EdwardsRap91-173] Jay-Z's early style is described by [*Vibe*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vibe_\(magazine\)) as "a distinctly [Das EFX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_EFX)-type, stiggety style" on his 12" single "Can't Get With That",[^cite_note-174] referring to the fast rhythms and vocal delivery of the group Das EFX. He is also known to write lyrics in his head, as described by [Pusha T](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pusha_T) of [Clipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clipse) in *How to Rap*,[^cite_note-EdwardsRap144-175] a style popular with many [MCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MC) such as [The Notorious B.I.G.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Notorious_B.I.G.),[^cite_note-EdwardsRap144-175] [Everlast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everlast_\(musician\)),[^cite_note-EdwardsRap144-145-176] [Bobby Creekwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Creekwater)[^cite_note-EdwardsRap145-177] and [Guerilla Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerilla_Black).[^cite_note-EdwardsRap145-177] [Shock G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_G) of [Digital Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Underground) describes Jay-Z's live performance style, saying he "rarely breaks a sweat, and instead uses smoothness and clever wordplay to keep the audience interested and entertained".[^cite_note-EdwardsRap292-178] Jay-Z's fast rapping technique, also known as the ["triplet time"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapping#Rhythm), was developed during his early years of creating music with former mentor [Jaz-O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaz-O).[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180]

## Business career

Jay-Z has also established himself as a successful entrepreneur with a business empire spanning a variety of industries from clothing lines, beverages, real estate, sport teams, and record labels.[^cite_note-181] In an interview, he stated that "my brands are an extension of me. They're close to me. It's not like running [GM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors), where there's no emotional attachment."[^cite_note-182]

Jay-Z and [Damon Dash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Dash) are the founders of the urban clothing brand [Rocawear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocawear).[^cite_note-NYTimes1-183] Rocawear has clothing lines and accessories for men, women and children. The line was taken over by Jay-Z in early 2006 following a falling out with Dash. ## Legal issues

### 1999 Lance "Un" Rivera stabbing

On December 2, 1999, Jay-Z, who had come to believe that record executive [Lance "Un" Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Rivera) was behind the bootlegging of *Vol. 3...*, allegedly stabbed him at the Kit Kat Klub, a now-defunct night club in [Times Square](31389.md), New York City, during a release party for [Q-Tip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Q-Tip_\(rapper\))'s album *[Amplified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amplified_\(Q-Tip_album\))*.[^cite_note-251] Jay-Z's associates at the party were accused of causing a commotion within the club, which Jay-Z allegedly used as cover while he stabbed Rivera in the stomach with a five-inch (127 mm) blade.[^cite_note-252] He surrendered to police the following evening and was placed under arrest, although he was soon released on \$50,000 [bail](170652.md).[^cite_note-253][^cite_note-254] When he was indicted in [Manhattan Criminal Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Criminal_Court) in late January 2000, he pleaded not guilty; he and his lawyers contended that they had witnesses and videotapes proving he had been nowhere near Rivera during the incident. Nevertheless, he later pleaded guilty to third-degree assault and accepted a three-year [probation](406786.md) sentence.[^cite_note-guiltyplea-255]

Jay-Z later addressed the case in his 2010 book *Decoded*:

> One night I went to Q-Tip's solo album release party and at some point in the night, I ran into the guy everyone's been telling me is behind the bootleg. So I approached him. When I told him what I suspected, to my surprise, he got real loud with me right there in the middle of the club. It was strange. We separated and I went over to the bar. I was sitting there like, "No the fuck this nigga did not..." I was talking to people, but I was really talking to myself out loud, just in a state of shock. Before I even realized what I was doing, I headed back over to him, but this time I was blacking out with anger. The next thing I knew, all hell had broken loose in the club. That night the guy went straight to the police and I was indicted. \[...] There was no reason to put my life on the line, and the lives of everyone who depends on me, because of a momentary loss of control. \[...] I vowed to never allow myself to be in a situation like that again.[^cite_note-256]

In 2023, Rivera stated that Jay-Z lied, and was not the one that stabbed him: "No. Jay-Z was not the guy that actually stabbed me that night (...) it's never been his history".[^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258][^cite_note-259] Rapper Nas, who feuded with Jay-Z in the early 2000s, had also claimed in his 2001 diss track "[Ether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ether_\(song\))" that Jay-Z was not Rivera's stabber. Rivera commented on this, stating, "The reality to it is Nas — and I don't know why didn't nobody believe him — on 'Ether' he talked about the incident. He let people know like, 'Yo, your man stabbed Un and you took the blame for it.'"[^cite_note-260]

Jay-Z with his mother Gloria Carter in September 2011

Jay-Z has said that "the greatest form of giving is anonymous to anonymous". In 2013, author [dream hampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_hampton), who co-wrote Jay-Z's book *[Decoded](#Further_reading)*, revealed that Jay-Z had established a trust fund for [Sean Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Bell_shooting_incident)'s children.[^cite_note-270]

Jay-Z donated to bail out protestors arrested during 2015 activism against [police brutality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality).[^cite_note-271][^cite_note-272]

In March 2020, Jay-Z donated \$1 million through the Shawn Carter Foundation to aid in relief during the [COVID-19 pandemic in New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_New_York_\(state\)).[^cite_note-273] In April 2020, along with [Meek Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meek_Mill), he donated over 100,000 face masks to U.S. prisons to assist in protecting prison inmates from [COVID-19](63030231.md).[^cite_note-274]

## Political involvement



Jay-Z with [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) and [Bruce Springsteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Springsteen) at a rally in 2012

In 2006, Jay-Z appeared with [Russell Simmons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Simmons) in a public service announcement denouncing racism and [antisemitism](1078.md), sponsored by the [Foundation for Ethnic Understanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_for_Ethnic_Understanding).[^cite_note-275] In 2008, Jay-Z was involved in the [2008 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election), supporting enhanced voter participation.[^cite_note-276] He supported the 2008 presidential candidacy of [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) and performed voter-drive concerts financed by the Democrats' campaign.[^cite_note-277] He also became acquainted with Obama, who in June 2008 said, "Every time I talk to Jay-Z, who is a brilliant talent and a good guy, I enjoy how he thinks. That's somebody who is going to start branching out and can help shape attitudes in a real positive way."[^cite_note-278]

During the [2010 mid-term elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_elections), Jay-Z appeared with other artists in a [HeadCount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HeadCount) advertisement, urging voters, especially younger ones, to register and vote.[^cite_note-279] In May 2012, Jay-Z endorsed President Obama's support of [same-sex marriage](92656.md)[^cite_note-280] and participated in his re-election campaign.[^cite_note-281]

Singer, actor, and [civil rights](49001.md) activist [Harry Belafonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Belafonte) was critical of Jay-Z and Beyoncé for what he saw as their safe political stances, saying that they "have turned their back on social responsibility".[^cite_note-282]

In the [2016 U.S. presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_U.S._presidential_election), Jay-Z and Beyoncé appeared at a [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) rally in [Cleveland](5951.md).[^cite_note-283] Clinton praised Jay-Z for addressing racism, oppression, and the criminal justice system.[^cite_note-284] He described [Donald Trump](4848272.md) as a "superbug" and condemned remarks he perceived as racist,[^cite_note-285] but later said that Trump's rhetoric forced people to come together and address difficult issues such as [white privilege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_privilege).[^cite_note-286]
