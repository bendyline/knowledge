# Tourism in Switzerland



Switzerland is notable for its landscapes and tourism facilities ([Matterhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matterhorn) from the [Gornergrat Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gornergrat_Railway)).

Tourists are drawn to [Switzerland](26748.md)'s diverse landscape as well as the available activities, which take advantage of the [Alpine climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_climate) and [landscapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_region), in particular [skiing](39576.md) and [mountaineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpinism), but also due to the many [old town centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altstadt), with their [historic buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Switzerland) that often preserve parts of their [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) [fortifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_castles_and_fortresses_in_Switzerland), and [Roman sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_in_the_Roman_era). Switzerland is also popular for tourism-oriented railways, as well as for its [cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_cheeses_and_dairy_products) and [chocolate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_chocolate). Tourist activities are rated by the [Quality Label for Swiss Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_Label_for_Swiss_Tourism).

As of 2016, tourism accounted for an estimated 2.6% ([CHF](361362.md) 16.8 billion) of Switzerland's [GDP](12594.md), compared to 2.6% (CHF 12.8 billion) in 2001.[^cite_note-1]

## History

Further information: [Exploration of the High Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration_of_the_High_Alps), [History of the Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Alps), [Golden age of alpinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_age_of_alpinism), and [Silver age of alpinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_age_of_alpinism)

Tourism began in Switzerland with [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) [mountaineers](20341.md) climbing the main peaks of the [Bernese Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernese_Alps) in the early 19th century.

**The [Alpine Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_Club_\(UK\))** in London was founded in 1857. Reconvalescence in the Alpine, in particular from [tuberculosis](30653.md), was another important branch of tourism in the 19th and early 20th centuries: for example in [Davos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davos), [Graubünden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graub%C3%BCnden). Due to the prominence of the Bernese Alps in British mountaineering, the [Bernese Oberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernese_Oberland) was long especially known as a tourist destination. [Meiringen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiringen)'s [Reichenbach Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichenbach_Falls) achieved literary fame as the site of the fictional death of [Sir Arthur Conan Doyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Arthur_Conan_Doyle)'s [Sherlock Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes) (1893). The first organised tourist holidays to Switzerland were offered during the 19th century by Thomas Cook and [Lunn Travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunn_Poly) companies. Tourism in Switzerland had been exclusively for the rich until it became widely popular in the 20th century.[^cite_note-2]

Natural regions

- [Bernese Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernese_Alps) (notably [Eiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiger), [Mönch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6nch) and [Jungfrau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungfrau)), with glaciers (notably the [Aletsch Glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aletsch_Glacier)), deep valleys (e.g. [Aare Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aare_Gorge), [Lauterbrunnental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauterbrunnental)), waterfalls (e.g. [Mürrenbach Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCrrenbach_Falls), [Staubbach Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staubbach_Falls)), lakes (e.g. [Oeschinen Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oeschinen_Lake)) and [caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Beatus_Caves)

- [Valais Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valais_Alps) and [Chablais Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chablais_Alps) (on the left side of the [Rhone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhone) valley), contain the highest mountains of the Alps (notably [Monte Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Rosa) and the [Matterhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matterhorn) on the [border with Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy%E2%80%93Switzerland_border)), ski resorts like [Zermatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zermatt), and a [waterfall in a cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grotte_aux_F%C3%A9es)

- [Gotthard Massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Gotthard_Massif), with the [Gotthard Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Pass) at its heart, notable for the historic Gotthard routes (north–south axis), the [Matterhorn Gotthard railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matterhorn_Gotthard_Bahn) (west–east axis) and the many other high road passes ([Nufenen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nufenen_Pass), [Grimsel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grimsel_Pass), [Furka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furka_Pass), [Susten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susten_Pass), [Klausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klausen_Pass), [Oberalp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberalp_Pass) and [Lukmanier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukmanier_Pass))

- [Grison Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grison_Alps), with the [Engadin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engadin) Valley or [Lake Cauma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caumasee)

- [Eastern Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Switzerland), with [Alpstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpstein) ([Mount Säntis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A4ntis), [Ebenalp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebenalp), [Seealpsee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seealpsee)) and [Churfirsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churfirsten) mountain ranges, [Tamina Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamina_\(river\)), [Lake Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Constance), the second largest lake in the country (bordering Austria and Germany) and [Lake Walen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Walen)

- [Lake Lucerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Lucerne), the largest lake in central Switzerland, notable for the many mountain railways in the surrounding mountains, notably the [Rigi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigi_Railways) and [Pilatus Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilatus_Railway)

- [Lake Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Geneva), the largest lake in the country, notable for the [Riviera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Riviera) and the many [vineyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_wine)

- [Seeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seeland_\(Switzerland\)), the region of [Lake Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Neuch%C3%A2tel), [Lake Biel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Biel) and [Lake Morat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Morat)

- [Jura Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_Mountains) and [Table Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Jura), a moderately elevated mountainous region north of the [Swiss Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Plateau) (e.g. [Chasseral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chasseral), [Creux du Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creux_du_Van), [Lägern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A4gern), [Randen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randen_\(mountain_range\)))

- [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) ([Alpine Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_Rhine), [High Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Rhine)), the largest river in the country, is notable for the [Rhine Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine_Falls) and [Ruinaulta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruinaulta)

- [Italian Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Lakes), a group of lakes on the Italian border (lakes [Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Maggiore) and [Lugano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Lugano)), notable for being the warmest place in the country

### Hiking



Switzerland comprehends an extensive hiking trail network (here the [Schöllenen Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sch%C3%B6llenen_Gorge) on the [Gotthard route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gotthard_Pass))

See also: [Swiss hiking network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_hiking_network)

[Hiking](147313.md) is one of the main sports activities in Switzerland and is often referred to as the "national sport". About one-third of the population practice hiking regularly, with a total of 520,000,000 kilometres (320,000,000 mi) in 130 million hours being travelled every year by the [Swiss](7351032.md). Along with cycling, walking, in general, is the preferred form of mobility, regardless of social origins. The total [hiking trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiking_trail) network is about 65,000 km (40,000 mi).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Hiking trails in Switzerland offer a wide range of difficulty levels, catering to both casual walkers and experienced [mountaineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountaineer), with many routes featuring breathtaking views of the [Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Alps) and pristine natural landscapes.[^cite_note-10] Examples are:

- [Balfrin Höhenweg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfrin_H%C3%B6henweg)

- [Jura ridgeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_ridgeway)

- [Haute Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute_Route) (international)

- [Rheintaler Höhenweg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheintaler_H%C3%B6henweg)

- [Swiss Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Path)

- [Trans-Swiss Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Swiss_Trail)

- [Via Alpina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Alpina) (international)

- [Via del Mercato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_del_Mercato) (international)

- [Via Francigena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Francigena) (international)

- [Via Jacobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Jacobi)

## Statistics



Official statistics of tourism were planned in 1852, but were only realized in 1934, and continued until 2003. Since 2004, the [Federal Statistical Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Statistical_Office_\(Switzerland\)) had discontinued its statistics, but collaborates with Switzerland Tourism in the publication of yearly "Swiss Tourism Figures". In the year 2011, a total number of 4,967 registered hotels or hostels, offered a total of 240,000 beds in 128,000 rooms. This capacity was saturated to 41.7% (compared to 39.7% in 2005), amounting to a total of 38.8 million lodging nights. 14% of hotels were in [Grisons](169850.md), 12% each in the [Valais](198789.md) and [Eastern Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Switzerland), 11% in [Central Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Switzerland) and 9% in the [Bernese Oberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernese_Oberland). The ratio of lodging nights in relation to resident population ("tourism intensity", a measure for the relative importance of tourism to local economy) was largest in Grisons (8.3) and Bernese Oberland (5.3), compared to a Swiss average of 1.3. 56.4% of lodging nights were by visitors from abroad (broken down by nationality: 16.5% Germany, 6.3% United Kingdom, 4.8% United States, 3.6% France, 3.0% Italy).[^cite_note-13]

The total financial volume associated with tourism, including transportation, is estimated to [CHF](361362.md) 35.5 billion (as of 2010) although some of this comes from fuel tax and sales of motorway vignettes. The total [gross value added](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_value_added) from tourism is 14.9 billion. Tourism provides a total of 144,838 [full-time equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-time_equivalent) jobs in the entire country. The total financial volume of tourist lodging is 5.19 billion CHF and eating at the lodging provides an additional 5.19 billion. The total gross value added of 14.9 billion is about 2.9% of Switzerland's 2010 nominal [GDP](12594.md) of 550.57 billion CHF.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

The most visited Swiss tourist attractions are first, the [Rhine Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine_Falls), second, the [Berne Bear exhibit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A4rengraben) (both without entrance fee), and third, with over 1.8 million paid entries: [Zoo Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoo_Basel).
