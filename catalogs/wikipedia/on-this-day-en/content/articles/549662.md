**Cauca Department** (Spanish pronunciation: [\[ˈkawka\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish), [Spanish](26825.md): *Departamento del Cauca*) is a [department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Colombia) of Southwestern [Colombia](5222.md). Located in the southwestern part of the country, facing the Pacific Ocean to the west,[^cite_note-4] the [Valle del Cauca Department](549733.md) to the north, [Tolima Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolima_Department) to the northeast, [Huila Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huila_Department) to the east, and [Nariño Department](549702.md) to the south. Putumayo and Caqueta Departments border the southeast portion of Cauca Department as well. It covers a total area of 29,308 km<sup>2</sup> (11,316 sq mi), the 13th largest in Colombia. Its capital is the city of [Popayán](657155.md). The offshore island of [Malpelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malpelo_Island) belongs to the department. It is located in the southwest of the country, mainly in [the Andean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_Region_\(Colombia\)) and [Pacific regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Region_\(Colombia\)) (between 0°58′54″N and 3°19′04″N latitude, 75°47′36″W and 77°57′05″W longitude) plus a tiny part ([Piamonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piamonte)) in [the Amazonian region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazonian_Region). The area makes up 2.56% of the country.

Cauca Department is divided into 42 municipalities, 99 districts, 474 police posts and numerous villages and populated places. The municipalities are grouped into 27 circles and 29 notaries: a circle-based registration in Popayán and eight sectional offices based in [Bolívar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bol%C3%ADvar,_Cauca), [Caloto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caloto,_Cauca), [Puerto Tejada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Tejada,_Cauca), [Santander de Quilichao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander_de_Quilichao), [Patia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patia,_Cauca), [Guapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guapi,_Cauca) and [Silvia](10709368.md), makes up the judicial district of Popayán. This district possesses 8 judicial circuit seats in Popayán, Bolívar, Caloto, Guapi, Patia, Puerto Tejada, Santander de Quilichao and Silvia. The department makes up the constituency of Cauca.

The relief of the territory of the department of Cauca belongs to the [Andean system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_Region_\(Colombia\)) at the macro level seven distinguishing morphological units:

1. the [Pacific Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Plate)

2. the [Pubenza Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pubenza_Valley)

3. the [Western Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Occidental_\(Colombia\))

4. the [Central Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Colombia\))

5. the Popayán Plateau

6. the [Colombian Massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Massif)

7. the [Patía Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat%C3%ADa_Valley_dry_forests)

8. a small sliver of the [Amazon Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Basin)

The Pacific Plate comprises two sectors, firstly the alluvial coastal belt or platform characterized by low, swampy, mangrove forest with many rivers estuaries subject to the ebb and flow of tides, the remainder is a plain or hills comprising the western slopes of the western cordillera.

The western cordillera in Cauca extends from southwest to northeast. Among the most important landmarks are the blade of Napí, the hills of Guaduas, Munchique, and Naya, and the Cauca River Valley. The central mountain range crosses the department from south to north; relevant landmarks include Sotará Colcano, Petacas Nevado del [Huila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huila_Department), and the departmental boundary.

The highlands of Popayán, sandwiched between the Western and Central Cordilleras, is seen as a landmark within the plateau of the hill of La Tetilla. Among the most representative landmarks of the Colombian Massif, shared with the department of [Huila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huila_Department), are the Páramo del Buey, the volcanoes of Cutanga and Puracé, the peak of Paletará, and the Sierra Nevada of Coconucos. Patia Valley, where the Patia River runs north–south and framed by the Central and Western mountain ranges, extends into [Nariño Department](549702.md). The Amazonian salient corresponds to the so-called Bota Caucana, through which flows the [Japurá River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japur%C3%A1_River).

## Colombian Massif



The Valley of the Cushions, part of the [Andes Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes).

The Colombian Massif, also called the **Nudo de Almaguer**, is a mountainous section of [Andean natural region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_natural_region) formed by the convergence of two major mountain ranges, the [Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Colombia\)) and the [Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Oriental_\(Colombia\)) cordilleras. The massif extends through the departments of Cauca, [Huila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huila_Department), and [Nariño](549702.md). Towards the south, the Colombian Massif is continued by the [Pasto Massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nudo_de_los_Pastos) and towards the north it diverges forming the Central and Eastern Andes. The [Colombian Massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Massif) is a strategic national and international level, given its significance for water production, biodiversity and ecosystems, an area that represents a special conformation of the regions with more potential for development in Colombia.

Cauca Department can be divided into the following hydrographic regions:

1. The Cauca river system, consisting of five major basins: Alto Cauca, Pacific, Alto Magdalena, Patia and Caqueta. Alto Cauca, the most important, is formed by the [Cauca River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauca_River) and its tributaries: Palo, Guengué, Negro, Teta, Desbaratado, and Quilichao, Mondomo, Ovejas, Pescador, Robles, Piedras, Sucio, Palacé, Cofre, Honda, Cajibío, Piendamó, Tunia, Molino, Timbío and Blanco.

2. Patia basin, consists of the Patia River and its tributary rivers Guachinoco, Ismita, Bojoleo, El Guaba, Sambingo and Mayo.

3. Outside the Patia basin, the Pacific slope is mainly drained by the rivers Guapi, Timbiquí, Saija and Micay.

4. Alto Magdalena, whose main river is the [Páez River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1ez_River) which is fed by the rivers: San Vicente, Moras, Ullucos, Negro y Negro de Narvaez, and the streams: Toez, Símbola, Salado, Gualcar, Gallo, Macana, Honda and Totumo.

5. Caquetá basin, consists of the Caquetá River into which the rivers Cusiyaco, Cascabelito, Verdeyaco, Mandiyaco, Fragua, Cascabel, Curiaco and Pacayaco flow.

Gorgonilla and [Gorgona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorgona,_Colombia) islands are located in the Pacific Ocean and belong to Cauca Department.

The Cauca economy is based primarily on agriculture and livestock production, forestry, fishing and trade. Agriculture has been developed and modernized in the northern department, with the main crops being sugar cane, cane panela, conventional maize, rice, corn tech, banana, agave, yucca, potatoes, coconut, sorghum, cocoa, groundnut, and palm.

In the Pacific region is extracted gold, silver and platinum. Other non-precious minerals that are exploited are sulfur, asbestos, limestone, talc, gypsum and coal. The manufacturing industry is located in Popayán, [Santander de Quilichao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander_de_Quilichao), [Puerto Tejada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Tejada,_Cauca) with factories of food, beverages, dairy products, paper, packaging, [wood processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_processing), [sugar industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_industry) and paper processing for export. The main centers of commercial activity are Popayán, Santander de Quilichao, [Patia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patia,_Cauca), Puerto Tejada, [Piendamó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piendam%C3%B3,_Cauca) and [Corinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corinto,_Cauca).
