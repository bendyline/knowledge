**Kursk Oblast**[^cite_note-6] is a [federal subject](701438.md) of [Russia](25391.md) (an [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast)). Its [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center) is the [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_inhabited_localities_in_Russia) of [Kursk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk). As of the [2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census), Kursk Oblast had a population of 1,082,458.[^cite_note-2021Census-7]

## History



A [Khorovod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorovod) in Kursk, 1860, painting by [Konstantin Trutovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Trutovsky)



Waffen-SS [Panzer Division *Das Reich*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_Das_Reich) with a [Tiger I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_I) tank, in June 1943 before the [Battle of Kursk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk)

The territory of Kursk Oblast has been populated since the end of the [last ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period). Slavic tribes of the [Severians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severians) inhabited the area. From 830 the current Kursk Oblast was part of the [Rus' Khaganate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rus'_Khaganate) and [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') states. The oldest towns in the region are [Kursk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk) and [Rylsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rylsk,_Russia), first mentioned in 1032 and 1152,[^cite_note-sgk-8] respectively, both capitals of small medieval eponymous duchies.[^cite_note-army-9][^cite_note-sgk-8] In the 13th century, the region was [conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Kievan_Rus') by the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire).

In the 15th century it was part of the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania) under the [Jagiellonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagiellonian) dynasty. It was lost in the 16th-century [Muscovite–Lithuanian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscovite%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Wars) to the [Grand Duchy of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow). A real growth of the area around Kursk began soon after that, with a large migration from Central Russia after the [Russian famine of 1601–1603](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_famine_of_1601%E2%80%931603). The region was affected by [Crimean–Nogai slave raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean%E2%80%93Nogai_slave_raids_in_Eastern_Europe) in the 16th and 17th centuries. The current southwestern outskirts of the oblast with the town of [Sudzha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudzha) were part of [Sloboda Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloboda_Ukraine) and populated by Ukrainians since the mid-17th century.[^cite_note-10] Between 1708 and 1719, Kursk was part of the newly created [Kiev Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiev_Governorate). From 1719 to 1727, it was a part of [Belgorodsky Uyezd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgorodsky_Uyezd) of Kiev Governorate. From 1727, [Kursky Uyezd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursky_Uyezd) was part of [Belgorod Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgorod_Governorate).[^cite_note-11] On 23 May 1779, [Kursk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_Governorate) was established.

During [World War I](4764461.md) and the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), in 1918, it was the site of fights of anti-Bolshevik Ukrainians and [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_German_Army) against Soviet Russia. In 1918, the western portion of the current Kursk Oblast with the towns of Rylsk and Sudzha was part of the [Ukrainian State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_State).[^cite_note-army-9] [Korenevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korenevo,_Korenevsky_District,_Kursk_Oblast) was the place of signing of a [ceasefire](592591.md) between the Ukrainian State, [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) and [Soviet Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Russia) in May 1918. Kursk was the place of establishment of the [Provisional Workers' and Peasants' Government of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Workers'_and_Peasants'_Government_of_Ukraine), and Sudzha was its first seat in November-December 1918.[^cite_note-12] Sudzha remained part of [Soviet Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Ukraine) until 1922.[^cite_note-13]

The Kursk Governorate existed until 1928, when the territory of Kursk Governorate became a part of [Central Black Earth Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Black_Earth_Oblast). As Central Chernozem Oblast was very large its administration was very difficult, on 13 June 1934 it was divided into two oblasts: Kursk Oblast and Voronezh Oblast. In the period between 1934 and 1954, oblasts' borders were frequently adjusted. However, the area and borders of the oblast have remained stable from 1954.

During [World War II](32927.md), the territory of Kursk Oblast was occupied by [German](21212.md) troops from the autumn of 1941 until the summer of 1943. The [Battle of Kursk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk), which was one of the major battles of World War II, took place in the region between 5 July 1943 and 23 August 1943.

The 4th [leader of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_leaders_of_the_Soviet_Union), [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev), was born in [Kalinovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinovka,_Khomutovsky_District,_Kursk_Oblast), a village that is now part of the Kursk Oblast.

As of 2024, the [Urengoy–Pomary–Uzhhorod pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urengoy%E2%80%93Pomary%E2%80%93Uzhhorod_pipeline) in [Sudzha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudzha) was the last remaining point through which [natural gas](42827258.md) flowed from Russia to Europe via Ukraine.[^cite_note-14]

In August 2024, Ukrainian forces [crossed the border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2024_Kursk_Oblast_incursion) into Kursk Oblast during the [Russo-Ukrainian War](70149799.md) resulting in a small part of the oblast coming under [Ukrainian occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_occupation_of_Kursk_Oblast).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] In April 2025, the [Chief of Russia's General Staff](13271754.md) [Valery Gerasimov](37587470.md) reported that the Kursk Oblast had been liberated from Ukrainian troops.[^cite_note-17] According to the Governor of the Kursk Oblast Alexander Khinshtein, 288 civilians had been killed during the Ukrainian incursion into the region.[^cite_note-18]

Location of Kursk Oblast

Kursk Oblast is bordered by [Bryansk Oblast](476473.md) to the north-west (border length: 120 km (75 mi)), [Oryol Oblast](480945.md) to the north (325 km (202 mi)), [Lipetsk Oblast](480918.md) to the north-east (65 km (40 mi)), [Voronezh Oblast](473401.md) to the east (145 km (90 mi)), [Belgorod Oblast](478857.md) to the south (335 km (208 mi)), and [Sumy Oblast](1567618.md) of [Ukraine](31750.md) to the west (245 km (152 mi)).

It occupies the southern slopes of the middle-Russian plateau. The surface is hilly and intersected by [ravines](2014227.md). The central part of the oblast is more elevated than the [Seym river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seym_River) valley in the west. The average elevation is 177–225 m (581–738 ft) and the Timsko-Shchigrinsky ridge contains the highest point at 288 m (945 ft) above sea level. The low relief, gentle slopes, and mild winters make the area suitable for farming, and much of the forest has been cleared. [Chernozem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernozem) soils cover around 70% of the oblast, and [podsol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podsol) soils 26%; chernozem is among the best soils for agriculture, and podsol among the worst.&#x20;

Kursk Oblast contributes to two major drainage areas: the [Dnieper River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_River) and the [Don River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River,_Russia) (78% and 22% respectively). There are 902 rivers and streams in the oblast, with a total length of approximately 8,000 km (5,000 mi). Major rivers include the [Seym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seym_River) and the [Psyol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psyol_River). The inland waters of Kursk oblast consist of 145 artificial lakes and about 550 small ponds.

### Natural resources



Kursk Oblast landscape

Kursk Oblast is one of Russia's major producers of [iron ore](145352.md). The area of the [Kursk Magnetic Anomaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_Magnetic_Anomaly) has one of the richest iron-ore deposits in the world. [Rare earths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earths) and [base metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_metal) also occur in commercial quantities in several locations. Refractory [loam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loam), [mineral sands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_mineral_sands_ore_deposits), and [chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk) are quarried and processed in the region. The oblast's reserves of [artesian-well water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artesian_aquifer) are proving useful for medical purposes.

### Climate

The oblast's location at the center of the European part of Russia gives the region a medium continental [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate): warm summers and relatively mild winters. In July the average daytime high temperature is +19.3 °C (66.7 °F). In January the average high is −8.6 °C (16.5 °F). The average number of frost-free days ranges from 150 in the north to 160 in the south. The growing season in Kursk Oblast varies, from 180 days in the north to 195 days in the southwest. The average annual precipitation for the oblast is 584 mm (23.0 in), but it ranges from 634 mm (25.0 in) in the northwest to about 500 mm (20 in) or less in the southeastern corner. Rainfall peaks during June and July. The snow depth in Kursk Oblast differs considerably, from 300–400 mm (12–16 in) in the north of the oblast, to 150–250 mm (5.9–9.8 in) in the south. Annual sunshine is 1775 hours.

### Flora and fauna

Kursk Oblast forms a part of the Eastern European forest-steppe. One-quarter of Kursk oblast was once heavily wooded. Hardwood timbers included [oak](39696.md), [ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_tree), and [elm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elm). Now forests cover only 10% of the oblast. Animals native to the area are numerous. [Pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pike_\(fish\)), [bleak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alburnus), and [perch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perch) abound in local rivers. [Otter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otter) and [badger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badger), as well as wild [boar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boar), [red deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_deer), and [roe deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe_deer) remain numerous in many parts of the area.

### Education

The largest universities of Kursk Oblast are [Kursk State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_State_University), [Kursk State Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_State_Technical_University), [Kursk State Medical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_State_Medical_University) and Kursk State Agricultural Academy, all of which are located in the city of Kursk. ## Politics



Kursk Oblast Duma seat, June 2012

During the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) period, the high authority in the oblast was shared between three persons: The first secretary of the Kursk CPSU Committee (who in reality had the biggest authority), the chairman of the oblast Soviet (legislative power), and the Chairman of the oblast Executive Committee (executive power). In 1991, CPSU lost power, and the head of the Oblast administration, and eventually the governor was appointed/elected alongside elected [regional parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_parliaments_of_Russia).

The Charter of Kursk Oblast is the fundamental law of the region. The [Kursk Oblast Duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_Oblast_Duma) is the province's standing legislative (representative) body. The Legislative Assembly exercises its authority by passing laws, resolutions, and other legal acts and by supervising the implementation and observance of the laws and other legal acts passed by it. The highest executive body is the Oblast Government, which includes territorial executive bodies such as district administrations, committees, and commissions that facilitate development and run the day to day matters of the province. The Oblast administration supports the activities of the Governor who is the highest official and acts as guarantor of the observance of the oblast Charter in accordance with the [Constitution of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Russia).



Russian children lay flowers at a memorial to children [allegedly killed by Ukrainian forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegations_of_genocide_in_Donbas) in [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas), a state-sponsored event in Kursk in July 2023

The pro-government [United Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Russia) Party and the [Communist Party of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Russian_Federation) are Kursk Oblast's major political parties. Traditionally, the Communist Party is the strongest in the Oblast's rural area.

## Economy



Shops in [Kurchatov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurchatov,_Russia) in 2013

### Industry

The oblast's industrial production dropped rapidly during the 1990s, as an industrial crisis was stimulated by the nationwide economic crisis which followed the collapse of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). However, by the end of the decade output was increasing. Moreover, the manufacturing sector, despite a sagging economy in the late 20th century, continues to account for about 40% of the oblast's [GDP](12594.md). The engineering, electric-power, metal-working, chemicals, and food processing are the dominant industries.

The Mikhailovsky Mining Plant is one of the largest [iron ore](145352.md) mining and processing facilities in Russia.[^cite_note-31]

The [Kursk Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_Nuclear_Power_Plant) is one of the three biggest nuclear power plants in Russia and one of the four biggest [electricity producers](31006441.md) in the country.

### Agriculture

Most of the main farming areas are used for natural pastures or cultivation, which involves mainly [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [sugar beet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_beet), and [fodder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fodder) crops. The main categories of productive holdings are wheat farms, dairy farms, poultry farms, and beef cattle. Agricultural lands cover 23,000 km<sup>2</sup> (8,900 sq mi), or 77% of the oblast's territory.

Kursk lies at the heart of Russia's [Central Black Earth Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Black_Earth_Region), so-named for its rich [black soils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernozem).[^cite_note-32]

### Transportation

The transportation industry of Kursk Oblast, with easy access to national and international markets, is the basis for the oblast's development. The most important modes of transport throughout the oblast are railway and road. Region roads serve towns and rural settlements through a 5,600 km (3,500 mi) road network. An airport in the oblast was opened to international flights in July 1997.

The oblast's railroads are one of the most important parts of the transportation system. They are part of the [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Railway) and [South Eastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Eastern_Railway_\(Russia\)) systems. Two major rail links pass through the oblast: Moscow–[Kharkiv](2788558.md) and [Kyiv](585629.md)–[Voronezh](467218.md). The total length of the railway network is 1,100 km (680 mi). The operational length of the railways is 1,561.2 km (970.1 mi), of which 500 km (310 mi) are access roads. The length of the electrified lines is 242 km (150 mi). The density of the railroads in Kursk Oblast is one of the highest in Russia. The largest railway junctions are Kursk, [Lgov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lgov,_Kursk_Oblast), and [Kastornoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastornoye). There are sixty-five railway stations in the oblast.

## Tourism



The [Maryino Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryino_Estate,_Kursk_Oblast) was built by the [princely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Russian_princely_families) [Baryatinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yury_Baryatinsky) family in the 19th century

Kursk Oblast's most prominent natural attraction is the [Central Black Earth Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Black_Earth_Nature_Reserve), which is well-suited for hiking and other outdoor activities. The Oblast's forests and other undeveloped areas are ideal for hunting, fishing, and camping. Traditional art and architecture are preserved in the town-museum of [Rylsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rylsk,_Russia) and other historical towns of Kursk Oblast.
