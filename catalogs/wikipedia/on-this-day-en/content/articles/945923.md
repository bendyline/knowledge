The **United States men's national ice hockey team**, also known as **Team USA**,[^cite_note-3] represents the [United States](3434750.md) in men's international ice hockey. The team is controlled by [USA Hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Hockey), the governing body for organized [ice hockey in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_in_the_United_States). As of June 2026, the team is ranked 3rd in the [IIHF World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF_World_Ranking).[^cite_note-4]

Team USA has won gold medals at the [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1960_Winter_Olympics), [1980](1400841.md), and [2026](75053831.md) Olympics. Their victory at the 1980 Olympics, known as the [Miracle on Ice](295721.md), is widely regarded as one of the greatest upsets in sports history, when a team composed largely of amateur and collegiate players defeated the heavily favored [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_men's_national_ice_hockey_team) en route to the gold medal.[^cite_note-5] In 2026, the United States won its first Olympic gold since 1980 after defeating [Canada](744439.md) in overtime in the final.[^cite_note-6] Team USA has won silver medals at the Olympics eight times overall, including most recently at the [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2002_Winter_Olympics) and [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2010_Winter_Olympics) Olympics. The US has also won the [World Cup of Hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Cup_of_Hockey), defeating Canada in the finals at the [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_World_Cup_of_Hockey) tournament. The team's most recent medal at the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Hockey_World_Championships) came in with a historic gold in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_IIHF_World_Championship),[^cite_note-7] their first victory in the tournament since 1960 and third overall (also in [1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933_World_Ice_Hockey_Championships)).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Unlike many European nations, the United States has often sent less experienced rosters to the World Championships, largely because the tournament overlaps with the [NHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL) playoffs and because not all eligible players choose to participate. By the 2020s, however, USA Hockey began placing greater emphasis on the tournament and assembling stronger, more competitive teams.[^cite_note-10] Overall, the team has collected twelve Olympic medals (three of them gold), 21 World Championship medals (three of them gold, including [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1960_Winter_Olympics)), and it reached the semi-final round of the Canada Cup/World Cup five times, twice advancing to the finals and winning gold once.[^cite_note-auto-11] Until 2025, the US had never reached a World Championship gold medal game, having lost in the semi-final round twelve times since the IIHF introduced a playoff system in 1992; this included six semi-finals appearances in ten tournaments from 2013 through 2023, and three consecutive in 2021, 2022, and 2023.

The US is one of the most successful national ice hockey teams in the world and a member of the so-called ["Big Six"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Six_\(ice_hockey\)), the unofficial group of the six strongest men's ice hockey nations, along with Canada, the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic_men's_national_ice_hockey_team), [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland_men's_national_ice_hockey_team), [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_men's_national_ice_hockey_team), and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_ice_hockey_team).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

## History

### Early history

The United States first entered international ice hockey competition at the [1920 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1920_Summer_Olympics) in [Antwerp](32149462.md), where the sport made its Olympic debut, earning a silver medal behind [Canada](744439.md). American teams continued to be competitive throughout the 1920s and early 1930s, claiming silver medals at the [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1924_Winter_Olympics) and [1932 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1932_Winter_Olympics), as well as at the [1931 IIHF World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_Ice_Hockey_World_Championships). In this era, the US national squads were typically composed of players drawn from amateur or collegiate programs, often representing leading American clubs rather than a permanent national team.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]



The 1936 US Olympic team

In [1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933_Ice_Hockey_World_Championships), the United States won its first IIHF World Championship title when the Massachusetts Rangers (aka Boston Olympics) defeated Canada in [Prague](23844.md). Sherman Forbes scored early, but the game remained deadlocked after regulation until [John Garrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Garrison_\(ice_hockey\)) netted the winning goal in a mandatory 10-minute overtime. The US squad, coached by [Walter A. Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_A._Brown) and backed by goaltender [Gerry Cosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerry_Cosby), edged Canada–a perennial powerhouse–to claim gold in front of some 12,000 spectators.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

Following their breakthrough in 1933, the United States remained a consistent contender on the international stage but often fell just short of the top prize. The Americans earned silver medals at the [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_Ice_Hockey_World_Championships), [1939](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939_Ice_Hockey_World_Championships), and [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_Ice_Hockey_World_Championships) IIHF World Championships, as well as at the [1952](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1952_Winter_Olympics) and [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1956_Winter_Olympics) Winter Olympics.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Many of these squads were built around standout amateur and collegiate players–such as [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) star [John Mayasich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mayasich), who led the US in scoring at the 1956 Cortina d'Ampezzo Games.[^cite_note-23]

### Olympic gold medals



The United States against the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_ice_hockey_team) in the final round of the [1960 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Winter_Olympics)

The United States claimed gold at the [1960 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_1960_Winter_Olympics) in [Squaw Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squaw_Valley,_Placer_County,_California), [California](5407.md). At those Games, the Americans captured gold by defeating teams including the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_men's_national_ice_hockey_team), Canada, [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_men's_national_ice_hockey_team), and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_ice_hockey_team). Because this achievement was later overshadowed by the more famous 1980 victory in Lake Placid, the 1960 championship became known as the "Forgotten Miracle."[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]



[Mike Ramsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Ramsey_\(ice_hockey\)) handling the puck in the *[Miracle on Ice](295721.md)* game

The American ice hockey team's greatest success was the "[Miracle on Ice](295721.md)" at the [1980 Winter Olympics](1400841.md) in [Lake Placid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Placid,_New_York), [New York](8210131.md), when American college players defeated the heavily favored seasoned professionals from the Soviet Union on the way to a gold medal. Though ice hockey is not a major sport in most areas of the United States, the "Miracle" is often listed as one of the all-time greatest American sporting achievements.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] The US clinched the gold medal by defeating [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland_men's_national_ice_hockey_team) in the final game.[^cite_note-29] Under the leadership of coach [Herb Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herb_Brooks), forward [Mark Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Johnson_\(ice_hockey\)) led the team in scoring, while goaltender [Jim Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Craig_\(ice_hockey\)) led all netminders in both saves and save percentage.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] The team's improbable triumph later inspired the critically acclaimed 2004 film [Miracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracle_\(2004_film\)), which brought the story of the "Miracle on Ice" to a new generation of fans.[^cite_note-32]

### World Cup of Hockey championship and NHL involvement

The United States ice hockey team experienced a spike in talent in the 1980s and 1990s, with future [NHL](21809.md) stars (many who would later be inducted into the [Hockey Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_Hall_of_Fame)) including [Tony Amonte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Amonte), [Chris Chelios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Chelios), [Derian Hatcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derian_Hatcher), [Brett Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Hull), [Pat LaFontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_LaFontaine), [John LeClair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_LeClair), [Brian Leetch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Leetch), [Mike Modano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Modano), [Mike Richter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Richter), [Jeremy Roenick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Roenick), [Kevin Stevens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Stevens), [Keith Tkachuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Tkachuk), and [Doug Weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Weight). Although the US finished no higher than fourth in any World or Olympic event from 1981 through 1994 (unlike other teams that used professionals, the US team was limited to amateurs at these tournaments), that long drought set the stage for a breakthrough on hockey's biggest professional stage. After a runner-up finish in the [1991 Canada Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Canada_Cup), the Americans finally broke through with a landmark victory at the [1996 World Cup of Hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_World_Cup_of_Hockey), the first edition of the tournament that replaced the [Canada Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Cup). Coached by [Ron Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Wilson_\(ice_hockey,_born_1955\)), Team USA stunned the hockey world by defeating a powerhouse Canadian squad in a best-of-three final. After dropping Game 1, the Americans stormed back with two straight wins on Canadian ice, including a dramatic 5–2 clincher in Montreal. Goaltender Mike Richter delivered a legendary performance, turning aside a barrage of shots and earning tournament MVP honors, while Brett Hull paced the offense with seven goals and Tony Amonte netted the series-winning goal late in Game 3.[^cite_note-33] Captain Chris Chelios anchored a formidable blue line that also featured Brian Leetch, while a deep forward corps led by Keith Tkachuk, John LeClair, and Mike Modano overwhelmed opponents with speed and scoring depth. The victory marked the United States' first senior men's title in a best-on-best tournament, signifying a coming of age for American hockey and proving that the US could defeat Canada and the world's elite on the international stage.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

Six years later, after the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) and NHL arranged to accommodate an Olympic break in the NHL schedule, the US earned a silver medal at the [2002 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2002_Winter_Olympics) with a roster that included NHL stars [Adam Deadmarsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Deadmarsh), [Chris Drury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Drury), [Brian Rafalski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Rafalski), and [Brian Rolston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Rolston). However, by [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2006_Winter_Olympics), many of these [NHL players](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Hockey_League_All-Star_Game) had retired or had declined with age. Though the 2006 Olympic team finished a disappointing 8th, it was more of a transitional team, featuring young NHL players like [Rick DiPietro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_DiPietro), [John-Michael Liles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John-Michael_Liles), and [Jordan Leopold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Leopold).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]



Team USA taking on [Canada](744439.md) at the 2010 Winter Olympics

The [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2010_Winter_Olympics) US Olympic team was composed of much younger and faster players than teams of previous years, including [David Backes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Backes_\(ice_hockey\)), [Dustin Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dustin_Brown_\(ice_hockey\)), [Jack Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Johnson_\(ice_hockey\)), [Patrick Kane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Kane), [Phil Kessel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Kessel), [Zach Parise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Parise), [Joe Pavelski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Pavelski), [Bobby Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Ryan), [Paul Stastny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Stastny), and [Ryan Suter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Suter). The team also had a solid group of veterans that included such stars as goalie [Ryan Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Miller), defenseman [Brian Rafalski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Rafalski), and team captain [Jamie Langenbrunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Langenbrunner). The US team upset Team Canada 5–3 in the round-robin phase of the tournament and went into the single elimination phase of the tournament as the number-one seeded team. After beating Finland 6–1, the US advanced to the gold medal game, where they lost in overtime 3–2 to Canada to claim the silver medal. The gold medal game between Canada and the US was watched by an estimated 27.6 million US households. This was the most watched hockey game in America since the 1980 "Miracle on Ice" game, including any [Stanley Cup Final](3314237.md) or [NHL Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_Winter_Classic) broadcast.[^cite_note-39]

The United States finished fourth at the [2014 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2014_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament), falling to Finland in the bronze medal game.[^cite_note-40] In a preliminary-round game against [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_men's_national_ice_hockey_team), [T. J. Oshie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._J._Oshie) became the centerpiece of one of the most memorable shootouts in Olympic history. With the game tied 2–2 after regulation and overtime, Team USA coach [Dan Bylsma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Bylsma) repeatedly turned to Oshie against Russian goaltender [Sergei Bobrovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Bobrovsky). Oshie opened the shootout as the first of three initial shooters, followed by [James van Riemsdyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_van_Riemsdyk) and Joe Pavelski. When the score remained deadlocked after the first three rounds, international rules allowed coaches to reuse shooters – and Bylsma kept sending Oshie. The American forward took five consecutive attempts, converting four of his six total shots, including the eighth-round winner that sealed a 3–2 US victory.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

### NHL players out

The NHL pulled out of the [Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2018_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament) for the 2018 competition in a dispute over insurance and the IOC's [ambush marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambush_marketing) restrictions, prohibiting the national teams from inviting any player it held under contract. The American team was put at a particular disadvantage, as more than 31% of NHL players are Americans (in comparison, only 4.1% are Russians). As a result, the US entered the tournament with a hastily assembled team of free agents, players from European leagues, [AHLers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) on one-way contracts, and college players.[^cite_note-44] The team proved unsuccessful, losing to [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia_men's_national_ice_hockey_team) and the [Olympic Athletes from Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Athletes_from_Russia_at_the_2018_Winter_Olympics#Ice_hockey) in the preliminary round and being eliminated by the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic_men's_national_ice_hockey_team) in the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-45] The Russian team benefited most from NHL's absence and ultimately won the tournament with a team that was composed primarily of [SKA Saint Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SKA_Saint_Petersburg) and [HC CSKA Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HC_CSKA_Moscow) players from the Russia-based [KHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kontinental_Hockey_League) and [featured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2018_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_team_rosters#Olympic_Athletes_from_Russia) ex-NHL all-stars [Pavel Datsyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Datsyuk), [Ilya Kovalchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilya_Kovalchuk) and [Vyacheslav Voynov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Voynov) (all SKA).[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

On March 31, 2021, [Stan Bowman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Bowman) was appointed the general manager of the US Olympic men's hockey team for the [2022 Beijing Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2022_Winter_Olympics).[^cite_note-48] On October 26, 2021, Bowman resigned in response to the results of an independent investigation into allegations of [sexual assault](218407.md) committed by a member of the Blackhawks' video coaching staff.[^cite_note-investigation-49] The lead investigator stated that Bowman's failure to report the alleged assault had eventually led to the perpetrator committing further acts of sexual abuse.[^cite_note-50]

With the NHL pulling out at the last minute due to scheduling issues caused by the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_sports), the United States finished fifth at the [2022 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_2022_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_tournament), after a stunning shootout loss to [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_men's_national_ice_hockey_team), marking their third consecutive Olympic Games without a medal. Despite fielding a team of mostly NCAA players, the team went undefeated in the preliminary round, highlighted by a victory over rival Canada, before falling in the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-51]

### NHL players return and success

In February 2024, [Bill Guerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Guerin) was announced as general manager of Team USA for the [2025 4 Nations Face-Off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_Nations_Face-Off) and [2026 Winter Olympics](75053831.md).[^cite_note-52] In May, [Mike Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Sullivan_\(ice_hockey\)) was named head coach of the team for both competitions.[^cite_note-53] The Four Nations Face-Off marked the first best-on-best international ice hockey competition since the [2016 World Cup of Hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_World_Cup_of_Hockey), ending an almost decade-long drought brought on by the NHL's failure to stage any international tournament since 2016, the league's decision not to release players for the Olympic Games, and the regular absence of top stars from the IIHF World Championships due to NHL playoff obligations or personal choice.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] The tournament was an NHL-exclusive event, meaning only NHL players were eligible to compete, and notably, Russia was excluded because of its ongoing IIHF ban due to the [war in Ukraine](42085878.md).[^cite_note-56] The opening game against Canada set an intense tone immediately, as three fights erupted within the first nine seconds, sparked by brothers [Matthew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Tkachuk) and [Brady Tkachuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brady_Tkachuk).[^cite_note-57] Team USA captured a spirited 3–1 victory in that contest, but ultimately fell to Canada 3–2 in overtime in the championship game.[^cite_note-58] Despite the narrow defeat, the United States demonstrated that it remained a top contender heading into the next Winter Olympics, which would once again feature NHL players after a 12-year hiatus.[^cite_note-59]



[Tage Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tage_Thompson) at the [2025 IIHF World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_IIHF_World_Championship)

In 2025, the United States captured its first IIHF World Championship title since 1933, defeating [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_men's_national_ice_hockey_team) in the gold-medal game to end a remarkable 92-year drought. The Americans' 1960 Olympic gold is also officially recognized by the IIHF as a world championship as all Olympic tournaments prior to 1968 also served as World Championships, but the 2025 win marked the nation's first title at a standalone tournament since 1933.[^cite_note-60] [Tage Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tage_Thompson) scored the overtime game-winner against Switzerland to secure the historic win.[^cite_note-61]

At the [2026 Winter Olympics](77611835.md), Team USA built upon its momentum from the previous year and captured its first Olympic gold medal since 1980.[^cite_note-62] The United States finished the tournament with a perfect 6–0 record, earning notable playoff round victories over Sweden and Slovakia before defeating Canada 2–1 in overtime in the gold medal game.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] [Jack Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Hughes) scored the overtime winner to secure the championship, while goaltender [Connor Hellebuyck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_Hellebuyck) made 41 saves on 42 shots, including a key stick save on [Devon Toews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devon_Toews) late in regulation.[^cite_note-65] The victory marked not only the United States' first Olympic gold medal in men's hockey since the "Miracle on Ice" in 1980, but also its first major best-on-best international tournament title featuring NHL players since the [1996 World Cup of Hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_World_Cup_of_Hockey).[^cite_note-66] Hellebuyck and defenseman [Quinn Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinn_Hughes) were both named to the Media All-Star Team, with Hellebuyck recognized as the tournament's top goaltender and Hughes as its top defenseman.[^cite_note-67] During the on-ice celebration, the American players honored late USA Hockey alumnus and former NHL player [Johnny Gaudreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Gaudreau) by holding his jersey and including his two children in the championship team photo.[^cite_note-68]



Members of the 2026 Olympic team posing with House Speaker [Mike Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Johnson) during visit to the White House

[Following the game](77611835.md#Aftermath), the team faced criticism for allowing FBI Director [Kash Patel](62202727.md) into their locker room to celebrate with them. Additional backlash arose after players laughed during a phone call from [President Trump](4848272.md), in which he joked that he would also need to invite the [U.S. Women's Olympic team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women's_national_ice_hockey_team)—who had also won gold—or risk being impeached.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-\:0-70] The majority of the team accepted the invitation to attend the [State of the Union address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_State_of_the_Union_Address) and visit the White House, while the women's team declined. During the address Trump announced that Hellebuyck would be awarded the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom).[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] After attending the State of the Union address and White House tour amid backlash, some players such as [Jeremy Swayman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Swayman) and [Auston Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auston_Matthews) stated that Trump's comments were distasteful, while other players responded directly to questions about the situation.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] Members of the U.S. Women's Olympic team responded by emphasizing unity between the programs. Captain [Hilary Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilary_Knight) described the president's remark as a "distasteful joke" but stated that the focus should remain on the historic achievement of both teams winning Olympic gold. [Megan Keller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megan_Keller) and [Abbey Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_Murphy) similarly noted the close relationship between the men's and women's teams and said there was no underlying animosity, stressing that the dual gold medals were the defining story of the Games.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

Main article: [List of IIHF World Championship directorate award winners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_IIHF_World_Championship_directorate_award_winners)

The IIHF has given awards for each year's championship tournament to the top [goalie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goaltender), [defenseman](567684.md), and [forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_\(ice_hockey\)) (all since 1954), and most valuable player (since 2004). The following American team members have won awards.

- 1955 – [Don Rigazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Rigazio) (goalie)

- 1956 – [Willard Ikola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willard_Ikola) (goalie)

- 1959 – [Bill Cleary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Cleary_\(ice_hockey\)) (forward)

- 1960 – [Jack McCartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_McCartan) (goalie)

- 1962 – [John Mayasich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Mayasich) (defenseman)

- 1967 – [Carl Wetzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Wetzel) (goalie)

- 2004 – [Ty Conklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ty_Conklin) (goalie)

- 2014 – [Seth Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Jones) (defenseman)

- 2018 – [Patrick Kane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Kane) (MVP)

- 2021 – [Cal Petersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cal_Petersen) (goalie)

- 2025 – [Zach Werenski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zach_Werenski) (defenseman)
