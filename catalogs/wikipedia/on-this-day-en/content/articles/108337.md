**Oxnard** ([/ˈɒksnɑːrd/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/af/En-us-oxnard.ogg/En-us-oxnard.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/af/En-us-oxnard.ogg/En-us-oxnard.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a city in [Ventura County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County,_California), United States. On California's [Central Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Coast_\(California\)), it is the most populous city in Ventura County and the [22nd-most-populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_California_cities_by_population) in California. Incorporated in 1903, Oxnard lies approximately 60 miles (97 km) northwest of downtown [Los Angeles](18110.md).

It is at the western edge of the fertile [Oxnard Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Plain), adjacent to agricultural fields with strawberries, [lima beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lima_bean) and other vegetable crops. Oxnard is also a major transportation hub in [Southern California](62520.md), with [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak), [Union Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad), [Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\)), [Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines), and Intercalifornias stopping there. It also has a small regional airport, [Oxnard Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Airport) (OXR). The town also has significant connections to the nearby oil fields [Oxnard Oil Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Oil_Field) and the [West Montalvo Oil Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Montalvo_Oil_Field). The high density of oil, industry, and agricultural activities around the city has led to several [environmental issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_California).[^cite_note-\:0-9]

Oxnard's population was 202,063 in 2020,[^cite_note-10] and is largely Latino.[^cite_note-\:0-9] It is the most populous city in the [Oxnard–Thousand Oaks–Ventura, CA Metropolitan Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County,_California#Metropolitan_Statistical_Area).

Oxnard was originally a part of [Rancho El Rio de Santa Clara o la Colonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_El_Rio_de_Santa_Clara_o_la_Colonia), granted in 1837 by [Governor Alvarado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Bautista_Alvarado) to seven retired soldiers from the [Presidio of Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidio_of_Santa_Barbara).



Downtown Oxnard, early 1900s

Before the arrival of Europeans, the area was inhabited by [Chumash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chumash_\(tribe\)) Native Americans. The first European to encounter the area was explorer [João Rodrigues Cabrilho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Cabrillo), who claimed it for [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) in 1542. During the [mission period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_missions_in_California), [Mission San Buenaventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_San_Buenaventura), established in 1782, used the area for raising cattle.

Ranching began to take hold among [Californio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Californio) settlers, who lost their regional influence when California became a US state in 1850. At about the same time, the area was settled by American farmers, who cultivated [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley) and lima beans.[^cite_note-11]

[Henry T. Oxnard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_T._Oxnard), founder of [Moorhead, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorhead,_Minnesota)-based [American Crystal Sugar Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Crystal_Sugar_Company) who operated a successful [sugar beet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_beet) factory with his three brothers (Benjamin, James, and Robert) in [Chino, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chino,_California), was enticed to build a \$2 million factory on the plain inland from [Port Hueneme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Hueneme).[^cite_note-12] Shortly after the 1897 beet campaign, a new town emerged. Oxnard intended to name the settlement after the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) word for "sugar", *zachari*, but frustrated by bureaucracy, named it after himself. Given the potential growth of Oxnard, in the spring of 1898, a railroad station was built to service the plant, attracting a population of Chinese, Japanese, and Mexican laborers.[^cite_note-13] The Oxnard brothers, who never lived in their namesake city, sold the Chino and the giant red-brick Oxnard factory in 1899 for nearly \$4 million. The Oxnard factory, with its landmark twin smokestacks, operated from August 19, 1899, until October 26, 1959. Factory operations were interrupted in the [1903 Oxnard strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1903_Oxnard_strike).



Oxnard, 1908. The public library is at the right.

Oxnard was incorporated as a California city on June 30, 1903, and the public library was opened in 1907.[^cite_note-14] A neighborhood of distinctive homes, established in 1909, has been placed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Ventura_County,_California) as the [Henry T. Oxnard Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_T._Oxnard_Historic_District). Before and during World War II, the naval bases of [Point Mugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Mugu) and [Port Hueneme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Hueneme) were established in the area to take advantage of the only major navigable port on California's coast between the [Port of Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Los_Angeles) and [San Francisco Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay), and the bases in turn encouraged the development of the defense-based [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace) and communications industries.

In the mid-20th century, Oxnard grew and developed the areas outside the downtown area, including homes, industry, retail, and a new harbor named [Channel Islands Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Islands_Harbor). [Martin V. ("Bud") Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_V._\(%22Bud%22\)_Smith) (1916–2001) became an influential developer. Smith's first enterprise in 1941 was the Colonial House Restaurant (demolished 1988) and then the [Wagon Wheel Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagon_Wheel,_Oxnard,_California) in 1947 (demolished 2011).[^cite_note-Demolition-15] He was also involved in the development of the high-rise towers at the Topa Financial Plaza,[^cite_note-16] the Channel Islands Harbor, Casa Sirena Resort, the Esplanade Shopping Mall,[^cite_note-17] Fisherman's Wharf, the Carriage Square Shopping Center, the Maritime Museum, and many other hotel, restaurant and retail projects.[^cite_note-gregg-18][^cite_note-19] In the late 1970s, as the demographic shifted towards becoming a majority-Latino city, the [Ku Klux Klan](16779.md), which historically had a weak presence in Ventura County, unsuccessfully [tried to form a chapter in Oxnard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Klan_Riot_of_1978).[^cite_note-20] Also in the 70's, the Oxnard School Board lost a court case, *[Soria v. Oxnard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soria_v._Oxnard)*, aimed at addressing decades of segregationist practices in Oxnard schools.

In June 2004, the Oxnard Police Department and the [Ventura County Sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County_Sheriff) imposed a [gang injunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_injunction) over a 6.6-square-mile (17 km<sup>2</sup>) area of the [central district of the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonia,_Oxnard,_California), to restrict gang activity.[^cite_note-21] The injunction was upheld in the Ventura County Superior Court and made a permanent law in 2005.[^cite_note-22] A similar injunction was imposed in September 2006 over a 4.26-square-mile (11.0 km<sup>2</sup>) area of the south side of the city.[^cite_note-23] Prohibited activities include associating with other known gang members, [witness intimidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witness_intimidation), possessing firearms or using [gang gestures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_signal).[^cite_note-24] Since then, court decisions have made adding people to the civil orders more stringent, stemming from lawsuits in Los Angeles and Orange counties. Judges determined that it was unconstitutional for people to be added to a gang injunction without a [due process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Due_process) hearing. As a result of budget cuts due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](63136490.md), the Oxnard police stopped maintaining and enforcing the injunction in 2020.[^cite_note-25]

## Geography

Oxnard is located on the Oxnard Plain, an area with fertile soil. With its beaches, dunes, wetlands, creeks, and the [Santa Clara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara_River_\(California\)), the area contains several critical biological communities. Native plant communities include: [coastal sage scrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_sage_scrub), California Annual Grassland, and Coastal Dune Scrub species; however, most native plants have been eliminated from within the city limits to make way for agriculture and urban and industrial development. Also native to the region is the endangered [Ventura Marsh Milkvetch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_Marsh_Milkvetch), and the last self-sustaining population is in Oxnard in the center of an approved housing development.[^cite_note-Centerforplantconservation.org-26]

### Rivers

The [Santa Clara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara_River_\(California\)) separates Oxnard and Ventura. Tributaries to this river include [Sespe Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sespe_Creek), [Piru Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piru_Creek), and [Castaic Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castaic_Creek).

### Geology

Oxnard is on a [tectonically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tectonics) active plate since most of [Coastal California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_California) is near the boundaries between the [Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Plate) and [North American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Plate) Plates. The [San Andreas Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Andreas_Fault), which demarcates this boundary, is about 40 miles away.

One active [fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)) that transverses Oxnard is the [Oak Ridge Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Ridge_Fault?action=edit\&redlink=1), which straddles the [Santa Clara River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara_River_Valley) westward from the [Santa Susana Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Mountains), crosses the [Oxnard Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Plain) through Oxnard, and extends into the [Santa Barbara Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara_Channel). The coastline is subject to inundation by a [tsunami](31161.md) up to 23 feet in height.[^cite_note-27]

The fault has significantly contributed to [seismic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seismology) activity in the Oxnard region and beyond. The January 17, 1994, 6.7 M<sub>w</sub> [Northridge earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Northridge_earthquake) is believed to have occurred in the Santa Clarita extension of the Oak Ridge Fault. [Landslides](37208.md) and ridge-top shattering resulting from the Northridge earthquake were observed above [Moorpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorpark), a city 19.6 mi (31.5 km)[^cite_note-28] east of Oxnard.[^cite_note-29]

### Climate

Oxnard is the location of the [National Weather Service](286989.md) forecast office that serves the Los Angeles area.[^cite_note-30] The city is situated in a [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) (dry [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical)) climate zone, experiencing mild and relatively wet winters, and warm, dry summers, in a climate called the [warm-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warm-summer_Mediterranean_climate). Onshore breezes keep the communities of Oxnard cooler in summer and warmer in winter than those further inland. The average mean temperature is 61 °F (16 °C). The average minimum temperature is 52 °F (11 °C) and the average maximum temperature is 69 °F (21 °C). Generally, the weather is mild and dry, with around 300 days of sunshine annually. The average annual precipitation is 15.62 in (397 mm).[^cite_note-31]

### Wildlife and ecology

See also: [California coastal sage and chaparral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_coastal_sage_and_chaparral)

The area contains several critical biological communities. Native plant communities include [coastal sage scrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_sage_scrub), California Annual Grassland, and Coastal Dune Scrub species; most native plants have been eliminated from within the city limits to make way for development. Also native to the region is the endangered [Ventura Marsh Milkvetch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_Marsh_Milkvetch), with the last self-sustaining population in Oxnard being at the center of a housing development.[^cite_note-Centerforplantconservation.org-26]

The balance of wildlife in Oxnard is similar to most places in southern California, with small mammals being common in urbanized areas, like squirrels, raccoons, and skunks. Coyotes prey on these smaller mammals. Small birds and mammals can be food for stray, feral, and pet dogs and cats.[^cite_note-34]

### Environmental issues

Oxnard has more coastal [power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_power_stations_in_California) than any other city in California, with three [fossil-fuel power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil-fuel_power_plant) providing energy for cities in both Ventura and Santa Barbara Counties.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] The [California Environmental Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Environmental_Protection_Agency) (CalEPA) has identified Oxnard as a city excessively burdened by multiple sources of pollution.[^cite_note-puente1-37] Two of the power plants use ocean water cooling.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] The [Office of Environmental Health Hazard Assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Office_of_Environmental_Health_Hazard_Assessment) (OEHHA) has categorized much of Oxnard in the top 10 percent of ZIP codes most negatively impacted by pollution in the state.[^cite_note-puente1-37][^cite_note-40] In May 2015, the Oxnard City Council unanimously voted to extend the city moratorium on power plant construction. This moratorium extension occurred due to NRG/Southern California Edison's proposal, also called the Puente Power Project,[^cite_note-puente1-37] to construct a new fossil-fuel power plant. The following day, an NRG representative stated their case to replace the old power generation plant at [Mandalay Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandalay_State_Beach) with a new, hi-tech, much cleaner, and more efficient plant.[^cite_note-41]

[Pesticides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pesticides) are used in the agricultural fields surrounding Oxnard, as the area is one of the nation's leading [strawberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberry) producers, with agriculture being one of the top contributors to Oxnard's economy. Strawberries depend on large applications of fumigants containing pesticides. The Center for Health Journalism reported four ZIP codes with the highest pesticide use in the state clustered around Oxnard.[^cite_note-center-42]

### Architecture

The historical architectural styles of Oxnard ranch family homes are Victorian era, Italian style, and [Carpenter Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpenter_Gothic).[^cite_note-43] In the [Henry T. Oxnard Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_T._Oxnard_Historic_District), there are five [Prairie School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prairie_School) and eight [Tudor Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_Revival_architecture) homes.[^cite_note-nrhpdoc-44] The district includes [Mission/Spanish Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission/Spanish_Revival_architecture), [Bungalow/craftsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungalow/craftsman_architecture), [Colonial Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Revival_architecture), and other architecture.[^cite_note-npgallery.nps.gov-45]

### Cityscape

Oxnard is a combination of neighborhoods and urban development focused on the downtown, coastline, and harbor areas.[^cite_note-46] The city's main land uses are industrial, residential, commercial, and open space.[^cite_note-47] One and two-story buildings characterize the city. The two tallest buildings in the county are in the northern part of the city at Topa Financial Plaza. The fourteen-floor high-rise was built in 1973, and the 21-floor high-rise was built in 1986.[^cite_note-48] The city is surrounded by agricultural land and the Pacific Ocean, as well as the [Santa Clara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clara_River_\(California\)). The city's primary development lies along Highway 101 and the other main roads.[^cite_note-49]

The [Henry T. Oxnard Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_T._Oxnard_Historic_District) is a 70-acre (28 ha) [historic district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_district_\(United_States\)) that is listed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) in Oxnard. Covering F and G Streets between Palm and 5th Sts., in the city, the district includes 142 houses, 139 which are "[contributing buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contributing_buildings)" and includes homes built mostly between 1906 and 1925.[^cite_note-npgallery.nps.gov-45] It contains abundant [American-Craftsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American-Craftsman?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revival_architecture) architecture. Eric Andrist, owner of the district's [Henry Levy House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Levy_House) since 2021, has created a new website with a database of all of the houses and their basic stats and histories. He found that the original research to create the historic district was full of errors and is setting out to find documentation to correct it all with evidence including old news articles.[^cite_note-50]

Ormond Beach is a beach along the Oxnard coast. The beach, which stretches for two miles,[^cite_note-51] adjoins the Ormond Wetlands, some farmland, and power plant remains. It covers the area in between Points [Hueneme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Hueneme) and [Mugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Mugu) and is a well-known birding area. The beach historically contained marshes, salt flats, sloughs, and lagoons, but surrounding agriculture and industry have drained, filled, and degraded the beach and wetlands. A dune-transition zone-marsh system is still along much of the beach.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-Kelley-53]

## Economy

The [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_system) of Oxnard includes [defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_contractor), [international trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade), agriculture, manufacturing, and tourism. Oxnard is a manufacturing center in the [Greater Los Angeles Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles_Area). The Port of Hueneme is the only deep-harbor commercial port between Los Angeles and San Francisco and moves trade within the [Pacific Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Rim) economies. Companies utilizing the Port include [Del Monte Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Monte_Foods), [Chiquita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiquita), [BMW](3772.md), [Land Rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Rover), and [Jaguar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_Cars).[^cite_note-65] Other industries include finance, transportation, the high tech industry, and energy, particularly petroleum. Two large active oil fields underlie the city and adjacent areas: the [Oxnard Oil Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Oil_Field), east of the city along 5th Street, and the [West Montalvo Oil Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Montalvo_Oil_Field) along the coast to the west of town. Tenby Inc.'s Oxnard Refinery, on 5th Street east of Del Norte Avenue, processes oil from both fields.[^cite_note-66]

### Agriculture

"The areas studied showed a high percentage of Group I soils, primarily located on the relatively flat [Oxnard Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Plain). The Oxnard Plain, because of these high-quality agricultural soils, coupled with a favorable climate, is considered one of the most fertile areas in the world."[^cite_note-76]

In 1995, SOAR (Save Open Space and Agricultural Resources) was initiated by farmers, ranchers, and citizens of Ventura County to keep land in the Oxnard Plain from development.[^cite_note-Schniepp-77]

#### Strawberries



Strawberry field

The [Oxnard Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Plain) is well known for its [strawberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberry). According to the [USDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Agriculture), Oxnard is California's largest strawberry producer, supplying about one-third of the State's annual strawberry volume.[^cite_note-78] From the end of September through the end of October, strawberries are planted and harvesting occurs from mid-December through mid-July in Oxnard. The peak harvesting season in California runs from April through June when up to 10 million pint baskets of strawberries are shipped daily.[^cite_note-79] The state of California supplies over 85 percent of U.S. strawberries, with the U.S. supplying a quarter of total world production of strawberries.[^cite_note-80]

The annual [California Strawberry Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Strawberry_Festival)[^cite_note-81] features vendors as well as food items based on the fruit such as strawberry nachos, strawberry pizza, strawberry funnel cake, strawberry sundaes, and strawberry champagne.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

[Pests that attack this crop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pests_of_California_strawberry) are economically impactful in this town.[^cite_note-NatIPM-Cal-straw-84] Much of the research and effort is expended here and in [Watsonville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watsonville,_California) and [Salinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinas,_California).[^cite_note-NatIPM-Cal-straw-84] Economically significant insects include the Greenhouse Whitefly (*[Trialeurodes vaporariorum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trialeurodes_vaporariorum)*).[^cite_note-NatIPM-Cal-straw-84]

#### Cannabis

Further information: [Cannabis in California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_in_California)

In 2018, 80% of the voters approved a cannabis tax.[^cite_note-85] The city council adopted a "go slow" approach upon the legalization of recreational cannabis in California.[^cite_note-86] Companies must be licensed by the local agency and the state to grow, test, or sell cannabis and the city may authorize none or only some of these activities. Local governments may not prohibit adults who comply with state laws from growing, using, or transporting marijuana for personal use. After an initial ban, businesses focusing on manufacturing, testing, and distributing cannabis were allowed to apply for a permit to operate in July 2019.[^cite_note-87] An initial process in May 2020 to select retail proposals was challenged by unsuccessful applicants.[^cite_note-88] After revising the city ordinance, the council decided in September 2020 to allow ten retail licences to be issued.[^cite_note-89] A social equity component to maximize the ability for communities of color to benefit from the new industry as owners and investors and managers and employees as allowed by state law was not included.[^cite_note-90] The city requires dispensaries to be a minimum of 600 feet (180 m) from schools or daycare centers.[^cite_note-91] A special-use permit was approved for a retail store in an Oxnard Shores neighborhood shopping center in February 2022 amidst organized opposition from the neighborhood.[^cite_note-92] The first dispensary in the city opened in the downtown area in December 2022.[^cite_note-93]

### Oil fields

#### Oxnard

This section is an excerpt from [Oxnard Oil Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Oil_Field).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Oxnard_Oil_Field\&action=edit)]



The Oxnard Oil Field in Ventura County, California. Other oil fields are shown in dark gray.

The [Oxnard Oil Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Oil_Field) is a large and productive [oil field](2910801.md) in and adjacent to the city of Oxnard, in [Ventura County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County,_California), in the United States. Its conventional [oil reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_reserves) are close to exhaustion, with only an estimated one percent of the original oil recoverable with current technology remaining: 434,000 barrels (69,000 m<sup>3</sup>) out of an original 43.5 million. However, the reservoir includes an enormous deposit of tar sands, ultra-heavy oil classed as an unconventional petroleum reserve, and potentially containing 600 million barrels (95,000,000 m<sup>3</sup>) of oil equivalent, should it become economically feasible to extract.[^cite_note-tr25-94] Present operators on the field include Tri-Valley Oil & Gas Co., Anterra Energy Services, Inc., Chase Production Co., and [Occidental Petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occidental_Petroleum) through its Vintage Production subsidiary.[^cite_note-Oxnard_Oil_Field_2008rpt-95] As of the beginning of 2009, there were 34 active wells on the field.[^cite_note-Oxnard_Oil_Field_2008rpt-95]

#### West Montalvo

This section is an excerpt from [West Montalvo Oil Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Montalvo_Oil_Field).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=West_Montalvo_Oil_Field\&action=edit)]



The West Montalvo Oil Field in Ventura County, California. Other oil fields are shown in dark gray.

The [West Montalvo Oil Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Montalvo_Oil_Field) is a large and productive oil field on the coast of [Ventura County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County,_California), in the United States, in and adjacent to the city of Oxnard, California. Discovered in 1947,[^cite_note-West_Montalvo_Oil_Field_sold-96] it has produced approximately 50 million barrels (7,900,000 m<sup>3</sup>) of oil, and originally contained up to 650 million barrels of oil in both the onshore and offshore areas. The offshore portion of the field is exploited from wells directionally drilled from onshore near McGrath Lake, from within an enclosure above the high-tide line at [McGrath State Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGrath_State_Beach).

## Arts and culture



Oxnard Post Office

Oxnard cultural institutions include the [Carnegie Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Art_Museum), founded in 1907 as the Oxnard Public Library by philanthropist [Andrew Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Carnegie); the [Chandler Vintage Museum of Transportation and Wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandler_Vintage_Museum_of_Transportation_and_Wildlife), founded by the late [Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times) publisher [Otis Chandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otis_Chandler), and the [Channel Islands Maritime Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Islands_Maritime_Museum).[^cite_note-97] The [Henry T. ## Education

The city of Oxnard is served by 54 public school campuses, which educate more than 53,000 students in grades K–12.

### Public elementary and junior high schools

The city of Oxnard and surrounding communities are served by four different school districts that oversee education for students grades [K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindergarten)–[8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_grade). They are:

- [Hueneme School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hueneme_School_District): Serves 7,600 students at 11 campuses in South Oxnard, Port Hueneme and Oxnard beach neighborhoods.

- [Oxnard School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Elementary_School_District): Serves 18,000 students at 21 campuses throughout Oxnard.

- [Ocean View Elementary School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_View_Elementary_School_District): Serves 3,000 students at six campuses in South Oxnard.

- [Rio School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_School_District): Serves 5,000 students at eight North Oxnard and El Rio campuses.

On February 12, 2008, a shooting involving students occurred at [E.O. Green Junior High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.O._Green_Junior_High_School) in Oxnard. [Larry King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.O._Green_School_shooting) was shot in one of the classrooms, from which he was taken to St. John's Hospital and later died.[^cite_note-117]

## Infrastructure

### Sanitation

Oxnard collects and processes [trash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste), [recyclables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recyclable), and [green waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_waste) for its citizens and businesses.[^cite_note-120] The city also has a large [treatment plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_treatment) for the collection of [wastewater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wastewater) through the [sanitary sewer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitary_sewer).[^cite_note-121] An [anaerobic digester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaerobic_digestion) breaks down solids as waste moves through the plant.[^cite_note-122]

## Transportation

### Road

The [Ventura Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_Freeway) ([US 101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_101_in_California)) is the major highway running through Oxnard, connecting [Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura,_California) and [Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara,_California) to the northwest, and Los Angeles to the southeast. The [Pacific Coast Highway (State Route 1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_1) heads down the coast south to [Malibu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malibu,_California). [Highway 34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_34) (Fifth Street) connects downtown Oxnard with Camarillo by running east parallel with the [Southern Pacific Coast Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Line_\(UP\)), which carries *[Coast Starlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Starlight)*, *[Pacific Surfliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Surfliner)* and [Ventura County Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County_Line) passenger trains. [State Route 232](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_232) (Vineyard Avenue) heads northeast, providing connections to [California State Route 118](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_118) to [Saticoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saticoy,_California) and the junction with [California State Route 126](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_126) which goes to [Santa Paula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Paula,_California), [Fillmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fillmore,_California) and [Santa Clarita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clarita,_California).

### Port

Further information: [Port of Hueneme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Hueneme)

The Port of Hueneme is located south of Oxnard in the city of [Port Hueneme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Hueneme) and is jointly operated by the [United States Navy](20518076.md) and the Oxnard Harbor District. The port is the only deep water port between the [Port of Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Long_Beach) and the [Port of San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_San_Francisco), as well as the only military deep water port between [San Diego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Bay) and [Puget Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puget_Sound).

The Port of Hueneme is a shipping and receiving point for a wide variety of resources with destinations in the larger population centers of the [Los Angeles Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Basin). Resources include automobiles, [pineapple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple), and bananas. Agricultural products such as onions, [strawberries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strawberry), and flowers are shipped.[^cite_note-VCS_2016-07-21-123]

The [United States Navy](20518076.md) maintains a facility at Port Hueneme in support of the naval air station at [Point Mugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Mugu,_California) to the south, with which it comprises [Naval Base Ventura County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Base_Ventura_County). [Port Hueneme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Hueneme) is the [West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States) home of the Naval Construction Force, the "[Seabees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabee_\(US_Navy\))", as well as a link in the coastal radar system.

### Harbor

[Channel Islands Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_Islands_Harbor) provides recreational boating and commercial fishing moorings. It shares the nickname "Gateway to the Channel Islands" with [Ventura Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_Harbor) seven miles (11 km) to the north because operations that sail to the islands out of the harbors. Both harbors are vital [fishing industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_industry) harbors.

### Airport

[Oxnard Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Airport) is a general aviation airport within the city that is owned and operated by the County of Ventura. While commercial service was offered in the past, no airlines currently provide service.

### Public transit



[Oxnard Transit Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Transit_Center)

The [Oxnard Transit Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Transit_Center) serves as a major transit hub for the city and the west county.[^cite_note-124]

#### Rail

[Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\))

Six round-trip trains from the [Ventura County Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County_Line) provide commuter service to Los Angeles on weekdays during peak hours.

[Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak)

Ten round-trip [Pacific Surfliners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Surfliner) daily through Los Angeles to San Diego. Some northbound trains to Santa Barbara continue to [San Luis Obispo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Luis_Obispo). The [Coast Starlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Starlight), which travels from [Los Angeles](18110.md) to [Seattle](11388236.md), stops twice a day (once in each direction), making the west Ventura County stop here (east county stop is [Simi Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Valley_station)).

#### Bus

[Gold Coast Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Transit) District

Operates local bus service in the city of Oxnard, [Port Hueneme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Hueneme,_CA), [Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura,_CA), and [Ojai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojai,_CA). Its hub is the Oxnard Transit Center.

[^cite_note-125]

[VCTC Intercity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VCTC_Intercity)

Operates three Conejo Connection buses during peak hours towards the [Warner Center Transit Hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Center_Transit_Hub) in the San Fernando Valley, connecting with the [Metro G Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)). The Conejo Connection does not go to the Oxnard Transit Center, but instead stops at the [Esplanade Shopping Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esplanade_Shopping_Center) near Highway 101.

[^cite_note-126]

&#x20;VCTC also operates the Coastal Connection through [Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura,_California) towards [Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara,_California) and [Goleta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goleta,_California) from the Esplanade.

[^cite_note-127]

A smaller transfer center at the Centerpoint Mall on C Street for Gold Coast Transit serves South Oxnard and Port Hueneme routes. VCTC also operates the Oxnard-CSUCI route to [California State University, Channel Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_University,_Channel_Islands) and [Oxnard College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_College) from this transfer center.[^cite_note-128]

## Sister city

Three stripes, red, white, and blue from top to bottom, line the top and bottom of a white flag. Red and white stripes fill an outline of the contiguous United States in the center, while the left third is blue with a single white star in the lower half. Text in blue below the outline reads, "Oxnard," and small red text below that reads "CIUDAD HERMANA DE OCOTLAN JALISCO MEXICO."

Digital reproduction of the Oxnard-Ocotlan sister city flag presented at the Sister Cities bicentennial flag presentation around 1976.

Oxnard is [sister cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_city) with  [Ocotlán, Jalisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocotl%C3%A1n,_Jalisco) (Mexico).[^cite_note-292] This relationship was commemorated with a flag at the Sister Cities bicentennial flag presentation sometime around 1976.[^cite_note-293]
