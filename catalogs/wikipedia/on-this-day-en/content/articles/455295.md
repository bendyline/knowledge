A **sub-orbital spaceflight** is a [spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight) in which the vehicle reaches [outer space](177602.md), but its [trajectory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajectory) intersects the surface of the [gravitating body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_\(astronomy\)) from which it was launched. Hence, it will not complete one [orbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit) revolution, will not become an [artificial satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_satellite) nor will it reach [escape velocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_velocity).

For example, the path of an object launched from [Earth](9228.md) that reaches the [Kármán line](738149.md) (about 83 km \[52 mi] – 100 km \[62 mi][^cite_note-2] above [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level)), and then falls back to Earth, is considered a sub-orbital spaceflight. Some sub-orbital flights have been undertaken to test spacecraft and [launch vehicles](1306157.md) later intended for [orbital spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_spaceflight). Other vehicles are specifically designed only for sub-orbital flight; examples include crewed vehicles, such as the [X-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_X-15) and [SpaceShipTwo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceShipTwo), and uncrewed ones, such as [ICBMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile) and [sounding rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sounding_rocket).

Flights which attain sufficient velocity to go into [low Earth orbit](47568.md), and then [de-orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De-orbit) before completing their first full orbit, are not considered sub-orbital. Examples of this include flights of the [Fractional Orbital Bombardment System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_Orbital_Bombardment_System).

A flight that does not reach space is still sometimes called sub-orbital, but cannot officially be classified as a "sub-orbital spaceflight". Usually a rocket is used, but some experimental sub-orbital spaceflights have also been achieved via the use of [space guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_gun).[^cite_note-3]

## Altitude requirement



[Isaac Newton's Cannonball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton's_cannonball). Paths A and B depict a sub-orbital trajectory.

By definition, a sub-orbital spaceflight reaches an [altitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude) higher than 100 km (62 mi) above [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level). This altitude, known as the Kármán line, was chosen by the [Fédération Aéronautique Internationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9d%C3%A9ration_A%C3%A9ronautique_Internationale) because it is roughly the point where a vehicle flying fast enough to support itself with [aerodynamic lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodynamic_lift) from the [Earth's atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_atmosphere) would be flying faster than [orbital speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_speed).[^cite_note-4] The US military and [NASA](18426568.md) award [astronaut wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronaut_Badge) to those flying above 50 mi (80 km),[^cite_note-5] although the [U.S. State Department](31975.md) does not show a distinct boundary between atmospheric flight and [spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight).[^cite_note-6]

## Orbit

During [freefall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freefall) the trajectory is part of an [elliptic orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliptic_orbit) as given by the [orbit equation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbit_equation). The [perigee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perigee) distance is less than the [radius of the Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radius_of_the_Earth) *R* including atmosphere, hence the [ellipse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellipse) intersects the Earth, and hence the spacecraft will fail to complete an orbit. The major axis is vertical, the [semi-major axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-major_axis) *a* is more than *R*/2. ## Speed, range, and altitude

To minimize the required [delta-v](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta-v) (an [astrodynamical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrodynamics) measure which strongly determines the required [fuel](21188370.md)), the high-altitude part of the flight is made with the [rockets](26301.md) off (this is technically called free-fall even for the upward part of the trajectory). (Compare with [Oberth effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberth_effect).) The maximum [speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed) in a flight is attained at the lowest altitude of this free-fall trajectory, both at the start and at the end of it.

If one's goal is simply to "reach space", for example in competing for the [Ansari X Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansari_X_Prize), horizontal motion is not needed. In this case the lowest required [delta-v](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta-v), to reach 100 km altitude, is about 1.4 [km/s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Km/s). Moving slower, with less free-fall, would require more delta-v.

Compare this with [orbital spaceflights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_spaceflight): a [low Earth orbit (LEO)](47568.md), with an [altitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude) of about 300 km, needs a [speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed) around 7.7 km/s, requiring a [delta-v](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta-v) of about 9.2 km/s. (If there were no atmospheric drag the theoretical minimum [delta-v](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta-v) would be 8.1 km/s to put a [spacecraft](37910.md) into a 300-kilometer [high orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Earth_orbit) starting from a stationary point like the [South Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pole). The theoretical minimum can be up to 0.46 km/s less if launching eastward from near the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator).)

For sub-orbital spaceflights covering a horizontal distance the maximum speed and required delta-v are in between those of a vertical flight and a LEO. The maximum speed at the lower ends of the trajectory are now composed of a horizontal and a vertical component. The higher the horizontal [distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distance) covered, the greater the horizontal speed will be. (The vertical velocity will increase with distance for short distances but will decrease with distance at longer distances.) For the [V-2 rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-2_rocket), just reaching space but with a range of about 330 km, the maximum speed was 1.6 km/s. [Scaled Composites SpaceShipTwo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scaled_Composites_SpaceShipTwo) which is under development will have a similar free-fall orbit but the announced maximum speed is 1.1 km/s (perhaps because of engine shut-off at a higher altitude).

For larger ranges, due to the elliptic orbit the maximum altitude can be much more than for a LEO. On a 10,000-kilometer intercontinental flight, such as that of an intercontinental ballistic missile or possible future [commercial spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_spaceflight), the maximum speed is about 7 km/s, and the maximum altitude may be more than 1300 km. Any [spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight) that returns to the surface, including sub-orbital ones, will undergo [atmospheric reentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_reentry). The speed at the start of the reentry is basically the maximum speed of the flight. The [aerodynamic heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerodynamic_heating) caused will vary accordingly: it is much less for a flight with a maximum speed of only 1 km/s than for one with a maximum speed of 7 or 8 km/s.

## Flight duration

In a vertical flight of not too high altitudes, the time of the free-fall is both for the upward and for the downward part the maximum speed divided by the [acceleration of gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gravity), so with a maximum speed of 1 km/s together 3 minutes and 20 seconds. The duration of the [flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight) phases before and after the free-fall can vary.

For an intercontinental flight the [boost phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boost_phase) takes 3 to 5 minutes, the free-fall (midcourse phase) about 25 minutes. For an ICBM the atmospheric reentry phase takes about 2 minutes; this will be longer for any soft landing, such as for a possible future commercial flight. [Test flight 4 of the SpaceX 'Starship'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starship_integrated_flight_test_4) performed such a flight with a lift off from Texas and a simulated soft touchdown in the Indian Ocean 66 minutes after liftoff.

Sub-orbital flights can last from just seconds to days. [Pioneer 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_1) was [NASA](18426568.md)'s first [space probe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_probe), intended to reach the [Moon](19331.md). A partial failure caused it to instead follow a sub-orbital trajectory, reentering the Earth's atmosphere 43 hours after launch.[^cite_note-8]

While there are a great many possible sub-orbital flight profiles, it is expected that some will be more common than others.



The X-15 (1958–1968) was launched to an altitude of 13.7 km by a [B-52](18933037.md) [mothership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_ship), lifted itself to approximately 100 km, and then glided to the ground.

### Ballistic missiles

The first sub-orbital vehicles which reached space were [ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missiles). The first ballistic missile to reach space was the German [V-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-2_rocket), the work of the scientists at [Peenemünde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peenem%C3%BCnde), on October 3, 1942, which reached an altitude of 53 miles (85 km).[^cite_note-9] Then in the late 1940s the US and [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) concurrently developed missiles all of which were based on the V-2 Rocket, and then much longer range Intercontinental Ballistic Missiles (ICBMs). There are now many countries who possess ICBMs and even more with shorter range [Intermediate Range Ballistic Missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRBM) (IRBMs).

### Tourist flights

[Sub-orbital tourist flights](67896.md) will initially focus on attaining the altitude required to qualify as reaching space. The flight path will be either vertical or very steep, with the spacecraft landing back at its take-off site.

The spacecraft will shut off its [engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engine) well before reaching maximum altitude, and then coast up to its highest point. During a few minutes, from the point when the engines are shut off to the point where the atmosphere begins to slow down the downward acceleration, the passengers will experience [weightlessness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weightlessness).

[Megaroc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megaroc) had been planned for sub-orbital spaceflight by the [British Interplanetary Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Interplanetary_Society) in the 1940s.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

In late 1945, a group led by M. Tikhonravov K. and N. G. Chernysheva at the Soviet NII-4 academy (dedicated to rocket artillery science and technology), began work on a stratospheric rocket project, [VR-190](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_VR-190), aimed at vertical flight by a crew of two pilots, to an altitude of 200 km (65,000 ft) using captured [V-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-2_rocket).[^cite_note-12]

In 2004, a number of companies worked on vehicles in this class as entrants to the Ansari X Prize competition. The [Scaled Composites SpaceShipOne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scaled_Composites_SpaceShipOne) was officially declared by [Rick Searfoss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_A._Searfoss) to have won the competition on October 4, 2004, after completing two flights within a two-week period.

In 2005, [Sir Richard Branson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Branson) of the [Virgin Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Group) announced the creation of [Virgin Galactic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Galactic) and his plans for a 9-seat capacity SpaceShipTwo named [VSS *Enterprise*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VSS_Enterprise). It has since been completed with eight seats (one pilot, one co-pilot and six passengers) and has taken part in captive-carry tests and with the first mother-ship [WhiteKnightTwo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhiteKnightTwo), or [VMS *Eve*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VMS_Eve). It has also completed solitary glides, with the movable tail sections in both fixed and "feathered" configurations. The [hybrid rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_rocket) motor has been fired multiple times in ground-based test stands, and was fired in a powered flight for the second time on 5 September 2013.[^cite_note-13] Four additional SpaceShipTwos have been ordered and will operate from the new [Spaceport America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceport_America). Commercial flights carrying passengers were expected in 2014, but became cancelled due to the [disaster during SS2 PF04 flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VSS_Enterprise_crash). Branson stated, "\[w]e are going to learn from what went wrong, discover how we can improve safety and performance and then move forwards together."[^cite_note-14]

### Scientific experiments

A major use of sub-orbital vehicles today is as [scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific) [sounding rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sounding_rocket). Scientific sub-orbital flights began in the 1920s when [Robert H. Goddard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_H._Goddard) launched the first [liquid fueled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_fuel) rockets, however they did not reach [space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space) altitude. In the late 1940s, captured German [V-2 ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-2_rocket) were converted into [V-2 sounding rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-2_sounding_rocket) which helped lay the foundation for modern sounding rockets.[^cite_note-15] Today there are dozens of different sounding rockets on the market, from a variety of suppliers in various countries. Typically, researchers wish to conduct experiments in [microgravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microgravity) or above the atmosphere.

### Sub-orbital transportation

Research, such as that done for the [X-20 Dyna-Soar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-20_Dyna-Soar) project suggests that a semi-ballistic sub-orbital flight could travel from Europe to North America in less than an hour.

However, the size of rocket, relative to the payload, necessary to achieve this, is similar to an ICBM. ICBMs have delta-v's somewhat less than orbital; and therefore would be somewhat cheaper than the costs for reaching orbit, but the difference is not large.[^cite_note-16]

Due to the high cost of spaceflight, suborbital flights are likely to be initially limited to high value, very high urgency cargo deliveries such as [courier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courier) flights, [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) fast-response operations or [space tourism](67896.md).

The [SpaceLiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceLiner) is a hypersonic [suborbital spaceplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suborbital_spaceplane) concept that could transport 50 passengers from [Australia](4689264.md) to [Europe](9239.md) in 90 minutes or 100 passengers from Europe to [California](5407.md) in 60 minutes.[^cite_note-17] The main challenge lies in increasing the reliability of the different components, particularly the engines, in order to make their use for passenger transportation on a daily basis possible.

[SpaceX](832774.md) is potentially considering using their [Starship](59118602.md) as a sub-orbital point-to-point transportation system.[^cite_note-trati20190530-18]

## Future of crewed sub-orbital spaceflight

[Private companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_companies) such as [Virgin Galactic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Galactic), [Armadillo Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armadillo_Aerospace) (reinvented as [Exos Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exos_Aerospace)), [Airbus](26220236.md),[^cite_note-25] [Blue Origin](887418.md) and [Masten Space Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masten_Space_Systems) are taking an interest in sub-orbital spaceflight, due in part to ventures like the Ansari X Prize. [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aeronautics_and_Space_Administration) and others are experimenting with [scramjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramjet)-based [hypersonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic) aircraft which may well be used with flight profiles that qualify as sub-orbital spaceflight. [Non-profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-profit) entities like [ARCASPACE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARCASPACE) and [Copenhagen Suborbitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Suborbitals) also attempt [rocket](26301.md)-based launches.
