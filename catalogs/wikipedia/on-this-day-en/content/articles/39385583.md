The **United Arab List**,[^cite_note-11] commonly known by its [Hebrew abbreviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_abbreviation) **Ra'am** ([Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): רע"מ), is an [Islamist](15012.md) and [conservative](6675.md) political party in [Israel](9282173.md). In 2021 it joined the [thirty-sixth government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_government_of_Israel). It is currently led by [Mansour Abbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansour_Abbas).[^cite_note-\:1-12]

The party functioned as the political wing of the Southern Branch of the [Islamic Movement in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Movement_in_Israel), which supported the [Oslo I Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_I_Accord) and began participating in [Israeli parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Israel)),[^cite_note-13] until July 2026, when Ra'am announced its separation from the Islamic Movement. It had been part of the [Joint List](45229828.md), but left the alliance in January 2021.

The party announced in January 2026 that it would open to Jewish candidates on its electoral list, in advance of the [2026 Israeli legislative election](71244652.md), with [Yoav Segalovitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoav_Segalovitz) announced in late August as the party's first Jewish candidate.

## History



United Arab List election ballot, 2013

### Establishment

The party was established prior to the [1996 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Israeli_general_election), unrelated to the original [United Arab List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_List_\(1977\)) that existed in the late 1970s and early 1980s. It was joined in an electoral alliance by the [Arab Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'an_%E2%80%93_Together_for_a_New_Era) (which held two seats in the [outgoing parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Israeli_legislative_election)) and the southern faction of the [Islamic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Movement_in_Israel), led by Sheikh [Abdullah Nimar Darwish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Nimar_Darwish).[^cite_note-14] The party initially went under the title of **Mada-Ra'am**, Mada being the acronym and common name for the Arab Democratic Party. In the election, the party won four seats. During the Knesset term, the Arab Democratic Party became a faction within the United Arab List, and its name was dropped from the party title.

### Late 1990s–2010s

The [1999 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Israeli_general_election) saw the party increase its share of the vote and pick up five seats. However, internal disagreements saw three MKs leave; [Muhamad Kanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhamad_Kanan) and [Tawfik Khatib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawfik_Khatib) left and established the [Arab National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_National_Party), whilst [Hashem Mahameed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashem_Mahameed) formed the [National Unity – National Progressive Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_National_Alliance) party. In the [2003 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Israeli_legislative_election), the party's support dropped by more than a third, with the party only just crossing the electoral threshold of 2%, and winning only two seats.

Ra'am logo

Logo used by the party before 2019

For the [2006 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Israeli_legislative_election), the party entered an alliance with [Ahmad Tibi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Tibi)'s [Ta'al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ta'al) party. Running together, the alliance won four seats, three of which were taken by the United Arab List. The partys' alliance was maintained for the [2009 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Israeli_legislative_election), which initially saw the Israeli [Central Elections Committee](4456077.md) ban the party from participating,[^cite_note-15] but this was overturned by the [Supreme Court of Israel](1666510.md).[^cite_note-16] In the election, the alliance again won four seats. Shortly before the [2013 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Israeli_legislative_election), [Taleb el-Sana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taleb_el-Sana) left the party to sit as an independent [Arab Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Democratic_Party_\(Israel\)) member.

### Joint List

After the electoral threshold to gain Knesset seats was raised from 2% to 3.25%, the party joined with Hadash, [Balad](44494596.md), Ta'al, and the [Islamic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Movement_in_Israel) to form the [Joint List](45229828.md) for the [2015 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Israeli_legislative_election).[^cite_note-TOI1-17] For the [April 2019 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/April_2019_Israeli_legislative_election), it ran on a list with [Balad](44494596.md).[^cite_note-18] The party again ran as part of the Joint List in the [2020 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Israeli_legislative_election).[^cite_note-19] The alliance split in January 2021.[^cite_note-20]

### Coalition government

In the [2021 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Israeli_legislative_election), the party won four seats in the Knesset.[^cite_note-21] Within the fragmented political landscape of Israel, these seats gave the party the role of kingmaker in determining the next government after [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) failed to form one.[^cite_note-\:0-22][^cite_note-\:2-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-\:3-25]

The party was open to forming a government with Likud, but [Religious Zionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionist_Party) leader [Bezalel Smotrich](46196834.md) opposed the idea.[^cite_note-26] Abbas stated in a 2026 interview with [Channel 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_12_\(Israel\)) that "foreign actors" were in favor of a Netanyahu government during those talks.[^cite_note-27]

On 2 June 2021, party leader Mansour Abbas signed an agreement to form a coalition government,[^cite_note-28] the first time an independent Arab party became a member of the Israeli government, and the first time in more than 50 years that any Arab party formed part of the Israeli government; a photograph of Abbas, sitting with [Yair Lapid](2448072.md) and [Naftali Bennett](39754840.md), signing the coalition agreement was widely circulated.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-\:2-23] The agreement included guarantees that more than 53 billion shekels (US\$16 billion) would be spent to improve infrastructure and reduce crime in Arab towns, provisions protecting homes built without permits in Arab villages, and recognition for [Bedouin towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negev_Bedouin) in the [Negev desert](142273.md).[^cite_note-\:1-12][^cite_note-30]

On 28 October 2021, the cabinet approved a plan to spend US\$9.4 billion to improve employment opportunities and health services for Israeli Arabs and improve housing, technology, and infrastructure in Arab areas;[^cite_note-\:4-31][^cite_note-\:5-32] it included a further US\$1 billion to address high crime rates in Arab areas.[^cite_note-\:4-31][^cite_note-33] In the scale of funding range of issues in the Arab community addressed, the plan had little precedent in Israeli history, and Abbas and Ra'am were widely credited with pushing forward what Abbas called a "historic step" forward for [Arab Israelis](492331.md).[^cite_note-\:3-25][^cite_note-\:5-32][^cite_note-34] The plan was signed into law when the budget passed on 4 November.[^cite_note-35]

At the United Arab List's urging the coalition government recognized several Bedouin villages and connected thousands of previously illegal homes to the electrical grid.[^cite_note-\:3-25][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

### Return to opposition

In the [2022 Knesset elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Israeli_legislative_election) the party won five seats, gaining an additional seat. The party received most of its votes in Bedouin and Sunni Muslim towns.[^cite_note-38]

On 5 January 2026, party leader Abbas announced that the party would be open to all Israelis for membership and "aims to recruit Jewish politicians who share Ra'am's agenda" as the Arab community continues to struggle with [high crime rates](15556664.md).[^cite_note-39]

The party is willing to work with "anti-Netanyahu parties", while that position is a nonstarter for other Arab parties, including Hadash and Balad.[^cite_note-40]

Abbas confirmed in August 2026 during a joint press conference with former [Yesh Atid](35515608.md) MK [Yoav Segalovitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoav_Segalovitz) that Segalovitz had joined the party's slate as part of a [technical bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_bloc).[^cite_note-41]

[Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud) announced on 13 September that it would submit a disqualification request to the [Central Elections Committee](4456077.md) (CEC) while [Otzma Yehudit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otzma_Yehudit) submitted one the previous week.[^cite_note-42] The CEC voted on 23 September to bar the party from the election, with 18 votes in favor and five against. [Attorney General of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_Israel) [Gali Baharav-Miara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gali_Baharav-Miara) opposed the disqualification.[^cite_note-43] The decision was unanimously overturned by the [Supreme Court of Israel](1666510.md) on 2 October.[^cite_note-44]

Ra'am is an [Islamist](15012.md) party.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] It has been described as a "religious Arab Muslim party".[^cite_note-49] The southern faction of the [Islamic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Movement_in_Israel) had been a major force in the party, where the movement's [Shura Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shura_council_\(Islamic_Movement\)) exerted influence on the party's votes in parliament.[^cite_note-50] This connection ended in December 2025 as the party announced its separation from the Southern Islamic Movement to transition towards becoming a "civic party",[^cite_note-51] with the separation finalized in July 2026.[^cite_note-52] Because of this separation, some sources, including *[The Jerusalem Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jerusalem_Post)*[^cite_note-53] and *[The Times of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times_of_Israel)*[^cite_note-54] described the party as transitioning away from being Islamist.

The party supports the [two-state solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-state_solution), and the creation of a Palestinian state in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, with East Jerusalem as its capital.[^cite_note-\:6-55] However, according to Khaled Elgindy, the [Middle East Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Institute)'s programme director on Palestine and Palestinian-Israeli Affairs, the party does "not emphasise the need to end the Israeli occupation of Palestinian territories".[^cite_note-56]

Under Abbas's direction, the party advocates for the full-fledged political involvement with the domestic politics of Israel in order to improve the quality of life of [Arab Israelis](492331.md), particularly with respect to crime, employment opportunities, housing, and infrastructure; this is a departure from other Arab parties, which historically form part of the opposition and focus on the larger [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](46216.md).[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-\:3-25][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-\:6-55] In the wake of the [2021 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Israeli_legislative_election), Abbass suggested that Ra’am could even join a Netanyahu-led coalition,[^cite_note-\:7-60] although it ended up being part a government led by opposition parties. In 2021, *The Times of Israel* reported that party officials have "regularly disparaged gay people with an Arabic slur meaning 'perverts'".[^cite_note-\:7-60]

Its constituency consists mostly of religious or nationalist Israeli Arabs, and enjoys particular popularity among the [Negev Bedouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negev_Bedouin): in the 2009 election, 80% of residents of Bedouin communities voted for the party.[^cite_note-61]
