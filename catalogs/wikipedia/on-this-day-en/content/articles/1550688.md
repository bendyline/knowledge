**Bubiyan Island** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): جزيرة بوبيان) is the largest [island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Kuwait) in the [Kuwaiti](7515890.md) coastal island chain situated in the north-western corner of the [Persian Gulf](24761.md), with an area of 863 km<sup>2</sup> (333 sq mi). Bubiyan Island is part of the [Shatt al-Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatt_al-Arab) delta.[^cite_note-Ancient_Bubiyan-2]

Bubiyan is the largest of a group of eight islands situated just southwest of the mouth of the [Shaṭṭ al-Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shatt_al-Arab), the river that divides Iraq and Iran.[^cite_note-3]

## History

### Antiquity

Bubiyan was formed by sediment from the [Tigris–Euphrates river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris%E2%80%93Euphrates_river_system).[^cite_note-Ancient_Bubiyan-2] There is archaeological evidence of [Sasanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) (300–650 AD) to early Islamic (650–800 AD) periods of human presence on Bubiyan as evidenced by the recent discovery of torpedo-jar pottery sherds on several prominent beach ridges.[^cite_note-Ancient_Bubiyan-2]

### Gulf War

During the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) of 1991, there was a large oil spill in the area. Four spans of the bridge were destroyed. They were rebuilt in 1999.[^cite_note-4] The island itself was converted into a military base in 1991.[^cite_note-5] In November 1994, [Iraq](7515928.md) formally accepted the UN-demarcated [border](63583868.md) with Kuwait which had been spelled out in [Security Council](31956.md) Resolutions [687](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_687) (1991), [773](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_773) (1992), and [833](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_833) (1993) which formally ended an earlier Iraqi claim to Bubiyan Island.[^cite_note-6]

### Ramsar Convention

In response to Kuwait becoming the [169th signatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_parties_to_the_Ramsar_Convention) of the [Ramsar Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsar_Convention), the Mubarak al-Kabeer reserve was designated as the country's first Wetland of International Importance. The 50,948 hectare reserve consists of small lagoons and shallow [salt marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_marsh) and is important as a stop-over for migrating birds on two migration routes, Turkey to India and Eurasia to Africa. Breeding waterbirds include the world's largest breeding colony of [crab-plover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crab-plover) (*Dromas ardeola*). The surrounding sea is a major nursery for many commercial fish species.[^cite_note-7]

### 2026 Iran war

On 1 May 2026, the island was subject to an [armed incursion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bubiyan_Island_raid) by six armed members of the [IRGC](1285472.md) as part of the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md). The incursion was repelled and resulted in the arrest of four of the attackers.[^cite_note-\:02-8][^cite_note-9]

## Geography

The island is mainly flat, while [salt marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_marsh) cover some coasts. There are several intermittent *[wadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi)* in the center of the island.[^cite_note-\:0-10] It is separated from the Iraqi coast in the northeast by the *Al-Zubayr* channel and from the Kuwaiti mainland in the southwest by the *Al-Sabiyyah* channel.[^cite_note-\:0-10]

The *Al-Sabiyyah* channel wraps around the northern end of Bubiyan Island, separating it from [Warbah Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warbah_Island). 5.4 km (3 mi) northwest of *Ras al Barshah*, the southernmost point, Bubiyan is linked to the mainland by a concrete girder bridge over the *Khawr as Sabiyah* channel, 2.38 km (1.48 mi)[^cite_note-11] long, built in 1981-1983 and opened February 1983.[^cite_note-12]

During high spring tides and southerly gales the wet, low lying mud flats that make up most of the island are encroached upon by sea water.[^cite_note-13] The island is considered to be at risk of inundation due to [sea level rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise).[^cite_note-14]

## Mubarak Al Kabeer Port

Main article: [Mubarak Al Kabeer Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubarak_Al_Kabeer_Port)

[Mubarak Al Kabeer Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubarak_Al_Kabeer_Port) is part of China's [Belt and Road Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_and_Road_Initiative).[^cite_note-beltroad2-15][^cite_note-bri-16] Under China's [Belt and Road Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_and_Road_Initiative), the Mubarak Al Kabeer Port is part of the first phase of the [Silk City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madinat_al-Hareer) project.[^cite_note-beltroad2-15][^cite_note-bri-16] In September 2020, it was reported that the port is 53% complete.[^cite_note-completion-17] In March 2021, it was announced that Kuwait and Pakistan will develop linkages between [Gwadar Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwadar_Port) and Mubarak Al Kabeer Port.[^cite_note-pk-18][^cite_note-pka-19] The Mubarak Al Kabeer Port is under construction.[^cite_note-phase-20][^cite_note-constructionweek-21][^cite_note-kun-22][^cite_note-full-23] As part of Mubarak Al Kabeer Port's development, Bubiyan Island will contain power plants and substations.[^cite_note-boubyan-24][^cite_note-constructionweek-21][^cite_note-mew-25][^cite_note-elsew-26] A 5,000-megawatt power plant has been built in [Subiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subiya,_Kuwait).[^cite_note-suba-27]
