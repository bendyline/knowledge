The **Ukrainian refugee crisis** began with the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) in February 2022. As of September 2025, the [UNHCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNHCR) has recorded 5.7 million Ukrainian refugees around the world, with 90% of this figure residing in various European countries outside of Ukraine.[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4] Older reports by the [International Organization for Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Organization_for_Migration_Mission_in_Ukraine) in May 2022 show approximately eight million Ukrainians as [internally displaced persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internally_displaced_persons).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] By 20 March 2022, nearly one-quarter of Ukraine's total population had been displaced due to active military hostilities with Russia.[^cite_note-Ramsay-8] About 90% of all Ukrainian refugees are [women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) and [children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_war_on_children);[^cite_note-Gdn9422-9] by 24 March, more than half of all children in Ukraine had left their homes, of whom a quarter had left the country as well.[^cite_note-Meduza24322-10][^cite_note-UNICEF24322-11] Men between the ages of 18 and 60 have been banned from leaving the country under [Ukrainian martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_Ukraine), which came into force a few hours into Russia's first military offensive into Ukraine. This ongoing escalation of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md) has caused the largest refugee crisis in the 21st century and the [fourth-largest refugee crisis in modern history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_refugee_crises) as a whole,[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] with the highest refugee flight rate globally.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] It is also the first European refugee crisis since the [Yugoslav Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Wars) in the 1990s and the largest one to have occurred in Europe [since World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_evacuation_and_expulsion).[^cite_note-16]

The vast majority of Ukrainian refugees initially entered neighbouring countries to the west of Ukraine ([Poland](12190815.md), [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_in_Slovakia), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_in_Hungary), [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_of_Romania), and [Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_in_Moldova)).[^cite_note-\:1-17] Around three million of these people then moved further west to other European countries.[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4][^cite_note-BHala10322-18] As of May 2025, the UNHCR reports that the countries in which the largest numbers of Ukrainians had applied for asylum, or other temporary protection, are [Germany](36302402.md) (1.2 million), Poland (1 million), and the [Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_in_the_Czech_Republic) (0.4 million).[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4] As of September 2022, [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) has documented that Ukrainian civilians, [particularly children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_abductions_in_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War),[^cite_note-19] have been forcibly transferred to Russia.[^cite_note-\:21-20] The [OHCHR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OHCHR) has corroborated this claim, stating that "There have been credible allegations of forced transfers of unaccompanied children to [Russian-occupied territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-occupied_territories_of_Ukraine), or to the Russian Federation itself."[^cite_note-\:22-21][^cite_note-\:23-22] The [United States Department of State](31975.md) estimated that at least 900,000 Ukrainian citizens (including children) were forcibly transferred to Russia in the first six months after the invasion.[^cite_note-\:24-23] Around the same time in late 2022, more than 4.5 million Ukrainians had returned to Ukraine, owing to the withdrawal of Russian troops from all fronts but the [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas) and parts of [southern Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_front_of_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4][^cite_note-24]

The [European Union](9317.md) (EU) has allowed entry to all Ukrainian refugees,[^cite_note-\:12-25] and it has also invoked the [Temporary Protection Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_Protection_Directive) to grant displaced Ukrainians the right to stay, work, and study in any [EU member state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_member_state) for an initial period of one year.[^cite_note-\:7-26]

## Refugees before the 2022 invasion

Further information: [Humanitarian situation during the war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanitarian_situation_during_the_war_in_Donbas)

Before the invasion, the [annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) and the [war in the Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_the_Donbas), both of which are aspects of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md), had already resulted in over two million refugees and [internally displaced persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internally_displaced_persons) since 2014.[^cite_note-The_Case_of_Ukraine-27][^cite_note-28] They have been referred to as Europe's forgotten refugees by some media,[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] due to their cool reception in the European Union, comparatively low asylum claim success rate, and media neglect.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

More than a million of the pre-2022 refugees, mainly from [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas),[^cite_note-bbc2014-35] had gone to Russia between 2014 and 2016,[^cite_note-ohchr2016-36] while the number of people displaced within Ukraine had grown to 1.6 million people by early March 2016.[^cite_note-UND3316-37]

## Journey

### Transportation



Cars heading westward on 25 February 2022

For many refugees heading westward, trains played a vital role in the journey within Ukraine and into neighboring countries. [Oleksandr Kamyshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandr_Kamyshin), the [CEO](52234.md) of [Ukrainian Railways](3149341.md), which operates the majority of train services in Ukraine, estimated that within three weeks of the start of the invasion, the network had transported 2.5 million passengers.[^cite_note-38] He also said at its peak, the network transported 190,000 people a day.[^cite_note-39]

To ensure trains can travel as safely as possible, the network had to constantly adapt to situations on the ground, such as if tracks are damaged by bombs or if they are no longer under Ukrainian control.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-CNNTrains-41] Trains have to move slower because they are often overloaded to fit as many people as possible, as well as minimising the risk of hitting damaged tracks.[^cite_note-CNNTrains-41] At night, trains also turn off their lights to reduce the chance of being targeted.[^cite_note-42]

Railway companies in several European countries, including Austria, Belgium, the Czech Republic, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Hungary, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Poland, Romania, Slovakia and Switzerland, allowed Ukrainian refugees to travel by train for free.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

Other refugees traveled by motor vehicles or on foot. In some border crossings, traffic jams of several kilometres long formed.[^cite_note-49] Air travel was not available in Ukraine as the country closed its airspace to civilian flights on the day of the invasion.[^cite_note-50]

### Staging grounds and border crossings

The city of [Lviv](49968.md) in the west of Ukraine became a key staging ground for refugees.[^cite_note-51] Shortly after the Russian invasion, up to 100,000 refugees were arriving in the city every day,[^cite_note-52] which prior to the invasion had a population of 700,000.[^cite_note-53] From Lviv, trains transport refugees to border crossing points such as [Medyka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medyka), Poland and [Uzhhorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzhhorod), close to the border with Slovakia and Hungary.[^cite_note-54] From Medyka, most refugees continue to [Przemyśl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl), Poland, and onward to the rest of Europe.[^cite_note-55]

Other major border crossings included [Siret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siret), Romania;[^cite_note-56] [Ocnița](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocni%C8%9Ba) and [Palanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palanca,_%C8%98tefan_Vod%C4%83), Moldova;[^cite_note-57] [Beregsurány](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beregsur%C3%A1ny), Hungary[^cite_note-58] and [Vyšné Nemecké](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vy%C5%A1n%C3%A9_Nemeck%C3%A9), Slovakia.[^cite_note-59]

Numbers of refugees can change quickly and are often only estimates. Movements from country to country are not necessarily registered officially. Ukrainians are allowed to travel to some countries in Europe without a visa and may be allowed to stay in the country for a longer period, without special permission. Elsewhere, they have to [apply for asylum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asylum_in_the_European_Union). Due to the [Schengen arrangements](16565139.md), having entered any Schengen country, refugees can travel on to other Schengen countries without any visas or border checks.

The [United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs](1602284.md) estimated on 27 February 2022 that in two months there would be 7.5 million [internally displaced people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internally_displaced_people) in Ukraine, 12 million people would be in need of healthcare and the number of people fleeing the war could reach 4 million.[^cite_note-62] The [United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees](31964.md) (UNHCR) stated that the situation was Europe's fastest growing refugee crisis since the Second World War.[^cite_note-63] By early November, according to the UNHCR, the number of Ukrainian refugees recorded across Europe was around 7.8 million.[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4] The countries receiving the largest numbers of refugees as of May 2025 were Germany (1.2 million), Poland (1 million), and the Czech Republic (0.4 million).[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4]

The communications chief of the [UN High Commission for Human Rights](22463.md) called the speed of the exodus of refugees from Ukraine "phenomenal".[^cite_note-64]

A study by the UN agency [International Organization for Migration](36893.md) released on 21 March 2022 found that 13.5% of displaced people had also been displaced in 2014–2015.[^cite_note-IOM-65] The study found that 60% of refugee households were traveling with children, and of the nearly 10 million people displaced within and outside Ukraine on that date, 186,000 were nationals of a third country.[^cite_note-IOM-65]

## Countries

### Neighbouring countries

Neighbouring countries are listed in order of the number of refugees who have entered them; others are listed alphabetically.

#### Poland

See also: [Ukrainians in Poland](12190815.md)



Refugees at [Warsaw Central railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warszawa_Centralna_railway_station)

People sitting on a tiled floor, some eating food wrapped in paper

Refugees near [Polish](22936.md)-border train station [Przemyśl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przemy%C5%9Bl) Główny

As early as 15 February 2022, Poland was expecting a possible Russian attack on Ukraine. The [Polish government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_government) asked communities to prepare for up to a million refugees.[^cite_note-66] By 25 July, more than 1.2 million Ukrainian refugees had been recorded in Poland.[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4] Poland greatly reduced the usual border formalities[^cite_note-67] and said that various identity documents would be accepte&#x64;*.*[^cite_note-68]

Assembly points for refugees have opened in every district of Poland.[^cite_note-69] Local authorities are providing free accommodation, food, and other necessary supplies.[^cite_note-70] Apart from that, a huge number of citizens and organisations are voluntarily offering assistance, free accommodation and other help.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-\:4-73] Websites with information for refugees are also in Ukrainian.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] The government is preparing legal changes that would simplify the employment of Ukrainians in Poland, since currently a working visa is required as Ukrainians are from outside of the EU.[^cite_note-76]

[President of the European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_European_Council) [Charles Michel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Michel) visited the Polish-Ukrainian border crossing on 2 March and praised Polish efforts "to guarantee safe passages for Ukrainians, for European citizens" and those from other countries "without any discrimination."[^cite_note-77] In a later interview for [France Inter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_Inter) he denounced alleged claims of racism of Ukrainian and Polish serviceman as "Russian propaganda" and part of Russia's [information warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_warfare).[^cite_note-78] (See also [§ Racial discrimination](#Racial_discrimination).) Many observers believe that most are likely to stay in Poland and other [Central European](5188.md) countries because "tight labor markets, affordable cities and a pre-existing diaspora have made those countries more appealing alternatives for Ukrainians, who find options slimmer in Europe's west".[^cite_note-79]



Ukrainian refugees in [Kraków](16815.md) protest against the war

The number of refugees arriving to Poland have been unparalleled in Europe. Modelling estimates show that by 1 April, Ukrainian people (including refugees but also those previously living in Poland) made up between 15% and 30% of the population of each of the major Polish cities.[^cite_note-Metropolie-80] For example, Ukrainians accounted for 10% of the total population of [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw) before the war[^cite_note-81] and this figure had increased to 23% by April 2022.[^cite_note-Metropolie-80]

As of May 2025, there were almost 1 million Ukrainian refugees in Poland.[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4] The migration has resulted in a 50% rise in the population of [Rzeszów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rzesz%C3%B3w), the largest city in south-eastern Poland. [Warsaw](32908.md)'s population has increased by 15%, [Kraków](16815.md)'s by 23%, and [Gdańsk](12099.md)'s by 34%.[^cite_note-eib.org-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] Ukrainian refugees have the legal right to reside and work across the European Union. They are also entitled to the same benefits as Poles, including health insurance, free public education, and child allowance.[^cite_note-eib.org-82][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

Less than a month after the invasion, the Polish government established the Aid Fund, run by [Bank Gospodarstwa Krajowego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Gospodarstwa_Krajowego), which funds all actions and programs aimed at assisting and integrating Ukrainian refugees.[^cite_note-eib.org-82][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] The [European Investment Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Investment_Bank) has made a €600 million loan to the Aid Fund, after authorizing an initial €2 billion loan in June. The fund is distributed by [Bank Gospodarstwa Krajowego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Gospodarstwa_Krajowego) (BGK) to local governments and other public bodies that welcome and house Ukrainian migrants.[^cite_note-eib.org-82][^cite_note-90]

In January 2025, Polish Prime Minister [Donald Tusk](648724.md) supported proposals to reduce benefits for Ukrainian refugees in Poland.[^cite_note-91] From 1 July 2026, Ukrainian refugees, including the elderly, were no longer provided with free refugee accommodation to encourage Ukrainians to return home after four years of support, or to support themselves financially. The cost of the support was estimated at 40 billion [złoty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C5%82oty) (£8 billion).[^cite_note-telegraph-20260702-92]

#### Romania



Refugees entering Romania, 5 March 2022

As of 27 May 2022, the [Romanian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_government) had reported 989,357 Ukrainians entering Romania.[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4] Romanian Defence Minister [Vasile Dîncu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasile_D%C3%AEncu) announced on 22 February 2022 that Romania could receive 500,000 refugees if necessary; the first refugees arrived two days later.[^cite_note-93] On 15 March 2022, Minister of Foreign Affairs [Bogdan Aurescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogdan_Aurescu) reported that about 80,000 remained in the country.[^cite_note-94]

Some [ethnic Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians_in_Ukraine) are among the Ukrainians who have fled to Romania.[^cite_note-95]

#### Russia

Further information: [2022 evacuation of the Donetsk and Luhansk People's Republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_evacuation_of_the_Donetsk_and_Luhansk_People's_Republics), [Russian filtration camps for Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_filtration_camps_for_Ukrainians), [Child abductions in the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_abductions_in_the_2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) and [Ukrainian refugees in Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_refugees_in_Russia?action=edit\&redlink=1)

At the start of the full-scale war, there was already a large number of Ukrainian refugees in Russia, as more than one million Ukrainians, mainly from [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas), had fled to Russia between 2014 and 2016.[^cite_note-bbc2014-35][^cite_note-ohchr2016-36]

Estimates of the number of Ukrainian refugees in Russia vary significantly. According to the Russian government 1.2 million Ukrainians were in a "refugee-like situation" in Russia in June 2023.[^cite_note-unhcr_aug25-96] A 2023 report by the [Civic Assistance Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Assistance_Committee), a Russian human rights organisation, concluded that reliable statistics on refugees do not exist in Russia but estimated the number at approximately 1.2 million based on government payouts to refugees and migration control data.[^cite_note-97]

According to the Ukrainian authorities, Russian troops in the territories of Ukraine occupied by Russia are engaged in the forcible deportation of people from Ukraine to Russia, passing them off as refugees. In March, the Ukrainian foreign ministry alleged that 2,389 Ukrainian children had been abducted from Russian-occupied territory in Donetsk and Luhansk, and transferred to Russia, as well as "several thousand" residents of Mariupol.[^cite_note-98] Near the end of April it was reported that Ukrainian officials believed the number of Ukrainian children forced from eastern Ukraine and entered into the Russian adoption system totalled around 150,000. It had been previously reported by Russian media on that children without parents in Donetsk were being placed with Russian parents through their adoption network.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

[Amnesty International](18947898.md) reported that "The abusive and humiliating process known as 'filtration' is a shocking violation of international human rights and humanitarian law. Our research shows that many displaced Ukrainians end up inside Russia or Russia-occupied territories involuntarily, even if they are not physically forced to move. Deportation and forcible transfer of civilians in occupied territory are prohibited by international humanitarian law and can constitute war crimes or crimes against humanity."[^cite_note-\:25-101]

#### Hungary

People in an ornate building handing out cartons and cans of food with a pile of clothes in the background

Hungarian volunteers assisting refugees

From the start of the Russian invasion up to 26 July 2022, 1,041,762 refugees from Ukraine had arrived in Hungary.[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4] As there are no border checks within the Schengen area, Hungary does not know how many people have moved to other Schengen countries. 500 people from third-party countries arrived by train in Budapest and asked the police for help; these were mostly students or migrant workers from Asia and Africa who had been living in Ukraine.[^cite_note-102]

On 21 August 2024 a law came into force in Hungary, restricting support to those Ukrainian refugees who have come from one of currently 13 regions in Ukraine which are, directly affected by military action. Those from the listed regions remain eligible for state support, which includes free housing and a basic monthly sum. Based on UN refugee agencies estimates, some 2,000 to 3,000 people will lose access to subsidised accommodation and will find it difficult to secure alternative properties to rent. Human rights groups said the worst affected are [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) from the Transcarpathian region, who are living in shelters which will now close.[^cite_note-Beeb21824-103]

#### Moldova

An indoor sports hall with people on camp beds inside a running track

One of the refugee centers set up in [Chișinău](56635.md)

Moldova was among the first countries to receive refugees from the [Odesa](61438266.md) and [Vinnytsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnytsia_Oblast) regions.[^cite_note-104] Moldovan authorities have activated a centre for crisis management to facilitate accommodation and humanitarian relief for refugees.[^cite_note-105] As of 26 July, 549,333 Ukrainian refugees had entered Moldova.[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4] Prime Minister of Moldova [Natalia Gavrilița](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalia_Gavrili%C8%9Ba) said on 5 April that 100,000 refugees are staying in Moldova, with almost half of them being children.[^cite_note-MoldovaAid-106] On 11 April, the UN said that Moldova was "hosting an estimated 95,000 Ukrainians."[^cite_note-11April2022UNSecGenSpokesman-107] According to [Médecins Sans Frontières](20498.md), the majority of refugees who do not stay continue on to Romania, Poland or other European countries.[^cite_note-108]

Moldova received the highest number of refugees per capita of any country[^cite_note-mee-moldova-109] despite being one of Europe's poorest countries.[^cite_note-abd-moldova-110] According to *[Middle East Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Eye)*, this has led to social tensions, and international aid was deemed crucial to help Moldovan institutions handle the influx of refugees.[^cite_note-mee-moldova-109] On 22 March, the *[Financial Times](136566.md)* estimated that 4% of the Moldovan population were currently refugees, and reported that the government of Moldova had requested financial aid to cope with the emergency.[^cite_note-ft-moldova-111] At a conference in Berlin on 5 April, Germany and several partners, including France, Romania and the European Union, agreed to provide €659.5 million in aid to Moldova.[^cite_note-MoldovaAid-106]

Moldova has a critical view of Russia's invasion, due to [its own internal conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transnistria_conflict) with Russian-backed [Transnistria](376581.md).[^cite_note-dw-moldova-112] The government of Moldova is providing free bus rides,[^cite_note-dw-moldova-112] and Romania has assisted Moldova with moving people onwards into Romania, in order to relieve the pressure in Moldova.[^cite_note-unhcr-moldova-113] On 12 March, Germany agreed to take 2,500 refugees who were in Moldova.[^cite_note-114] On 5 March, Germany announced it will take an additional 12,000 refugees.[^cite_note-115]

Some Ukrainian refugees have also gone to the unrecognized breakaway state of Transnistria. The state-owned newspaper *[Novosti Pridnestrovya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosti_Pridnestrovya?action=edit\&redlink=1)* reported on 4 April that a total of around 27,300 Ukrainian citizens had arrived in Transnistria, of whom 21,000 had applied for temporary residence permits.[^cite_note-116]

#### Slovakia



Refugees entering Slovakia on 10 March

As of 8 March 2022, Slovakia had taken in over 140,000 people.[^cite_note-\:9-117][^cite_note-118] By 26 July 2022, 627,555 Ukrainian refugees had entered Slovakia.[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4] However, most of the refugees continued further west, mainly to the Czech Republic.[^cite_note-reliefweb1-119] About 80,000 refugees have settled so far in Slovakia.[^cite_note-120]

#### Belarus

According to [Belarusian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Government) figures, 30,092 had gone to Belarus by 29 May 2022.[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4]

### Other European countries

#### EU legal framework

Ukraine has an [Association Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_Agreement) with the [European Union](9317.md) and since 2017 Ukrainians with [biometric passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometric_passports_in_Ukraine) have had the right to 90 days visa-free stay in the Schengen Area.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] Following the invasion of Ukraine, the [European Commission](9974.md) has called upon member states to authorise the entry and stay of those without biometric passports on humanitarian grounds, and member states had done so since the refugees started to arrive across the borders.[^cite_note-\:12-25][^cite_note-\:62-123]

On 4 March 2022, the [Council of the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_the_EU) unanimously agreed to implement the [Temporary Protection Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_Protection_Directive) for the first time in its history, so that refugees fleeing from Ukraine do not have to go through the standard [European Union asylum procedure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asylum_in_the_European_Union).[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-\:62-123] Temporary protection is an emergency mechanism which gives the right to stay in an EU member state for an initial period of one year, which may be extended for up to a maximum of three years.[^cite_note-\:7-26] The beneficiaries enjoy harmonised rights across the EU such as residence, access to the labour market and housing, medical assistance, and access to education for children.[^cite_note-\:7-26] The Council did not adopt a system of quotas of displaced persons, but left it to the beneficiaries to choose their destination freely.[^cite_note-\:62-123]

#### Austria



Evacuation of people from vulnerable categories to Austria, 17 March 2022

#### Czech Republic



Ukrainian refugees in [Brno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brno), Czech Republic



Volunteers providing help to refugees at Praha Hlavní nádraží

The Czech Republic is providing financial, humanitarian and other types of aid to Ukrainian refugees as well as state accommodation and free education for their children. The Czech Republic had received over 100,000 Ukrainian refugees by 7 March 2022,[^cite_note-\:8-143] approximately 200,000 by 10 March 2022,[^cite_note-RPI10322-144] over 270,000 by 17 March 2022,[^cite_note-Expats17322-145] with the number rising to over 300,000 by 23 March 2022[^cite_note-reuters-cruelty-146] and relatively slower rise afterwards. By 1 April 2023, altogether 504,107 Ukrainian individuals had been granted temporary protection in the country, of whom 325,742 had applied for extension of their refugee status beyond March 2023. 68% of the refugees were of working age, of whom 65% were female and 35% male, 28% were children and 4% elderly.[^cite_note-147] In summer 2022, the country hosted the largest number of Ukrainian refugees per capita of any European country.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-reliefweb1-119]

A network of Regional Centres for Help and Assistance to Ukraine (*Krajská asistenční centra pomoci Ukrajině*) was created in the regional capitals of the country to help refugees obtain registration, accommodation, health insurance or other assistance.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] On 17 March 2022, a law known as *Lex Ukrajina* was passed by [Parliament](3991200.md) to make it easier for refugees to obtain residence permits and access healthcare, which was followed by passing four follow-up laws in the following year.[^cite_note-151]

Also on 17 March 2022, some media, including Associated Press and BBC News, incorrectly reported that the [Czech Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Prime_Minister) had said: "Czech Republic can no longer accept refugees from Ukraine".[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] However, someone had mistranslated his Czech quote: "We are at the very limit of what we are capable of absorbing without any major problems (...) we must continue in the steps that will allow us to cope with more high numbers". The BBC later corrected the mistranslation.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]

As of May 2025, there were around 374,000 Ukrainian refugees in the Czech Republic.[^cite_note-UNHCR-numbers-4]

#### France

#### Germany

Person holds high a cardboard sign reading "food donations, follow me"

A volunteer holding a sign to guide refugees at Berlin's rail station

The first refugees from Ukraine arrived in [Brandenburg](3765.md) on the evening of 25 February 2022, and the state was initially preparing for some 10,000 people. Other [states of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) pledged their help.[^cite_note-rbb-brandenburg-187] In addition, [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](38168.md) decided to stop the [forced return](1481067.md) of people to Ukraine.[^cite_note-br-repatriation-188]

On 8 March 2022, a legal norm was enacted (*Ukraine-Aufenthalts-Übergangsverordnung*), which temporarily legalizes the entry and residence of Ukrainians and third-country nationals in Germany who were legally resident in Ukraine at the beginning of the Russian attack on 24 February.[^cite_note-hessischer-rat-verordnung-189][^cite_note-190]

[Deutsche Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Bahn), the German national railway company, allowed refugees with a Ukrainian passport or ID card to travel free of charge on long-distance trains from Poland to Germany.[^cite_note-191] The company also gave out free tickets to refugees who wanted to continue to another destination; by 17 March, more than 100,000 had been issued.[^cite_note-APBerlin-192] The [Association of German Transport Companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_German_Transport_Companies) decided to also cancel charges for all short-distance travel with buses and trains for Ukrainian refugees within Germany.[^cite_note-193]

German media debated whether there was a difference in the portrayal of refugees from Ukraine compared to those from other countries, particularly those arriving during the [2015 European migrant crisis](46415102.md).[^cite_note-taz-difference-194][^cite_note-welt-difference-195][^cite_note-spiegel-difference-196]

#### Portugal

As of 25 April 2022, Portugal had received 33,106 refugees from Ukraine.[^cite_note-\:16-237] The majority of the refugees are Ukrainian citizens, while 5% are non-Ukrainian nationals living in Ukraine at the time of the invasion.[^cite_note-238] There were 22,208 women refugees, 10,898 men and 11,410 minors.[^cite_note-\:16-237] As of 6 April 2022, 350 minors had arrived without a parent or legal guardian. In most cases, these minors arrived with close relatives,[^cite_note-\:20-239] but 16 of them arrived unaccompanied;[^cite_note-\:20-239] this number rose to 45 by 7 April 2022.[^cite_note-240] As of 29 March 2022, 1,800 Portuguese families had offered to provide foster care. The Portuguese authorities expect to find next-of-kin for the majority, and only a tiny number will need permanent adoption.[^cite_note-241]

Before the 2022 invasion, Portugal already had 27,200 Ukrainian immigrants.[^cite_note-242] As a result of the crisis, the Ukrainian immigrant community has become the second-largest in Portugal, almost doubling to over 52,000 in the month of March.[^cite_note-\:11-243] That community helped in organizing the transport of refugees.[^cite_note-244]

By 6 April 2022, 2,115 Ukrainian refugee children had been enrolled in Portuguese public schools, up from over 600 on 22 March.[^cite_note-245][^cite_note-246] Employment wise, as of 6 April 2022, 359 refugees had been hired, 4,261 registered as looking for work and 2,880 enrolled in Portuguese classes.[^cite_note-247] As of 6 April, Portuguese social security had processed 1,412 requests from Ukrainian refugees.[^cite_note-248] The Portuguese government reiterated that it had set no limit for Ukrainian refugees.[^cite_note-249][^cite_note-250]

The Spanish [Ministry of Inclusion, Social Security and Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Inclusion,_Social_Security_and_Migration) enabled reception, attention and relocation centres (CREADE) for Ukrainian refugees in [Pozuelo de Alarcón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozuelo_de_Alarc%C3%B3n), [Barcelona](4443.md), [Alicante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicante) and [Málaga](184763.md), resolving around 40,000 applications for temporary protection over the course of the first three weeks of the conflict.[^cite_note-259]

#### Sweden

By January 2025, Sweden had received about 50,000 Ukrainian refugees since Russia's invasion of Ukraine on 24 February 2022.[^cite_note-261] In January 2023, 8500 of them had left Sweden again.[^cite_note-262] Meanwhile, new refugees continued to arrive, and by January 2025, there were 46.500 Ukrainian refugees in Sweden.[^cite_note-263] These refugees are covered by the EU's [Mass Refugee Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_Refugee_Directive), which grants them temporary residence permits until 4 March 2026.[^cite_note-264]\


Ukrainian refugees in Sweden have faced significant challenges in adapting to their new lives, including language barriers, difficulties accessing healthcare,[^cite_note-265] and finding employment.[^cite_note-266] There have been concerns about the risk of labor exploitation among Ukrainian refugees, particularly due to their vulnerable legal and economic situation.[^cite_note-267] There have also been reports indicating that female Ukrainian refugees have been subjected to sexual exploitation.[^cite_note-268]

However, their situation has gradually improved. There have been positive developments in almost all aspects. For example, there has been an increase in the rates of employment and the proportion of Ukrainian refugees who have acquired Swedish language skills. Many Ukrainians are content with their life in Sweden and grateful for the support they have received.[^cite_note-269] Since November 2024, new regulations have allowed a large group that was previously ineligible to now register as residents.[^cite_note-270] This means, among other things, that they have broader access to healthcare services beyond the emergency services that were initially provided.[^cite_note-271]

#### Switzerland

Prior to the invasion, Swiss laws already allowed any Ukrainian citizen with a [biometric passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometric_passport) to enter [Switzerland](26748.md) without a visa and to stay for up to three months. On 28 February 2022, Justice Minister [Karin Keller-Sutter](28856581.md) announced that from then on refugees without passports could enter too and staying in the country would no longer be time limited. The federal government and the cantons committed to providing accommodation for 9,000 refugees.[^cite_note-272] On 11 March, the head of the Justice ministry [Karin Keller-Sutter](28856581.md) said that 2,100 refugees had already been registered in Switzerland and up to 60,000 refugees could arrive in total.[^cite_note-273] As of 5 April, 24,837 refugees had been registered and 18,149 of them had already received S permits.[^cite_note-CHSEM-274][^cite_note-switzerland-2022-04-05-275] By October 2022, there were about 70,000 refugees in Switzerland.[^cite_note-276]

In August 2025 the Swiss [State Secretariat for Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Secretariat_for_Migration_\(Switzerland\)) (SEM) issued an internal security assessment of seven western Ukrainian oblasts (Volyn, Rivne, Lviv, Ternopil, Zakarpattia, Ivano-Frankivsk and Chernivtsi). The report, drawing on [ACLED](43040226.md) and UN data up to July 2025, found these rear-zone areas under full Ukrainian control experience only sporadic Russian long-range air and drone strikes, with “very low civilian casualties” (defined in the report as 0-9 deaths and 7–83 injuries per oblast in the preceding 12 months).[^cite_note-277] [^cite_note-278] Based on this assessment, the Swiss government ruled on 8 October 2025 that refugee returns to these seven oblasts are generally reasonable. Effective 1 November 2025, new applicants for temporary Protection Status (Status S) from these regions are generally ineligible for automatic collective protection and must apply through the regular asylum procedure. Existing Status S holders remain unaffected. [^cite_note-279] [^cite_note-280]

#### United Kingdom

Britain had issued about 1,000 visas by 13 March 2022 and was criticised for placing too many bureaucratic obstacles to entry to the UK for refugees by both international and UK sources, and for only granting admission to refugees who already had family in the UK.[^cite_note-Casey13322-281][^cite_note-Obs13322-282][^cite_note-GdnSmith11322-138] On 4 March 2022, the United Kingdom announced that British nationals and Ukrainian residents of the UK would be allowed to bring in members of their extended family from Ukraine.[^cite_note-\:5-283][^cite_note-Obs3422-284] Prime Minister [Boris Johnson](19065069.md) stated that the country could take in 200,000 Ukrainian refugees.[^cite_note-\:5-283] On 7 March 2022, French Interior Minister [Gerald Darmanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Darmanin) said that many Ukrainian refugees had been turned away by British officials in [Calais](85468.md) and told to obtain visas at UK consulates in Paris or Brussels.[^cite_note-285][^cite_note-286] [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) also criticised the United Kingdom for not helping Ukrainian refugees enough, specifically criticising Britain's visa policy which required applicants to apply for visas in person in Brussels or Paris before entering Britain.[^cite_note-287]

On 12 March 2022, [Michael Gove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Gove) announced the "Homes for Ukraine" scheme whereby Britons who offered their home to Ukrainian refugees would receive £350 a month.[^cite_note-HelmGdn12322-288][^cite_note-289]

On 28 March 2022, the Home Office announced it had issued 21,600 visas under the Ukraine Family Scheme, under which refugees could join close family members already resident in the UK.[^cite_note-290] The government was criticised however for the slow and bureaucratic procedures in the "Homes for Ukraine" scheme previously announced by Gove. The heads of the Refugee Council, the British Red Cross, Save the Children and Oxfam made a statement warning that the system was "causing great distress to already traumatised Ukrainians".[^cite_note-Gdn28322-291] 2,500 applications for visas under this scheme had been approved by 30 March 2022.[^cite_note-united-kingdom-2022-03-30-292] In April 2022, *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* revealed that female refugees may be at risk of being exploited by UK men offering to be their hosts, with some proposing sexual relationship or even marriage.[^cite_note-293] On 13 April 2022, UNHCR asked the United Kingdom to stop pairing single British men with lone Ukrainian women refugees under the "Homes for Ukraine" scheme because the women were at risk of sexual exploitation.[^cite_note-Syal13422-294]

By 8 April 2022, a total of 12,000 Ukrainian refugees had entered Britain. 1,200 of them under the "Homes for Ukraine" scheme for those who are sponsored by UK hosts and 10,800 under the Ukrainian family scheme for those with prior family connections to the UK.[^cite_note-uk-2022-04-08-295] The British government had received 79,800 applications for visas from Ukrainians and had issued 40,900 up to 7 April 2022,[^cite_note-uk-2022-04-08-295] but only 21,600 refugees had actually entered the UK by 22 April[^cite_note-uk-2022-04-18-296] and 27,100 by 22 April under both schemes.[^cite_note-Gdn28422-297] By 29 May 2022, this figure had reached 65,700.[^cite_note-Gdn3622-173]

A report by the [British Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Red_Cross) found that the [cost-of-living crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_cost-of-living_crisis) of the early 2020s had affected host families' ability to support Ukrainian refugees by the latter half of 2022.[^cite_note-298]

A study by the [University of York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_York) also found that, though children of Ukrainian refugees had been successful in learning English, after 12 to 18 months younger children had begun to struggle with simple tasks in Ukrainian, raising questions over future bonds to their extended families.[^cite_note-299]

### Other countries

#### Argentina

In May 2022, the first private plane carrying refugees arriving under humanitarian visas arrived in Buenos Aires carrying 9 passengers. The Argentine government has stated its intent to continue such flights.[^cite_note-300]

#### Australia

In the wake of the Russian invasion in February, Prime Minister [Scott Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Morrison) said that visa applications from Ukrainian nationals would be sent "to the top of the pile".[^cite_note-301] Several Australians have opened their homes to host Ukrainian refugees, with more than 4,000 visas having been processed.[^cite_note-302] On 20 March, the federal government announced that Ukrainians who arrive or are already in the country will be allowed temporary humanitarian visas, which allows them to work, study, and access [healthcare](58499.md).[^cite_note-ABC220322-303] By 20 March, about 5,000 Ukrainians had been granted visas to travel to Australia, and 750 have arrived.[^cite_note-ABC220322-303]

#### Brazil

On 3 March, Brazil announced that Ukrainians would receive humanitarian visas as refugees, with a period of 5 months to apply for asylum.[^cite_note-304] The country has around [600,000 people of Ukrainian descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Brazilians), about 38,000 of whom live in [Prudentópolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudent%C3%B3polis), according to the Ukrainian-Brazilian Central Representation.[^cite_note-305] From 3 February until 19 March, Brazil had received almost 900 Ukrainian refugees according to Brazilian police.[^cite_note-306] On 22 March, the country's police reported that 1,100 Ukrainians had landed in Brazil up to that date.[^cite_note-307]

#### Canada

On 3 March 2022, the [Government of Canada](616255.md) announced a plan to permanently reunify Canadian citizens with their Ukrainian family members.[^cite_note-\:3-308] [Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada](546737.md) stated the country will allow an unlimited number of Ukrainians to apply to temporarily stay in Canada and provide work permits for Ukrainians that have either been accepted under these migration schemes or cannot safely return to Ukraine.[^cite_note-309][^cite_note-\:3-308] On 17 March 2022, the Government launched the *[Canada-Ukraine Authorization for Emergency Travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada-Ukraine_Authorization_for_Emergency_Travel)* (CUAET),[^cite_note-\:13-310] which gives Ukrainians and their families a visa to come to Canada temporarily, and allows them to work and study there for up to three years. There is no limit to the number of people who can apply, and applicants who are overseas can apply online and provide their biometrics (fingerprints and a photo).[^cite_note-\:13-310][^cite_note-311] The online application takes 14 days to process.[^cite_note-312] Ukrainians approved under the CUAET will be able to travel to Canada up until 31 March 2024. Afterward, they will be subject to the standard immigration measures available to others around the world.[^cite_note-313]

Canada is home to nearly 1.4 million Ukrainian-Canadians, making Canada the second-largest [population of the Ukrainian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Canadians), after Russia.[^cite_note-314] Between 17 March and 8 July 2022, 1,084,599 applications through CUAET were received, of which 784,272 were approved, with 164,626 having arrived in Canada under said program.[^cite_note-315] In March 2022 Canada announced it was investing an additional [CA\$](101846.md)117 million in the implementation of new immigration programs for Ukrainian refugees.[^cite_note-316]

People [coming to Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Canada) from Ukraine under the CUAET scheme were legally considered to be temporary residents rather than refugees. They were granted the right to work or study in Canada for three years but do not get the right to automatic permanent residency like refugees from [Syria](7515849.md) or [Afghanistan](737.md). This policy was reportedly adopted to simplify and speed up the application process.[^cite_note-317] In 2024, Canada admitted only 6,780 new permanent residents from Ukraine.[^cite_note-318]

#### Egypt

When the conflict broke out, there were an estimated 16,000 to 20,000 Ukrainian tourists, now refugees, in Egypt.[^cite_note-319][^cite_note-\:6-320] The [Egyptian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_government) assisted them to leave Egypt, with free flights to Poland, Slovakia, and Hungary – as of 4 March, almost 4,000 had left the country.[^cite_note-\:6-320]

#### Israel



A Ukrainian family arrives in Israel on 6 March 2022

As of 22 May, 36,600 Ukrainians had arrived in Israel, of whom 18,600 [had the right to enter Israel](351513.md).[^cite_note-321]

As of 23 March, more than 15,200 Ukrainian refugees had arrived in Israel, of whom only 4,200 would have otherwise been eligible for citizenship[^cite_note-\:14-322] In addition, another 20,000 Ukrainians who were already inside Israel when the conflict broke out (on tourist visas or illegally in the country) were also regarded as refugees and given permission to stay.[^cite_note-323] On 3 July 2022, the [Supreme Court of Israel](1666510.md) abolished the quotas on Ukrainian refugees in Israel and allowed unlimited entry of refugees into Israel. Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelenskyy welcomed the decision and said it is a sign of "a true, developed democracy"[^cite_note-324]

As of January 2023, 15,000 Ukrainian refugees remained in Israel, of the about 47,000 Ukrainians who had traveled to Israel since the start of the invasion but who are not entitled to citizenship under Israel's [Law of Return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Return). None of the 15,000 had been granted refugee status by Israel.[^cite_note-325]

#### Japan

Japan opened its borders to refugees from Ukraine fleeing the war on 15 March 2022.[^cite_note-326] On 12 March, [Chief Cabinet Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Cabinet_Secretary), [Hirokazu Matsuno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirokazu_Matsuno) confirmed that 29 Ukrainians had entered Japan to seek shelter with friends or relatives in [Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima).[^cite_note-327]

As of 6 April 2022, 437 Ukrainian refugees had entered Japan,[^cite_note-328] reaching 1222 by 8 June 2022.[^cite_note-ideasforgood.jp-329] The largest number of Ukrainian refugees were in [Tokyo](30057.md) (215 people), [Fukuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukuoka_Prefecture) (92 people) and [Kanagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanagawa) (70 people),[^cite_note-ideasforgood.jp-329] 284 being under the age of 18 according to the prefectures.[^cite_note-ideasforgood.jp-329]

In 30 November 2022 the number of refugees reached 2158,[^cite_note-moj.go.jp-330] the highest number being in Tokyo (545 people), Osaka (154 people), Kanagawa (139 people), Fukuoka (122 people) and [Hyōgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hy%C5%8Dgo) (105 people),[^cite_note-331] 417 were under the age of 18, 1463 between the ages of 18 and 61 and 278 over 61.[^cite_note-moj.go.jp-330]

#### Mexico

Main article: [Ukrainian refugee crisis in Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_refugee_crisis_in_Mexico)

Mexico has been the Latin American country that has received the most Ukrainians since February 2022.[^cite_note-332]

#### New Zealand

[New Zealand](4913064.md) promised to accept 4,000 Ukrainian refugees. By July 2022, 227 Ukrainians had arrived in New Zealand.[^cite_note-333]

#### Philippines

The Philippines' [Department of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Justice_\(Philippines\)) stated that the country would be willing to accept Ukrainian refugees and asylum seekers as a response to the Russian invasion of Ukraine. President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) on 28 February 2022 institutionalized the Philippines policy to protect refugees, stateless persons and asylum seekers under international law.[^cite_note-334]

#### Sri Lanka

Early in the conflict [the government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Sri_Lanka) announced that it would grant and extend free visas by two months for over 15,000 Russians and Ukrainians who are stranded in Sri Lanka due to the ongoing conflict.[^cite_note-335][^cite_note-336][^cite_note-337]



American president [Joe Biden](145422.md) meeting with a group of Ukrainian refugees in [Warsaw](32908.md), [Poland](22936.md), in March 2022

#### Turkey [^cite_note-338]

On 3 March 2022, Turkey announced that 20,000 Ukrainian refugees had entered Turkey since the Russian invasion. Interior Minister [Süleyman Soylu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BCleyman_Soylu) said that Turkey was glad to welcome them.[^cite_note-339] By 8 March 2022, official figures put the number of Ukrainian refugees in the country at 20,550, of whom 551 were of [Crimean Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars) or [Meskhetian Turk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meskhetian_Turk) origin.[^cite_note-340][^cite_note-trtukrayna-341] The Ukrainian winner of the [2016 Eurovision Song Contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Eurovision_Song_Contest), [Jamala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamala), who is of Crimean Tatar origin, also sought refuge in Turkey.[^cite_note-342] By 23 March 2022, the number of Ukrainian refugees had risen above 58,000.[^cite_note-343][^cite_note-hurriyet-344] As of 25 April 2022, the number of Ukrainian refugees in Turkey exceeded 85,000.[^cite_note-DH-131]

#### United States

The United States announced on 4 March 2022, that Ukrainians would be provided [Temporary Protected Status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_Protected_Status).[^cite_note-345] This was estimated to impact 30,000 Ukrainian nationals in the United States.[^cite_note-346][^cite_note-347] On 24 March 2022, US President [Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biden) announced that up to 100,000 Ukrainian refugees would be accepted into the United States; especially, focusing on those with family already in the country.[^cite_note-348] As of June 2022, only 300 Ukrainians had been resettled under the [U.S. Refugee Admissions Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Refugee_Admissions_Program), and most Ukrainian refugees came to the United States on visas they held or by crossing the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border).[^cite_note-349] By late 2022 approximately 85,000 Ukrainians had utilized the "Uniting for Ukraine" program which allows refugees with an American sponsor to remain in America for two years.[^cite_note-350] On 21 December 2022, President [Joe Biden](145422.md) announced during his meeting with [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) that the U.S. had accepted roughly 221,000 [Ukrainian](145144.md) refugees via earlier Title 42 encounters, Uniting for Ukraine and other organisations.[^cite_note-WHjoint211222-61] On 18 August 2023, the Biden administration announced it would extend Temporary Protected Status from 19 October 2023, to 19 April 2025.[^cite_note-351] In January 2025, President Biden's administration extended Temporary Protected Status for Ukrainians until October 2026. However, this temporary protection is only available to Ukrainians who had arrived in the US by August 2023.[^cite_note-352][^cite_note-353]

On 20 January 2025, newly inaugurated President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) signed the [Executive Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order) [Securing Our Borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securing_Our_Borders). Due to this order [The Uniting for Ukraine parole program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parole_\(United_States_immigration\)#Uniting_for_Ukraine) is paused, and is not accepting new applications.[^cite_note-354][^cite_note-355]

The [killing of Iryna Zarutska](80994478.md), a Ukrainian refugee from Kyiv, on a light rail train in [Charlotte, North Carolina](57447.md), gained national attention, prompting statements and legislative oversight from local, state, and federal politicians.[^cite_note-356][^cite_note-357] Addressing the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) on 24 September 2025, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy paid tribute to Zarutska.[^cite_note-358]

#### Uruguay

On 10 March 2022, President [Luis Lacalle Pou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Lacalle_Pou) stated that Uruguay was willing to accept Ukrainian refugees, despite the issue not yet being discussed with any international organization, and noting that the country "has historically had open arms in this regard".[^cite_note-359] In the first half of 2022, ten Ukrainians arrived in Uruguay as refugees.[^cite_note-360][^cite_note-361]

## Controversy and concerns

### Human trafficking concerns

Further information: [Women in the Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), [Human trafficking in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_trafficking_in_Ukraine), and [Child abductions in the Russo-Ukrainian war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_abductions_in_the_Russo-Ukrainian_war)

The [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe)'s [Group of Experts on Action against Trafficking in Human Beings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_Experts_on_Action_against_Trafficking_in_Human_Beings) (GRETA) and aid organizations such as the [Human Trafficking Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Trafficking_Foundation) and [World Vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Vision) warned that refugees are at risk of falling into [human trafficking](19333662.md), exploitation and violence, including sexual violence.[^cite_note-366][^cite_note-367][^cite_note-Reuters031522-199]

Concerns about human trafficking and sexual violence have been realized during the refugee crisis, with spotty documentation and identification, language barriers and the large numbers of refugees creating opportunities for traffickers.[^cite_note-368][^cite_note-369][^cite_note-370] A Ukrainian refugee who stayed to help at a border location told reporters of calling the police on three men holding transportation signs, who were later arrested for looking for women for the [sex trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_trade). Another refugee spoke of men who attempted to coerce her and her children into a van full of only women, and refused to show her proof of identification and attempted to intimidate her from other travel options.[^cite_note-371] At least one man was arrested on suspicions of raping a 19-year-old refugee after promising her work and shelter.[^cite_note-372]

[La Strada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Strada_International_Association) worked on a case where Ukrainian girls were offered tickets to [Mexico](3966054.md), [Turkey](11125639.md) and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) without ever meeting the men who invited them.[^cite_note-373] European Commissioner for Home Affairs [Ylva Johansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ylva_Johansson) said: "We have some indications on online services that the demand for Ukrainian women for sexual purposes has gone up."[^cite_note-374] According to *USA Today*, "there has been a skyrocketing increase in all forms of illegal trafficking of women and girls in the region – and also boys – including forced sex and labor, [prostitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitution), [pornography](5710507.md) and other forms of sexual exploitation... In recent weeks, online searches for Ukrainian women and keywords like escorts, porn or sex have shot up dramatically in European countries, according to the [Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe](22796.md) (OSCE)."[^cite_note-375]

Polish, Romanian and Slovak law enforcement deployed patrols to border crossings to look out for criminal activity.[^cite_note-376][^cite_note-Guardian220312-377] Both men and women have attempted to procure female refugees at stations.[^cite_note-GdnFallon24322-378] The Polish government passed an amendment which raised the minimum sentence of human trafficking from 3 years to 10 years, and the sex trafficking of children from 10 years to 25 years.[^cite_note-Guardian220312-377] In Berlin, German authorities advised refugees not to accept help directly from people at train stations, and for Germans to register their offers of help on coordinated websites rather than approaching refugees directly.[^cite_note-Reuters031522-199] German police also increased the number of uniformed and undercover police officers at train stations, and asked volunteers to report suspicious activity at train stations.[^cite_note-Reuters031522-199]

#### Unaccompanied minors



Internally displaced children from other parts of Ukraine in the [Zakarpattia Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakarpattia_Oblast) of western Ukraine

UNICEF and UNHCR raised concerns about minors who were traveling unaccompanied, and urged neighbouring countries to identify and register the children before sending them to relocation services. They also highlighted a number of institutional care and boarding schools in Ukraine which held about 10,000 students that have been caught up in the invasion. In some countries, accommodation has been created specifically for orphaned children in foster homes or orphanages.[^cite_note-379][^cite_note-380] UNICEF set up "Blue Dot" safe spaces in neighboring countries, which included support for unaccompanied children.[^cite_note-381] In addition, some protection for separated and unaccompanied minors is now provided through a system of temporary guardianship in the country where the children arrive, which Poland among other countries has set up under new legislation.[^cite_note-382]

### Deportations

See also: [War crimes in the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine § Deportations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_crimes_in_the_2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine#Deportations)

According to Ukrainian authorities, thousands of refugees arriving in Russia have been [forcibly relocated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_transfer) using 'filtration centres', evoking the memory of [Soviet era population transfers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_transfer_in_the_Soviet_Union) and prior Russian use of such centres in the [Chechen War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechen_War_of_Independence).[^cite_note-\:18-383] Forcible deportation by a warring party is a human rights violation,[^cite_note-\:18-383] and experts warn that the forcible transfer of Ukrainian children for "adoption" in Russia is a [genocidal act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claims_of_genocide_of_Ukrainians_in_the_2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-384]

RIA Novosti and Ukrainian officials stated that thousands were dispatched to filtration centres in Penza Oblast,[^cite_note-385][^cite_note-386] [Taganrog](1288065.md), Donetsk, [Ryazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazan), [Yaroslavl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslavl) and the Russian-occupied Ukrainian cities of [Dokuchaievsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dokuchaievsk),[^cite_note-387] Izium[^cite_note-388][^cite_note-389] and Bezimenne.[^cite_note-390] The Ukrainian government claimed that 400,000 Ukrainian citizens have been forcibly taken to Russia where "some could be sent as far as the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) island of [Sakhalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhalin) and are being offered jobs on condition they don't leave for two years", while "the Kremlin" claimed the relocated people wanted to go to Russia.[^cite_note-391] It was reported that underground networks of Russians and Russian exiles, had been helping Ukrainian refugees to leave Russia and Russian controlled areas.[^cite_note-392] More than 20,000 Ukrainians have entered Estonia from Russia since the war began.[^cite_note-393]

[Human Rights Watch](46530.md),[^cite_note-\:21-20][^cite_note-\:02-394] the [UN Human Rights Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Human_Rights_Office),[^cite_note-\:22-21][^cite_note-\:23-22] [Amnesty International](18947898.md),[^cite_note-\:25-101] the [United States Department of State](31975.md),[^cite_note-\:24-23][^cite_note-\:19-395] and *[The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept)* have reported Russian deportations of Ukrainians.[^cite_note-\:02-394] According to the U.S. State Department, the number of Ukrainians who have been deported or forcibly transferred into Russia is between 900,000 and 1.6 million, citing various sources including the Russian government.[^cite_note-\:19-395]

### Military-age men and travel restrictions

See also: [Mobilization in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobilization_in_Ukraine) and [Ukrainian conscription crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_conscription_crisis)

Following the February 2022 Russian invasion, Ukrainian men between the ages of 18 and 60 were initially barred from leaving the country,[^cite_note-396][^cite_note-397][^cite_note-398] a restriction that was later narrowed to ages 23 to 60,[^cite_note-CNN-2025-399] though an estimated 600,000 to 850,000 Ukrainian men still fled to Europe.[^cite_note-400] To address this, the Ukrainian government in April 2024 suspended the issuance of new passports and the provision of non-emergency consular services for military-age Ukrainian men residing abroad.[^cite_note-401] Among those who fled illegally, Romanian border police reported that more than 30,000 Ukrainian men crossed into Romania alone, often undertaking hazardous transits through the [Carpathian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Mountains) and across the [Tisza River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tisza_River) that have resulted in numerous rescues and fatalities.[^cite_note-CNN-2025-399]

Throughout 2024, the [Polish government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Cabinet_of_Donald_Tusk) and Lithuanian leaders, including President [Gitanas Nausėda](58519561.md), Prime Minister [Ingrida Šimonytė](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingrida_%C5%A0imonyt%C4%97), and Defence Minister [Laurynas Kasčiūnas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurynas_Kas%C4%8Di%C5%ABnas), repeatedly expressed readiness to assist in the repatriation of military-age Ukrainian men to support Kyiv's conscription efforts.[^cite_note-guardian-repat-402][^cite_note-403] At the time, more than 650,000 military-age Ukrainian men were registered as refugees across the European Union, Norway, Switzerland, and Liechtenstein,[^cite_note-404] with estimates suggesting that up to 400,000 resided in Poland alone.[^cite_note-guardian-repat-402]

In June 2026, European Commission President [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md) signaled plans to alter the terms of the [Temporary Protection Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_Protection_Directive), an EU emergency mechanism that grants immediate collective [protection to displaced persons](1621432.md), in response to the ongoing Ukrainian conscription crisis. Consequently, EU member states began debating the exclusion of new arrivals of Ukrainian men aged 23 to 60 who are subject to the [2022 Ukrainian mobilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ukrainian_mobilization), aiming to avoid undermining Ukraine's self-defense capacity.[^cite_note-Reuters_2026-06-04-405][^cite_note-406] The proposal reflected a position also expressed by German Chancellor [Friedrich Merz](1967626.md), who stated during a bilateral meeting with Ukrainian President Zelenskyy that Berlin would actively support Kyiv's efforts to limit the exodus of military-age men and facilitate their return, emphasizing their importance for Ukraine's defense and future reconstruction.[^cite_note-407][^cite_note-408] Furthermore, representatives from [Poland](22936.md), [Finland](10577.md), [Sweden](5058739.md), and [Austria](26964606.md) also voiced support for the proposal during EU ministerial meetings, arguing that restricting automatic residency rights for individuals subject to mobilization is necessary to help maintain Ukraine's military mobilization capacity and support its war effort.[^cite_note-Reuters_2026-06-04-405][^cite_note-409]

Following these discussions, on 26 June 2026, the [European Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_der_Leyen_Commission_II) formally proposed extending the temporary protection framework for displaced Ukrainians until March 2028.[^cite_note-410] At the request of Kyiv, the proposal introduced restrictions excluding newly arriving Ukrainian men of military age from receiving automatic temporary protection status.[^cite_note-411]

### Hostility towards refugees

According to a 2023 survey, 57% of Ukrainian refugees in the Czech Republic experienced verbal— aggression because of their nationality, and 5% said they had been physically attacked.[^cite_note-412] The Czech Republic hosted more Ukrainian refugees per capita than any other country in the world. Hostility towards Ukrainian refugees was widespread among poorer and less educated Czechs,[^cite_note-balkaninsight-czech-413] and among the [Romani minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_the_Czech_Republic) as Institutional flaws that have pitted two of Europe's most vulnerable against each other.[^cite_note-414] Japanese-born Czech far-right politician [Tomio Okamura](37423659.md) claimed that the "widespread aid to Ukrainians" by the Czech government "is disproportionate to the aid to our socially vulnerable citizens".[^cite_note-balkaninsight-czech-413]

There was also growing hostility towards Ukrainian refugees in Poland.[^cite_note-415]

### Racial discrimination

See also: [Disinformation in the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine § Anti-refugee sentiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disinformation_in_the_2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine#Anti-refugee_sentiments)

#### Treatment at the borders

A few days into the crisis, claims of discrimination by border guards and other authorities against non-European and [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) were reported by some of those fleeing Ukraine. There were reports of people being forced to move to the backs of queues, deboarded from buses, prevented from crossing the border, and, in one report, being beaten by guards.[^cite_note-416][^cite_note-417][^cite_note-France24_28_Feb_2022-418][^cite_note-\:0-419][^cite_note-420][^cite_note-421] Some Indians in Ukraine said they were targeted after India chose to abstain from condemning Russia at the UN.[^cite_note-422][^cite_note-423] Some Indians and Africans were reportedly harassed and threatened by Polish nationalists after crossing into Poland.[^cite_note-424] On 1 March, [Filippo Grandi](24484119.md), of UNHCR, acknowledged that discrimination against non-Ukrainians had occurred at some borders, but he did not believe it was from state policies.[^cite_note-Indy1322White-425][^cite_note-\:17-426]

The [African Union](10634933.md) called attempts to prevent Africans from crossing the border [racist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racist) and not in line with [international law](8195726.md).[^cite_note-427][^cite_note-BBC_1_March_2022-428] On 2 March, Ukrainian foreign minister [Dmytro Kuleba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmytro_Kuleba) stated that Africans "need to have equal opportunity" to leave the country and he also stated that "Ukraine's government spares no effort to solve the problem."[^cite_note-\:17-426][^cite_note-429] On 3 March, Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) held talks with Indian prime minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) and told him that he had instructed Russian soldiers to "ensure the safe exit of Indian nationals from the armed conflict zone."[^cite_note-430]

In 2020, Ukraine had over 76,000 foreign students, with [India](14533.md) and African countries each making up one quarter of the total number. With their affordable tuition, straightforward visa requirements, and the possibility of permanent residency, Ukrainian universities were seen as an entry point to the European job market.[^cite_note-431] [Afghans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghans_in_Ukraine) constitute the largest immigrant group in the country, having arrived as early as the 1980s.[^cite_note-432] Andriy Demchenko, a spokesperson for the [Ukrainian border guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Border_Guard_Service_of_Ukraine), said that allegations of segregation at the borders are untrue.[^cite_note-\:0-419] On 28 February, [Krzysztof Szczerski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzysztof_Szczerski), Poland's ambassador to the UN, reported that the refugees who were admitted from Ukraine on that day alone represented 125 countries.[^cite_note-433] Since then, EU Commissioner [Ylva Johansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ylva_Johansson) has stated that its borders are open to people in Ukraine from third countries who want to travel to their home countries, and individuals in need of protection can apply for asylum.[^cite_note-434][^cite_note-435]

On 2 March, the German embassy[^cite_note-436] as well as the EU delegation in Kenya called for verification of the postings on Kenyan social media, cautioning that unsubstantiated claims had been spread.[^cite_note-437][^cite_note-438] According to the German news program *[Tagesschau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagesschau_\(German_TV_programme\))*, such allegations are grave because they are in line with the narrative of Vladimir Putin, who has justified his attack on Ukraine with, the need to free the country from "Nazis".[^cite_note-439] On Polish social media, the amount of both pro-Russian and racist content saw an increase following the invasion, and [fake news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_news) about supposed crimes which were committed by, or against refugees were partially spread by [pro-Kremlin accounts](142690.md#Russo-Ukrainian_War).[^cite_note-440][^cite_note-441][^cite_note-442][^cite_note-443]

#### By politicians and mainstream media

Differences between the policies, border treatment and [media portrayal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_portrayal_of_the_Ukrainian_crisis) of Ukrainian refugees compared to other groups, in particular those during the [2015 European migrant crisis](46415102.md), have been criticised. Specific issues include alleged harsher treatment and more restrictions placed on [Syrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_refugee), [Afghan](3657017.md), [Iraqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_of_Iraq), and other refugees, in contrast to the relatively liberal and welcoming response to native Ukrainian refugees.[^cite_note-444][^cite_note-445][^cite_note-446] Portrayal by some Western media and politicians of Ukraine as a country "where you wouldn't expect that" \[war] and its people as "white", "Christian", "relatively civilized", "relatively European", "like us", and having "blue eyes and blonde hair" has also been criticised.[^cite_note-447][^cite_note-448] *[Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* columnist [Kenan Malik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenan_Malik) wrote that there is an irony in such Western reporting, as there was a ["long history of bigotry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Slavic_sentiment) towards [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slav)", seeing them as "primitive" and “Asiatic” and "a born slave" [^cite_note-449]

Professor of philosophy Serena Parekh suggested that besides racism there are other factors explaining the different treatment in earlier crises:[^cite_note-\:10-450] including that current Ukrainian refugees are almost entirely women, children and elderly people.[^cite_note-451] Under the purview of the visa liberalisation agreement in force since 2017, Ukrainians with [biometric passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biometric_passport) were already allowed to enter the [Schengen zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen_zone) and stay for up to 90 days within any 180 days period without a visa;[^cite_note-452] therefore, there was never any question of whether they should be allowed to enter any Schengen zone country.[^cite_note-\:10-450] The welcoming approach witnessed in [Central and Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_and_Eastern_Europe) has been further explained by its geographical and language proximity to Ukraine, large Ukrainian diasporas, shared history and traumatizing experiences of [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet) aggression and occupation.[^cite_note-\:10-450]

[Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) have suffered since they often lack the civil status documentation needed to access humanitarian assistance.[^cite_note-453] Some local officials have allegedly refused to accommodate Romani refugees from Ukraine in their territories.[^cite_note-454]

## Pets and zoo animals



[Kyiv Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Zoo) in 2020

Domestic animals and animals in [zoos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoo) were caught up in the invasion, with many border crossing regulations of [microchipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microchipping) and [vaccinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_vaccination) in effect. Pets entering the EU from a third country would normally have to include an identification document or [pet passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_passport) that includes information on anti-rabies vaccinations and any other preventative health measures. Additionally, dogs, cats and [ferrets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferret) must undergo a [rabies](16946852.md) antibody [titration test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titration).[^cite_note-455] Many EU authorities and governments of neighboring countries have since removed or relaxed the requirements needed for pets to cross the borders with their owners.[^cite_note-456][^cite_note-457]

While some delayed leaving Ukraine in order to leave with their pets, others were forced to give their pets to shelters or leave them with relatives who were staying behind.[^cite_note-458] Some international organizations, such as the [International Fund for Animal Welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Fund_for_Animal_Welfare) and [PETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PETA) and independent organizations or sanctuaries, have offered support in the form of food, veterinary supplies for the animals and wages along with housing for the caretakers.[^cite_note-459] Others who work with animal shelters or the [Kyiv Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Zoo) have refused to evacuate, when it would be impossible to safely evacuate all the animals due to their numbers or size.[^cite_note-460][^cite_note-461] The Feldman Ecopark Zoo (outside [Kharkiv](2788558.md)) reported the death and wounding of some of their animals due to damage to their facilities.[^cite_note-462] A lion and a wolf were evacuated from a zoo in [Zaporizhzhia](1873579.md), Ukraine to a zoo in [Rădăuți](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C4%83d%C4%83u%C8%9Bi), Romania.[^cite_note-463]
