# Eastern Ukraine



The [oblasts](994124.md) included in "eastern Ukraine" vary.\


&#x20; Red – always included

&#x20; Orange – sometimes included

**Eastern Ukraine** or **East Ukraine** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Східна Україна, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Skhidna Ukrayina*; [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Восточная Украина, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *Vostochnaya Ukraina*) is primarily the territory of [Ukraine](31750.md) east of the [Dnipro](380138.md) (or Dnieper) river, particularly [Kharkiv](494901.md), [Luhansk](494904.md) and [Donetsk](494889.md) [oblasts](994124.md) (provinces). [Dnipropetrovsk](494909.md) and [Zaporizhzhia](1873579.md) oblasts are often also regarded as "eastern Ukraine".&#x20;

Almost a third of the country's population lives in the region, which includes several cities with population of around a million. Within Ukraine, the region is the most highly urbanized, particularly portions of central Kharkiv Oblast, south-western Luhansk Oblast, central, northern and eastern areas of Donetsk Oblast.

## Geography

The region stretches from southern areas of the [Central Russian Upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Russian_Upland) to the northern shores of the [Sea of Azov](78019.md), from the eastern border with [Russia](25391.md) to [Black Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Lowland) and [Dnieper Lowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Lowland) (including the left bank of the [Dnipro](989795.md)) to the west. Other than the Dnipro, the major river of eastern Ukraine is the [Siverskyi Donets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets). The main economic region of that part of the country is the [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas), whose name is a contraction of "Donets Basin", named after the Siverskyi Donets. The region became the scene of an armed conflict between Ukraine and Russian proxy forces.

### Cities and population

See also: [List of cities in Ukraine](4077166.md)

The territory is heavily urbanized and commonly associated with the Donbas. The three largest metropolitan cities form an industrial triangle within the region. Among the major cities with population of over 200,000 people are [Kharkiv](2788558.md), [Dnipro](989795.md), [Donetsk](562951.md)-[Makiivka](1388010.md), [Zaporizhzhia](1873579.md), [Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariupol), [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk), [Horlivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horlivka) and [Kamianske](1261408.md). Donetsk and Makiivka create [urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl), with very close proximity to other important cities such as Horlivka and [Yenakieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yenakieve).

## Demographics

### Ethnicity and language

According to the 2001 census, the majority of eastern Ukraine's population are ethnic [Ukrainians](145144.md), while ethnic [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Ukraine) form a significant minority. The most common language in urban areas of the Luhansk and Donetsk Oblasts is [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language_in_Ukraine), having long dominated in government and the media. When Ukraine became independent, there were no Ukrainian-language schools in [Donetsk](562951.md).[^cite_note-\:0-2]

Noticeable cultural differences in the region (compared with the rest of Ukraine except [Southern Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ukraine)) are more "positive views" on the Russian language[^cite_note-RatingJuly12-3][^cite_note-4] and on the [Soviet era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_era)[^cite_note-snyder-5][^cite_note-KIISS1313-6] and more "negative views" on [Ukrainian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_nationalism).[^cite_note-snyder-5]

Effective in August 2012, a [law on regional languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislation_on_languages_in_Ukraine) entitled any local language spoken by at least a 10% of the population to be declared official within that area.[^cite_note-7] Within weeks, Russian was declared as a regional language in several southern and eastern oblasts and cities.[^cite_note-8] From that point Russian could be used in those cities'/oblasts' administrative office work and documents.[^cite_note-NewUklang2892012-9] On 23 February 2014, the law on regional languages was abolished, making Ukrainian the sole state language at all levels even in eastern Ukraine,[^cite_note-BBCRLLout-10] but this vote was vetoed by acting President [Oleksandr Turchynov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandr_Turchynov) on 2 March.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] A February 2015 survey found that eastern oblasts (61%) preferred "second official regional language" over (31%) "state language" status for Russian.[^cite_note-LangApr2015-13] The 2012 law on regional languages was repealed by the [Constitutional Court of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Ukraine) on 28 February 2018 when it ruled the law unconstitutional.[^cite_note-14]

According to a 2016 survey of [religion in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Ukraine) held by the [Razumkov Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razumkov_Center), 73.5% of the population in eastern Ukraine were [Christians](18337522.md) (63.2% [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox), 8.1% simply Christians, 1.0% [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism), and 0.3% [Latin Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church)), 0.5% were [Muslims](19541.md), 0.3% were [Jewish](25955086.md), and 0.3% were [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu). Not religious and other believers not identifying with any of the listed major religious institutions constituted about 24.7% of the population.[^cite_note-Razumkov2016-15] It also showed that approximately 55.6% of the population of eastern Ukraine (which in Razumkov's mapping excluded Donbas and consisted of the regions immediately to the west of it) declared to be believers, while 13.4% declared to be undecided or non-believers, and 3.5% declared to be atheist.[^cite_note-Razumkov2016-15]

## Politics



[1991 Ukrainian independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Ukrainian_independence_referendum), "Yes" votes by region

A large majority of voters in eastern Ukraine (83% or more in each [oblast](994124.md)) approved Ukraine's declaration of independence in the [1991 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Ukrainian_independence_referendum), though the majority was not as big as in the west.[^cite_note-ESlower-16][^cite_note-17]

A 2007 survey by the Razumkov Centre asked "Would you like to have your region separated from Ukraine and joined another state?" In eastern Ukraine, 77.9% of respondents disagreed, 10.4% agreed, and the rest were undecided.[^cite_note-Raz070618-18]

In [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Ukraine), voters of the eastern (and southern) oblasts of Ukraine voted for parties ([Communist Party of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Ukraine) (CPU), [Party of Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Regions)) and presidential candidates ([Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych)) with a [pro-Russian](2541825.md) and [status quo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_quo) platform.[^cite_note-EWparties-19][^cite_note-Umland-20][^cite_note-KuzioPORvotersdontgoaway-21] The electorate of CPU and Party of Regions was very loyal to them.[^cite_note-KuzioPORvotersdontgoaway-21] But following the [2014 Ukrainian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Ukrainian_Revolution) the Party of Regions collapsed[^cite_note-22] and the CPU was banned and declared illegal.[^cite_note-23]

In a poll conducted by the [Kyiv International Institute of Sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_International_Institute_of_Sociology) in the first half of February 2014, 25.8% of those polled in eastern Ukraine believed that "Ukraine and Russia must unite into a single state", nationwide this percentage was 12.5%.[^cite_note-KIIS140304-24]

In 2014, the [2014 pro-Russian unrest in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_pro-Russian_unrest_in_Ukraine) took place in parts of eastern Ukraine. Some protesters came from Russia to support the unrest.[^cite_note-protour-25][^cite_note-MT140408-26] The [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)) resulted in thousands of deaths and over a million people leaving their homes.[^cite_note-OCHA2732015-27] Today, parts of the region are controlled by the self-proclaimed and internationally not-recognized *Donetsk People's Republic* and *Luhansk People's Republic*. On 24 February 2022, the region became the scene of the [eastern Ukraine offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ukraine_offensive).

A November 2015 poll carried out by [Rating Group Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rating_\(sociological_group\)) in the Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, except in the [Donetsk People's Republic](42425747.md) (DPR) and the [Luhansk People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_People's_Republic) (LPR)-controlled areas, found that 75% of residents wanted the entire Donbas region to stay in Ukraine, 7% said that it should join Russia, 1% wanted it to become an independent country, and 3% said that the DPR and the LPR-controlled territories should leave and the rest of Donbas remain in Ukraine.[^cite_note-IRI-Nov2015-28] When asked if Russian-speaking citizens are under pressure or threat, 82% said 'no' and 11% said 'yes'.[^cite_note-IRI-Nov2015-28] 2% "definitely" and 7% "somewhat" supported Russia sending troops to "protect" Russian-speakers in Ukraine, while 71% did not.[^cite_note-IRI-Nov2015-28] 50% wanted Ukraine to remain a unitary country, 14% wanted it to be a federal country, 13% said it should remain unitary but without [Crimea](163045.md), and 7% wanted it to be divided into several countries.[^cite_note-IRI-Nov2015-28] If they had to choose between the [Eurasian Customs Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Customs_Union) and the [European Union](9317.md), 24% in eastern Ukraine (including [Kharkiv Oblast](494901.md)) preferred the ECU and 20% preferred the EU (in Donbas: 33% for the ECU, 21% for the EU). On joining NATO, 15% were for, 15% were against, and most said that they would not vote or it was difficult to answer (in Donbas: 16% for, 47% against).[^cite_note-IRI-Nov2015-28] Eastern Ukrainians were less likely to vote in parliamentary elections.[^cite_note-IRI-Nov2015-28]
