The **minister of defence of Ukraine** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Міністр оборони України, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Ministr oborony Ukrainy*) is the head of the [Ministry of Defence](14284651.md), which is in charge of the [Armed Forces of Ukraine](42799055.md), the second-largest military power in Europe after its Russian counterpart. The Minister of Defence is appointed by the [president](20688479.md), but this has to be confirmed by a majority vote in the [Verkhovna Rada](844993.md), the parliament of Ukraine.[^cite_note-Heletei_appointed_Ukrainian_defense_minister_LV-1] From 1 January 2019, Ukraine has had [civilian control of the military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilian_control_of_the_military) by requiring that the Minister of Defence be a civilian, although they may have recently resigned from the military.[^cite_note-818Poltorak537-2]

Since [Ukrainian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_independence) from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in 1991, there have been 20 defence ministers (not including acting ones).

The current Minister of Defense is [Yevhenii Khmara](82035553.md), officially since 19 August 2026 and acting since 16 July 2026. The longest-serving Minister of Defense is [Oleksandr Kuzmuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandr_Kuzmuk) who served for five years, a total of 2,063 days.

The longest serving as an acting Minister of Defense is [Valeriy Ivashchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeriy_Ivashchenko) who served for a total 279 days (over 9 months).

### First deputy ministers

Created on 27 May 1992, the office of deputy ministers was expanded on 4 June 1992 with the [chief of the General Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_General_Staff_\(Ukraine\)) holding the post until 8 February 2002. Since 10 September 2003, the post was "demilitarized", held only by civilian or retired military personnel. The first deputy serves as an acting minister in absence of officially appointed minister unless specified otherwise.

## Earlier military ministers and secretaries

Historically, the ministry was preceding by various other governmental institutions. The very first Ukrainian representative in military affairs was [Symon Petlyura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symon_Petlyura), appointed by [Volodymyr Vynnychenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volodymyr_Vynnychenko) to [General Secretariat of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Secretariat_of_Ukraine) in the summer of 1917. Later in December 1917 after establishing the Bolshevik government in [Kharkiv](2788558.md) the Military Secretary of Ukraine was opposed by the Military Secretary of Soviet Ukraine whom the first was [Vasyl Shakhrai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasyl_Shakhrai). Note that the first ministers of Ukraine were not specialists in military affairs, particularly such as [Mykola Porsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykola_Porsh).

The [Ukrainian People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Army) was in terrible condition and it was not until the power in the country was taken over by the former head of the [Russian Imperial Retinue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._I._M._Retinue) and hereditary Ukrainian Cossack [Pavlo Skoropadsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlo_Skoropadsky), under leadership of which the new minister became Aleksandr Rogoza (also known as Oleksandr Rohoza). Rogoza was instrumental in restructuring the ministry and recruiting numerous former Russian Imperial generals who pledged their allegiance to the government of Ukraine. By the end of 1918 Bolsheviks recreated the Ukrainian Soviet government and to its office of military affairs was appointed [Nikolai Podvoisky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Podvoisky), former [narkom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narkom) of Military Affairs of [Soviet Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic) who played a key role in the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution). Around that time there was created the government of the [West Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ukrainian_People's_Republic), the office of military affairs of which was headed by [Dmytro Vitovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmytro_Vitovsky) who was a specialist in [special operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_operations), particularly the [mountain warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_warfare). Vitovsky played a key role in securing the city of [Lviv](49968.md) and ensuring the proclamation of independence of the new Ukrainian state from the disintegrating [Austro-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungary).
